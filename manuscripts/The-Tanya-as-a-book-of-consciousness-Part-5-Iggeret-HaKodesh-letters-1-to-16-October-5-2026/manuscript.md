The Iggeret HaKodesh (the Holy Letters) are real letters, sent by the Alter Rebbe to his Chassidim over many years: thanks for Talmud finished, a warning after his release from prison, appeals for the poor and for the Land of Israel. Read as a map of consciousness, they are the part of the Tanya where the inner states meet the hand and the purse. Almost every letter comes back to charity (tzedakah), and each time it opens a different state: faith as the floor the mind stands on, the smallness that grows with nearness, the inner point of the heart set free, the truth that only comes as a gift, the seed that has to vanish, the still wine after the lees have fallen, the good that is great even when it hides. The movement these sixteen letters trace is from a self that lives "for itself" (legarmeihu) to a self that is a channel: it feels how poor every world is before Him, and so it gives the way He gives, past the measure. Each letter below is one section, read from the Hebrew, in the brief's fixed shape.

## Letter 1 · Faith that holds the thinking up

**The state.** Emunah (faith, the plain knowing that the One is here) as the "loins" (motnayim) of the soul: the part that holds up the thinking head and the feeling arms. It gets its strength from learning His will in halacha (practical Torah law), and it is exercised in long morning prayer.

**The chapter as consciousness.** It opens in joy. The Rebbe has heard that the whole Talmud was finished in many towns, and he reads a verse over it: "She girds her loins with strength." The loins, he says, are what hold up the whole body with the head standing on top, and they are what carry the body to where it wants to go.

In the soul, the loins are true faith in the One Infinite, who fills all worlds and surrounds all worlds, with no place empty of Him, above without end and below without end, in all four directions, and so too in time and in the soul. From inside, this faith is not a thought you have. It is what you are standing on while you think. Take it away and the head has nothing under it. The "head" is the mind that looks deep into His greatness in space, time and soul, and into His kindness in making us a people near to Him, able to cleave to Him truly.

Here he sets a surprising scale of nearness. "One hour of return and good deeds in this world is finer than all the life of the World to Come." The World to Come is only a glow from the Shechinah (His dwelling presence), made with a single letter of His Name. Return and good deeds bring a person near to His very Being and Essence. So the one standing here, in a plain body, doing a plain deed, is closer than the soul bathing in paradise. The state is strange: the lowest place turns out to be the nearest.

From this looking, "as water shows a face back to a face," love and awe are born in the heart, whether reasoned or natural. Sometimes it is a cry of the heart, sometimes sparks and a strong flame: ratzo, the running toward. Then comes shov, the turning back: the fear of G-d settles in the heart, a shame before His greatness, like the people at Sinai who "trembled and stood far off." These two, love and awe, are the arms and body of the soul.

Then the question the letter was written for. Who gives the loins their strength, so they can hold up the head and the arms? Learning the halachot of the Oral Torah. Halacha is His revealed will. Torah comes out of wisdom, but its root is higher than wisdom, in the supreme will, which sits "like a crown" over the head. Here the picture breaks, and the break is the teaching. In a body, the loins are below the head and get nothing from above it. In the soul, faith sits below the thinking mind, yet it is fed from a crown above the mind. The lowest support and the highest source touch, and the mind in the middle is held from both sides.

The time to strengthen all of it is the morning prayer, an hour of mercy. He asks them to pray from the depth of the heart, to pour out the soul like water, "until the soul is wrung out." Then he turns practical. On weekdays only those who have time, and can pray for about an hour and a half, should lead the prayers; the busy should lead on Shabbat and holidays, when they have time and carry even more duty to pray slowly. And he warns, sharply, that he will send quiet observers, and one who could pray at length and is lazy will be kept at a distance when he comes to hear "the words of the living G-d." The harshness is honest: he treats laziness in prayer as closing the door on yourself.

**The movement.** From thought about G-d that floats, or a heart that flares and fades, to a mind that stands on faith, with faith fed daily by learning His will and brought into a long, slow morning prayer. What carries a person across: a halacha learned every day, and time given to prayer.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "He fills this place, and no place is empty of Him." - Go through it: under your feet, above your head, on four sides; in this year and this hour; in this soul reading. Then bring it to one real thing: the floor you are standing on right now is not empty of Him. - The picture breaks here: the loins hold the head from below, yet their strength comes from a crown above the head. - Stop at the edge: His very Essence, which the deed reaches and the mind cannot picture. - Say to Him: "Hold me up, so I can think of You." - One act: learn one halacha today, and make tomorrow's morning prayer a little longer.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: thinking about G-d feels grounded and it goes somewhere, into prayer and into a deed. - Fake: bright ideas about G-d that thrill and float, and never show up for the morning prayer.

## Letter 2 · The nearer, the smaller

**The state.** Shiflut ruach (lowness of spirit) that grows with every kindness received: the more a person is kamei (before Him), the more he feels as nothing. Its opposite is the "kindness of the husk," which swells with every gift.

**The chapter as consciousness.** This letter was written after the Rebbe came home from prison in Petersburg, freed, while his opponents were shamed. He opens with Jacob's words: "I am too small for all the kindnesses." With every kindness G-d does for a person, he says, the person should become very low in spirit.

Here is why, from inside. Kindness is His "right arm," the embrace: "His right hand holds me close." To receive a kindness is to be drawn nearer to G-d than before. And before Him, everything counts as nothing. So the nearer you are, the more you are nothing, empty, zero. This is not a rule laid on top of nearness. It is what nearness feels like. When you stand next to something truly great, you do not feel bigger. You feel how small you are, and the feeling is not painful. It is almost relief. "From afar G-d appeared to me": the closer He comes, the farther you feel from being anything.

That is the holy right side, the kindness of Abraham, who said "I am dust and ashes." It was also Jacob's trait. G-d had promised him, "I am with you," and still he was afraid of Esau. Why? Because so many kindnesses had made him very small in his own eyes, so small that he felt unworthy of being saved, and he feared "perhaps sin will cause it," as if he had sinned. The state here is subtle: the gift itself makes the receiver doubt that he deserves it, and that doubt is holiness, not weakness.

The opposite state is Ishmael, "kindness of the husk" (chesed dekelipah). There, the bigger the gift, the taller the person grows, the coarser the spirit, the wider the heart swells. Most of us know both. After a win, something in us straightens up and wants to look down.

So he makes a strong announcement to all his followers, about the many kindnesses G-d has done. Hold to Jacob's trait, "the remnant of His people": a person who sees himself as leftovers, as extra, with no need of him at all. Do not let the heart rise over your brothers. Do not open your mouth against them, and do not jeer at them; he forbids it with a fearful warning. Lower your spirit and your heart before every person, with the trait of truth that is Jacob's. "A soft answer turns away anger." And maybe then G-d will put into your brothers' hearts, "as water shows a face back to a face," the same softness toward you.

Where the picture breaks: Jacob's fear and "perhaps I sinned" could look like low self-worth. The letter means the reverse. The lowness grows from being held, not from being rejected.

**The movement.** From the swelling that follows a gift or a victory, to the quiet smallness of one who feels how near he has been brought. What carries a person across: seeing the gift as nearness, and nearness as the place where everything counts as nothing; then the act of the mouth, a soft answer.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "The nearer to Him, the more I am nothing." - Go through it: name one real kindness in your life this week. See it as His arm drawing you close. Before Him, what are you? - The picture breaks: a child hugged by a king grows proud; before Him the embrace makes you small. - Stop at the edge: "before Him," where all is nothing, which the mind can taste only as smallness. - Say to Him: "I am too small for all Your kindnesses." - One act: today, give a soft answer to someone you would rather beat in an argument.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: after something good, you speak more quietly, and people who opposed you find you gentler. - Fake: a show of humility that still keeps score of who lost.

## Letter 3 · Armor made of small coins

**The state.** The steady state of one who gives small amounts again and again, and trusts that each one joins "a great sum." Charity (tzedakah) as a surrounding light (or makif, a light that rings a person from outside) that opens the way for blessing and closes it against harm.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "He put on charity like armor." The sages explain: armor is made of many small scales that join into one great coat, and so with charity, each small coin joins into a great sum. Armor is scales laid over holes, so no arrow can get in through the holes. Charity works the same way.

To see how, he sets out a map, which I compress. Every commandment makes a garment for the soul out of the Infinite light that "surrounds all worlds" (sovev). There is also the light that "fills all worlds" (memale), which shines inside the ten sefirot (His traits as the soul knows them) all the way down, through a great contraction. When a person does a commandment, the surrounding light comes and shines inside, and it joins with the inner light. That joining is called the union of the Holy One and His Shechinah. A faint glow of it, greatly narrowed, becomes the garment the soul wears in Eden.

This is why "there is no reward for a mitzvah in this world." This coarse, narrow world can only receive light through the sefirot, which are called a "body," and even the highest of them is like a body compared to Him. But charity is different: "a person eats its fruits in this world." So charity makes "holes," as it were, in the high garment around that body, and through them long life, wealth, honor and joy flow down into this world.

From inside, this is the state of an open hand that is also a protected one. If there were only holes, the outside forces would feed on the blessing as it came down, and harm could reach the giver. So the surrounding light shines again and closes each hole, since it is Infinite and beyond anything that could grab at it. That is why the sages said "joins into a great sum," with stress: "Great is G-d and very much praised," without end. The small coin does not add up the way coins add up in a jar. Each one is joined into something that has no end.

What causes the blessing to come down at all is that union, which adds light beyond what came at the start of creation, and it begins at the "head," the first of the sefirot. That is the "helmet of salvation on his head." "Salvation" means a turning of His face toward you, as in "G-d turned to Abel and his offering." "May G-d make His face shine upon us": "upon us" through charity. And "He sows charities and grows salvations."

Where the picture breaks: real armor is cold iron you hide behind. This armor is His own light around you, and it is made by opening your hand, not closing it. And the holes are the blessing.

**The movement.** From the thought "my small gift doesn't matter" to the settled sense that every coin joins something endless, and that giving both opens you and guards you. What carries a person across: understanding the picture in detail, then the act itself, repeated.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Each coin joins a great sum." - Go through it: think of the smallest coin you gave this month. See it as one scale over one hole, and the scales joining, and the sum having no end, because He has no end. - The picture breaks: this armor opens you; the holes are where the blessing comes in. - Stop at the edge: the surrounding light that closes each hole is Infinite. You can name it and not see it. - Say to Him: "Let this small coin be joined to You." - One act: give a small coin today, and another tomorrow.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: you stop needing to give big to feel it counted; small gifts feel full. - Fake: giving as insurance, a deal to buy protection, with the heart shut.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 3 (line 1035): אף צדקה כל פרוטה ופרוטה מצטרפת לחשבון גדול*

## Letter 4 · The inner point of the heart

**The state.** The two layers of the heart: its outside, the fire of love that comes from contemplating His greatness, and its inside, nekudat pnimiyut halev (the inner point of the heart), which is above thought and is the spark of G-d in the soul. The state is that point in exile, wrapped in this world's wants, and its release through giving from one's own life.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "Israel is redeemed only through charity." He asks why the verse says "Charity walks before him" and not "goes." The answer starts from "My heart says to You, seek My face," which he reads as: seek the inside of the heart. The flame of G-dly fire in the heart has two sides.

The outside of the heart is the fire that rises when you understand His greatness and think it through, until a strong love burns like sparks. It is real, and it is born from thought. The inside of the heart is a point in its very depth, higher than any understanding a person could reach by thinking. "From the depths I called You": from the depth of the heart. He gives a picture from ordinary life. Sometimes a matter is so large that a person's whole life hangs on it, and it touches the inner point of the heart. Then he does things and says things with no reasoning at all. Everyone has felt this: a moment when something came from further in than thought. The same is true in the service of the heart. That point is above thought because it is a glow of the highest wisdom, and in it the light of G-d Himself is dressed and hidden. It is the spark of G-d in the soul.

Why doesn't everyone reach it? Because in most of us that point is in exile. It is the exile of the Shechinah itself, since this spark is His. How did it get there? The person dressed the inner point of his heart in the opposite side: in the soiled clothes of worldly matters and this world's cravings, called "Babylon." This covering is the "foreskin of the heart."

Just as circumcision has two steps, cutting the coarse layer and peeling the fine one, so the heart has coarse desires and fine ones. If only the coarse is cut, the point is still covered by a thin sack. The coarse layer a person removes himself: "Circumcise the foreskin of your hearts." The fine one is hard for a person; of it, the verse says for the days of Mashiach, "G-d will circumcise your heart, to love G-d with all your heart and all your soul, for the sake of your life." Meaning: because G-d alone is all your life. That is why this love comes from the inner point, above thought.

So the inner point is uncovered in two ways, and both come "when the mind is not looking." For the whole world, Mashiach. For each person, for a moment, in prayer from the depth of the heart, when the spark comes out of exile and rises to cleave to Him with a strong longing, "for the sake of your life." This is a gift from above, the shining of His face. It cannot be manufactured by thinking harder.

And yet a gift from above answers a move from below. The person has to begin the cutting himself. How? By giving tzedakah to G-d from his money, which is his life. Especially one who has little and gives from his very life. Especially money earned by his own hands, because in earning it his inner point was surely busy, many times, in buying and selling. When he now gives it away gladly, he redeems his soul from the pit: the inner point that was held captive in the coarse or fine husk. "Guard your heart above all keeping": "keeping" means a prison. Charity is the ransom and the paying of a debt. Then "charity walks before him": it leads the inside of the heart to G-d, and after that every mitzvah and all Torah rise through the inner heart, higher than they would rise through the outer heart alone. Charity is called "peace," because it makes peace between a person and his Father in heaven. He ends with thanks for the charity that stood by them in hard days.

**The movement.** From a heart that burns from the outside, or does not burn at all because its core is dressed in wanting, to the inner point uncovered, loving G-d because He is all your life. What carries a person across: first your own cut, giving from your earnings with gladness; then the gift from above, which comes in prayer when you are not looking for it.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "G-d alone is all my life, truly." - Go through it: what does my inner point, the place where I act without reasons, hang on right now? Money? Being right? Someone's approval? Name the one real thing. - The picture breaks: a prisoner cannot free himself, yet here you begin the cut and He finishes it. - Stop at the edge: the fine layer, which only He removes, when you are not looking. - Say to Him: "Take the thin covering off my heart. You are my life." - One act: give something that costs you, from money you worked for, and give it gladly.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: a moment in prayer when the longing comes from further in than any idea, and afterward what you earn feels less like your life. - Fake: a big stirring born only of thought, or a dramatic "from the depths" feeling worked up on purpose.

## Letter 5 · Wisdom that comes down into a word

**The state.** Wisdom that stays "his wisdom alone" versus wisdom that comes down and gives life. The movement from one to the other is a narrowing, like a great mind shrinking itself into one spoken letter for someone else. What draws it down is chesed (kindness) done below, and so the one who gives "makes the holy Name."

**The chapter as consciousness.** "And David made a name." The Zohar asks: who makes the holy Name each day? One who gives charity to the poor. And Rabbi Shimon wept as he said it. The letter explains how a coin could make a Name.

It starts inside the soul. The joy of souls in Eden is understanding: grasping something of the light there, each by his level. That is why the World to Come is called binah (understanding, the mind that spreads a thing out). Understanding draws from something higher: chochmah (wisdom), the first flash of a mind before it has become anything you could explain. It is hidden, and only a little trickles out into understanding. It is the small point of the yud in His Name, and it is called Eden, of which "no eye has seen."

Then he turns to something every speaker knows from inside. The letters of speech do not come from reasoning, and not from the nature of the mouth either. When you say b or v or m or p, your lips move differently for each, but you never decide how to move them. You want the letter, and the lips follow. Say a kamatz and the lips close by themselves; say a patach and they open. The letters come from a hidden place in the soul, before thought, from its will. That is why a baby can understand everything and still not speak. There is a depth in you below thinking that moves your mouth, and you have never once watched it work.

Letters have two sides. Their form, the shape of each sound, comes from that hidden mind. Their body is the breath: breath out of the heart becomes a plain voice in the throat, then splits into twenty-two sounds at the five places of the mouth. The breath is the letter heh, the "light letter," the one you say with almost nothing. So "this world was made with heh." The heh is fed from a higher heh, which is understanding, that spreads out and ends in the heart, and from there the breath comes out. The heh's written shape, spread in length and width, shows His kingship spreading everywhere through His word. I compress here a longer passage on how the letters above differ from ours.

Countless worlds, angels and souls are all made from endless combinations of His twenty-two letters, and they all know their Creator, because they live from the inside of the letters. This lowly world is too small to hold that. It gets only the outside of the letters, the breath, and inside the breath, hidden, are His traits, will and wisdom, fully one with the Infinite. (A line of Kabbalah about the outer vessels of the lowest sefirah becoming the soul of this world is compressed here.)

Now the picture. A very wise man who narrows his great mind into a single letter of speech makes a huge contraction and descent of his wisdom. So, and infinitely more, when "by the word of G-d the heavens were made." At the start, this came with no awakening from below at all; "there was no man to work the ground," only "He desires kindness." But once man was made, every stirring above comes from a stirring below, through the charity and kindness people do here. That is why the sages said that one who has only Torah without kindness does not even have Torah. Torah draws wisdom into the letters that build the world, a great descent, and a descent needs kindness above, which flows like water from high to low, and that kindness is woken by kindness below that revives the spirit of the lowly. "Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom... but in this... that I am G-d who does kindness." Kindness is what draws the life of wisdom down. Without it, it is "his wisdom" alone, and no life flows from it.

So there are two kinds of souls, the Arizal said: those who give their days to Torah, and those who give their days to kindness. The scholars' Torah draws light to the worlds of souls and angels. To draw it all the way down to this world, which is the greatest narrowing, needs those who spend most of their days in charity. They are called "the supporters of Torah." That is why charity is called a "deed" (maaseh): it draws light down to the world of deeds. And the Zohar says "who makes the Name," with stress on makes: charity below wakes kindness above, drawing from the yud of the Name to its heh, so it can reach this world.

He ends with the picture again, "to distinguish infinitely": a person does not speak to himself when he is alone, only to others, and only then narrows his mind into speech. Here the picture breaks: our speaking to others is a small thing; His is the whole world's life. "The wise will understand."

**The movement.** From wisdom kept inside, rich and useless, to wisdom narrowed into a word or a deed for someone who needs it. What carries a person across: kindness, above all charity; the chesed that bends down.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Kindness is what brings wisdom's life down." - Go through it: you want to say "b," and your lips move without your knowing how. Something deep in you is always coming down into your mouth. Now picture the whole world as His breath. - Bring it to one thing: something you understand well that someone near you needs, in small words. - The picture breaks: we talk to others because we lack; He speaks the world from pure giving. - Stop at the edge: the hidden point of wisdom that no eye has seen. - Say to Him: "Let what I know go down to someone." - One act: give charity today, and teach one small thing to someone who needs it.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: what you know starts to feel alive only when it reaches someone else. - Fake: learning that stays a private treasure, polished, and never bends down.

## Letter 6 · Mercy for your own spark, and the truth that is given

**The state.** Rachmanut (compassion, Jacob's trait) felt for the spark of G-d in your own soul, far from His face; and emet (truth): the love and awe that come as a gift from above, which alone are called "the truth of G-d," beyond anything the created mind can make.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "One who sows charity has a reward of truth." The reward for sowing charity is the trait of truth. And the verse says, "You give truth to Jacob." Does Jacob have no truth until G-d gives it from above?

Jacob's trait is compassion. To serve G-d by compassion is to feel a great pity rise in the heart for the spark of G-d in your soul, which is far from the light of His face while you walk in the dark of the world's empty things. This pity is not self-pity. It is the grief you would feel seeing a prince in rags. It comes from understanding His greatness: even the highest worlds count as truly nothing before Him, living only from the glow of a single letter of His Name.

Within that glow there are differences: upper worlds and lower ones, this world made with one letter and the World to Come with another, and every change in every world, every change of past, present and future, all from changes in how the letters combine. But of His very Being it says, "I, G-d, have not changed." As He is found above, so He is found below, exactly and equally. As He was alone before the six days of creation, so He is alone now. Because everything is as nothing before Him, the way one letter of your speech, or even of your thought, is nothing before your whole soul. And even that picture is only "to ease the ear"; truly "nothing compares to You." "The King alone exalted from then": as He was alone before creation, He is alone now, lifted above time.

Hold both at once and something breaks open in the heart. He is equally here. And the spark in you lives in a dark, dim body, "a snake's skin," easily soiled by every craving, and it would be lost if He did not shield it and give it strength to fight the body and win. The pity that rises is huge. That is the state: seeing where your soul actually lives, and feeling for it.

Now the difference that makes this a letter about truth. There are two kinds of love and awe. The first are born from understanding His greatness; they are the children of a created mind. The second come afterward from above as a gift: "I give you your priesthood as a service of gift." There is no comparison between them. Only the second are called "truth," because "the seal of the Holy One is truth," and all the truth in created beings counts as nothing before Him.

How does a person come to G-d's truth? By waking great mercy before G-d for the spark in his soul. That is Jacob, "the middle bar that runs from end to end," reaching from the highest heights down to the lowest, to bring G-d's truth into this dark world. "When I sit in darkness, G-d is my light."

But here the state meets its own trap, and he names it honestly. The mercy has to be true. And even if it is true by my own truth, how can my truth, which is created, wake the mercy of G-d's truth? Every person who has tried to feel sincerely knows this loop: am I really feeling this, or making it?

The answer is charity: mercy for someone who has nothing of his own, to revive the spirit of the lowly. A stirring below wakes a stirring above. "He wakes the sleeping": great hidden mercies come out into the open. That is why charity is called "sowing": it grows the truth from above. And especially charity for the Holy Land, of which it says, "Truth will sprout from the earth."

Where the picture breaks: a seed grows what was sown; here you sow your small mercy and what grows is His truth, which you could not sow.

**The movement.** From love and awe that are mine, always a little suspect, to love and awe given from above, which are true. What carries a person across: understanding in detail (He is equally here and above time, the spark is far and endangered), mercy for the spark, and then the act of mercy for someone who has nothing, which breaks the loop of trying to be sincere.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "As He is above, so He is here, equally." - Go through it: He was alone before the world, and He is alone now. Then look at where your own spark spent today: which rooms, which wants. - Bring it to one thing: feel pity for that spark the way you would for a child lost in a crowd. - The picture breaks: you cannot make your pity true; you can only sow. - Stop at the edge: "His seal is truth," which no created feeling reaches. - Say to Him: "Have mercy on the spark You put in me." - One act: give to someone who truly has nothing, and stop checking whether you felt enough.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: a love that arrives without your working it up, and you know you did not make it. - Fake: self-pity dressed as mercy for the soul, or endless checking of your own sincerity.

## Letter 7 · Your portion and your lot

**The state.** The state of one who has found his portion (chelek) and his lot (goral): the one particular light that his soul, in this life and this generation, is meant to hold with extra care. It is above reason, received like a lot is drawn, and it is felt as happiness.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "Happy are we, how good is our portion, how pleasant our lot." "G-d is my portion and my cup." "The lines have fallen for me in pleasant places." How can anyone have a "portion" of G-d, when He cannot be divided into parts?

Here is the map. G-d fills all worlds, above and below, down to this physical earth: "Do I not fill heaven and earth?" "I," truly, His very Being, not only His glory. And still He is holy, set apart from all of it, never caught inside it the way a soul is caught in a body. So nothing can take its life from His Being alone. What gives life is a glow from His Name, and He and His Name are one. That glow dresses itself inside every creature through many steps and many great narrowings.

Above, the glow is without limit. Coming down, it divides, in general, into 613, matching the 613 commandments, which are 613 kinds of this light, shining to the soul of a person, which is built of 248 limbs and 365 sinews. The person is the purpose of the whole descent. In detail, each commandment divides endlessly into halachot without number: "sixty queens," the tractates, "and maidens without number," the laws.

So too the souls. All souls were once in the first man. In general his soul divided into 613; in detail, into sparks without number, the souls of all Israel from the fathers until Mashiach. Jacob's beauty was like Adam's; he mended Adam's sin, and his soul too held all souls and was a chariot for the Torah above, which is called "Man." So "He called him El, G-d of Israel": El means the light of the Infinite drawn out from hidden into open inside a soul. And after Jacob, all the upright of heart who engage in Torah and commandments have the light shine openly in their souls, and most strongly in prayer.

Now the state itself. In general this is the same for everyone: one Torah for all. In detail, souls are not the same, according to when they come into the world. The Talmud asks a son, "In what was your father most careful?" and he answers, "In tzitzit." Generations are not the same either. Like the limbs of a body, each has its own work: the eye sees, the ear hears. In every commandment a particular light shines. Every soul must keep all 613, over its lives, but it needs one of them only for "extra" care, doubled and redoubled, far above its care for the rest. "Most careful": stress on "most."

And this extra light for particular souls does not come from reason or understanding. It is above knowledge. "So it rose in His thought." Its likeness below is a lot, drawn.

From inside, this is a quiet happiness. You did not argue your way into it. You find that one thing lights up for you with a force that has no reason, and you are careful with it beyond duty. It is not your hobby and not your talent; it is the place where His light meets you most. "How pleasant our lot."

Where the picture breaks: a lot looks like chance. This lot is the most intended thing about you.

**The movement.** From a generic life, or from measuring yourself by other people's gifts, to receiving your own portion with joy and guarding it with extra care, while keeping the whole. What carries a person across: seeing the one light divided into 613 and endless sparks, and noticing, over time, which commandment draws you beyond reason.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "How good is our portion, how pleasant our lot." - Go through it: He fills heaven and earth, His Being itself; His glow divides into commandments, into laws, into souls; one of those sparks is you. - Bring it to one thing: which commandment or kind of good do you do with more care than reason can explain? - The picture breaks: an eye does not choose to be an eye, and neither did you. - Stop at the edge: "so it rose in His thought," which no reason reaches. - Say to Him: "Thank You for my lot." - One act: do your one thing today with extra care, and do one other commandment you tend to neglect.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: gladness in your own part and no envy of someone else's. - Fake: a "special mission" that makes you feel above others, or picks the commandments that suit you and drops the rest.

## Letter 8 · The seed that has to disappear

**The state.** Giving as a seed: the gift has to leave you entirely, "rot in the earth," so that a power not yours can grow it. And what grows is gilui (revelation) of His light in prayer, far more than the giver gave, "a light that shines in darkness."

**The chapter as consciousness.** "He sows charities and grows salvations." Why is charity called sowing? He starts from Rabbi Elazar, who gave a coin to a poor man and then prayed, because "through charity I will see Your face."

What a person sees of G-d in his thought and intention during prayer, each by his measure, comes as a kind of charity: "the kindness of G-d... on those who fear Him." Above, in the higher worlds, the Infinite light shines so openly that those worlds dissolve in it and count as nothing, and those are the "palaces" with their angels and souls through which the order of the prayers climbs. From there the light shines down to this low world, on those who fear G-d and want to serve Him with the service of the heart. "G-d lights my darkness." This coming down is called "the kindness of G-d," and it is like water, which runs from high to low.

Above there is also gevurah (holding back), which narrows and hides His light so it will not show below. What decides it is the awakening from below. If a person behaves with chassidut (going past the line), giving life and kindness, the same is woken above. "By the measure a man measures, he is measured."

But then a real question, and he asks it plainly. That is not measure for measure. The giver gives this world's life, bread and coins. Fairly, he should get the World to Come's life in return. Why should he get the light of G-d itself lighting up his darkness in prayer, which is a higher return, above all of the World to Come?

The answer is the picture of the seed. When you sow a seed or plant a pit, the ear of grain or the tree and its fruit are not the seed's own essence. The seed rots in the earth. It is the growing power of the earth that brings up the ear and the tree. But the earth does not bring its power out into action except through a seed that rots and whose whole power is lost into the power of the earth, until they become one. Then the earth's power acts, and grows an ear like the seed, but many grains on one ear, many fruits on one tree, and fruit far finer than the pit that was planted. The seed is only a waking from below.

So too, every act of charity rises up as that seed to the root of the souls above, which the Zohar calls Knesset Yisrael, the Talmud calls the Shechinah, which holds all His traits, and first of them kindness. By that rising, "the kindness of G-d" itself wakes, His light showing itself to souls, openly, at least in prayer. His greatness has no end, "all before Him counts as nothing," yet "where you find His greatness, you find His humility," like water running down.

"A light shines in the darkness for the upright, gracious, merciful and righteous." By being gracious and merciful, a person causes G-d's light to shine on his soul, which is dressed in a body that stands in darkness, "a snake's skin." That is "salvation": darkness turned into light. It grows from charity sown in the higher earth, the Shechinah, called "a land of desire" because it dresses itself into the lower worlds to give them life. And most of all when sown in the lower Holy Land, set right opposite the higher one: there it is taken in at once, nothing in between, "this is the gate of heaven." Not so outside the land.

From inside, the state is letting go. The gift that you keep holding, keep counting, keep watching, does not rot, and so it cannot grow. The giver who lets the coin vanish into the other person's need, as if it never was his, has let his power be swallowed into a power not his. Then what comes back is not his size.

Where the picture breaks: a seed does not choose to rot; the giver does. And the earth grows by nature; here the "earth" is His kindness, and what grows is His face.

**The movement.** From giving that expects a fair return to giving that disappears, and from there to prayer lit from above past any measure. What carries a person across: the act of charity, given away fully, then prayer right after it.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "The seed rots in the earth, and they become one." - Go through it: a pit, buried, gone; a tree with a hundred fruits. Then your coin, gone into someone's hunger, and a light in your prayer you did not earn. - Bring it to one thing: something you gave that you are still keeping count of. - The picture breaks: you choose to let it rot. - Stop at the edge: His greatness and His humility in the same place. - Say to Him: "I let it go. Light my darkness." - One act: give a coin before you pray today, as Rabbi Elazar did.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: you forget what you gave, and your prayer is lit more than you can explain. - Fake: giving with an eye on the harvest, or reminding the receiver.

## Letter 9 · The Shema with your eyes open

**The state.** Two consciousnesses in one person: with eyes closed at the Shema, "G-d is One" everywhere; with eyes open at work, a world that stands alone and a life lived legarmeihu (for oneself). The letter calls that split a false witness, and the state it asks for is one sight: even earthly business done to give life, which makes a person resemble the One who gives life every moment.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "My beloved ones, my brothers and friends, dear as my own soul." He writes, he says, to remind and to wake sleepers who are deep in the sleep of "vanity of vanities," and to open the eyes of the blind so they will look and see. He wants all their hope, all their wanting, all their aim, the life of their spirit, to be in "the Source of living waters," the Life of all life, all the days of their lives, from soul down to flesh.

Then he names the sleep exactly. It is not that people have forgotten G-d. It is that their worldly affairs and their earning are done as if for themselves. He says the house of Israel should not be "like all the nations," who feed and support and honor their wives and children out of love, and leave it there. "Who is like Your people Israel, one nation on earth": one even on earth. Even in earthly matters, not to separate from the One of truth.

Here is the picture, and it cuts. Morning and evening a person says the Shema with his eyes closed: G-d is One, in all four directions, in the heavens above and the earth below. Then he opens his eyes, and "your eyes fly over it and it is gone." The One he just declared vanishes, and he walks into a world where his shop and his salary and his deals stand on their own and belong to him. The letter calls this testifying falsely. From inside, most of us know the two rooms: a real moment of unity in prayer, and then a door that shuts behind us.

What does it look like to keep the eyes open and the One still there? Not to leave business. To do all of it, not for ourselves, but to give life to souls that are parts of G-d, and to fill their lack with free kindness. By that we "make the form like its Maker," the One, whose "kindness is all day," a true kindness that gives life to the world and everything in it every single moment. He does not earn and keep. He gives. The one who earns in order to give is, in that very act, like Him. There is an order, from the Torah: a person's wife and children come first, then the righteous of the generation before his own children, then the righteous of the Land of Israel before those outside it.

Then the letter widens. The main service of G-d in these times, "the heels of Mashiach," is charity. "Israel is redeemed only through charity." When the sages said Torah study is equal to all acts of kindness, that was in their days, when Torah was the main service, and so they were great scholars. But now "the sukkah of David" has fallen down to the level of the feet and heels, which is asiyah (the world of doing). There is no way to truly cleave to it and turn its darkness into light except through doing as well: the act of charity. In G-d, "doing" means drawing life down, lower and lower, to the one who has nothing of his own. Whoever sacrifices his own inclination in this, and opens his hand and his heart, bends the other side, turns darkness into G-d's light, and will "see eye to eye when G-d returns to Zion."

Where the picture breaks: closed eyes look like the holy state and open eyes like the fallen one. The letter wants the open eyes to be the holy ones. The Shema is fully true only when you can say it looking at your cash drawer.

**The movement.** From a split life, one in prayer and alone at work, to a single life in which work itself is giving. What carries a person across: seeing the lie in the split, then turning the purpose of earning into giving life, in the Torah's order, and opening the hand.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Even in earthly things, not apart from the One." - Go through it: say the Shema's first line with your eyes closed. Then open them and look at the thing you work with: a desk, a tool, a phone. Is He gone? - Bring it to one thing: the next hour of work. Whose life is it for? - The picture breaks: the open eyes are meant to be the holy ones. - Stop at the edge: His kindness that gives life to the world every moment, which you can copy but never contain. - Say to Him: "Let my work be for giving, as Yours is." - One act: from today's earnings, set something aside for someone who has nothing, and do it before you spend on yourself beyond your family's needs.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: the One stays with you when you open your eyes after prayer, and your wallet feels like a tool for giving. - Fake: calling every gain "providing for my family," or giving that neglects the family the Torah puts first.

## Letter 10 · Kindness past the measure

**The state.** The state of one who knows he is "not whole" (lo tamnu) and answers it with rav chesed (great kindness), giving past the limit. Beneath it is a sight: every world is dalet, "poor," with nothing of its own before Him, and so He, too, gives without measure.

**The chapter as consciousness.** After his greetings he opens with a rebuke that gives life, from the prophet who saw the Temple burn: "G-d's kindnesses, for we are not finished." The grammar is strange; it should say "they are not finished." And the Zohar says there are two kinds of kindness: "world kindness," and a higher kindness called "great kindness."

He builds the map, which I compress. Torah is called "strength," a word for gevurah (holding back, measure), and yet its root is His kindness, His right hand. It draws G-d's light into the worlds through a person who draws the light onto himself by doing the commandments, which are like the limbs of the King, vessels for His light; and from that light, love and awe come into each commandment. But first this light was dressed in His gevurah, called "fire," which narrows it, so it can dress itself in physical deeds: tzitzit, tefillin, offerings, charity.

Even the commandments of the heart, love and awe, are measured. A love for G-d with no end, no limit, no measure, no person could hold in his heart and still stay alive in his body for a moment. At Sinai, when the Infinite light showed itself in speech, "their souls flew out." So almost every commandment has a set size: tzitzit twelve thumbs long, tefillin two fingers square, a lulav of four handbreadths, a sukkah of seven, a shofar of one, a mikveh of forty measures, offerings of set ages. Charity too, though the world stands on it, has a measure: a fifth is the finest, a tenth is middling. That is "world kindness," His kindness dressed inside the worlds, woken by the charity people do for each other. The world is measured, so the kindness in it is measured.

Here is the turn. All that is for one who kept the Torah without turning a hair to the right or left. But one who has sinned has made his way crooked. He has lessened the holiness above, the light he could have drawn down if he had kept the Torah. "The crooked cannot be made straight," except by drawing a light from above the worlds, one that never dresses itself inside them: "higher kindness," "great kindness," which shines without end and surrounds from above. When a person draws it down by his deeds, it shines into the worlds, straightens everything crooked, fills every lack, and renews their light and goodness more than before, as a truly new light. That is why "where those who return stand, the perfectly righteous cannot stand."

From inside, return lives in the heart. Regret from the depth of the heart wakes the depth of this high light. But to bring it down into the worlds, the heart is not enough; it needs a stirring from below in the deed. That is charity without limit or measure. Here he reads the words themselves: rav chesed spells chesed to the dalet, kindness to the dal, the poor man who has nothing of his own. As a person gives to him and sets no limit on it, so the Holy One pours His light and goodness as higher kindness, without limit, into all the worlds, which are all "dalet" before Him, with nothing of their own, "all before Him counts as nothing." And by this, every flaw a person caused above by his sins is mended.

So "doing charity and justice is more chosen by G-d than an offering." An offering has a measure; charity can be scattered without limit to mend sins. And the rule "one who spends should not spend more than a fifth" is for one who has not sinned, or who already mended his sins by fasting. For one whose soul still needs healing, it is obvious: healing the soul is no less than healing the body, where no one counts money. "All that a man has he will give for his life."

This limitless kindness is called by His Name: "G-d's kindnesses." All Israel are merciful and kind, but a person's mercy has a limit. He is called Infinite, and His traits have no end. So the prophet said after the destruction and exile: "G-d's kindnesses, for we are not whole." Because we are not whole, not without sin and flaw, we must act with G-d's own kindnesses, without limit, to wake on ourselves the higher mercy that has no limit. And "Israel is redeemed only through charity": charity given even where the law does not require it.

Where the picture breaks: we speak of "holes" and "crookedness" above as if He were damaged. The text's picture is of a light a person failed to draw; the only damage is the distance.

**The movement.** From measured, correct piety, which fits a whole person, to the measureless giving of a person who knows he is not whole and is healing his soul. What carries a person across: regret from the depth of the heart, then a deed that breaks the measure, given to the one who has nothing, while seeing that before Him every world is that poor.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Before Him, all of them are poor, with nothing of their own." - Go through it: the highest worlds, the angels, this planet, you: each has nothing of its own. Then think of one thing in your life you made crooked. - Bring it to one thing: the amount you usually give, and why you stop there. - The picture breaks: the measure is holy for the whole; for the one healing, the measure itself is the wall. - Stop at the edge: the kindness that never dresses in the worlds, "great kindness," which your mind cannot hold. - Say to Him: "I am not whole. Treat me with Your kindness past measure." - One act: give once today beyond your usual measure, to someone with nothing.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: regret that turns outward into an open hand, and a lightness after the gift, not a bargain. - Fake: throwing money at guilt with no regret in the heart, or a fixed limit held up as piety when the soul still needs healing.

## Letter 11 · Made from nothing, this moment

**The state.** Bittul haratzon (the will gone quiet about this world's goods), resting on a faith lived moment by moment: that you are being made from ayin (nothing), His ungraspable wisdom, right now. From that comes simcha (joy) at every hour and hishtavut (evenness), where "yes" and "no" in worldly things weigh the same.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "To make you understand": this is not the way the light of G-d dwells, in a person who wants the life of the flesh, children and food. The sages said, "Set aside your will," meaning let your will be set aside in its very existence, with no will at all in this world's matters, all of which fall under children, life and food. As they said, "Against your will you live."

Hard words, and he does not leave them bare. The explanation is only true faith in "the Maker of the beginning." Creation is something from nothing, and that nothing is called "the beginning of wisdom": His wisdom, which no created being can grasp. And this creation happens at every moment. All creatures come into being from nothing at every instant, from His wisdom that gives life to all.

Now the state. If a person thinks this through in the depth of his understanding, and pictures in his mind his own coming into being from nothing, at every moment, truly, how could it enter his mind that things are bad for him? That he has troubles with children, or health, or livelihood, or any other suffering in the world? The "nothing" he is being made from, His wisdom, is the source of life, of good and of delight. It is the Eden above the World to Come. It only seems bad or painful because it cannot be grasped. In truth no bad comes down from above. All is good, only too great and too good to be grasped. The pain is real as felt; the letter's claim is about what it is made of.

This is the main faith a person was created for: to believe "there is no place empty of Him," and "in the light of the King's face is life." So "strength and joy are in His place," since He is only good, all day. So, first of all, a person should be glad and rejoice at every hour and moment, and truly live by his faith in G-d, who gives him life and does him good at every moment.

Here he is sharp, and it should be heard straight. One who is sad and complains shows that, to him, something is a little bad, a little painful, some good is missing; and that is like denying, G-d forbid. That is why the masters of the hidden wisdom pushed sadness very far away. The believer does not feel suffering in the world, and in all its matters "yes" and "no" are equal to him, in a true evenness.

One for whom they are not equal, he says, shows that he belongs to the "mixed multitude" who "work for themselves." He loves himself, wants to slip out from under G-d's hand and live the life of the nations out of self-love, and so he wants the life of the flesh, children and food, because they are good for him. "Better for him not to have been created." Read gently, this is not a curse. It is the claim that a person was put in this world for exactly this test: to know what is in his heart, whether it turns after "other gods," the body's cravings that come down from the other side, or whether he wants the true life that comes down from "the living G-d."

And let him believe that he truly does live in them. All his needs and all his affairs, down to their smallest details, truly come not from the other side, because "a man's steps are set by G-d," and there is no word on my tongue without Him. If so, all is completely good, only not grasped.

And the last line is the turn from inside out. By this faith, held truly, everything becomes good even openly. When a person believes that the bad he seems to see draws all its life from the higher good, His ungraspable wisdom, the Eden above the World to Come, then by that faith the seeming bad is truly taken up and raised into the hidden good.

Where the picture breaks: "made from nothing" sounds like a thin, fragile existence. The "nothing" is the richest place there is; it is only nothing to the grasp.

**The movement.** From a will tied to children, health and money, where each "no" is felt as bad, to a will gone quiet, joyful at every moment and even in "yes" and "no." What carries a person across: contemplation in depth, picturing yourself being made from nothing right now, and holding that faith until it turns the bad itself toward good.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "I am being made from nothing, this moment." - Go through it: this moment, and this one; the nothing I come from is His wisdom, the source of all good. - Bring it to one thing: the worry about money, health or a child that is nearest right now. What is it being made from? - The picture breaks: "nothing" here means too full to grasp, not empty. - Stop at the edge: a good too great to be grasped. Do not force it into a reason. - Say to Him: "You are making me now. It is good, even where I cannot see it." - One act: today, when you get a "no," say "this too is from His good," and go on gladly.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: a steady gladness under the day's ups and downs, and a will that does not grip. - Fake: numbness that stopped caring, or a forced smile that hides the pain instead of lifting it.

## Letter 12 · Fire and water, and wine settled on its lees

**The state.** Two kinds of inner peace. Shalom (peace): opposing forces, the flow of water and the rising of fire, made one by a greater light, felt in prayer and able to fade. And hashket (settled quiet): the calm of wine whose lees have sunk for good, which comes from charity given as avodah (service against one's nature).

**The chapter as consciousness.** "The act of charity shall be peace, and the service of charity quiet and security forever." What is the difference between an "act" and a "service," and between "peace" and "quiet and security"?

He starts above. "He makes peace in His heights": Michael is the prince of water and Gabriel the prince of fire, and they do not put each other out. Michael is kindness, called water, which runs from high to low: life flowing down to the lower worlds. Fire rises up: that is gevurah, withdrawal, the pull back upward, giving only with great narrowing. As plain traits, they are opposites. Inside, everyone knows them: the urge to give and pour out, and the urge to hold back and rise away.

How does G-d make peace between them? By a great light from the Infinite showing itself in them, a light that is not a trait at all but far above traits, even above the mind that is their source. Then the opposite traits are gathered into their root and become truly one, dissolved in His light as it shines on them. Then the fire is sweetened into the water by a middle trait that leans toward kindness: mercy, called tiferet (beauty), because it holds two colors, white and red. So the plain Name of G-d in the Torah points to tiferet, where the Infinite light shows more than in His other traits. What wakes this light, to make this peace, is a stirring below: the act of charity, giving life, grace and mercy to one who has nothing of his own.

He adds: one who learns Torah for its own sake makes peace in the upper household and the lower. The lower household is this world, mixed of good and bad since the first sin, where bad rules the good. You can see it in the "small world," a person: sometimes the good wins, sometimes the reverse. There will be no peace in the world until the end, when the good is sifted from the bad and cleaves to its source, and the spirit of impurity passes from the earth once the good that feeds it is taken out. That will happen by G-d showing Himself below: "the earth shall be filled with knowledge of G-d."

That is for the whole world, later. But in the small world, a person, in every "time of finding," which is prayer, or other times set aside to be alone with his Maker, each by his deeds, he gets a taste of that sifting now, through Torah for its own sake and through charity. Rabbi Elazar gave a coin to a poor man and then prayed: "In charity I will see Your face." Charity opens a light of knowledge and understanding to think about His greatness and give birth to love and awe. By that, the good is sifted out for G-d and the bad falls away, "a crucible for silver, a furnace for gold, and a man by his praise": by how deeply he praises G-d, the dross parts from him.

Now "act" and "service." Israel are merciful by nature, because their souls come from His traits, where kindness wins over judgment. Charity that comes from this nature is called an "act," since "act" is said of something already done, or done all the time by itself: a trait stamped in the soul from its making. "Service" is said only of something a person does with great toil against his nature, setting aside his nature and his will before G-d's will, like wearing himself out in Torah and prayer "until the soul is wrung out." In charity, that means giving much more than his natural mercy would give: "Give, you shall give," even a hundred times.

So "the act of charity shall be peace": even charity that is only an "act," natural, still wakes the great light and makes peace above and below. But in this lowly world, the peace and the sifting will not be revealed until the end. In the small world, the person, it shows in prayer. And after prayer, the bad can easily wake again and mix with the good, as he walks in the dark of this world. Anyone who has left a clear prayer and lost it by noon knows this state.

But charity as "service," giving against your nature, is precious beyond words. Because he set aside his nature and his bodily will, the other side is bent, and G-d's glory rises, "as light is better than darkness." Then the bad cannot wake again so easily by itself, only if the person himself wakes it and draws it onto himself. That is "quiet and security forever." "Quiet" as in "Moab has been quiet, settled on its lees": the lees separate completely from the wine and sink completely to the bottom, and the wine above is pure and clear. So in the service of charity, the lees, the mixture of bad in the soul, are sifted and separated little by little until they fall down to their source: "You will cast all their sins into the depths of the sea."

Where the picture breaks: wine settles by waiting; this settling comes from a deed that goes against the grain.

**The movement.** From a natural goodness that gives peace in prayer and loses it after, to a settled clarity where the bad has sunk and stays down. What carries a person across: charity beyond one's nature, given again and again, together with prayer and Torah for its own sake.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "The lees fall away, and the wine is clear." - Go through it: the urge to pour out and the urge to pull back, both in you; a light above both that makes them one. Then your clear prayer, and how it muddied by noon. - Bring it to one thing: the place where your giving stops by nature. - The picture breaks: this wine clears by an act against the grain, not by sitting still. - Stop at the edge: the light above all traits, which makes fire and water one. - Say to Him: "Let what is mixed in me sink, and let me stay clear." - One act: give once today more than you naturally would, where it costs.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: the calm of prayer outlasts the prayer, and old pulls do not wake by themselves. - Fake: a quiet that is only tiredness or avoidance, calm because nothing is being asked of you.

## Letter 13 · The good that hides, and the good that spreads

**The state.** Two roots of the one who serves G-d: yamin (the right), kindness that spreads out wide and gives past duty, and smol (the left), holding back and hiding, doing the service quietly and by measure. Every person holds both. And the good hidden in the left is tov hagenuz (the stored good): hidden, and still as great and as limitless as the open good of the right.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "How great is Your good that You have hidden away for those who fear You." Among those who serve G-d there are two kinds, by the root of their souls above: right and left.

The left is the trait of narrowing and hiding in the service of G-d. "Walk humbly." "My soul will weep in secret." "One who learns Torah in secret." From inside, this is the person whose goodness goes underground. He does not want to be seen. From the same trait comes a service of limits: in charity, giving according to what you have, not more than a fifth; in Torah and the other commandments, being satisfied with doing what the Torah plainly requires, setting the fixed times. And from it comes the sages' advice to a teacher to "cast awe on the students."

The right is the trait of kindness and spreading out in the service of G-d, wide open, with no narrowing or hiding at all. "I will walk in a wide place." And also with no limit at all: nothing holds back his generous spirit, whether in charity or in Torah or in the other commandments. It is not enough for him to fulfill his duty. He goes on without end.

Every person needs to be made of both, since "there is nothing that has no place." That is why we find some leniencies of the House of Shammai and some strictness of the House of Hillel. Shammai's souls were rooted in the left above, so they always ruled strictly; Hillel's were rooted in the right, so they found merit and permitted things, so they could rise. Yet sometimes Shammai was lenient, because his root also held the right, and Hillel's root also held the left. Above there is no cutting and no separation; every trait holds the others, and so they are one. Abraham was love and kindness, and yet "Now I know that you fear G-d," when he put on the trait of gevurah and bound Isaac and took the knife.

When the verse says "Abraham My lover" and "the Fear of Isaac," the difference is what is open and what is hidden. In Isaac the fear is open and the love is hidden. In Abraham it is the other way.

So David said, "How great is Your good that You have hidden for those who fear You." The good and kindness that are hidden in anyone whose soul is rooted in the left, called "those who fear You," like the House of Shammai, are stored away and hidden, and still they are very great, as great as the open kindness of the right. Both, in truth, are without limit or measure. "How great is Your good": without limit, both the good "hidden for those who fear You," and the good "You have done for those who trust in You," the people of trust whose root is the right, whose goodness shows openly, "before all people."

A small point of language opens the inner picture. The verse says "for (li) those who fear You," not "in (bi) them." Whatever is hidden in a soul is not dressed inside the body, in its mind and heart. It surrounds from above, as a makif (a surrounding light). From there it shines into mind and heart at the times that need it, so that it wakes and leads to real action. From inside, this is the quiet person who seems to have little warmth, until the moment comes, and a kindness rises in him from somewhere he never keeps it.

Then he turns it into a prayer. Since the good of the house of Israel, hidden and open, is without limit by the measure of souls dressed in bodies, so You, G-d, treat them with Your great kindness without limit, "great kindness." There is "world kindness," which has judgment standing against it to lessen and narrow it. And there is the higher kindness, "great kindness," which nothing stands against, because it comes from the light that surrounds all worlds, the most hidden of all hidden, called the supreme crown. "You hide them in the shelter of Your face."

Where the picture breaks: "hidden" sounds like smaller or colder. In this letter hidden means stored: kept, not less.

**The movement.** From judging yourself or others by how open the goodness looks, to seeing the hidden good as just as great, and from living only one root to holding both. What carries a person across: knowing your root, and at the needed moment letting the hidden side come into the deed.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Stored and hidden, and still very great." - Go through it: am I more right or more left? Where do I spread, where do I hold back? Then think of someone quiet whose goodness you have missed. - Bring it to one thing: one act you would usually do in the other root's way. - The picture breaks: hidden here means kept, not less. - Stop at the edge: the kindness that nothing stands against, from the most hidden of all. - Say to Him: "Bring out the good You have stored in me when it is needed." - One act: if you hold back, give openly once today; if you spread out, do one good thing in secret.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: you stop measuring goodness by how warm it looks, and your own hidden side comes out when it is needed. - Fake: stinginess called "restraint," or showy giving called "kindness."

## Letter 14 · A light that has never shone before

**The state.** Hitchadshut (renewal): each year, and in detail each morning, a new and higher light comes down that has never shone before. Last year's light withdraws, there is a covered moment, and then a higher one comes. The state asked for is a love woken "as if today," which gives more each year.

**The chapter as consciousness.** This letter asks for help for the Holy Land. He wants to wake the old love for it, so it burns like sparks from deep inside a person, "as if G-d had put His spirit on us today," a generous spirit, to give with a full, open hand, more and more each year, climbing higher. Like the holiness that shines on that land, which is always renewed and growing: "The eyes of G-d your G-d are always on it, from the beginning of the year to the end of the year."

Then a question. "To the end of the year" is strange, because at the end of one year the next begins. It should have said "forever."

The answer comes from "G-d founded the earth with wisdom." The foundation of the "higher land," which is His light filling all worlds, and of the lower one set right opposite it, the "land of desire," called "the land of life," is drawn from chochmah ila'ah (the highest wisdom), the source of life above. And this drawing is renewed as a truly new light each year. Because He and His wisdom are perfectly one, and it is called the Infinite light: there is no end to how high and great the light and life that come from Him and His wisdom can rise, level over level, without end.

So every year a new, renewed light comes down from the highest wisdom, a light that has never yet shone on the higher land. The light of each year goes back up to its root every eve of Rosh Hashanah, "when the moon is covered." Then, through the blowing of the shofar and the prayers, a new higher light is drawn from a higher place in wisdom, to shine on the higher land and all who live on it, all the worlds that take their life from it. "With You is the source of life; in Your light we see light." (The Kabbalistic mechanics of what renews each Rosh Hashanah are compressed here into a sentence.)

In finer detail, the same happens every day. In each morning prayer, higher mind-states (mochin) come down, not the ones that left after yesterday's prayer, but higher ones. And across the whole world's six thousand years, it happens each Rosh Hashanah.

From inside, this changes how a person meets his own dry spells. There is a time, every year and in a small way every night, when the old light has gone up and the new one has not yet come. It feels like loss. The letter reads it as the covered moon: the old light returning to its root so that a higher one can come, a light that has never been in the world before. Last year's inspiration is not supposed to be recovered. Something higher is supposed to come.

"The eyes of G-d are always on it": "eyes" means the drawing of wisdom's light, which is why the wise are called "the eyes of the community," and "the air of the Land of Israel makes one wise." The drawing is constant, but it is never the same level. Each year is a new, higher light, and the light that shone this Rosh Hashanah goes up on the eve of the next. So "from the beginning of the year to the end of the year," only that far. And the word for "beginning" is spelled missing a letter, hinting at the light withdrawn on the night of Rosh Hashanah until after the shofar, when a higher light comes down that has never shone in all the days of the world, and hides itself in the land of life above and below, to give life to all the worlds for the whole year.

But its coming out of hiding into the open depends on what people below do: their merits and their return in the Ten Days of Return. "Enough for one who understands."

Where the picture breaks: the moon comes back the same moon; this light comes back higher.

**The movement.** From a service that repeats last year and mourns its fading, to a service that expects a light never seen, and gives more each year to match it. What carries a person across: the shofar and the prayers, return in the Ten Days, each morning's prayer as new, and a love for the Holy Land woken as if today.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "A new light, which has never shone before." - Go through it: last year's light has gone back to its root; a higher one comes. The same, smaller, every morning. - Bring it to one thing: something good you are trying to feel again the way you felt it once. Let it go up. - The picture breaks: the moon returns the same; this light returns higher. - Stop at the edge: a light that has never been in the world. You cannot picture it ahead of time. - Say to Him: "Give me today's light, not yesterday's." - One act: give a little more this year than last, to the Holy Land or the poor.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: dry times feel like the covered moon, a waiting, and your giving grows year by year. - Fake: chasing an old high, or calling "new" whatever is only exciting.

## Letter 15 · From my own flesh, and dust and ashes

**The state.** Knowing something of G-d "from my own flesh," from the traits of your own soul, while knowing that your love next to the love above is like ash next to a living tree. The state it leads to is each trait of the heart given to Him alone, including hodaah (acknowledging): bowing to what you do not grasp. Holding it together is da'at (knowing as binding, the mind staying fastened to what it saw).

**The chapter as consciousness.** He opens with a warning: the sayings of the sages about the sefirot (G-d's traits) are parables and riddles. To bring it a little closer to the mind, he takes "From my flesh I will see G-d": you can understand a little of G-d from the soul dressed in your own flesh, because "He who blew, blew from within Himself." Even the lowest soul comes, through many steps, from what is G-dly itself, and the Infinite light shines in the soul, hidden in its wisdom, to give the person life. So from it a person can understand a little of the traits above, which all shine in his soul. (The chain of Kabbalistic steps is compressed here.)

But first a correction he heard from his teacher, on Abraham's words "I am dust and ashes." Abraham said it about the light of his soul, which shone in his body from the love above; he loved G-d so greatly that he became a "chariot" for Him. You might think the love above is the same kind of thing as Abraham's great love, only infinitely bigger. So Abraham said: I am ash. When wood burns, fire, water and air leave in smoke; the earth in the wood stays, and that is the ash. All the wood's length, width and thickness were really from its earth. And yet the ash has no likeness to the tree, not in size and not in kind, though it is its very essence. So Abraham's love, though it truly was the love above shining in him, came down through so many narrowings into a body that it was to the love above as ash to "a tree lovely to see and good to eat." And far less than that; the Torah speaks in human language.

From inside, this is a double knowing. Your best love is truly from Him. And it is ash next to Him. Both at once, and neither cancels the other.

Then he lays out the traits of the soul, which I compress. In general there are seven. Chesed: to give without limit. Gevurah: to hold back from giving so much, or at all. Tiferet (mercy, beauty): the middle trait, mercy on one who needs it, like a garment woven of many colors, which is beautiful. Then, when giving comes to action, a person must take counsel how to give so the receiver can receive. He gives the picture of a father teaching his son. If the father says it as it is in his own mind, the son cannot take it in. He must put it in another order, bit by bit. That counsel is netzach and hod: the "advising kidneys," the "millstones" that grind wheat into fine flour, the father making the idea smaller and breaking it into many pieces. Netzach is also standing firm against whatever blocks the teaching, from outside, and from inside: the father's own holding back, which whispers that the son is not ready yet. And yesod is the bond: the father binding his mind to his son's with love and desire, face to face. Without it, the son hearing the same words would not understand as much. The greater the desire and delight, the more the father gives and the son receives. (The sages compared this to the union of husband and wife, where the desire draws much from the mind; the text says it plainly and so do I.)

Inside these outer traits are inner ones, love and awe: the father gives from love and holds back from fear that the son will stumble. And all of them come from the mind: a child's traits are about small things because his mind is small; "a man is praised by his mind." Above all it is da'at, the knowing that binds.

Now the turn. All of this is only a parable, because it is the lower thinking soul, from kelipat nogah (the neutral shell of ordinary life). In truth, in the higher G-dly soul, "a part of G-d above," all the traits, inner and outer, are for G-d alone. Kindness, from love and the wish to cleave to Him by walking in His ways. Gevurah: to hold himself back even in what is allowed, and build fences, from awe of G-d. Tiferet: to glorify G-d and His Torah, in thought and in speech. Netzach: to stand victorious against everything that blocks him from serving G-d. Hod: to bow and to thank G-d, who gives life to all, before whom all is truly nothing. "And though we do not grasp how everything is truly nothing before Him, still we acknowledge, with a true acknowledging, that it is so." Also to thank Him for every good and not be ungrateful. Yesod: his soul bound to G-d, the Life of all life, with wonderful longing and delight. Malchut: taking on the yoke of His kingship as a servant before his master, with awe.

The source of them all is the mind that grasps His greatness, understands it wide and deep, and gives birth to love and awe: lower awe, and higher awe that is shame; great love and small love. And in each of them da'at must be dressed: the soul tied and fastened to the thing it grasped. The moment the mind takes itself off that thing, the trait born from it goes back into hiding, there in potential but not in fact. Above that is a higher da'at, where the depth of an idea flashes on the mind like a point, like lightning, and then spreads out into understanding.

Where the picture breaks: the father and son teach us about His giving, but He does not hold back from doubt, and He needs no counsel. And at hod the picture stops entirely: we bow to what we cannot grasp.

**The movement.** From traits that are natural and serve the self, to the same traits turned to G-d one by one, held in place by a mind that stays fastened to what it saw. What carries a person across: contemplation that gives birth to the trait, da'at that keeps the mind on it, and a true acknowledging where understanding ends.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "I do not grasp it, and I acknowledge that it is so." - Go through it: my love, my holding back, my pride, my stubbornness, my bond, my service. One by one: whom do they serve today? - Bring it to one thing: the trait strongest in you. Turn it toward Him in one real act. - The picture breaks: my love is ash to His; and still it is from Him. - Stop at the edge: "everything is truly nothing before Him." Bow there instead of explaining. - Say to Him: "I thank You, and I acknowledge what I cannot grasp." - One act: teach one thing to someone, broken small, with love; or thank G-d aloud for one good He did you.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: feelings stay alive as long as the mind stays on the thing that bore them, and your strongest trait starts working for Him. - Fake: taking your own soul as a literal picture of G-d, or "traits for G-d" that are temper and pride in holy clothes.

## Letter 16 · If we do not have mercy, who will?

**The state.** Kvishat rachamim (holding back one's mercy) in hard times, when the hand that was always open closes; and the opposite state, the open hand of a person who knows he himself needs mercy every moment, and so goes lifnim mishurat hadin (beyond the line of the law).

**The chapter as consciousness.** This letter goes to a community in hard times. "My beloved ones, my brothers and friends, dear as my own soul." He knows how tight things are: livelihoods have shrunk, and some he knows among them have nothing to lean on and are literally borrowing to eat. "May G-d have mercy on them and widen their straits soon."

And yet, he says, from what he hears, they are not doing well by their own souls. They have closed a hand that was open all their lives until now, a hand that gave fully and with a generous eye for the real needs of the poor, "whose eyes are lifted to us. If we do not have mercy on them, who will?" "Your brother shall live with you."

From inside, this is a very common state. When money is tight, the heart tightens with it, and it feels like prudence. The letter does not scold the fear. It takes apart the reasoning.

The sages' rule "your life comes first" is said only where two people are in the desert with one jug of water that both need equally to stay alive. But if the poor man needs bread for his little children, or wood and clothing in the cold, then all of that comes before your own fine clothes, your family feast, meat and fish and every delicacy for you and your household. "Your life comes first" does not apply, because those things are not life the way the poor man's needs are life. That is the plain law.

But truly, he says, even where that reason does not quite fit, every person should not stand on the law. He should squeeze his own life and go in, beyond the line of the law. And he should worry for himself, because the sages said: one who is exacting about this ends up coming to it himself.

Then the inner reason, which is the heart of the letter. All of us need mercy from heaven at every moment. It is woken from below, at every time and every hour, by waking our own mercy on those who need mercy. And whoever hardens his heart and holds back his mercy, for whatever reason, causes the same above: a holding back, G-d forbid. The closed hand here and the closed hand above are one motion.

More reasons, stacked plainly as the letter stacks them. There is no righteous person on earth who only does good and never sins, and charity atones and shields from harm; so it is healing of body and soul, and "all a man has he will give for his life." Charity is a loan to G-d, "and He will repay him," double, in this world: the reward for other commandments does not come in this world, but charity does, because it is good for people. And there is reason to fear when one's friends are counted for a good deed and he is not counted with them.

He ends in blessing: to those who listen, sweetness, and good blessing in everything good.

Where the picture breaks: "who will have mercy on them" sounds as if He depends on us. The letter's point is that He chose to send His mercy through our hands, and to open it above as we open it below.

**The movement.** From a heart tightened by hard times, reasoning from the law, to a hand that opens past the law because it knows it lives on mercy too. What carries a person across: seeing what is life and what is comfort, seeing your own need for mercy, and then the act of giving now, not when things get better.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "If we do not have mercy on them, who will?" - Go through it: the poor man's bread, wood, a coat in the cold; then my own table, my good clothes. Which is life? - Bring it to one thing: one expense this week that is comfort, not life, and one person whose need is life. - The picture breaks: He does not need our mercy; He chose to send His through us. - Stop at the edge: my own need for mercy at every moment, which I cannot measure. - Say to Him: "Have mercy on me, as I open my hand." - One act: give today, from this tight month, before the comfort.

**Signs and the fake.** - Sign: in a hard time your hand stays open, and the fear loosens as you give. - Fake: "I can't afford it" while the feast and the fine clothes stay, or a token gift that buys a good conscience.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 16 (line 1232): ואם אנו לא נרחם עליהם חס ושלום, מי ירחם עליהם*
