Gate 8, "Essence and Conduct" (Mahut veHanhagah), is the longest gate before the lexicon, and for the guide it may be the most useful. It asks what the sefirot are like as we meet them: which is mercy, which judgment, where judgment comes from, and how the upper world is moved. Its answers are the working theology of the whole Safed school, and most of them passed into Chassidus: judgment is not God's anger but His face hidden; near the Source there is no judgment at all; the good in the world grows while the harsh wears itself out; above cannot unite until below wakes first; a blessing needs a vessel to rest in; the living, not the dead, are the ones who move the worlds; and even in exile, one who unites the Name holds up the fallen Shechinah. It also holds some of the Zohar's most tender images, read closely: the Mother whose milk washes the red eyes of judgment, the nose that draws the smoke back to its source until anger becomes joy, the kiss that is "spirit cleaving to spirit".

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**Judgment is a hidden face.** Keter, closest to the Source, is mercy with no judgment in it. So where does judgment come from? "Judgment is done by the hiding of His face" (אמנם הדין נפעל בהסתר פניו ח"ו, line 774, Pardes 8:3:2). If His light spread through all the sefirot "there would be no judgment at all" (line 774). This is how he reads "Why, God, do You stand far off?" and "from afar God appeared to me": the light is not revealed as it should be, "and this is the cause of judgment and its being, in truth" (וזהו סבת הדין והווייתו לפי האמת, line 775). The repair is teshuvah, which uncovers His providence again.

**Near the Source, judgment cannot live.** Judgment in Binah is "like a candle at noon" (שרגא בטיהרא לא מהני, line 822): it is there, but the sun makes it useless. Only when sin shuts the other springs does the Mother open only her spring of judgment.

**The good grows, the harsh shrinks.** The Sages said "the measure of good is greater than the measure of punishment". The Ramak explains why: "the measure of good grows as it goes, and the measure of punishment grows less. And this is from the greatness of our Maker and His desire for mercy and for the world to stand" (כי מדה טובה מתרבה והולכת ומדת פורעניות מתמעטת. וזהו מגדולתו של יוצרינו וחפצו ברחמים וקיום העולם, line 847). Judgment below can only be as much as is given from above; mercy multiplies on its way down. "What looked like our enemy has become our friend" (line 853).

**When the reasons run out.** How can limit come from the limitless, judgment from pure mercy, something from nothing? "It is near to say that there is no answer but that there is no reason in will; so it rose in His will" (שאין טעם ברצון וכך עלה ברצונו להמשיך הדברים בענין הנהגת העולם, line 829). He then gives an answer anyway, but the sentence stands: three centuries before Samach Vav's "will that has no reason", the Ramak had already reached it.

**All things in Him.** God does not know the world by looking at it from outside. "Things that exist are found and united with Him in His essence, for He is the mold of all that exists, and they are in Him in the finest and choicest existence" (כי הדברים שבמציאות הם נמצאים ומיוחדים אליו בעצמותו. כי הוא דפוס כל הנמצאים כלם, line 855). "As they go down from before His face, so they go down from their wholeness."

**The whole emanation is His Name.** We have no way to speak of the Infinite "except from the side of the emanation and its revealing, which is a handle to hold Him by" (line 776). The sefirot are His Name the way a name is the handle by which we speak of a person.

**The Shema is an act of recognition, not of adding.** There are three unions. The first is the constant one, by which the sefirot are held together; "if one could think of that flow stopping, the world would be destroyed and lost" (line 860). The Shema's intention is not to add to that flow but "to crown Him over all that exists, great and small, to show that He is the cause of their standing, and that this is our complete faith" (ונמליכהו בכל הנמצאים גדולים וקטנים להורות על שהוא סבת קיומם ושזהו אמונתנו השלימה, line 860). The second union draws extra blessing for a need: forgiveness, wisdom, livelihood, strength against an enemy. The third is the "marriage" of the sefirot.

**Mitzvot are the shape of the sefirot.** "The mitzvot did not force the sefirot; the sefirot forced the mitzvot" (לא המצות הכריחו הספירות כי הספירות הכריחו המצות, line 760). Doing a mitzvah joins its branch to its root, "and this is our Maker's will, that we unite His sefirot... so that His Shechinah be among us" (line 760). One who kept not a single mitzvah "has no share in His measures, since he repaired and united nothing there to cleave to" (line 760).

## 3. States of consciousness

**Eyes washed in milk (RK-S06, proposed).** The Zohar: when judgment spreads, "the eyes burn red"; then the Ancient One shines His white, the Mother fills with milk, "and all the eyes are washed in that milk of the Mother, which flows out always" (ואסתחון כולהו עיינין בההוא חלבא דאימא דאתנגיד ונפק תדירא, line 803). The Ramak explains that when mercy shines fully "even the left becomes mercy" (והשמאל נעשה ג"כ רחמים, line 806). Inside: my eyes were hot, seeing only fault and threat; something like a mother's milk washed them, and now I see the same things without burning. How to enter: in the Zohar's own words, "that your eyes be washed in milk and grow quiet and rest" (line 714). The guide's way: stay with the thought "the flow from Him never stops" (line 803) until the harshness softens. This is between S15 (Angry at someone) and S54 (Compassion for the soul); the map has no state for the softening of the eyes themselves.

**Anger drawn back to its root becomes joy (RK-S07, proposed).** The Zohar on "God smelled the pleasing scent": smoke comes from fire bound to something coarse; when the nose draws the scent inward, "further and further in", back to its place and near to Thought, anger rests, "rest of all, rest of spirit, joy of all together, light of the lamps, light of the faces" (נייחא דכלא נייחא דרוחא, חדוותא דכלא כחדא, line 844). The Ramak: "when judgment returns to its source and is sweetened, that very judgment becomes joy" (ועתה בחזרת הדין אל מקורו ומתבסם, אז הדין ההוא נעשה שמחה, line 845). There are two kinds of "wine": one that makes drunk, and "the wine of Binah that gladdens". Inside: the heat that was anger is still there, but it has been drawn inward and has become warmth. Ties to E3 (Raising the fallen love or fear to its root) and C7 (darkness to light).

**Friends and lovers (S13, S42-S44).** The Zohar distinguishes two kinds of closeness. "Those who long for each other and are not always found together are called lovers (dodim); those who are always found together, not hidden and not parted, are called friends (re'im)" (line 867). Friends are "in will, in oneness, in joy, always"; lovers are "in longing, at times". A person knows both with God: the steady companionship and the returning longing. The guide can name them apart.

**Spirit cleaving to spirit (S60 The essential bond).** The kiss is "the cleaving of spirit to spirit" (אדבקותא דרוחא ברוחא, line 930), because the mouth is where breath comes from; "one whose soul goes out in a kiss cleaves to another spirit, a spirit that does not part from him" (line 930). The Song of Songs as a whole is "body cleaving to body"; "let him kiss me" is spirit to spirit. The Ramak adds: the embrace is love; the kiss is higher, the inner joining.

**Poor in deeds (RK-S08, proposed).** The Zohar says that when the Shechinah wakes first, the birth is "male", mercy; when He wakes first, judgment comes with it. The Ramak lays out three conditions: many righteous (she wakes first); mixed (she cannot, and He wakes first out of compassion); mostly wicked (bitterness). For the middle case he gives the prayer: "our deeds are poor, and in Your shame; bring Righteousness near from Yourself" (דלות מעשינו בשורך קרב צדק מאליך, line 921), and explains: "draw her near Yourself, by waking first" (line 921). Inside: I have not earned it and I know it; I ask Him to come first anyway. This is the honest state of most people who pray. Ties to S11 and S84; it turns the verdict (S11) into a request.

**Not in sadness.** "The Shechinah does not rest amid sadness" (לית שכינתא שריא מגו עצבות, line 967), which is why Elisha called for a musician before prophecy (S53 Glad).

**God weeps (S14 Loss and grief).** "The Holy One has a place where He weeps, and its name is Hidden Places" ( line 841). The Ramak places the weeping in Binah and ties it to exile: the Shechinah must feed her children through foreign princes, and "because of the wickedness of the children, the Mother's food is taken away" (line 850). For a person in grief, this is not a small thing: the tradition says that in the innermost place, God weeps too.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**The awakening from below (RK-T06, proposed: "Wake first").** This is the center of the gate. "Were it not that the Assembly of Israel begins with an awakening first, the One above would not wake to meet her; by the longing below it is completed above" (line 899). "The mist rose from the earth" means "the longing of the female for the male" ( line 899). "The repair above hangs on the repair below" (כי בתיקון התחתונים תלוי תקון העליון, line 900). Why? Because the whole emanation exists for those below, so "blessing does not rest above unless it rests below" (כי אין ברכה שורה בעליונים אלא א"כ שורה בתחתונים, line 914). His image is a nursing mother, whose milk comes in while the child nurses and dries up when he stops (lines 807, 914). And he insists that it is the living who do this, not the souls in Eden, "for this was given only to those below, who have choice" (כי לא ניתן ענין זה אלא לתחתונים שהם בעלי הבחירה, line 903). What it is for: to know that one's small act of turning toward Him is the first move of a meeting, not a petition to a closed door. How: before asking for anything, make one real move of the heart toward Him. Ties to the Tanya's own "by an awakening below, an awakening above" (באתערותא דלתתא אתערותא דלעילא, Tanya line 239).

**Prepare a vessel (RK-T07, proposed).** The Zohar on Elisha and the widow: he could not help her until she had something, even a little oil. "Blessings from above do not rest on an empty table" (דהא ברכאן דלעילא לא שריא על פתורא ריקנייא, line 914). "I am my beloved's: first to prepare a place for him, and then his desire is for me" (אני לדודי לאתקנא ליה דוכתא בקדמיתא, ולבתר ועלי תשוקתו, line 914). The Ramak: "the light is not revealed without a sheath to be hidden in" (line 915). How: before asking for help, bring the small thing you have, a real effort, one deed, one honest sentence. The blessing comes to rest on it. Ties to `act` and A5.

**Falling on the face (B3).** The Zohar on nefilat apayim, after the Amidah: at that hour a person should "set his heart and will on this, and give his soul in cleaving to that will" (ישוי לביה ורעותיה לדא ויהיב נפשיה בדבקותא בההוא רעותא, line 909); if he is accepted, he is "bound in the bond of life". And: "every person must be ashamed before his Master and cover his face in great shame" (line 910). Handing over the soul at the end of prayer, face covered. This is the Kabbalist root of E1 and B3; the guide's version keeps it to its heart: after speaking, give yourself over, and be quiet.

**Midnight study (B4).** One who rises at midnight to learn is "as if that river were poured on his head" (כאלו ההוא נהרא אתרק על רישיה, line 912), and "a thread of kindness" is drawn over him by day.

**Melody before the spirit (B1, `niggun`).** The Levites' song "sweetened the judgment and opened those channels" ( line 808), so that "the eyes were washed in milk". Elisha called for a musician three times so that "a whole spirit" would wake (line 967). For the Ramak melody is not decoration; it opens the channels.

**Letter combination (L1, `letters`).** Lines 785-786 describe the craft of combining the letters of a Name: follow the combinations out and back until they return to the root. And the guard: "whatever comes into the student's hand by combining, let him not think he invented what was not in the world" (line 785); the letters are forces already there, "and from this a person can recognize his Maker's glory and greatness". What the guide keeps is the guard and the aim: the combinations humble; they are not a technique for power.

**What not to ask (`edge`).** On "send away the mother, and take the children": the Zohar says the upper world is not to be asked about. The Ramak refines it: the first three sefirot are His very mind; to try to grasp their essence "is to touch the essence of the Emanator Himself, and to break the fence" (line 877). The seven lower ones may be studied, "for from His kindness in the world we know the way He leads by kindness" (כי מתוך חסידותו בעולם נדע דרך הנהגת חסדו בעולם, line 877), and "the more one busies himself with them, the more praiseworthy, provided he guards against error" (line 878). Even the prophets grasped Wisdom only as "its flow and light, by running and returning" (line 878). The rule for the guide: learn God from His ways in the world; stop at His mind.

**Holding up the fallen (RK-T08, proposed).** What use is prayer in exile, when the channels are closed? The Zohar: "one who knows how to unite the holy Name, even though blessings are not found in the world, he supports and holds up the Assembly of Israel in exile" (איהו סמיך וסעיד לכנסת ישראל בגלותא, line 979). The Ramak: the "fallen booth of David" is always falling, and "needs those who hold it up so it will not fall entirely" (line 980); "there is no day whose curse is not greater than the day before" (line 980). Those who hold it up have double reward, "rejoice with her, all who mourn for her" (line 981). What it is for: to pray when nothing seems to change, knowing that holding up is itself the work. This answers a question the guide meets often: "why pray if nothing happens?"

## 5. The big human questions

**Suffering.** Three answers in this gate, and they fit together. Judgment is His hidden face, not His rage (lines 774-775). The good grows and the harsh shrinks (line 847). And Gevurah itself is "a strap to discipline people with its correction, to strengthen them and wake them to serve the Creator" (והיא רצועה לייסר האנשים במוסרה לחזקם ולעוררם בעבודת הבורא, line 887). Strength against one's own worst impulse also comes from Gevurah, "and that strength is from the side of complete mercy" (line 773).

**Being heard.** "The hearing of every cry and prayer is in this place" (ושמיעת כל דבר צעקה ותפלה הם במקום הזה, line 888), in Gevurah with Binah: the place of judgment is the place that hears.

**Love.** "First a man is roused to love his wife from the side of fire and heat, and afterward that love is planted in his heart" (תחלה יתעורר האדם לאהבת נוקבתו מצד ההתלהבות והחום ואח"כ יתקע האהבה ההיא בלבבו, line 882): love begins in heat and settles in kindness. And Chesed's own joy "is in its doing, which is the doing of kindness" (line 880).

**The soul and its other half.** Souls come out male and female together and part as they descend; "the Holy One pairs them afterward", and "happy is the one who is worthy in his deeds and walks in truth, so that soul joins soul as they were at the start" (line 937), "for everything hangs on a person's deeds" (line 937).

**Joy and sadness.** The Shechinah does not rest in sadness (line 967); joy below wakes joy above (line 802).

**Moses in every generation.** Moses' soul "spreads in every generation into 600,000" (line 949), and his Torah lights them. The Tanya (Iggeret HaKodesh 27) says the same of his light after his passing (מתפשטת הארתו בכל דרא ודרא לששים רבוא נשמות, Tanya line 1469).

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Awakening from below.** Chassidus takes this over whole: "by an awakening below, an awakening above" (Tanya line 239, and often). The Ramak's addition the guide should keep is its reason (the worlds exist for those below, so blessing rests above only when it rests below, line 914) and its limit (only the living, who choose, line 903).
- **Joy and sadness.** The Tanya's "the Shechinah rests only amid joy" (אין השכינה שורה אלא מתוך שמחה, Tanya line 448) is the Zohar line the Ramak reads at line 967.
- **Judgment as hiding.** The Ramak's "judgment is His face hidden" becomes, in the Gate of Unity, the hiding of the divine speech in things (F03). The Ramak's picture is relational (a face turned away because of sin); the Alter Rebbe's is ontological (the hiding that makes a world possible at all). The guide needs both: the first speaks to a person who feels punished, the second to a person who feels God is absent.
- **The Shema.** The Ramak's first union is an act of acknowledgment, "to crown Him over all... to show He is the cause of their standing" (line 860). This is very close to the Chassidic Shema of bitul, but the Ramak explicitly says it adds nothing to the flow; the Chassidic Shema is held to draw down the Essence. A real difference.
- **Will without reason.** Line 829 anticipates Samach Vav's central claim, and then turns away from it to give reasons. Samach Vav makes it the foundation.
