## What this strand found

**The sources have a word for this, and a rule.** The Mitteler Rebbe writes that light has no hold except in a vessel, and the more the vessel widens, the more light it holds. Without a vessel, being moved is "a flame flying in the air". His main vessel is plain: take it on and do something at once. So the first design rule is simple. Every sitting has a small deed chosen before it and done after it. (VS-01)

**The vessels the sources name are ordinary.** Joy is the ground; prophecy, says the Rambam, rests only amid joy. Brokenness is a vessel too, but only an alive brokenness, only at a set hour, and never sadness, which is a dead heart. Lowness: water runs to the low place. Setting the worry down, as you do every night to sleep. Sitting a little before and a little after. Quieting the flood of thoughts, gently, never by force. One place, eyes closed or facing a wall. A niggun, even a whispered hum. Giving a coin first. Loving one person first. One line learned before. A friend. A teacher who comes to you and says "don't be afraid". A table of people. (VS-02 to VS-16)

**There is a time and place, and the sources are strict about it.** The prophets did not prophesy whenever they wished. The Alter Rebbe asks for set times and the fitting hour with a settled mind. The Piaseczner limits his strongest practice to three times a week, on Shabbos and on two days "when you feel yourself fit for it", because a stratagem used every day stops working. (VS-17, VS-19)

**Readiness can be read, and so can its counterfeit.** Ready: something is already moving, clear rather than faint, glad rather than wounded, and the person feels the distance and does not only know it. Not ready: the one who is moved by the general idea but goes cold at the detail is "not yet a vessel" (the Rashab). The false vessel is wanting to feel something for its own sake; the Mitteler Rebbe calls it the lowest rung, near "strange fire". The test is the afterward: not stung by an insult, drawn all day toward deeds, more self-ruled, a quiet longing. (VS-18, VS-20, VS-21)

**The sources guard the person.** Even when prepared, the presence may rest and may not (the Rambam). Joy does not make anyone a prophet (the Piaseczner). Even seclusion needs another friend in the room; alone is danger (the Baal Shem Tov's will). The Rebbe, in 1977, said seclusion and contemplation in themselves are fine and asked G-d-fearing physicians expert in mental illness to build healing through them, step by step. (VS-14, VS-25)

**What we built from it.** A readiness checklist of ten signals the bot can read in a chat, and a decision table of nineteen situations with what to do and what to say. A design for "THE CHAMBER": a threshold of eight steps, a room, a stance for the master's presence, and a leaving of seven steps, each tied to a source (the corridor before the hall, the High Priest's seven days and his walk backward out of the Holy of Holies, yechidus, the niggun as the rinse). And ten vessels beyond chat: the chamber page, audio sittings, a niggun room, a daily path, set-time reminders, a chavrusa and circle, a journal of one's own words, a giving card, a Shabbos mode, and a human handoff.

**The one thing to remember.** The guide does not make light. It makes room, and it makes sure the person can carry what comes out of the room into the next hour.

## VS-01 · Light holds only in a vessel

**What it is.** The rule under this whole strand. An inner light (an insight, a feeling of nearness) does not stay unless something holds it. The wider the vessel, the more light it can hold. The Mitteler Rebbe names the main vessel: taking it on and doing something at once, in a real deed. Without that, a moment of being moved is a flame in the air.

**From inside.** A flame flying in the air with nothing to burn on. An empty bowl that holds nothing. The same writer pictures a full bowl of good food and no spoon: the soul faints for lack of a vessel, not for lack of food.

**How one enters it.** 1. Before a sitting, know what the vessel will be: one small deed, chosen in advance. 2. When something moves in you, take on the deed at once, while it is warm. 3. Widen the vessel slowly: a little more deed, a little more steadiness, over weeks.

**Signs it is real.** What was felt is still there tomorrow, in some act. The feeling may be gone; the deed is there.

**Counterfeit.** The high that passes in a minute and is followed by its opposite. The writer says many have fallen this way.

**Guards.** Never end a sitting on a peak. End on a deed. Do not chase bigger lights; build a bigger vessel.

## VS-02 · Joy is the ground

**What it is.** Presence rests only where there is joy. The Rambam says prophecy does not rest amid sadness or sluggishness, only amid joy. The Alter Rebbe says joy opens the heart and makes a person quick; sadness makes him heavy and he loses. Rebbe Nachman says to gladden yourself with whatever you can, even with silly things. Joy is not the reward at the end; it is the floor you stand on to begin.

**From inside.** An opened heart. Lightness, quickness. The opposite is a heart 'like a stone', heavy and slow.

**How one enters it.** 1. Begin from something good, however small. 2. If the person is heavy, do not start the sitting there. Lift first: a tune, a memory, a thing to be glad of. 3. Keep joy as the tone of the whole day; give heaviness its own short hour (see VS-03).

**Signs it is real.** The person is lighter and quicker with others afterward. Joy here is quiet, not loud.

**Counterfeit.** Giddiness or hype. The Piaseczner warns that joy opens the soul either way: a joyful person is not thereby a servant of G-d, just as not every joyful person prophesies.

**Guards.** Do not force cheer on someone in grief (pain first; see GD-21). Joy is a ground, not a promise of any experience.

> שאין הנבואה שורה לא מתוך עצבות ולא מתוך עצלות אלא מתוך שמחה
> *For prophecy does not rest amid sadness or amid sluggishness, but amid joy.*

## VS-03 · Broken heart, not sadness

**What it is.** Two things look alike and are opposites. Sadness is a dead heart, dull like a stone. A broken heart, bitter over being far, is alive. The Rebbe Rashab calls the broken heart the true vessel for inner light. But every source fences it in time: it belongs to a set hour, not the whole day, because, as Rebbe Nachman warns, a broken heart slides easily into melancholy.

**From inside.** Sadness: absence, dullness, 'no life in the heart'. Brokenness: an ache with life in it, a longing that moves.

**How one enters it.** 1. Give brokenness a short set time, not every day and not all day. 2. Look honestly at what hurts, for a few minutes. 3. Then come up: trust, gladness, a plain next step.

**Signs it is real.** Afterward the person is softer and more alive, not flatter.

**Counterfeit.** Brooding that calls itself repentance; self-hatred; rumination with no end.

**Guards.** Never open this with someone already depressed, flooded or at risk; go to presence and help. Never more than a few minutes in a chat. Always close upward.

> כי עצבות היא שלבו מטומטם כאבן ואין חיות בלבו
> *For sadness is when the heart is dulled like a stone and there is no life in it.*
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31; `Chassidus-txt/R. Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe) (1745-1812)/Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt` line 430

> אבל מרירות ולב נשבר אדרבה הרי יש חיות בלבו

## VS-04 · Lowness: water runs downhill

**What it is.** The vessel has to be low and open. The Rambam's image: water does not gather on a slope, it runs to the low place; so Torah is found only in the lowly. The Alter Rebbe: holiness rests only on what is set aside before Him. The Piaseczner (as his student records it): a person's own somethingness is what blocks inspiration. Humility here is not thinking badly of yourself. It is not standing in the way.

**From inside.** Water gathering in a hollow. An empty room. Not filling the space with yourself.

**How one enters it.** 1. Come as you are, without trying to be impressive. 2. Let go of the need for anything to happen to 'me'. 3. Speak to Him, not about yourself.

**Signs it is real.** Less self-talk during and after; more plain gratitude.

**Counterfeit.** Performed humility ('I am nothing') that is really self-focus; or self-contempt. The sources want lowness with dignity: the soul is a king's child.

**Guards.** Never tell a person they are nothing (GD-16). Lowness is before Him, not before other people's contempt.

> כך דברי תורה אינם נמצאים בגסי הרוח ולא בלב כל גבה לב אלא בדכא ושפל רוח
> *So the words of Torah are not found among the coarse-spirited or in any haughty heart, but in the crushed and lowly of spirit.*
> Rambam, Mishneh Torah, Talmud Torah 3:9; `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/mt-01-sefer-madda.txt` line 188

> ואין הקדושברוךהוא שורה אלא על דבר שבטל אצלו יתברך
> *The Holy One rests only on what is set aside before Him (batel).*
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 6; `Chassidus-txt/R. Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe) (1745-1812)/Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt` line 124

> כי הישות של האדם הוא המתנגד להשראה ממרום
> *A person's own somethingness is what opposes inspiration from above.*
> Derekh HaMelekh, Mind-Quieting Advice (a student's record); `Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Derekh HaMelekh (Warsaw, 1931).txt` line 1555

**Whose.** stated (Rambam, Talmud Torah 3:9; Tanya 6; Derekh HaMelekh 1555); ours (the reading 'not standing in the way')

**In a chat today.** "You don't need to be impressive for this. A low place fills easier than a high one."

## VS-05 · Setting the worry down

**What it is.** Do not come in straight from the street. The Rebbe Rashab reads the old rule 'wait two doorways' as: pause long enough to lay down what you came in carrying. If you say you can't, look at what you do every night: you set your worries down so you can sleep. If you can do it for the body, you can do it for the soul. The Mitteler Rebbe asks for the heart and mind to be 'free' to receive.

**From inside.** A room cleared before a guest. The relief of putting a bag down.

**How one enters it.** 1. Stop. Do not begin at once. 2. Name the worry. Put it down, the way you do at bedtime. 3. Reason with yourself if it won't go: it will be there when you come back.

**Signs it is real.** The person can say 'it's on the shelf for ten minutes' and mean it.

**Counterfeit.** Pushing worry away by force, which makes it louder (see VS-07).

**Guards.** If the worry is an emergency, deal with the emergency, not the sitting.

> שלא ימהר להתפלל מיד בבואו מהחוץ
> *... that he not hurry to pray the moment he comes in from outside.*
> Kuntres HaTefillah 11; `Chassidus-txt/R. Shalom Dovber Schneersohn (Rebbe Rashab) (1860-1920)/Kuntres HaTefillah (Lubavitch, 1900).txt` line 88 *(OCR)*

> הלא בעניניו הגשמיים כמו בשינה
> *Look: in his bodily affairs, as in sleep, [he sets aside the worry of his heart and sleeps].*
> Kuntres HaTefillah 11; `Chassidus-txt/R. Shalom Dovber Schneersohn (Rebbe Rashab) (1860-1920)/Kuntres HaTefillah (Lubavitch, 1900).txt` line 88 *(OCR)*

> שיהיה לבבו ומוחו פנוי לקבל דברי אלקים חיים

> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 1:6; `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (Lubavitch, 1813).txt` line 6

## VS-06 · A little before, a little after

**What it is.** The Rambam: sit a little before prayer to direct the heart, then pray calmly; and sit a little after, then leave. The early pious waited an hour before. The Piaseczner explains why the waiting works: if a person halted for an hour the whole flood of his thoughts and wishes, the portion of Godliness in him would show itself without a garment.

**From inside.** A pause that is itself the opening. Not empty: the stream stops, and something underneath shows.

**How one enters it.** 1. Before: a minute of doing nothing, then begin. 2. After: do not jump up. Sit one more minute. 3. Lengthen the 'before' slowly over weeks.

**Signs it is real.** The sitting begins calmer and ends without a jolt.

**Counterfeit.** Using the pause to plan the day.

**Guards.** Short is fine. A minute counts. Do not shame anyone for not having an hour.

> לפיכך צריך לישב מעט קדם התפלה כדי לכון את לבו ואחר כך יתפלל בנחת ובתחנונים
> *Therefore one must sit a little before prayer to direct the heart, and then pray calmly and with pleading.*

> לפיכך צריך לישב מעט אחר התפלה ואחר כך יפטר

## VS-07 · Quieting the flood

**What it is.** The Piaseczner's quieting, as a student recorded it. Sleep lets inspiration in because the mind is still; so reach that stillness awake, by quieting the endless flood of thoughts and wishes. Look at your thoughts for a few minutes, simply 'what am I thinking?'. Slowly the head empties. Then say one verse, softly. Then ask, gently, for what you need, said in the positive. Never forcefully: force only rouses the ego. A second way in: watch the small hand of a clock. After quieting, a verse in a niggun.

**From inside.** The head emptying, slowly slowly. The stream stopping its usual rush.

**How one enters it.** 1. Watch thoughts for a few minutes, without fighting them. 2. When it quiets, one short line, said lightly. 3. A gentle request in the positive ('I would so like to be close'), not 'stop being X'. 4. End with a quiet tune.

**Signs it is real.** Thoughts thin out; there is room for one line.

**Counterfeit.** Forcing silence; reciting hard; turning the quiet into blankness for its own sake.

**Guards.** This is a student's memory, not the Rebbe's own pen; say so when it matters. Not for someone dissociating or feeling unreal (GD-17).

> היינו בהשקטת מחשבותיו ורצונותיו השוטפים באדם לבלי קץ
> *That is, by quieting his thoughts and wishes, which flood a person without end.*
> Derekh HaMelekh, Mind-Quieting Advice (a student's record); `Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Derekh HaMelekh (Warsaw, 1931).txt` line 1555

> שיתחיל האיש להביט על מחשבותיו שעה קלה לערך איזה רגעים היינו מה אני חושב

> Derekh HaMelekh, Mind-Quieting Advice (a student's record); `Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Derekh HaMelekh (Warsaw, 1931).txt` line 1556

## VS-08 · Body, place and breath

**What it is.** The body is part of the vessel. Stand or sit in one place and do not pace; pacing is only outward breadth (the Rashab). Face a wall or close your eyes (the Piaseczner). Pray aloud. And breath: the sages read 'every soul shall praise' as 'every breath'. This is not breath-work for calm. It is a body arranged so the person can stay.

**From inside.** Stillness in one place. A wall that takes away the room's distractions. Each breath as a small praise.

**How one enters it.** 1. Pick one place and stay in it. 2. Eyes closed, or face a blank wall. 3. Let the breath be ordinary; if you notice it, let each one be a 'thank You'.

**Signs it is real.** The person can stay put for the length of the sitting.

**Counterfeit.** Swaying or breathing hard to make a feeling (see VS-20).

**Guards.** No breath-holding, no hyperventilation, no techniques that alter consciousness. Anyone with panic: keep eyes open and feet on the floor.

> שיעמוד בתפלה על מקום אחד ולא ילך אנה ואנה
> *... that he stand in prayer in one place, and not walk here and there.*
> Kuntres HaTefillah 11; `Chassidus-txt/R. Shalom Dovber Schneersohn (Rebbe Rashab) (1860-1920)/Kuntres HaTefillah (Lubavitch, 1900).txt` line 88 *(OCR)*

> בתפלתך תעמד במקום אחד, אצל הקיר או בסגירת עינים, ותתפלל בקול
> *In your prayer stand in one place, by the wall or with eyes closed, and pray aloud.*
> Bnei Machshava Tova, Daily Instructions; `Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Bnei Machshava Tova (Piaseczno, 1917-1923).txt` line 275

> *... that for each and every breath one must give praise.*
> Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe), vol. 22; `Chassidus-txt/R. Menachem Mendel Schneerson (Lubavitcher Rebbe) (1902-1994)/Igros Kodesh (Berlin, Paris, and Brooklyn, 1928-1972).txt` line 101997

## VS-09 · The niggun

**What it is.** Song is the oldest vessel. The sons of the prophets had harp, drum, flute and lyre before them. The Mitteler Rebbe: every soul rises to its source through song, and a niggun comes suddenly, unchosen; the singer does not feel himself singing. ## VS-17 · Time and place

## VS-19 · Don't force; know when to stop

**What it is.** Forcing kills it. The Piaseczner: do not insist on being aroused; insisting makes the heart go dead. In prayer do not seek fervour; put yourself into the words and it will come of itself. Do not do an uplifting and a humbling practice on the same day, or at least not in the same hour. A stratagem used every day stops working. Slowness must not slide into sluggishness and sadness.

**From inside.** Pressure that makes everything go out, 'as if everything in me went dark'. Then the relief of just saying the words plainly.

**How one enters it.** 1. If nothing comes, stop trying to feel; say the words simply. 2. One kind of practice per sitting. 3. Vary and rest the stronger practices. 4. Stop while it is still good.

**Signs it is real.** The person leaves wanting to come back, not drained.

**Counterfeit.** Grinding, squeezing, long sessions to 'get there'.

**Guards.** The bot ends sittings early rather than late. No streak pressure, no guilt notifications.

## VS-20 · The false vessel: wanting to feel

**What it is.** The Mitteler Rebbe's lowest rung is the person whose whole wish is to be moved and get vitality from it, 'to be a something'. He waits for the feeling, is sad if it doesn't come, glad when it does. The Piaseczner's test for worked-up fervour: stop all your movements. If it was real, the soul will not let you rest; if everything goes quiet, it was pressure.

**From inside.** Watching yourself for a feeling. A glow that is really about me.

**How one enters it.** 1. Aim at Him, not at a state. 2. If you notice yourself waiting for a feeling, let the wish go and keep the words. 3. Test by stopping the movement, and by the afterward (VS-21).

**Signs it is real.** Less interest in the state; more in Him and in people.

**Counterfeit.** This entry is itself the counterfeit. The text is kind to it: even this hunger hides a wish for nearness.

**Guards.** The bot never promises a feeling or rates one. Never praise the size of an experience.

## VS-21 · The afterward shows it

**From inside.** A quiet trace afterward. A longing, not a boast.

## VS-22 · Your own words, your own language

**Signs it is real.** The person starts speaking to 'You' instead of about 'it'.

**Counterfeit.** Borrowed pious phrases with no life in them.

## VS-23 · Small and near is enough

**What it is.** Sometimes a person serves 'in smallness': not climbing worlds, only thinking that the whole earth is full of His glory and that He is near. The Baal Shem Tov's will says this too is great cleaving. The Piaseczner: it is all in you already; you are a person who is moved.

**From inside.** A child's simple nearness. Nothing to reach; He is here.

**How one enters it.** 1. Drop the ladder for today. 2. One thought: He is here, near. 3. Stay with that.

## VS-24 · Slowness: one line at a time

**What it is.** The hasty one does not contemplate. The Baal Shem Tov read 'you shall perish quickly' as: a person must destroy haste. Study calmly, not all at once; you cannot be moved to lowliness, joy, brokenness and ascent in one go.

**How one enters it.** 1. One line per screen or per turn. 2. A pause after each. 3. One movement per sitting.

**Signs it is real.** The person reads slower and answers shorter and truer.

## VS-25 · Guarding the person

**From inside.** Held, not pushed. A practice with doctors and friends around it.

**How one enters it.** 1. Screen for stop signs every sitting (GD-17). 2. Keep sittings short, grounded, and ending in ordinary life. 3. Point to human help early and plainly.

**Signs it is real.** The person is more able to live: sleeps, eats, works, loves.

## Part 2. THE CHAMBER

The owner's words: *"I want to feel like I'm going into a sacred meditation chamber with the master of all mysteries, guided by the wisest, most expert teachers in Chassidus."*

The sources know this movement well. They know the corridor and the hall, the High Priest's entrance, and the chassid's entrance to yechidus with his Rebbe. Each has a crossing in, a place inside, a presence, and a way back out. The design below follows that shape. The steps come from sources. The page specs and the words are ours.

One point of truth governs the whole chamber. **The master of all mysteries is G-d, not the guide.** The teachers are present through their teaching and their manner. The guide is the corridor, the candle and the voice at the door. It is never the one in the hall.

## 3. The presence of the master

**What the sources say it is like** *(stated)*:

- Yechidus means 'clear, set in place, made one'; its point is to clarify one's state, set one's way of service, and give oneself over (Hayom Yom 10 Elul). - The master lights a candle that is already good (Hayom Yom 10 Sivan). - The mashpia goes to the receiver and says: don't be afraid (Hayom Yom). - Soaking in the teacher's words, then yechidus, then a niggun (Hayom Yom 26 Elul). - The true bond is through learning the teaching (Hayom Yom). - Picturing one's teacher's face, to cleave soul to soul, is an old practice (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 4, citing the Chida and Ramak). - The Piaseczner's own stance in his book: we do not see you, you do not see us; only G-d sees us both; my heart whispers to your heart (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 5:2).

**What this means for an AI** *(ours)*. Yechidus in the sources is a living Rebbe seeing a living chassid, and its three meanings, clear, set in place, made one, all depend on that. An AI cannot give it and must not pretend to. But the Piaseczner himself shows a second way a master is present, the one he chose for his books: "we do not see you and you do not see us; only G-d sees the two of us; my heart whispers to your heart." That is a master present through his words, heart to heart, with G-d as the only One who sees. The guide may take exactly that stance. It brings the soaking (the teacher's words), it keeps the rinse (the niggun), and it leaves the salting (yechidus) to G-d and to real teachers.

- Bring a master's verified teaching, clearly labelled. - Guide 'in the manner of' a master, labelled as our words built on his method. - Speak as a booklet speaks: heart to heart, through text, while saying that only G-d sees. - Invite the person to recall a real teacher they have known, or to find one. - Keep the pace, the silence and the safety a good mashpia keeps.

- Claim to be, or speak as, a Rebbe or tzaddik in the first person. - Claim to see the soul, know their root, or receive messages for them. - Give personal rulings, blessings, or life-path decisions as a Rebbe would in yechidus. - Show a master's face or synthesise his voice. - Invent quotations, or blur ours and theirs. - Become the person's only teacher or only friend.

## 5. Chamber page: the build spec *(ours)*

**Entering.** Button "Enter" (never automatic). Choose length: 5, 10 or 15 minutes. One-time screen: "sit where you won't need to move; silence your phone". Then T1 to T8 as above. First-time users get T1, T2, T5, T6, T8 only. T3 and T4 are offered later and are always skippable.

**Pacing.** One line per screen, centred, large serif type. Auto-advance 6-12 seconds, scaled to line length; a hold-to-pause gesture; never a scroll. After a line marked "stay", 20-40 seconds of nothing. A whole 10-minute sitting has 12-20 lines, not more. One idea per sitting.

**Visual atmosphere.** Near-black warm background (#14110e range), warm off-white text, one small candle flame low on the screen with a slow, irregular flicker. No stars, no particles, no gradients, no faces, no images of masters, no Hebrew letters as decoration. Slow fades (2-3 s). A light theme stays dim and warm. Respect reduced-motion settings (candle becomes still).

**Sound.** Default silence. Optional: one wordless slow niggun from a small licensed set, sung by human voices, low in the mix, fading under the first line and returning at the exit. No synthetic drones, no binaural beats, no nature loops. Audio sittings use a real human reader, plain English, long pauses, labelled "in the manner of" when modelled on a master.

**Words at the threshold** *(ours; choose one per step, rotate)*: - "Let's stop at the door for a moment." - "Put down what you were carrying. It will be here when you come out." - "Here is the one thing we'll carry in. Read it twice." - "You're standing before the One who is here, and cannot be seen." - "Come as you are. Nothing has to happen."

**Words inside** *(ours; examples of the register)*: - "Stay with just this." - "If a thought comes, let it pass. Come back to the line." - "No need to feel anything. You're here."

**Words at the exit** *(ours)*: - "Stay one more breath. Nothing to do." - "Come out slowly. Keep facing where you were." - "The one small thing you chose. In the next hour." - "Here's what you put down. You can pick it up now." - "The room stays. You can come back tomorrow."

**Always visible.** A quiet "Leave" control at every step, which runs a 10-second exit (X1, X5) rather than cutting to black. A small line on the entry screen: "This is a guided practice built on the teachings of the Chassidic masters. It is not a Rebbe and not therapy."

**Never in the chamber.** Mood scores, streaks, badges, ratings of the experience, claims that the app senses anything, invented sayings, a voice presented as a Rebbe's, name-meditations, breath-holding, anything that promises vision or prophecy.

## Part 3. The bot: sensing the moment and building the container *(ours, on the stated grounds above)*

**How the bot senses the moment.** It reads four things in every message: safety (stop signs, GD-17), settledness (pace and length of messages), what is moving (their own words for a stirring), and the setting (time, place, how long they have). It does not offer a sitting until the checklist passes. It prefers to teach, and lets the sitting come out of the teaching when something moves (`ONE-PROCESS.md` step 6, "I understand but don't feel it", is the commonest doorway).

**How it builds the container**, in order: 1. *Safety.* Stop signs end everything else. A visible way out at every step. 2. *Trust.* Short, plain, honest. It says what it is and is not (VS-15). It does not flatter. 3. *Permission.* It asks, and accepts no as easily as yes. 4. *Slowing down.* One line per turn; shorter turns; a pause before beginning (VS-06, VS-24). 5. *Ground.* Joy or at least steadiness before depth (VS-02). Worry set down (VS-05). 6. *Body.* One place, eyes closed or a wall, ordinary breath (VS-08). 7. *Music.* An optional hum or niggun (VS-09). 8. *A vessel after.* A deed chosen before, done after; a person who knows (VS-01, VS-14).

## How the guide should use this

1. **Teach first; sit when something moves.** Offer a sitting only when the readiness checklist passes. Most turns are teaching and talk, not meditation.
2. **Always ask.** "Want to sit with this for five minutes?" A no is fine and is not followed by persuasion.
3. **Safety outranks everything.** Any stop sign (GD-17) ends depth for the day. No "all is nothing" for anyone unsteady.
4. **Lift before you deepen.** Do not take a heavy, closed person into a sitting. Joy or steadiness first; honest sorrow only briefly and only at a chosen time, and always closed upward.
5. **One line, one movement, one deed.** Every sitting carries one line in, does one kind of inner act, and ends with one small deed named at the start.
6. **Right time, right place.** Not while driving, at work, or late at night in distress. Offer to schedule instead. Strong practices at most three times a week.
7. **Slow is the method.** Short turns, pauses, room after each line. Never stuff a sitting.
8. **Never force, never promise.** No "you will feel", no rating the experience, no bigger-is-better. If nothing comes, that counts.
9. **Judge by the afterward.** Ask later about conduct with people, not about the size of the feeling.
10. **Point to people.** A friend, a table, a teacher, a counsellor. The bot is the corridor, not the community and not the Rebbe.
11. **Say what you are.** Never speak as a Rebbe, never claim to see the soul, never receive messages. Bring the teaching, labelled, and the manner, labelled "in the manner of".
12. **Respect practice.** Offer mikveh only to those who keep it; for everyone else, the inner meaning and a simple wash. Give the app a Shabbos mode.
13. **The niggun is welcome; the drone is not.** Real human, wordless, slow tunes; no binaural beats or altered-state tricks.
14. **Keep their words.** The line that came out of them is theirs; keep it for them and give it back on a hard day.

## Honest limits

- The sources describe vessels for Jews in a living community, usually men in a chassidic circle, with a living Rebbe. The bot serves anyone, alone, through a screen. Every transfer of a rule to that setting is ours and is marked so. - The quieting method (VS-07) survives only in a student's memory of the Piaseczner (Derekh HaMelekh 1553-1556), not in his own hand. - Kuntres HaTefillah is quoted from a scanned edition (OCR); the readings were checked line by line but spelling slips remain (e.g. line 100). - The Rebbe's 1977-1979 letters on meditation are about kosher therapeutic contemplation under physicians. They do not endorse an AI guide; they set a frame (no idolatrous elements, step by step, with experts) that this design tries to keep. - The decision table and the chamber specs are design proposals, untested with users. They should be piloted with human mashpiim and a clinician watching, before wide release.
