## Why a meditation behind each subject

Every concept in Chassidus points to one thing, the unity of God, and that unity cannot be understood: by definition it is beyond the mind. It can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (hisbonenus). So behind each subject below is a meditation, and the meditation, not the idea, is what matters. Each one specializes the meditation of the subject's first face.

## How to use it

This is hisbonenus, the Jewish way of contemplating as Chabad does it, not mindfulness. Take one subject per sitting.

- **Hold** is one line of teaching. Say it, and fix the mind on it.
- **The move** thinks it through slowly. It opens the line detail by detail, brings it down into your own day, gives the sources' own picture and says in a few words where the picture breaks, and makes it particular: you, here, now. The pauses between its steps are time to think, not silence to watch anything.
- **Stay** binds the mind: say the line again slowly, and when the mind drifts, come back to it without arguing with the stray thought (Tanya 3 and 28).
- **From inside** describes the glimpse. It is never asked for, and if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.
- **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, then gives one mitzvah or act of Torah in your own measure, and a line to return to during the day.

## Labels

- **Tie** says whether the move rests on steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our construction (*reading*).
- **Thin** follows the entry's own marking (its 'thin?' column; 'partly' or 'split' counts as thin). A thin subject's meditation is kept modest and says why in one line.
- **Where the sources differ** keeps a disagreement the entry records, where the meditation touches it, rather than smoothing it.
- **Note** marks any other place where the meditation leans on our reading.
- **Anchors** are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly (reference, line and Hebrew), each with the entry's own gloss. *OCR* marks uncorrected machine transcription, quoted as the entry quotes it.

## S125 · Teshuvah · תשובה

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F02 The higher and the lower unity; F07 The home below **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Teshuvah is not paying off a debt but going back to where your soul was one with Him before it came down; nothing but the will ever stood in the way.

**Hold.** 'To rise higher and higher to its source and cleave to Him, as it was united with Him in utter unity' (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 8, on complete teshuvah).

**The move.** Open it. The soul is a part of Him, drawn from within Him. Nothing physical or spiritual separates before Him, since He fills everything; only a will set against His will does. So teshuvah is not fasting but return, and Iggeret HaKodesh 1 says it brings a person close to His very Essence, beyond the World to Come; the Rebbe calls it going out of your own limits. Bring it down to one thing in your life today that you know is against His will. The picture is the Alter Rebbe's, a rope, one end held above and one end below; it is only a picture, since He is not above in a place, and the distance was never one of place but of will. You, here, now: nothing stands between you and Him but the will.

**Stay.** 'To cleave to Him, as it was united with Him', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Going home rather than settling a bill; the Rebbe calls it going out of your own limits. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'You shall return to the Lord your God with all your heart.' Then give a coin to charity today, the Alter Rebbe's way in place of fasting. Line for the day: 'Return, with all the heart.'

**Where the sources differ.** Fasting or not? The mussar books and the Arizal's tikkunim: many fasts. The Alter Rebbe (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 1-3): teshuvah is not fasting; the Rambam and the Smag require only confession and a request for forgiveness; in 'our generations' redeem fasts with charity. Fasts are for pleasing Him after forgiveness, not the teshuvah itself.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 5:4 (line 910): אך באמת, אין שום דבר גשמי ורוחני חוצץ לפניו יתברך IT 5: nothing physical or spiritual separates before Him, since He fills heaven and earth ('ein od'); so the distance teshuvah crosses is not one of place. - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 8:4 (line 946): ולעלות מעלה מעלה למקורה, ולדבקה בו יתברך ביחוד נפלא, כמו שהיתה מיוחדת בו יתברך בתכלית היחוד Iggeret HaTeshuvah 8: complete teshuvah is the soul rising to its source and cleaving to Him 'as it was united with Him in utter unity' before it was breathed into the body. - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 1:4 (line 987): אבל תשובה ומעשים טובים מקרבין ישראל לאביהם שבשמים ממש, למהותו ועצמותו כביכול Iggeret HaKodesh 1: this world's hour of teshuvah is better than the World to Come, because the World to Come is a radiance while teshuvah and good deeds bring Israel close to His very Essence.

## S126 · Higher and lower teshuvah · תשובה עילאה ותשובה תתאה

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F11 Darkness turned to light; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Two letters of the one Name come back to their place: the lower one brought home from exile, and the upper one where the soul cleaves to Him as it once was. The second, the Alter Rebbe says, is for all your days, in great joy.

**Hold.** 'Return the heh: the lower heh is lower teshuvah, the upper heh is higher teshuvah' (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 4, from the Zohar).

**The move.** Open it. The lower heh is the flow of the Name that our deeds sent down into exile; it is brought back by mercy for it, and by the humbling the Tanya calls a broken heart, at its set time. The higher comes after: the soul, with its mind, feeling, thought, speech and deed, cleaving to His, as it was united with Him before. The Alter Rebbe says the second is for all your days, in great joy, and the Rashab sharpens it: the lower breaks the animal soul's selfhood, only the higher changes its nature. Bring it down to your own speech today, one of the parts that can cleave to His. The picture is the Zohar's, two letters of one Name returned to their place; it is only a picture, since the letters stand for His flow of life into the soul, not for ink. You, here, now: your speech returning to His.

**Stay.** 'Return the heh', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Coming home, not only turning back. R. Tzadok says that in teshuvah from love the distance itself turns out to have been closeness, though that is shown only later. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, in today's Amidah: 'Bring us back to You in complete teshuvah', meaning both returns, the lower and the higher. Then learn one line of Torah today, gladly, as the higher teshuvah of your speech. Line for the day: 'All my days, in joy.'

**Where the sources differ.** Who does the higher teshuvah? Tanya (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 8-10): the person, through Torah, kindness and prayer, after lower teshuvah. Karlin and R. Tzadok (per pillar 15, not re-verified): God, 'I will return to you'.

**Note.** The entry lists the face of the higher and lower unity first, but reading the two letters heh as that pair (the Shema and 'Blessed be the name') is our reading; the meditation rests on the two letters as the Zohar and the Tanya give them.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 4:2 (line 889): תשוב ה׳, ה׳ תתאה – תשובה תתאה, ה׳ עילאה – תשובה עילאה IT 4: the Zohar reads teshuvah as 'tashuv heh', return the heh: the lower heh is lower teshuvah, the upper heh is higher teshuvah. - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 8:5 (line 947): והנה, בחינת יחוד זה ותשובה זו, היא בחינת תשובה עילאה שלאחר תשובה תתאה IT 8: the union of the soul with its source is higher teshuvah, which comes after lower teshuvah. - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11:12 (line 977): ומשום שצריך להיות כל ימיו בתשובה עילאה, שהיא בשמחה רבה IT 11: 'my sin is before me always' does not mean sadness; one must be all one's days in higher teshuvah, which is in great joy.

## S127 · Sin · חטא

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F09 The unity of opposites; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** Once is the practice. If going over a wrong has become a loop that no confession settles, that loop is not this practice: say it once, at its time, and set it down.

**The glimpse.** Nothing physical or spiritual separates you from Him; only a will set against the will that gives you life does. That is all sin is, and why it matters.

**Hold.** Sins separate 'between you and your God' because 'they are against the supreme will that gives life to all' (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 5).

**The move.** Open it. Nothing separates before Him, since He fills everything; what separates is a will set against the will that gives life to all. The Tanya calls that separation from His unity, like idolatry, because it holds itself apart. The Maggid says whatever power the wrong thing had was holiness fallen, which confession lifts back. And the Alter Rebbe adds that His forgiveness is the same for once as for a thousand times, since before Him all is as nothing. Bring it down to one thing you did this week against His will, named once, in a word. The picture is the Alter Rebbe's, a rope from the soul to its source, which a grave sin cuts and a lighter one frays; it is only a picture, since the tie is of will, not of cord. You, here, now: only the will separates, and the will can turn.

**Stay.** 'Only a will against His will separates', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Sober rather than frightened. The Rebbe says that inwardly there was no blemish from the start, while the outer levels of the soul are really blemished and really atoned; both are kept. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, at the bedtime Shema tonight, one plain sentence of confession for that one thing. Then say the rest of the bedtime Shema and go to sleep. Line for the day: 'The will can turn.'

**Where the sources differ.** Is sin a real separation, or does evil have no existence? Tanya 24: every prohibition is 'separated from His unity in utter separation'. Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11: before Ein Sof a thousand sins are as one (said of forgiveness, not of the sin's reality). R. Tzadok: for Shlomo, whose teshuvah reached the Throne, evil 'has no existence at all', said of that level, not of everyone. The Rebbe: inwardly there was no blemish from the start, while the outer soul-levels are truly blemished and atoned. Reading these as the two knowledges is ours.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 5:5 (line 911): והטעם, לפי שהם נגד רצון העליון ברוךהוא המחיה את הכל IT 5: sins separate 'between you and your God' because they oppose the supreme will that gives life to all. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 24:1 (line 316): הם נפרדים מיחודו ואחדותו יתברך בתכלית הפירוד ממש, כמו הסטרא אחרא והקליפה הנקראת ״עבודה זרה״ Tanya 24: every prohibition, being against His will and wisdom, is separated from His unity in utter separation, like the kelipah called idolatry. - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11:7 (line 972): ולגבי בחינת איןסוף אין הפרש כלל בין מספר קטן לגדול, ד״כולא קמיה כלא – ממש – חשיב״ IT 11: He forgives a thousand times as once, because before Ein Sof small and great numbers are the same: 'all is as literally nothing before Him'. That the sin's separation is therefore real only from below is our inference, not the text's.

## S128 · Confession and regret · וידוי וחרטה

**Face:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F06 The soul as part of the One; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** Once is the practice. If going over a wrong has become a loop that no confession settles, that loop is not this practice: say it once, at its time, and set it down.

**The glimpse.** Confession is a mitzvah in words: the will that went out from Him is pulled up and returned, and He draws down a will higher than the one that was lost.

**Hold.** 'True teshuvah is true regret in the very essence of the will's attachment', more than abandoning the sin (the Mitteler Rebbe, Sha'arei Teshuvah).

**The move.** Open it. Regret pulls up the will that attached itself to the thing. The heart's turning takes the life out of what the wrong made, but only confession spoken with the mouth undoes its matter. And what comes back, the Tzemach Tzedek says, is not the same will but one drawn fresh from the will of all wills, higher than before, which is why sins can become merits. After contemplation, the Alter Rebbe says, the plea 'wipe away my transgressions' comes from mercy for the Godliness in your soul and above. Bring it down to the one sentence of confession you would say for one thing. The picture is the Mitteler Rebbe's, a destroying angel made by the sin, with a life and a matter of its own; it is only a picture, since its power, the Maggid says, was fallen holiness all along. You, here, tonight: the words said, the will returned.

**Stay.** 'True regret, in the very essence of the will', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Compassion more than shame: the plea comes from mercy for the Godliness in your soul and above. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, once, tonight: the one sentence of confession, and 'wipe away my transgressions'. Then give a coin to charity tomorrow, as the Alter Rebbe advises in place of fasting. Line for the day: 'Returned, higher than before.'

**Where the sources differ.** Fasting or not? The mussar books and the Arizal's tikkunim: many fasts. The Alter Rebbe (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 1-3): teshuvah is not fasting; the Rambam and the Smag require only confession and a request for forgiveness; in 'our generations' redeem fasts with charity. Fasts are for pleasing Him after forgiveness, not the teshuvah itself.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Sha'arei Teshuvah, חינוך. להבין הפרש שבין תפלה לתורה. סד"פ / קטז, ד:1 (line 650): כמו התשובה האמיתית שהיא ענין חרטה אמיתית בעצם המשכת התקשרות הרצון The Mitteler Rebbe: true teshuvah is true regret in the essence of the soul's attachment of will, more than abandoning the sin (lower teshuvah). - Sha'arei Teshuvah, שער התשובה. כתיב אם יהי' נידחך כה"פ / ו, ג:1 (line 27): ויש לזה ג"כ תיקון לאבד גם החומר דמלאך הרע והוא ע"י הוידוי דוקא באמרו בדבור בפה The Mitteler Rebbe: heart-teshuvah removes the vitality of the destructive angel made by the sin; its 'matter' is destroyed only by spoken confession. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of confession of sin 3 (line 85): וענינה הוא החזרת הרצון שנסתלק ע"י העוונות … אלא מבחי' רעוא דרעוין מקור הרצונות שממנו נמשך השראת הרצון מחדש The Tzemach Tzedek: forgiveness is the return of the will that departed through sin, drawn anew from 'the will of all wills', higher than the will that was lost; hence sins become merits.

## S129 · Self-accounting · חשבון הנפש

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; F06 The soul as part of the One **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**Before you start.** Briefly, at a set time, and never as the door for a mood that is already low (a limit of ours, from the craft of these sittings). If going over your faults has become a loop, that loop is not this practice: once, at its time, and then set it down.

**The glimpse.** The account asks one question of each thought, word and deed: was it for Him or not? That is all 'the other side' means.

**Hold.** 'Whatever is not for God, His will and His service, is what "the other side" means' (Tanya 29, on the masters of accounting).

**The move.** Open it. The masters of accounting reckon every thought, word and deed by that one line, and every holy thought made you, at that moment, a chariot for holiness. The Besht adds that what you saw wrong in someone else is a trace of it in you: repair it in yourself, and include him with you, since all are one person. And R. Nachman gives the frame: judge yourself before God who does you good at every moment. Bring it down to today's hours, from morning until now. The picture is Hayom Yom's, a merchant going over his books; it is only a picture, since this account is not of profit but of what was for Him, and the Hayom Yom line itself does not speak of the unity. You, here, tonight: which moments were for Him.

**Stay.** 'For Him, or not for Him', said again slowly over the day's hours. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Sober and even-handed, done before God who does you good at every moment. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, after two minutes with the day: the bedtime Shema, from its first line. Then close the account and sleep. Line for the day: 'For Him, or not.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie is stated in Tanya 29 (through what 'the other side' means), Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7, the Besht and R. Nachman; the Elul 'merchant's account' of Hayom Yom is a practice with no unity stated, and using it here as the picture is our reading.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 29:15 (line 395): להיות מ״מארי דחושבנא״, דהיינו, לעשות חשבון עם נפשו … אשר לא לה׳ המה ולרצונו ולעבודתו, שזהו פירוש לשון ״סטרא אחרא״ Tanya 29: be of the 'masters of accounting', reckoning every thought, word and deed; whatever was 'not for God, His will and service' is what 'sitra achra' means. - Keter Shem Tov 1:59:1 (line 97): יבין שיש בו שמץ עבירה זו ושירגיש לתקן א"ע … אחר שיכלול אותו עמו ע"י אחדות שכולם אדם א' The Besht: seeing another's sin shows a trace of it in oneself; repair oneself, and the wicked one returns too, included 'through unity, for all are one man'. - Likkutei Etzot, Hitbodedut 1 (line 326): וידין וישפט את עצמו בכל עת על כל עסקיו ומעשיו, אם כך ראוי והגון לו לעשות, ולהתנהג כך מנגד השם יתברך R. Nachman (Likkutei Etzot): in hitbodedut a person judges all his affairs 'before God who does him good at every moment'.

## S130 · Refining the traits · תיקון המדות

**Face:** F11 Darkness turned to light · **also touches:** F06 The soul as part of the One; F07 The home below; F09 The unity of opposites **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** The traits you were born with can become His traits, your mercy joined to His mercy, your nature changed and not only held back. The lower this reaches, the Rebbe says, the more the essence shows.

**Hold.** 'The whole of Chassidus is to change the nature of one's traits' (the Alter Rebbe, as the Rebbe cites him).

**The move.** Open it. The Rashab says the soul came down to refine the natural traits of its own portion, and that general contemplation leaves the main one untouched, so you must know your particular trait and give it your strength. The Maggid says a bad trait is a good one fallen from its root, and bound to God in love and awe it goes back up. The Alter Rebbe's aim is that each trait cleave to His, 'as He is merciful'. And the Rebbe adds that the lower this reaches, down to changing the animal soul's nature, the more the essence of Chassidus shows. Bring it down to the one trait that is most yours to repair, a quick temper, say, and the moment it usually shows. The picture is the Alter Rebbe's, as the Rayatz tells it, a judge and an officer at each gate of the traits; it is only a picture of method, and the method itself does not speak of the unity. You, here, now: this trait is your portion to raise.

**Stay.** 'To change the nature of my traits', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** The dignity of the struggle, and something of the sweetness of light that came out of darkness. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'I have set the Lord before me always.' Then, when that trait stirs today, say 'for You' before you act on it. Line for the day: 'My trait, back to its root.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie is stated by the Rebbe (as a branch of the one point), the Rashab (the purpose of the descent) and Iggeret HaTeshuvah 9; the techniques themselves are usually taught without the unity, so the meditation keeps to the aim, not a method.

**Where the sources differ.** Is refining the traits the point of Chassidus or a branch of it? The Alter Rebbe (as the Rebbe cites him): 'the whole of Chassidus is to change the nature of one's traits'. The Rebbe: a branch of the one point, yet where the point shows most. The Rashab: the purpose of the soul's descent, reached only by specific toil, not general contemplation.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Kuntres HaAvodah, scan p. 46 ¶4 (line 249) *(OCR)*: ובכדי להשלים הכוונה העליונה שבשביל זה ירדה נשמתו למטה, דהיינו לברר המדות טבעיים המגיעים לחלקו The Rashab: to complete the supreme intent for which the soul descended, one must refine the natural traits that belong to one's portion (OCR). - Maggid Devarav leYaakov 12:13 (line 44): וכשהוא מקשר אותם בהש"י באהבת הבורא וביראת הבורא הוא מחזיר אותם לשרשם The Maggid: a bad thought is a trait fallen from the seven days of building; bound to God in love and awe it returns to its root. - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 9:4 (line 951): המדות – במדותיו יתברך IT 9: love of God is cleaving of spirit to spirit with all parts of the soul; the traits cleave to His traits ('as He is merciful').

## S131 · Pride · גאוה

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Pride says 'I, and none besides me', the exact reverse of 'there is none besides Him'. That is why the Tanya calls it idolatry itself, not only a bad trait.

**Hold.** 'Haughtiness equals idolatry, for the root of idolatry is being regarded as a thing in itself' (Tanya 22).

**The move.** Open it. The other side 'raises itself like an eagle, saying I, and none besides me', the exact reverse of 'there is none besides Him'. So the root of idolatry is not denying God but taking yourself as a thing apart, and holiness rests only on what sets itself aside before Him. The Tzemach Tzedek says every 'I' is the source of evil and parts hearts; the Maggid's way back is to hold yourself as nothing, which keeps you from wrong and returns you whole. Bring it down to the moment today you are about to take the credit. The picture is the Tanya's, an eagle raising itself; it is only a picture, since there is no height to climb to that He is not already filling. You, here, now: there is none besides Him.

**Stay.** 'Not a thing in itself: there is none besides Him', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Relief at putting down a weight: nothing to defend or inflate. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'There is nothing besides Him.' Then give a coin to charity today where no one sees it. Line for the day: 'Not I: with God's help.'

**Where the sources differ.** How is pride undone? Tanya 22 and the Maggid: by bitul and contemplation of the unity, holding oneself as nothing. R. Nachman: by closeness to the tzaddik, whose spirit turns 'acher' (another) into 'echad' (one).

## S132 · Anger · כעס

**Face:** F01 The world said into being now · **also touches:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F02 The higher and the lower unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** This is for ordinary irritations. It does not mean accepting harm: the one who harms is still answerable, and if someone is hurting you, protect yourself and tell someone who can help.

**The glimpse.** The person who hurt you is, at that very moment, being kept alive by His word, and what reached you came from Him through a messenger. Anger is the moment that is forgotten.

**Hold.** 'Had he believed that this came to him from God, he would not be angry at all' (Iggeret HaKodesh 25).

**The move.** Open it. The one who hurt you chose and is answerable; yet what reached you was decreed, and there are many messengers. Even at that very moment, the power of God and the spirit of His mouth were clothed in him and kept him alive, as in Shimi, when David said 'the Lord told him, curse'. And the ground is the Besht's 'Your word stands in the heavens': creation from nothing at every moment, which is providence. Bring it down to one small recent irritation, a rude driver or a sharp word, not a deep wound. The Tanya's case is King David and Shimi; it is only a case, not a rule for bearing harm, since the one who harms still answers for it. You, here, now: the person who irritated you is being kept alive by His word right now.

**Stay.** 'This came from God, through a messenger', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Like finding that the one who hurt you is a messenger, held in being by the same word that holds you. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'Forever, Lord, Your word stands in the heavens.' Then, the next time someone irritates you today, answer with one kindness, as the Tanya's beinoni repays with kindness. Line for the day: 'His word is in him too.'

**Where the sources differ.** Does 'everything is from God' dissolve anger or excuse it? Iggeret HaKodesh 25: whoever believes the harm came from God is not angry at all. Izhbitz (Beit Yaakov): Cain pleaded that everything, even his anger, was in Heaven's hands; the answer shows he had choice, though the text grants the plea some truth for the first generations. The first applies providence to what others do to me; the second refuses to let it free me from what I do.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 25:2 (line 1342): לפי שבעת כעסו נסתלקה ממנו האמונה, כי אילו היה מאמין שמאת ה׳ היתה זאת לו – לא היה בכעס כלל IH 25: 'whoever is angry is as if he worshipped idols', because in his anger faith left him; had he believed it was from God, he would not be angry at all. - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 25:4 (line 1344): אפילו בשעה זו ממש שמכהו או מקללו – מתלבש בו כח ה׳ ורוח פיו יתברך המחייהו ומקיימו IH 25: even at the very moment one strikes or curses him, the power of God and the breath of His mouth are clothed in the other and sustain him (Shimi). - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11:13 (line 978): וזו עצה טובה להנצל מכעס וכל מיני קפידא IT 11: remembering one's sin makes a person accept whatever comes with joy, from Heaven or from people: 'a good counsel to be saved from anger'.

## S133 · Desire · תאוה

**Face:** F11 Darkness turned to light · **also touches:** F09 The unity of opposites; F01 The world said into being now; F06 The soul as part of the One **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** The pull toward what is sweet and beautiful is His own love, fallen and dressed in a thing; traced back it points to Him, and held back even for a moment it raises His glory.

**Hold.** 'The root of beauty is a divine power; why be drawn after the part? Better to cleave to the root of all worlds' (the Besht, Tzava'at HaRivash).

**The move.** Open it. The Besht says the sweetness of food is its divine life, and the love you feel for it is love fallen from its root: if you love this, how much more must you love Him. The Maggid says a thought of desire fell from the world of love, and bound to love of the Creator it goes back to its root. And the Tanya draws the line: the pull toward what is permitted can be raised, what is forbidden cannot; and holding back even a little from the permitted subdues the other side and raises His glory above. Bring it down to the food on your plate at the next meal, before the first bite. The picture is the Mitteler Rebbe's, a soul scattered into many pieces by many wants and gathered by its one deepest will; it is only a picture, since the soul is not broken, only pulled many ways. You, here, now: this pull, traced back, is love for Him.

**Stay.** 'Better to cleave to the root of all worlds', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** A small, quiet strength: the pull is not fought as an enemy but traced home. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips the blessing over your food at the next meal; before it, hold back from the first bite for a moment, the Tanya's 'even a little'. Line for the day: 'Love fallen, lifted back.'

**Where the sources differ.** Desire: trace it to its root, subdue it, or burn it? The Besht and the Maggid: desire is fallen love, so trace beauty and sweetness to the root of all worlds. The Alter Rebbe: permitted desire is nogah, forbidden desire wholly impure; subdue even in the permitted. The Rashab: contemplation produces fiery love that burns the animal soul's desire. The contrast is our characterization.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tzava'at HaRivash 90:5 (line 179): נמצא שורש היופי הוא כח אלוקי ולמה לי למשוך אחר החלק טוב לי להתדבק בשרשא ועקרא דכל עלמין The Besht: beauty comes from the divine power spread in the woman; why be drawn after the part, when one can cleave to the root of all worlds? - Tzava'at HaRivash 120:2 (line 243): אם אני אוהב זאת שהוא רק אהבה שנפלה בשבירה מלובשת בטיפה סרוחה. כמה יש לי לאהוב אותו ית' The Besht: love is in exile, clothed in a woman or in food; if I love this fallen love, how much more must I love Him. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 8:3 (line 150): מה שאין כן היצר הרע וכח המתאוה לדברים המותרים למלאת תאותו, הוא שד משדין יהודאין, לפי שיכול לחזור לקדושה Tanya 8: the pull toward permitted pleasures is kelipat nogah, which can return to holiness, unlike forbidden desire.

## S134 · Jealousy and baseless hatred · קנאה ושנאת חנם

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F09 The unity of opposites; F07 The home below **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**Before you start.** This is for ordinary resentment. It does not ask you to stay close to someone who harms you: the Tanya keeps hatred of the evil and love of the hidden good both true.

**The glimpse.** Every Jew you resent has the same Father you do; the bodies are divided, the souls are not. Baseless hatred splits what is one at the root.

**Hold.** 'One Father to them all, and so all Israel are called brothers, from the root of their souls in the one God; only the bodies are divided' (Tanya 32).

**The move.** Open it. When all souls are joined, Iggeret HaKodesh 31 says, the flow goes round to bind them all to the One; and the Temple fell over baseless hatred and the parting of hearts. What parts hearts, the Tzemach Tzedek says, is the 'I', every sense of self. And the Tanya holds two things true together, even toward a sinner: hatred for the evil, and love for the hidden good in him. Bring it down to one person you resent a little. The picture is the Tanya's, brothers of one father; it is only a picture, since the Father here is not a man, and the brotherhood is at the root, not in the body. You, here, now: this person has your Father.

**Stay.** 'One Father to us all', said again slowly, with that person in mind. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Something like recognizing family in a stranger's face. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, before you pray: 'I take upon myself the mitzvah, love your fellow as yourself', with that person in mind. Then do one small kindness today for someone you find hard. Line for the day: 'One Father to us all.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: for baseless hatred the tie is stated (Iggeret HaKodesh 31, Tanya 32, Hayom Yom 10 Tevet); for jealousy as a trait of its own no verified passage ties it to the unity, so jealousy is not taken up here beyond the 'I' that parts hearts, and even that application is ours.

**Where the sources differ.** Who does the higher teshuvah? Tanya: the person, through Torah, kindness and prayer. Karlin and R. Tzadok (not re-verified): God. Polonoye: baseless hatred cannot be repaired by any reprover, only by the Holy One Himself.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 32:2 (line 444): ואב אחד לכולנה, ולכן נקראו כל ישראל ״אחים״ ממש, מצד שורש נפשם בה׳ אחד רק שהגופים מחולקים Tanya 32: all souls have one Father, so Israel are literally brothers from their root in the one God; only the bodies are divided. - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 31:8 (line 1484): כי כאשר כל הנשמות דבוקות ומקושרות יחד … לקשר ולחבר כולן להוי׳ אחד ולדבקה בו יתברך IH 31: when all souls are joined, the flow circulates and binds them all to 'Havayah echad'. - Hayom Yom, תשרי / ו תשרי:1 (line 491): ואהבת לרעך כמוך, כמו שאמר רבינו הזקן, אשר זהו כלי לואהבת את הוי' אלקיך Hayom Yom 6 Tishrei, in the Rashab's explanation of the five ways of teshuvah: love of one's fellow is a vessel for love of God, 'as the Alter Rebbe said'.

## S135 · Speech: slander and idle words · לשון הרע ודברים בטלים

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F11 Darkness turned to light; F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** We are one body of many limbs, so a word against another Jew wounds the one body; and a word held back from idle talk raises His glory.

**Hold.** 'We are all literally like one man', made of many limbs (Iggeret HaKodesh 22).

**The move.** Open it. Because we are one, an evil thought about a fellow is to be pushed out like a thought of idolatry, and slander weighs as much as the gravest sins. The Besht's counsel on seeing another's fault is 'guard your tongue', and then include him with you, since all are one person. And the Tanya says holding back even worldly talk your heart wants to say subdues the other side and raises His glory above. Bring it down to one conversation today where something about someone absent is about to be said. The picture is one body with many limbs; it is only a picture, since souls are not limbs, but it shows why a word against one wounds all. You, here, now: the one you would speak of is part of you.

**Stay.** 'We are all like one man', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** The quiet of a word kept back, and the one body left whole. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'My God, guard my tongue from evil', from the end of the Amidah. Then keep one remark about someone absent unsaid today. Line for the day: 'One body.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: for slander the tie is stated through the unity of Israel as 'one man' (Iggeret HaKodesh 22), and that this is the unity of God rests on joining it to Tanya 32's 'one Father', which is ours; for idle words the tie runs through the husks and devekut, which is indirect.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 22:19 (line 1314): אם כן אנחנו היות כולנו כאיש אחד ממש IH 22: all Israel are 'like one man' made of many limbs; therefore evil thoughts about a fellow are to be driven out 'like a thought of idolatry'. - Keter Shem Tov 1:59:1 (line 97): ואמר הפסוק תחבושת לזה שינצור לשונו The Besht: the remedy on seeing another's sin is 'guard your tongue', then include him through the unity of all as one man. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 27:12 (line 369): וכן אם בולם פיו מלדבר דברים שלבו מתאוה מאד לדברם מענייני העולם … דאתכפיא סטרא אחרא לתתא – אסתלק יקרא דקודשאבריךהוא וקדושתו לעילא הרבה Tanya 27: holding back from worldly talk the heart craves subdues the other side below and raises the glory of God above.

## S136 · Forbearance · מעביר על מדותיו

**Face:** F05 The Essence beyond both · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F09 The unity of opposites; F01 The world said into being now **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** For slights, not harm: passing over is not staying silent about abuse, and if someone is hurting you, tell someone who can help.

**The glimpse.** Letting a slight go by lifts you above your own measures, to the place above measure where His forgiveness keeps no count.

**Hold.** 'Whoever passes over his traits, all his sins are passed over' (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11).

**The move.** Open it. His forgiveness, the Alter Rebbe says, is the same for once as for a thousand times, because before the Infinite small and great numbers are the same. The Chernobyler says whoever passes over his traits for one who angered him goes above the traits, to the place of teshuvah, where there is only kindness; and so all is forgiven him. The Maharash says stepping past your own measure is what makes your soul a vessel for forgiveness from above measure. And R. Nachman puts it into practice: hear your shame and be silent. Bring it down to one small slight from this week that you are still keeping score on. The picture is the Maharash's, the measure a person measures with; it is only a picture, since what is above it has no measure at all. You, here, now: letting this one go, above measure.

**Stay.** 'Passing over my traits', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** The quiet of not answering back; R. Nachman calls hearing one's shame and staying silent the main teshuvah. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Let one small slight pass today without a reply. Tonight, at the bedtime Shema, say with the lips: 'I forgive anyone who angered or wronged me.' Line for the day: 'Above measure.'

**Note.** The Alter Rebbe states infinite forgiveness above (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11:7) and forbearance below (11:13) without joining them; the joining is stated by the Chernobyler and the Maharash, and read into the Alter Rebbe's own texts it is ours.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11:13 (line 978): לקבל בשמחה כל המאורעות המתרגשות ובאות, בין מן השמים, בין עלידי הבריות … ו״כל המעביר על מדותיו – מעבירים לו על כל פשעיו״ IT 11: accept everything with joy, from Heaven or people; 'whoever passes over his traits, all his sins are passed over'. - Me'or Einayim, Ha'Azinu 19 (line 647): וזהו כל המעביר על מדותיו שעובר למעלה מהמדות למקום תשובה אז מוחלין לו כל פשעיו Chernobyl: teshuvah preceded the worlds and is above the traits, where all is kindness; one who passes over his traits goes above the traits to the place of teshuvah. - Toras Shmuel, תרכ"ז / מאמרים / מי אל כמוך כו' / תמה:1 (line 1360): וכדי שיתגלה למטה על נפש האדם צריך להיות מעביר על מדותיו אז הוא כלי שיתגלה לו בחי' זו The Maharash: forgiveness from Arich Anpin (above the measuring line) is revealed in a soul only if it passes over its traits and so becomes a vessel.

## S137 · Toil · יגיעה

**Face:** F05 The Essence beyond both · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F06 The soul as part of the One; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** What is hidden deepest is found only by digging: work against your own grain uncovers the awe buried in every heart, and in Torah, the Rebbe says, the Essence itself is there to be found.

**Hold.** 'One must dig with great toil to uncover the treasure of awe of Heaven hidden in the understanding of the heart of every Jew' (Tanya 42).

**The move.** Open it. 'I toiled and found, believe it.' The Alter Rebbe calls only this 'service': what a person does with great toil against his nature, setting his will aside before His. The Rashab says the dominant trait is refined only by toil of flesh and toil of soul. And the Rebbe gives the reason for toil in Torah in particular: the Essence is found in it, so the toil has no limit. Bring it down to one passage of Torah you find hard, open in front of you today. The picture is the Tanya's, a hidden treasure dug out; it is only a picture, since what is found was never buried in the ground but in your own understanding. You, here, now: the treasure is in you.

**Stay.** 'Dig with great toil to uncover the treasure', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The plain satisfaction of work against the grain; what is found was there all along. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'I toiled and I found.' Then go over that hard passage once more today than you would have. Line for the day: 'Dig once more.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie of human toil to the Essence is stated only late, by the Rebbe and for toil in Torah (Likkutei Sichos 35); the earlier sources tie toil to bitul and to uncovering hidden awe, and the flint and coal of Rosh Hashanah 5741 is said of God's drawing down of the soul, not of human work, so it is not used here.
