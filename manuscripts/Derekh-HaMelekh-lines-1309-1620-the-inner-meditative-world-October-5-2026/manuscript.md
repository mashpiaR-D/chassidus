## 1. What this range is

This is the last stretch of the book: the end of the Pesach and Shavuot talks, followed by the whole appendix of lectures, letters and short pieces. For the inner world it is one of the richest ranges in the book. It holds the only record of the **hashkatah** (quieting) teaching, written down by a student (L.1553-1556), and the *Tzav veZiruz* pieces on self-scrutiny and foreign thoughts (L.1548-1552). It also contains a guided prayer of longing (L.1588-1595) and an almost autobiographical account of closing the eyes and being "lifted by the forelock" (L.1486).

- **Pesach 7:6-9 (L.1309-1312).** Oral against written revelation. What is "written" into a person turns the body itself holy, where the oral kind only rests on top of it. Song is the start of this.
- **Pesach 8, Seventh day of Pesach 5690 (L.1313-1329).** A key text on *inner seeing*. People see a single whole *picture* (tmunah), which is made in the mind and not handed over by the eyes. Every good stirring is a spark of higher light being "seen" inside. To get there you must *gather yourself into one aim* (yichud halev). Faith has to come down into "the mirror of the soul". Moses as against the other prophets ("tov ro'i").
- **Pesach 9 (L.1330-1335).** Pesach gives the power of *dilug*, skipping far above one's level for a moment. The text breaks off (defective).
- **Pesach 10 (L.1336-1344).** **"Ayekah, where are you in yourself?"** The person as a guest-house for passing thoughts. The fire analogy: will and mind can gather strength without limit. Reflection "between himself and himself" on the Infinite and one's own emptiness, until one is "not the same man". The rebbe's fervour has to show up in his chassidim.
- **Pesach 11 (L.1345-1349).** Voice (kol) against articulated speech (dibbur). The voice shows the inner depth. How Shema and Baruch Shem are said aloud and in a whisper.
- **Shavuot 1, 5685 (L.1350-1366).** All pleasure is God's pleasure, and craving is a counterfeit of it. **The self is a hidden, abstract essence.** The world only draws out of us "the shadow of the world". The Torah must "make us" (na'aseh before nishma) and bring out the hidden self.
- **Shavuot 2 (L.1367-1379).** The alarm one feels at a sinful thought is already a piece of Godliness revealed. Treat the thought as if it were the deed. "In the shadow of God" (Betzalel) against "wholly in the light". Set fixed times when God is revealed within.
- **Shavuot 3, 5689 (L.1380-1389).** Torah grows whatever is already inside the person (rain on seeds). Israel is a piece of Supernal Thought. The longing to know is the unity that came before separation showing itself. The sign of real will is that one *still longs after one already knows*.
- **Shavuot 4 (L.1390-1403).** Two types. One feels holiness only while doing a mitzvah. The other "is suddenly lifted above the world". The *reshimu* (trace) of the one light is found by unifying one's life around a single path. **Find your own inclination (netiyah).**
- **Shavuot 5 (L.1404-1417).** Dig within your own "earth" until you find living water. A hidden power of growth, and the push (dechifah) to create new holiness. One's own new Torah and new will are born through union with God.
- **Shavuot 6 (L.1418-1428).** Everyone has a spark of prophecy. "Sealed prophecy" is received at Sinai. Know yourself as a king's son. The pure, causeless awe of Shavuot is to be widened within.
- **Shavuot 7 (L.1429-1447).** The Messiah-spark in each soul is brought out by *will*, since mere thought holds only "the shadow of the wanted thing". "Is God among us or not?" Daily self-reckoning. Craving comes from making oneself a separate thing.
- **Shavuot 8, 5694 (L.1448-1460).** A letter to Eretz Yisrael. Hearing the Shekhinah moaning "like a dove" within. Resolve. Don't look sideways at others. Reach out to others. Toil in Torah.
- **Shabbat Teshuvah sermon (L.1461-1479).** Faith is like a child bound to his father, with no reasons. Prince against peasant. Shabbat in Piaseczno as one song rising from every house. Study circles. The parable of the queen's last fortress.
- **Introduction to the lost Zohar commentary (L.1480-1499).** Ezra looks for "the road into the depths of a person". Midrash is "work of the heart". Seeing the soul of things with closed eyes. The ascent of the Zohar's authors. Studying Zohar means "dwelling with one's soul".
- **Zohar commentary fragment (L.1500-1501).** Upper and lower unity. An awakening begun only in thought already reaches Malkhut.
- **Chovat HaAvreichim fragment (L.1502-1515).** A cry of prayer (L.1504). Human wisdom is "empty depiction". Holy knowledge is *being and life*, felt in the whole body, and that is the spirit of prophecy.
- **Chovat HaTalmidim basis, in Yiddish (L.1516-1529).** Tender rules for a yeshiva boy, including going to sleep in holiness.
- **Siyum HaShas (L.1530-1532).** One who learns is in the World to Come *now*.
- **Letters (L.1533-1547).** His wife's death (1937). A short note: why look for proofs of God outside yourself? (L.1546).
- **Talk to yeshiva students, 5681 (L.1548-1552).** Ups and downs. Ulterior motives (peniyot) and foreign thoughts: *don't fight them*.
- **Inyan HaShkatah (L.1553-1556).** The quieting method, written from memory by a student.
- **Letters for the High Holidays and Pesach (L.1557-1567).** "I have stripped off the world and myself." Picture that you are in Eden at the Seder.
- **Educational essay "Talmid Vatik" (L.1568-1611).** Middot are holy vessels. Longing is the wings. A guided outpouring prayer. The soul of Knesset Yisrael shows itself in the individual as fervour.
- **Sermon at the Yeshiva Congress (L.1612-1620).** The stirring towards Torah is itself the Shekhinah's voice. A public appeal.

## 2. The method: concrete instructions

**a. Quieting (hashkatah), L.1555-1556.** This is the core practice, recorded by a student and not in the Rebbe's own hand. 1. The rationale: the self's own busyness is what blocks inspiration from above, and sleep lets it in because the mind is quiet. So the aim is to reach a sleep-like quiet while awake, "by quieting his thoughts and wishes that flood a person without end" (L.1555: "היינו בהשקטת מחשבותיו ורצונותיו השוטפים באדם לבלי קץ"). 2. **Watch your thoughts** for a few minutes: "that the person begin to look at his thoughts for a short while, about a few minutes, that is, 'what am I thinking'" (L.1556: "שיתחיל האיש להביט על מחשבותיו שעה קלה לערך איזה רגעים היינו מה אני חושב"). 3. The result: "he will feel, slowly, slowly, that his head is emptying and his thoughts have stopped their usual flow" (L.1556: "אז ירגיש לאט לאט שראשו מתרוקן ומחשבותיו עמדו משטפן הרגיל"). 4. **Then say one verse**, "such as *Hashem Elokim Emet*", to tie the emptied head to one holy thought (L.1556: "ואז יתחיל לאמר פסוק אחד כגון ד' אלקים אמת"). 5. **Then ask for what you need**, such as strengthening faith, love or awe. For faith he gave the Yiddish formula (roughly): "I believe with complete faith that the Creator is the only existent in the world... everything is only the shining of God's light." For love: "I would so want to be close to the holy Creator" (L.1556: "איך וואלט געוואלט זיין אזוי נאהנט צום הייליגען באשעפער"). 6. **Softly, not forcefully.** "He repeated it several times, but not saying it forcefully" (L.1556: "וכך שינן איזה פעמים אבל לא שיאמר זאת בחזקה"). Saying it hard "can only rouse his ego; rather, in a very light way" (L.1556: "ובאמירת בתוקף יכול הוא רק לעורר את האנוכיות שלו רק אדרבה באופן קל מאוד"). 7. **Positive, not negative.** Used for fixing traits, it should work "not in a negative way but in a positive way" (L.1556: "אבל לא באופן שלילי רק באופן חיובי"). A lazy person speaks of acquiring alacrity and not of banishing laziness. His image: tell a crying child "don't cry" and he cries more. 8. **An alternative way in:** gaze at "the small hand of a clock, which hardly moves" (L.1556: "בהבטה על שעון על המחוג הקטן שכמעט אינו זז"). 9. **Melody at the end:** after quieting, to draw down inspiration, "he instructed to say the verse *Horeni Hashem darkekha* in the Rebbe's special melody" (L.1556: "צוה לאמר הפסוק הורני ד' דרכיך בניגונו המיוחד של אדמו\"ר"). 10. **Duration and promise:** practise for several weeks. Then when saying "*Zeh Keli ve'anvehu*" it will be like pointing with a finger (L.1556: "יהי' בחינת מראין באצבע").

**b. The daily inventory of thoughts, L.1337.** "Let a person gather his thoughts over a twenty-four-hour period and look at them" (L.1337: "יצבור נא האדם מחשבות מן המעת לעת ויסתכל בהם"). He will see that they are only events of time and the world. This is the diagnostic that comes before *Ayekah*. It pairs with the hashkatah remark that a day's stream of thought hardly differs from a madman's (L.1555: "אז יראה שכמעט אין חילוק בינו לבין משוגע").

**c. Reflection "between himself and himself", L.1343.** "The Jew who reflects between himself and himself" (L.1343: "לכן הישראלי שמתבונן בינו לבין עצמו"). Here is the Infinite source of holiness, "and why am I an empty vessel" (L.1343: "למה אני כלי ריק"). This lets longing flare up from the innermost point and take over the body.

**d. Unifying the self around one aim, L.1324-1325, 1399-1402.** First "unify the heart" (L.1324: "צריכים מקודם ליחוד הלב"). One must not be "one person in the street and another in the study hall" (L.1399: "ראשית כל צריכים שלא נהיה אחר ברחוב ואחר בבהמ\"ד"). Take one trait at a time as the aim of all your actions. Fix set sessions (shiurim) to deepen that aim (L.1325: "לכן מי שאינו עושה לעצמו שעורים"). Later, **find your own inclination**: "every person must look into himself to find his inclination" (L.1402: "וכל איש צריך להסתכל בעצמו למצוא את הנטיה שלו"). That is the one thing around which all his service comes together.

**e. Reaching the "picture" behind a teaching, L.1319.** When reading a tzaddik's book, don't stay with the reasoning. "Strive to reach the picture of revelation, and then he will feel some effect within" (L.1319: "לכן ישתדל לבא עד התמונה של התגלות ואז ירגיש איזה פעולה בקרבו").

**f. Deepening one word of a verse, L.1322.** When saying Psalm 23, he sees his own and Israel's troubles all around, then "deepens greatly in *You are with me*". This is contemplation through a single charged word.

**g. Digging, all day, before prayer, L.1413.** "Also before prayer... all day, to deepen in his mind: why am I so low and dark" (L.1413: "גם בכל היום להעמיק בדעתו למה אני כ\"כ שפל וחשוך"). Then ask why you feel a push toward worldly things and none toward holiness. The more he deepens, "the low garment strips off this push of his" (L.1413: "וככל אשר יעמיק כך במחשבתו יתפשט הלבוש הנמוך מן דחיפתו זו").

**h. Set times of revelation, L.1379.** If one cannot always be aflame, "at least let him fix for himself times when God is revealed within him" (L.1379: "עכ\"פ יקבע לו זמנים שיתגלה ד' בקרבו"). Compare L.1595: keep fixed "times of love" of outpouring, especially on Shabbat, festivals and at Seudah Shlishit ("רק תתמיד לקבוע עיתים עת דודים אלו").

**i. Posture: closed eyes, facing the wall, L.1486 and L.1493.** "When a Jew closes his eyes and turns his eyes to the wall, especially on Friday evenings in prayer, and says *Tov lehodot*" (L.1486: "וכאשר איש ישראל סוגר עיניו ומסב עיניו אל הקיר"). On closing the eyes as a discipline of perception: "when he closes his eyes and does not let the things of the world draw it out" (L.1493: "וכשסוגר את עיניו. ולא יתן לדברי העולם שיוציאוה"), sight withdraws back into the unity of the soul.

**j. Treat a passing sinful thought as a done deed, L.1372.** "Let it be in his eyes as if he had already done the sin in action" (L.1372: "יהיה בעיניו כאלו כבר עשה את החטא בפועל"). The soul then releases the strength that matches it.

**k. Daily question, L.1438 and L.1440.** Don't settle for "no worse than yesterday". Ask "in what did I improve today" (L.1438: "רק תמיד יסתכל בעצמו במה היטבתי היום"). And ask "have I already found and revealed today the light of Messiah within me?" (L.1440: "האם כבר מצאתי וגיליתי היום את אור המשיח אשר בי").

**l. Picturing (tziyur) at the Seder, L.1567.** Stripping off: "I have stripped off from me the whole world, even myself" (L.1567: "גם את כל העולם אף את עצמי פשטתי ממני"). Then "let him picture that he is in the Garden of Eden" (L.1567: "ואח\"כ כשבא אל הסדר יצייר לו עתה שהוא בג\"ע").

**m. Guided outpouring (hitbodedut-like), L.1588-1595.** A prayer written out for the student to contemplate and repeat, on God as the only existent who is hidden only from our side: "And if you think so and persist in contemplating" (L.1591: "ואם כה תחשוב וכה תתמיד להתבונן"). Its goal: "that I may find my Father who hid from me" (L.1594: "ואזכה למצוא את אבי שהתחבא ממני"). He tells the student to read these pieces at least once a week, whenever his heart feels longing (L.1611).

**n. Resolve after hearing the Shekhinah, L.1452.** Say: "I throw everything off" (L.1452: "משליך אני את הכל") and return to serve with all one's strength. Fix set times for Torah.

**o. Self-reckoning for the middot even when they are quiet, L.1584.** "Make for yourself sessions of self-reckoning, not only when your traits are stirred up" but also when they rest (L.1584: "עשה לך שעורים לחשבון הנפש. לא רק בעת אשר מדותיך תתעוררנה").

**p. Studying Zohar, L.1498.** Prepare the heart. Know that you must leave the world for a while, and "all his effort must be to dwell with his soul" (L.1498: "וכל השתדלותו צריכה להיות שידור עם נפשו").

**q. Sleep, L.1529 (Yiddish).** Say the bedtime Shema seriously, don't chatter before sleep, and hold a holy thought as you fall asleep, so the soul comes back refreshed with "rtzon from Gan Eden".

## 3. The inner experience

- **Inner seeing is a picture made from within.** "With his eyes he sees separate things, and in his mind he sees one picture" (L.1317: "בעיניו רואה דברים בודדים. ובמוחו רואה תמונה אחת"). The servant of God, steeped in holiness, sees a *holy* picture in everything.
- **Every good stirring is a seeing.** "Every small uplift and every good spark a Jew sometimes feels within him is a kind of revelation of a spark of higher light that is seen within him" (L.1318: "כי כל מעט התרוממות וכל נצוץ טוב שמרגיש האיש הישראלי לפעמים בקרבו. מין התגלות נצוץ אור עליון שנראית בקרבו היא"). This holds even when he doesn't know what he sees: "his mazal sees".
- **"You are with me" felt in the body.** "Then he feels the *You are with me* dwelling within his heart and upon his heart, in his mind and upon his mind" (L.1322: "אז מרגיש את האתה עמדי שוכן בקרב לבו ועל לבו במוחו ועל מוחו"). And it can't be had on demand: "he cannot always feel it so, even if he wants to" (L.1322: "ולא תמיד יכול להרגיש כן בהרגשה ממשית אף אם רוצה להרגיש תמיד").
- **Being flooded and not the same man.** When holiness is gathered "from beyond all worlds", his whole body recoils and lies nullified beneath its weight, "until he is really not the same man he was before" (L.1343: "עד שממש אינו אותו האיש שהיה מקודם"). He "stands wholly about to burst" from the light. The essence of fervour is *inside*, not in shouting or clapping (L.1343: "אבל עיקר התפעלות והתלהבות דקדושה היא בקרב האיש").
- **The sudden lifting (aliyah), L.1396.** This is the fullest description in the range. There are times "he suddenly feels he has been raised and lifted above the world to the upper palaces" (L.1396: "פתאום מרגיש שהונף והורם מעל לעולם אל היכלי עליון"). In one verse of Song of Songs he feels such delight that he sees himself wholly in light. "The world has withdrawn from him and all the thoughts and ways of the world; not that he must overcome them, but they are blurred and wiped entirely from before his eyes" (L.1396: "העולם נתרחק ממנו וכל מחשבות ודרכי העולם, לא שהוא צריך להתגבר עליהם רק נטשטשו ונמחקו לגמרי מנגד עיניו"). The praises are not a deep subject to investigate. Instead "he snatches and eats one in a thousand of the higher light and holiness his soul is surrounded by" (L.1396: "רק הוא חוטף בזה ואוכל אחת מני אלף מן אור וקדושה עליונה שנפשו מוקפת בהם"). He wonders "at the whole world, why they stand silent" (L.1396: "עד שמשתאה ומשתומם הוא על כל העולם למה הם עומדים דום"), and calls on sun and moon to praise.
- **Melting in prayer.** The sign of one whose spark shows itself in prayer: "moved by every word and verse until his heart almost melts within him" (L.1402: "מתפעל מכל תיבה ופסוק עד שכמעט נמס לבו בקרבו").
- **Lifted by the forelock, L.1486.** At the wall with closed eyes, sometimes "the person is astonished at himself: what is this happening to me?" (L.1486: "ולפעמים משתאה האיש מעצמו. מה זאת נעשה עמי"). "He really feels how he has now been taken by the forelock" (L.1486: "וממש מרגיש הוא איך שלקחוהו עתה בציצית ראשו") and raised from the profane to a pure world. From the sparks of his soul he makes crowns for his Beloved.
- **Pure awe without a reason.** At Shavuot, "simply fear before God without any reason or cause" (L.1428: "סתם פחד מפני ה' בלא שום טעב וסבה"). One should "widen it within and be stirred by it more and more" (L.1428: "צריך להרחיב אותה בקרבו ולהתרגש יותר ויותר ממנה").
- **The heart's own seeing (prophecy in miniature).** What chesed or gevurah *is* can be known only "by the part of prophecy in Israel, whose heart gazes and sparkles in the light above" (L.1421: "יכולים רק בחלק הנביאות שבישראל שלבו מסתכל ומתנוצץ באור של מעלה").
- **Seeing the soul of things.** One who keeps up his fervour and "the spirit that has overpowered to burst over the banks of his body" comes to see the spirit of what he looks at first and its body only in passing, "until he merits to gaze at the soul as it is in itself" (L.1493: "עד שיזכה להסתכל בנפש כשהיא לעצמה").
- **The Zohar sages' ascent.** "He went and drew near, another step and another pace" (L.1495: "הלך והתקרב. עוד צעד ועוד פסיעה"), until nothing individual remained, "like a candle before a torch they were included and nullified" (L.1495: "וכנר בפני אבוקה נתכללו ונתבטלו"). Even there the thirst continues. Joy that bursts past words becomes dancing and shouting without speech (L.1494: "רק מתחיל לרקוד ולצעוק בקול תרועת השמחה").
- **Standing before God.** Holy knowledge fills him with certainty "clearer than daylight", and "especially when he prays he really stands before God" (L.1513: "ובפרט כשמתפלל ממש עומד הוא לפני ד'").
- **The soul sees God directly.** "Don't you hear and see how your soul, seeing God, speaks to Him as *You*?" (L.1546: "הוי ואבוי האם אין אתה שומע ורואה איך נפשך בראותה את ד'").
- **The self bursting into the collective soul.** When Knesset Yisrael is revealed in him, "his body and particular soul become too narrow to contain" it (L.1604: "גופו ונפשו הפרטית נעשת צרה מהביל את נשמת הכנסת־ישראל"). His boundary stands ready to burst and rises like waves of the sea.
- **Hearing the Shekhinah.** One whose heart is broken over Israel's ruin "hears within him the voice of the Shekhinah moaning like a dove and pleading with him" (L.1451: "שומע בקרבו את קול השכינה שמנהמת כיונה ומבקשתו"). The stirring towards Torah *is* that voice (L.1614: "וההתעוררות אשר בלבבכם קול השכינה היא שמנהמת כיונה בנפשכם").
- **The Seder word you can't let go.** Every word feels so sweet "he wants to repeat it a thousand times" (L.1567: "רוצה הוא לכופלה עד אלף פעמים").
- **The low point, in his own voice.** "Is not service a flight to You, God?... Sometimes despair seizes me" (L.1504: "והאם אין העבודה טיסה אליך ד'").

## 4. The self and God

This range supports the owner's thesis directly, and more explicitly than most of the book. The argument has five steps.

1. **The ordinary person has no self.** He is a lodging-house for passing thoughts. "The person's own essence is not there" (L.1337: "ועצם שלו של האדם אין"). God's question to Adam is a question about selfhood: "*Ayekah*: where are you, in yourself?" (L.1337: "ואיכה, איה אתה בעצמך"). The goal is that a person "be found by himself" and not be "a trampled threshold for the events and ideas of the world" (L.1338: "ולא יהיה כאסקופה הנדרסת למאורעות ורעיונות העולם").
2. **The self is found only through the pull toward God.** How does one come home to oneself? Only by stirring holy fervour, gathering holiness "from beyond all worlds". "As long as he lacks this form, the man is not yet in his home and his essence" (L.1344: "וכל זמן שאין לו צורה זו אז אין האיש עוד בביתו ועצמותו"). In this text, finding oneself and being filled with God are the same event.
3. **The true self is hidden, and the self you know is the world's shadow.** "The essence of a person is an abstract, invisible essence" (L.1358: "נמצא שעצם האדם הוא עצם מופשט בלתי נראה"). "The self you know is only the shadow of the world" (L.1360: "הלא את עצמך שאתה מכיר רק את צל העולם אתה מכיר"). Hence na'aseh before nishma: "What will we make? Ourselves. The Torah will give birth to the hidden essence of the person" (L.1361: "את מה נעשה. את עצמנו. התורה תוליד את עצם האדם הנסתר"). This is "the hidden Joseph, revealed" (L.1365: "יוסף הנעלם שנתגלה").
4. **The self is a piece of the One.** Why does a Jew believe in God's unity despite a fragmented world? "Because there is in him a part of the One" (L.1387: "למה הוא מאמין מפני שיש בו חלק האחד"). The longing to know is "the unity before separation" showing itself. So one finds God by digging inward: "The main thing is to go deep into oneself and find the holiness also in oneself" (L.1412: "העיקר הוא להעמיק בעצמו ולמצוא גם בעצמו את הקדושה"). Also "dig within himself and in the element of earth in him" (L.1413: "וזה ענין החפירה שצריך איש הישראל לחפור בקרבו וביסוד העפר שבו").
5. **You don't need proofs outside yourself.** "Why do you need proofs outside yourself for His existence?" (L.1546: "ומעתה למה לך ראיות מחוץ לעצמך על מציאותו"). Studying the Zohar is "dwelling with one's soul", and what one grasps "is only the light of his soul" (L.1498: "אבל רק אור נפשו הוא"). He cites "if you want to know what is above, know it from yourself" (L.1499: "אם תרצה לדעת למעלה תדע זאת ממך").

**Where he qualifies the thesis (keep this):** - The found self is **not the separate ego.** "A person must know that he is not a separate something" (L.1447: "שהאדם צריך לדעת שאינו יש ודבר נפרד"). Craving comes from separateness: "the separated one seeks craving, the whole one seeks good will" (L.1445: "הנפרד מבוקשו תאוה, והשלם מבוקשו רצון טוב"). - In prayer the individual gives way: fervour shows that "Knesset Yisrael" has been revealed in him, and coldness shows he is only "a private man" (L.1605: "אם לב הישראלי קר ואינו מתלהב בעבודתו את ד' סימן הוא שאיש פרטי הוא"). - **The self-as-ego blocks.** "The person's selfhood is what opposes inspiration from above" (L.1555: "כי הישות של האדם הוא המתנגד להשראה ממרום"). That is the reason for hashkatah.

So in his terms: one seeks oneself, finds that the self one knew was borrowed, and finds the hidden essence, which turns out to be "a part of the One". The finding reaches God only when the separate "I" gives way. The father image closes the loop: "that I may find my Father who hid from me" (L.1594). - **Dignity as the ground.** "I am a pure essence, a king's son" (L.1422: "אני עצם זך בן מלך"). Even when one feels lowness, "let him not fall inwardly saying: I am evil in essence" (L.1422: "אל יפול בקרבו לאמר עצם רע הוא"). Faith is like a child's bond with a father, with no reasons behind it: "he is my father and I am bound to him" (L.1465: "רק אבי הוא ואני קשור בו").

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

- **Thinking in place of wanting.** Everyone believes the holy is in his mind, but "only the shadow and image of the wanted thing is in him, thought alone" (L.1434: "רק צל הנרצה ודמותו בו. מחשבה לבד בו"). Only a strong will draws the thing itself in. His test: "I walk around with good thoughts, but is God among us or not?" (L.1436: "הן אמנם אני מהלך במחשבות טובות, אבל היש ד' בקרבנו אם אין"). The sign that God is not within is that "Amalek" (the evil inclination) can get in.
- **Understanding without the picture.** "If he feels nothing, it is a sign he understood only the intellect" (L.1319: "ואם אינו מרגיש מאומה. סימן הוא שרק את השכל הבין"). Human wisdom is "empty depiction". The test for real knowledge is whether love and awe come with it: "when with his wisdom love and awe also awaken, it is a sign that essence and being are in him" (L.1514: "משא\"כ כשיחד עם חכמתו גם אהבה וגם יראה מתעוררת בו סימן הוא שגם עצם וישות בו").
- **Imagination gone astray.** Seeing the soul of things is "not that a person should see himself in empty fantasies" (L.1493: "לא שיראה האדם א\"ע בדמיונות שוא"), like a castle floating in the air. Your own reading of mystical texts is "a sliver of soul mixed with a jumble of desires" (L.1497: "שביב נשמה כשהיא מהולה בערבוביה של חמדות").
- **Forcing.** In quieting, a forceful declaration "can only rouse his ego" (L.1556). Fighting a trait head-on is like telling a crying child not to cry.
- **Foreign thoughts: don't fight them.** "The more he wants to push them away, the stronger they grow in him" (L.1551: "כי כל מה שירצה לדחותם הם יתחזקו בו"). Just keep praying.
- **Over-scrutinising motives.** He mentions (citing *Bnei Machshavah Tovah*) people who examine every stirring to see if it is real or self-deceived (L.1550: "בקונטרס בני מחשבה טובה נזכר מאנשים שחוקרים בדעתם את כל התעוררות והתרגשות"). His advice: "the main thing is not to pay any attention to the motives that come to mind during service, but just serve" (L.1550: "והעיקר הוא שלא לחוש כלל להפניות שבאות במחשבתו בעת עבודתו ורק יעבוד"). Stopping for their sake hands the yetzer a weapon.
- **Expecting constancy.** "Whoever thinks he does not fall from his level, it is a sign he never rose to a state of uplift" (L.1549: "ומי שחושב בדעתו שאינו נופל ממדרגתו, סימן הוא שמעולם לא עלה למצב התרוממות"). On low days serve "like a simple soldier" (L.1549: "ועבודתו תהיה אז כחייל פשוט"), still longing to glimpse the palace through the cracks. Sadness over uneven prayer leads to despair.
- **Passing inspiration that bears no fruit.** Some often feel a spirit from above and stay low, because they didn't dig and connect it to their own power of growth: "the spark that awakened in them went as it came" (L.1413: "וניצוץ הקדושה שהתעורר בהם לעומת שבא כן הלך").
- **Outward display.** Shouting and clapping may come, but the real fervour is inward (L.1343).
- **Craving as misdirected holy pleasure.** "Let a person remember always, in every craving that rises in his heart" (L.1355: "וזאת יזכור האדם תמיד בכל תאוה שעולה ח\"ו על לבו") that it is God's pleasure turned into lust. It is not an alien evil.
- **Mere longing to know.** Wanting to know until you know is ordinary curiosity. "Also after he knows, he longs and wants. This is the sign of true will" (L.1389: "אלא גם אף אחר שיודע משתוקק ורוצה. זהו סימן לרצון אמיתי").

## 6. Compared with Chabad hisbonenus

**Where they agree** - Contemplation must reach the heart. Understanding with no effect on the heart counts as a failure (L.1319 and L.1514; compare the Chabad "daas that reaches the heart"). - Take one truth and stay with it. He uses the word itself: "If you think so and persist in contemplating" (L.1591), and "reflects between himself and himself" (L.1343). The objects are classical Chabad objects: God as the sole existent, "all is the shining of His light" (L.1556, L.1590), and Ein Sof against one's own emptiness (L.1343). - Bittul of the separate self as the goal (L.1447, L.1495, L.1604). Real feeling comes with a change of the person (L.1343, "not the same man"), which is close to the Rashab's test of whether one's dominant trait has changed. - Both warn that the ego spoils experience. Compare the Chabad obstacle "being aware of yourself in the state" with his "forcefulness rouses the anochiyut" (L.1556).

**Where he differs (keep these)** - **He quiets before he thinks.** Hashkatah first *empties* the mind by watching thoughts, then plants one verse. Chabad hisbonenus *fills* the mind, extending one idea in length and breadth. His rationale, that the working mind (yeshut) blocks inspiration (L.1555), has no Chabad equivalent as a method. - **Softness, not intellectual effort.** Formulas are repeated "very lightly" (L.1556). Chabad asks for sustained, effortful binah. - **Picture and imagination.** His central word is *tmunah*, picture (L.1317-1319). Inner experience is a *seeing*, and he tells people to *picture* themselves in Eden (L.1567) and to reach "the picture" behind a teaching. Chabad hisbonenus runs mainly on concept and analogy (mashal) and is wary of imagery. - **Melody** is written into the practice: a set verse sung to a set niggun after quieting (L.1556). - **Will over intellect.** For him the driver is ratzon and teshukah: "the longing is the wings" (L.1589: "שהתשוקה וההשתוקקות הן המה כנפי השחר"), and thought alone holds only a shadow (L.1434). In Chabad, mind produces feeling ("the mind rules the heart"). He says the reverse: human wisdom is "empty", and holy knowledge is "being and life" felt in the whole body (L.1511-1515). - **Prophecy language.** Every Jew has a "part of prophecy" through which the heart *sees* (L.1421, L.1503). Chabad does not frame contemplation this way. - **Positive framing.** Work on the opposite virtue and don't attack the vice (L.1556). Chabad's method includes bitterness (merirut) over one's lowness, though he has his own "why am I an empty vessel" (L.1343). - **Outpouring speech.** Speaking directly to God as "You", facing the wall (L.1486, L.1546, L.1588-1595), is closer to Breslov hitbodedut than to Chabad hisbonenus. - **Suddenness.** His highest state comes "suddenly" (L.1396) and can't be summoned (L.1322). Chabad stresses a path of cause and effect from contemplation to arousal.

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before we begin, just watch your thoughts for a few minutes. Don't push them away. Only notice: what am I thinking now?" (L.1556)
2. "When your head feels a little emptier, say one short verse, *Hashem Elokim Emet*, gently and without force. Then say quietly what you want, such as: I would so like to be close to God." (L.1556)
3. "If a stray thought comes, leave it alone. Fighting it makes it stronger. Go back to your verse." (L.1551)
4. "Ask yourself God's first question: where are you, in yourself? Look at the last day's thoughts. How many were really yours?" (L.1337)
5. "You are not bad at your core. You are a king's child who has picked up some dirt." (L.1422)
6. "If you felt something today and it faded, that is the path and not a failure. Someone who never falls never rose. On the flat days, serve like a simple soldier." (L.1549)
7. "Don't ask if the feeling is 'real'. Ask whether it is still here after you already understand, and whether it leaves love and awe behind." (L.1389, L.1514)
8. "End by singing one verse, *Horeni Hashem darkekha*, slowly, and let it carry what you can't say." (L.1556)

--- Summary: - This range closes the book with the appendix, and it holds the only record of the hashkatah method (a student's memory, L.1553-1556): watch your thoughts, feel the head empty, say one verse softly, make a positive request, and end with a niggun. - Its strongest support for the owner's thesis is Pesach 10 and Shavuot 1: "Ayekah, where are you in yourself?" The known self is "the shadow of the world", and the hidden self, "a part of the One", is found only by being drawn to God (L.1337, 1344, 1358-1361, 1387). - Its inner-experience texts are among his most vivid: a sudden lifting where the world is wiped from view (L.1396), being taken "by the forelock" with eyes shut facing the wall (L.1486), and seeing the soul of things (L.1493). - On counterfeits: thought without will holds only a shadow (L.1434), feeling without love and awe is only depiction (L.1514), don't force, don't fight foreign thoughts, and falls are normal. - Against Chabad: they agree on reaching the heart and bittul. He differs in quieting before thinking, picturing, melody, softness, and in putting will and longing before intellect.
