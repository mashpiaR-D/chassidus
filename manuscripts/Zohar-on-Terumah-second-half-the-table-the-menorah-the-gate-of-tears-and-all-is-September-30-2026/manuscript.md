## 1. What the range is

The second half of Terumah moves through the vessels of the Tabernacle, but each one opens onto the life of an ordinary person:

* the town where the angel of death has no power, and the letter *tet* (lines 2957-2970); * the table: a person's own table as an altar, eating before God, blessing after food, the cup of blessing (lines 2975-3025, 3046-3062, 3259-3277); * a donkey driver named Chanan who teaches on "a time to act for God" (lines 3026-3050); * a Raya Mehemna on the menorah, the ark, and humility (lines 3063-3085); * the letters that built the Tabernacle (lines 3089-3112), a technical passage we summarize and do not teach; * the Shema and the two names made one (lines 3113-3153); * loving God with the evil inclination too, and the parable of the harlot sent by the king (lines 3158-3169); * the faces of the righteous as the face of the Shekhinah (lines 3170-3181); * the ten curtains, the firmament of joy, and the gate of tears (lines 3182-3214); * the story of the boy, son of Rav Safra, who kept silent for two months and then spoke (lines 3215-3292); * livelihood and matches as "hard as splitting the sea" (lines 3298-3320); * the stars and their windows (lines 3321-3350), which we set aside; * midnight study, thanking for good and bad, Moses who did not die (lines 3351-3382); * the middle bar that holds both ends, and the passage that ends: "all is found one" (lines 3383-3415).

## 2. The unity of G-d

**One and unchanged; the many are only from our side.** The strongest unity passage in the range comes at the end. Abraham is kindness, Isaac judgment, Jacob mercy, and each came out of the other: judgment out of mercy, mercy out of judgment, "and so it is above, this from this, each nursing from the other," עד דאשתמודע כלא דהוא חד, ומחד תליין כלהו, וכלא אשתכח חד, "until all is known to be one, and all hang from One, and all is found one" (line 3409). Then: כולי האי לא אתקרי אלא מסטרא דילן, ומסטרא דילן אשתמודע כלא, "all this is called so only from our side, and from our side all is known" (line 3411). Above, all rises on one scale, לא שני, ולא ישתני, "it does not change and will not change" (line 3411), as it says "I, God, have not changed" (Malachi 3:6). And R. Yehudah: all the lights shine from One and are one, and ומאן דפריש לון, כאלו אתפרש מן חיי עלמא, "whoever separates them is as if he separated himself from the life of the world" (line 3411).

**(ours)** This is as close as the Zohar comes to the Chabad and Ramak position that the sefirot are distinct only in relation to us, while He is unchanged. The phrase "from our side" is the key. The Gate of Unity says the same of tzimtzum: the hiding is real for us, not for Him.

**Moses would not cut the unity.** Why was the menorah hard for Moses? He says: ובגין דלא עבידנא קצוץ ופרוד ביחודא דלעילא, דכלא יחודא חד, "because I would not make a cutting and a separation in the unity above, for all is one unity" (line 3071). The Shekhinah is sometimes a crown on the head, sometimes in the middle, sometimes underneath serving; Moses did not want to fix her in one place and so divide her. God answered that the menorah "will be made" by itself, of one piece with Him, without separation.

**Two names, one Name.** "You were shown, to know that the Lord is God" (Deuteronomy 4:35). Here is "the secret of all secrets," יהו"ה אלהים שם מלא, וכלא חד, "the Lord God is a full name, and all is one" (line 3124). When each name is unified in itself, כדין תרין שמהן אלין אתעבידו חד, ואתכלילו דא בדא, "then these two names become one and are included in each other" (line 3137). *HaShem* is the written Torah, *Elokim* the oral; general and particular need each other. **(ours)** The Chabad reading of this verse (HaShem is the light, Elokim the garment that hides it, and they are one) has its root here.

**Hidden inside the righteous.** When R. Elazar meets his friends on the road he says he has seen the face of the Shekhinah, בגין דשכינתא אסתתרת בגווייהו, איהי בסתימו, ואינון באתגליא, "because the Shekhinah hides inside them; she is hidden and they are shown" (line 3170).

**Nothing hangs on the world's order alone.** He feeds everyone, righteous and wicked, "the horns of wild oxen down to the eggs of lice," and ואיהו עביד עמהון לגו משורת הדין, "He deals with them beyond the line of the law" (line 3302).

## 3. States of consciousness

**Tears through the locked gates (proposed ZS2-19).** The firmament of joy: מאן דעייל לקמיה דהאי רקיע, אצטריך למיעל בחדוה, ולא בעציבו כלל, "whoever enters before this firmament must come in joy and not in sadness at all" (line 3202). So what about a person in pain, who cannot make his heart glad? Should he not pray? The answer: כל תרעין ננעלו ואסגירו, ותרעין דדמעין לא אסגירו, "all gates are locked, but the gates of tears are not locked" (line 3205). The guards break the locks and the tears go in. And then the most tender line: כד מלכא עאל לגבי מטרוניתא, אשכח לה בעציבו, כדין כל מה דאיהי בעאת, בידהא אתמסר, "when the King comes to the Queen and finds her sad, then whatever she asks is given into her hand" (line 3206). זכאה חולקיה דההוא בר נש, דאושיד דמעין קמי קודשא בריך הוא, בצלותיה, "happy is the one who sheds tears before the Holy One in his prayer" (line 3206). Inside: you cannot make yourself glad, and you come anyway, and the sadness itself opens the door. Near S49 (Weeping and joy in one heart) and S51 (The sudden cry), but new: it is the permission to come sad, and the promise that sadness brought to Him is not refused. **(ours)** It balances the many Chabad warnings against sadness: sadness kept to yourself closes, tears given to Him open.

**Lowered, and He comes down (fills in S59).** The poor man's offering "goes down" because בגין דמאן דאשפיל גרמיה בגין שכינתיה, דקודשא בריך הוא איהו נחית עליה, "whoever makes himself low for the sake of His Shekhinah, the Holy One comes down upon him" (line 3079). And: מאן דאתדבק בה לתתא, איהי מסלקא ליה לעילא, "whoever cleaves to her below, she lifts him up," while one who tries to climb above her is brought down (line 3075). **(ours)** A useful test for S59 (True lowliness) against false humility: real lowliness does not try to skip the step in front of it.

**What you are bound to shows in your face.** Jacob saw Esau's face as the angel's face, דהא בכל אתר דבר נש אתקשר, הכי אתחזי באנפוי, "for wherever a person is bound, so it shows in his face" (line 3175). A plain sign for S60 (The essential bond).

**He stretches out His hand.** "Everything in the world hangs only on will": כל מלין דעלמא לא תליין אלא ברעותא, רוח אמשיך רוח ואייתי רוח, "spirit draws spirit and brings spirit" (line 3150). When a person wakes love with heart, soul and means, וכדין קודשא בריך הוא אתער ימיניה לגביה, ופשיט ליה לקבליה, ומקבלא ליה, "then the Holy One wakes His right hand toward him, stretches it out to meet him, and receives him" (line 3151). Tie to S41 (The hidden love wakes) and S42 (Love like water).

**Shekhinah on the head.** When a person takes on the yoke of heaven in the Shema, כדין שכינתא אתיא ושריא על רישיה, וקאים עליה כסהיד, "then the Shekhinah comes and rests on his head and stands over him as a witness" (line 3119). Tie to S83 (Accepting the yoke).

## 4. Meditation and practice

**The table as an altar (proposed ZT2-08).** This range gives a full practice for a meal:

1. *Not empty.* Blessing does not rest on an empty place, דהא ברכאן דלעילא לא שריין באתר ריקניא (line 2994), so leave bread on the table when you bless.
2. *Enough is a matter of will.* Even an olive's worth counts as "being satisfied" if you make it your meal: ההוא רעותא דשוי על ההוא מיכלא, שבעא אקרי, "the will one sets on that food is called satisfaction" (line 2989). It says "He satisfies every living thing with *will*," not with eating.
3. *Two things on the table.* זכאה איהו, מאן דאלין תרין קיימין על פתוריה. מלי דאורייתא. וחולקא למסכנין, "happy is the one who has these two on his table: words of Torah and a portion for the poor" (line 2997). A table with no words of Torah is "vomit and filth" (line 2995).
4. *A moment of grief.* One who remembers the ruined Temple at his table and is sad for it in the middle of his joy, קודשא בריך הוא חשיב עליה כאלו בנה ביתיה, "the Holy One counts it as if he built His house" (line 3058).
5. *Joy at the cup.* At the cup of blessing אצטריך למחדי ולאחזאה חדוה ולא עציבו כלל, "one must be glad and show joy, and no sadness at all" (line 3264).
6. *Clearing with care.* R. Abba covered the table when it was cleared: סליקו האי פתורא בצניעו, דלא יהא בכסופא קמי שלוחי מלכא, "take this table away modestly, so it is not shamed before the King's messengers" (line 2999).

**(ours)** A daily practice anyone can do: the meal becomes an offering by one sentence of Torah, one gift, one moment of longing, and a glad blessing. Builds on technique 21 (The act that closes) and 22 (Carrying a line through the day). (The Zohar reads the second cup as permitting a blessing after drinking; the guide does not use that and never recommends drinking.)

**Rising at midnight (proposed ZT2-09).** At midnight "the Holy One enters among the righteous in the Garden of Eden and delights with them." זכאה איהו מאן דאשתדל באורייתא, בההיא זמנא, "happy is the one who toils in Torah at that hour" (line 3368); he takes a portion with the righteous in the garden (line 3370). **(ours)** For someone who wakes in the night: instead of lying in worry, get up, wash hands, and learn a few lines. Near technique 18 (Saying it with the lips).

**Thanking for both.** "I will thank God with all my heart": דהא על כלא אית לאודאה ליה לקודשא בריך הוא, ביצרא טבא וביצרא בישא, "for everything one must thank the Holy One, with the good inclination and the evil inclination" (line 3373), for good that comes and for trouble that comes. Tie to technique 24 (The evening look-back) and S85 (Seeing Him in what happens).

**Two who learn.** Like the boards joined by two tenons, דא אוליף לדא, ודא אוליף לדא, "this one teaches that one, and that one teaches this one" (line 3318); each takes his own and his friend's.

**Keeping silence.** The boy says: I am young, so שוינא ברעותי דלא למללא עד תרין ירחין, "I set my will not to speak for two months" (line 3225). A vow of silence as humility before speaking Torah.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why was I born?** Before a soul comes down, it stands before the King, and He makes it swear דתשתדל באורייתא למנדע ליה, ולמנדע ברזא דמהימנותא, "that you will toil in Torah to know Him, and to know the secret of faith" (line 3133). One who lives here and does not try to know Him, "it would be better for him not to have been created" (line 3134). **(ours)** The purpose of life, in one line: to know Him.

**Does my study matter to the world?** God looked in the Torah and made the world. השתא כל מאן דאסתכל בה באורייתא, ואשתדל בה, כביכול, הוא מקיים כל עלמא, "now whoever looks in the Torah and toils in it, as it were, holds up the whole world" (line 3130). When Israel keep the Torah, קודשא בריך הוא חדי בעובדי ידוי, "the Holy One rejoices in the work of His hands" (line 3027); when they neglect it, ובשעתא דישראל מתבטלי מאורייתא, כביכו"ל, תשש חיליה, "His strength, as it were, grows weak" (line 3028).

**Why is there an evil inclination at all?** The parable of the harlot: a king who loves his only son warns him away from a bad woman. Then, to test his love, he sends a beautiful harlot to tempt him. If the son refuses her, the king brings him into the inner chamber and gives him gifts. Does she deserve praise? ודאי שבחא אית לה מכל סטרין, "surely she deserves praise from every side" (line 3165): she did the king's will, and she brought the son all that good. And so loving God "with all your heart," both hearts, means כד האי יצר הרע אתכפיא ליה, בגין רחימו דקא מרחם ליה לקודשא בריך הוא, "when this evil inclination is bent to him because of the love he loves the Holy One" (line 3161). **(ours)** The pull is not an enemy of God but a servant of His test. This is the Tanya's view in chapter 9 and 27.

**Why is earning a living so hard?** Livelihood and finding a spouse are "hard before Him like splitting the sea" (lines 3300-3308), because He judges the world each day and still must feed the guilty, so He feeds beyond the law. The comfort is David's: יי' רעיא דילי, כהאי רעיא דמדבר עאנא דיליה, "the Lord is my shepherd, like a shepherd who leads his flock" (line 3300).

**What if I meant to do good and could not?** מחשבה טובה הקדוש ברוך הוא מצרפה למעשה, "a good thought the Holy One joins to the deed" (line 3083).

**Do the great ones die?** "R. Shimon said: משה לא מית, Moses did not die" (line 3378). For one in whom the full faith hangs, death does not hang on him; it is called death only from our side. And to Jacob: כי אתי אתה לא כתיב, אלא כי אתך אני, "it does not say 'you are with Me' but 'I am with you'" (line 3380): the Master comes to join His dwelling to his.

**Why do we get sick before we die?** Jacob asked for it, so that a person could lie ill a few days, ויפקד לביתיה, ויתוב מחובוי, "set his house in order and return from his sins" (line 3388). Hezekiah asked that the sick might recover and give thanks (line 3389). **(ours)** Illness has room in it for return and for thanks.

**The righteous who look poor here.** Ink is black outside and white inside; so the sages: אוכמים בהאי עלמא דאיהו לבר, שפירין בההוא עלמא דאתי, דאיהו מלגו, "dark in this world, which is the outside, beautiful in the world to come, which is the inside" (line 3085).

**Where does our knowing stop?** Above the seven firmaments is one without color. Past it, ואית ליה לבר נש למסתם פומיה, "a person must close his mouth" and not look (line 3195). Whoever looks goes back.

## 6. For the guide (ours)

1. **"All this is only from our side ... it does not change" (line 3411)** is the best short Zohar text for the Chabad claim that divine change and hiding are real only from our side.
2. **"The gates of tears are not locked" (line 3205) and the King who finds the Queen sad (line 3206)** give the guide a way to meet a person who cannot be glad: come anyway, and bring the tears to Him.
3. **The table as altar (lines 2989-3264)** is a simple, universal daily practice.
4. **"Moses would not cut the unity" (line 3071)** is a strong image for not boxing God into one place.
5. **The harlot sent by the king (lines 3163-3165)** reframes temptation as the King's test, told with love.
6. **"Spirit draws spirit" and the stretched-out right hand (lines 3150-3151)** show that love from below is met.

Proposed for the map (details in `ZOHAR-PART-2.md`): ZS2-19 *Tears through the locked gates*; techniques ZT2-08 *The table as an altar*, ZT2-09 *Rising at midnight*.

Set aside: the star windows and omens (lines 3321-3350), the letter permutations of the Tabernacle (lines 3089-3112), and passages contrasting Israel and the nations (lines 2976-2978, 3183, 3337). These are not used in a guide meant for everyone (D28). The dream-sign of the letter *tet* (line 2960) is noted only as the Zohar's own image of the letter of "good".
