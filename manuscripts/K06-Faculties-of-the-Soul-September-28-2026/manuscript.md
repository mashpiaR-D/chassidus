## What this cluster shows

The faculties of the soul look like a psychology: ten powers, a mind and a heart, a will, a delight, three garments. Read against the spine, they turn out to be the Chabad instrument for thinking the unity at all. The Alter Rebbe builds the analogy of creation out of them: one word is as nothing before the soul that speaks, as the world before Him (Tanya 20; 33:2). He also says where it breaks: man's speech leaves him and His does not, 'for there is nothing outside Him' (Tanya 21:2); Chochmah, first in man, is 'the end of action' for Him (SH 9:1). The faculties show the unity (G06) twice, once by resembling it and once by failing to.

Each faculty is also defined by what it is turned toward. Binah is contemplating 'filling, surrounding, all as nothing before Him' (Tanya 3:8). Chochmah is where Ein Sof is clothed and where one sees each thing come from nothing (Tanya 18, 43). Da'as is the bond to Ein Sof as firm as sight (Tanya 3, 42). The emotions are love and fear of God. The garments are the thought, speech and action of the mitzvot, higher than the soul itself because 'Torah and God are one' (Tanya 4). In Chabad the faculties do not become religious later. They are defined by the unity from their first definition.

The deepest layer is the soul's essence behind its powers. The Rashab says delight is that essence, present in every Jew as delight in Godliness (Ayin Beis, line 5648), and that will is drawn into every limb 'in its essence' (line 363). The Rebbe says the power of action is rooted there too (Mikeitz 5746), and that human delight reaches 'the bones of the foot' as God's 'desire' reached the lowest world (Beshalach 5731). This is F05 and F07 shown in the person: the highest is found at the lowest.

What the cluster adds to the spine is a set of kept disagreements: where the emotions come from (intellect, will and delight, or the soul's essence); whether the mind always rules the heart; whether katnus is a fall or a mode of devekus; whether imagination counterfeits faith or is its seat (Chabad against Breslov); and what becomes of the left chamber's evil. The tie is thinnest in the technical anatomy (Lurianic mochin, the Mitteler Rebbe's developmental stages, the two chambers as physiology) and in 'mind rules the heart' taken alone, which is a natural law until the texts say what the mind rules with.

## S064 · The powers of the soul · כחות הנפש

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F09 The unity of opposites · F05 The Essence beyond both · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** כחות הנפש · עשר כחות · כחות פנימיים · כחות מקיפים

> The powers of the soul are the unity of God as the soul's own ten powers mirror it: drawn from the ten sefirot, included without division in the soul's essence, and nullified in their source the way all creation is nullified in Him, with the soul itself as the analogy and the place where the analogy fails.

## Derivation

1. The divine soul, a part of God (F06), is made of ten powers, 'corresponding to the ten supernal sefirot from which they descended'. Its intellect and emotions are therefore not a private psychology but a copy of the divine order (Tanya 3:1; IH 25:26: the soul's ChaBaD, emotions and speech are each a 'dugma' of the corresponding sefirot). (*stated*)
2. Because the powers mirror the sefirot, they are the Chassidic analogy for the unity itself (G06). The Alter Rebbe's model for 'all creatures are nullified in existence before Him' is how the letters of speech and thought are nullified in their source, 'the essence of the soul, its ten powers' (Tanya 33:2, pointing back to chs. 20-21). (*stated*)
3. The analogy then carries the unity of the sefirot within Or Ein Sof, before the tzimtzum (the Rashab says 'and all the more so in Or Ein Sof before the tzimtzum'); that this is F02's higher unity is our mapping. In the soul's essence, the Rashab says, all the powers 'dwell together', fire and water, because there they are not yet distinct existences; so too the sefirot within Or Ein Sof are 'mixed and united with one another' (Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 121). The unity of opposites (F09) is first shown in the soul. (*stated*)
4. The analogy also marks its own limit. In man, will, wisdom and kindness are distinct powers of one soul; in God, 'His will and wisdom and His attribute of kindness add no multiplicity' to His Essence (SH 8:1). And what is the first of the soul's powers, wisdom, is for Him 'the end of action' (SH 9:1). So the soul's powers point to the Essence precisely by failing to describe it (F05). (*stated*)
5. The Mitteler Rebbe asks which soul-analogy is truest for emanation: not a flame bound to a coal, but the revealing of what is hidden in the soul's own essence, 'like the power of kindness in the essence' (Shaar HaYichud 19:6-15). The Rebbe makes the whole chain of worlds 'in the likeness of the levels of the soul's powers': included, then revealed, then asleep (Purim 5744). (*stated*)
6. The Rebbe adds the lowest point (G05): the power of action, 'the lowest power', is rooted in the essence of the soul, above the roots of all the other powers; therefore only through action are all the powers raised (Mikeitz 5746). That the ten powers are thus a small 'home below' in which the Essence is found at the bottom is our reading of how F07 applies to the soul. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 3 (ten powers against ten sefirot; two groups, intellect and emotions); Tanya 20-21 and 33:2 (the powers as the analogy of bitul); SH 8-9 (the analogy's failure); IH 25:26 (each power a 'dugma' of a sefirah, so that speaking words of Torah arouses the supernal speech).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 4:7 and 19:6-16: the powers revealed from the hidden essence of the soul as the truest analogy for emanation, better than the flame bound to a coal; the essential trait of kindness in the essence brings forth will and intellect of kindness.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha (Tefillin 3-4, Confession 2): will and delight as powers above intellect that bring the others out of concealment (see S070, S071).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel uses 'kochos hanefesh' 63 times on the clean text (count, not read in context here); see S067 and S074 for the passages read.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 121: powers included in the soul's essence 'dwell together' without the opposition they have when revealed; lines 814-815: the powers are not the essence, since they change between small and great, yet they are united with the soul, unlike the garments.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim (Riga-Otwock) uses the term 60 times (count only); his reading of the garments is under S072.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Purim 5744 (the chain of worlds modelled on the powers: included, revealed, asleep); Mikeitz 5746 (the power of action rooted in the essence of the soul); Ekev 5727 (will surrounds, other powers are enclothed: see S070).
- **Other schools.** The Maggid reads 'I toiled in Torah with my ten fingers' as the ten attributes (sefirot) with which one serves (Maggid Devarav LeYaakov 24:1). Outside Chabad the term 'kochos hanefesh' is rare in what was searched (Sefat Emet: one line; R. Tzadok and Radomsk: a handful, not read). The systematic ten-power anatomy is a Chabad construction.

## Thin or forced?

Not thin. The powers of the soul are the Chabad analogy for the unity, and the texts say so (Tanya 20-21, 33; SH 8-9; Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 121). The analogy is stated together with its failure, which keeps it honest. The Maggid's 'ten fingers' passage links the powers to service, not to the unity; that link is ours.

## S065 · Wisdom, understanding and knowledge in man · חכמה בינה ודעת

**Faces:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F01 The world said into being now **Search terms:** חכמה בינה ודעת · חב"ד · השגה · מוחין

> Wisdom, understanding and knowledge are the unity of God as the mind that contemplates it: Chochmah is the 'koach mah' in which Ein Sof itself is clothed and which sees each thing coming from nothing, Binah unfolds 'filling, surrounding, all as nothing before Him', and knowing a halacha joins the mind to His wisdom in a union unlike any other.

## Derivation

1. The intellect of the divine soul has three powers. Chochmah is 'koach mah', the potential of 'what?'; Binah unfolds a point 'to its length and depth'; they are the 'father and mother' of love and fear (Tanya 3:5-7). (*stated*)
2. Their proper object is named in the definition itself: Binah is to contemplate 'how He fills all worlds and surrounds all worlds and all is as nothing before Him' (Tanya 3:8). The mind is defined by F02 and F04 from its first description. (*stated*)
3. Chochmah is above comprehension, 'and therefore the light of Ein Sof, which no thought can grasp, is clothed in it', in every Jew, and from it the whole soul lives (Tanya 18:7-10). The mind's summit is the place of F06, and the source of the hidden love. (*stated*)
4. The wise man 'who sees what is born' is the one who sees each thing 'coming to be from nothing, by the word of God'; hence higher awe (Tanya 43:3-4). Chochmah as a faculty is the seeing of F01. (*stated*)
5. Chochmah is also bitul. The Maggid: through awe one reckons oneself as nothing, 'and ayin is called Chochmah' (Maggid Devarav LeYaakov 19:1). The Mitteler Rebbe: the deepest delight in comprehension takes one 'entirely out of oneself', the essential bitul of the 'mah' of Chochmah (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39:5). Tanya says the same of Chochmah itself: it is 'batel be-metziut' in the Or Ein Sof clothed in it, the opposite of kelipah's 'thing in itself' (Tanya 19:7, not quoted here). So Chochmah is named as bitul (F08); that the whole mind is thereby bitul is not claimed. (*stated*)
6. When the mind grasps a halacha, it grasps His will and wisdom, and is clothed in it: 'a wondrous union, like no other' (Tanya 5:5-6). This is F10 lived in the intellect. (*stated*)
7. The limit: even Chochmah, 'the beginning of thought', is 'the end of action' for Him (SH 9:1). The mind that knows the unity is itself nothing before Him. That this is why the Chabad intellect must end in bitul and faith, not in a sense of mastery, is our summary of F12's kept disagreement. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 3 (definitions), 5 (the union of knowing halacha), 18-19 (Ein Sof clothed in Chochmah; faith above reason), 43 (Chochmah sees yesh from ayin), SH 8-9 (Chochmah is the first of creatures and the last before Him).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39:3-5: essential delight in the depth of intent in Godliness, reaching the bitul of 'mah' of Chochmah; the whole kuntres grades comprehension by whether it moves the knower out of himself.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillin 3-4: 'mochin' as the light of intellect in the emotions (see S068); Ein Sof is above Chochmah entirely, and 'He is the knower and the knowledge' is true only after Chochmah is emanated (Tefillin 4).
- **Maharash.** Not quoted in this pass for this subject.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, lines 364-365: the mochin are 'in essence bitul' and so produce bitul in the emotions, 'chiefly from Chochmah'. Line 5648: comprehension of Godliness depends on delight in it (see S071).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 280:1: 'the revelation of the mochin, and of da'at in particular, must reach the feet' (see S066, S072).
- **The Rebbe.** Inyanah 17:3: recognition, not only understanding, comes from the yechidah (see S066).
- **Other schools.** The Maggid ties Chochmah to ayin and bitul (19:1), the same move as Tanya 18. Me'or Einayim makes the mind's growth (katnut to gadlut) the drama of service (see S068). Breslov treats the intellect more warily: faith begins where intellect stops (see S074).

## S066 · Da'as: bond and recognition · דעת

**Faces:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F01 The world said into being now · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** דעת · התקשרות הדעת · לשון התקשרות · הכרה

> Da'as is the unity of God as a bond: the mind fastened to the greatness of Ein Sof as firmly as to a thing seen with the eyes, until one sees Ein Sof clothed in heaven and earth, recognizes that the yesh has no standing of its own, and joins with Him even in physical acts.

## Derivation

1. Da'as is 'from the language of "and Adam knew Eve"', a language of bonding: one binds one's mind 'with a strong bond' to the greatness of Ein Sof and does not look away. Without it, wisdom and understanding breed 'false imaginings', not real love and fear (Tanya 3:13-15). (*stated*)
2. The measure of the bond is sight: the thought must be bound to God 'as it is bound to a physical thing that he sees with his eyes' (Tanya 42:4). Da'as turns knowledge of the unity into something like perception. (*stated*)
3. What da'as sees is F01: 'through seeing with the eyes of flesh the heavens and the earth and all their host, in which the light of Ein Sof is clothed to give them life' (Tanya 42:16). This is 'know today and return it to your heart' (F12) made a faculty. (*stated*)
4. Before Chabad the Besht read 'know Him in all your ways' as 'da'ehu, a language of joining', even in physical acts (Tzava'at HaRivash 94). Me'or Einayim: da'as is the root of love and fear, for one who knows 'the whole earth is full of His glory' will not tear any pleasure from its source (Vaera 1). (*stated*)
5. Da'as holds the two Names as one (G14): by da'as, 'know today... that Havayah is Elokim', one accepts judgment as kindness and it becomes kindness (Me'or Einayim, Vaera 2). (*stated*)
6. The Rebbe locates the true recognition in the yechidah: 'to recognize (and not only understand) that the yesh has no standing of its own' (Inyanah 17:3). The Rebbe speaks of hakarah (recognition), not of the faculty da'as; that this recognition is da'as at its root, the soul's F06 knowing F05, is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 3:13-15 (da'as as bond; 'it includes chesed and gevurah'), 42 (da'as from Moshe's soul in every Jew; the bond like physical sight; the analogy of the king's hidden life), SH 1 and 6 ('know today and return it to your heart').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut grades da'as by whether it is 'hearing' the concept or its Godliness (21:2-10); see S074 for the imaginary counterfeit.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sciatic nerve 3: the exodus from Egypt is from 'the poverty of da'as' to 'the broadening of da'as and comprehension in Ein Sof' (see S068).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627 (spine F02): the two knowledges, lower and higher; not re-quoted here.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, lines 5647-5648: contemplation without life and delight stays in the mind; da'as that reaches the heart needs taste (see S071).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 280:1: 'the revelation of the mochin in general and of da'as in particular must reach the feet', so that all one's walking is by Torah.
- **The Rebbe.** Inyanah 17:3: recognition, not only understanding, from the yechidah.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 94: 'da'ehu, language of joining'); Me'or Einayim (da'as in exile in Egypt; da'as makes judgment kindness; the newborn mind grows into da'as at thirteen). Breslov: 'whoever has any da'as and knows a little of the greatness of the Creator' finds the coarse test to be folly, but that greatness cannot be explained in writing or speech, 'to each according to what he measures in his heart' (Likutei Moharan 72:2). The difference: Chabad makes da'as a long labor of contemplation (Tanya 42: 'great and doubled toil'); Breslov says it cannot be conveyed and rests in the heart's own measure. The two also overlap: Tanya 42:4 likewise says da'as is not knowledge 'from the mouths of books', and 42:6 that its measure differs from soul to soul. The difference is one of emphasis (toil against inexpressibility), not a flat contradiction.

## S067 · The emotions of the heart · מדות שבלב

**Faces:** F09 The unity of opposites · F06 The soul as part of the One · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F11 Darkness turned to light **Search terms:** מדות · מדות שבלב · חג"ת · התפעלות המדות

> The emotions of the heart are the unity of God as love and fear that belong to Him: born from contemplating Ein Sof, rooted in a will and delight above reason, sparks of His own attributes in every thing, opposites that only the 'nothing' joins, and in Him no multiplicity at all.

## Derivation

1. The emotions of the divine soul are defined by their object: 'love of God, dread and fear of Him, and glorifying Him' (Tanya 3:3). Contemplation of His greatness gives birth to them: 'his heart will blaze with a mighty love like flames of fire… for the greatness of Ein Sof' (Tanya 3:8-9). (*stated*)
2. Emotions are thus the heart's share in F12, 'return it to your heart'. The Maharash: the good inclination's emotions are born when the mind 'contemplates that there must be bitul to Godliness', through 'Havayah our God, Havayah is One' (Toras Shmuel 5627, 354:2-3). (*stated*)
3. Where do emotions come from? The sources disagree (kept below). Tanya 3: from ChaBaD, their 'mothers'. The Tzemach Tzedek: their root is above intellect, in simple will and delight; intellect is only their passage (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillin 4). The Mitteler Rebbe: the essential trait in the soul's essence itself produces a will and an intellect of kindness (Shaar HaYichud 19:15). The Rashab: emotions reveal the soul's simple rootedness and so are stronger than mind (Ayin Beis, line 2069). The Tzemach Tzedek himself reconciles his view with Tanya 3 ('both are true': the root is above intellect, yet below the mind rules the emotions), and the Rashab says the same (the emotions are not produced by the mind, which only 'shows them the place', Ayin Beis, line 2070). That all three later positions root emotion closer to the soul's essence, and so closer to F05, is our reading. (*stated*)
4. The Maggid places human emotion inside God's own attributes: He created the worlds so that the attributes would be revealed; every love and fear is first included in thought; 'in every single thing are the attributes of God' (Maggid Devarav LeYaakov 25:1-2). A love or fear that falls into foolishness is still 'a limb of the Shechinah' clothed there, to be raised to its attribute (25:2); that this is F11 is our mapping. (*stated*)
5. Love and fear are opposites, like fire and water. What joins them is 'the attribute of ayin' (Maggid 3:1), in God alone (F09, G10). (*stated*)
6. The contrast that keeps the analogy honest: in God, 'kindness and might, mercy and beauty… all is simple unity, His very Essence' (SH 8:1). In man they are distinct powers that can war. That the work of the heart is to make human emotions approach this inclusion (the Rayatz's 'emotions that know each other's worth', see S069) is stated for the sefirot and our reading for the heart. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 3 (emotions born of ChaBaD, all branches of love and fear), 9 (seated in the right chamber, see S075), SH 8 (His attributes are His Essence).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 19:14-16: the essential trait in the soul's essence produces will and intellect of kindness (or of severity in a cruel person). Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 2:17-19 defends felt arousal of mind and heart as 'the essence of the truth and foundation' against those who ban it (line 38, read, not listed).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillin 4: emotions' first root is above intellect, in simple will and delight; intellect only 'passes them through'.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627, 'Mi manah afar Yaakov' 354:2-3: the two inclinations are emotions; the good one is born of contemplating bitul to Godliness, through the Shema; so 'with both inclinations' is possible.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 2069: emotions are stronger than mind because they reveal the soul's simple rootedness; the mind rules them only when they are weakened (see S069).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 363:1: emotions ruled by the mind become 'included', each recognizing the other's worth; tzaddikim 'resemble their Creator' (see S069).
- **The Rebbe.** Not separately located in this pass for this subject.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (25:1-2: every attribute in the world is God's attribute clothed; 3:1: ayin joins love and fear). Pillar 21 (Tikkun HaMiddot) holds the Polish and Galician ethical treatments (Ropshitz, Vitebsk, Ruzhin), which are organized around repairing traits, not around the unity; tying them here would be our reading.

## S068 · Expanded and constricted mind · מוחין דגדלות ודקטנות

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F11 Darkness turned to light · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** מוחין דגדלות · מוחין דקטנות · קטנות · גדלות

> Expanded and constricted mind are the unity of God as a rhythm of contraction for the sake of revelation: gadlus is the mind held open to the greatness of Ein Sof, katnus is faith or the bare thought 'the whole earth is full of His glory', and the smallness is itself a tzimtzum 'from Him and in Him', to be included in the greatness.

## Derivation

1. In Tanya the terms are defined by their content: prayer is 'the time of mochin de-gadlus', when one binds ChaBaD to the greatness of Ein Sof; after prayer, 'with the departure of the mochin de-gadlus of Ein Sof', the evil in the left chamber wakes again (Tanya 12:3-4). Gadlus is the unity held in mind; katnus is its withdrawal. (*stated*)
2. The Tzemach Tzedek maps this onto the exodus: Egypt is 'faith alone, the utmost katnus'; the good broad land is 'the broadening of da'as and comprehension in Ein Sof', which is gadlus (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sciatic nerve 3). In Tefillin 3 the love every soul inherits as 'a part of God above' stands to the love born of contemplation as natural traits stand to traits lit by intellect; the text does not call it katnus, and that mapping is ours. (*stated*)
3. The Besht's circle makes katnus a mode of the unity, not its absence: sometimes one serves only in katnus, not entering upper worlds, 'only thinking that the whole earth is full of His glory and He is near', and still 'with great devekus' (Tzava'at HaRivash 67). The oscillation is 'the secret of the tzimtzum, which is from Him and in Him' (Keter Shem Tov 1:77:4): F03 in the mind. (*stated*)
4. Katnus comes first so that it may be included in gadlus: 'afterwards, when he comes to the great mind, he joins everything, even the smallness, to greatness', as darkness longs to be included in light (Me'or Einayim, Bereshit 6-7). This is F11 in the mind's own history. (*stated*)
5. The Rashab draws the metaphysical conclusion: because the powers change between small and great, they are not the soul's essence; 'every essence does not change'; in the child the great intellect is already present in concealment (Ayin Beis, line 814). That the unchanging essence behind katnus and gadlus is F05 in the soul is our mapping; the Rashab speaks of the soul, not of the Essence. (*stated*)
6. The Mitteler Rebbe gives the developmental anatomy (the intellect in the emotions grows from thirteen to twenty and is called 'mochin de-gadlus'; Pirush HaMilos 2:1). That anatomy, and the Lurianic partzuf mechanics behind it, do not state a tie to the unity; linking them is our reading via F03. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 12:3-4 (prayer as the time of mochin de-gadlus of Ein Sof), 43:2 (lower fear has 'katnus and gadlus').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Pirush HaMilos, 'HaShamayim mesaprim' 1-2: the intellect-in-emotion grows from infancy to twenty; Shabbat is mochin de-gadlus, weekdays mochin de-katnus (lines 30, 51, seen in search).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sciatic nerve 3 (Egypt = faith alone = katnus; the land = da'as in Ein Sof = gadlus); Tefillin 3 (natural inherited love vs contemplative love; mochin de-yenikah and de-gadlus); Amalek 5-6 ('mind rules heart' is mochin de-katnus, outer; gadlus is inner transformation; see S069).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel has 44 clean-text lines with 'mochin de-gadlus' (count only).
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 814: small and great show the powers are not the essence.
- **Rayatz.** Not located for this subject in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located for this subject in this pass.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:16, 1:27, 1:77; Tzava'at HaRivash 67-69: katnus with devekus; a joke lifts one from katnus to gadlus). The Maggid: God 'contracted His brightness like a father who narrows his mind and speaks words of smallness to his little son' (Maggid 1:1): divine katnus as love. Me'or Einayim (Bereshit 5-7): katnus first, then included in gadlus. Pillar 16 adds Koretz, Berditchev, Degel, Apta and Krakow (not re-verified here). Real difference: the Besht's circle honors katnus as a place of devekus in its own right; in Tanya and Derekh Mitzvotekha katnus is the state to be broadened.

## S069 · The mind rules the heart · מוח שליט על הלב

**Faces:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F09 The unity of opposites · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** מוח שליט על הלב · המוח שליט · מוחא ולבא · שליט על הלב

> The mind's rule over the heart is the unity of God as a lever: the natural power to act from what one understands, used to carry 'all is as nothing before Him' from the mind into conduct, which ends in bitul ('not "I love"') and, in the Rayatz's reading, draws down the Essence itself.

## Derivation

1. The principle is a law of nature: 'the mind rules the heart by its birth and the nature of its creation', so every person can, by the will in his mind, restrain the desire of his heart (Tanya 12:5, from Ra'aya Meheimna). As stated there, it is a psychological fact, not yet the unity. (*stated*)
2. It becomes the unity by what rules. The 'great rule for beinonim' is to rule the left chamber 'by the light of God that shines to the divine soul in the mind' (Tanya 16:1), that is, by contemplating Ein Sof. The Tzemach Tzedek names the content: the mind grasps 'that all is as nothing before Him', and the heart must say amen, which is iskafya (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sciatic nerve 3). (*stated*)
3. Mind and heart are 'in essence two opposites': fire of the heart diminishes depth of mind and vice versa. Their inclusion comes through Torah, which draws 'the revelation of Or Ein Sof in Chochmah' from mind to heart (Likkutei Torah, Matot 7:9). F09 in the person. (*stated*)
4. The goal of the rule is bitul: that the heart's love not be 'I love, I exist', but 'with all your soul… to You, God, I lift my soul' (Likkutei Torah, Matot 6:16). F08. (*stated*)
5. The Rashab qualifies the law. The mind's rule is not coercion but an inner light that weakens and refines the heart; and when emotions are strong (they reveal the soul's rootedness) they are not nullified to the mind (Ayin Beis, lines 364 and 2069). The rule holds for weakened emotions. This qualification is kept, not reconciled. (*stated*)
6. The Rayatz gives the purpose: drawing mind into emotion in the Omer count is meant to reveal 'the Essence of Ein Sof that is above the mochin', which reaches 'below below' (Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 364:1). The Alter Rebbe, via Hayom Yom, calls using 'the nature of mind rules heart' the first step of Chassidus. That this is G11 (the idea verified in action) is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 12:5, 16:1, 17:5 (except the truly wicked, 'whose hearts rule them'), 30:5; LT Matot 6:15-16 and 7:9 (mind and heart as opposites; bitul of 'I love'); Hayom Yom 19 Adar II ('the aleph of Chassidus is to use the natural powers, such as mind rules heart').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim has 15 clean-text lines with the phrase (count only); not read in context here.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sciatic nerve 3 (iskafya by grasping 'all is as nothing'); Amalek 3-6: 'mind rules the heart' is the outer mode, like a master compelling a servant; it is mochin de-katnus, while gadlus is inner transformation (lines 175-178, seen in search).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627, 260:2: drawing mochin into emotions, as in honoring parents and Torah study, draws from Or Ein Sof above the mochin; 'his heart in his power' is the tzaddik's likeness to the Creator (line 778, read).
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, lines 16, 364 and 2069: not rule by force but inner light; possible when emotions are weak; strong emotions resist.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 362-364: 'mind rules the heart' in the Omer as the tikkun of the emotions; tzaddikim 'resemble their Creator'; the goal is revealing the Essence above the mochin.
- **The Rebbe.** Compiled Hayom Yom 19 Adar II; not separately located.
- **Other schools.** Almost none: across the whole shelf the phrase appears in 50 Chabad works (566 lines) and one non-Chabad work (4 lines). Other schools treat the same field as da'as and the heart (Breslov: the heart's measure, S066) or as katnus and gadlus (S068).

> — The Rashab: the rule is not dominion but inner light (contrast the will, which rules by dominion).

> — The Rayatz: the purpose of mind ruling heart is the revelation of the Essence of Ein Sof above the mochin.

> — Hayom Yom (the Alter Rebbe via R. Moshe Meisels): the aleph of Chassidus is to use the natural powers, such as mind rules heart.

## Thin or forced?

Partly. The principle itself (Tanya 12:5) is a natural law of the soul and does not state the unity; the tie is stated when the sources say what the mind must rule with (Tanya 16: 'the light of God'; Derekh Mitzvotekha: 'all is as nothing') and what for (LT Matot: bitul; the Rayatz: the Essence). The term is almost exclusively Chabad.

## S070 · Will · רצון

**Faces:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F04 Filling and surrounding · F05 The Essence beyond both · F07 The home below **Search terms:** כח הרצון · רצון שלמעלה מן הטעם · רצונו · רצון פנימי

> Will is the unity of God as the power that is present everywhere without being enclosed anywhere: in the soul it rules every limb as its essence, surrounding them like sovev; in God it is the mitzvah and the halacha itself, the formless will of Ein Sof that every letter clothes and every event carries out.

## Derivation

1. Will is the first mover of the powers: 'the will causes the powers to be revealed from concealment, through the delight clothed in the will' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Confession 2). Above the sefirot, Keter is will, the intermediary between Ein Sof and the emanated. (*stated*)
2. The soul's will differs in kind from its other powers: 'its essence is drawn, not only a glimmer', into every power and limb, so it rules by dominion, not by influence (Ayin Beis, lines 363-364). What is essential is present everywhere; this is the spine's 'the essential is found in everything' (Inyanah 7:3) shown in the soul. The parallel is our reading. (**reading**)
3. The Rebbe draws the parallel explicitly: the other powers are enclothed in limbs as memale is in worlds; the will, 'not an existence of its own, only the inclination of the soul', surrounds all limbs equally as sovev. So too above: Torah (His wisdom) is internalized by the learner, the mitzvot (His will, 'revelation of the Essence') surround (Ekev 5727). F04 and F10. (*stated*)
4. In God, will is not a separate attribute: 'His will and His wisdom… are all simple unity, His Essence' (SH 8:1). And His will is not only behind the Torah but is the halacha: 'the supernal will is itself the matter of the halacha he thinks and speaks' (Tanya 23:8). F10. (*stated*)
5. The Maggid: the 'priority of the will' (Israel arose first in thought) is why God contracted Himself into creation (Maggid 1:1). The will for Israel precedes and grounds the world; that this is the dirah (F07) is our reading. (**reading**)
6. Other schools make the will the center. Breslov: every letter clothes a particular will of God, and all these wills draw from 'the will of Ein Sof, which has no form'; confession returns Malchus to Ein Sof, 'the will of all wills' (Likutei Moharan 4:9). Izhbitz: everything done in this world 'is bound to the will of God, and without His will nothing is done', a truth that can lead to levity and so needs the priestly garments of awe first (Mei HaShiloach, Tetzaveh). Both are unity claims, organized around will rather than Essence. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 4 (He contracted His will and wisdom into the mitzvot), 12:5 (the will in the mind restrains the heart), 23 (the will is the halacha itself), 35-37 (the mitzvah as His will, 'without any hiding of face'); SH 8:1.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 10:33: the powers revealed 'from will to action' (seen in search); 19:15: the essential trait produces will (S067).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Confession 2 (will reveals the powers through the delight within it; Atik and Arich); Ark 3 (will is the outside, delight the inside; see S071).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel: 'will above reason' appears in five clean-text lines (count only).
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, lines 363-364: the will's essence is drawn into every limb; it rules by dominion, unlike the mind.
- **Rayatz.** Not located for this subject in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Ekev 5727 (will as sovev; mitzvot as the will that is the revelation of the Essence); Inyanah 16:1 (spine F10: mitzvot are His will and so their own purpose).
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (priority of will; Maggid 1:1). Breslov (Likutei Moharan 4:9: letters clothe wills; Ein Sof as 'will of all wills'). Izhbitz (Mei HaShiloach: all is bound to God's will; pillar 18 notes Beit Yaakov's extension to one's own failures, not re-verified). Apta (Ohev Yisrael, per pillar 18: the will to serve counts as the deed; not re-verified). The difference (our construction, not a debate in the texts): for Izhbitz, divine will is the explanatory center, including of human acts; for Chabad, will (Keter) is itself only the outer side of delight and below the Essence.

## S071 · Delight · תענוג

**Faces:** F07 The home below · F05 The Essence beyond both · F06 The soul as part of the One · F11 Darkness turned to light **Search terms:** תענוג · תענוג עצמי · תענוג פשוט · עונג

> Delight is the unity of God as His desire for a dwelling below, 'a desire that asks no reason', mirrored in the soul's essence: the delight above every power that nonetheless reaches the bones of the foot, present in every Jew as delight in Godliness, and turned from worldly pleasures to 'love in delights'.

## Derivation

1. Delight is the inner side of will: 'the mitzvot are called the supernal will, and the inwardness of will is delight' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Ark 3). Will is the same in each mitzvah; delight differs as the eye from the ear. (*stated*)
2. In the soul, delight is the essence, not a power: 'intellect and comprehension are powers of the soul, and delight is the essence of the soul'. Therefore delight in Godliness exists in every Jew, 'even simple people', and comprehension without delight is not real comprehension (Ayin Beis, line 5648). F06 as delight. (*stated*)
3. Above, the reason for creation is not a reason but a desire: 'He desired a dwelling below'. The Rebbe quotes the Alter Rebbe, 'on a desire there is no question'; reasons like 'that they know Me' would be satisfied by the upper worlds; only a desire above reason explains the lowest world (Beshalach 5731). F07 and F05. (*stated*)
4. The same shape holds in man: delight, higher than will, is for that very reason drawn into every power and every limb, 'even into the bones of the foot' (Beshalach 5731, citing 'good news fattens the bone'). The highest reaches the lowest (G05). (*stated*)
5. The Maggid's plain version: God delights in His works like a father whose little son rides a stick; for that delight He contracts Himself (Maggid 4:1). Me'or Einayim: He is 'the delight of all delights', so one who knows this will not tear a pleasure from its root (Vaera 1). That every human pleasure is His delight in exile is our summary; Me'or Einayim says only that its root and life is He, and that taking it apart from Him 'separates'. (*stated*)
6. The work is transformation (F11): the divine soul's 'love in delights', delight in grasping God, 'turns to good the waters of the animal soul, from which the desires for worldly pleasures came' (Tanya 9:6). The Mitteler Rebbe's highest contemplative state is 'essential delight to delight in God' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39:3). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 9:6 ('love in delights'; delight is in the mind of Chochmah); Likkutei Torah, Nitzavim 5:19 (the supernal delight clothed in the mitzvot, 'delight above delight', line 3625, seen in search); 'on a desire there is no question' (as quoted by the Rebbe).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39:3-5: essential delight in God in the depth of intent, which removes the self.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Ark 3 (will outside, delight inside; David's 'songs'); Confession 2 (Atik as 'the King's delight in Himself').
- **Maharash.** Not quoted for this subject in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, lines 5647-5648: delight is the essence of the soul; contemplation without delight does not move the heart; 22 clean-text lines on 'essential delight' (count).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim (OCR) carries 'essential delight' 27 times (count only; not quoted).
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Beshalach 5731 ('nisaveh' is above reason; human delight reaches the foot); Erev Rosh Hashanah 5742 (the essential delight in Israel's service, 'a dwelling for His Essence', lines 89-90, read); Simchat Torah 5741 (a father's delight in his son's return is essential delight, line 410, seen in search).
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (4:1; 25:2: He delights in their going up). Me'or Einayim (Vaera 1: He is the delight of delights). Pillar 18 adds R. Tzadok's 'oneg / nega' (the rest that wants nothing against the craving that wants more) and Piaseczno (not re-verified). Difference: Chabad places delight at the soul's essence and the reason for creation; R. Tzadok's account is ethical and physiological, and tying it to the unity is our reading.

## S072 · Thought, speech and action: the garments · מחשבה דבור ומעשה

**Faces:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F07 The home below · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** מחשבה דיבור ומעשה · לבושי הנפש · ג' לבושים · מחדו"מ

> Thought, speech and action are the unity of God as garments: separate from the soul and so able to hide it, but when they are the thought, speech and action of Torah and mitzvot they are His own will and wisdom, higher than the soul they clothe, and the lowest of them, action, reaches the soul's essence and the Essence of God.

## Derivation

1. The divine soul has three garments, 'thought, speech and action of the 613 mitzvot of the Torah' (Tanya 4:1). Its ten powers are clothed in them; the emotions in doing the mitzvot, the intellect in grasping Torah. (*stated*)
2. By themselves the garments conceal. The Rashab: the powers are united with the soul, but thought, speech and action are 'separate garments, not of the soul's kind', letters that in their essence hide (Ayin Beis, line 815). F03 at the level of the person. (*stated*)
3. When the garments are Torah and mitzvot the hiding is undone: 'their virtue is infinitely higher than that of nefesh, ruach and neshamah themselves', because 'the Torah and the Holy One are all one' (Tanya 4:5). He 'contracted His will and wisdom into the 613 mitzvot' so that the soul could grasp and fulfil them (Tanya 4:7-8). F10, and G03: the contraction is for revelation. (*stated*)
4. The inner garments (thought and speech of Torah) are 'included in the light of Ein Sof and united with it in complete union'; the third garment, action in a mitzvah, brings the body and even the animal soul into the same union (Tanya 35:8-10). The Tzemach Tzedek: 'binding the three garments of the soul to His thought, speech and action', and through them the essence of the soul to Ein Sof (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 42). (*stated*)
5. The lowest garment is the highest root (G05). The Rebbe: 'the root of the power of action is in the essence of the soul', above the roots of all the powers; therefore action is the main thing, and the future will say 'great is action' (Mikeitz 5746). This is where F07 is completed: the dwelling is made by deeds (Tanya 37). (*stated*)
6. The Rayatz requires joining the garments: intention (thought, 'the bones') with the physical deed, so that the mitzvah has life (Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 279:2). That this is his definition of Chassidus, joining form and matter, applied to the garments, is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 4 (the three garments of the 613 mitzvot; higher than the soul), 6 (garments of kelipah), 9:7 (all three garments to be God's alone), 12 (the beinoni: only the divine soul's garments act), 23 and 35 (the garments united with the supernal will).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 10:33 (the powers revealed from will to action; seen in search); not quoted here.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 42:1: 'with all your soul' means binding the soul's three garments to His, Torah and mitzvot, so the soul's essence binds to Ein Sof.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel uses 'garments of the soul' 24 times on the clean text (count only).
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, lines 814-815: two kinds of garment; the powers reveal the soul, thought-speech-action conceal it.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 279:2-280:1: thought, speech, action as bones, sinews, flesh; join the intention to the deed; da'as must reach the feet.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Mikeitz 5746: action's root is in the soul's essence; it elevates all the powers, like a lever placed under the lowest beam.
- **Other schools.** The Besht and Maggid speak of the 'world of speech' and 'world of thought' and of joining them (see S073), and of the Creator in 'every movement and word' (Keter Shem Tov 2:12:3, spine). The triad as 'garments of the soul' is a Chabad formulation; the searched non-Chabad works do not use 'levushei hanefesh'.

## S073 · Speech · דבור

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual **Search terms:** כח הדיבור · דיבור · קול ודיבור · דבור

> Speech is the unity of God as the analogy of creation and the place where the analogy fails: one word is nothing before the soul that speaks, as the world is nothing before Him, yet man's speech leaves him and His does not; every human word is hewn from the Shechinah's speech, and speaking is going out of oneself.

## Derivation

1. The world exists by His speech (F01: 'Your word stands in the heavens'). The human faculty that names this is speech: one word 'is as nothing' before the soul's power of speech, which could say endless words, before thought, and before the soul's ten powers (Tanya 20:5). The world, likewise, is nothing before Him (F02). (*stated*)
2. The analogy breaks at one point, which is the unity itself. 'When a man speaks, the breath of speech is felt and seen as a thing in itself, separate from its root'. 'But the Holy One, His speech is not separate from Him, for there is nothing outside Him' (Tanya 21:1-2). His speech and thought remain united with His Essence after creation as before (21:6). G06. (*stated*)
3. The soul's speech is a 'dugma' of the supernal speech, Malchus and Shechinah; so speaking words of Torah 'arouses the supernal speech to unite the Shechinah', which is why reading Shema by thought alone does not suffice (IH 25:26). F10. (*stated*)
4. The Maggid: every word spoken in this world 'is hewn from that speech', the Shechinah; idle words raise it in vain (Maggid 16:3). The Besht: 'all the words people speak are the world of speech, and their thought the world of thought'; speaking good with attached thought joins the two worlds (Keter Shem Tov 2:12:4). (*stated*)
5. Speech is bitul (F08). The Rebbe: speech is not a revelation of the soul but 'the speaker goes out of his existence (nullifies himself) for the other'; so Malchus, the supernal speech, descends to give life to creatures (Vayigash 5732). (*stated*)
6. Breslov: 'all things and all the yesh in the world are from the letters, that is from Malchus', and each letter's form clothes a will of God drawn from the will of Ein Sof; confession of words returns Malchus to Ein Sof (Likutei Moharan 4:5-9). The same unity, with confession as the act. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 20-21 (speech as analogy and its failure), 35 (speech of Torah united with Ein Sof), IH 25 (speech as dugma of Shechinah), SH 1-2 and 11 (the letters of the Ten Utterances; spine F01).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 19:10: letters of thought and speech as the 'truer' analogy of revealing the hidden (with motion from the living being).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 42 (binding speech in Torah to His speech; S072).
- **Maharash.** Not quoted for this subject in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 815: letters of speech conceal in their essence (S072).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 'the garments of thought and speech' as included in their cause (lines 86, 614, seen in search).
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Vayigash 5732: speech as bitul, going out of oneself; Malchus rooted in Keter.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 2:12:3-4; spine F01: 'His word stands'); the Maggid (16:3, 17:1); Breslov (Likutei Moharan 4: speech of confession returns Malchus to Ein Sof; 38: speech as 'the breath of His mouth', Malchus, sea). Pillar 12 holds the rest (not re-verified). Difference in emphasis: in Chabad human speech is chiefly the analogy that fails; in the Besht, Maggid and Breslov it is an active force that joins or damages the upper worlds.

## S074 · Imagination · כח המדמה

**Faces:** F09 The unity of opposites · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** כח המדמה · כח הדמיון · דמיון · המדמה

> Imagination is the unity of God as a dream: the power that joins opposites in one subject, falsely in the waking mind but truly in their root above, where 'what is divided below is joined'; so it both counterfeits the love of God and pictures how the world exists only in Him.

## Derivation

1. Chabad's first word on imagination is a warning. Without da'as, contemplation breeds 'not real fear and love but false imaginings' (Tanya 3:14). The Mitteler Rebbe names the lowest counterfeit of divine arousal 'a false imagining from the imaginative power in the Binah of his soul', which mixes truth and falsehood (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 21:7-9). F12 guards against it. (*stated*)
2. The same power is why God cannot be pictured: 'a person pictures in his mind whatever he wants to understand as they are in him' (SH 8:2); His attributes cannot be so pictured. The imagination's limit marks the limit of all images of God. SH 8:2 speaks of 'picturing' in the intellect, not of the imaginative power by name, so the tie to imagination is ours. (**reading**)
3. Torah Ohr turns this around. A dream 'joins two opposites in one subject', because in sleep only imagination remains (Vayeshev 3:1-2). In exile the soul is asleep, so a person joins prayer's love of God with a day spent in business: two opposites (3:3). (*stated*)
4. But the dream's power 'has its root above': in the world of 'circles' there is no division, 'all things divided below are joined and included together', like 'these and these are the words of the living God'. The imagination's joining of opposites is thus a glimpse of the unity of opposites in its root (F09), revealed precisely in exile's sleep and darkness (F03) (Torah Ohr, Vayeshev 3:6-10). (*stated*)
5. Imagination also serves as an analogy for the higher unity (G06). R. Yitzchak Eizik of Homel: a person imagines a stone, heavy and dead, yet the image is not dead but the living power of his imagination; so the worlds exist in the one thought of Ein Sof, and in sovev they are like an image felt only by the one imagining (Chanah Ariel, Vayechi 2:2; Ki Tisa 4:2). F02 and F04. (*stated*)
6. Breslov holds the opposite valuation: 'the essence of faith depends on the imaginative power', since faith begins where intellect stops; imagination repaired by prophecy yields true faith, unrepaired it yields false beliefs (Likutei Moharan II 8:7). Chabad subordinates imagination to intellect (Torah Ohr 3:2: 'the intellect rules the imagination'). Both agree that faith lives where intellect stops (Tanya 18:11: 'faith is above da'as and comprehension'); they disagree on its seat: Tanya places it in Chochmah, where Or Ein Sof is clothed (18:10), Breslov in the imagination. The disagreement is kept. (*stated*)

- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 21:7-10 (imaginary arousal, the error 'most common among our people'); Shaar HaYichud 41:5, 41:14 (thought as picture without letters, like a child's; 'by the hand of the prophets I am imagined', seen in search); Pirush HaMilos (in sleep only imagination remains, 'also false like dreams', line 329, seen).

- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627 (line 2982, read): in exile only the last power of hearing shines, imagination alone, 'we were like dreamers', which can join two opposites; follows Torah Ohr.
- **Rashab.** Not located on the clean text (zero lines with 'koach hamedameh' in Hemshech Ayin Beis).
- **Rayatz.** Not located in the clean works searched.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat (line 2014, seen in search): exile as sleep, when intellect departs and only imagination remains.
- **Other schools.** Breslov (Likutei Moharan II 8:7: faith depends on the imagination; 25 and 54: the imagination as the animal power, source of desires and false innovation, repaired by joy, music and the hand of the prophets; 54:6: 'by a flaw in da'as the imagination grows strong'). ## S075 · The heart and its two chambers · חלל הימני והשמאלי

**Faces:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F07 The home below · F09 The unity of opposites **Search terms:** חלל השמאלי · חלל הימני · בשני יצריך · לב חכם לימינו

> The heart's two chambers are the unity of God as a battlefield meant to become one: the animal soul's desire in the left, the divine soul's love in the right, and the task 'with all your heart, with both your inclinations', that love from contemplating Ein Sof fill the left chamber too and turn its desire into love of God, until it shows 'in his portion of the world'.

## Derivation

1. The seat of the animal soul 'is in the heart, in the left chamber, which is full of blood'; the divine soul dwells in the brain and spreads to 'the right chamber, which has no blood' (Tanya 9:1-3). Two souls, two chambers. Reading this as F02's 'something' from below, placed in the body, is our mapping. (*stated*)
2. The right chamber's love is not native to the heart; it is 'love of God like flames' in those who contemplate with the da'as in their minds (Tanya 9:4). In prayer the evil in the left chamber is 'bent and nullified to the good spreading in the right chamber from ChaBaD bound to the greatness of Ein Sof' (Tanya 12:3). (*stated*)
3. The aim is not a truce but one heart: love so full that it spreads 'also into the left chamber, to subdue the other side… to change and turn it from the pleasures of this world to love of God', as 'with all your heart: with both your inclinations' (Tanya 9:5). This is F11 inside the heart. (*stated*)
4. The contemplation that achieves this is the unity. The Mitteler Rebbe: raising the blood of the left chamber is done 'by the neshamah in the brain that grasps Godliness in the lower unity' (Sha'arei Orah, Chanukah 10:2). The Tzemach Tzedek: 'with both inclinations, that the evil inclination too return to love of God by the contemplation mentioned', i.e. of the higher and lower unities (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 41-42). The Maharash: the two inclinations are emotions, and both can love because emotions are born of the mind (Toras Shmuel 5627, 354). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe carries it outward: the soul's love 'is drawn also into the left chamber, and spreads into all the powers and limbs, and is seen even in his portion of the world' (Bereshit 5751). That the transformed left chamber is a 'dwelling below' in miniature is our reading of F07. (**reading**)
6. Breslov differs. For one with even a little da'as, the coarse inclination of 'the blood in the left chamber' is folly and no test at all; David had killed it outright ('my heart is hollow within me'); what remains is a higher 'inclination' of gevuros, sweetened by inclusion in Ein Sof, 'where all is good' (Likutei Moharan 72:2). On David the two agree: Tanya also reads 'my heart is hollow within me' as the tzaddik who killed his evil inclination (Tanya 1; 13:8). They differ on everyone else: Tanya holds that in the beinoni the left chamber's evil stays at full strength all his life, and that he should regard it so (Tanya 12:7; 13:5-6), where Breslov makes the coarse inclination no test for anyone with a little da'as. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 9 (the two chambers; 'with both your inclinations'), 12-13 (the beinoni; the evil sleeps in the left chamber during Shema and prayer), 16:1 (rule the left chamber by the light of God), 27 (iskafya of the left chamber even in permitted things).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Sha'arei Orah, Chanukah 10:1-11:1: the two chambers (spirit vs blood; Chochmah vs Binah as sources); the blood raised by contemplating the lower unity; the tamid's blood before the lamps; Shaar HaYichud 30:29, 37:19 (the heart's pulse as ratzo vashov, seen in search).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 42: 'with all your heart' when both chambers are full of love; both inclinations return by contemplation of the two unities; Supplements 1:10 (summary of Tanya 9).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627, 354:1-3: the evil inclination 'must be nullified and secondary' to the good; both can love because emotions come from the mind contemplating Shema; also 6259 ('you have made My heart', seen in search).
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis: five clean-text lines (count only).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim: two clean-text lines (count only).
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Bereshit 5751 (via the Tzemach Tzedek's 'Noach Noach'): love drawn into the left chamber, all the limbs, and one's portion of the world.
- **Other schools.** Breslov (Likutei Moharan 6:2 and 72:2: the blood of the left chamber as the coarse yetzer; David 'killed' it; the higher yetzer sweetened in Ein Sof). The two-chamber anatomy is overwhelmingly Chabad: 48 Chabad works (448 lines) against 11 non-Chabad works (29 lines) on the whole shelf.

Me'or Einayim: katnus comes first in order to be included in gadlus. | S068 |
| Is imagination the counterfeit of faith or its seat? | Chabad (Tanya 3:14; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 21; Torah Ohr Vayeshev 3:2): imagination breeds false love and must be ruled by intellect, though its joining of opposites reflects their true unity above. Breslov (Likutei Moharan II 8:7): the essence of faith depends on the imaginative power, repaired by prophecy. Both place faith where intellect stops; they differ on its seat (Tanya 18: Chochmah; Breslov: imagination). | S074 |
| What becomes of the left chamber's evil? | Tanya 9: it is to be transformed into love ('with both your inclinations'); in the beinoni it stays at full strength all his life and is only kept from acting (Tanya 12-13). Breslov (Likutei Moharan 72): for anyone with a little da'as the coarse inclination is folly and no test; David killed it (Tanya agrees about David: Tanya 1, 13:8); the remaining higher inclination is sweetened by inclusion in Ein Sof. The disagreement is about the ordinary person, not the tzaddik. | S075, S066 |
| Is will or delight the higher, and what is will's place? | The Tzemach Tzedek and the Rebbe: will is the outside, delight the inside; delight is above will. Breslov: Ein Sof is 'the will of all wills'. Izhbitz: God's will is the explanatory center of all that happens, including human acts. That Chabad would place this under the Essence rather than under will is our contrast; no Chabad text read here answers Izhbitz, and Chabad also holds that nothing happens outside His providence (spine F01). | S070, S071 |
| Is the soul a valid analogy for God? | Tanya 21 and SH 8-9: the analogy fails at the decisive point (man's speech is separate from him; His is not; Chochmah is 'the end of action' for Him). The Rebbe: the whole chain of worlds is 'in the likeness' of the soul's powers. The Mitteler Rebbe: some analogies are 'truer' than others (revealing the hidden rather than flame from coal). The tension is between using the analogy and marking its failure; all three do both. | S064, S073 |
| Is da'as a labor of contemplation or the heart's own measure? | Tanya 42: da'as needs 'great and doubled toil' of body and soul, contemplating for a long time. Breslov (Likutei Moharan 72:2): God's greatness cannot be explained in writing or speech; each grasps it 'according to what he measures in his heart'. | S066 |
