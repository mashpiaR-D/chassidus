## The shape of the range

This is the range where the book's central teaching is argued in full. Part 2 opens: "I already wrote at the opening of this book that service is a need on high, not only a need of man" (line 362). Then fourteen chapters prove it, answer the objection that G-d needs nothing, and teach how to pray so that the prayer is wholly for Him.

1. **Love, outer and inner** (343-360). Love for reward is small love, a gate. Real love is service with no aim for oneself. Why G-d loves the one who unites His Name.
2. **Service helps Him** (361-370). "As if helping the unique Name in His work." "As if you made Me." The pillar called Righteous grows strong when there are righteous below.
3. **The need on high is a foundation of faith** (371-380). The Ramban: the Presence among Israel is a need on high. "The main place of the Presence was among the lower ones."
4. **The objection: "What does G-d care?"** (381-399). The answer: Ein Sof is untouched; the need is of His Glory.
5. **Kavanah** (400-428). Service without intention is not service. Thought, will, voice and word make a chariot. The world to come is not the goal.
6. **Not for oneself at all** (429-467). The whole servant asks nothing for himself, not even for his soul. Prayer (tefillah) makes one's needs "tafel" (secondary). The prayers the sages added after the Amidah, wholly for Him. "They leave their own pain and ask about the Queen."
7. **The Amidah, blessing by blessing** (468-529). Praise first is unity; every request is for the need on high; the eighteen rungs.
8. **Against the Rivash** (530-544). A defense of praying with kabbalistic intention.
9. **The daily offering** (545-553). The olah, wholly for Him. "Learn from love, and honor will come in the end."

## The need on high: the full doctrine

**The servant helps G-d.** This is stated without softening:

> ולכן החסיד העובד על הכוונה הראויה כאלו עוזר את השם המיוחד בפעולתו, וכמו שכתוב רוכב שמים בעזרך (Avodat HaKodesh 2:1:2; line 362)
> So the pious man who serves with the fitting intention is as if he helps the unique Name in His work, as it is written: "He rides the heavens with your help."

The Bahir: "whoever learns Torah does kindness to his Maker ... when a man learns Torah for its own sake, then you help Me, and I ride the heavens" (line 363). "Everyone needs help" (הכל צריכים סיוע, line 364). The Zohar on "you shall do them" (ועשיתם אותם): "as it were, you make them ... the Holy One says: as if he made Me" (אמר קב"ה כאלו עשאני, line 365). And "David made a Name": "did David make it? Because he walked in the ways of the Torah ... as it were, he made a Name above" (line 365).

**The pillar that grows strong from below.** "One pillar reaches from the earth to the firmament, and Righteous is its name, after the righteous; when there are righteous in the world it grows strong, and if not it grows weak, and it bears the whole world ... so even if there is only one righteous man in the world, he upholds the world" (line 368). *Ours.* This is the Chassidic tzaddik yesod olam with its root visible: the tzaddik below strengthens the Tzaddik above (the sefirah Yesod), and through him the world is fed.

**Why it matters to believe this.** Ibn Gabbai says openly why he keeps repeating it:

> לפי שענין זה שאנו בביאורו, והוא היות העבודה צורך גבוה יסוד גדול באמונה הקדושה, כי הוא מחזיק הלבבות ומושכן אל העבודה והייחוד (Avodat HaKodesh 2:2:1; line 371)
> For this matter we are explaining, that service is a need on high, is a great foundation of the holy faith, for it strengthens hearts and draws them to service and unity.

*Ours.* This is a psychological claim, and the guide should hear it. A person serves with a different heart when he believes his service matters to G-d, not only to himself. That is the force the Tanya taps when it tells a person that his one deed makes a dwelling for G-d (Tanya ch. 33, 36).

**The Presence belongs below.** The Ramban: "the Presence among Israel is a need on high, not a need of man" (line 372); his students: "if we are not here, I am not here" (שאם אין אנו כאן אין אני כאן, line 373). Rabbeinu Bachya: the exodus was "on condition that I dwell among them" (line 374). Daniel prayed "for the sake of the Lord" (line 376), and then the line that is the Chassidic "home below" in the Midrash's own words:

> ובחזרתה למטה תחזור גם כן למעלה ואז תשלם הכוונה בבריאה, כי כן אמרו ז"ל בבראשית רבה עיקר שכינה בתחתונים היתה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:2:6; line 376)
> And when she returns below she will return above as well, and then the purpose of creation will be complete; for so the sages said in Bereshit Rabbah: the main place of the Presence was among the lower ones.

And from the Bahir: "when she dwells below she dwells above too, and she dwells above only when she dwells below" (ואינה שורה למעלה כי אם כשהיא שורה למטה, line 380).

*Ours.* "The main place of the Presence was among the lower ones" (עיקר שכינה בתחתונים היתה) is the very Midrash with which the Tanya ch. 36 and the Rebbe's first maamar (Bati LeGani 5711) open. Ibn Gabbai sets it at the center of his Part of Service two and a half centuries earlier. Chassidus takes it up whole, and adds one move that ibn Gabbai does not make: in Chassidus the lower place is the goal because the Essence, which is above the sefirot, is revealed there (Samach Vav, F05, F07). For ibn Gabbai the lower place is the goal because it completes the unity of the sefirot, Tiferet with Malchut.

**"What does the Holy One care?" and the answer.** The Midrash says: "What does the Holy One care whether one slaughters an animal and eats, or stabs it and eats? ... the mitzvot were only given to refine (לצרף) the creatures" (line 381). And Job: "if you are righteous, what do you give Him?" It seems to say that our deeds reach only ourselves. Ibn Gabbai's answer is the key to his whole system:

> כי הכוונה להם ע"ה באמרם וכי מה איכפת ליה להקב"ה היא על אדון יחיד שרש השרשים יתברך דליה לא איכפת ליה כלל (Avodat HaKodesh 2:3:6; line 386)
> For their intent in saying "what does the Holy One care" is about the Unique Master, the Root of roots, to whom it indeed makes no difference at all.

> ואמנם לצורך הכבוד להשפיע על הראשים העליונים ליחד ראש המחשבה בסופה, כי כן היה הרצון הטהור והחפץ הקדוש צורך גמור הוא (Avodat HaKodesh 2:3:6; line 386)
> But for the need of the Glory, to flow onto the upper heads and unite the head of the thought with its end, for so was the pure will and holy desire, it is a complete need.

And "to refine" (לצרף) means "to join": "they are the thread that joins those who do them to the unique Name" (כי הם החוט המחבר בין עושיהם ומקיימם ובין השם המיוחד, line 387). The Zohar: "above is only found when it is found below" (line 383).

*Ours.* This is the exact point where Chassidus both takes and goes beyond ibn Gabbai. Ibn Gabbai splits the two truths by level: Ein Sof is untouched; the Glory (the sefirot) needs. Chassidus agrees that nothing touches Him "as He is", but it says the desire for a dwelling below is the Essence's own desire, "a desire that has no reason" (Samach Vav line 208 in that index). So in Chassidus the need reaches higher than the sefirot: the mitzvah touches His inner will, which is the Essence itself (F05, F10). Ibn Gabbai keeps the Essence clear of all need; the Rebbe Rashab says the Essence is exactly what wanted to be needed. Both guard against making G-d lack something.

**Intention is the heart of service.** "Service without intention is not whole and does not rise with favor" (line 400). "When you pray, know before Whom you stand" (דעו לפני מי אתם עומדים, line 402). "A priest who does not know for Whom he sprinkles, for Whom he burns, his service is no service" (line 403). Prayer is higher than offerings, and both unite the powers of the Name; the offering by deed, the prayer by speech (line 403).

## States of consciousness

**Love that depends on nothing.** Love for success or reward is "small love" (אהבה זוטא), good as a gate, "not for its own sake" (lines 343-346). Real love:

> יראה מדבריהם אלה, שהעובד לתכלית מה שיגיע אליו מעבודתו אינו אוהב כי אם את עצמו, לפי שאין בעבודתו חלק לגבוה (Avodat HaKodesh 1:28:6; line 348)
> From their words it is seen that one who serves for what will come to him from his service loves only himself, for there is no share for the High One in his service.

The one who serves from love "will not spare himself; if it comes to giving his body for this, he will give and not hold back, all the more his wealth" (line 348). The Zohar: whoever loves the Holy One is crowned with Chesed and does kindness to all, sparing neither body nor money, as Abraham (line 349). "He need not intend for himself ... it will come of itself" (מאליו יבא וימשך, line 350).

**Why G-d loves the one who unites His Name.** A question the Tanya also asks: how can there be love between G-d and man? Ibn Gabbai gives two ways. The first:

> כי המיחד על דרך זה עושה נחת רוח ליוצרו, והוא אוהב אותו כדרך הנהנים שזה נהנה מזה ואוהבים זה את זה (Avodat HaKodesh 1:28:9; line 351)
> For the one who unifies in this way gives pleasure to his Maker, and He loves him, as those who give each other pleasure, this one delights in that one and they love each other.

The second is the love of a father for a son, "for he is a part of him", which grows into "desire" (חשק), "the edge and end of love" (line 352). *Ours.* This is a state: **loved by the One I give joy to**. The Tanya's "nachat ruach" (pleasure above) is its Chassidic name, and it is one of the most healing ideas the guide can offer a person who thinks G-d only tolerates him: the one who serves gives G-d delight, and is loved for it (S44, S60).

**The servant who leaves his own pain to ask about the Queen.** This is one of the most moving passages in the book. R. Yochanan's prayer "look at our shame and see our trouble" is read as a plea for the Presence, who is in exile with us and "as it were, shame and trouble reach her from her children's shame" (line 459). The Zohar on Ruth: the Presence says "I am full of pain for my children in exile" (line 459). "In all their trouble He was troubled." And the Zohar on Isaiah: G-d gathers His household and says:

> כדין קב"ה כניש לפמליא דיליה ואמר חמו בני רחמאי דאינון דחקין בגלותא ושבקין צערא דילהון ותבעין לי על מטרוניתא (Avodat HaKodesh 2:8:3; line 461)
> Then the Holy One gathers His household and says: see My beloved children, who are pressed in exile, and leave their own pain, and ask Me about the Queen.

> ודרכם של חסידים ואנשי מעשה להניח צרכיהם וצערם ולבקש ולהתפלל על צרכי גבוה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:8:2; line 460)
> And the way of the pious and the men of deeds is to leave their own needs and their pain, and to ask and pray for the needs on high.

*Ours.* A state the guide should name: **your pain set aside for His**. From inside: in the middle of one's own trouble, a turning outward, to the pain of the Presence in exile, to what G-d lacks in this world. It is not denial of one's own pain; it is a larger heart. It is close to S54 (compassion for the soul, reversed: compassion for the Shechinah) and to the Chassidic prayer "for the Shechinah's sake" (Tikkun Chatzot). It heals self-absorption in suffering. (JSON.)

**Seeing in the healing: G-d feels the pain.** "When a man is in pain, what does the Presence say? My head is heavy, my arm is heavy" (line 487). "Like twins: if one has a headache, the other feels it; so the Holy One says: I am with him in trouble" (line 487). *Ours.* This is being held (S63, S84) in its most tender form: the sufferer is not alone even in the body.

**The righteous doubt, and are shown.** Those who live this way "doubt themselves, whether in all their toil they have done their duty ... 'I said, I have toiled in vain'"; when they pass from this life they are shown that their work bore fruit (line 455). R. Abahu at his death was shown thirteen rivers of balsam and said "these are Abahu's?" (line 456). *Ours.* A comforting honesty: the one who works for the need on high usually does not see its fruit. The not-seeing is part of the path.

## Meditation and practice

**Prayer makes one's needs secondary (tafel).** Ibn Gabbai reads "tefillah" (prayer) from "tafel" (bland, secondary):

> וזה סוד תפלה מלשון היאכל תפל מבלי מלח, וענינה שעושה צרכיו תפל אצל צרכי גבוה, והשואל ומכוין גם לעצמו הנה הוא משתף עצמו לגבוה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:6:8; line 436)
> This is the secret of "tefillah", from "can the tasteless (tafel) be eaten without salt": that he makes his own needs secondary to the needs on high; and one who asks and intends also for himself makes himself a partner with the High One.

> צריך שלא תהיה לו שום כוונה בייחוד ההוא לדבר מצרכי עצמו כלל, לא מצרכי הגוף אף לא מצרכי הנפש עם שכל זה ימשך מכוונת הייחוד (Avodat HaKodesh 2:6:5; line 433)
> He must have no intention at all in that unity for any of his own needs, not the needs of the body, not even the needs of the soul, though all of this will flow from the intention of unity.

Rabbi at his death raised his ten fingers: "I toiled with my ten fingers in Torah and did not benefit even with my little finger" (line 438). *Ours.* This is the Chassidic "prayer not for oneself" in its strongest pre-Chassidic form. The guide should hold it with its guard: ibn Gabbai does not forbid asking for needs; he says ask for them as needs on high (see below).

**Asking for bread as a service to Him.** In the blessing of the years: the one who prays needs food and health to serve, "yet his intention should be for the need of the Glory ... he asks for his needs not for his own pleasure except by the way" (line 490). The Zohar: whoever prays each day for his food "causes a blessing each day through the One in whom all is"; so even one who has food should ask daily, and "is called a son of faith" (ברא מהימנא, line 491). And on the blessing of salvation: "we leave our needs and ask for the needs on high ... for His salvation depends on ours" (ישועתו תלויה בישועתינו, line 502). "Redeem us ... for Your Name's sake": "we pray that He redeem us so that He redeem Himself" (line 484). *Ours.* Technique proposed: **asking for His sake**. Say each request, and then turn it: health so that I can serve, bread so that a blessing is found above, rescue so that the Name is not shamed. It keeps asking honest and still selfless.

**Praise before request is unity, and unity makes the vessel.** "One who comes to ask for his needs must first of all unify His Name ... for this is the right way to receive his needs" (line 470). Why do Israel pray and are not answered? "Because they do not know how to pray with the Explicit Name", that is, with the inner meaning of its letters (line 470). The first three blessings are the unity. Then the core line:

> ואז תהיה כוונתו בייחוד ההוא לכלול הכל באין סוף ... והייחוד השלם להביא ולכלול הנשמה הקדושה ממדרגה למדרגה עד סוף כל המדרגות כדי שתהיה נמצאת בכלם, מתפשטת בכלם, כוללת את כלם, לדעת ולהורות שהוא הכל והוא לבדו ואין זולתו (Avodat HaKodesh 2:9:4; line 471)
> Then his intention in that unity will be to include all in Ein Sof ... and the whole unity is to bring the holy Soul from level to level to the end of all levels, so that it be found in all, spread through all, including all, to know and to show that He is all, and He alone, and there is none besides Him.

*Ours.* This is a full unity meditation for the opening of prayer, and it ends in "He is all, and He alone, and there is none besides Him": the Tanya's "there is nothing besides Him" (ain od milvado) in ibn Gabbai's words, three centuries before the Gate of Unity. Proposed technique: **the Soul through all the levels** (JSON): at the first blessings, picture the one Life flowing down through every level of being, found in all, spread through all, holding all, until the thought rests on "He is all, He alone". It joins technique 6 (walking the truth down to one thing) and S62 (only Him).

**Thought, will, voice and word.** The Zohar on perfect prayer: "prayer is made of thought, will of the heart, voice and word of the lips ... these four tie knots and make a chariot for the Presence to rest on" (line 409). Ibn Gabbai: as above the pure Thought brought forth Will, Will brought Voice, Voice brought Speech, so below the prayer must be whole in the same four, "and then it has power to awaken the things it resembles" (line 413). *Ours.* A clear sequence for the guide (technique 18, saying it with the lips): begin in thought, let it become wanting, then sound, then words.

**The early pious waited an hour.** R. Ezra: "the word 'service' (avodah) means to conquer the thought that is busy with the world's affairs and bring it into bondage to the intention; so the early pious would wait one hour and then pray" (line 415). "The main service of the wise and those who think of His Name ... is to bring the thought to agree with the faith, as if it clung above" (line 415).

**The fire kept burning in the heart.** When the Temple order was destroyed, "the Great Name remained for Israel; and the righteous and the pious and men in solitude unify the Great Name and keep the fire burning on the hearth in their hearts" (ומאחזין את האש במדורת המוקד בלבותם, line 431). "And whoever unifies the branches with the root ... clings to the Great Name" (line 431). *Ours.* This is hisbodedus (solitude) as the heart's altar: short, set times alone to unify the Name. The guide can offer it (technique 20).

**Pray for peace in the study hall.** A sage prayed "that You put peace in the household above and the household below, and among the students who study Your Torah, those who study for its sake and those who do not; and that those who do not, come to study for its sake" (lines 463-467). When students argue only to bring truth to light, "peace is awakened above too" (line 466).

**A prayer after the prayer, wholly for Him.** The sages added prayers after the Amidah "wholly for the need on high ... for such a prayer is the most chosen" (line 449). Proposed technique: **the prayer after the prayer**. After the fixed prayer, a few words asking nothing for oneself: that His Name be one, His Presence at rest, His children at peace.

**G-d asks to be blessed.** R. Yishmael saw G-d on the throne and heard "Yishmael My son, bless Me", and blessed: "May it be Your will that Your mercy conquer Your anger" (line 446). "The Holy One asks the righteous to bless Him with this intention, for in this He receives pleasure from them" (line 446).

**Against praying to a sefirah.** The Rivash quoted his teacher: "I pray with the mind of a child" and asked "who brings us into all this? Better to pray plainly to G-d with intention" (line 533). Ibn Gabbai answers sharply: prayer is always to the Cause of causes, but directing the flow to the fitting trait is "the way" the blessing comes (lines 535-539). "Cast your way on the Lord" means "prepare a way for the light to come down" (line 539). *Ours.* The guide sides with neither in full: its technique is plain presence before Him (technique 2), with the kabbalistic map as a picture, not a requirement. But ibn Gabbai's point that intention needs a picture of how the blessing flows is the same as the Kuzari's (4:5, line 283): the mind cannot carry a great thing without a form.

**The offering wholly for Him.** The daily burnt offering was burned whole, "wholly for the High One ... not for sin but for favor, to fill the higher will" (line 545). Our prayers stand in its place: "It is not fitting that one's own table be full when he asks his needs with intention, and the table on high empty, without intention" (line 550). *Ours.* A clear, simple picture for daily prayer: set G-d's table too.

**Honor comes by itself.** "Learn from love, and honor will come in the end" (line 552). "The one who serves this way need not intend for himself; in the end it is the nature of this service to rest a blessing on his house" (מטבע העבודה על הדרך ההוא להניח ברכה אל ביתו, line 553).

## The big human questions

**Why do we exist?** "The whole purpose of life is to thank and praise the Great Name without stop" (line 513); "so the whole one longs for long life, to unify without stop" (line 517).

**What is the world to come?** Not the last goal. It comes "by the way" (במקרה) from the true goal, "the greatness of the Great Name and its unity in its glory to Ein Sof" (lines 423-426). One may ask for it, because "in the soul of the righteous the Glory is completed" (line 424).

**Does G-d feel our pain?** Yes: "I am with him in trouble" (line 487); His salvation is bound to ours (line 486).

**Does my service matter?** It helps Him ride the heavens (line 362); it strengthens the pillar that bears the world (line 368); it makes His Name, "as if you made Me" (line 365).

**Love and awe.** Love for oneself is small; love for Him "does not spare the body" (line 348). And He loves in return, as those who delight each other (line 351).
