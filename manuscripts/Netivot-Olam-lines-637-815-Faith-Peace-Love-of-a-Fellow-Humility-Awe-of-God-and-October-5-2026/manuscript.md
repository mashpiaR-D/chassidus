This range holds six paths. Together they give the Maharal's clearest teaching on the inner stance of a person before G-d. Two ideas carry it.

The first is **simplicity** (peshitut). G-d is simple: not bounded, not made of parts, not defined by anything. Humility is a person's share in that simplicity. So humility is not only good manners. It is likeness to G-d.

The second is **cause and effect** (ilah and alul). G-d is the Cause of all. Awe is simply knowing that you are the effect: that you stand on Him and have nothing of your own. So awe is not an acquisition. It is seeing what is true.

## 2.1 Peace is His name because He unites opposites

Why is G-d's name "Peace"? Because He is the final form of the world, and so He "כולל הכל ומקשר ומאחד הכל וזה הוא השלום בעצמו" (648, Shalom 1:2): "includes all and binds and unites all, and this is peace itself." Fire and water, Michael and Gabriel, should cancel each other. They do not, "כי העולם הוא אחד ואיך יתחברו כאשר הם הפכים" (652): "for the world is one, and how could they join when they are opposites?" They join through Him. Peace is not a quality He has; it is His very self, which is why it is His name.

## 2.2 Humility is His own trait

The deepest unity teaching in the range is in the Path of Humility. "Wherever you find His greatness, you find His humility" (Megillah 31a). The Maharal reads this as: "כי הענוה היא עצם הגדולה והיא הגדולה על כל הגדולות" (693): "for humility is the essence of greatness, the greatness above all greatness."

Why? "High" is always high *above* something; it is a relation. But He is "שהוא פשוט והפשוט הגמור לא מוגבל" (693): "simple, and the fully simple is not bounded." What is simple has no edge. So He judges the orphan as He judges the great. That is His humility, and it is higher than His height.

A humble person shares this: "ולפיכך בעל ענוה הוא מתלבש במדת בוראו" (692): "and so the humble one clothes himself in his Creator's own trait." Awe only knows He is the Cause. Humility is *like* Him. So humility is higher than awe.

## 2.3 All is His and there is nothing besides Him

"כי מי שרוחו נשברה כאלו הוא מבטל עצמו מן המציאות לגמרי" (695): "for one whose spirit is broken is as if he cancels himself from existence entirely." That is equal to all the offerings, "שאין ענין הקרבנות רק להודיע כי הוא יתב' הכל ואפס זולתו" (695): "for the point of the offerings is only to make known that He is all and there is nothing besides Him." And since man is king of all creatures, when man counts himself as nothing, all creatures are nothing before Him.

The same point from the side of awe: "כי אין לנמצאים שום מציאות עמו ית'" (744): "for beings have no existence at all alongside Him." So using His Name in vain brings loss: one drags the One down among things that cannot stand with Him.

## 2.4 Pride denies the Cause

Pride is "אין אני והוא יכולין לדור בעולם" (705): "I and he cannot dwell together in the world." Why? He is simple and unbounded; pride is a bounded, heavy thing that fences itself off. Two opposites cannot share one place. And "ואם אין עלול אין עלה" (710): "and if there is no effect, there is no Cause." The proud man does not count himself an effect; so in his world there is no Cause. That is why the sages call pride "as if denying the Root".

## 2.5 The Shema is the essence of cleaving

Among the "great rules" of the Torah, one sage says the Shema is the greatest: "ובמצות שמע ישראל הוא עצם הדבוק הזה" (672): "and in the mitzvah of Shema Yisrael is the very essence of this cleaving." All other mitzvot prepare for it.

**Proposed MH-S11 "The simple one".** Inside: I am not defined by my standing. I do not measure myself against others. Like water I go to the low place and there is room there. How to enter: notice where you are bounding yourself ("I am the one who..."). Let the edge go and stay plain. Source: Netivot Olam 692-693, 699 ("לפשוט אין מתנגדים", "the simple has no opponents"), and Netiv HaTorah 46.

## 4.1 Make yourself a remnant (new: `mh_remnant`)

G-d forgives "למי שמשים עצמו כשיריים" (700): "for one who makes himself as a remnant": one who does not make himself the main thing in the world. How: in a moment of friction, say inside: I am not the center here. Let yourself be the leftover. Source: 700; also 1053-1055 in the Path of Return.

## 4.2 Hold back in the quarrel (`hold`)

The world stands on the one "שמשים עצמו כמי שאינו" (665): "who makes himself as one who is not", and on the one who holds himself in (belimah) when there is strife. Like the earth that stays in the middle and needs nothing under it.

## 4.3 Awe as recognition (`before_whom`, `discern`)

"כי עצם היראה מה שהוא עלול אל השם יתברך שהוא העלה" (727): "for the essence of awe is being the effect of G-d, who is the Cause." And "ולפיכך הכרת העלה היא יראה" (731): "and so recognizing the Cause is awe." The practice is not to make yourself afraid. It is to see: this breath, this body, this moment are His effect. Awe follows the seeing.

"כי היראה מה שמחשיב עצמו לאין וללא כלום לפני העלה ית'" (729): "for awe is counting oneself as nothing and naught before the Cause." This is why "all is in heaven's hands except awe of heaven": G-d gives being; He does not give nothingness. Counting yourself as nothing is the one thing you do from your side.

## 4.4 Guard the tongue as your life (`lips`)

A person was made "לרוח ממללא" (764): "a speaking spirit". Speech is the elixir of life because it sits on a person like a salve, closer than the intellect and freer than the body. The Maharal's practice for one who has spoken ill: learn Torah, or, if one cannot, lower oneself (779).

## 4.5 Be the middle that makes peace (new: `mh_middle_tongue`)

The tongue sits between two ears, two eyes, two nostrils. It "מכריע בין אביו שבשמים" (775): "decides between his Father in heaven" and people, and between friends. It can bring the far near or split the near apart. How: before speaking of others, ask: will this join or divide? Source: 774-775.

## 5. The big human questions

**Faith and what we receive.** When G-d shows His power in the future, "לא יהיה נודע לו רק כפי האמונה שהיה מאמין" (646): "it will be known to him only by the measure of the faith he had." Faith is not a small thing; it is the vessel.

**Joy.** "וליועצי שלום שמחה" (647): "and for those who counsel peace there is joy." Joy is the feeling of completeness, and peace is completeness. Mourning is over loss; joy is over wholeness.

**Love of people and love of G-d.** "כי מי שאוהב את אחד אוהב כל מעשה ידיו" (669): "for one who loves someone loves all his handiwork." One who loves G-d cannot hate His creatures. And "הרי בצד הזה חבירו והוא עצמו דבר אחד" (671): "in this respect his fellow and he himself are one thing", since both bear one image of G-d. This line is universal: it rests on the image in every person (D28).

**Shame.** Shaming someone puts out the light of his face, the image of G-d, which is the light of the world to come (673). It is like bloodshed. One insulted who does not insult back is like the sun going out in its strength (676).

**Awe and purpose.** "לכך יראת שמים היא הבית" (747): "so awe of heaven is the house." Learning is the gate; awe is the home. And the world was made so that there be awe, since for there to be a world at all there must be an effect that knows its Cause (731).

**Awe is not something to own.** "אבל היראה אינו דבר קנין של מה" (730): "but awe is not an acquisition of anything." Wisdom and strength are gains. Awe is only knowing you are nothing before Him. That is why it is hard and also why, once there, it feels like nothing special. For Moshe it was "a small thing", like bread: you do not boast of having bread; you only notice when it is missing.

**Awe that cancels.** "שהירא מן אחר מבטל עצמו לגמרי נגד אותו מי שהוא ירא ממנו" (755): "for one who stands in awe of another cancels himself wholly before the one he is in awe of." Two lovers each keep their own being; one who stands in awe does not. So one who stands in awe of the wise becomes wise.

**Speech and pride.** The slanderer says "אשר אמרו ללשוננו נגביר שפתנו אתנו מי אדון לנו" (789): "we will be mighty with our tongue; our lips are with us; who is master over us." Making one's own speech master is denying the Master.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **Awe as being the effect** is close to the Tanya's "lower awe" (yirah tata'ah) that comes from knowing G-d fills and sustains all (Tanya ch. 41-43). The Maharal uses cause and effect, not the constant word of creation. The conclusion is the same: nothing stands on its own.
- **Humility as likeness to G-d** has a match in Chabad: the Alter Rebbe's teaching that true bittul is the vessel for the infinite. The Maharal adds a reason Chabad does not state: G-d's own humility is His simplicity, His not being bounded.
- **Rambam's negative attributes**: the Maharal's "simple, not bounded" (693) is a negative description, in line with the Rambam. But he goes further than the Rambam would: humility is G-d's *own* trait, "a trait of His self".
- **Shema as the essence of cleaving** (672) agrees with Shaar HaYichud's opening: the Shema is where unity is taken in.
- **Tzimtzum** is absent. For the Maharal the world is "nothing besides Him" because an effect has no standing before its Cause (695, 744), not because of a contraction.
