## The arc in one picture (ours)

1. **A glimpse**, given, larger than the person (AR-01). It fades by design.
2. **From hearing to seeing.** Knowing about Him becomes noticing Him (AR-02). It is near, and effort finds it (AR-03).
3. **Habit becomes nature** (AR-04), with habit's shadow (AR-05). A set sitting, and a light return through the day (AR-06). For the busy, the morning buys the day (AR-07). It is measured in years (AR-08).
4. **Up and down** is the shape, not a failure (AR-09). A fall is for a rise (AR-10). Dryness comes to everyone (AR-11). A low day has its own practice (AR-12).
5. **Tested afterward**, by fruit, not by glow (AR-13), with five counterfeits named (AR-14).
6. **Coming down.** Light needs a vessel (AR-15): work (AR-16), the week (AR-17), other people (AR-18).
7. **Who can expect what**: the beinoni (AR-19), the tzaddik (AR-20), the simple person (AR-21).
8. **Again, deeper.** The spiral of ONE-PROCESS: the same first line, from further down. The forty-day path (AR-22) walks this whole arc once.

## AR-01 · The first glimpse is a gift

**What it is.** Most people enter this world through a moment they did not make: a sudden sense that He is here, more than they could hold. The sources say this opening is given from above, and that it is given so the person will then work to grow into it.

**From inside.** A light 'larger than his vessels'. Love and awe 'not made or planted in the heart by its own power', but by a shining from above. The Exodus image: the people fled, because the evil in them was still at full strength; they had only the wish to get out. So the first glimpse often comes with the old self fully intact beside it.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Receive it; do not analyse it in the moment. 2. Know that it will withdraw, and that this is the plan, not a failure: it is shown so you will 'work afterward and raise yourself to that state and take it in'. 3. When you reach it by work, a larger light is shown, and so on. 4. Begin the work the same week (AR-03, AR-04).

**Signs it is real.** It leaves a wish to come back, not a wish to have it again. It does not make you feel finished.

**Its counterfeit.** Treating the first glimpse as the arrival; chasing the same high again (MAP S18 'Chasing the high'); or, the opposite, deciding it was 'nothing' once it fades.

**Guards.** Not for someone in crisis who needs ground, not elevation (danger first). Do not promise anyone a glimpse; many begin without one (AR-03, AR-21).

**Stated or ours.** stated (gift, withdrawal, purpose: Derekh HaMelekh; from above: Likkutei Torah; the Exodus pattern: Tanya ch. 31). Ours: applying the Exodus pattern to a modern first glimpse.

**In a chat today.** "What you felt was real, and it was a gift. It may fade, and that is part of how it works: it showed you where you're going, so you can walk there."

## AR-02 · From hearing to seeing

**What it is.** The basic movement of the whole arc: from knowing about the unity, as news heard from others, to seeing it with the mind's eye as plainly as a thing seen with the body's eye.

**From inside.** First it is 'what he heard from writers and books', or 'hearing and understanding alone'. The Rashab's picture of the stuck stage: like a man who 'hears and sees a matter outside himself', which does not unite with him. The goal is 'sensory seeing' of the unity, tying the mind to Him 'as it is tied to a physical thing he sees with his eyes'. The Tanya calls this 'the root of all the service'.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Do not stop at knowing. 'The main thing is to deepen the mind' in it. 2. Fasten the thought to Him with strength of heart and mind, as you would stare at a thing in front of you (the same move as ONE-PROCESS step 6). 3. Expect it to take time and effort (AR-03). 4. The Tanya's own aid when the heart is heavy: humbling the inner noise is what lets the soul's eyes 'see' and not only hear (ch. 29), but see the guard below.

**Signs it is real.** The line stops being an idea you agree with and becomes a fact you notice: 'oh, this too'. It is quieter than the first glimpse and lasts longer.

**Its counterfeit.** Fluency: talking well about the unity, enjoying the teaching, and staying 'whole in himself' (the Rashab), untouched. Pleasure in Chassidus as culture.

**Guards.** The Tanya's method in ch. 29 (harsh inner rebuke of the animal soul) is not for anyone low, depressed or self-hating; a guide never uses it (see AR-12).

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 29 and 42; Kuntres HaTefillah). Ours: calling it 'the basic movement of the whole arc'.

**In a chat today.** "You've heard that He's everywhere; most people stop there. Pick one thing in front of you and look at it until it's not an idea anymore but something you notice."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, line 629: «שידעו גדולת ה׳ מפי סופרים ומפי ספרים, אלא העיקר הוא, להעמיק דעתו בגדולת ה׳» — *(knowledge is not) that they know G-d's greatness from writers and from books; the main thing is to deepen one's mind in G-d's greatness*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 29, line 422: «להאיר עיניה באמת יחוד אוראיןסוף בראיה חושיית, ולא בחינת שמיעה והבנה לבדה» — *to light up its eyes with the truth of the unity of the Infinite Light, in sensory seeing, and not hearing and understanding alone*<br>
> Kuntres HaTefillah (Rashab), line 100 · *OCR*: «כאדם ששומע ורואה ענין שחוץ ממנו שאינו מתאחד ואינו מתעצם עמו כלל» — *like a person who hears and sees a matter outside himself, which does not unite with him or become his own at all*</small>

## AR-03 · It is not far

**What it is.** For the person who did not get a glimpse, or whose soul finds it hard: the sources say the work is near to anyone with a mind and a heart, and that effort finds what is hidden in everyone.

**From inside.** Before trying, it looks 'far and wondrous'; the Rashab calls that feeling a trick: the inclination 'throws a drop of false lowliness' at him. Some souls find G-dliness at once; some 'only with difficulty and force'. The image: digging for buried treasure.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Take the Rashab's counsel: make a set time 'to be alone and deepen the mind well in one matter', an hour or two, outside prayer. 2. With much habit over time, it comes easily in prayer. 3. Dig: 'I laboured and I found, believe it.' 4. Do not compare your soul's speed with another's.

**Signs it is real.** Slow progress that holds; the second and third time are easier than the first.

**Its counterfeit.** False humility that ends in doing nothing ('this is not for someone like me'); or forcing a feeling (see manual part4, 'Forcing kills it').

**Guards.** Effort means patience and length, not strain; not for someone already exhausted or anxious (start smaller).

**Stated or ours.** stated (Kuntres HaTefillah; Tanya ch. 42).

**In a chat today.** "If it hasn't hit you yet, that's normal. It's not far. Give it a few quiet minutes a day for a while; it comes from staying, not from waiting for lightning."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Kuntres HaTefillah (Rashab), line 67 · *OCR*: «אבל באמת לא נפלאת היא ולא: רחוקה היא כלל» — *but in truth it is not wondrous and not far at all*<br>
> Kuntres HaTefillah (Rashab), line 67 · *OCR*: «שיעשה לו עת וזמן להתבודד ולהעטיק דעתו היטב באיזה ענין» — *that he make himself a time to be alone and deepen his mind well in some matter*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, line 632: «ולא תוכל למצוא במחשבתה האלהות, כי אם בקושי ובחזקה» — *(some souls) cannot find G-dliness in their thought except with difficulty and force*</small>

## AR-04 · Habit becomes nature

**What it is.** How the unity stops being an event and becomes a way of seeing: repetition. 'Habit rules over everything and becomes second nature.' Even the word emunah (faith) means training, like a craftsman training his hands.

**From inside.** At first it is effort and reminding. Over weeks it begins to come by itself. The Tanya: when he trains himself to delight in G-d by contemplation, 'perhaps a spirit from on high will be poured on him'. Training the mind is like training the hands: after a while the hand knows.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Same time, same place, every day. 2. The main thing is 'the habit, to accustom his mind and thought always' that all he sees is the King's outer garments. 3. Short and daily beats long and rare (manual part3 for timings). 4. Keep going when it feels mechanical; that is the training.

**Signs it is real.** Things you used to have to remember begin to arrive on their own; the line comes to mind unasked at odd moments.

**Its counterfeit.** Rote: the habit that keeps the form and loses the life (AR-05).

**Guards.** Habit is gentle and daily; it is not a vow, and a missed day is not a failure. Never shame a short day.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 14, 42; also ch. 44, line 664, already cited in manual part3).

**In a chat today.** "This grows the way any skill grows: a few minutes, same time, every day. After a while it starts to come to you on its own."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 14, line 239: «שההרגל על כל דבר – שלטון, ונעשה טבע שני» — *habit rules over everything and becomes second nature*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, line 644: «אלא העיקר הוא ההרגל, להרגיל דעתו ומחשבתו תמיד» — *the main thing is the habit: to accustom one's mind and thought always*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, line 644: «שהוא לשון רגילות, שמרגיל האדם את עצמו, כמו אומן המאמן ידיו» — *(emunah) is a word for training: a person trains himself, like a craftsman who trains his hands*</small>

## AR-05 · The hundred-and-first time

**What it is.** Habit's shadow. Once something is habitual, it is no longer 'service'. The Talmud's line, read by the Tanya: the one who serves G-d is the one who reviews his chapter 101 times, not 100. The Rashab warns that good done 'only from habit' has no firm foundation.

**From inside.** The practice runs smoothly and nothing in you moves. You are good at it. The Rashab's picture: good and bad can live together in such a person for years, until some event shows the old nature untouched.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Notice when the practice has become your nature. 2. Add one thing past it: one more minute, one detail more, one harder case (manual part3 day 9 'the feared thing itself'). 3. Return to the line as if for the first time (ONE-PROCESS's spiral: the same rung, deeper).

**Signs it is real.** The practice costs something small again, and you are present in it.

**Its counterfeit.** Mistaking smoothness for depth; or the opposite, throwing out a good habit because it feels dull.

**Guards.** The 'one more' is small. Never turn it into a quota or self-reproach.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 15; Kuntres HaTefillah). Ours: setting it beside AR-04 as habit's shadow.

**In a chat today.** "When it gets easy and smooth, add one small thing past the habit. That little extra is where it's alive again."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 15, line 248: «היינו מי ששונה פרקו מאה פעמים ואחד» — *(one who serves G-d) is one who reviews his chapter a hundred and one times*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 15, line 241: «״עובד״ הוא לשון הוה – שהוא באמצע העבודה» — *'one who serves' is in the present tense: he is in the middle of the work*<br>
> Kuntres HaTefillah (Rashab), line 99 · *OCR*: «ועושה את הטוב רק מצד הרנילות» — *and he does the good only out of habit*</small>

## AR-06 · The light return through the day

**What it is.** The deep contemplation is a set time; the rest of the day runs on a light return to it. The Tanya: whoever contemplates 'a long while each day' that He fills everything and sees all he does, the awe is fixed in his heart for the whole day 'when he returns to it even with a light contemplation, at every time and hour'.

**From inside.** The Rashab explains why two modes are needed: contemplation cannot be constant; 'after prayer the grasp and the contemplation depart', and only an imprint remains. But remembering can be constant: the King seen through whatever the eyes see. Through the day it feels like a glance, not a sitting.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. A morning sitting (long or short). 2. Through the day, a light return: one glance, one sentence, at set anchors (waking, eating, doorways, before a hard conversation). 3. Renew the intention each hour of long work, because 'each hour is a different flow' of life from above (Tanya ch. 41). 4. Let what you see be the reminder: sky, people, objects as His garments.

**Signs it is real.** Small corrections in the moment: a word not said, a kindness done, because He is here.

**Its counterfeit.** Mental muttering that becomes a tic; or performing awareness for others.

**Guards.** A light return, not an interruption of real duties or of attention to people and safety (driving, care work).

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 41-42; Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 3).

**In a chat today.** "You don't have to meditate all day. Sit once in the morning; then, a few times today, just glance back at it for one breath: at the door, at lunch, before that call."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, line 639: «כשיתבונן בזה שעה גדולה בכל יום» — *when he contemplates this for a long while every day*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, line 639: «אזי, תקבע בלבו היראה לכל היום כולו, כשיחזור ויתבונן בזה אפילו בהתבוננות קלה, בכל עת ובכל שעה» — *then the awe will be fixed in his heart for the whole day, when he returns and contemplates it even with a light contemplation, at every time and hour*<br>
> Kuntres HaAvodah (Rashab), line 101 · *OCR*: «שאין זה עניני התבוננות כ"א ענינים שצריכים להיות תמיד בזכרון האדם» — *for these are not matters of contemplation but matters that must be always in a person's memory*</small>

## AR-07 · The morning that buys the day

**What it is.** For the busy person. The Rashab passes on the Mitteler Rebbe's counsel to businesspeople who cannot pray long: a short, real connection in the morning leaves an imprint that guards them all day.

**From inside.** Every extra moment of stillness in the morning is 'like buying life for his soul for the whole day'. The imprint 'guards him even in the pressure of his business', and lifts him back toward G-d whenever he turns from work for a moment.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Before the day's business, don't rush into talk of business. 2. Learn a little, then pray (or sit) slowly, with attention on the words themselves. 3. A minute longer counts. 4. Through the day, use any good deed (charity, a kindness) to touch the morning again.

**Signs it is real.** The day feels held by something; work does not swallow you entirely.

**Its counterfeit.** A long morning that makes you late and resentful; or skipping the morning because 'it's only five minutes'.

**Guards.** Short is fine; the Tanya says 'as your free time allows'. Not for crowding out sleep or family duties.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Kuntres HaTefillah 16, quoting the Mitteler Rebbe).

**In a chat today.** "Even five quiet minutes before you open your phone count. The old teachers said a single extra minute in the morning buys life for the whole day."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Kuntres HaTefillah (Rashab), line 115 · *OCR*: «וכל מה שיאריך בתפלה אפילו רגץ יותר הרי זה כקונה חיים לנפשו על כל היום» — *every moment longer he stays in prayer, even one moment, is like buying life for his soul for the whole day*<br>
> Kuntres HaTefillah (Rashab), line 115 · *OCR*: «דרשימה זאת היא שומרת אותו גם בהיותו כטרדת עסקיו» — *this imprint guards him even in the pressure of his business*</small>

## AR-08 · Months and years

**What it is.** The sources speak in years, not days. The refinement of a trait comes 'little by little'. The struggle may last 'all his days'. Repentance (return) is not only for sins: a person should be 'all his days in return', turning from his former way.

**From inside.** Plateaus. A year or two of work and still no steady state; the Piaseczner warns this is where despair comes in. The Tanya: if he is in this war all his days, let his heart not fall, 'perhaps for this he was created'. Real change shows slowly, in traits, not in peak moments.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Pick the particular trait that is yours to fix and work on it in detail, not only in general (the Rashab). 2. Measure by seasons, not by sittings. 3. Accept the hours and moments of uplift with joy, without demanding they stay. 4. Expect to return to the beginning, deeper (ONE-PROCESS's spiral).

**Signs it is real.** Over months: slower anger, less need for credit, more ease with people. The trait 'is refined little by little'.

**Its counterfeit.** Despair after a year or two; or the spiritual career, measuring oneself by levels reached.

**Guards.** A guide never tells someone how far along they are; it only points at the next small thing.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 9; Tanya ch. 27; Derekh HaMelekh, talk to students; Likkutei Torah Re'eh 11:2 for 'all his days in return').

**In a chat today.** "This is a years thing, and that's good news: nobody expects you to arrive this month. Pick one trait and watch it soften over the season."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Kuntres HaAvodah (Rashab), line 243 · *OCR*: «מתכרר ומזדכך מעט מעט המדה ההיא» — *that trait is sorted and refined little by little*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 27, line 385: «אל יפול לב אדם עליו, ולא ירע לבבו מאד, גם אם יהיה כן כל ימיו במלחמה זו, כי אולי לכך נברא» — *let no one's heart fall, or be very troubled, even if he is in this war all his days, for perhaps for this he was created*<br>
> Derekh HaMelekh (Piaseczner), line 1549: «אפשר לבוא ח"ו לידי יאוש עי"ז שרואה שנה שנתים וכו' שעובד ואינו יכול להשאר כן תמיד» — *one may, G-d forbid, come to despair from seeing a year, two years, that he works and cannot stay that way always*</small>

## AR-09 · Up and down is the shape

**What it is.** Running and returning (ratzo v'shov) is not only a moment in prayer; it is the shape of the whole life. 'A person must always be going up and coming down.' Darkness comes before light, and they are close together.

**From inside.** Days of fire, then days of nothing. The Maharash: 'just as there is no light without darkness first', there is no love and joy in prayer without bitterness first; and the running and returning are 'close to each other', so the dark is hardly dark. The Piaseczner: 'whoever thinks he never falls from his level, it is a sign he never rose' (manual part4).

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Expect the fall before it comes; name it as part of the path. 2. On the down day, keep the service, smaller: 'even when he is not aflame and his service is weaker, then too let him be a servant of G-d'. 3. Do not judge the path by the day. 4. Welcome the up moments with joy without demanding they stay.

**Signs it is real.** You keep going through the down day, and it passes.

**Its counterfeit.** Reading every low day as proof it was all fake; or forcing a constant high (Piaseczner: a person who flips between serving and quitting has a weak faith, not a low day).

**Guards.** A down day is not depression; if lowness is lasting or dangerous, a person needs real help (danger rule).

**Stated or ours.** stated (Derekh HaMelekh, Chazon 6; Toras Shmuel 5629, 'VeYached Levavenu').

**In a chat today.** "Up and down is how this goes for everyone; a flat day doesn't erase a good one. Do a smaller version today, and let it pass."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Derekh HaMelekh (Piaseczner), line 704: «כי תמיד מוכרח האיש להיות בעולה ויורד. רק גם כשאינו מתלהב וגם כשעבודתו חלושה יותר גם אז יהיה עבד ד' ויעבוד אותו» — *a person must always be going up and coming down; only, even when he is not aflame and his service is weaker, then too let him be G-d's servant and serve Him*<br>
> Toras Shmuel 5629 (Maharash), line 1982: «כשם שא"א לאור בלא קדימת החשך להאור» — *just as there can be no light without darkness first*<br>
> Toras Shmuel 5629 (Maharash), line 1983: «הרצוא ושוב הם תכופים זל"ז» — *the running and the returning are close upon each other*</small>

## AR-10 · Descent for the sake of ascent

**What it is.** The Tanya's own frame for why a soul is in a body at all, and so for every fall: 'this descent is for the sake of an ascent'. The soul came down to raise the animal soul and its world; the low place is where the work is.

**From inside.** From inside, a fall feels like loss. The frame turns it: the lower you are, the more the G-dly soul in you is in exile, 'and the compassion for it is very great'; and the soul's going out of that exile, in Torah and prayer, is the greatest joy, like a prince freed from prison going home.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. After a fall, say the Tanya's sentence: 'I did not make myself. Why did G-d do this? Only because this descent is for the sake of an ascent.' 2. Turn the fall into compassion for your own soul (MAP S54). 3. Take one step up from exactly where you are (a word of Torah, a kind act).

**Signs it is real.** Energy comes back after the fall, more than before; the fall becomes a reason to rise, not a verdict.

**Its counterfeit.** Using 'descent for ascent' to excuse planning a fall; or to explain away someone else's suffering.

**Guards.** Never said about another person's pain, loss or abuse. Only about one's own fall, and only by the person themselves.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 31). Guard on others' suffering: ours, following ONE-PROCESS step 5's rule.

**In a chat today.** "A fall isn't the end of the story; it's often where the next climb starts. You're still you underneath it."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 31, line 463: «אין זה, כי אם ירידה זו – היא צורך עליה» — *it can only be that this descent is for the sake of an ascent*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 31, line 455: «יש בקרבי ״חלק ה׳״ ממש, שישנו אפילו בקל שבקלים» — *within me is truly 'a part of G-d', which is in even the lightest of the light*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 31, line 457: «וזאת תהיה עבודתו כל ימיו בשמחה רבה» — *and this will be his service all his days, with great joy*</small>

## AR-11 · Dry and cold

**What it is.** Timtum halev, the heart turned to stone. The Tanya says it comes to ordinary people 'sometimes, and many times', and gives counsel for it. Hayom Yom's description of the start of a descent is exact: 'it all becomes dry and cold'.

**From inside.** No feeling at all in prayer or sitting. The mind understands, but it 'does not grip'. Mitzvot done by rote become heavy; one hurries; Torah loses its taste. The Piaseczner adds the comfort: it seems to him his heart is sealed, 'and in truth even now it is full of holiness', hidden in his own vessels.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Distinguish: sadness is a heart 'dull as stone, with no life'; bitterness and a broken heart are alive. Aim for the second, not the first. 2. Don't hurry: slow down the very things that went dry (one blessing, one line) (ours, from Hayom Yom's symptom list). 3. The Tanya's counsel: at set times, lower the self's noise (see guard). 4. Keep the deed going; feeling follows later (AR-15). 5. Remember the Piaseczner: full, not empty.

**Signs it is real.** Dryness that passes; a returning taste in one small thing.

**Its counterfeit.** Numbness taken as proof of being a fake (MAP S17); or 'fixing' dryness with stimulation.

**Guards.** The Tanya's medicine for stone-heart (breaking the self's pride at fixed times) is for a healthy person with a proud, thick heart. Never for depression, trauma or self-hatred; for them, comfort first (DM 1240) and professional help where needed.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 29, 31; Hayom Yom 23 Iyar; Derekh HaMelekh, Purim 13). Ours: 'slow down what went dry', from Hayom Yom's list of symptoms.

**In a chat today.** "Dry and cold happens to everyone who does this; it doesn't mean it's gone. Do one small thing slowly today, and trust that what's in you is still there."

> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 29, line 403: «אשר לפעמים ועתים רבים יש להם טמטום הלב שנעשה כאבן, ולא יכול לפתוח לבו בשום אופן» — *who sometimes, and many times, have a dullness of heart turned to stone, and cannot open their heart at all*<br>
> Hayom Yom, line 292: «ראשית הירידה, ר"ל, הוא העדר העבודה בתפלה, עס ווערט אלץ טרוקען און קאלט» — *the beginning of a descent, G-d spare us, is the absence of service in prayer: everything becomes dry and cold (Yiddish)*<br>
> Derekh HaMelekh (Piaseczner), line 1240: «ונדמה לו שלבו אטום. ובאמת גם עתה מלא קדושה הוא» — *it seems to him his heart is sealed; in truth, even now it is full of holiness*</small>

## AR-12 · What to do on a low day

**What it is.** The Tanya's practical counsel for heaviness. Sadness makes a person lose the struggle, like a wrestler who is lazy and heavy; joy and an open heart win it. And stray thoughts in prayer are not proof the prayer is worthless.

**From inside.** Heavy, slow, self-critical. Stray thoughts crowd in, and one concludes 'my prayer is nothing'. The Tanya: that is 'the error of the world'; there are two souls in the mind, and the stronger the G-dly soul pushes, the harder the other pushes back.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Do not treat the low day as the time to judge yourself; reckoning has its own set time, never during prayer or study. 2. Turn worldly sadness, if it is there anyway, into a short honest reckoning, then straight to joy: 'it is true I am far; but within me is a part of G-d'. 3. Ignore stray thoughts like a heckler; do not argue with them. 4. Every thought pushed aside is itself a victory that gives joy above (ch. 27). 5. If it will not lift, ask Him for mercy in your own words (ch. 28).

**Signs it is real.** The day ends with one small win and less self-attack.

**Its counterfeit.** Using the low day for a marathon of self-criticism; or forcing cheerfulness.

**Guards.** A guide gives the gentle half first (joy, the part of G-d within, no arguing with thoughts). Reckoning only for someone stable, briefly, and always followed by joy. Lasting low mood or danger: reach a person now.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 26-31). The order of what a guide offers first: ours.

**In a chat today.** "On a heavy day, don't put yourself on trial. Thoughts wandering in doesn't mean it isn't working; let them pass like noise from the street and come back."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 26, line 371: «אי אפשר לנצחו בעצלות וכבדות – הנמשכות מעצבות וטמטום הלב כאבן, כי אם, בזריזות – הנמשכת משמחה ופתיחת הלב» — *it cannot be overcome with laziness and heaviness, which come from sadness and a stone-dull heart, but only with quickness, which comes from joy and an open heart*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 28, line 398: «ולא כטעות העולם, שטועים להוכיח מנפילת המחשבה זרה – מכלל שאין תפלתם כלום» — *not like the error of the world, who prove from a stray thought falling in that their prayer is nothing*<br>
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 31, line 452: «כי עצבות – היא שלבו מטומטם כאבן ואין חיות בלבו, אבל מרירות ולב נשבר, אדרבה, הרי יש חיות בלבו» — *sadness is a heart dull as stone with no life in it; but bitterness and a broken heart, on the contrary, have life in the heart*</small>

## AR-13 · Signs it was real

**What it is.** The sources test an experience by what it leaves, not by how it felt. Manual part4 already gathers the main signs; this entry adds the Rashab's sign over time: the particular trait changes.

**From inside.** Afterward: true lowliness, and an insult does not sting 'because he has nothing of his own'. Awe and love added. 'I find myself another man.' More master of yourself than before. And over weeks: the trait you work on 'is refined little by little'.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Do not check during the experience. 2. Check later, sometimes, not always. 3. Ask: am I a little more my own master? Did the next insult sting less? Did anything change in my deeds this week? 4. Look at one trait over a month, not at one sitting.

**Signs it is real.** Lowliness without misery; ease with people; deeds slowly changed.

**Its counterfeit.** Testing so often that every spark is killed (manual part4, 'don't kill the fire').

**Guards.** For the anxious and scrupulous, testing is turned down, not up (manual part4, 'when each fits').

**Stated or ours.** stated (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 40; Bnei Machshava Tova; Derekh HaMelekh 553; Tzav VeZeruz 75; Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 9).

**In a chat today.** "Don't measure it by how strong it felt. Notice it later: did the next sharp word sting a bit less? That's the real sign."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (Mitteler Rebbe), line 828: «וסימנו שאחר כך יבוא לכלל שפלות אמיתית» — *its sign: afterward he comes to true lowliness*<br>

## AR-14 · The counterfeits

**What it is.** What looks like the unity and is not. The sources name five: imagination and nerves; pleasure in one's own feeling; pride after; floating (fervour with no deed); and false bittul, chasing self-nullification as one more acquisition.

**From inside.** Imagination: excitement that may be 'only empty imagination and irritation of the nerves'. Self-pleasure: the Rashab's word is the Yiddish 'tsufridnkeyt', satisfaction with one's own arousal, so that after prayer the bad trait is 'even stronger'. Pride: 'as soon as it passes, he comes to feel himself'. Floating: 'a flame flying in the air, with nothing to hold it'. False bittul: he wanted bittul 'because it was the one thing he lacked'. And the Rashab's quiet one: no change at all; the traits 'appear in their strength as before'.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Do not filter during; check after (AR-13). 2. If the feeling was the goal, turn the goal back to Him. 3. If nothing changed, go from the general to the particular trait. 4. If you notice pride, notice it without drama; even a mixed motive becomes pure 'little by little' (Piaseczner). 5. Give every high a deed (AR-15).

**Signs it is real.** When the counterfeit is seen, it loses its grip; the service continues.

**Its counterfeit.** Suspicion of everything: calling every feeling fake (also a counterfeit, per Tzav VeZeruz 84 in manual part4).

**Guards.** Never used to tell a person their experience was fake. A guide only offers the after-question.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 9; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39-40; Chovat HaTalmidim 324; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 38, line 778; Derekh HaMelekh 1550). The five-way list: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Here's an honest check, for later, not now: did it change anything in how you treat people? If not, no shame; it just means take it down into one small act."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Kuntres HaAvodah (Rashab), line 243 · *OCR*: «ואדרבא יכול להיות בתוקם יותר מפני שמחת נפשו (די צופרידענקיים) מההתפעלות והתלהבות שלו» — *on the contrary, (the trait) may be stronger, because of his soul's satisfaction ('tsufridnkeyt') with his own arousal and fervour*<br>
> Kuntres HaAvodah (Rashab), line 243 · *OCR*: «אין שום שינוי כלל בהמדות הטבעיים, ונראים בתקפם כמו מקודם» — *there is no change at all in the natural traits; they appear in their strength as before*<br>
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (Mitteler Rebbe), line 814: «והיה להיפוך שרצה להגיע למדריגות הביטול כי לא יחסר לו רק זה» — *it was the reverse: he wanted to reach the levels of bittul because that was the only thing he lacked*</small>

## AR-15 · Light needs a vessel

**What it is.** The descent's first law: what was found will not stay without vessels. The Alter Rebbe on Elul: even if he found what was lost, 'the light of G-d will not remain in him without vessels', and vessels are the letters of Torah and the deeds. ONE-PROCESS section 3 gives the deed side; this adds the time side.

**From inside.** Without a vessel the experience evaporates by evening. With one, a word learned or an act done, something remains. 'Like a fire that cannot hold without a wick.'

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. After any opening, put it into one word of Torah you can carry, and one act today. 2. Choose the act in the domain of the trait you're working on. 3. The Piaseczner's form: when a good thought comes, do a mitzvah with it or learn with it.

**Signs it is real.** The day after, something is still there.

**Its counterfeit.** Endless inner experiences with no outward trace (the 'flame in the air').

**Guards.** For someone not Jewish, a vessel is a kind act and a few lines of wisdom held through the day; no Jewish practice is imposed (MAP D17).

**Stated or ours.** stated (Likkutei Torah Re'eh 10:8; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 38).

**In a chat today.** "That feeling needs somewhere to live. Give it one thing today: a kind act, a few words you carry with you."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Likkutei Torah (Alter Rebbe), line 3346: «אף גם אם מצא את האבדה הנה לא יתקיים בו אור ה׳ בלתי כלים» — *even if he found what was lost, the light of G-d will not remain in him without vessels*<br>
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (Mitteler Rebbe), line 778: «הרי זה כשלהבת הפורח באויר שאין לו קיום» — *it is like a flame flying in the air, with nothing to hold it*</small>

## AR-16 · Work as a dwelling

**What it is.** Business and work are not a break from the unity but one of its places. The Tanya: the rest of the day, spent in trade, becomes a dwelling for Him through the charity he gives from his earnings. The Mitteler Rebbe even saw an advantage: businesspeople can see G-dliness palpably more than those who sit and study.

**From inside.** 'In all your ways know Him': the person who puts his heart and mind on all that happens to him and around him 'sees G-dliness palpably'. The Maharash (the Rayatz's grandfather, via Hayom Yom) to a simple chassid who travelled to the fairs and, in the middle of business, shared a word of Torah: 'Eli, I envy you.'

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Two parts (Hayom Yom): with yourself, carry a few lines you know by heart and repeat them in the street; with others, turn business talk once toward something meaningful. 2. Give from earnings; that money lifts the rest. 3. See the day's events as hashgacha: what happened to me today, and Who sent it. 4. Morning first (AR-07).

**Signs it is real.** Work feels less like exile; colleagues are people, not obstacles; money becomes a tool.

**Its counterfeit.** Preaching at work; using spirituality to avoid doing the job well.

**Guards.** No proselytising at work; 'turning the talk' means a human, meaningful word, never pressure. Not for someone whose job is in danger from it.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 34; Hayom Yom 4 Nissan, 16 Iyar, 7 Tishrei).

**In a chat today.** "Your workday isn't time away from this. Today, notice one thing at work and ask who sent it; and give a little from what you earn."

> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 34, line 489: «וגם שאר היום כולו, שעוסק במשא ומתן, יהיה מכון לשבתו יתברך, בנתינת הצדקה שיתן מיגיעו» — *and the rest of the whole day, in which he is busy with trade, will be a place for Him to dwell, through the charity he gives from his labour*<br>

> Hayom Yom, line 492: «ביתרון הבעלי עסקים על היושבי אהל בענין ראית אלקות במוחש» — *the advantage of businesspeople over those who sit in the tent, in seeing G-dliness palpably*</small>

## AR-17 · Shabbos and the weekday

**What it is.** The week is a rhythm of the unity: six days of sorting and work, one day when it rises (MAP S75-S76 hold the Torah Or source). The Maharash (via Hayom Yom) adds the bridge: from Shabbos all the days are blessed, and the preparation for Shabbos starts from Wednesday.

**From inside.** Weekday: buried in detail; the sorting. Shabbos: 'when the lights are brought in, I find myself another man'. Midweek worry ('where will I get what I need for Shabbos') is met with trust, and on Thursday night by learning.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Sunday to Tuesday: carry the last Shabbos. 2. From Wednesday: prepare (things, and the mind). 3. Shabbos: no new technique; let the day itself do it (manual part3). 4. After Havdalah: one line to take into the week.

**Signs it is real.** Shabbos changes you a little; the week carries something of it.

**Its counterfeit.** Shabbos as a day off only; or contempt for the weekday as 'unspiritual'.

**Guards.** Never impose Jewish practice on someone not Jewish; for them, a set hour of stopping on their own day of rest (MAP S76, D17).

**Stated or ours.** stated (Hayom Yom 23-24 Kislev; Tzav VeZeruz; Torah Ohr via MAP S75-S76).

**In a chat today.** "Let the week have a shape: carry the rest day for a couple of days, then start getting ready for the next one midweek."

> Tzav VeZeruz (Piaseczner), line 75: «וכשמביאים את הנרות מוצא אני את עצמי לאיש אחר» — *when the candles are brought in, I find myself another man*</small>

## AR-18 · The unity between people

**What it is.** Ahavas Yisrael is the unity lived sideways. All souls are 'matched, with one Father', so they are truly brothers; only the bodies are divided. The Alter Rebbe: love of G-d and love of Israel are engraved together; love of Israel is 'the gate of entry' to prayer.

**From inside.** When the body is the main thing, love is conditional. When the soul is the main thing, the other is felt as family. Toward the distant: 'draw them with cords of love'. Toward the one who wronged: compassion for the soul in exile within him 'cancels the hatred and wakes the love'.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Before morning prayer or sitting, take on 'love your fellow as yourself' (Alter Rebbe's siddur, via Hayom Yom). 2. One act for another person each day. 3. With a difficult person: see the good hidden in them and the exile of their soul; let compassion lead. 4. Lowliness 'before every person', even the lightest: do not judge until you reach his place (Tanya ch. 30).

**Signs it is real.** Less rivalry; the other's good is felt as your own; you can be kind to someone who annoys you.

**Its counterfeit.** Being a pushover; tolerating harm in the name of love (MAP S86).

**Guards.** Where harm is ongoing: no counsel to forgive or tolerate (D04). Love never means staying unsafe.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 30, 32; Hayom Yom 2 Tammuz, 28 Nissan). The extension to all people for a non-Jewish user: ours, following the guide's rules.

**In a chat today.** "The same One is holding them as is holding you. Today, do one kind thing for someone, maybe even the one who's getting on your nerves."

> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 32, line 466: «בשגם שכולן מתאימות, ואב אחד לכולנה, ולכן נקראו כל ישראל ״אחים״ ממש» — *for they are all matched, with one Father for all; so all Israel are called truly 'brothers'*<br>
> Hayom Yom, line 350: «מצות אהבת ישראל היא שער הכניסה» — *the mitzvah of loving a fellow Jew is the gate of entry*<br>

## AR-19 · The beinoni: everyone's measure

**What it is.** The Tanya's honest promise. The beinoni (in-between person) is 'the measure of every person': anyone can be one at any moment, because it concerns deed, speech and thought, which are in our hands. The heart's feelings are not always in our control; 'not all times are equal'.

**From inside.** During prayer the bad in him 'sleeps'; afterward it can wake. His love passes after prayer, and still, for him, it is called 'whole service' and true, because he can wake it again each day. He knows he may only reach delight 'in imaginings', and 'does his part, and G-d does what is good in His eyes'.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Hold the beinoni's three keys: do, say and think what is right, even against the heart. 2. Wake the love daily; do not demand it stay. 3. Accept that the old nature may remain; win in the garments. 4. Leave the rest to Him.

**Signs it is real.** Steady deeds; love that returns each day; no despair at the pull that remains.

**Its counterfeit.** Calling oneself a beinoni as false modesty while not trying; or despairing at not being a tzaddik.

**Guards.** Not a verdict on anyone; the label is a person's own, never the guide's.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 12-14).

**In a chat today.** "You don't have to feel holy all the time. What you do, say and think is yours; the feelings come and go, and that's fine."

> <small>**Sources.**

> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 13, line 229: «לגבי מדרגת הבינונים – נקראת ״עבודה תמה״ באמת לאמיתו שלהם» — *for the level of the beinonim it is called 'whole service', truly, by their truth*<br>

## AR-20 · The tzaddik: given, not demanded

**What it is.** The tzaddik has finished the war: the bad is gone and the heart delights in G-d. The Tanya says plainly this is not in everyone's choice; it is 'like a reward' and a gift. One trains toward it, and 'perhaps a spirit from on high will be poured' on him.

**From inside.** For the tzaddik, the body is a stranger he feeds as a kindness (Hillel); the G-dly soul is the man himself. For everyone else the tzaddik is a direction, not a demand.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Set times to see the world's pleasures for what they become, and to delight in G-d by contemplation, even if it is only imagination now (Tanya ch. 14). 2. Habit makes the loathing of the bad 'a little bit true' (ch. 14). 3. Leave the gift to the Giver.

**Signs it is real.** Desire for G-d grows; what used to pull pulls a little less.

**Its counterfeit.** Thinking oneself a tzaddik (the Tanya, ch. 13: even if the whole world says you are righteous, hold yourself a beinoni); or measuring others against the tzaddik.

**Guards.** A guide never says a person has reached this or has not.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 10, 14, 15).

**In a chat today.** "The great ones got there as a gift, after work. Your job is the work; the gift isn't on a schedule."

## AR-21 · The simple Jew

**What it is.** The sources hold the simple person in high regard. Faith is above understanding, so 'even women and the unlearned' believe. 'You' (Atah) is said by all 'in complete equality', the scholar and the simple. Simple people saying Psalms wholeheartedly are high in traits and fear of G-d; in the days of Moshiach the worth of their simplicity will be revealed. In practice, the 'workers' reach the depth more than the 'thinkers'.

**From inside.** No concepts, or few; a direct 'Thank G-d', a Psalm, a kindness. Hayom Yom's ladder of feeling (in study, before prayer, in prayer) adds: 'sometimes one hears G-dliness without any work at all', from the essence in the soul.

**How one enters or deepens it.** 1. Do not force a beginner into concepts; offer the plain act and a few words to Him. 2. Psalms with a whole heart; charity with money and body. 3. For the learned: learn from the simple; the essence is equal in all.

**Signs it is real.** Wholeness, warmth, no pretence.

**Its counterfeit.** Contempt for learning ('I don't need to understand'); or the learned looking down on the simple.

**Guards.** Never call a user 'simple'. This entry is for the guide's stance: no one is too plain for this.

**Stated or ours.** stated (Tanya ch. 18; Rayatz, Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 'Margela'; Samach Vav notes, Q.5; Hayom Yom 24 Iyar, 20 Tammuz). The phrase 'the guide's stance': ours.

**In a chat today.** "You don't need big ideas for this. A whole-hearted 'thank You', a psalm, a kindness: that reaches as deep as anything."

> Sefer HaMaamarim 5689-5693 (Rayatz), line 1318: «וישנם אנשים פשוטים גבוהים במעלת המדות טובות ויראי אלקים באמירת תהלים בתום לבב» — *there are simple people high in good traits and fearing G-d, saying Psalms in wholeness of heart*<br>

## AR-22 · A path of the first forty days

**What it is.** A forty-day entry for a modern person, built only from the sources above; the arrangement is ours. The number forty has a source: from Rosh Chodesh Elul to after Yom Kippur are forty days, a time of favour, when the King is met 'in the field', before the palace. The five weeks follow R. Zusya's five-part teshuvah (Hayom Yom 3 Tishrei): Tamim, Shivisi, VeAhavta, BeChol Derachecha, HaTznea.

**From inside.** Not a ladder of states (that is manual part3's forty-day course, which can be the morning sitting here). This is the arc in small: begin, make a habit, carry it through the day, take it into work and people, test it, and start again deeper.

**How one enters or deepens it.** See the day-by-day table in AR.md, section 'The path'. Every day has the same four pieces: a morning sitting (manual part3 b or c), a light return at three anchors, one deed, a two-line check at night (not on Shabbos).

**Signs it is real.** By day 40: the practice holds most days; there is one trait you are working on; a difficult person is a little easier; falls are shorter.

**Its counterfeit.** Turning the forty days into a performance or a streak.

**Guards.** Danger first, always. Not Jewish: keep the shape; Shabbos becomes one's own rest day, Torah becomes a few lines of wisdom, no Jewish practice imposed. A low day replaces the day's task with the low-day practice (AR-12) and the path resumes tomorrow.

**Stated or ours.** ours (the arrangement, days, weeks, and pieces); stated (forty days of favour, the King in the field, Zusya's five, and every practice placed in it, each from the entry cited).

**In a chat today.** "Let's try forty days, a few minutes a morning plus a few glances during the day. Week one is just about starting simply and honestly."

## The path, day by day (arrangement ours)

**Why forty, and why this shape.** Forty is the number of Elul's days of favour, from Rosh Chodesh Elul to after Yom Kippur, when "one goes out to greet the light of His face in the field" (Likkutei Torah, Re'eh). The field is the point: the King comes out to where people are, before anyone enters the palace. So this path asks nothing grand. Its five weeks follow R. Zusya's five-part teshuvah, as Hayom Yom gives it: **T**amim (be whole with Him), **Sh**ivisi (I set Him before me always), **V**eAhavta (love your fellow), **B**eChol Derachecha (in all your ways know Him), **H**aTznea Lechet (walk humbly). The last five days close the circle and open the next round.

**Every day has four pieces.** 1. **Morning sitting.** The manual's ten-minute practice, or its three-minute one (`../manual/part3-programs.md` b, c). If you want a ladder, its forty-day course can run here in parallel. Before the phone if you can (AR-07). 2. **The light return.** One breath back to the morning's line at set anchors: three to start (waking, a meal, a doorway) (AR-06). 3. **One deed.** Chosen in the morning, done by evening (AR-15). 4. **Night check.** Two lines: *What moved me today? What did I do with it?* No grading (ours). Not on Shabbos; write after Havdalah.

**Standing rules.** Start on a Sunday, so days 7, 14, 21, 28 and 35 are Shabbos. On a low day, replace the day's task with the low-day practice (AR-12) and resume tomorrow. Never skip; shorten. A missed day is not a broken path. Danger overrides everything. Someone not Jewish keeps the shape: their own rest day for Shabbos, a few lines of wisdom for Torah, a kindness for a mitzvah.

**What to expect (stated, from the entries; the timing is ours).** Week one may hold a glimpse, or nothing; both are normal (AR-01, AR-03). In weeks two and three some of it starts to come by itself (AR-04). Somewhere in weeks three to five there is often a fall or dryness (AR-09 to AR-11); the path is built so that it happens inside the practice, not outside it. By day 40 the sign is not a feeling but a fruit: a trait a little softer, a person a little easier, a fall a little shorter (AR-13).

## Where the sources pull different ways (kept, not smoothed)

- **Habit: praised and warned against.** The Tanya says habit becomes second nature and makes loathing the bad "a little bit true" (ch. 14, 42). The same Tanya says the habitual is no longer "service" (ch. 15, the 101st time), and the Rashab says good done only from habit has no firm foundation (Kuntres HaTefillah). Both hold: build the habit (AR-04), then add past it (AR-05).
- **The heavy heart: break it, or comfort it.** The Tanya's medicine for the stone heart is to lower the self at set times, even sharply (ch. 29), and the Rashab adds fasting and accepting rebuke for the one "whole in himself" (Kuntres HaTefillah, scan p. 26). The Piaseczner comforts: the sealed heart is full of holiness (Derekh HaMelekh, Purim 13). The guide uses the comfort first and never the harsh method with anyone low, depressed or self-hating (AR-11, AR-12). Fasting and self-affliction are not carried into the guide at all (ours).
- **Business: an exile or an advantage.** The Rashab, quoting the Mitteler Rebbe, describes business as a drunkenness that drowns the mind and needs a morning guard (Kuntres HaTefillah 16). The Mitteler Rebbe also says businesspeople can see G-dliness palpably more than those in the study hall (Hayom Yom 7 Tishrei). Both are true in sequence: guard the morning (AR-07), then see Him in the day (AR-16).
- **Testing.** See `../manual/part4-inside.md`, "Where they differ". AR-13 and AR-14 follow its rule: no checking during; the after-question later; less testing for the anxious.

## What is stated, what is ours

- **Stated:** the gift and purpose of a first revelation; hearing versus seeing; nearness and effort; habit and its shadow; the light return through the day; the morning that guards the businessman; years and little-by-little; up and down; descent for ascent; stone-heart and dryness, with the Tanya's counsel; the low-day counsel; the signs and counterfeits; light needing vessels; work as a dwelling; Shabbos blessing the week; love of Israel as one with love of G-d; the beinoni, the tzaddik and the simple person; forty days of Elul; Zusya's five.
- **Ours:** the arc's order and the word "arc"; the five-counterfeit list as a list; "slow down what went dry"; the order a guide offers things on a low day; the guards about depression, others' suffering and non-Jewish users (following the guide's rules); every "in a chat today" line; the whole forty-day path, its days, weeks, tasks, the four daily pieces and the night check.
- **Unsure:** whether R. Zusya's five, given as five parts of teshuvah, are meant as a sequence in time (we use them as one); where on the arc the tzaddik's "gift" can be expected at all (the Tanya says it is not in a person's choice).
