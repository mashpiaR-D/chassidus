## Summary

Group-08 has thirty-two works: twenty-two flagged Chabad in the index and ten not. The index's non-Chabad flag includes the Alter Rebbe's Hilkhot Talmud Torah and Strashelye's Sha'arei Avodah. In the Chabad core the one idea is the stated center, and each work shows a different side of it. Tanya makes it the inheritance of every Jew (ch. 33, 36) and grounds creation in the Essence (IH 20). The Alter Rebbe's late maamarim state 'yachid, not echad' and require both knowledges 'below as above' through mitzvot. The Gate of Unity joins every detail, down to the physical particular and even kelipah, to the Essence ('the opposing force also comes from the Essence'). The Mitteler Rebbe's Sha'arei Teshuvah and letters sharpen ein od past 'subordinate existence', and teach that the unity dwells only in bitul. Ner Mitzvah VeTorah Or puts a gate of faith before the gate of unity; R. Hillel's commentary in it gives the non-literal tzimtzum. Ayin Beis names the Essence the 'true yesh', and ties the dwelling 'for Himself' to the concealed essential power. The Tzemach Tzedek's Sefer HaLikutim indexes his whole corpus by concept and marks where the soul analogy fails for ein od. The Rayatz's Likkutei Dibburim tells the idea as providence and story, and demands knowledge over faith alone. The Yiddish original sets that demand beside the simple woman's prayer. Hayom Yom gives it one line a day. Pelach HaRimon ties the dwelling to the animal soul. Kuntres Bar Mitzvah carries the Rebbe's 5728 reading of 'Atah' as three modes of the Essence. Outside Chabad, Sha'arei Avodah (Strashelye) is the strongest statement in the group: the unity is to be revealed 'from yesh and from the opposite', and the power of concealment is itself from the Essence. It has the highest rates of 'ein od' and 'yichud' in the group. Tzofnat Paneach carries the Besht's unity as joining form and matter and uniting opposites. Yisrael Kedoshim reads 'olam' as 'he'lem'. Chayyim VaChesed reads the unity as delight and teshuvah in this world. Ohev Yisrael gives it as the tzaddik's sweetening of judgments. Beit Yaakov (Izbica) states 'no being but God', organized around will and not the Essence. Disagreements kept: faith against comprehension (R. Avraham of Kalisk's letters in Toldos Chabad beEretz HaKodesh; Shivchei HaRan's 'no wisdom in service'; the Rayatz's 'faith alone does not fulfill'), and the spatial tzimtzum wording of Tiferet Shlomo against R. Hillel's 'fills the void even now'. Thin, and said plainly: the two Kehot source apparatuses (except the Rebbe's and the Tzemach Tzedek's notes), Hilkhot Talmud Torah, Hilchos Amirah leNochri, Meshiv HaTa'anah (calendar), the four Chabad histories and memoirs (bePolin, MiBeis HaGenazim, Zikaron Livnei Yisrael, Lubavitch; Toldos beEretz HaKodesh is thin as history but keeps the Kalisk dispute), Shivchei HaRan, and Tiferet Shlomo, whose center is the tzaddik. Toras Chaim is not thin in content, but its OCR is unreadable on most pages, so its counts are noise and only a few pages give evidence.

**Signature** (*stated in many entries; that the calendar as a whole is one expression of the idea is our reading*). Hayom Yom gives the one idea in its smallest units: one line a day, and often in the same entry as a custom or a correction to a printed maamar. Its angle is the Essence in practice. A mitzvah 'joins' the one who does it with the Essence who commands it, and so it makes no difference whether the command is great or small (8 Cheshvan). The 'Anochi' hidden even from the highest emanations clothed itself in contraction upon contraction 'to make this physical earth', and man and his 613 mitzvot are the purpose of it (22 Cheshvan, from the Maggid). The essence of every Jew is 'a part of the Essence', which Chassidus reveals (13 Kislev). The form itself is a reading of the grammar (G12, every event and every day as instruction): the book does not state that its dates are one idea. It is the source of several of the spine's own anchors: Chassidus as vitality (24 Teves), the Rayatz's definition of Chassidus as joining form and matter (7 Kislev), and Shaar HaYichud as 'the key' (15 Adar II). It also keeps a counter-voice the spine records, the Alter Rebbe's 'spirituality and physicality are opposites in essence'.

**Structure.** Daily entries from 19 Kislev to 18 Kislev, arranged by month, each with one to four short items: a custom or nusach, a saying, a story, or a correction to a printed maamar. The Chassidus sayings carry the idea; the customs and the printer's corrections (e.g. 597, 603) do not, and their tie is thin.

**Thin.** Partly. The customs, nuscha'os and textual corrections have no stated tie to the unity; only the Chassidus sayings carry it.

**Signature** (*stated in entries; the claim that the index as a whole is one expression is our reading*). Sefer HaLikutim turns the Tzemach Tzedek's corpus into a concordance of ideas. Its method is the Mitteler Rebbe's G04 carried out by alphabet: take any term, even 'Ohel Mo'ed', and trace it to joining and to the revelation of Ein Sof. There 'mo'ed' is 'no'dati', a joining, and 'I made Myself known' means 'the revelation of Ein Sof itself' (2098). What it adds is the Tzemach Tzedek's precision on 'ein od'. 'Od' is something that exists in its own right though subordinate, like the body to the soul, and 'there is no od' means the worlds are not even that before Him (8893). This marks exactly where the soul analogy fails (G06). The two knowledges, from above (the lower is nothing) and from below (the lower is yesh), are to be made 'equal', 'below as above', so that yesh takes no place (10212). The compilers do not claim the book is one idea. It is an index. But the entries repeatedly end in the same place, and the spine's F02 and G06 appear here in the Tzemach Tzedek's own words.

**Structure.** Entries by letter. Each opens with a list of sub-headings (e.g. 'Ohel Mo'ed': joining; your tents, Yaakov; tent and Temple; modesty; the camps around it), then excerpts with references to Or HaTorah, Derekh Mitzvotekha and the maamarim, and editorial notes. The quartile counts are even across the length (יחוד 206/229/390/240; דירה בתחתונים 18/21/21/24), so the idea is not bunched in a few entries.

'Echad' fits only things that were separate and then united; of the Essence, since there is nothing besides Him, one may say only 'yachid' (16). The descent is then made concrete. It is no great novelty to draw light into the sefirot, which are one with Him. The novelty of Sinai is that the mitzvot are done in physical things 'in the world of separation', and so draw the revelation of Godliness 'below as above' (1416-1420). The two knowledges, the world as nothing from above and as yesh from below, must both be present in man, and that is what 'in Our image' means. Learning with bitul makes the world take no place below as above, 'for there is nothing besides Him below as above' (1421). A 5572 maamar applies the same point against the nations. They grant God the heavens and leave the earth to the stars; Israel says 'in the midst of the earth' and counts by the moon (3580-3582). That is G05, the highest in the lowest, stated as the difference between Israel and the nations.

Many maamarim appear in several versions, so the same doctrine is seen through different listeners. Most of the text is sefirot, partzufim and Zohar exegesis; the tie to the unity is usually stated at the opening (a question on a verse) and the close (avodah). **Signature** (*stated for each gate; that the pairing of faith and unity is the book's signature is our reading*). This book puts two gates side by side: faith, then unity. The Mitteler Rebbe, who more than any Rebbe demanded comprehension, begins by calling faith 'the foundation and root of all the mitzvot', to be strengthened every day (9-11). He builds it on matzah: the bread of lowliness, in which the soul says 'who am I, with my limited mind, to grasp the Essence of Ein Sof, above all the chain of worlds' (119). Only then comes Shaar HaYichud, contemplating the unity in every detail. We read the pairing as the Mitteler Rebbe's own side of the spine's knowledge-or-faith dispute: comprehension stands on faith and does not replace it. The book does not say this in so many words. R. Hillel of Paritch's commentary adds a clear non-literal statement of tzimtzum: 'from the side of the Essence of the light, it fills all the void now too, after the tzimtzum, as before', with no change at all, 'I Havayah have not changed' (1022).

**Structure.** Part I, Shaar HaEmunah (chapters on Pesach, matzah and the counting to Sinai); Part II, Shaar HaYichud; R. Hillel's Likkutei Biurim on Shaar HaYichud; then the Kehot sources, which account for most of the file's length and much of its later counts.

**Signature** (*stated*). Sha'arei Teshuvah brings the unity to people with businesses to run, through teshuvah and prayer. Its precision is on 'ein od'. 'Only one' excludes even the kind of bitul in which something still exists and merely submits, like a chariot to its rider: 'not that there is something else that submits and is batel, but there is none besides Him at all' (509). That is the spine's 'yachid, not echad' applied to bitul. The same precision divides service into two sizes. 'Gadlus' in prayer is contemplating the Essence of Ein Sof, how there is none besides Him (786). 'Katnus' is the work of the whole exile: refining noga, turning darkness to light, small work like a servant's cooking (787-788). The book holds both, and so it states F12 and F11 together: the highest knowledge and the lowest labor.

**Structure.** Part I: Shaar HaTeshuvah, Shaar HaBechirah, Shaar HaTefillah and a treatise on prayer, then single maamarim. Part II: a second Shaar HaTeshuvah and the long treatise on prayer and Torah. The quartiles are steady (עצמות about 70-90 per quarter).

**Signature** (*stated*). The book states its own signature: 'the unity of Godliness in every single detail of the chain of worlds' (Introduction 1). Its method is G04, joining every particular to the general, and the general to the Essence. The spine already anchors 7:10-12 and 7:44-46. What the rest of the book adds is the extent of that joining. After passing through all the worlds, one contemplates 'who created these in physicality', and sees that 'even the very last particular is not separated from the very first general source', in every part of heaven, earth, mineral, plant, animal and man (8:52-53). At the top it gives the same precision as the spine: the Essence 'is called yachid, for there is none besides Him, of whom to say union or joining' (10:22). At the bottom it brings kelipah into the account. Whatever spreads out from the Essence has a force opposing it, 'which also comes from the Essence' (54:6). So even the opposite is included (F09).

**Structure.** Chapters 1-7: the stages of contemplation and arousal, and the method (join the particulars to the general). Chapters 8-19: Keter, Chochmah and Ein Sof traced through every world. Chapters 20-50: points, sefirot, partzufim and letters in detail. Chapters 50-53: speech, the ofanim and the constellations, the roots of the elements and the lowest physical world. Chapter 54: kelipah. The order is itself the method: top to bottom, each detail joined to the whole.

He is also the spine's strongest voice for knowledge: 'know today' requires 'knowledge and comprehension; with faith alone one does not fulfill the obligation' (1706). **Signature** (*stated in places; the tie to the dwelling below is our reading*). The Amdurer carries the unity as delight (ta'anug). God's pleasure is hidden in every pleasure, so 'in seeing, hearing, eating and drinking', one looks not at the body 'but at the inner vitality in it' (164). He also says this world has an advantage over the next. The world to come is good without effort, 'since it is unity with God'. In this world there is opposition, 'and nevertheless one becomes one with the Creator, that is the novelty' (447). That is very close to the Chabad dwelling below and darkness turned to light (F07, F11), but it is stated in terms of pleasure and teshuvah. The link is our reading. The tzaddik is the one who draws the light of Ein Sof to himself until it surrounds him, as oil drawn into a flame becomes light (129).

**Structure.** Numbered teachings (over 360), a code of conduct, letters, and directives of the Maggid of Mezritch.

> **Chayyim VaChesed 144:1** (line 164) · faces F07, F01 · *stated*
> והרחבות יבוא לו כשיסתכל על תענוג שיש לו ממנה, הן בראיה הן בשמיעה הן באכילה ושתיה, לא על הגוף אלא חיות פנימיות שיש בו
> — A great rule of service: walk always in expansiveness, which comes from looking at the delight in each thing, in seeing, hearing, eating and drinking, not at the body but at the inner vitality in it.

> **Chayyim VaChesed 358:1** (line 447) · faces F11, F07 · *stated (the parallel to the dwelling below is ours)*
> אבל בעולם הזה שיש לו התנגדות ואף על פי כן נעשה אחדות עם הבורא זהו רבותא
> — One hour of teshuvah in this world is better than all the world to come: there one is one with God as a matter of course, here there is opposition, and nevertheless one becomes one with the Creator.

> **Chayyim VaChesed 113:1** (line 129) · faces F08, F05 · *stated*
> כך הצדיק ממשיך אור אין סוף אצלו עד שמקיפו
> — Oil drawn into a flame thinks it will overcome the fire, and in the end becomes light; so the tzaddik draws the light of Ein Sof to himself until it surrounds him.
