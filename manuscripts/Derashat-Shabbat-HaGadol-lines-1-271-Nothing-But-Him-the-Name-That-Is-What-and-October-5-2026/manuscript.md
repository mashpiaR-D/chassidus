This is the Maharal's sermon for the Shabbat before Pesach (1589). It opens with "G-d made all for His sake", explains the Pesach offering as service to the One, then reads the offerings of Parashat Tzav (olah, mincha, chatat, todah, shelamim) as a map of a person's inner life: pride and lowness, the evil inclination, thanks, and peace.

Six teachings matter most for the guide:

- **"There is none else" means nothing else.** Not only no other god: "there is nothing else in the world but G-d" (2). Creatures are like a chair, which "has no name of its own" apart from the one who sits on it.
- **Twoness inside.** "The evil inclination is a twoness to the good inclination" (49). When one reads the Shema and cleaves to His oneness, "he removes the twoness".
- **The Name is "What" (Mah).** The four-letter Name adds up to 45, "mah" (what). His self has no "what". Man ("adam") also adds up to 45: he is "what" from his own side and "very much" ("me'od", also 45) from G-d's side (79). So He dwells with the crushed (77-78).
- **Hidden in Himself, shown in His acts.** "He is revealed from the side of His acts just as He is hidden from the side of Himself", and most of all to the lowly (137).
- **When all returns to Him, He is One.** Confession and teshuvah make "all return to G-d, and in this G-d is One" (214).
- **Walk toward peace.** Peace is wholeness and comes last. A living person is always walking toward it, and "all is drawn toward peace" (238, 249). He is called Shalom "because He is the peace of all" (254).

## 2.1 Nothing else but Him

The verse "you shall know today... that G-d is the G-d in heaven above and on the earth below; there is none else": "אין פירוש "אין עוד" כי אין עוד אלהים, אבל פירושו כי אין עוד דבר בעולם, רק השם יתברך" (2, 1:2): "'there is none else' does not mean there is no other god, but that there is nothing else in the world but G-d." Everything was made to praise Him, and so it has no standing of its own: "כי הכסא אשר ישב עליו בעל הכסא, אין לכסא שם בפני עצמו" (2): "for the chair that its owner sits on has no name of its own." So all creatures "אין להם שם בפני עצמם" (2): "have no name of their own." If anything stood for itself, there would seem to be something besides Him. Even a person eating: "הרי הוא עצמו נברא לכבוד השם יתברך, והכל הוא אל השם יתברך" (3, 1:3): "he himself was made for G-d's honor, and all is toward G-d."

He does not need this honor: "אין צריך כבוד אל השם יתברך מן הנבראים" (58, 8:4): "G-d needs no honor from the creatures." Rather, "ראויים כל הנבראים להיות נבראים מפני שנותנים כבוד" (58): "all creatures are worthy of being made because they give honor." And His kingship does not hang on us: "בין יקבלו מלכותו או לא יקבלו עליהם, הרי הוא מלך עליהם מצד עצמו" (60, 8:6): "whether they accept His kingship or do not accept it, He is King over them from His own side." That is why "Anochi" is said as a truth, not an order.

## 2.2 One world through one man, one Maker

Man was made last and from both upper and lower, because "האדם מקשר העולם עד שהוא אחד לגמרי" (15, 3:3): "man ties the world together until it is fully one." He was made single so that "עליונים ותחתונים הם אחד על ידי אדם אחד, ודבר זה מעיד כי הפועל אחד" (16, 3:4): "upper and lower are one through one man, and this bears witness that the Maker is One." "והשם יתברך רוצה בעולם אחד" (18, 3:6): "and G-d wants one world."

## 2.3 A many that is one points to the One

"וכאשר יש כאן רבוי שהוא אחד" (42, 5:12): "and when there is a many that is one," there is before it One "ואין בו רבוי, וזהו השם יתברך, שהוא אחד לגמרי" (42): "with no many in Him, and that is G-d, who is fully one." Shabbat is one day with no pair, yet it is made of hours and moments; so it too points past itself: "יש אחד גמור בלי שום שניות כלל, והוא השם יתברך" (47, 6:2): "there is One, whole, with no twoness at all, and that is G-d."

## 2.4 The Name is "What"

The four-letter Name, spelled out, adds up to 45, "mah" (what), because His self has no "what": "כי אין מהות לעצמו" (135, 15:1): "for there is no 'what-it-is' to His self." Who could hold Him? "מי יכיל את כבוד ה' ורוממותו, כי הוא יתברך הכל" (78, 10:2): "who can hold the glory of G-d and His height, for He is all." Only one who is "what", with no fixed shape of his own: "ובזה לא יוגבל שכינתו יתברך על האדם" (78): "and so His Shechina is not bounded on the person." Man ("adam") is also 45: "כי הוא 'מה' מצד עצמו, והוא 'מאד' מצד השם יתברך" (79, 10:3): "for he is 'what' from his own side, and 'very much' from G-d's side." So "כל מקום שאתה מוצא גדולתו של הקב"ה שם אתה מוצא ענותנותו" (77, 10:1): "wherever you find the greatness of the Holy One, there you find His humility."

## 2.5 Hidden in Himself, shown in His acts

The Name begins with yud, the letter of the hidden third person. "Ehyeh" (I will be) begins with alef, the one who speaks and is present. "והוא נגלה מצד פעולותיו כשם שהוא נסתר מצד עצמו" (137, 15:3): "and He is revealed from the side of His acts just as He is hidden from the side of Himself," and most of all to the one who is low and crushed. So the answer to Moshe: "אהיה עמהם בצרה זו" (136, 15:2): "I will be with them in this trouble."

## 2.6 When all returns, He is One

The todah comes with chametz, for the one who has "sacrificed his inclination" and confessed. "כאשר הכל שב אל השם יתברך, בזה השם יתברך אחד" (214, 23:6): "when all returns to G-d, in this G-d is One." The thanks psalm says "He made us and we are His": "וכאשר הכל שלו הנה הוא אחד לגמרי" (209, 23:1): "and when all is His, He is fully one."

## 2.7 He is the peace of all

"עושה שלום ובורא רע": peace is wholeness and evil is its opposite (248). "כי הכל נמשך אל השלום" (249, 29:2): "for all is drawn toward peace," and so no quarrel that is not for Heaven can last. He is called Shalom "מפני כי הוא יתברך שלום הכל" (254, 30:3): "because He is the peace of all."

**Proposed MH-S29 "What, and very much".** Inside: the double knowing that from my own side I am "what?", nothing fixed, and that from His side I am "very much", lifted and full. Both at once, with no swing between shame and pride. "כי הוא 'מה' מצד עצמו, והוא 'מאד' מצד השם יתברך" (79, 10:3): "for he is 'what' from his own side, and 'very much' from G-d's side." It is the only vessel that can hold Him, because it does not bound Him (78). Moshe and Aharon said "we are what", and the world stands on them: "ומכל זה יש ללמוד מעלת מי שעושה עצמו מה" (87, 10:11): "and from all this learn the height of one who makes himself 'what'." How to enter: see 4.3. Map: near S59 True lowliness, S60 The essential bond and MH-S28 The desert; the answer to S11 The verdict: I'm my mistakes. Guard: "what" is not "worthless"; it goes with "very much".

**Proposed MH-S30 "Walking toward peace".** Inside: not yet whole, and at peace with not yet being whole, because one is walking toward it. To the living we say "go toward peace", not "go in peace" (237). Everything unfinished moves toward its wholeness: "ולפיכך מחוייב האדם לרדוף השלום" (238, 27:7): "and so a person must pursue peace." "כי אחרית לאיש שלום" (234, 27:3): "for there is a future for the man of peace." One who walks toward it is kept from harm (251). How to enter: see 4.7. Map: near S47 Running and S48 Returning; the answer to S02 Holding it all up and S07 Restless wanting.

## 4.1 Deposit the spirit at night, wake to serve (`accept`; new: `mh_deposit_the_spirit`)

At night a person is like the dead; in the morning He returns the soul, and it is as if one were made anew (28). So: pray close to waking, before other things; and at night say "בידך אפקיד רוחי" (30, 4:4): "into Your hand I deposit my spirit." The Maharal draws the lesson: "ובזה נראה כי האדם אינו דבר לעצמו, רק הכל אל השם יתברך" (30): "and in this it shows that a person is not a thing for himself, but all is toward G-d."

## 4.2 Four steps against the inclination (`quieting` / `bittul`; new: `mh_four_steps`)

"לעולם ירגיז אדם יצר טוב על יצר הרע" (35, 5:5): "let a person always stir his good inclination against the evil inclination." If that does not win, learn Torah; if not, read the Shema; if not, remember the day of death. Why it works: "יצר הרע אינו שולט רק מצד הבריאה" (35): "the evil inclination rules only from the side of being born"; remembering the end takes away its ground. And the Shema works because "יצר הרע הוא שניות ליצר טוב, ומביא לידי שניות" (49, 6:4): "the evil inclination is a twoness to the good inclination, and brings to twoness"; "וכאשר יקרא קריאת שמע, ויהיה דבק באחדותו יתברך, יהיה מסלק השניות" (49): "and when he reads the Shema and cleaves to His oneness, he removes the twoness." Guard: the fourth step is a calm remembering, not dwelling on death; it is not for someone with thoughts of self-harm (see NY 252).

## 4.3 "What" and "very much" (`bittul`; new: `mh_mah_and_meod`)

Say slowly: "From my side, what am I?" Rest there a moment without judging. Then: "From Your side, very much." The Name itself is "what" (135): not a thing, and so it can dwell with one who is not a thing (78). End with Moshe and Aharon's words: "ואילו במשה ואהרן כתיב (שמות טז, ז) "ונחנו מה"" (87, 10:11): "but of Moshe and Aharon it is written, 'and we are what'."

## 4.4 Bless before you take (`before_whom`; with `mh_aim_at_the_giver`)

Using the world without a blessing is like using holy things (5-6); the blessing gives praise in place of the fruit. Do not be too proud to learn how: "איך יקפיד על כבודו, והרי השם יתברך בראו לכבודו" (7, 2:4): "how can he insist on his own honor, when G-d made him for His honor." And notice small things: "בקטנים יותר נראה שבח יוצר הכל" (4, 2:1): "in small things the praise of the Maker of all shows even more."

## 4.5 Bring a broken heart (`heart_after`; new: `mh_broken_vessel`)

A plain man is ashamed to use broken vessels, "אבל הקב"ה, כל כלי תשמישו נשברים הם" (160, 17:10): "but the Holy One, all the vessels He uses are broken." "קרוב ה' לנשברי לב" (160): "G-d is near to the broken-hearted." Practice: on a bad day, do not wait to feel whole before turning to Him. Bring the broken heart as it is. Guard: if the brokenness is lasting depression, also get help.

## 4.6 Thanks for what was (`own_words`; with `mh_todah_all_to_him`)

"כל ההודאה היא לשעבר, והתפלה היא להבא" (217, 23:9): "all thanks are for what was, and prayer is for what is coming." In the end all prayer stops and only thanks remain. Practice: each evening, thank Him in your own words for three things that already happened today. Say "הוא עשנו ולו אנחנו" (208, 22:15): "He made us and we are His," and "כי אנו תלויים בו" (208): "for we hang on Him."

## 4.7 Seek peace and pursue it (`act`; new: `mh_pursue_peace`)

Other mitzvot wait until they come to you; peace you must chase: "בקש שלום ורדפהו" (236, 27:5): "seek peace and pursue it", in your place and in another's. Practice: once a week, make one move toward peace with someone you are apart from. Say to a friend leaving "go toward peace" (237). Guard: pursuing peace does not mean giving in to abuse.

## 4.8 Bind the bundle (`act`; new: `mh_bind_the_bundle`)

The four species are four kinds of people, from the learned and good to those with neither. "Destroy them? Impossible. Bind them all in one bundle, and they atone for each other." "ואם עשיתם כן, אותה שעה אני מתעלה" (270, 32:7): "and if you do so, at that hour I am raised up." For "התאספות והתקשרות כאחד הוא הנפה ועליה" (228, 26:2): "gathering and binding as one is a raising and a going up." Practice: pray or learn with people unlike you, and count them as part of you.

## 4.9 Study as offering (`act`)

Learning the order of the offerings counts as offering them, because an offering is a joining and order is the mind of a joining (242-247). "אין עיקר הבית העצים והאבנים" (247, 28:6): "the core of the house is not the wood and the stones," but its order.

## 5. The big human questions

**Does G-d care about the small and low?** Yes, more than about the great. "באמת השגחת השם יתברך יותר בשפלים" (134, 14:9): "in truth G-d's watching is more on the lowly." He feeds all "מקרני ראמים עד ביצי כנים" (122, 13:6): "from the horns of wild oxen to the eggs of lice." He "נתון באור, ורואה מה שהוא בחושך" (112, 12:11): "is set in light and sees what is in darkness." "ה' מה אדם ותדעהו" (63, 8:9): "G-d, what is man that You know him."

**Why are some poor?** So that the rich can give and both gain: Avraham told his guests "וכי משלי אכלתם" (99, 11:8): "did you eat of mine?" And "יותר ממה שעושה בעל הבית עם העני, העני עושה עמו" (101, 11:10): "more than the householder does for the poor man, the poor man does for him."

**Why did G-d make an evil inclination?** Bad in itself, good in the whole: "והנה במה שברא השם יתברך הכל, בזה יצר הרע הוא טוב" (195, 22:2): "and in that G-d made the all, in this the evil inclination is good." Without it there would be no Torah to choose. But it is not a creation in its own right, so it will end: "נעקר יצר הרע מן העולם, אין אופל וצלמות בעולם" (268, 32:5): "when the evil inclination is uprooted from the world, there is no gloom and death-shadow in the world."

**Why is judgment so hard?** Because we stand apart. "כי כל דין אינו רק על אחר" (232, 27:1): "for all judging is only of another." When one is joined to Him, "אין כאן דין, רק נשיאת פנים, שהיא לפנים משורת הדין" (232): "there is no judgment here, only lifting of the face, which is beyond the line of law."

**What makes my good deeds real?** That they stand on themselves, not on envy or imitation: "כי כל יסוד נבנה על עצמו, ולא נבנה על זולתו" (74, 9:11): "for every foundation is built on itself, not on another." Hevel brought an offering only because Kayin did.

**Why is pride so bad?** Because before Him greatness is nothing: "אין שום שררה וגדולה אצל השם יתברך, שהכל נחשב לכלום אצל השם יתברך" (146, 16:5): "there is no rule or greatness before G-d, for all counts as nothing before G-d." "השם יתברך כנגד בעל הגאוה, הוא מתגאה על גאים" (143, 16:2): "G-d, against the proud, is high above the high," and yet "מרום וקדוש אשכן ואת דכא" (147, 16:6): "I dwell on high and holy, and with the crushed." True wisdom lowers: "הקרן של חכמה הוא מביא אל השפלות" (159, 17:9): "the horn of wisdom brings to lowness."

**Is there peace in this world?** Not fully yet. "אם אין שלום אין כלום" (194, 22:1): "if there is no peace there is nothing." We walk toward it (236-238), and "צדיקים ישכינו שכינה לארץ" (91, 10:15): "the righteous will make the Shechina dwell on earth."

**What is fair to others?** Each has his own share: "אין אדם נוגע במוכן לחבירו" (124, 13:8): "no one touches what is set aside for another," as each hair has its own root.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **"There is nothing else in the world but G-d"** (2). This is the strongest "ein od" line in the Maharal so far, and it reads the same verse as the Tanya (Gate of Unity ch. 6). The reasons differ. The Maharal: creatures exist for His honor, so they have "no name of their own", like a chair. The Tanya: creatures are nullified in the divine word that gives them being every moment. Both deny the world any standing apart from Him; only the Tanya says it is literally as nothing from His side.
- **Tzimtzum.** Not named. "Hidden in Himself, shown in His acts" (137) and the Name as "what" (135) are close to the Chabad teaching that the Essence is beyond all names and is found through His acts (the "light"), and that the lowly receive most. The Maharal gives no account of how the hiding happens.
- **The Rambam's negative attributes.** "There is no 'what' to His self" (135) is the Rambam's teaching that we know that He is, not what He is. The Maharal turns it into a teaching about humility: only one who is "what" can hold Him.
- **The Ramak's formula.** The chair (2) comes close to the first half ("all is He": nothing has a name apart from Him); "hidden in Himself" (137) to the second ("all is not He").
- **Twoness inside** (49) matches the Tanya's two souls and its call to bring the animal soul into the oneness through the Shema. The Maharal's frame is oneness against twoness, not two souls.
- **Man joins upper and lower** (15-18) matches the Chabad theme of the "home below" (S82) and of man as the one who unites the worlds.
- **Confession makes Him One** (214) is close to the Chabad reading of teshuvah as return of all to the source, and to the Rebbe's teaching that "He will be One" in the future means His oneness revealed below.
