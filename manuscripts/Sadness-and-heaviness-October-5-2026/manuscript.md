<a id="c13"></a> ## C13 · A heavy, gloomy mood and a dark lens on everything

*8 findings from Kuntres HaHispaalus, Toras Chaim (part 3 of 4), Toras Chaim (part 4 of 4), Kuntres HaTefillah, Sefer HaSichos (part 2 of 2), Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 3 of 3)*

> I wake up heavy, sad and irritable for no reason. I'm melancholy by nature. The future looks dark and I only notice what's missing. Even joy comes out heavy.

**What is really going on.** The library separates kinds of heaviness. Kuntres HaHispaalus sets the natural soul's melancholy over bodily lack against the soul's grief at distance from God, which can turn to joy. The Rebbe Rayatz names a temperament 'on a line of sadness'. He says the evil inclination uses it to paint the future black, and he traces the heaviness to a lack of wholehearted service. Toras Chaim reads days of dullness and irritability as the soul's night before a day, and says service done then still rises. Kuntres HaTefillah treats sadness over worldly things that comes on its own as a cue for a short private accounting that must end in joy. The Lubavitcher Rebbe's letters call sadness baseless, put the doctor first, and prescribe trust, small daily anchors, and turning the mind off oneself by doing good. Toras Chaim notes in passing that song lifts sadness.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"I'm just a sad person by nature; that's all there is to it."* → The heaviness shows that whole, engaged service is missing and matter is ruling form, which can change. The natural soul's gloom is over bodily lack, and it is not the same as the soul's grief. `SHS2·Q25` · `SHS2·Q26` · `KH·Q21`
- *"The dark way I see things is simply how things are."* → The black coloring works on a temperament. It notices only what is missing, never what one has. How one sees depends largely on the seer, and the mind can be made to rule. `SHS2·Q19` · `SHS2·Q20` · `LS3·Q17` · `LS3·Q18`
- *"Yesterday I was alive, today I'm dead inside, so something is wrong with me."* → There is no light without darkness first. Smallness is the night before a day, and a person should not say it is hopeless when he falls, even many times. `TC4·Q02` · `TC4·Q08` · `TC4·Q06`
- *"Prayer or good deeds without feeling are worthless."* → Words said in smallness, only to do the Creator's will, rise. A deed done in simple faith without feeling stands higher than fervor. `TC4·Q03` · `TC4·Q18`
- *"My sadness is just the honest response to my situation."* → Sadness is refuted from the outset, and even bitterness has its time only occasionally. With the doctor followed and melancholy set aside, one sees there is reason for contentment. `LS2·Q16` · `LS2·Q17`
- *"At least feeling bad about my state counts for something."* → Feeling the not-good must become a resolve to be different. Satisfaction taken from having felt bad is worthless, and joy that comes from bitterness is worthless too. `SHS2·Q22` · `SHS2·Q23` · `SHS2·Q24`

**Where the works agree.** All agree the heavy mood is not the final truth about the person or the world, that action should go on through it, and that joy is the goal. Where bitterness is allowed, it is kept to set times, and sadness is never treated as a virtue.

**Where they differ.** On the cause: temperament used by the evil inclination and missing service (Rayatz); the natural soul's gloom over bodily lack (Kuntres HaHispaalus); the rhythm of life-force (Toras Chaim); something baseless that affects health (Lubavitcher Rebbe). On the remedy: Toras Chaim accepts smallness and serves inside it; the Lubavitcher Rebbe says to reject melancholy entirely while following the doctor; Kuntres HaTefillah turns the sadness into a brief accounting; Toras Chaim also mentions song.

**Order and timing.** Medical care first where there is illness. Daily: small anchors, and turning outward through doing good. On low days: serve in smallness without forcing, and don't declare it hopeless. Only occasionally and in private: a short accounting that ends in joy. Over time: retrain the lens, and learn until understanding is felt in the heart.

- 'Sadness is baseless', 'reject it', 'you can choose your lens' can shame someone with clinical depression. The letters themselves keep the doctor first. - Kuntres HaHispaalus speaks of 'loathing one's life constantly' (KH:Q22). This must never be offered to anyone depressed or at risk of suicide. - Night accountings can deepen despair and cost sleep for a depressed person. - Calling the dark lens 'the evil inclination' can make a person feel attacked by his own mind; offer it gently. - Reading mood swings as the soul's night and day must not delay assessment for bipolar or other illness. - Distraction is not for someone who needs to grieve.

<a id="c12"></a> ## C12 · Sad that I'm not the person I should be

*4 findings from Tanya, Likkutei Torah (part 2 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 3 of 3), Biurei Tanya*

> I'm crushed that I'm not a tzaddik, not the great fiery person I should be. It feels like honesty and piety, but it drains all the life out of me.

**What is really going on.** Tanya says the sadness that feels like honesty is a heart gone stone-dead. When it arrives mid-work, even over spiritual things, it is a ploy that leads to falling. Living bitterness belongs to a set hour. The verdict belongs to the body alone, and within the person is a real portion of God. Likkutei Torah traces this sadness to demanding greatness at the level of the greatest sages, and says joy in Torah is open even to one who has fallen. The Biurei Tanya go further: lifeless grief at not being a tzaddik is ego, like grief at not being rich. The gap to the tzaddik is infinite, so there is nothing to envy, and a little ache is enough. The Lubavitcher Rebbe's letters judge such thoughts by their fruits and tell the gloom-prone to drop self-examination for now.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"My sadness over my failings shows I'm serious about being good."* → Lifeless sadness is ego, grief at not being important. Real remorse is alive and moving, like joy. Thoughts that breed sadness, laziness or despair come from the evil inclination dressed as piety. `TAN·Q13` · `BT·Q14` · `BT·Q15` · `BT·Q18` · `LS3·Q09`
- *"I should be at the level of the great ones."* → Sadness comes from demanding greatness. The distance to the great is without limit, so there is nothing to envy, and God takes pleasure in the ordinary struggler's service as in the saint's. `LT2·Q11` · `BT·Q23` · `BT·Q20`
- *"If I'm really that bad, I have no right to joy."* → All that badness is 'I alone', the body and its vital soul. Within is a real portion of God, even in the lowest. `TAN·Q14`
- *"I'm not a saint anyway, so why try."* → This is what sadness is: despair of oneself, which then lets the heart do whatever it wants. The same ego that is coarse when it has completeness is sad when it lacks it. `BT·Q21` · `BT·Q14`
- *"I have to sort out my inner state before I can live properly."* → I am simple, not an investigator; my task is to do. For now, drop thoughts about yourself and put your mind into study, prayer and work. `LS3·Q04` · `LS3·Q05` · `LS3·Q06`
- *"Humbling myself will crush whatever joy I have."* → Humility belongs in the mind, as clear knowing; it need not sit in the heart. Joy in Torah is open even when one has fallen from feeling. `LT2·Q14` · `LT2·Q12`

**Where the works agree.** All agree that sadness over not being great is not piety. They separate living remorse from dead sadness, keep sadness out of the middle of work and prayer, and make joy the goal. They agree that joy does not have to wait for spiritual achievement.

**Where they differ.** Tanya and the Biurei Tanya keep set times of self-humbling, and Likkutei Torah even says dwelling on one's lowliness doubles joy. The Lubavitcher Rebbe tells the gloom-prone to drop self-accounting altogether for now. On diagnosis: the Biurei Tanya calls it ego, Tanya splits the selves, Likkutei Torah blames the demand for greatness, and Likkutei Sichos judges by fruits.

**Order and timing.** In the moment: set sadness aside and do the next thing. Daily: joy in learning and work, with humility held in the mind. Only if the mood is stable: a brief chosen reckoning with a small ache, ending in joy. If caught in self-scrutiny loops: stop self-accounting for now, learn the teaching against sadness with a guide, and test thoughts by their fruits.

- 'Despicable in his own eyes' and 'regard himself as leftovers' (TAN:Q11, LT2:Q13) can harm the depressed or self-loathing. - Telling a depressed person that their sadness is ego or arrogance is shaming and wrong for clinical depression. - Calling sadness 'the evil inclination' can make a sufferer feel attacked by their own mind. - Study and diversion are not substitutes for professional care. - Diversion must not become a way to avoid repairing real harm. - Perfectionists may turn 'a little ache' into a new standard to fail.

**Truths for the guide** *(candidates, each checked against its passages; see CHECKS.md)*

- **Living ache or dead weight** · Regret that is alive moves you toward something. Sadness that stops you cold is something else wearing regret's clothes. *Check: yes.*
- **No contest with giants** · You are not a smaller copy of someone greater. You are measured by your own effort in your own place, and a little longing to be better is enough. *Check: yes.*
- **More than the worst of you** · The worst true things about you are not the whole of you. Inside there is something good that nothing you have done has touched, and gladness can come from there without denying anything. *Check: yes.*

**What the core works do not cover.** The works do not separate perfectionism or clinical depression from spiritual gloom, and they do not address self-worth outside a religious frame or healthy ambition beyond 'a little ache'. This condition overlaps heavily with C13 and C15.
