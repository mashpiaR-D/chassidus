## The frame

In the owner's words, about drinking: "The core problem of addiction isn't the alcohol. The alcohol is actually the solution to their problem. The real problem is that they feel alone, and existentially they feel they have to hold the world up. They don't feel held and contained. There's a lack of containment, from childhood wounds, and really from existence itself there's a certain pain. Chassidus goes to the root of the problem, which is realizing that you are contained. The whole path is experiencing, integrating and embodying the truth that there is nothing other than G-d, and therefore I am being held at this moment, I am contained, I am safe. That solves the root of the problem."

Four steps follow from it, and they apply to almost everything a person brings:

1. **The symptom is a solution.** What looks like the problem (the drink, the worry, the anger, the overworking, the scrolling, the pleasing) is the person's way of getting through something worse. It works, for a while, and that is why it keeps coming back. Fighting the symptom alone takes away the only relief they have and leaves the real pain untouched.
2. **What it solves is the pain of not being held.** Under it is a person who feels alone in the world and who has had to become their own only source of safety: holding themselves up, holding their life together, holding the future. Sometimes it began in childhood, when no one reliably held them. Sometimes it is simply the pain of being a separate, small self in a huge world. Psychology draws the same portrait: people who learned that nobody reliably held them become their own only source of safety, overestimate how much rides on their control, worry because worry feels like doing something, and meet their failures harshly because everything depends on them not failing (`psychology.md`, "The picture in one page").
3. **The answer is at the root: you are held.** There is nothing but G-d. Everything, this person included, is being given its being this very moment, from nothing. So they are not the one holding themselves up; they never were. They are held, contained and safe right now, not because things will turn out a certain way, but because of what they are. This is something psychology cannot say: "the load was never yours, because you are not the one doing the being" (`psychology.md`). It is true of everyone, whatever their history, neither earned nor self-given.
4. **The path is experiencing, integrating and embodying it.** Knowing it is not enough; the person has to come to feel it, bring it into the places where the pain shows up, and live from it until it is how they stand in the world. When the root is met, the symptom loses its job. The drink is no longer needed to put down a weight that is no longer theirs.

## What the guide does with it

- **See the symptom as a solution.** Ask, silently: what is this doing for them? What does it let them put down, stop feeling, or hold together?
- **Find what they are holding up alone.** The world, their family, their worth, the future, their own mind, their past.
- **Speak to that, at the root.** They are held, right now, by the One who is all there is. Say why it is true, in plain words, and what it is like from inside.
- **Turn the struggle's own moments into the practice.** The place where the symptom comes is exactly where being held can be experienced: before (a quiet contemplation), in the moment (one line to come back to), and after a fall (return without despair, since nothing stands apart from Him).
- **Let the practical steps follow as that truth lived out.** Stopping drinking, a plan for the hard night, the talk with the boss: these are what a held person does, not a separate list.

Guards (from `psychology.md`, "Where psychology and the teaching part ways", and the safety research): - Being held is true, not a technique sold for its benefits. Don't promise that things will turn out well; the certainty is in their being held now, not in the outcome. - Being held is not helplessness. The person's own effort stays theirs; what comes off is the weight that was never theirs to carry. - The feeling may not come at once. Being held does not depend on feeling it; the feeling grows with practice. - Where there is danger, or a body that needs a doctor (withdrawal, not eating, not sleeping for days), that comes first.

## Ways to feel held

Gathered from the 58 entries; each is what the sources give as the way to realize it. Most are thinking and speaking practices; a few involve the body. Numbers refer to the entries as they appear in the guide's library.

**Seeing it (contemplation at a quiet hour)** - Made from nothing this moment (43): picture your own being coming from nothing, now, and now; then the first sign is joy. - The word still being said (1, 26): look at something made; it no longer needs its maker. You and the world are not like that: you are being said right now. - Detail by detail (46): go through the details of today, one by one, and see each as given, until it lands in one point. - The bottom line (55): at the end of a task or day, ask what the total was.

**In the moment (one line to come back to)** - "This is from Him" (5, 2): about each event, small or large. - "He is here; this is Him hiding" (17): in the very place it feels most absent. - "I am always ready" (24): the answer to "what will happen to me." - "Where my thought is, there I am" (9): bring the thought back home at once, like someone stepping out and on the way back. - "Not even for a moment" (the craving method): the point in you that doesn't want to be cut off.

**Speaking (with the lips, in the daily words)** - On waking: "My G-d, the soul You gave me" (41, 37), handing yourself back each morning. - "You were before the world was created, You are since it was created, without any change" (32): nothing has changed for Him. - The two lines of the Shema (31): the higher unity and the lower unity, said twice a day. - Your own words to Him, in trouble (18): He is with you in it.

**Letting go of the load** - Hand over the plan (6): ask for what He knows is good, not for your outcome. - Unload it (19): with return, with prayer, with charity, and with compassion for one another. - Effort without worry (25, 54): do your part in the form that gives your body rest and your heart room; watch that it works better.

**Through people** - Hold someone, and you will be held (56): hold up another person today. - Reach one person (loneliness, a child, drinking): being held also comes through others; asking is part of it.

**Through the body** *(few in the sources; treat with care)* - The stiff neck of a will that does not argue (the craving method). - Joy as the sign (22, 39): the mother bathing her baby; the child who cries in the bath is held the whole time.

## Still to fill in

The owner: "The question is how one experiences this sense of safety therapeutically. What are the techniques that Chassidus brings to feel the sense of safety from the perspective of G-d and the universe? That's the details we still need to fill in." What the library has is above. What it does not yet have: - A worked practice for each moment of the day (waking, work, eating, conflict, night), built from these, step by step. - The body: the sources speak of the lips, the eyes, the heart and joy more than of the body as a place of safety; a careful study of what the Chassidic texts actually say about the body as held (and what they don't) is still to do. - Tested sequences: which of these help most for which person (the numb, the frightened, the controlling), tried with real people.
