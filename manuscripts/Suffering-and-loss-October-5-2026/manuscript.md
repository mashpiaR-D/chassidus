<a id="c14"></a> ## C14 · Depression or mental illness, in me or someone I love

*2 findings from Toras Chaim (part 3 of 4), Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3)*

> I've lost all desire, even for my wife and children. Or: someone I love has broken down mentally and the doctors give no hope. What do we do?

**What is really going on.** The evidence is thin, and this entry keeps to it. Toras Chaim names melancholy as an illness in which a person longs for nothing, not food, not beauty, not even the love of his wife and children, because he has lost love of himself. It mentions this only in passing, as proof about where emotions come from, and gives no treatment. The Rebbe's letters (Likkutei Sichos, health appendix) are practical and hopeful. Such illness is treatable. Consult expert doctors and follow them. Stay in constant contact with experts about new treatments. Keep the person's time full with activity that doesn't call for brooding, including some physical work. Take attention off the condition through real activity that gives satisfaction, above all doing kindness for others. Where it fits, give responsibility for those weaker, which builds self-confidence. Strengthen trust in God, and help put the past out of mind. Medicine comes first, with the spiritual and practical alongside it.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"He doesn't want anything, not even us, so he must not love us anymore."* → Losing desire for everything, even for wife and children, is described as an illness rooted in losing love of oneself, not as a verdict on the relationship. `TC3·Q31`
- *"The doctors give no hope, so nothing can be done."* → Stay in constant contact with experts and ask regularly about new treatments and medicines, which are developed often. `LS2·Q32`
- *"We just need to tell him to stop thinking about it."* → Distraction works only through real activity that gives inner satisfaction, such as doing kindness for someone, not through lectures. `LS2·Q23` · `LS2·Q22`

1. **Family care in mental illness** · *from Likkutei Sichos* (1) Consult expert doctors in the field and follow their directions. (2) Keep in constant contact with experts, asking regularly about new treatments and medicines. (3) Keep the person's time full with things that don't call for deep brooding. (4) Include some physical work. (5) Strengthen his trust in God, and help him put the past out of mind. *When:* Ongoing, through treatment and after returning home. *For:* Families of someone who has had a breakdown or suffers mental illness. *Not for:* No limit is stated. 'Put the past out of mind' should not replace trauma therapy where that is needed. `LS2·Q25` · `LS2·Q32`
2. **Healing through doing good for others** · *from Likkutei Sichos* (1) Take the person's attention off his condition as fully as possible. (2) Do it through real activity unrelated to his health, not by telling him to stop thinking about it. (3) Choose activity that gives inner satisfaction, above all a kindness for another person. (4) Where it fits, give him responsibility for others who are weaker, such as leading a group, to build self-confidence. *When:* Throughout treatment and recovery, as the person is able. *For:* Patients and those caring for them, including in emotional illness. *Not for:* Not a substitute for professional treatment; it goes alongside it. Not to be imposed in an acute phase when the person cannot manage it. `LS2·Q22` · `LS2·Q23` · `LS2·Q24`

- From *"He doesn't want anything, even us; he's stopped loving us."* to **Wanting nothing, even those you love, is described as an illness that grows from losing love of oneself.** Naming the loss of desire as illness separates it from the relationship, so the family can respond with care rather than hurt. *(Toras Chaim)* `TC3·Q31` - From *"Taking his mind off it is just avoidance."* to **The more attention is drawn off the condition, the stronger the body's own healing powers and the other positive factors become.** Meaningful occupation is placed inside healing, not opposed to it. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS2·Q22`

**Where the works agree.** Both works treat it as an illness, not a moral or spiritual failure. The only practical source, the Rebbe's letters, puts medical care first and adds occupation, kindness and trust.

**Where they differ.** There is little to compare. Toras Chaim uses melancholy only to illustrate a theory of emotion, while the Rebbe's letters are practical guidance to families. The evidence here is too thin to map disagreements.

**Order and timing.** First: expert medical care, followed faithfully, with ongoing contact about new treatments. Alongside it: a full day, some physical work, and activity that takes attention off the illness, especially kindness to others. Later, when able: responsibility for others to rebuild confidence. Throughout: trust and gentleness.

- The guide must direct anyone depressed or suicidal to professional help and crisis resources, and must not treat illness as spiritual failure. - None of the bitterness, broken-heart, self-accounting, crushing or 'despair is the evil inclination' material from other entries may be used here. - Never recommend fasting or self-denial. - Don't tell the person that sadness comes from arrogance or that a life without Godliness is no life. - Responsibility for others should come only when the person is ready, so it doesn't become another failure. - 'Put the past out of mind' must not block needed trauma therapy.

<a id="c28"></a> ## C28 · Grief for someone I lost

*2 findings from Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 3 of 3)*

> Grief for someone I lost is eating me and I can't take comfort. Now I'm alone.

**What is really going on.** The evidence is thin: two letters by the Lubavitcher Rebbe (Likkutei Sichos, appended letters), one to a father who lost his only son. They do not deny the loss. Mourning is kept within its set measure. The soul lives on and rises through the good deeds of the living, and those deeds need joy; sadness and even bitterness beyond the measure weaken them. The test of any thought about the loss is what it does to action: if it brings weakness, sadness and despair, it is not the way. Loneliness is not a valid conclusion: the bereaved has been handed an added role, the strength for it was given beforehand, and the loss should lead to more activity among people.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"My grief shows how much I loved him."* → Sadness and even bitterness beyond the measure lessen the good deeds that raise the soul, and so lessen the soul's ascent. `LS2·Q18`
- *"With this loss, I'm alone now."* → There is no room to conclude loneliness; such a loss should lead to more activity among people, and the strength for the added role was given beforehand. `LS3·Q13` · `LS3·Q14`
- *"Nothing good can be found in this."* → Despite the feeling of loss, search for the good in it and turn it into practical life. `LS3·Q12`
- *"The bond ended at the death."* → The soul still rises, and the learning and good deeds of the living reach it. `LS2·Q18`

1. **Turning grief into action for the soul** · *from Likkutei Sichos* (1) Mourn within the set measure; the loss is real and acknowledged. (2) Remember that the soul lives on and still rises. (3) Add study, good deeds and charity for the soul's sake, in gladness at being able to help it; each weekday morning set aside coins in the departed's memory, and have the mezuzos checked. (4) Carry on in practice what the departed was engaged in. (5) Test your thoughts by what they do: push away those that bring weakness, sadness and despair. (6) Widen your activity among people, with more energy, knowing the strength for this was given beforehand. *When:* Daily, through the mourning year and beyond, after the first days. *For:* The bereaved who cannot take comfort or are sliding into isolation. *Not for:* The raw first days of grief. Complicated or traumatic grief, depression or suicidal thoughts, which need care. Anyone who would hear 'sadness is the opposite of the intent' as a charge of weak faith. `LS2·Q18` · `LS2·Q19` · `LS3·Q12` · `LS3·Q13` · `LS3·Q14`

- From *"My grief shows how much I loved him."* to **The soul lives and rises through your good deeds, and those need joy; sorrow beyond measure lessens what you can give it.** Moving love from sorrow to action gives the bereaved a living bond with the soul and a reason for joy. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS2·Q18` - From *"With this loss, I'm alone."* to **There is no room to conclude loneliness: you were handed an added role, and the strength for it was given beforehand.** Grief is turned into a mission among people, which keeps a link with the departed. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS3·Q12` · `LS3·Q13` · `LS3·Q14` - From *"These dark thoughts about why it happened are just facing the truth."* to **Judge a thought by what it does to you. If it brings weakness in good deeds, sadness and despair, it is not the way.** The effect on action becomes a touchstone, so the bereaved need not settle unanswerable questions before living again. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS2·Q19`

**Where the works agree.** Both letters, by the same author, move love from sorrow into action, keep the bond with the departed alive through deeds, and deny that the loss must mean despair or loneliness. Both treat the strength for what follows as already given.

**Where they differ.** No other Rebbe or chassid in this corpus speaks to grief, so there is no real divergence. Within the two letters the stress differs: one frames action through the soul's ascent and joy in the commandments, the other through an added role and wider activity among people.

**Order and timing.** Mourning first, in its measure, never skipped. Then small daily acts in the departed's memory. Then carrying on their work. Then, gradually, more activity among people. Thoughts are tested along the way by whether they lead to action or to despair.

- Fresh grief: telling someone that sadness is 'the opposite of the intent' or a weakness can wound deeply. - Complicated grief, depression or suicidal thoughts after a loss need professional care; the program is not treatment. - Loss by suicide, violence or of a child carries guilt and trauma the letters do not address. - The touchstone (judge a thought by its effect on action) must not be used to police tears. - 'Search for the good' must not be pressed on a traumatic loss. - The soul's continued life is a belief; with someone who does not share it, speak of love done in their name, not of claims about the soul. - Where the relationship with the departed was abusive, 'carry on their work' does not apply.

<a id="c26"></a> ## C26 · Why is this suffering happening to me?

*11 findings from Tanya, Torah Ohr (part 1 of 2), Likkutei Torah (part 2 of 3), Likkutei Torah (part 3 of 3), Sha'arei Orah, Toras Chaim (part 1 of 4), Toras Chaim (part 3 of 4), Likkutei Sichos (part 1 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3)*

> Loss, illness, poverty, humiliation keep coming. It looks like plain evil and punishment. God seems angry, or blind to what I'm going through. The pain floods me and I can't stop crying.

**What is really going on.** The library gives three readings and does not blend them. Root: from its source nothing bad comes down; what looks bad is a good too great to see, 'this too is for good' (Tanya, Likkutei Torah Chukat). Purpose: suffering is not a verdict of unworthiness. It comes to soften a hard spirit (Torah Ohr, Toras Chaim), to let the soul's light rise (one parenthesis in Sha'arei Orah), or, as a trial beyond reason, to lift a person past his own measure (Likkutei Sichos). Presence: the hiding of God's face is not real anger and not blindness. He sees; the hiding is on the receiver's side, a father hiding to be sought, for a small moment (Likkutei Torah, Likkutei Sichos). Toras Chaim adds that nature cannot turn pain into joy, and acceptance means receiving it willingly while it still hurts. Sha'arei Orah and Toras Chaim honor tears. Tanya also calls complaint near-denial and counsels examining one's deeds; see the contraindications.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"This is simply bad, and it is all there is."* → Do not judge by what the eyes see alone. The root of what looks bad is good, too high to be revealed here, and it is 'also for the good'. `LT2·Q15` · `LT2·Q16` · `TAN·Q26` · `TAN·Q06`
- *"God is angry with me, or doesn't see what I'm going through."* → The hiding is not true concealment or real anger, and the leaving is 'a small moment'. He sees the distress; it only seems to the sufferer that He does not. `LT3·Q26` · `LT3·Q31` · `LS2·Q06`
- *"I'm being punished, or it's pointless and out of all proportion."* → Torah Ohr says suffering is not because one is unworthy of good; it comes to soften a hard spirit. Likkutei Sichos reads a blow that seems to have no reason as a trial that raises a person higher. These are offered as meaning, not as verdicts. `TO1·Q12` · `TC2·Q27` · `LS1·Q24` · `LS1·Q25`
- *"If I had real faith it wouldn't hurt, so my pain proves I've failed."* → Nature cannot turn suffering into pleasure. Joy in suffering means accepting it willingly, through faith that good is hidden in it, while the pain stays real. `TC1·Q14` · `TC1·Q15`
- *"My tears show I'm broken."* → Tears come when pain is beyond measure and the mind cannot hold it. At a release, the same weeping holds relief and grief together. `SO·Q29` · `SO·Q32` · `TC3·Q12`
- *"I've been brought so low that I'm worthless."* → By its nature the highest light dwells precisely in the lowest place. `SO·Q31`

1. **Hold suffering inside faith: being from nothing, face to face** · *from Tanya* (1) At a settled time, contemplate that you and everything are brought into being from nothing at every moment. (2) Ask gently: how can it be that something bad is mine? What seems bad may be a good beyond grasp. (3) Turn toward God in humility, not only from counsel to counsel. This never means refusing medical, legal or professional help. (4) Receive it, as far as you can, face to face with a loving Father rather than as rejection. Tanya adds examining one's deeds; see the contraindications. *When:* At a settled time of contemplation, and again when complaint rises. Not in the first shock of loss. *For:* A believer who is steady enough to contemplate and who wants to hold suffering inside faith. *Not for:* Anyone in fresh grief, trauma or depression. Never to link a person's illness, loss or abuse to their sins. Never to discourage professional help. `TAN·Q26` · `TAN·Q06` · `TAN·Q28` · `TAN·Q29`
2. **Accept willingly while the pain stays real (soft as a reed)** · *from Toras Chaim* (1) Let the mind, through faith, loosen the grip of the natural traits a little, as a rein holds an animal. Do not try to erase the pain. (2) In suffering, look for the good hidden in it and accept it willingly. The hurt is allowed to remain. (3) Accept a lean season with contentment, as far as you can. (4) Where someone else has wronged you, the text asks for softness and quick reconciliation (see the anger condition), but never submission to mistreatment. *When:* In the moment of loss or hardship, and as a slow shaping of character. *For:* Someone who believes faith should make pain disappear and blames himself because it doesn't; someone rigid under hardship. *Not for:* Victims of abuse or injustice. 'Accept insult as praise' must never mean staying in mistreatment. `TC1·Q13` · `TC1·Q14` · `TC1·Q15` · `TC1·Q16`

- From *"This suffering is simply bad."* to **Its root is good, too high to be seen with the eye of flesh: 'this too is for good'.** Contemplating that the event's life comes from God places it back inside its source's goodness, and the sense of 'bad to me' loosens. *(Tanya, Likkutei Torah)* `TAN·Q26` · `TAN·Q06` · `LT2·Q15` · `LT2·Q16` - From *"God has turned away in anger; He doesn't see my pain."* to **He sees fully. The hiding is on your side, like a father hiding so his son will seek him, and it lasts 'a small moment'.** Absence becomes a matter of perception, not abandonment, so seeking becomes possible at once. *(Likkutei Torah, Likkutei Sichos)* `LT3·Q26` · `LT3·Q31` · `LS2·Q06` - From *"My suffering means God is against me."* to **Stand face to face with a loving Father. His rebuke is love, like a king washing his only son with his own hands.** Accepting in love turns the person toward God instead of away, and the hidden love behind the hardship is revealed. *(Tanya)* `TAN·Q29` - From *"This means I'm being punished or don't deserve good."* to **It is not unworthiness. Suffering can soften a hard spirit and let the soul's light rise into a broken heart.** Purpose replaces punishment. Offer this only if the person himself reaches toward such meaning. *(Torah Ohr, Toras Chaim, Sha'arei Orah)* `TO1·Q12` · `TC2·Q27` · `SO·Q12` - From *"This new blow has no point at all."* to **A trial beyond reason can raise you past your own limits, to a peace you could not reach by yourself.** Ordinary struggles refine a person within his own measure; a trial 'with no reason' raises him beyond it. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS1·Q24` · `LS1·Q25` - From *"If I had faith I wouldn't hurt."* to **Nature cannot turn suffering into pleasure. Faith holds pain and trust in one heart together.** Faith and understanding hold two opposites at once without asking the pain to disappear, so the pain stops counting as failure. *(Toras Chaim)* `TC1·Q14` · `TC1·Q15` - From *"My tears show I'm broken; and why am I crying now that it's over?"* to **Pain beyond measure and delight beyond measure share one root. At a release, joy at the light and grief for the long dark weep together.** Naming the tears gives them legitimacy and turns them from a sign of ruin into a sign of depth or of release. *(Sha'arei Orah, Toras Chaim)* `SO·Q29` · `SO·Q32` · `TC3·Q12` - From *"I've been brought so low; I'm worthless."* to **The highest light, by its nature, dwells in the lowest place.** Height seeks depth, so the low state becomes a place where the Essence can settle. *(Sha'arei Orah)* `SO·Q31`

**Where the works agree.** Suffering is not abandonment and not the last word. Its root, or its aim, is good even when that cannot be seen. God has not stopped seeing. The fitting response is to turn toward Him and not away. Each work reads hiddenness as belonging to the sufferer's view, not to God's intent.

**Where they differ.** Tanya is the most demanding. It calls sadness and complaint close to denial and counsels finding sins that need cleansing. Toras Chaim insists the pain remains pain and acceptance is willing, not joyful. Torah Ohr explicitly denies unworthiness. Likkutei Sichos speaks of a trial beyond reason. The Mitteler Rebbe's works honor tears as depth rather than failure. Sha'arei Orah marks its reading as a parenthesis.

**Order and timing.** The texts give no order. For safety, the guide should go: first, the pain and the tears are allowed (Toras Chaim, Sha'arei Orah); then 'you are seen, not abandoned'; only later, and only if the person reaches for it, meaning (root, purpose). Examining one's deeds comes last, only for the robust, and never about illness, loss or abuse.

- Never tell someone grieving, traumatized or in fresh shock that it is for good, a purpose or a punishment. Presence comes before meaning. - Never link a person's illness, a death, poverty or abuse to their sins. Tanya's 'examine your deeds' must not be offered to sufferers in that way. - Tanya's line that sadness and complaint are like denial can crush a depressed person. Do not use it. - The criticism of running from counsel to counsel must never discourage medical, psychological, legal or practical help. - 'Accept insult as praise' and 'the low place holds the light' must never justify staying in abuse or humiliation. - Anyone in danger needs safety and practical help first. Tears that do not stop for weeks may need care, not interpretation.

<a id="c27"></a> ## C27 · The obstacles are too big for me

*6 findings from Likkutei Torah (part 2 of 3), Sha'arei Orah, Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, Likkutei Sichos (part 1 of 3), Biurei Tanya, Chanah Ariel*

> Trials and resistance come at me from every side, and the darkness keeps getting stronger. I'm too weak. There's nothing left in me to stand with. Why am I being put through this test?

**What is really going on.** The works read the obstacle as a hiding, not as the final reality. Behind it lies the person's own strength, which the obstacle calls out. Likkutei Torah: trials are a hiding of the Face, and within the hiding itself is Godliness. Chanah Ariel: before the test, the power to overcome is hidden in one's nature; standing up to it brings that power out. Likkutei Sichos: the opposition exists only through concealment, and it is there so that you bring out powers already given you. Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus: resistance grows as the goal comes near, God asks only according to the strength He gives, and He dwells with us even at the lowest. Biurei Tanya defines a trial starkly: understanding and feeling are withdrawn, desire is added, and only bare faith is left. Sha'arei Orah: when the lamp fails, a point beyond reason stands. The evidence is almost all reframes; there is little method.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"These trials mean God has abandoned me or is punishing me."* → A trial is a hiding, and within the hiding itself there is Godliness. Standing firm brings out the strength that was hidden in your nature, and He dwells with you even at your lowest. `LT2·Q26` · `CA·Q36` · `INY·Q17`
- *"The opposition is too strong for me."* → Its existence is only concealment. It is there so that you will bring out the powers you have already been given, and nothing is asked beyond the strength given. `LS1·Q17` · `LS1·Q18` · `LS1·Q22` · `INY·Q15`
- *"It keeps getting darker, so I must be failing."* → The closer the work is to its goal, the harder the opposition pushes. Growing resistance is a sign the thing matters. `INY·Q16` · `INY·Q22`
- *"My understanding and feeling are gone and the desire is stronger than ever, so I've lost my spiritual life."* → This is what a trial is: understanding and feeling are taken away, desire is added, and bare faith is left. It is the shape of a test, not proof of decline. `BT·Q32`
- *"I have nothing of my own left to stand on."* → When the soul's own lamp fails, a point beyond reason, never extinguished, stands in its place. It can dwell even in total darkness without any feeling. `SO·Q08` · `SO·Q05` · `SO·Q30`

1. **Standing when there is nothing to stand with** · *from Biurei Tanya, Sha'arei Orah, Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus* (1) Name it as a trial: understanding and feeling have been withdrawn. Do not take the darkness as a verdict. (2) Do not wait for feeling to come back before acting. (3) Remember the point beneath: 'I don't want to be cut off from God.' (4) Govern what you do and say even while the heart pulls the other way. (5) Keep the plain good act, and take its joy from having done it. (6) Draw strength from learning, which gives the power to succeed. *When:* In the darkest stretches, day by day. *For:* Someone whose inner resources feel spent, facing inner resistance or temptation. *Not for:* Anyone in real outside danger or abuse. It is about inner resistance, not a reason to endure harm. It is also not for someone in fresh grief or depression who would hear 'test' as blame. `BT·Q32` · `BT·Q07` · `BT·Q11` · `SO·Q23` · `SO·Q22` · `SO·Q08` · `INY·Q26`

- From *"This trial is a burden or punishment; God has abandoned me."* to **A trial is a hiding, and inside it is Godliness. Before the test your power to overcome lay hidden in your nature; standing up to it brings that power out.** The obstacle becomes a shell around fruit. Standing firm breaks it and brings out strength that was only latent. *(Likkutei Torah, Chanah Ariel)* `LT2·Q26` · `CA·Q36` - From *"The opposition is too strong, and it keeps growing."* to **It has no true existence apart from concealment. It is there so you bring out powers already given, the demand is matched to your strength, and growing resistance shows the goal is near.** Demand and ability are matched from above, so the resistance reads as a sign that the task matters, not proof of failure, and the person is not alone. *(Likkutei Sichos, Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus)* `LS1·Q17` · `LS1·Q18` · `LS1·Q22` · `INY·Q16` · `INY·Q15` · `INY·Q17` - From *"I've lost my spiritual life; the desire is stronger than ever."* to **This is what a test looks like: understanding and feeling removed, desire added, bare acceptance left.** Seeing the darkness as the form of a test keeps the person from despair and points him to the one faculty still available. *(Biurei Tanya)* `BT·Q32` - From *"I'm too weak, and I have nothing of my own to stand on."* to **When the lamp fails, a point beyond reason stands for you, and joy can rest even in darkness without feeling.** The essential point is higher than the lamp and never fully swallowed. *(Sha'arei Orah)* `SO·Q08` · `SO·Q30` · `SO·Q05`

**Where the works agree.** The obstacle does not have the last word. It is a hiding or a test, and the strength needed is already present and hidden in the person. None of the works reads the resistance as proof of one's worthlessness or of abandonment.

**Where they differ.** They differ on where the strength comes from. Likkutei Sichos, Chanah Ariel and Likkutei Torah: it is revealed by the fight itself. Inyanah: new strength is given from above as the darkness grows. Sha'arei Orah: a point beyond reason rises only when one's own resources fail. Biurei Tanya is the starkest: the test is precisely the removal of feeling. Likkutei Sichos goes furthest in denying the opposition any real existence.

**Order and timing.** First name the darkness as a test, not a verdict (the reframes). Then keep the plain act and remember the point beneath. Draw strength from learning. The evidence gives almost no ordering beyond this.

- 'The opposition has no true existence' must never be used to minimise real danger, illness, poverty or abuse, or to keep someone in a harmful situation. - Calling suffering a 'test' can feel like God inflicting pain. It is not for fresh grief or trauma. - 'God asks only according to the strength He gives' can shame someone who has collapsed ('so I failed a test I could have passed'). Use it with great care with the depressed. - Language about giving one's life and self-sacrifice must not reach anyone suicidal.
