Sefer Yetzirah (the Book of Formation) is the oldest book of Jewish mysticism: 32 paths of wisdom, ten sefirot and twenty-two letters by which God made the world. The Ramak wrote a commentary on it as "gates" 14 to 19 of a larger work. Most of it explains the sefirot and the letters. Chapter 2 is long tables of letter-pairs (lines 231-331), which are technical and not for the guide.

What the guide needs is in chapter 1 and the end: the ten sefirot are "bli mah" (without what); the prophets saw them only "like a flash of lightning"; the mind must "run and return"; God is one "not as one that is counted"; and the three witnesses (world, year, soul) lead back to the One.

## 2. The unity of God in this book

**Not one as in counting.** God is called one, "and so the careful ones said: not as one that is counted" (ולזה אמרו מהמדקדקים לא כאחד המנוי, line 46). A person calls a thing "one" when he reaches its edges; Ein Sof has no edges, "so it is said of Him: not as a grasped one" (לכן נאמר עליו לא כאחד המושג, line 46). So His oneness is said only "by the way of negation: there is no second to Him" (אלא על דרך השלילה דהיינו ואין לו שני, line 46).

**The sefirot are one with Him and with each other.** They are His garments, so they must be close to Him. They are tied end to beginning and beginning to end, "and even more: in one sefirah itself there is direct light and returning light" (וגדולה מזו כי אפילו במדה אחד בעצמה יש בה אור ישר ואור חוזר, line 46). At the end of the book: "He is one, and His emanations are one, united in oneness" (שהוא אחד ונאצליו אחד מיוחדים באחדות, line 521).

**Like a flame on a coal.** The flame has a root above in fire and a root below in the coal, "and from its great desire for its roots it rises and falls and is never still" (ומרוב חשקה ביסודותיה עולה ויורדת ולעולם אינה שוקטת, line 45). So the light of the sefirot runs up and down without end.

**Something from Nothing.** Chochmah is called Yesh (Something) and comes from Keter, called Ayin (Nothing): "this is Something from Nothing" (דהיינו החכמה הנק' י"ש מכתר הנק' אי"ן, והיינו י"ש מאי"ן, line 228).

**One will, many acts.** Why is the world so many things if it came from one word? The soul is a model: it wants many things at once, "a unified power in the soul, able to do all it wants in a moment" (הרי כח אחדיי בנפש ובידה לפעול כל חפציה ברגע, line 335), but the body can do only one thing after another. So God "at the time of making, makes all creation" at once (אבל בעת העשיה עושה את כל היצור, line 334); the many is only how it looks from below.

**He rules even the conflict.** When the stars and seasons and organs fight each other, do not think they run themselves. "By His will are their acts, their order and their conflict" (ועל רצונו פעולתם והנהגתם ומחלוקתם, line 519).

## 3. States of consciousness

- **A flash and gone** (new, RK-S34). What the prophets saw of the sefirot was "like a flash of lightning": "a light that sparkles and does not stand still to be looked at, but shows and hides" (ואפשר כי בזק זה אורו דבר מתנוצץ בלתי עומד בקיום להביט בו, אלא נגלה ומתעלם, line 39). The state: a moment of real light that cannot be held. The mistake is to grab it. Links S19 (after the high), S35 (the edge of knowing), and RK-S19 (too bright to see).
- **Run and return.** The Ramak reads the angels' "running and returning" as "running to look, and returning out of awe" (פירוש רצוא להביט ושוב מהיראה, line 42). They "run to leave their place to gaze at the upper glory, and return out of humility to their place" (רצים לצאת ממחיצתן להסתכל בכבוד עליון, ושבים מחמת ענוה אל מקומם, line 50). Both movements are holy. Links S47 (running) and S48 (returning).
- **The question without an answer.** Thinking about Binah is allowed but speaking of it is not, "for it is called Mi (Who), meaning a question without an answer" (ועם כל זה ההרהור מותר בבינה, שכן נקראת היא מ"י פירוש שאלה בלא תשובה, line 49). A state: to live inside a true question and not rush to close it. Links S35 and S36 (faith above knowing).

## 4. Meditation and practice

**RK-T45. Close the mouth, return to the place** (new). Sefer Yetzirah says: "close your mouth from speaking and your heart from thinking, and if your mouth runs to speak and your heart to think, return to the Place" (ואם רץ פיך לדבר ולבך להרהר שוב למקום, line 48). The Ramak explains "bli mah": "a person should close his mouth and not add speech about them, so as not to make them physical" (שעם כל זה ראוי לאדם לבלום פיו וראוי שלא להוסיף הדבור בעניינם להגשים, line 20); they are "without a grasped essence" (ואמר שהם בלי מהות מושג, line 20). The practice: when the mind reaches past where it can go, stop. First stop speaking. Then, at the highest point, stop thinking. Then return "to the place", which the Ramak reads as Chesed and below, your own station, where speech and action are allowed (line 50). Ours: run up in contemplation, then come back to kindness and deeds. Links edge, quieting, and S35.

**RK-T46. The three witnesses** (new). World, year and soul each testify alone that God is one (lines 491-493). Take any one of them. In the soul: twelve functions (seeing, hearing, and so on). Follow them back to their causes: seven, then three, "and when we follow their causes further, we find one cause alone, the Cause of all causes and Reason of all reasons" (וכאשר נרדוף עוד אחר סבתם נמצא להם סבה אחת לבד, שהוא מסבב כל הסבות ועילת כל העילות, line 493). Do the same with the year (months, days, seasons) or the world. The Ramak says this proves "the oneness of the Emanator" (אמנם עתה במשנה זו בא להכריח על אחדות המאציל, line 492). Links cheshbon and before_whom.

**Believe He watches through all of it.** "Let all your intent be to believe in the Emanator's care through His emanations, and do not remove His care from them" (שכל כוונתך יהיה להאמין בהשגחת המאציל על ידי נאצליו ולא תסלק השגחתו מהם, line 29). The sefirot are not a screen between you and Him. They are how He cares.

**Bow to the One who is not grasped.** The prophets bowed before the Throne, "their intent to the Rider on the Throne, though He was not grasped by them" (וכוונתם אל הרוכב על הכסא, עם היותו בלתי מושג אליהם, line 42). Aim past every image. Links before_whom.

## 5. The big human questions

**Can I understand God?** No, and the Ramak says why: "how can a bounded thing grasp the unbounded?" (ואיך ישיג בעל תכלית אל בלתי בעל תכלית, line 40). The effect cannot surround its cause. What is possible: the flash (line 39), the question without answer (line 49), and bowing (line 42).

**Why is there evil?** So that a human can choose. Angels have only good and get no reward; animals have no choice. The person who has both and chooses good rises higher than the angels. "This is why the Holy One created evil: it completes the good and raises him above the angels" (אם כן זהו טעם שברא הקב"ה הרע שהוא משלים הטוב ומעלהו למעלה מהמלאכים, line 514).

**Is God harsh?** The seven "double" letters can be soft (mercy) or hard (judgment). "When those below cause a hiding of the face, the traits of mercy turn to judgment, but their root was only for mercy" (אמנם כאשר יגרמו התחתונים הסתר הפנים הם מתהפכין מדות רחמים לדין, אבל עיקרם לא היה אלא לרחמים, line 389). Hardness is a surface; the root is kind.

**What did Abraham find?** He looked, saw, searched and understood, and then the Lord of all revealed Himself, "kissed him on his head: the secret of the kiss, spirit with spirit" (נשקו על ראשו - סוד הנשיקה רוח ברוח, line 526). The end of seeking is not an answer but closeness.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Run and return.** The Tanya uses Sefer Yetzirah's "run and return" for the life-force of every hour, which "returns to its source, in the secret of 'run and return' in Sefer Yetzirah" (והמשכת החיות שבשעה שלפניה חוזרת למקורה בסוד רצוא ושוב שבספר יצירה, Tanya line 623). The Ramak uses it for the mind: run to gaze, return in humility. Chabad Chassidus later makes ratzo and shov (longing to leave, and coming back to serve) the core rhythm of the soul. The Ramak's reading ("return out of humility to your place") is its early form.
- **End fixed in the beginning.** Line 43-46 (direct and returning light, even inside one sefirah) is what the Tanya cites: "'their end fixed in their beginning', which the Arizal's writings call direct light and returning light" (נעוץ תחלתן בסופן וסופן בתחלתן, ובכתבי האריז"ל מכונה בשם אור ישר ואור חוזר, Tanya line 1517). The Ramak had the teaching before the Arizal; the Tanya receives it through the Arizal.
- **Something from Nothing.** The Ramak places yesh me'ayin between Keter and Chochmah (line 228). The Gate of Unity applies it to every created thing every moment: "the coming into being of all worlds, upper and lower, from Nothing to Something" (כי התהוות כל העולמות עליונים ותחתונים מאין ליש, Tanya line 302), held only by His word. The Ramak's is a map of the sefirot; the Tanya's is a meditation on this table and this hand.
- **"Not one as counted."** Same as Ohr Ne'erav line 432 and the Gate of Unity: His oneness is not a number. Full agreement. The Ramak stresses negation ("no second"); the Tanya stresses that nothing else truly exists ("ein od").
- **Tzimtzum.** Not here. The flash-and-hide of line 39 is the closest thing: light that shows and hides.
