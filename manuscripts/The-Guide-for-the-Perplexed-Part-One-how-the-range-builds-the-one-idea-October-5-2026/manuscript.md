## The shape of the range

Part One is a single long argument that ends in one sentence: G-d is one, and one in a way no word can hold. It gets there in three movements.

1. **Clearing the words (chapters 1-49).** The Torah says G-d has an image, a face, a back, a place, a throne; that He comes down, goes up, sits, stands, sees, hears. The Rambam walks through each word and shows it has a second, spiritual meaning. "Image" is the mind, not a shape. "Near" is knowing, not place. "Face" is His true being, which no one sees. The point of the whole survey is stated early: "there is no unity except by removing bodiliness" (line 240, I:35). A body is made of parts, and what has parts is not one.
2. **The attributes (chapters 50-70).** Having removed the body, he removes something subtler: qualities. G-d has no wisdom "in Him", no power "in Him", no life "in Him", as things added to Him. He is wise not by a wisdom, alive not by a life, one not by a oneness. Everything true we can say is either a description of what He does, or a denial of what He lacks. Here stand the two teachings Chassidus quotes most: the negative attributes (I:58-60) and "He is the knower, the known and the knowing" (I:68).
3. **Against the theologians (chapters 71-76).** He rejects the Kalam, the Muslim and Karaite theologians who proved G-d by first "proving" that the world was created, and who to do so denied that nature has any lasting order. He proves G-d's existence, unity and bodilessness in a way that holds even if the world were eternal.

## 1. The unity of G-d in this range

**One means simple, not "a single item".** "There is no oneness at all except in believing one simple essence, with no composition in it and no multiplicity of matters... from whatever side you look at it, and in whatever way you examine it, you find it one" (line 324, Guide 1:51:4):

> ואין אחדות כלל אלא בהאמין עצם אחד פשוט אין הרכבה בו ולא ריבוי ענינים (line 324)

Even "one" is too much: oneness is itself a kind of quantity, so "He is one, not by a oneness" (line 375, 1:57:4):

and "He exists, not by an existence" (line 373, 1:57:2):

**The attributes are not in Him.** "Know that He has no essential attribute in any way" (line 318, 1:50:2). Whoever says "one, with many attributes" has said one with his mouth and believes many in his thought, "like the Christians who say He is one but three" (line 319):

> כבר אמר שהוא אחד – במלתו והאמינו רבים – במחשבתו (line 319)

He is not even related to the world in the ordinary sense. Two things can be compared only if they share a kind; He shares none. Even the word "exists" is said of Him and of us "in complete equivocation", the same word for two unrelated things (line 340, 1:52:14):

> כי המציאה אמנם תאמר אצלנו עליו ית׳ ועל זולתו בשיתוף גמור (line 340)

**Negation is the true way to describe Him.** "Know that describing G-d by negations is the true description" (line 381, 1:58:2). A negative attribute tells nothing of what He is; it only removes something wrong. The Rambam gives the ladder (line 385): we proved something must exist; we say "He exists", meaning His absence is impossible; "living", meaning not dead; "not a body"; "eternal", meaning nothing caused Him; "able, wise, willing", meaning not weak, not ignorant, not confused or neglectful, since the world runs in order; "one", meaning no multiplicity. "We grasp only that He is, not what He is" (line 384):

> ולא נשיג אלא ישותו בלבד לא מהותו (line 384)

The end of the chapter is a hymn (line 388): when minds look at His essence their grasp turns to short-fall, and when tongues try to praise Him every flourish turns to weakness.

> ישתבח מי אשר בהשקיף השכלים עצמו – תשוב השגתם קיצור השגה וידיעה (line 388)

**Only the Name is His.** Every other name is taken from an action (merciful, gracious, judge). The four-letter Name alone names Him and not an act; before the world "there was only the Holy One and His Name" (line 409, 1:61:3, quoting Pirkei deRabbi Eliezer). At Ehyeh asher Ehyeh the subject is repeated as its own predicate, "as if to show that the one described is the description itself", which is "He exists, not by an existence" (line 423, 1:63:3).

**He is the knower, the known and the knowing (I:68).** The philosophers say He is "the intellect, the one who knows, and the thing known, and these three are one thing with no multiplicity in it", and the Rambam adds that he wrote this in the Mishneh Torah "because it is a foundation of our faith" (line 443, 1:68:1):

> שהוא השכל והמשכיל והמושכל, ושאלו השלשה ענינים בו ית׳ הם ענין אחד, אין רבוי בו (line 443)

His proof is from how any mind works. When I understand the form of a tree, the understood form *is* my mind in act; there is no second thing, "for the intellect is nothing other than the thing understood" (line 445). So in every act of real understanding, knower, knowing and known are one (line 446). In us this happens only now and then, as we pass from potential to act. In G-d, who is always in act, it is always so (line 449). And, a line Chassidus builds on without naming him: "in us too the knower, the intellect and the known are one thing, when we have intellect in act" (line 450, 1:68:8):

> ובנו גם כן המשכיל והשכל והמושכל דבר אחד, כשיהיה לנו שכל בפועל (line 450)

**The world exists only by Him, now.** In I:69 he calls G-d the agent, the form and the end of the world. "Form" does not mean He is in the world like a soul in a body. It means: as a thing is what it is by its form, "so by the Creator's existence everything exists", and He keeps it standing by what is called "overflow" (shefa). "If the Creator could cease to exist, all existence would cease" (line 456, 1:69:5). That is why He is called Chai HaOlamim, "the life of the world". He then attacks the theologians who said the world would survive its maker, as a chest survives the carpenter: "it is false that the one who keeps drawing it would cease and the thing drawn remain" (line 459, 1:69:8). This is the same argument the Tanya makes against the craftsman and the vessel in Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah chapter 2. The Rambam does not say that the world is nothing before Him. He says it cannot stand without Him.

**The world as one person.** In I:72 he asks the reader to picture the whole cosmos, heavens and elements, as one living individual, with the outer sphere as its heart. The picture helps prove "that the One created one" (line 497). But the comparison fails at one point: G-d is not a power in the world's body. He is separate from all of it, yet His governance and providence reach "every part of it, even the smallest and lowest" (line 508). The chapter ends, "Blessed is He whose perfection has overcome us" (line 508).

## 2. States of consciousness in this range

The Rambam does not describe feelings in the Chassidic way. But he describes places a person stands, and they are real.

- **The flash of lightning.** Truth shines "until we think it is day", and then our natures and habits hide it again, "and we are like one on whom lightning flashes time after time, in a very dark night" (line 33). Moses was "as if in constant light, and the night was like day for him" (line 34). Some see the flash once in a whole night; some never see even a glowing stone. *Inside:* a moment of clarity that does not stay, and knowing it will return. *Ties to:* S51 (The sudden cry) in its brevity and S19 (After the high) in its fading; closest to current C5 (Running and returning). *Proposed new state* **R-S1 "The flash that comes and goes"**: see JSON in `RAMBAM.md`.
- **Standing at the doubt.** "If you stop at the doubt, and do not persuade yourself that there is proof where there is none... you have reached human perfection, and you will be like Rabbi Akiva, who entered in peace and left in peace" (line 210, 1:32:3). The one who pushes past his limit becomes Acher, and "imaginations grow strong" in him. *Inside:* calm at the edge of what the mind can do, without forcing, without denying. *Ties to:* S35 (The edge of knowing), and technique `edge`. The Rambam's version adds a guard Chassidus also has: forcing the mind past its reach damages even what it could see (lines 208-209, the eye that strains at fine writing sees less).
- **Silence as praise.** "Silence before You is praise" (line 393, 1:59:5, reading Psalm 65). Every word of praise carries some defect when said of Him, "so silence is more fitting, and to be content with what minds grasp". *Inside:* the praise that stops speaking because words would lessen what it sees. *Ties to:* S35 and S56 (The self quieted), but the Rambam's silence is a silence of the mind that has understood, not of a self that has quieted. *Proposed new state* **R-S2 "Silent praise"**.
- **Near and far by knowing.** "Nearness to Him is by grasping Him, and distance from Him is for one who does not know Him" (line 144, 1:18:5). The one who knows Him "is the one who is wanted and brought near" (line 353). *Inside:* closeness felt as clarity, not as place. *Ties to:* S27 (Knowing that holds).
- **Knowing and the known as one.** Line 450 is a description of a state: the moment when the mind is not *looking at* an idea but *is* the idea in act. *Ties to:* S79 (Learning: mind clothed in His wisdom), which the Tanya (chapter 5) builds on the same structure.
- **Belief that is only said.** Many "keep beliefs they do not picture at all" (line 317). "Near in their mouth and far from their kidneys" (line 320). *Ties to:* S16 (Knowing it, not feeling it) and S17 (Feeling fake), with a Rambamist twist: the gap is between saying and *picturing in the mind*, not between mind and heart.

## 3. Meditation and practice in this range

- **Belief means picturing, not saying.** "Belief is not what is said with the mouth but what is pictured in the soul" (line 317, 1:50:1). His goal: "you will be one who pictures the unity of the Name, not one who says it with his mouth" (line 320). His practice sentence is Psalm 4: "say it in your heart upon your bed, and be silent" (line 320, line 393). *Ties to:* technique `depth`, `hold`. This is the Rambam's version of "know today and return it to your heart".
- **The negation ladder as contemplation.** Chapter 58 is a meditation in steps: say "He exists", and mean "His absence is impossible"; "He lives", and mean "He is not dead"; and so on to "one", meaning "no multiplicity" (line 385). Chapter 59 says the ladder has real rungs: each proven negation brings you "nearer to grasping" (line 389), and chapter 60 gives the ship parable: one who knows it is not a mineral, not an animal, not a plant, not flat, not round, has almost pictured a ship (line 400). "Here is the way; if you walk in it you will come near Him; walk in it if you wish" (line 402), and "be glad in it". *Guard:* "negate by proof, not by speech alone" (line 401). *Ties to:* technique `edge` (hasagas hashlilah). *Proposed new technique* **R-T1 "The negation ladder"**.
- **Preparation before seeking G-d.** Before one turns the mind to G-d one must train in the sciences, "refine his traits a great deal, and kill his imaginative desires", and then "be ashamed, hold back, and stand still until he rises step by step" (line 92, 1:5:2). Moses hid his face and was rewarded; the nobles of Israel rushed in and saw a body (lines 93-94). The ones given the secrets are humble, "with a worried heart", "humility, submission and great fear of sin joined to wisdom" (line 231). Only a person "trained in the virtues, calm and settled" can reach true understanding (line 230). *Ties to:* the Chassidic insistence that contemplation needs prior avodah and bitul; closest to `before_whom` and `discern`.
- **Do not over-strain.** Straining the eye weakens it; straining the mind confuses it; honey eaten beyond measure is vomited (lines 208-211). *Guard* for every contemplation practice.
- **Guard against the imagination.** "The work of the imagination is not the work of the intellect but its opposite" (line 555, 1:73:45). The imagination can never free itself from matter. Much false theology comes from treating what can be pictured as possible and what cannot as impossible. *Against:* technique `picturing` (the Piaseczner's tziyur) and any practice that pictures G-d. The Rambam allows only what the mind grasps.
- **Imitating the actions.** The thirteen attributes are His actions, not traits; the leader should act "merciful and gracious" or "avenging" as needed, but without being moved, "for every being-moved is bad" (line 358). "The height of a person's rank is to become like Him as far as possible" (line 362). *Ties to:* technique `act`; the Chassidic reading of "as He is merciful, so be you merciful".
- **Prayer: say little.** He praises Rabbi Chanina, who silenced the man adding praises, and warns against poets who pile up praises of G-d (lines 394-397): keep to the words the Men of the Great Assembly set. *Ties to:* the Chassidic tension between the fixed text and one's own words (`own_words`).

## 4. The big questions in this range

- **Why we are here.** The image of G-d in us is the intellect (line 71); the goal of all actions is to know Him (line 263, on "with all your heart"). The purpose of everything is "to become like His perfection as far as possible" (line 457).
- **Providence and its absence.** When Scripture says "I will return to My place", it means providence withdraws, and then a person is "abandoned and left a target for whatever may happen" (line 165, 1:23:3). This is the Rambam's reading of hester panim (the hiding of the face): not a hidden presence but a lifted guard. The Chassidic reading (S68, "The hiding seen as His") is very different; see `RAMBAM.md`.
- **Shelter.** "Under whose wings you have come to take shelter" means "under His covering" (line 281). A small but real line for the guide's "being held" (C4).
- **Why G-d gives.** The world differs from a body at one point: the heart gains from the limbs, but in the whole, the one who gives "gains nothing back... like the gift of a kind and gracious giver, done out of generosity, not in hope of return" (line 506).

## 5. Where Chassidus reads this range, and where it departs

(Details and full verified Chabad lines in `RAMBAM.md` section 4.)

- **I:68 "knower, known and knowing".** Quoted by the Tanya four times: chapter 2 (the soul from His wisdom, "He and His wisdom are one, as the Rambam wrote"), chapter 4 (Torah as His wisdom), chapter 48 gloss, and Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah chapters 7-9. The Rambam states it for G-d in Himself. The Tanya (Shaar HaYichud 9) limits it: it applies only after the tzimtzum, to the vessels of Atzilut; before that G-d is not in the category of knowledge at all.
- **I:58-60 negative attributes.** Read directly by Likkutei Torah (Pekudei), the Rayatz (Sefer HaMaamarim 5689-5693), and the Rashab (Ayin Beis, and Samach Vav's "hasagas hashlilah"). The Chassidic reading keeps the method but places it: negation is the highest grasp of the *revealed light*, and the Essence is beyond even that.
- **I:69 "if the Creator ceased, all would cease".** Parallels the Tanya's attack on the craftsman image (Shaar HaYichud 2) but stops short of it: for the Rambam the world *depends*; for the Tanya the world *is nothing* in its source.
- **I:73 Kalam's constant re-creation.** The Rambam mocks the view that G-d creates every accident anew each instant. Chassidus teaches constant renewal from the word of G-d (Shaar HaYichud 1-2). The two are not the same: the Kalam denied nature; Chassidus keeps nature and says its very being is renewed. But on this point Chassidus stands nearer the view the Rambam called a joke than to the Rambam.

## What this range adds

- A clean, argued account of *why* G-d cannot be pictured, which the guide can use as a guard for every contemplation. - The negation ladder as a real practice with a stated goal ("come near") and a stated joy ("be glad in it"). - The flash of lightning (line 33) as an honest picture of how understanding of G-d comes and goes. - Knowing as union (line 450), the root of the Tanya's "wondrous union".

## Where the thread is thin

- Chapters 1-49 are almost wholly word study; their inner content is small and repeated. - Chapters 73-76 are a critique of Kalam. Their only inner line is the Rambam's respect for nature as it is (line 476: "what exists does not follow opinions; true opinions follow what exists"). - Nowhere in this range does the Rambam speak of the heart moving. Love and awe appear only as knowing. See Part Three for his fullest account.
