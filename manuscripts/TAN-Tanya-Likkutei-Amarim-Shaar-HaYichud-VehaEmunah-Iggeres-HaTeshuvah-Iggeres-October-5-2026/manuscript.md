*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A handbook for the "beinoni", the in-between person: someone whose evil still has its full strength inside but never gets to rule his deeds, speech or thought. Likkutei Amarim maps the two souls and gives a graded method: contemplation that produces love and awe, rules of timing for sadness and bitterness, and ways to handle intrusive thoughts, pride and love of others. Iggeres HaTeshuvah redefines return as leaving the sin, not fasting. Iggeres HaKodesh is pastoral letters: suffering and trust, humility after being saved, charity, anger, and a rebuke of seeking worldly counsel.

**How much of it is medicine.** core. Most of Likkutei Amarim and Iggeres HaTeshuvah is written directly to a person's inner struggle. Shaar HaYichud and many Iggeres HaKodesh letters are cosmology or Kabbalah, and those parts were not mined.

**Its model of the person.** A person has two souls. The animal soul is the "I" of the ordinary person. The divine soul is a real portion of God, in exile inside it. Each soul has intellect, emotions and three garments: thought, speech and deed. The mind rules the heart by nature, so choice works on the garments, not on feelings. Feelings are born from sustained understanding, or they lie hidden in the mind and are drawn out by habit. Change is a daily war, won by joy and alacrity and lost to heaviness. Everything depends on timing: reckoning belongs at a fixed hour, and joy belongs in service.

**Its philosophy.** God re-creates everything from nothing at every moment. So nothing is truly bad, and what looks bad is a good too great to be seen. The purpose of creation is a dwelling for God in this lowest world, built by ordinary deeds. God's mercy has no limit, so forgiving once and forgiving a thousand times are equal. Every Jewish soul holds a hidden love that faith cannot deny, so no one is outside the path.

**What it adds.** Tanya is the founding operating manual. It gives an achievable identity (the beinoni) instead of perfection, rules the garments rather than feelings, and makes timing a clinical tool: sadness now is a trick, bitterness only at a fixed hour, joy always in service. It separates sadness from bitterness, locates self-contempt in the body alone, strips self-torment from teshuvah, and grounds trust in suffering on creation from nothing at every moment.

## I understand it all and my heart feels nothing, and when I try to feel, it feels fake

Years of learning and contemplation with no felt love or awe. Words of love in prayer feel manufactured. He wonders if his deeds without feeling are worth anything.

**What is really going on.** He has taken felt emotion as the proof that love exists. Tanya says the love is real but hidden, conceived in the mind and never born into the heart for some souls. Its job is to drive the deed. A feeling that seems imagined is still true in itself.

**Program: Draw out the hidden love and let it carry the deed.** (1) Contemplate God's greatness with the mind, as far as your understanding reaches. (2) Do not wait for the feeling. Let the understanding bring you to study or a mitzvah, with mouth and heart agreeing. (3) Say words of love (e.g. at the Shema) with the mind on their meaning, even if it feels imagined. (4) Count the deed done with understanding as service. The habit may become nature over time. *When:* Daily at the Shema and before study or a mitzvah; the contemplation at a fixed time. Expect the artificial texture for a long time. *For:* Those who understand but feel nothing, and those whose feeling seems fake. *Not for:* Never force breakthrough feeling, and never send this person to the ch.29 crushing. A ceiling is not a blockage. `TAN·Q04` · `TAN·Q17`

**Reframe.** From *"If I don't feel it, it isn't real. And when I try, it's fake."* to **The love is hidden in the mind and heart. It is real, and its work is to move you to the deed. Even when it seems imagined, it is true in itself.** Tanya separates the love's truth from its felt presence. The hidden love lives in chochmah, above feeling. So the deed done with understanding already counts, and repeated speech draws the love out until habit becomes nature. `TAN·Q04` · `TAN·Q17`

## Everyone thinks I'm good; inside I'm not. I'm a fraud and I'll never be a tzaddik

The inner cravings are still at full strength. Praise feels like a lie. Years of effort have not removed the evil, so what is the point.

**What is really going on.** He is grading himself against the tzaddik's rank, where the evil is gone, and that rank is a gift. The beinoni's measure is open to every person at every hour: the evil stays at full strength, and what he governs is thought, speech and deed.

**Program: The beinoni's governance: count the turning, not the craving.** (1) Rename the state: the evil at full strength is the beinoni's normal condition, not proof of fraud. (2) When a craving or thought rises outside prayer, turn your mind from it at once. (3) Count each turning as a commandment kept, and rejoice in your portion. (4) Keep governing thought, speech and deed. Do not measure yourself by whether the craving still comes. *When:* At every arising, outside prayer; and when praise lands and shame rises. *For:* Those who feel like frauds or who despair of changing. *Not for:* Not a promise that cravings will vanish; that is a gift. Not for scrupulous/OCD patterns without care (see cautions). `TAN·Q01` · `TAN·Q03` · `TAN·Q08` · `TAN·Q09`

**Reframe.** From *"The evil in me is as strong as ever, so I'm a fraud and I'll never get anywhere."* to **You are a beinoni. The evil at full strength is your normal condition. What is yours to win is thought, speech and deed, and every person can be a beinoni at every hour.** The word 'rasha' is moved from the inner feeling to who rules the garments. The task becomes one choice can actually do, which removes the despair of chasing a rank that is a gift. `TAN·Q01` · `TAN·Q03`

## I'm crushed by sadness over what I am and what I've done

A heaviness that reads as honesty. It arrives mid-work or mid-prayer and saps all movement.

**What is really going on.** Sadness is a heart gone stone-dead, not remorse. Sadness about spiritual things in the middle of business is a trick of the evil inclination that leads to falling. Bitterness is alive but belongs to a fixed hour. The verdict belongs to the body alone.

**Program: Sadness is not for now.** (1) Recognise that sadness arriving mid-work or mid-prayer, even over spiritual matters, is the yetzer's trick. (2) Set it aside deliberately and name a later fixed time for any reckoning. (3) Do the next obligation with alacrity. Victory comes only from joy and an open heart. (4) If the verdict on yourself comes up, say: all this is I alone, the body; within me is a real portion of God. *When:* Immediately, whenever heaviness arrives during work or service. *For:* Anyone slowed by guilt or heaviness. *Not for:* Not a way to avoid real repair of harm done to others. `TAN·Q05` · `TAN·Q07` · `TAN·Q14`

**Program: The reckoning hour: bitterness, then joy.** (1) At a set time, not during prayer or business, make an honest reckoning of your deeds. (2) Contemplate how the sins exiled the divine soul, and let the heart break with living bitterness, not deadness. (3) Where the heart resists, lower its self-importance (ch.29). (4) Then ask mercy from the depth of the heart. (5) Close with the double consolation, and remove the sadness the moment the reckoning ends. *When:* An hour each day or night before midnight (Iggeres HaTeshuvah ch.7), or at the least weekly before Shabbos; ch.29 says 'fixed times'. *For:* A heart that is alive but coarse or resisting, with a stable mood. *Not for:* Never for someone flat, depressed, or self-hating. For them it deepens the sadness it meant to break. `TAN·Q11` · `TAN·Q13` · `TAN·Q20`

**Reframe.** From *"Feeling terrible about myself is the honest, serious thing."* to **Sadness is a dead stone heart, and in the middle of work it is the yetzer's ploy. Bitterness is alive, brief, and belongs at a set hour.** In Tanya's psychology, victory needs alacrity born of joy, and heaviness loses the wrestle. Moving grief to its proper hour keeps the honesty and removes the paralysis. `TAN·Q05` · `TAN·Q07` · `TAN·Q13`

**Reframe.** From *"If I'm really that bad, I have no right to joy."* to **All that badness is 'I alone', the body and the animal soul. Within you is a real portion of God, in exile, and freeing it is a joy.** The verdict and the joy are addressed to two different selves. No fact has to be denied, so the joy that comes after bitterness is stable. `TAN·Q14`

## I fell again. I've done this so many times, how can I be forgiven?

After a repeated sin he feels his standing is gone and forgiveness is used up.

**What is really going on.** Return is decided in the heart: to leave the sin. God's forgiveness comes from limitless mercy, where once and a thousand times are equal. He should trust that forgiveness comes at once when asked, without any doubt.

**Program: Teshuvah without self-torment.** (1) Decide with a whole heart to leave the sin and not return to it. That is the commandment of return. (2) Confess and ask forgiveness. (3) Trust at once, without doubt, that He forgives, and no less the thousandth time. (4) Do not fast if it could harm your health. Give charity in its place. (5) Keep the sin 'before you' only as a guard against pride and anger, and serve in joy. *When:* Return is immediate; the confession daily; a deeper reckoning at the fixed hour (G05). *For:* Anyone after a fall, especially one prone to self-punishment. *Not for:* Never prescribe fasting or bodily penance; the text forbids harmful fasts. `TAN·Q18` · `TAN·Q19` · `TAN·Q21` · `TAN·Q22` · `TAN·Q23`

**Reframe.** From *"I've done it too many times; there's no forgiveness left for me."* to **For God there is no difference between once and a thousand times. Trust at once, without doubt, that you are forgiven when you ask.** Forgiveness comes from limitless mercy, where large and small numbers are equal. Trusting it is itself part of teshuvah and makes joyful service possible again. `TAN·Q21` · `TAN·Q22`

## I can't stop worrying about money, the children, health

Worry follows him into prayer and work. He is heavy and slow.

**What is really going on.** Worry is a form of the heaviness that makes a wrestler lose. It is cured by receiving everything with joy as hidden good that the eye cannot see, not by first solving the cause.

**Program: Worry: receive it as hidden good, and not in the hour of service.** (1) Notice the worry as heaviness that will cost you the struggle. (2) Recall: 'this too is for good', a good that is hidden, not visible to the eye of flesh. (3) Receive what happens with the same joy as revealed good, out of love for nearness to God. (4) Return to the work or prayer with alacrity. *When:* At each arising of worry, especially during prayer and work. *For:* Those whose worry about livelihood, children or health invades their day. *Not for:* Not to be handed to someone in acute grief or crisis, and not a substitute for practical action or help. `TAN·Q05` · `TAN·Q06`

The sense of 'bad to me' loosens, and with it the sadness and complaint. `TAN·Q06` · `TAN·Q26` · `TAN·Q27`

## Something bad is happening to me and I'm sad and complaining

Loss, illness or poverty. He feels something is lacking and that God is against him.

**What is really going on.** Seen from its source, nothing bad descends from above; what looks bad is a good too great to grasp. Complaint shows he believes some bad has reached him. Suffering should be received face to face with a Father, in love.

**Program: Suffering: contemplate being from nothing, and accept it face to face.** (1) Contemplate deeply that you and everything are brought into being from nothing, at every moment, by His wisdom. (2) Ask: how can it occur to me that something bad is mine? What seems bad is good beyond grasp. (3) Turn to God in humility rather than fleeing to outside counsel for worldly matters. (4) Examine your deeds and see His love, like a king washing his only son with his own hands. *When:* In contemplation, at a settled time; again when complaint rises. *For:* A believer who wants to hold suffering inside faith and is steady enough to contemplate. *Not for:* Not for someone in fresh grief, trauma or depression. ## Things went well for me and I feel above everyone

*Its own term:* גסות הרוח / קטנתי מכל החסדים

Success, rescue or knowledge swells the heart. He looks down on others.

**What is really going on.** Pride is the kelipah's own stance. Holy kindness should make a person lower, because the nearer to God, the more one is as nothing. Kindness that swells pride is the kelipah's kind. And one has not reached the other's place.

**Program: Lowliness by exactness.** (1) When you are blessed or saved, say 'I am too small for all the kindnesses'. Each kindness brings nearness, and nearness demands more lowliness. (2) Before judging, ask about his place: his livelihood, what his eyes see, his temperament. (3) Ask whether you have fought a war as hard as his. (4) Refuse to lord it over others or speak against them. Answer softly. *When:* At the moment the verdict or the swelling arises, and at the fixed reckoning hour. *For:* The successful, the learned, the habitual judge. *Not for:* Not a mood of worthlessness; that is sadness, which the text rejects. Never used to excuse harmful conduct. `TAN·Q25` · `TAN·Q12`

**Reframe.** From *"I was saved and blessed, so I'm someone."* to **Every kindness from God should make you lower. The nearer you are to Him, the more you are as nothing.** Nearness is defined as nullification before God. So a blessing felt truly produces humility, and pride shows the kindness has gone to the kelipah's side. `TAN·Q25`

## Someone wronged me and I can't let go. I get angry

The grudge replays. Anger flares at insults or losses.

**What is really going on.** The rancour is a rising of the heart, and the mind by nature rules it. Anger is a moment when faith departs, because what happened to him was decreed from Heaven, whoever the messenger. Remembering one's own sins helps one accept slights without anger.

## I'm supposed to love every Jew and I feel nothing for whole kinds of people

Love stops at the edge of his own kind. Some people repel him.

## Cautions

- The ch.29 crushing and 'despicable in his own eyes' (Q11, Q20) are for a living but resisting heart. Given to a depressed or self-hating person, they deepen the despair Tanya itself forbids. - 'This too is for good' and 'no evil descends' (Q06, Q26) handed to someone in fresh grief or trauma is an intrusion. These are readings a person may reach, not verdicts to give. - Iggeres HaKodesh 22 (Q29) asks the sufferer to find sins that need cleansing. Never link a person's illness, loss or abuse to his sins; that breeds shame and self-blame. - 'Bear with him to the utmost' and 'render good to the guilty' (Q02) describe an inner response to a slight. They are never counsel for someone facing abuse, coercion or danger. - Calling complaint 'like denial' (Q27) and anger 'loss of faith' (Q30) can shame people who are suffering. Validate the pain first. Never use them to silence grief. - The rebuke of seeking worldly counsel (Q28) is about running to holy men for material advice. It must never discourage medical, psychological or professional help. - Commands to push thoughts out instantly, including thoughts likened to idolatry (Q10, Q32), may feed scrupulosity or OCD. Watch for compulsive checking. - Never prescribe fasting or bodily penance. The text itself forbids fasts that harm health (Q19), and the risk is high for people with eating disorders.
