**First, one line about danger.** If what you're carrying is real danger (thoughts of harming yourself or not wanting to be here, someone hurting or controlling you, fear of hurting someone, not eating or functioning at all, or racing, sleepless certainty about a special mission), stop the sitting. This isn't a meditation problem. The guide follows its crisis protocol and nothing else: it says plainly that this needs a person now, and helps you reach one (the guide's constitution, SAFETY). Everything below is for everything else.

## 1. "I sat, and nothing happened."

**Short answer:** Something did. You held the line. That was your part, and it counts.

Your mind is in your control and your heart isn't. When you contemplate, the Alter Rebbe says, love is born by itself, "in the mind, at least" [Tanya ch. 17]. If you can't bring it out into the heart, you can still hold it as a picture in your mind and act on it [Tanya ch. 44], and what you do is counted "as if" you did it with open love and awe [Tanya ch. 16]. The Tanya even says why it can feel like nothing: God is right there in you, "only his soul doesn't feel it, because of the screen of the body" [Tanya ch. 46]. The Piaseczner, in the ghetto, said the same of faith itself: a person "believes even then, only he doesn't feel it" [Esh Kodesh 578].

Also check three plain things. Did you **stay** long enough, and on **one** line? The length is different for every soul [Tanya ch. 42]. Did you go into **details**, or stay general? General contemplation only hovers around you [Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 6]. Did the heart just answer **late**? For some it comes gradually [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 753].

**What to do:** the same line tomorrow, a little deeper, and the one act today. Each person goes by their own measure [Tanya ch. 44].

## 2. "My mind won't stop."

**Short answer:** Don't fight it. Come back to the line, again and again, without arguing. Coming back *is* the meditation.

The Tanya: give the stray thought no answer and no argument; act as if you didn't hear it, and put more strength into your intention [Tanya ch. 28]. And don't get sad about it [Tanya ch. 28]. The Piaseczner agrees on not fighting: the more you try to push thoughts away, the stronger they get [Derekh HaMelekh 1551], and darkness is only driven out by light [Chovat HaTalmidim 249].

If the whole stream is wild, use his way of quieting first: stop, sit or lean on something, and watch the thoughts "with a restful heart, not with agitation and not with too much effort". They dim, and thin, "as if you swept your mind with a broom" [Hakhsharat 310-311]. If one thought or craving keeps grabbing you, look at it from the side for twenty seconds: "so this is what I'm thinking" [Hakhsharat 302]. A thought you look at straight on melts [Mevo HaShe'arim 390].

**Two ways, and when:** ignore the passing ones (the Tanya); look straight at a stubborn one (the Piaseczner). Both say: never fight. (Part I, "Where the sources disagree", no. 3.)

Also try the simple Piaseczner fixes: the day's worries set down on purpose for an hour [Tzav VeZeruz 64]; "I gird my loins..." said before you start [Chovat HaTalmidim 245]; the line said aloud; a different place [Chovat HaTalmidim 179].

## 3. "I feel it in my head, not in my heart."

**Short answer:** That's the most common place to be stuck, and there are three ways out: more time on the same line, bringing it down into something you can see and touch, and saying it with your mouth.

The Piaseczner put this gap in one sentence: "I knew this before too, but I only knew it" [Tzav VeZeruz 72]. Chabad's word for crossing it is *da'at*: binding the mind to the truth until it is as real as something you see with your eyes [Tanya ch. 42]. You don't cross it by explaining more. You cross it by:

- **Time.** Stay on the same line longer, and for more days. "The main thing is habit" [Tanya ch. 42].
- **Length.** Bring it down into this room and this problem (Part I, Stage 4). "Bring the matter close to yourself" [Kuntres HaTefillah §4].
- **The mouth.** "Put yourself into the words and the service, and the fervour will come by itself" [Mevo HaShe'arim 198]. "Even if he speaks with his mouth alone, through it his heart too will wake" [Esh Kodesh 504].

**Where the sources differ:** the Tanya comforts you. If the heart doesn't move, holding the thought and doing the deed still gives the mitzvah its wings [Tanya ch. 16]. Kuntres HaHitpa'alut is sterner: heart arousal is "the essence of the revelation of Godliness", and it warns those who pray dry (KUNTRES-HAHITPAALUT.md, "Where it differs"). The Piaseczner thinks most people should start from the heart and picture, not the mind [Chovat HaTalmidim 291]. **When each fits:** if you've been dry for a day or two, take the Tanya's comfort and keep going. If you've been dry for weeks, change the door: start from what's already moving in you, use a picture, and say it aloud.

> Tanya ch. 42 (line 644): «אלא העיקר הוא ההרגל, להרגיל דעתו ומחשבתו תמיד»
> Kuntres HaTefillah §4, OCR (line 106): «ולקרב לעצמו את הענין»
> Mevo HaShe'arim (line 198): «האיש יכניס עצמו בהדיבורים ובעבדות, וההתלהבות תבא ממילא»
> Esh Kodesh (line 504): «מ״מ ידבר בפיו בלבד, ועי״ז גם לבו יתעורר»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 291): «יותר יקל לאיש לעורר את עצמו כשיתחיל לעורר את הנפש והרוח»

## 4. "I got emotional, and then nothing changed."

**Short answer:** The feeling was a beginning. It needed a vessel, a deed, and it didn't get one.

The Mitteler Rebbe describes exactly this: someone hears a deep idea, gets excited, and says "Aha, I'm warm, I've seen light", and then goes straight back to business without fixing it in himself [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 675]. Light only holds in a vessel [line 769]. Without one, the arousal is "like a flame flying in the air, with nothing to hold it" [line 778]. And he warns about the moment the heart first moves: people cut it short there, and "this is the bad sickness that loses everything" [line 737]. Let the feeling add its strength to understanding instead, to widen and deepen further [line 742].

The Alter Rebbe's teaching in Likkutei Torah says the same: the running toward God is only a beginning; the main thing is drawing it down into Torah and mitzvot [Likkutei Torah, Metzora]. The Piaseczner: longings are good only if you work with them [Tzav VeZeruz 65]. When a good thought comes, do a mitzvah or learn with it, and so clothe it in holiness [Hakhsharat 270].

**What to do next time:** when the feeling comes, go back into one more detail of the line before you finish. Then say one resolution aloud from the heart [Chovat HaTalmidim 136], and do one small thing today. At the end of prayer, the Piaseczner says, grip the holiness "like a peg that won't move" [Derekh HaMelekh 463].

## 5. "I feel fake."

**Short answer:** Keep going anyway. Mixed is not the same as false, and doubting every stirring is its own trap.

The Alter Rebbe: even if it seems to you that it's only imagination, "don't worry" [Tanya ch. 44]. R. Aharon of Strashelye, in his teacher's name: don't worry that your service is mixed with something; "though mixed, it is whole" [Shaarei Avodah, introduction]. The Piaseczner: "among ten deceptive fervours you will find one true one" [Bnei Machshava Tova 247], and the urge to doubt what you felt in holiness is itself a temptation [Tzav VeZeruz 84]. About mixed motives in the middle of the work: "the main thing is not to pay them any attention at all, and just work" [Derekh HaMelekh 1550].

If you feel fake because you're *saying* words you don't feel: say them anyway. The mouth comes first and the heart follows [Mevo HaShe'arim 198].

If you feel fake because you're *performing* (swaying hard, pushing out feeling): stop the movements and see what's left [Hakhsharat 145]. Then go back to the line, softly.

And don't turn "I'm fake" into "I'm worthless". "Whoever feels himself empty and vile behaves empty and vile" [Esh Kodesh 541].

## 6. "I'm too heavy, too sad, to sit."

**Short answer:** Then don't do the full sitting. Do one small thing, gently. And know that the low place is not a detour.

First, the plain things. A heavy mood may come from hunger, tiredness or a hurt someone gave you; the Piaseczner says to check that first [Derekh HaMelekh 937]. Then ask "since when?" and trace it back to the hour it began. If you find it, the heart often lightens at once [Hakhsharat 313].

Second, sadness is not the same as a broken heart. The Piaseczner: what feels like brokenness may be "only a kind of absence and dullness" [Bnei Machshava Tova 46]; and "with all that, don't stay in sadness" [Hakhsharat 149]. Start with a clearer, gladder door if there is one, not with the soul's pains [Hakhsharat 89].

Third, you aren't alone in it. The Mitteler Rebbe: when a person is crushed, God dwells with him, "to revive the spirit of the crushed" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 704], and then even his sigh and his natural melancholy are turned into joy, from the Godliness resting on him [line 708]. The Piaseczner, writing in the Warsaw ghetto: in the inner rooms God weeps, and a person who weeps *with* Him is held [Esh Kodesh 585]; and when we can't find ourselves, the One who lost us comes looking [Esh Kodesh 82].

**What to do:** one small thing only. Modeh Ani, or a thank-you in your own words. Giving yourself to God in general, as in Modeh Ani, is "very near to every Jew" [Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 1]. Or say one line with your mouth and tell Him in your own words that it's heavy. No self-examination today, no accounting, no "letting go" (the guide's constitution, WHEN THINGS CHANGE).

## 7. "I don't have time."

**Short answer:** Do the three-minute thread. Every moment counts, and a short, real one carries into the day.

The Tanya sets the length by two things: how far your mind can go and "how much free time he has" [Tanya ch. 41]. The Rashab passes on the Mitteler Rebbe's advice to busy working men: every moment longer is like buying life for your soul for the whole day, and the imprint of even a little bond shines through your business [Kuntres HaTefillah §16]. The Piaseczner: even someone swamped earning a living must make himself free times [Derekh HaMelekh 688].

And once you've done it, a "light contemplation" at any moment brings it back [Tanya ch. 42]. So the sentence you carry (Part I, "After") does a lot of the work. Setting God before you can happen in the middle of anything: a person doesn't sit and move alone at home the way he does before a great king [Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1:5].

**What to do:** one line, one detail in front of you, a verse, one act. Three minutes, at the same time each day.

> Kuntres HaTefillah §16, OCR (line 212): «וכל מה שיאריך בתפלה אפילו רגץ יותר הרי זה כקונה חיים לנפשו על כל היום»

## 8. "I'm not religious," or "I'm not Jewish."

**Short answer:** This is for you too. The oneness of God is the truth about everyone. Only the clothing changes.

**If you're Jewish and not observant:** the full way is yours, at your own pace. The Shema, a coin for charity, a line of Torah. Nobody is measuring how much you keep.

**If you're of another faith:** use the name for God you use. Think the line through the same way. For the lips, say "I have set God before me always" (Psalm 16:8), a verse from your own tradition, or your own words to Him. For the act, a kindness, or a coin given. Every person is made in the image of God (claims U01). The practices given to all people are faith in the one God and decency to people (claims U02). You're never asked to leave your faith or take on what isn't yours (claims U03).

**If you're not sure God exists:** treat the line as something to think through, not something to sign. Start from what you can check: this moment isn't holding itself up, and nothing you can see is either. Follow the reasoning as far as it goes. Where thought can't reach, the sources say, knowing stops and faith begins (claims F44). From there it's an invitation, and your answer is your own. A kind act and, if you're willing, a few honest words to Him, are the practice.

What never changes: one line, thought through slowly, the mind brought back, one act.

> **Source.** Shulchan Aruch HaRav OC 1:5 (line 71): «כי אין ישיבת האדם ותנועותיו ועסקיו והוא לבדו בביתו כישיבתו ותנועותיו ועסקיו והוא לפני מלך גדול»
> Likutei Moharan 52 (line 2696), pouring out your own words: «ואי אפשר לבוא לידי בטול, כי אם על ידי התבודדות»

## 9. "I felt something big. Now what?"

**Short answer:** Don't examine it, don't chase it, and don't make it yours. Do something with it today, and look at it again in a few days by its fruit.

- **Don't pick it apart.** Investigating a high moment with the intellect spoils it [Esh Kodesh 541]. And don't deny it later either: "What I felt, I felt" [Tzav VeZeruz 85].
- **Don't make it yours.** Whoever truly wants closeness to God "will see that he doesn't make a something of his heart's arousal" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 741]. The false kind shows itself afterward, when you feel fuller of yourself "from that very experience" [line 808].
- **Put it into a vessel.** Engrave it in your heart so the awe stays [Esh Kodesh 86]. One act, one resolution said aloud (question 4).
- **Expect to come down.** "A person must always be going up and coming down" [Derekh HaMelekh 704]. "Whoever thinks he never falls, that's a sign he never rose" [Derekh HaMelekh 1549].
- **Judge it later, by the fruit.** Are you a little more your own master? [Bnei Machshava Tova 252]. Are love and awe a little more? [Derekh HaMelekh 553]. Does an insult sting less? [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 829].

**One caution.** If "something big" comes with sleepless nights, racing thoughts, a sense of a special mission, or not eating, that is not a spiritual state to work with. That is the danger line above, and the guide's crisis protocol applies (the guide's constitution, SAFETY).

> Tzav VeZeruz (line 85): «אבל את אשר הרגשתי הרגשתי»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 741): «יראה שלא יעשה יש ודבר מהתפעלות לבו ולא ירגישנו כלל»; (line 808): «וסימן לזה שמיד שחלף זה ועבר, בא לכלל הרגשת העצמיות מזה עצמו דוקא»; (line 829): «ואינו מרגיש זה כלל כי נבזה בעיניו נמאס באמת מפני שאין לו מצד עצמו כלום»

## 10. "It worked yesterday and not today."

**Short answer:** That's how it goes. Up and down is built in. Same line, one step deeper.

The Piaseczner: "a person must always be going up and coming down" [Derekh HaMelekh 704]. When the heart seems sealed, "in truth it is full of holiness even now" [Derekh HaMelekh 1240].

Three reasons the sources give for a flat day, and what each asks of you:

- **The newness wore off, and the real work started.** The Rashab: when you begin real contemplation you go cold, because the work is heavy and you're not used to it [Kuntres HaAvodah]. *Stay.*
- **You repeated instead of going deeper.** Remembering the general idea by saying it every day has "no life of Godly light in it" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 395]. *Add one new detail today, one new place in your day, and explain less.*
- **You weren't prepared.** The Rashab in another place: some go cold because they aren't prepared in their soul to receive the light [Kuntres HaTefillah §11]. *Do the "before" again: the coin, a few words of Torah, gathering your strength.*

And one from the Piaseczner: a special device used every day stops working in the end [Hakhsharat 436]. If yesterday's "trick" (a certain tune, a certain picture) isn't working, let it rest. Keep the line.

Don't change the line just because today was flat. Change it only if it's been cold for a long time; for a cold heart the Chabad advice is a different object, not more effort (the Hisbonenus diagnostics: "Cold: change the object, not the effort").
