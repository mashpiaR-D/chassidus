## How the Preface is built

The Preface defends writing the book at all. It says knowing the unity is a duty for everyone (lines 14-30), that the late generations need more explanation, not less, and it gives three of its own meshalim for that (the king's treasuries, the near and far light, the city man and the villager: lines 31-35). Then it tells the chain of teachers. The Ari hid the wisdom in meshalim and many took them literally (line 36). The Baal Shem Tov 'made many meshalim from the bond of soul and body, as it says, from my flesh I will see G-d' (line 37), and the Maggid and the Alter Rebbe continued (38-40). After the Alter Rebbe passed, his teachings were left in transcripts (41-42). Torah must be weighed like a scale, not leaning even to the right (42-45). Then comes the centre: the danger of meshalim and the three conditions (46-63), the danger of reading transcripts literally (64-67), the author's claim that all of this is received from his teacher (68-75), and two warnings to readers: knowledge must become felt life (81), and the Torah's 'bread' feeds only one who is alive through prayer (82-86). The Introduction (89-113) gathers kabbalistic sayings that nothing may be pictured of Ein Sof, and ends with the Alter Rebbe's sunlight mashal and its break (110-111). In Gate 2 (lines 384-393) he returns to the method and makes the making of a mashal itself a mashal for creation.

## PR-L01 · Handles for the basket

**The law.** A mashal exists to give the mind a handle on something it cannot hold. It is a handle, not the thing.

R. Aharon says the mind, because it lives in a body, has no grip at all on what is above it. A mashal gives it 'some hold'. He uses the Sages' picture: the Torah was like a basket with no handles until Shlomo made handles with his meshalim. You carry the basket by its handles, but the basket is what matters. The Baal Shem Tov made 'many meshalim from the bond of soul and body' for the same reason: so that the unity could be 'grasped in the heart of each person, by his own measure'.

## PR-L02 · Three conditions

**The law.** Explaining by mashal is dangerous, so it has three main conditions, besides smaller ones.

Before he gives the conditions, R. Aharon names three mistakes they guard against: (1) thinking that because you have the mashal you have reached the root; (2) thinking the thing explained really is like the mashal; (3) making up meshalim from your own head and applying them above. He cites the kabbalists' warning (Etz Chaim) that explaining by mashal is 'a great danger'. Laws L03, L06 and L07 are the three conditions.

## PR-L03 · Know the root first

**The law.** Condition one: only someone who first understands the teaching itself, from its root, as received from a teacher, may make a mashal for it.

A mashal is found, not invented. You can only see which thing in the soul or in nature matches a truth above if you already hold that truth from the inside. Scientists who do not even agree about nature cannot use nature to explain what is above it. Shlomo could speak 3,000 meshalim because G-d gave him wisdom at the root, not human cleverness. One who has not reached that 'may not invent meshalim from his human wisdom'.

> «מוכרח מקודם להבין עצם החכמה ולעמוד על שרשה על פי קבלתו אשר קיבל מרבו» — *he must first understand the wisdom itself and reach its root, through what he received from his teacher.* · Sha'arei HaYichud VeEmunah (R. ## PR-L04 · Why a true mashal is possible

**The law.** A mashal can be true because everything below is built like what is above, and because with G-d all is one.

This is the ground under every mashal. 'The lower world is like the upper world.' A person is 'a small world'. So there are two places to find meshalim: the soul in the body, and the nature of the world. The correspondence is real because 'with Him everything is one level', so the one who knows the root can see the same pattern in both.

> «יכול למצוא גם כן משל מצד הבריאה הן בהתקשרות הנפש עם הגוף והן בטבעית העולם» — *he can also find a mashal in creation, both in the bond of the soul with the body and in the nature of the world,* · Sha'arei HaYichud VeEmunah (R. ## PR-L05 · Levels of mashal

**The law.** The deeper the maker's grasp, the more meshalim he can make: thousands, hundreds, or only a few.

Shlomo spoke 3,000 meshalim; R. Meir had 300 'fox fables'; R. Yochanan said 'we have only three'. R. Aharon reads this as rank, not talent: the one who stood deeper in the root could find more meshalim. He maps the three numbers onto the three lower worlds: thousands, hundreds, single units. Later (Gate 2, line 385-387) he adds that the wiser one is, the further away the mashal he can reach for.

*Where:* stated (Preface lines 51-53; Gate 2 lines 385, 387).

## PR-L06 · Only for His presence in the worlds

**The law.** Condition two: a mashal may describe only how G-d is drawn into the worlds and is one with them. It may never describe what He is in Himself.

Verses seem to clash: 'in Our image' and 'a likeness like a man' allow a picture; 'to whom will you liken Me?' forbids one. The Sages say both 'a mashal of a king of flesh and blood' and 'not like the way of flesh and blood'. R. Aharon's answer: the mashal of soul in body fits how He fills the worlds, 'no place empty of Him', and the worlds are one with Him without change. But of His Essence, which has no relation to worlds, 'it is forbidden to make any mashal at all'. Even the allowed mashal is 'only from the side of how it appears to us'. The Introduction adds Etz Chaim: before Atzilut we speak in mashal only 'to soothe the ear'.

## PR-L07 · Never on every side

**The law.** Condition three: even an allowed mashal must not be matched to its subject on every side. Name the one point where it holds, and say where it fails.

R. Aharon takes this from the Alter Rebbe (Shaar HaYichud 6 and Tanya 42, see PR-O01, PR-O02). Soul and body are two things from the root; heaven and earth come from nothing and stay inside His word. The soul feels the body's pain; nothing in the world changes Him. So a mashal is true 'only in this', at one point. His own writing follows the rule again and again: 'the mashal is not like the thing on every side, except that in this it is a mashal' (Gate 1 line 282; Sha'arei Avodah 88, 225). He says he has marked in the book what may be likened 'as a likeness and pattern' and what may not, and that where he does not repeat the warning, it still applies.

## PR-L08 · The one point of the person-mashal

## PR-L11 · The dangers

**The law.** A mashal taken literally does more harm than no mashal: it makes G-d physical, splits Him into parts, and lets everyone think he understands.

R. Aharon is blunt. Later kabbalists took the Ari's meshalim 'almost literally, with great corporealizing'. A person of plain faith knows G-d is above understanding. But one who learns a mashal and thinks 'that is how it is above' can come 'to make Him physical and to divide', to picture Him as having wisdom and feelings, as part of the worlds; and anyone, even a child, may then think he knows. 'His loss is greater than his gain.' The risk grew after the Alter Rebbe's passing, because his teachings were left in transcripts that a hurried reader takes at face value.

## PR-L14 · Fit it to the listener

**The law.** The further the listener is from the source, the more light and the more detail the mashal needs.

Three of R. Aharon's own meshalim make this point. Those near a light need only a small light; those far away need a great one or nothing reaches them. A city man who sees the king every day is not impressed; a villager who sees him once tells every detail. So for the souls of today, far from their root, the unity must be explained 'at length, in great detail', and a teacher must find a mashal 'in the measure of the receiver'.

## PR-L15 · Making a mashal is itself a mashal

**The law.** A wise teacher hides his wisdom in a foreign garment so a student can receive it, and his whole self is inside the mashal. This is how G-d makes a world.

In Gate 2 R. Aharon turns the method on itself. A teacher whose wisdom is far above the student must 'hide his wisdom and dress it in a foreign garment' (a tree, a body, water). 'There is no difference at all between his being as it is in his own wisdom and as it is drawn into the mashal.' So G-d dresses His kindness in a world of separate things, the curtain (parsa) between Atzilut and the lower worlds. ## Tensions, kept (ours)

**PR-T1 · Every detail fits vs. never on every side.** The Rebbe says every detail of a Torah mashal fits, because the mashal came down from the nimshal (PR-O14). R. Aharon says never match the mashal on every side (PR-L07). Our reading: they speak of different things. The Rebbe speaks of the pattern: each part of a true mashal points to something real above, so read the details closely. R. Aharon speaks of the being: the mashal and the thing are never the same kind of thing, and the place where the mashal is two (soul and body) is exactly where He is one. Both can be kept: match the parts, deny the sameness. This reconciliation is ours.

**PR-T2 · The mashal is the nimshal vs. a foreign garment.** The Rashab says 'the mashal is the nimshal itself' (Ayin Beis line 1094, 1849), and also that the mashal is 'a foreign matter' and the idea through it is different in kind (lines 316, 2617). The Rayatz says one can see 'how they are truly one thing'. Our reading: from below, the mashal is foreign and hides; from its root, the physical thing is the G-dly power in a garment, so the one who sees through it sees the thing itself. This matches R. Aharon's Gate 2 (PR-L15): the teacher's whole self is in the mashal; only the receiver sees a picture.

**PR-T3 · Where the depth sits.** R. Aharon's warnings could suggest that the mashal is a weak step below the truth. The Mitteler Rebbe and the Maharash say the root of the mashal is higher than the idea, and the deepest point lives in the last, most physical mashal (PR-O06, PR-O10). R. Aharon himself says the wiser one is, the further the mashal (PR-L05, PR-L15). So the warnings are about the reader who stops at the picture, not about the picture's worth. That reading is ours.

## A test card for a new mashal (ours, built on the laws)

1. Do I know the point myself, from inside, before I picture it? (PR-L03)
2. Is the picture about how the One fills and holds the world and me, not about what He is in Himself? (PR-L06)
3. Is it from the person's own life, something they can find in themselves first? (PR-L09)
4. Which one point carries the likeness? Can I name it in a sentence? (PR-L07, PR-L08)
5. Where does it break, and does the break bring Him closer, not further? (PR-L07, PR-O02, PR-O05)
6. Does every part of the picture point to something true, so the details are worth asking about? (PR-O14)
7. Is it sized for this listener: enough detail for someone far, little for someone near? (PR-L14)
8. After it works, can I take the picture away and leave the point? (PR-L10)
9. Could someone take it literally and make G-d a body, a part, or a person with moods? If so, fix or drop it. (PR-L11)
10. Does it end in feeling and in a deed, and in wonder rather than 'now I get it'? (PR-L12, PR-L13)

## Summary

R. Aharon of Strashelye wrote down, as fixed law, what his teacher the Alter Rebbe did case by case. A mashal is a handle on a basket: it gives the mind a grip on what it cannot hold, and the basket is what matters. It has three conditions. First, only someone who holds the teaching from its root may make a mashal for it; meshalim are found, not invented, and the deeper one stands, the more and the further the meshalim. Second, a mashal may picture only how G-d fills and is one with the worlds, never His Essence. Third, it must never be matched on every side: name the one point where it holds (a person is one person in many powers) and say where it fails (the soul is moved by the body; He is not moved by anything). The best mashal comes from the soul's own life in the body: 'from my flesh I see G-d'. After it works, the picture is stripped away. Taken literally it is worse than none, because it makes G-d physical and divided. Its aim is felt life, and its end is wonder: 'the purpose of knowing is that we do not know'. The later Rebbeim add the theory behind the craft. The mashal is a foreign garment that hides so that it can show (Mitteler Rebbe, Tzemach Tzedek, Rashab); its root is higher than the idea, and the deepest point lives in the plainest mashal (Mitteler Rebbe, Maharash); from the root, 'the mashal is the nimshal itself' (Rashab, Rayatz); and a Torah mashal is exact because it came down from what it explains, so its details matter (the Rebbe). R. Aharon also turns the method on itself: a teacher hiding his wisdom in a mashal is the way G-d hides Himself in a world. The checklist at the end turns these laws into tests for making new meshalim.
