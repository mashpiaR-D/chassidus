# Pardes Rimmonim, lines 983-1225: wholeness of opposites, colors that move the soul, and the hidden lights

Four gates. Gate 9 asks who makes peace between Chesed (giving) and Gevurah (holding back), and answers that the whole one is the one who holds both. Gate 10 is about the colors of the sefirot: they are not real colors, yet a color seen by the eye or pictured in the mind can wake the soul. Gate 11 climbs as high as the book goes, to the three "hidden lights" (tzachtzachot, "brightnesses") said to be inside Ein Sof itself, and there the Ramak says some of his clearest things about how the world lives inside God's knowing of Himself. Gate 12 lays out the 32 paths of wisdom and the 32 times "Elokim" appears in the creation story; it is mostly a table, with a few lines on teshuvah and renewal that the guide should keep.

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**No difference between the Emanator and what He thinks.** Our thoughts are unfinished until we act, the Ramak says. His are not. "When it rose in His will to bring into being... then it was emanated and pictured whole within His essence, with no change in Him... until there is no difference at all between the Emanator and that being, but He and they are all one" (עד שאין בין המאציל והמציאות ההוא הבדל כלל אלא הוא והם הכל אחד, line 1061). This is the Ramak's floor for everything else: before anything is outside, it is already inside, and inside it is simply Him.

**He knows all things by knowing Himself.** The deepest line of the range: "by grasping His own essence He grasps every being other than Himself, and He and they are all one thing, united" (כי בהשגת עצמותו ישיג כל נמצא זולתו והוא והם הכל דבר אחד מיוחד, line 1086). His knowing does not break into parts, because "He and His knowing are one". So the hidden roots of all things must be inside Him, since that is the only place He looks. Then he adds a second knowing: after things come to be, their light returns upward, "like being that clothes nothing". God knows the world before it is and after it is, and both knowings are Him. (The Alter Rebbe cites this very teaching, "as explained in the Pardes of the Ramak", in the Gate of Unity, Tanya lines 835-836: man knows by a knowing outside himself, "and the Holy One, blessed be He, by knowing Himself", בידיעת עצמו, Tanya line 835.)

**Lighting a candle from a candle.** How can many come from One with no loss or change? R. Hamai Gaon's answer: "emanation is only like lighting a candle from a candle, as to the Emanator not changing" (כי האצילות אינו אלא כמדליק נר מנר לענין העדר השינוי במאציל, line 1067). And the hidden lights are so much one "that if you picture yourself pointing with your finger at one of them, you are pointing at all of them" (עד שאם תצייר שאתה רומז באצבעך לאחת מהם אתה רומז לכלם, line 1067).

**The Essence needs no root.** The hidden roots in Ein Sof are roots of the vessels, not of the light. "For the Essence is all one, and needs neither root nor source" (כי העצמות הכל אחד, ואין צריך לא לשרש ולא למקור, line 1081). Here the Ramak draws a clean line: what can be counted (ten, three) belongs to the vessels and their roots; the light in them is one and uncounted. R. Hai Gaon says the same: "the simple One is not divided into parts" (אין האחד הפשוט נחלק לחלקים, line 1053); the division is from their side.

**No will in Ein Sof, strictly speaking.** "If we said that He wills, He would be found changing from will to will" (שאם נאמר שהוא רוצה נמצא משתנה מרצון לרצון, line 1074). Will is the first thing emanated, Keter; Ein Sof "is called willing" through it, just as He is called wise through Chochmah. Vessels change; the Essence works the change through them and does not change.

**Nature is His will.** On why creation says "Elokim" 32 times: Elokim is the Name of nature, "for nature is divine will" (כי הטבע רצון אלקי הוא, line 1104). The world was made first in strict judgment and then joined with mercy.

**Wholeness is the holder of opposites.** Gate 9's great sentence: "and he is called truly whole, the master of opposites" (ויקרא שלם באמת בעל ההפכים, line 990). Giving alone is not whole; holding back alone is not whole. Jacob, "a whole man", is the one who holds both. And the whole in the middle is not complete by itself: "the decision in Tiferet is not complete except through Malchut" (אין שלימות ההכרעה אל הת"ת אלא ע"י המלכות, line 992). Fire and water are reconciled by air, but only with earth under them. Unity here is not sameness; it is opposites held together, and it needs the lowest to be finished.

## 3. States of consciousness

**Whole in opposites (RK-S09, proposed).** "Truly whole, the master of opposites" (line 990). Inside: I feel the pull to give and the pull to hold back, and I do not have to kill either one; I am the place where they meet. The Ramak ties it to peace that needs the lowest (line 992): the settling is not finished in the head; it is finished in the body, in the deed, in the ground. How to enter: when torn between two good pulls, stop looking for the winner; ask what deed would let both live, and do that. It is the healed side of S10 (Torn in two), and a cousin of S49 (Weeping and joy in one heart); the map has no state for holding a real contradiction whole.

**Smelling, not touching (RK-S10, proposed).** The Zohar on the highest places: "they know nothing but a scent, like one who smells a fragrance and is refreshed" (לא ידעין בר ריחא כמאן דארח בריחין ואתבסס, line 1073). The Ramak: Chochmah and Binah know the hidden roots only "like one who smells a good scent without touching the spice" (כריח המריח בריח הטוב בלתי פגעו בבושם, line 1076). Inside: I have not reached Him and I know I will not; yet something of Him reaches me, and it is enough to live on. This is the honest state of a seeker at the top: not grasp, not nothing, a scent. How to enter: in prayer or study, when the mind reaches its edge, stop pushing and notice what is left in the air. Near S35 (The edge of knowing) and technique `edge`; the map lacks the "enough" in it.

**The longing that chases (Chochmah's chase).** Chochmah itself "desires and longs to cleave to the roots" (חשקה ותאוותה להדבק בשרשים, line 1081), chasing what it cannot reach, "touching and not touching". The Ramak puts longing into the highest wisdom itself. This is S13 (The unnamed longing), given a cosmic root: the longing is not a flaw in the lover, it is how wisdom is made. No new id.

**Running and returning before the Root.** Twice he says this contemplation must be "running and returning" (ratzo vashov): "this matter is one of those where too much looking is forbidden, and it is in running and returning" (והענין הזה הוא מהענינים שהעיון בו ביותר אסור והוא ברצוא ושוב, line 1081); "let the one who looks put his hand on his mouth, and let his study of this be running and returning, and from God we ask pardon" (line 1086). This is the Ramak's own guard on RK-T01 (Touch and draw back). It is also the source of the Chabad term ratzo vashov (S47 Running, S48 Returning).

**Rising where I fell (RK-S11, proposed).** On the fourth day the moon was made small, and on that same day she is filled. The Zohar: a voice cries, "do not rejoice over me, my enemy; though I fell I rose", "rising on the day of falling" (קימה ביום נפילה, line 1198). The Ramak: "in the place where she fell, there she will rise" (ובמקום שנפל, בו יקום, line 1198). Inside: the fall is not a detour from the path; the rising starts right here, on the same day, in the same place. How to enter: do not move away from the place of failure to feel better; stand in it and begin there. It answers S11 (The verdict: I'm my mistakes) and sits inside S77 (The lower return); what is new is the "same day, same place".

**Moved by what the eye sees.** See §4 (RK-T09). The state behind it: the soul is not only thought. "From colors seen by the eyes or pictured in the mind, the spiritual is moved, and the nefesh wakes the ruach and the ruach the neshamah" (והנפש תעורר לרוח והרוח לנשמה, line 1019).

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Let the senses move the soul (RK-T09, proposed).** Gate 10 opens by forbidding a literal reading of colors: one who thinks the sefirot are colored "destroys the world" by making Him bodily. Then it turns. The soul is moved by what the body takes in. Doctors "run streams of water before" the sick to calm them (יריצו לפניהם סלוני המים, line 1019). Mourners wear black so they will be moved. And "the pious would pour water when praying for rain, so that their hearts would be directed to that matter and their souls be moved" (כדי שיתכוון לבם אל הענין ההוא ותתפעל נשמתם, line 1019). The picture in the mind is also allowed: one who needs to draw mercy may picture the sefirah's name in its color (line 1019). What it is for: to let the body help the heart instead of fighting it. How: before prayer or contemplation, let one real thing be in front of the eyes or ears that matches what you are asking: water for rain, light for clarity, a plain white cloth for mercy. Guard: the color is a helper for the soul, never a picture of God; and he says flatly that amulets and color-magic are "not our portion" (line 1019). Ties to `picturing`, `mashal`, `niggun` (the same logic for sound). The map has nothing on sight as a door.

**Look to the Cause (RK-T10, proposed).** Path 18, "the house of flow": "when all the effects look to their Cause, great flow and blessing are drawn to them" (כי בהבטת העלולים כלם אל עילתם ימשך להם רוב שפע וברכה, line 1203), so "all who cleave to Him and search His essence receive great good". What it is for: blessing comes by looking toward its Source, not by grabbing at the blessing. How: when you need something, turn your attention first from the thing to the One it comes from; hold that look for a while before asking. Ties to `before_whom` and `binding`; what is new is the promise that the look itself draws the flow.

**Settle a quarrel at its root.** Of the three places a conflict can be decided, the best is the root: "if the fire is not put out at its source, putting it out outside its source will not help" (ואם לא יכבו האש במקורו לא יועיל כבייתו לחוץ ממקורו, line 1010). And an argument "for the sake of Heaven" wants to be decided, while Korach's band "do not want a decision, only a quarrel to vex" (שאינם חפצים בהכרעה רק מחלוקת לקנטוריא, line 988). Hillel's house was followed because they "cleave to the side of Chesed in humility and lowliness" (line 988). Practice (ours, from his words): in a quarrel, first ask yourself honestly whether you want it settled. Ties to `cheshbon`.

**Torah study draws the wind.** Path 2 from the Tikkunim: "this wind blows on those who busy themselves with Torah" (האי רוח נשיב על אלין דמתעסקין באורייתא, line 1175), because the Shechinah is found among them. Study as a place where the spirit blows. Ties to `chazarah`.

**Prayer with lifted hands.** Before speaking of the hidden lights, R. Shimon "lifted his hands in prayer"; the Ramak says he "made himself holy" first (line 1080). Before a hard contemplation, prepare with prayer. Ties to `arrive`.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why is there judgment, and why is it good?** Chesed, the Zohar says, "does good to all creatures, righteous and wicked, and does not care" (ועביד טיבו בכל בריין בצדיקיא וברשיעיא ולא חייש, line 986). The Ramak adds that pure giving would burn the world out; Chesed "cools the foreign heat so that a person can live" (line 994). The whole rule over creatures runs "mainly through Chesed", and the closer to the earth, the more mercy (כי כל הקרב אל העפר הוא רחמים יותר, line 991). The lowest place is the most tenderly held.

**Suffering and falling.** Gate 12 on the moon: "rising on the day of falling" (line 1198). And darkness itself comes in two kinds (line 1185). There is holy darkness, the moon that receives from the sun; its separation is "a separation that has union and oneness after it" (הבדלה שיש אחריה חיבור ויחוד). Only the darkness of the shells has no joining after it. So much of what feels like being cut off is the first half of a union.

**Teshuvah.** The Tikkunim: "if a person returns in teshuvah, the flow that had gone up comes down to her, and what was dry land He calls earth" (דאם בר נש יחזור בתיובתא נחית לה נביעו דאסתלק, line 1194). "His right hand is stretched out to receive those who return" (וכן ימינו פשוטה לקבל שבים, line 1194). Dryness is not a verdict; it is the flow gone up, waiting.

**The soul.** Souls come each from its own root: "one is wise in Chochmah, one understanding in Binah, one kind in Chesed" (יש חכם בחכמה ונבון בבינה וחסיד בחסד, line 1205). There is no one right shape for a soul. The righteous wear a garment of light in Eden, and some "saw their world in their lives" (וראו עולמם בחייהם, line 1201).

**Renewal and the point of it all.** Path 32: all things "are renewed from time to time" (כלם מתחדשים מזמן לזמן, line 1224), and "very good" means the renewal is a good above the first good of creation. The world is not a finished thing slowly running down; it is being made new.

**Awe and knowing.** Why can the highest not be known? Not because it is far, but because it is one: there is no part to hold. The right response, twice in this range, is the hand on the mouth and the running and returning.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Knowing all by knowing Himself.** Full agreement. The Alter Rebbe takes it straight from here: "as explained in the Pardes" (Tanya line 836), "and the Holy One... by knowing Himself" (Tanya line 835). What the Gate of Unity adds is the use: because He knows all through Himself, the world is not "other" to Him at all, and that is the ground of bitul. The Ramak already says "He and they are one thing" (line 1086).
- **Will.** The Ramak denies will to Ein Sof itself, to protect Him from change (line 1074). Later Chassidus speaks of a will and delight in the Essence itself that no reason precedes (see Samach Vav in this index). This is a real difference in language; whether it is one in substance is a question for the guide to leave open. Ours: the Ramak is guarding against a changing God; Chassidus is guarding against a God bound by reasons. Both guards are sound.
- **Candle from a candle.** The Ramak's picture of emanation without loss (line 1067) is the picture Chassidus also uses for the soul and for giving. But in Chabad, the move from the infinite light to the world needs tzimtzum, a withdrawal, not only a lighting. The Ramak's gate 11 has no tzimtzum at all; the hidden lights are one with the Root, and the vessels simply come out from them.
- **Ratzo vashov.** The Ramak's guard on high contemplation (lines 1081, 1086) is the source of the term Chabad makes central. In the Ramak it is a caution (do not stare); in Chabad it becomes the heartbeat of the soul and of all service. The guide should keep the caution too.
- **Colors and the senses.** The Ramak gives a psychology of the senses (line 1019) that Chabad mostly leaves out in favor of the mind. The guide should take it: some people reach the heart through the eye first.
- **Nature is divine will.** Line 1104 (Elokim = hateva) is the root of the Gate of Unity's reading of Elokim as the Name that hides (Shaar HaYichud ch. 6). The Ramak reads it as will and judgment; the Alter Rebbe as concealment.
