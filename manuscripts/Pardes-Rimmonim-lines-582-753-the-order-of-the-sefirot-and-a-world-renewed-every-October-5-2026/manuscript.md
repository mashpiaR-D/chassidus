Three technical gates: in what order the sefirot came out (Gate 5), how to picture them standing (Gate 6), and the "channels" by which one passes light to another (Gate 7). Most of the pages settle disputes among earlier Kabbalists. But inside them the Ramak says some of his strongest things about the unity: that every level is nested inside the one above it, all the way up to the Infinite, so that "one cannot say this is Malchut and not the Infinite"; that He brings all things into being "at every time, every moment, every hour", like a river whose water is new each instant; that the channels are faces turned toward one another, and sin is a face turned away. He also gives a reading of "I have set God before me always", a picture of how a person leaves prayer, and a frank word on what to do with doubt.

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**Before anything, He was hidden even from Himself being known.** "Before the emanation, the Infinite was simple in utter simplicity, hidden in utter hiddenness, and His Godliness was not revealed to another, for lack of beings" (ולא היה אלקותו מתגלה אל זולתו להעדר ההוויות, line 600, Pardes 5:4:1). The sefirot were in Him the way fire is in a flint: "there is no difference or separation at all between the stone and the fire inside it" (line 600), "until the name 'sefirot' hardly applies, only true unity" (עד שכמעט לא יוצדק בו שם מציאות הספירות כלל אלא יחוד האמיתי, line 600). And then: "when it rose in His simple will to reveal His Godliness and to do good with His hidden measures to another" (line 600), He brought out the first.

**Near the Source there is no judgment.** Why did judgment appear only at the third level? Because close to the Source, "in the light of the King's face is life" (כי באור פני מלך חיים, line 601), and "all would be light and life, the will that does good, and the decrees of judgment would not act" (line 601). The "kings who ruled in Edom and died" are the forces of judgment that could not survive so near Him. "They did not die in the sense of being undone, but He hid them away" (line 606). Here, before the Ari, is a teaching about the "death of the kings" that is really a teaching about nearness: harshness cannot live close to God.

**The hiding is the revealing.** Again, as in Gate 4, and now as a rule: "the cause of revealing is the cause of hiding, and the cause of hiding is the cause of revealing" (line 606); "if it were not hidden it would not be revealed" (שאם לא היה מתעלם לא היה מתגלה, line 607). His example: you cannot look at the sun; you can look at it through a screen.

**Nested inside Him.** Every cause surrounds its effect, as the heavens surround the earth (line 668). So the sefirot are one inside another, "all of them inside the Infinite", and inside Malchut the worlds of creating, forming and doing, then the chariots, the heavens, the earth, and the seven earths at its center (line 671). This lets him read the old saying: "He is the place of the world, and the world is not His place" (line 673). And at once the warning: this is not space. "He has no place, and He brought place into being" (כי אדרבה הוא מקומו של עולם ואין לו מקום והוא המציא המקום, line 673). Whoever takes it as space makes God a body.

**"One cannot say: this is Malchut and not the Infinite."** Chapter 8 of Gate 6 (lines 696-699) is one of the Ramak's great passages on the unity. The worry: if the Crown is not the Infinite, then there is a line where the Crown ends and the Infinite begins, and the Infinite has a limit. His answer is the soul and the body. A body cannot live without the soul; a soul can be without a body (אמנם יוצדק אמרנו נשמה בלא גוף, line 697). So the cause is inside its effect, while the cause also has a side that needs nothing. And therefore:

> כי המלכות הוא היסוד והיסוד הוא ההוד ... והחכמה הוא הכתר והכתר הוא הא"ס. ולא זה בלבד אלא מתפשט בכולן עד שלא יצדק לומר זהו מלכות ולא א"ס (line 697, Pardes 6:8:2)

"Malchut is Yesod, and Yesod is Hod... and Chochmah is Keter, and Keter is Ein Sof. And not only this: He spreads through all of them, until one cannot say 'this is Malchut and not the Infinite'." He is the life of the sefirot, the chariots, the angels "and all the work of creation, from the first point to the last". The Infinite still "needs no other, and all need Him" (כי אינו צריך אל זולתו והכל צריכין לו והוא מקור לכל, line 700).

**New every moment.** From "You give life to them all" (Nehemiah 9:6), read by the Sages as "You bring them all into being", he draws the conclusion that Chabad would make its first face (F01): since He gives them life, He "brings them into being at every time, every moment and every hour" (שהוא ממציאם ומהוום בכל עת ובכל רגע ובכל שעה, line 697). "In His goodness He renews each day, always, the work of creation" (line 698), "and if one could think of His flow withdrawing from them for an instant, they would cease" (ואם ח"ו יעלה על הדעת הסתלקות שפעו מהם כרגע יספו, line 698). They are "like the water of a river, which is new at every moment" (line 698). His essence "pierces and goes down... to the last point in the depths; the whole earth is full of His glory" (line 698), and "it does not change in any place" (ולא אשתני בכל אתר, line 698).

**The channels are faces.** A "channel" between sefirot is not a pipe. When a lower sefirah wants to receive, "it must light up a face toward the higher" (תצטרך המדה התחתונה להאיר כנגדה פנים מאירים, line 706), and its light wakes the upper one to give. His picture is the eye, which (as the natural scientists of his day held) sends out a fine light to take in what it sees. When a person closes his eyes the "channel" of sight stops. So too, "when through many sins the faces turn away" (line 706), the channels are "broken". The Zohar he quotes: when people's deeds are right, the crowns above "look at one another straight... and then God is called One and His Name One" (line 707).

**The aleph.** Some drew the sefirot as the letter aleph, "to hint that it is all one complete unity" (line 641), with an opening above the Crown to show that over it is "the one Master who rules all", and an opening below to show it is all for the sake of the worlds below.

## 3. States of consciousness

**Being renewed (S65, Given right now).** Lines 697-698 are S65 in the Ramak's words, with his own image: I am like river water, new at each instant; if His flow stopped for a moment I would not be. This is the Kabbalist root of what the Baal Shem Tov and the Gate of Unity will build on.

**Nested (RK-S04, proposed).** Inside: I am the innermost of many circles, and each circle holds the one inside it; the last and widest is not a circle, and it holds everything and is held by nothing. How to enter: follow the Ramak's list outward from where you are (the earth, the heavens, the forces, the worlds, the sefirot) to "all of them inside the Infinite" (line 671); then remember his warning, "He has no place" (line 673), and let the picture go. What it adds to S63 (The world held): the feeling of being inside rather than under. Guard: the picture must break at the end, or it makes God a place (`mashal`).

**Before the King always (S33 Before Whom).** The Ramak reads "I have set God before me always" as David's practice: "he said this out of fear of Heaven before him all day, and he would think as if the Shechinah were opposite him always, and he like a servant before the king, not to waver in his service, little or much":

> והיה חושב כאלו שכינה כנגדו לעולם והוא כעבד לפני המלך לבלתי יתמוטט מעבודתו לא מעט ולא הרבה (line 662, Pardes 6:2:9)

**Heard (RK-S05, proposed).** At the end of the Amidah we take three steps back and bow to the left, which is God's right. Why? So that a person "should be sure that his words were heard, like a man who spoke with the king in his inner room and leaves the king in peace and love":

> שיהיה מובטח שנשמעו דבריו כאדם המדבר עם המלך החדרה ונפטר מהמלך בשלום ואהבה (line 663, Pardes 6:2:10)

And more: though he united the upper worlds only through "the back that can be seen", "the Holy One listened to him with the awesome face" (עכ"ז הקב"ה הקשיב לו בפנים הנוראים, line 663). Inside: I said what I had to say, and it was received. I am leaving the room, not being sent out. The map has S55 (Own words to Him) but no state for after: the quiet certainty of having been heard. How to enter: end prayer, or any words to Him, with a deliberate leave-taking, three steps and a bow, and the thought "He listened to me with His face".

**Rest.** Of the chapter on how the sefirot are bound together: "let the student study this chapter, and in it he will find rest for his soul; his soul will lodge in goodness and dwell securely, and none will make him afraid" (line 687). For the Ramak, understanding the unity is itself rest (S32 Quiet, S84 Trust).

**Separation as the root sin.** The same passage names what Acher (Elisha ben Abuya) did and what Adam did: Adam "came to take apart the tent, and so was taking apart on Shabbat" (line 687). The sin under sins is to see the one as two. This is S10 (Torn in two) and S12 at their root.

**The humility of faces.** "The human being is a shadow from above that became solid and thick in this world" (והאדם הוא צל עליון שנתגשם ונתעבה בעה"ז, line 664). The person's right is the right above.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Shiviti as a posture (C3).** The reading of line 662 gives the guide a plain instruction: think, through the day, as if the Shechinah were in front of you, and you a servant before the king. What it is for: "not to waver in his service, little or much". Not a vision; a way of standing.

**Leaving prayer (RK-T04, proposed).** The three steps back (line 663), read as an exercise in trust: say your words, then take your leave deliberately, sure they were heard, "in peace and love". What it is for: to close prayer with confidence, not with worry about whether it "worked". Ties to `hold` (the quiet after) and `act`. Guard: the Ramak grounds it in the King's listening, not in the quality of our words.

**The ten turning circles (RK-T05, proposed).** At the end of Gate 7 the Ramak gives a real contemplative exercise. The ways the sefirot face each other cannot be written down. So draw ten circles, each with ten faces, and turn them in your mind so that different faces meet: "let him occupy himself with the drawn form before him, and keep at it in contemplation many days, until God lights the eyes of his mind":

> ויתנהג בה בעיון ימים רבים עד יאיר ה' עיני שכלו (line 751, Pardes 7:5:5)

This is a Kabbalist's visual meditation: a diagram held and turned in the mind over days. It is not in the guide's technology (the closest is `picturing` and I1). It belongs to a learner of Kabbalah, not a beginner; we record it and do not propose it for general use. What it teaches the guide: some understanding comes only from long, patient looking at one form, "or from mouth to mouth".

**Doubt (lines 672-673).** On a hard teaching about seven earths, he admits that even the Zohar's sages "were not fully clear" about it. What should a student do with a doubt he cannot answer? "Even if doubts arise for the student, as they happen to us, it is right to hide them and set them aside... and our eyes are to the Lord our God until He favors us":

> ואפי' נמשך מזה אל המעיין ספק או ספיקות כאשר יקרה לנו ראוי להעלימם ולהסתירם (line 673)

The guard is pastoral, not censorship: do not let a doubt become a weapon for "the slack", and do not pretend it is gone; wait on God for the answer. For the guide: a person with doubts is not a failed person; the Ramak had them too ("as happens to us").

**Why parables at all.** "The Torah was like a basket with no handles" until Solomon made parables (line 673): the bodily picture is the handle by which the spiritual is carried. And the student must "take care and guard himself" not to take the handle for the basket (`mashal`).

## 5. The big human questions

**Why do we exist?** Because it rose in His simple will "to reveal His Godliness and to do good with His hidden measures to another" (line 600). Before there was an "other", His Godliness was not known.

**Suffering and judgment.** Judgment cannot live near Him (line 601). It exists only at a distance, and even there it is a matter of faces turned or turned toward (lines 706-707). When many are righteous, "the Assembly of Israel raises good fragrances and is blessed from the holy King, and her face shines" (line 745). The weather above is made by the deeds below.

**Being held.** "He is the place of the world" (line 673); if His flow stopped "for an instant, they would cease" (line 698). Being held is not an event; it is what existing is.

**Love.** The emanation is "a lamp of joined pieces, united and bound in utter unity and bond and desire (החשק)" (line 687). Desire is part of the bond. And of study that goes wrong: "in her love you will always err" (באהבתה תשגה תמיד, line 752), said of his own possible mistakes.

**The soul and prayer.** Prayer is heard "with the awesome face" (line 663); the soul is "a shadow from above" ( line 664).

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Creation renewed every moment.** Line 697-698 is the doctrine of the Gate of Unity, chapters 1-2, in the Pardes: "He brings them into being at every moment... if His flow withdrew for an instant they would cease." The Ramak reaches it from "You give life to them all" (Nehemiah 9:6); the Baal Shem Tov and the Alter Rebbe from "Forever, God, Your word stands in the heavens". The conclusion is the same. What Chassidus adds is the reason the renewal does not show: tzimtzum and the hiding of the speech.
- **"Not Malchut and not the Infinite."** Line 697 comes close to the Tanya's "there is nothing but Him" but stays within the sefirot and the worlds as a chain of cause and effect. The Alter Rebbe's objection (Iggeret HaKodesh 20, Tanya line 1287, quoted in pardes-429-581.md) applies here directly: cause and effect explains the sefirot among themselves, not how something comes from nothing.
- **Kings of Edom.** For the Ramak, the "death" of the kings is a hiding of judgment too close to the Source ( line 606). In the Ari and in Chassidus it becomes the "breaking of the vessels", with sparks fallen into the world that our deeds must raise (F11). The Ramak has no sparks here; he has nearness.
- **Faces.** The Ramak's channels as faces turned toward each other (line 706) is the root of the Chassidic "face to face" and "back to back" (panim and achor). The Tanya's "face" of the soul turned to God is the same image moved inside the person.
