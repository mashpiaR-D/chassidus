## The five findings that most change the guide

1. **The guide already keeps a formulation; it keeps the wrong half.** The live listener (engine.html, `LENS_RULES`) stores a verdict (`root`, `conclusion`, `stance`) and a mood word. It keeps nothing about *what keeps the trouble going* (the cycle) or *what the person already lives on* (strengths). CBT's working model says the cycle is the lever, not the belief alone (Kuyken, Padesky and Dudley 2009; Moorey 2010), and the Chassidic map says the same in its own words: the guide moves a person along a current, and every current ends in the hand (map C3). So the formulation adds two fields that decide the next reply: `cycle` (which of eleven named loops is running, with the person's own links) and `strengths` (what they already do that works, in their words).
2. **Formulation is unreliable where it guesses, so the guide must not act on its guesses.** Trained clinicians agree on the facts of a case and disagree on the inferred core belief; only 44% of formulations were rated good enough (Kuyken et al. 2005). Evidence that individual formulation beats a standard protocol is thin (Ghaderi 2006; Easden and Kazantzis 2018). So every inferred field carries `confidence` and `evidence` (the person's exact words), and the guide may only *teach from* a core perception the person has said or accepted. Until then it may only *offer* it, once, as its own guess (the owner's D08b: mark your construction as yours, let them confirm or reject).
3. **Collaboration here cannot mean questions.** Collaborative conceptualization is built on asking; the owner has forbidden interviewing, feeling questions, X-or-Y and repeat questions (D25, D36). The translation (ours): the guide collaborates by *offering a link and letting the person correct it* ("so the worrying is doing the job of holding it all up"). A correction is the richest update the formulation gets. A silence is not a confirmation.
4. **Strengths are the Chassidic entry, not a garnish.** Strengths-based CBT (Padesky and Mooney 2012) and the Rebbe's accounting both start from what already works: "to know the strengths in their service and strengthen them, and the lacks, to fix them" (Hayom Yom 27 Menachem Av, verified below). The Chassidic reading goes further: a strength is where the hidden love already shows (map S41, C8 "uncovering, not installing"). The guide builds the day's act on a strength the person named, never on a deficit.
5. **Risk lives in the formulation, and it only ratchets up inside a conversation.** A block from `safety.json` (for example no bittul after "I'm nothing anyway", no nothingness after unreality) stays for the rest of the conversation and is carried across days while it is recent. The person's mood improving in one message does not lift it. Danger overrides all fields.

## 1. What CBT means by a case formulation, and what we take

**The idea.** A case formulation (conceptualization) is a working model of one person: what is happening now, what triggers it, what keeps it going, what lies underneath it, and what the person has going for them. Kuyken, Padesky and Dudley describe it as a *crucible* where the person's experience meets theory, built *collaboratively*, *in levels* (first description, then the trigger and maintenance cycle, then the longitudinal story of how beliefs formed) and *incorporating strengths*, not only problems.

- Kuyken, W., Padesky, C. A., & Dudley, R. (2009). *Collaborative Case Conceptualization: Working Effectively with Clients in Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy.* New York: Guilford Press (hardcover dated December 2008). [search] *Theory and clinical method.* - Padesky, C. A., & Mooney, K. A. (2012). Strengths-based cognitive-behavioural therapy: A four-step model to build resilience. *Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy, 19*(4), 283-290. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.1795 [search] *Theory and clinical method.* - Moorey, S. (2010). The six cycles maintenance model: Growing a "vicious flower" for depression. *Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapy, 38*(2) (online November 2009). [search] *Theory; a teaching evaluation with trainees, not an outcome trial.* Six loops keep low mood going: automatic negative thoughts, rumination and self-attack, withdrawal and avoidance, unhelpful behaviour, the mood itself, and low motivation and body symptoms.

**How good is the evidence?** Honestly, modest.

- Reliability: 115 practitioners formulated the same case. They agreed on descriptive facts; agreement fell as the inference deepened (core beliefs); only 44% of formulations were at least "good enough", and quality rose with experience (Kuyken, Fothergill, Musa and Chadwick 2005, *Behaviour Research and Therapy, 43*(9), September 2005) [search]. *Observational.* - Outcome: in a randomized trial for bulimia, individualized CBT did slightly better than a standard manual (Ghaderi 2006, *Behaviour Research and Therapy, 44*(2), 273-288, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2005.02.004) [search]; *one small RCT.* In a naturalistic series, formulation-driven CBT for anxious and depressed outpatients produced outcomes comparable to the RCT benchmarks (Persons, Roberts, Zalecki and Brechwald 2006, *Behaviour Research and Therapy, 44*(7), 1041-1051, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2005.08.005) [search]; *observational, no control.* A state of the science review concluded that the evidence that formulation itself improves outcomes is still limited (Easden and Kazantzis 2018, *Journal of Clinical Psychology, 74*(3), 356 ff.) [search]; *review.* - Tracking progress and adjusting helps: feedback systems that flag people "not on track" improved outcomes for exactly those people (Shimokawa, Lambert and Smart 2010, *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology, 78*(3), 298-311, https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019247) [search]; *meta-analysis.* Self-monitoring of progress promotes goal attainment, more so when the monitoring is recorded (Harkin et al. 2016, *Psychological Bulletin, 142*(2), 198-229, https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025) [search]; *meta-analysis of experiments.*

**What we take (ours).** A formulation is worth keeping for three narrow reasons: it stops the guide repeating itself (D18, D36), it tells the guide which loop the next reply should interrupt, and it lets tomorrow start where today ended (D32's ladder log). It is not worth keeping as a diagnosis, and the guide must not trust its own deep inferences more than the research trusts a trained clinician's.

## 2. The fields, and where each sits on the Chassidic map

Each field has a psychological job and a Chassidic place. The places come from consciousness-map/map.json (86 states, 8 currents, rules R1 to R14) and from the transformation cards (seed_cards.json; the full set in transformations/_build/cards.py, to be built as transformations/cards.json).

| Field | CBT job | Chassidic place | Filled from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `presenting` | the problem list, in their words | the **entry state** they are in (S01 to S21), placed fresh each turn (map R2) | what they said this turn |
| `core_perception` | the core belief or conclusion | the **starting perception** of a card group ("everything depends on me", "I'm my mistakes") | their words; confirmed or offered |
| `triggers` | situations that fire the problem | where the **hiding** is thickest for them (S12, S73): the bills, the 4 am, the ex's name | their words |
| `cycle` | the maintaining loop | the **current running backwards** (see §3): the loop is the current they need, turned the wrong way | inferred, then offered |
| `strengths` | resources, resilience | the **hidden love already showing** (S41, C8), the act already joined to good thought (S29) | their words only |
| `open_to` | engagement, values, fit | **frame** (D17, D28: jewish, faith, unsure), how religious language lands, what they can hold today | their words; never guessed |
| `risk` | risk assessment | **S21 danger**, and the safety.json blocks | every turn, before anything |
| `target` | the new, more helpful belief | the card's **target perception**, and the **state** it opens (S65 given right now, S84 trust, S86 the other held) | the card |
| `program` | the treatment plan | the rung of the manual's ladder and the day of the track | PROGRAMS |

**Why "open to" matters (D28).** The same core perception gets different lips. "I'm my mistakes" gets "a part of G-d above" for a Jew who speaks in Jewish terms, "made in the image of G-d" for anyone else (claims U01), and for someone unsure, the plain experiential test with the name of G-d last (D27, map R13). The formulation carries `open_to.frame`, `open_to.religious_language` (allergic, neutral, curious, practicing) and `open_to.can_hold` (exhausted, steady, seeking), and every card is dressed from them.

## 4. How the formulation is updated each turn

These rules are in `formulation.json` under `update_rules`. The short form:

1. **Risk first.** Before any other field, scan the message against `safety.json` signs. Danger sets `risk.level = "danger"` and nothing else is updated this turn; the protocol runs. A contraindication adds its blocks to `risk.blocks` with the turn number.
2. **Place fresh.** `presenting.state` is set from this message alone (map R2). The previous state goes into `presenting.history` (last five).
3. **Their words or nothing.** Every value that describes the person carries `evidence`: a short quote of theirs. No quote, no value. Never infer religion, background, diagnosis or a body symptom (D31, D43).
4. **Three levels of certainty.** `said` (they said it), `accepted` (the guide offered it and they agreed or built on it), `guess` (inferred). The guide teaches only from `said` or `accepted`. A `guess` can be offered once, marked as the guide's own; after one offer that is ignored or rejected it is dropped for the day.
5. **A correction beats everything.** "No, it's not that, it's..." replaces the field and records the old guess in `rejected` so it is never offered again.
6. **The cycle needs two links in their words** before the guide names it back, and it is named once, as a picture of the loop, never as a diagnosis.
7. **Strengths are only what they did or do.** Not traits the guide praises. "I still get the kids to school" is a strength; "you're so resilient" is not recorded and not said.
8. **One target at a time.** `target.card` changes only when the card's loop has run (locate, teach, operate, experience, name, integrate) or the person has clearly moved to another trouble. A new trouble is taken into the same meditation (D43), so the cycle usually stays and the trigger changes.
9. **Blocks ratchet.** A block is never lifted inside the conversation. Across days, a block lapses after seven days with no new sign, except danger history, which is kept while the person keeps the save on.
10. **Nothing is shown.** The formulation is never displayed, recited or summarized to the person, and its ids are never said (map R7). The person hears only its effect: a reply that fits.

## 5. Across days

The engine already has a "remember" switch (engine.html `load()`/`save()`, keys `oo_lens`, `oo_sits`, `oo_walks`). The formulation is saved under the same switch and nowhere else.

- **Kept:** the fields above, the last five states, the program and day, the last seven carry lines and whether the act was done, active blocks with dates. Evidence quotes are capped at twelve words each.
- **Not kept:** the transcript, anything the person said about another named person beyond "my son", "my boss", and any danger wording (only the fact and the date).
- **On return:** the guide does not recite the formulation or ask how the week went (D25, D36). It opens with one line that shows it remembers the *thread*, not the file ("Last time it was the rent and the 4 am loop"), then meets what they bring today, placed fresh. The program resumes only if they pick it up.
- **Forgetting:** "forget this" or turning off the switch clears everything. The guide says plainly that it is stored on this device (SAFETY R15: never promise secrecy it cannot keep).

## 6. Where psychology and Chassidus part ways

- **What the target is.** In CBT the new belief is *more realistic or more helpful*, and it is tested against evidence. In Chassidus the target perception is *truer about what holds*: "I am not the one keeping it in being" is not a balanced thought but a claim about reality (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 1, line 785, verified in map S02). The guide uses CBT's method (test it on something real, D44) and keeps Chassidus's claim. It never presents the teaching as "a helpful reframe".
- **What lies underneath.** CBT's longitudinal level digs for where a belief formed (childhood, trauma). The guide does not dig: no interviewing (D36), no therapy (owner's rule), and the sources point elsewhere. What is underneath the verdict is not an old wound to be processed but a self the verdict never reached: "the second soul in a Jew is truly a part of G-d above" (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 2, line 86, verified in map S11), and for anyone, the image of G-d (claims U01). The `origin` field is kept only if the person volunteers it.
- **What a strength is.** In CBT a strength is a personal resource. In Chassidus it is also evidence of the hidden love, "like a candle flame that always flickers upward by its nature" (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 19, line 286, verified in map S41). The guide names the act, not the flame; the flame is named only when they are steady.
- **What accounting is for.** CBT self-monitoring measures change. The Rebbe's accounting is a merchant's ledger kept "from time to time", strengths first, and it ends in a good year, not a score: "to know the strengths in their service and strengthen them, and the lacks in them, to fix them" (Hayom Yom 27 Menachem Av, line 425, below). Both agree it should be concrete. The sources add a timing rule psychology also found (see PROGRAMS: worry and sadness get a fixed time, not all day).
- **What collaboration is.** In CBT the client is a co-investigator. Here the person is also a soul the guide addresses, and the guide never rules (D02). Collaboration is offering and being corrected; authority stays with the sources, "each person by the measure of his own understanding" (Hayom Yom 27 Menachem Av, line 425: כל חד וחד לפום שיעורא דילי').

## 7. WHAT THE GUIDE SHOULD DO

1. **Keep the formulation every turn** in the JSON below, replacing the lens. Run the listener once per message; the reply prompt gets only `presenting.state`, `core_perception` (if said or accepted), `cycle.id` (if two links are in their words), one `strength`, `open_to`, `risk.blocks` and `target`. Test: the reply prompt never receives a field with `certainty = "guess"` as a fact.
2. **Risk before formulation.** A danger sign stops all other updates that turn and runs the protocol. Test: in a turn with a danger sign, no card, program or strength appears in the reply.
3. **Offer, don't ask.** To check a guess, the guide states it once as its own ("I might be wrong, but it sounds like the worrying is standing guard") and never follows it with a question. Test: no reply both offers a guess and ends in "?" (D25), and no guess is offered twice.
4. **Name the loop once, as a picture.** When the cycle has two links in their words, one reply may draw it back ("the more it matters, the more you check, the more tired you are, the louder it gets") and then show where the teaching cuts it. Test: the loop picture appears at most once per conversation and uses their links, not clinical terms (never "rumination", "avoidance", "cognitive").
5. **Build the act on a strength.** The integration step of every card uses a strength from `strengths` when one exists. Test: when `strengths` is non-empty, the day's act names or extends one of them.
6. **Dress by `open_to`.** Jewish practices only for `frame = jewish`; the practices given to all people for `faith`; the name of G-d last for `unsure` (D17, D28, map R13). Test: no mitzvah word to a person whose frame is not jewish unless they used it first.
7. **Respect blocks for the whole conversation.** Test: after an R03 sign ("I'm nothing anyway"), no bittul, humility or "as nothing" in any later reply that day.
8. **On return, one line of thread, no questions about the week.** Test: the first reply of a returning day contains no question about how the practice went.
9. **Never show, name or summarize the formulation.** Test: no reply contains a field name, a state id, a cycle name or "your pattern is".
10. **Forget on request**, and describe storage truthfully.
