## 1. The short answer

The Piaseczner Rebbe is the Chassidic master who wrote most plainly about what meditation is like from the inside. His whole method rests on one claim: the soul is already there, already moved, already singing and crying toward God, and we simply do not hear it. So the work is not to manufacture a feeling. It is to get quiet enough, and honest enough, to notice what is already moving, to give it a shape (a picture, a sentence, a tune, words of one's own), to let it grow until it carries the whole person, and then to put it into a deed before it fades. He uses almost every door there is: quieting the stream of thoughts, watching them, picturing scenes "as if before your eyes", melody, talking to God in your own language, the body and its effort, a small secret fellowship, and the Shema said as if you were ready to give your life. He says the owner's thesis nearly word for word: "the knowledge of God is in the knowledge of oneself" (Esh Kodesh 542), "the first work is to find ourselves" (Derekh HaMelekh 1027). But the self he means is the hidden holy soul, not the ego. The ego is exactly what blocks the way, and the soul, once found, turns out to be "a part of the One" and wants to give itself back. Set against Chabad contemplation (hisbonenus), he agrees on the goal (knowledge that reaches the heart and changes the person, and a self that gets out of the way) and differs sharply on the road: he starts from feeling, picture and body rather than from a concept, he empties the mind before he fills it, and he trusts arousal from almost any source, where the Mitteler Rebbe's Kuntres HaHitpa'alut suspects every arousal that the self can feel.

**How they fit.** The graded series is Chovat HaTalmidim (boys), Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim (young men), then Mevo HaShe'arim as the gate of the third book, which he did not live to finish. Bnei Machshava Tova is the private manual for the fellowship that the second book's Chevraya chapter proposes. Tzav VeZeruz is the same teaching lived in the first person. Derekh HaMelekh is the teaching given aloud, week by week, and its appendix holds the one account of hashkatah. Esh Kodesh is what remains of all of it when the person is being crushed.

**On hashkatah specifically.** The brief for this file expected hashkatah in Bnei Machshava Tova or Tzav VeZeruz and not in Esh Kodesh. The reports confirm the second half and correct the first. Hashkatah is not in Bnei Machshava Tova (its report says so) or in Tzav VeZeruz (its report says so), and a letters-only count of the word in all seven files finds it only in Derekh HaMelekh. There it is "Inyan HaShkatah" (lines 1553-1556), written from memory by a student who heard it in 5696 or 5697 (1935-37). It sits right after a 1921 talk to yeshiva students that cites the "Tzav VeZeruz" booklet (line 1552), which may be where the association came from. Its seeds are older and in his own hand: "waiting an hour" to halt the stream of thought (Derekh HaMelekh 89-90) and "calm yourself and watch your thoughts" (Hakhsharat 310-311).

## 3. The method, as one ordered path

He never lays out one master sequence, but his books agree on an order, and Bnei Machshava Tova sums it up (336-338): first conduct and thought change, then prayer warms, then one learns to use joy and brokenness and to "look", and reaches some self-giving in the Shema, and only then does higher sight begin. Below, every concrete practice the reports found, arranged as one path. Each has its book and line and one short Hebrew line.

## Step 0. Before anything: feel the lack, and start from what is already moving

- **Feel the distance, don't just know it.** Only one who feels the pain may join the fellowship. «ולא שידע בדעתו לבד את מצבו הנמוך» (Bnei Machshava Tova 21)
- **Everything is already in you; get to know it.** Don't ask for new feelings. Notice what is stirring now. «ועליך להשתדל רק להכיר אותך ואת אשר בך מתרחש» (Hakhsharat 87)
- **Any feeling is a door, even a bodily one.** A business worry, a joy, a sigh. «כל התעוררות אפלו אם גופנית, היא מפתח לנפש» (Bnei Machshava Tova 100)
- **Grab whatever corner of the soul is awake**, even a prayer for your own needs; don't shut it down to wait for a holier feeling. «מוטב שיאחז בקצה הזה של הנפש שמצא עתה» (Derekh HaMelekh 828)
- **Start with the clearer and more joyful feelings**, not the faint ones or the soul's pains (Hakhsharat 89). «ומוטב שלא תתחיל בהתרגשויות כאבי הנפש» (Hakhsharat 89)
- **Borrow a love you already have.** Before prayer, remember your love for your children, "even for your money", then turn it toward God. «יזכיר לו אהבתו לבניו אף לממונו» (Esh Kodesh 213). The same "straw before the logs" is in Mevo HaShe'arim: «אז מדליק מקודם קש או תבן» (Mevo 166).

## Step 1. Set a time and a place, and step out of the world

- **Separate from the world before prayer**, and know whom you are about to speak to. «וצריך להפריש עצמו קודם התפלה מן העולם ולכוון שהוא מתקרב עתה לדבר עם ד'» (Derekh HaMelekh 83)
- **Hide, or face the wall.** «הסתר את עצמך בחדר מיוחד אם אפשר לך, ואם לאו הסב פניך אל הקיר» (Tzav VeZeruz 15)
- **Stand in one place, by the wall or with eyes shut, and pray aloud.** «בתפלתך תעמד במקום אחד, אצל הקיר או בסגירת עינים, ותתפלל בקול» (Bnei Machshava Tova 275)
- **Or go alone, to the forest if you can, for an hour or two**, and sing with the sun and the birds. «והתבודד יחידי. ואם אפשר לך לך היערה» (Tzav VeZeruz 48)
- **Put your worries down on purpose, for at least an hour.** Only one who can do this can leap above himself. «רק מי שיכול להפקיר ולהשליך את דאגותיו על שעה עכ״פ» (Tzav VeZeruz 64)
- **Set fixed times** when God is revealed within you, if you cannot be aflame always. «עכ"פ יקבע לו זמנים שיתגלה ד' בקרבו» (Derekh HaMelekh 1379). A working man too must make "free times" (Derekh HaMelekh 688).

## Step 2. Quiet the stream (hashkatah)

His reason: the self's own busyness is what blocks inspiration from above, and in sleep it gets in because the mind is still. So reach a sleep-like quiet while awake. «כי הישות של האדם הוא המתנגד להשראה ממרום» (Derekh HaMelekh 1555). The student's record gives the steps (Derekh HaMelekh 1555-1556):

1. **Watch your thoughts for a few minutes: "what am I thinking?"** «שיתחיל האיש להביט על מחשבותיו שעה קלה לערך איזה רגעים היינו מה אני חושב» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
2. **Feel the head slowly empty.** «אז ירגיש לאט לאט שראשו מתרוקן ומחשבותיו עמדו משטפן הרגיל» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
3. **Say one verse**, such as "Hashem Elokim Emet", to tie the emptied head to one holy thought. «ואז יתחיל לאמר פסוק אחד כגון ד' אלקים אמת» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
4. **Then ask for what you need** (faith, love, awe), in plain words. His Yiddish for love: "I would so want to be close to the holy Creator." «איך וואלט געוואלט זיין אזוי נאהנט צום הייליגען באשעפער» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
5. **Softly, never forcefully**, because force wakes the ego. «אבל לא שיאמר זאת בחזקה» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
6. **Positively, not negatively**: speak of gaining alacrity, not of banishing laziness (like telling a crying child "don't cry"). «אבל לא באופן שלילי רק באופן חיובי» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
7. **Another way in: gaze at the small hand of a clock**, which hardly moves. «בהבטה על שעון על המחוג הקטן שכמעט אינו זז» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
8. **Close with a verse in melody** (step 6 below). Practise for several weeks.

The older, first-hand form of the same move is **"waiting an hour"**: the first chassidim halted the whole stream of plans before prayer, and the divine part appeared without a garment. «ובאם היה האדם מעמיד ומפסיק על שעה את כל שטף מחשבותיו ורצוניו, היה חלק אלקות נראה לו בלא לבוש» (Derekh HaMelekh 90). His tool for halting it: think that you have no tomorrow to plan.

## Step 3. Watch the thoughts: look *at*, not *in*

This is his most original technique (Hakhsharat 290-313), and it is the working heart of hashkatah.

- **The law: looking at a thought dims it; dwelling in it feeds it.** Try it with the image of a relative: ask "what am I picturing now?" and it fades. «מן ההבטה בעצמה שאתה מביט עלי׳ מיד תכהה התמונה מדמיונך» (Hakhsharat 294). Mevo HaShe'arim states it as a law of the soul: «שכאשר מביטים על מחשבה אחת שבנו, היא נמסת» (Mevo 390).
- **Stray thoughts in prayer: stop, calm yourself, sit or lean, and watch them** "with a restful heart, not with agitation and not with excessive effort". «ודוקא במנוחת לב ולא בהתרגזות ולא בהתאמצות יתירה» (Hakhsharat 310). They thin out each moment, «עד שממש כנ״ל כמו במטאטא נקית את מוחך» (Hakhsharat 311).
- **For a bad thought, think *about* it, never the thought itself.** «השמר והזהר שלא את המחשבה רעה תחשוב, רק אודות המחשבה» (Hakhsharat 299). If you cannot break away, ask yourself for twenty seconds (Hakhsharat 302).
- **For the trait you keep falling in: look again and again.** The tenth time you see the thought is foolish. «ריבוי ההבטה, להרבות הבט על כל מחשבה המתרחשת בקרבנו» (Tzav VeZeruz 136)
- **A day's inventory.** Gather a day's thoughts and look at them; most are only the world's events. «יצבור נא האדם מחשבות מן המעת לעת ויסתכל בהם» (Derekh HaMelekh 1337)
- **Don't fight them.** «כי כל מה שירצה לדחותם הם יתחזקו בו» (Derekh HaMelekh 1551). Strengthen a good thought instead: «א״א לדחות את החושך בלתי אם באור» (Chovat 249).
- **An unnamed heavy mood: ask "since when?"** and trace it to the hour it began. If you find it, the heart lightens at once. «אז העצה שיתחיל לחפש בקרבו מאיזה שעה התחילה קשיות ערפו» (Hakhsharat 313)

## Step 4. Give it a shape: strong thought and picturing

- **Thought and imagination are one kind**, and strong thought needs pictures in it. «המחשבה חזקה, והדמיון, מין אחד הם» (Hakhsharat 99); «אבל אי אפשר למחשבה חזקה בלא ציורים בה» (Hakhsharat 98)
- **Simple and broad, not deep and clever.** «לא בשכליות ועמקיות תחשוב אז רק במחשבה פשוטה ורחבה» (Hakhsharat 94)
- **The permitted picture: before the Throne, like a son.** He knows God has no image, but a person of clay needs something to hold (the Raavad). «ציר לך שאתה עומד לפני כסא כבודו ואתה מתפלל ומבקש ממנו ית' פשוט כבן שעומד ומתחנן מאביו» (Bnei Machshava Tova 91). It is a handrail and falls away by itself: «אז התמונה הציור גופני הזה ממילא יתבטלו» (Bnei Machshava Tova 90)
- **Eyes to the sky; picture the other side of it.** «הבט נא השמימה והתבונן, אמץ את מחשבתך וחשוב» (Chovat 132)
- **Take part in a holy scene "as if you were there"** (the Exodus, the Temple). «תשתדל להשתתף בכל מאורעות הקודש שעברו כאילו גם אתה היית אז» (Hakhsharat 199)
- **Se'udah shelishis in the dark, with friends.** «ובחושך אתם יושבים» (Hakhsharat 106). Stretch one feeling of awe, slowly (step 5).
- **When nothing moves: picture yourself a tzaddik**, your soul in its root, and stay with it. «צייר בעצמך שאתה צדיק, וראה בדמיונך את גודל נשמתך בשורשה» (Tzav VeZeruz 66)
- **Picture yourself lost in a desert, your father near**, and cry "teach me Your way". «יצייר האדם בעצמו כאלו תועה הוא במדבר» (Derekh HaMelekh 1143)
- **If you cannot picture the holy thing, come at it sideways.** Straining kills the image. Start from a memory you do not care about (being called up to the Torah) and let the Name appear inside it. «רק יתחיל לחשוב מדבר שאין רצונו בו» (Mevo 395)
- **Give a stirring a form and look at it (histaklus).** «להוציא בה צורה ולהסתכל בצורת הרגשה» (Bnei Machshava Tova 144). In Derekh HaMelekh this is the soul's "sketch" (reshimah), which he fences off from fantasy: «אבל לא רשימה של דמיון וציורי דמיונות רושמת, רק ג"כ רשימה של מחשבה» (Derekh HaMelekh 271)
- **Don't hunt for images once the feeling is there.** Linger in it instead. «לא תחפש דמיונות ומשלים רק תתעכב ותתבונן בהתרגשותך הקדושה» (Hakhsharat 184)

## Step 5. One sentence, slowly, repeated until it widens

- **Say one line of awe calmly, and repeat it only while it grows; think of God, not the words.** «ובנחת חשוב או אמור זאת, וכפול אותם אם תרגיש שמוסיפים ומרחיבים את יראתך לד׳» and «ובלבד שלא תחשוב את התיבות בלבד שאתה אומר, רק את ד׳» (Hakhsharat 110)
- **The affirmation for the day:** "You surround me and fill my body and my spirit." «ואתה מקיף אותי וממלא את גופי ורוחי» (Chovat 129). Turned over and over, it widens until the room is full of it: «עד שגם חדרו אף כל סביבו מתמלאים מן מחשבה זו» (Chovat 228)
- **The daily "everything is God except me".** «ורק אני ברצוני הפרטי הוצאתי את עצמי לדבר נמצא בפני עצמו» (Bnei Machshava Tova 182). In the street: «כל העולם אלקות הוא, גרגיר חול אשר תחת רגלי, האויר שאני נושם בקרבי» (Bnei Machshava Tova 286)
- **Meditate, picture and spread one truth through mind and heart.** «רק שיתמיד במחשבתו זו להגות בה לדמותה ולפשטה במוחו ולבו, שהוא ית' הנמצא היחידי» (Derekh HaMelekh 1308)
- **Widen a small good stirring by saying it over.** «כל הרהור טוב ורצון טוב ורוח התשובה הבא אל האדם מתחילה הוא קצר וצריכים לעבוד להרחיבה» (Derekh HaMelekh 696)
- **Join every thought of God to yourself at once:** "and what am I doing before Him?" «רק יחשוב תיכף ומה אני עושה לפניו» (Derekh HaMelekh 193)

## Step 6. Melody

- **Take a tune, face the wall or close your eyes, and picture yourself before the Throne.** At first you sing to wake the soul; then it sings by itself. «קח לך איזו תנועה של ניגון, תסב את פניך אל הקיר, או רק תסגור את עיניך» and «מעט מעט תרגיש שנפשך התחילה כבר לנגן מעצמה» (Hakhsharat 279; the same in Bnei Machshava Tova 237)
- **No new tunes needed; a whispered hum is enough.** «כי יש מי שמנגן רק המיה בלחש וקולו נשמע במרום» (Bnei Machshava Tova 245)
- **Let words come out while singing**, even words not in the siddur. «כי כגון אלו מחצב הנפש הם» (Hakhsharat 281)
- **Learn with melody**: the taste of Torah shows mainly in niggun. «הטוב שבתורה נתגלה בעיקר בנגינה» (Derekh HaMelekh 692)
- **After quieting, sing one verse**: "Horeni Hashem darkecha" in the Rebbe's own tune. «צוה לאמר הפסוק הורני ד' דרכיך בניגונו המיוחד של אדמו"ר» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)

## Step 7. Speak to God in your own words

- **Pour out your heart before the pictured Throne, in any language you understand.** «ושופך לפניו את לבך בשיח ותפילה כפי שעולה על לבך בכל לשון שאתה שומע» (Tzav VeZeruz 15). Name the thing itself (his sample prayer leaves a blank for it, lines 17-19). Why it works: «הדבור בעצמו גדול כחו להוציא את החרצובות מקרב הנפש» (Tzav VeZeruz 15)
- **A sorrow or joy in the day: step aside at once, face the wall, say a matching psalm, then speak freely.** «ילך תכף הצידה ויעמיד עצמו אל הכתל ויאמר איזה קאפיטלאך תהלים» (Bnei Machshava Tova 119). Say the psalm as if David wrote it for you: «רק כאילו תיקן דוד זאת במכוון בשבילך» (Hakhsharat 274)
- **Every stirring, however faint, should be spoken**, or it fades. His model: «רבש"ע משתוקק אני אליך וממעמקי המצולות» (Derekh HaMelekh 431). «כשמדברה כל נפשו יוצאת» (Derekh HaMelekh 494)
- **Keep the words that burst out of you**, and use them again. «כי דבורים אלו ניצוצי נפשך הם, ממנה נחצבו» (Bnei Machshava Tova 274)
- **Put yourself into the words first; then God becomes "You"**, as a face appears in water. «שעצמותו ימצא בהדיבורים של תפילתו» (Esh Kodesh 251)
- **Turn every desire into a prayer**, and keep praying until the deeper wanting surfaces. «ובכל רצון שעולה על לבו צריך לדעת כי תפילה לה' היא והוא צריך לקדשו» (Derekh HaMelekh 1044)
- **When words stop, keep reaching without them**, again and again, until the stone on the heart splits. «בלא דיבור רק במוח ולב וכל קרביו. עוד ועוד פעם, מרגיש שהאבן מתפוצץ» (Derekh HaMelekh 860)
- **Stress the word "You".** «תדייק את ה-״אתה״-, ותצייר בדעתך שלד׳ אשר לנגדך אתה אומר» (Chovat 134)

## Step 8. Body and effort

- **For most people it is easier to start from the lower soul** (body, senses, picture) than from reasoning. «יותר יקל לאיש לעורר את עצמו כשיתחיל לעורר את הנפש והרוח» (Chovat 291)
- **Hard effort drags the soul out of hiding**: dance hard at Simchat Torah, pray aloud. «אוחז הוא את נפשו בערפה ובע״כ מוציאה מן מחבואה» (Hakhsharat 54)
- **Pray loudly when the heart is shut**, like rolling a stone off a well. «כמי שרוצה לגול בחוזק את האבן ממקור לבו ונפשו» (Chovat 294)
- **Mouth first; the heart follows.** «האיש יכניס עצמו בהדיבורים ובעבדות, וההתלהבות תבא ממילא» (Mevo 198); «מ״מ ידבר בפיו בלבד, ועי״ז גם לבו יתעורר» (Esh Kodesh 504)
- **Push through the crowd.** When you cannot concentrate, picture forcing your way through a crowd toward God and strain the body a little with it, but keep the mind on "toward God", not on the strain. «צייר בדמיונך שרוצה אתה להדחק דרך המון עם רב אל המקום אשר שם האלהים» and «ובלבד שלא תשמש את מוחך להתאמצות והתכווצות גופך» (Chovat 250)
- **Eyes closed, gather everything inward and push the whole soul toward God**, again and again, from the feeling of your own lack. «דחיפה אחר דחיפה אל ד'» (Derekh HaMelekh 608)
- **Change your place** to change your mind. «תשתדל בכל יכולתך ללכת אל מקום אחר» (Chovat 179)
- **But do not insist on feeling.** Forcing feeling makes the heart go dead. «אל יתעקש להתעורר דוקא» (Hakhsharat 59). The effort he asks for is effort of service, not squeezing out an emotion (see section 9).

## Step 9. The fellowship

- **A small group, no officers, meeting at least three times a week**, learning guidance slowly and applying it to themselves (Bnei Machshava Tova 314-332; Hakhsharat 340-382).
- **Each takes one confidant** to open his whole heart to. «יקח לו כל אחד מהם חבר לעצמו שלפניו יגלה את כל מצפוני לבבו» (Bnei Machshava Tova 325)
- **Talk together about God, even with nothing new to say.** «ואף שאין להם להגיד חדשות זה לזה» (Chovat 211)
- **Quiet, not publicity.** «כן כל עבודה שיש בה התגלות הנפש מתנגדת היא לשאון ופרסום» (Bnei Machshava Tova 326)
- **A little drink, then a song, then a dance if it comes** (Bnei Machshava Tova 320; Hakhsharat 276; Mevo 305). He wants no drinking to get drunk (Bnei Machshava Tova 320), and he warns that the coarse use the same opening for sin (Hakhsharat 276). *This is his practice for a supervised group of chassidim. The guide does not recommend drink (mashkeh), ever; see section 8.*

## Step 10. The Shema: the leap

- **Stop a few moments before Shema.** «קדם ק״ש הפסק איזה רגעים וחשוב» (Chovat 200)
- **Say aloud first that you accept the yoke and are ready to give your life**, because at first it will be hard to say, which shows it is not yet real. «הנני מוכן למסור את עצמי לשריפה» (Hakhsharat 426). Then pause, picture the fire, and only then begin: «ולא תתחיל מיד את שמע רק הפסק רגעים אחדים» (Hakhsharat 434). Only three times a week, because a device used daily stops working (Hakhsharat 436).
- **The leap itself.** «ובשמחה אתה עושה קפיצה אחת דרך המוקד אל האין סוף» (Bnei Machshava Tova 188)
- **The daily acceptance before the morning Shema.** «רבש"ע מכניע ומבטל אני את כל עצמי גופי ונשמתי אליך» (Derekh HaMelekh 1255)

## Step 11. Turn the feeling into an act, and hold on

- **Clothe a good stirring at once in learning or a mitzvah.** «שכשבא איזה הרהור טוב באיש יעשה בו מצוה או ילמוד בו, ובזה ילבישו בקדושה» (Hakhsharat 270)
- **Longings are good only if you work with them.** «התשוקין והכסופין טובים רק אם עובדים בהם» (Tzav VeZeruz 65)
- **In the high moment, say a resolution aloud, from the heart, several times.** «כי זאת חקת הנפש, נכנעת היא לפני דבורים היוצאים בחזקה מן הלב» (Chovat 136). Do the accounting right after prayer, not during it (Chovat 137).
- **At the end of prayer, grip.** «עכ"פ בסוף תפלתו צריך לאמץ את נפשו ולהדקה אל הקדושה כיתד שלא תמוט» (Derekh HaMelekh 463)
- **Leave an hour after prayer for what rises by itself**; the pause is also work. «גם הזמן הזה של החדלה עבודה היא, עבודה של עליית הטפיים מעצמם» (Derekh HaMelekh 224)
- **Engrave what you felt in the crisis so it stays.** «צריכים לחקוק את הדברים בלבנו שתשאר ההכנעה והיראה מלפניו ית׳» (Esh Kodesh 86)

## Step 12. Afterwards, at a set time: the honest check

- **Test yourself only sometimes, never in the middle of prayer.** «נסה את הנסיונות לפעמים ורק לפעמים» (Hakhsharat 148)
- **At set times, strip off everything and stand "like a naked golem"**, asking whether it has all been a mistake. «צריך האיש לעשות לו זמנים להתפשט מכל התלבשות עבודתו ויעמוד כגולם ערום בלא לבוש» (Derekh HaMelekh 215)
- **After every hour of study or prayer: are you the same man?** «אינו אותו האיש של אחר שעות הבטלה» (Esh Kodesh 525)
- **Measure by will, not wish.** «הסתכל ברצונך, אבל רק ברצונך ולא בברכתך» (Tzav VeZeruz 37)

## 4. The inner world from inside

What follows is the order of the states as his books describe them, in his own images.

**1. Deafness: the soul cries and we don't hear it.** The soul trembles over the blows we give it, but we hear it the way a sleeper stung by a fly dreams a crate fell on him. «משל לאיש ישן אשר עקץ אותו זבוב במצחו» (Hakhsharat 84). Its unease is taken for hunger: «ויחשוב שרעב הוא, צמא» (Hakhsharat 85). It lives next door and weeps while we celebrate: «יכול להיות שהנשמה יושבת ובוכה על פגמיה» (Derekh HaMelekh 116). A vague unease drowned in food or vodka was really the soul putting out a limb: «מין הושטת אבר מאברי הנשמה החוצה» (Bnei Machshava Tova 139).

**2. The pain of distance, which is already nearness.** The worldly man's restlessness is a hidden longing: «בנפשם פנימה יש מין דאגה של עמל ותלאה» (Derekh HaMelekh 130). The ache is not even his: «לא האיש הוא המרגיש את הצער הזה, רק הניצוץ אלקי שבקרבו» (Derekh HaMelekh 518). Worry over one's low state is God holding a person by his garment (Derekh HaMelekh 315).

**3. Stillness: the head empties.** In hashkatah, «ראשו מתרוקן» (Derekh HaMelekh 1556). What is left when the flood stops is not empty: the divine part «נראה לו בלא לבוש» (Derekh HaMelekh 90). On Shabbat, rest is «השבת הנפש אל קרבו», the soul returning into itself (Esh Kodesh 540).

**4. The first stirring: a feather moves.** The aim is to make the soul tremble a little, «לזעזע קצוות נוצותי׳ בבחירתו» (Hakhsharat 31). Feeling is the moment the soul becomes visible to itself: the lover says «לא יאמר את ראובן האהוב אני מרגיש, אך את האהבה אני מרגיש» (Hakhsharat 44).

**5. Pictures rise by themselves.** After real practice, thoughts you never imagined «מעצמן מתחדשות ועולות לך מקרבך» (Hakhsharat 256), from "the picturing soul", the faculty that makes our dreams (Hakhsharat 112). A new kind of thought appears «שלא ידעת מקדם שהיא נמצאת בך» (Bnei Machshava Tova 130).

**6. Warmth, then fire.** Being moved (hisragshus) still wants the world too. Being aflame (hislahavus) cannot stop: «אי אפשר לך לחדול מן התרגשותך זו ולהתפלל בקרירות» (Hakhsharat 51). The fire image: ordinary love grips its object like fire gripping wood; love of God «רק באש היא נאחזת, כעין שלהבת הקשורה בגחלת הבוערת» (Hakhsharat 48).

**7. The soul sings and leaves its sheath.** «ויותר תצא נפשך מנרתיקה לעוף אל על» (Bnei Machshava Tova 240). Then words fall away: "Father, Father." "What do you want, my son?" "Nothing." «רק נפשך מרננת ומשתפכת ומפלטת רק» (Bnei Machshava Tova 242): only "Master of the world, Master of the world". In joy one weeps and in weeping one is joyful (Hakhsharat 283).

**8. The dark room.** At se'udah shelishis the chariot passes through you: «גלגלי המרכבה שעוברים דרך נפשך» (Bnei Machshava Tova 152). Two darknesses wrestle, heaven's and the coming week's: «התרי גוני של חשוכא נאבקים עתה בך» (Bnei Machshava Tova 158). You cling like a prince about to be taken away from his father (Hakhsharat 108).

**9. Face to face, and the wish to hide.** «ועתה הנני נמצא לפניו יתברך פנים אל פנים» (Tzav VeZeruz 71). «רוצה אני להסתיר עצמי בין אנשים, תחת השלחן» (Tzav VeZeruz 71). What he knew becomes real: «הן גם מאז זאת ידעתי, אבל רק ידעתי» (Tzav VeZeruz 72). When the soul truly goes past its garments, «תרגיש כאילו אתה נקרע כרגע מן עמידתך» (Tzav VeZeruz 87), and one is «עד שאף לירא יראת, ולאהב פחדת» (Tzav VeZeruz 87): afraid even to fear, afraid to love.

**10. Contraction toward Him, in moments.** «כאילו נתכווץ כלו אל מקום אחד אליו ית׳» (Esh Kodesh 504), "and that too is cleaving, at least for that moment". Prayer moves between "He" and "You": «באים לו באמצע תפילתו רגעים של התגלות והתקרבות בחי׳ נוכח» (Esh Kodesh 505).

**11. The sudden lifting.** It comes suddenly and cannot be summoned: «פתאום מרגיש שהונף והורם מעל לעולם אל היכלי עליון» (Derekh HaMelekh 1396). The world is not overcome; it simply goes: «לא שהוא צריך להתגבר עליהם רק נטשטשו ונמחקו לגמרי מנגד עיניו» (Derekh HaMelekh 1396). At the wall with eyes closed: «וממש מרגיש הוא איך שלקחוהו עתה בציצית ראשו» (Derekh HaMelekh 1486).

**12. The peak is not delight: the self goes quiet.** «לא עונג ולא שמחה אתה מרגיש בשעה זו רק בטול העצמיות» (Chovat 344), and in the same moment «וגם אתה עצמך בשעה זו מעט לאחד מבני אלהים אתה רואה» (Chovat 344). In the dance: «הכל בטל עתה, אין עולם ולא דאגות» (Tzav VeZeruz 103).

**13. Not knowing what was seen.** «ואף שהוא לא ידע מה ראתה נפשו אף לא ידע אם בכלל ראתה» (Derekh HaMelekh 553). He feels the events of his soul, «אבל בשכלו לא ידע מה הרגיש» (Chovat 302). And afterwards he does not understand himself: «ואח״כ אם נופל גם א״ע ואת ראיתו הקדומה אינו מבין» (Chovat 169). The sign it was real is what it leaves: «אבל יראה ואהבה נתוספות לאיש אחרי עליות נשמה כאלו» (Derekh HaMelekh 553).

**14. Another man.** «וכשמביאים את הנרות מוצא אני את עצמי לאיש אחר» (Tzav VeZeruz 75); after the Shema said this way, «ותרגיש שאחר ק״ש כזו כאיש אחר תהי׳» (Hakhsharat 436). Brokenness and joy «טהרה אחת וכביסה אחת היתה» (Tzav VeZeruz 75). Seeing with the soul begins small: glimpses "through the lattice" (Hakhsharat 335-338), «ראיות הללו אם כי מצערות תהיינה בראשיתן» (Bnei Machshava Tova 338).

**15. The fall, which is built in.** «כי תמיד מוכרח האיש להיות בעולה ויורד» (Derekh HaMelekh 704). «ומי שחושב בדעתו שאינו נופל ממדרגתו, סימן הוא שמעולם לא עלה למצב התרוממות» (Derekh HaMelekh 1549). A heart that feels sealed may be full: «ונדמה לו שלבו אטום. ובאמת גם עתה מלא קדושה הוא» (Derekh HaMelekh 1240).

**16. Under catastrophe.** At his son's bed: «אין כל רגש ולא כל מחשבה מתרחשים, רק אופל ועצב» (Tzav VeZeruz 174); «לבי כמו נסתם מלהתפלל תפילה חזקה» (Tzav VeZeruz 179). In the ghetto, a man loses himself: «עד שגם האיש בעצמו אובד א״ע [ער פערלירט זיך] וא״ע אינו מכיר» (Esh Kodesh 81). Faith is there without being felt: «מאמין הוא גם אז רק שאינו מרגישה» (Esh Kodesh 578). And in the inner rooms God weeps, and one who weeps *with* Him is held up: «ושם בוכה ומילל עמו כביכול יחד» (Esh Kodesh 585). When a person cannot find himself, God goes looking: «בעל האבדה יחזור עלינו למצוא אותנו» (Esh Kodesh 82).

## 5. Self and God

**He states the owner's thesis, more than once and in so many words.**

- «ונודע מהרמב״ם ז״ל ומביאים אותו המקובלים שידיעת ד׳ היא בידיעת עצמו» (Esh Kodesh 542): "the knowledge of God is in the knowledge of oneself." (He turns the Rambam's phrase, which is about God knowing by knowing Himself, to the person's own inner knowing; the Esh Kodesh report notes this.) - «וכשאין לו בה ידיעה גם בבורא ית׳ לא ידע» (Mevo 398): without knowing your soul you will not know the Creator. - «וראשית עבודה היא למצוא את עצמנו ונפשנו הנפזרה בכל העולם» (Derekh HaMelekh 1027): "the first work is to find ourselves and our soul scattered through the whole world", then «כי אם מצאנו נפשנו נוכל לזכות לרוח» (Derekh HaMelekh 1027). - «ואיכה, איה אתה בעצמך» (Derekh HaMelekh 1337): God's first question is "where are you, in yourself?" - «בעל אבידה מחזר על אבדתו, אחר עצמו מחזר» (Derekh HaMelekh 403): the joy of understanding is the one who lost something finding it, and what he lost was himself. - «ראשית ההתגלות בקדושי אֵל, היתה נפשם הישראלית» (Hakhsharat 26): the first revelation the holy ones had was their own soul. - «איפה תמצאהו? בך ובכל סביבותיך» (Bnei Machshava Tova 147): "Where will you find Him? In you, and all around you." - «וכשמגביר את נפשו ומסיר את המחצלת של קנים מרגיש הוא אז את תשוקתו» (Derekh HaMelekh 232): we don't feel our longing because we are far from our own soul; uncover it and you feel the longing, and that longing *is* nearness. - Every creature is searching, and mistakes the object: «תשוקה פנימית וחפישה בלתי נודעת זו לבקש את אדונכם תכלית מציאותכם» (Tzav VeZeruz 149), like orphans who «ואת העץ יחבקו לאמר, אבי אתה» (Tzav VeZeruz 149). This is the owner's "people seek themselves" from the other side: what they chase is Him, unrecognised. - «ואם לא מצא תכליתו אין הוא נמצא» (Derekh HaMelekh 1003): until you find what you were made for, you are not found; and that purpose is your portion of God's thought. - «למה הוא מאמין מפני שיש בו חלק האחד» (Derekh HaMelekh 1387): he believes because there is in him a part of the One. - The true "I" is a spark of God's "I": «האנכי ניצוץ אנכי ד׳ אלהיך» (Chovat 221). - And the book of the series closes with the prayer to see inward: «האר נא את עיני, אשר בקרבי ובסתרי נפשי יסתכלון» (Mevo 408).

**His qualifications. Keep them; they are not footnotes.**

1. **Two selves.** The self to be found is the hidden holy soul. The self to be lost is the ego, "the bodily I", «היינו האני והאנכי הגופני» (Chovat 290), the one who «ברצוני הפרטי הוצאתי את עצמי לדבר נמצא בפני עצמו» (Bnei Machshava Tova 182). «האנכיות של האדם זה עומד ומבדיל בין ד' וביניכם» (Derekh HaMelekh 209). That is the whole reason for hashkatah: «כי הישות של האדם הוא המתנגד להשראה ממרום» (Derekh HaMelekh 1555).
2. **The self you know is borrowed.** «הלא את עצמך שאתה מכיר רק את צל העולם אתה מכיר» (Derekh HaMelekh 1360). So "finding yourself" is not affirming the person you think you are.
3. **The self is found by being drawn to God, not before it.** Until holiness gathers in him, «אז אין האיש עוד בביתו ועצמותו» (Derekh HaMelekh 1344). The Derekh HaMelekh 337-683 report puts it well: the true self is found *in* reaching toward God, not first in introspection and then God.
4. **The more nullified, the more it is himself.** «אם נתבטל אליו ית׳ יותר אז נעשה עצמותו יותר אלוקית» (Esh Kodesh 199). And the order in the ghetto: «צריך להקטין את הרגשת עצמותו» (Esh Kodesh 390).
5. **Handing the self back.** The point of revealing the soul is «לקשרה בקדושתו ולבטל אותה ואת עצמותנו בנשמת ש-ד-י» (Hakhsharat 278). The closing prayer of Mevo HaShe'arim: «וכל אורך ממנה לפני יגלה, ואליך אותה ואותו אשובה» (Mevo 408): "to You I will return it and him".
6. **But the self is not erased.** Each person must reveal «אך שגם עצמית אישיות ודיוקן מיוחד לעצמו יגלה מקרבו» (Tzav VeZeruz 30), a likeness unique to him, and a person must feel himself «צריך האיש להרגיש עצמו לישראל, לחסיד, ולעובד ד'» (Esh Kodesh 541). Feeling worthless is not humility (section 6).
7. **Past oneself, into the community.** The individual soul is a branch of the one soul of Israel, and cannot rise to God until he rises out of his separateness into it (Esh Kodesh 358); in fervour Knesset Yisrael is revealed in him (Derekh HaMelekh 1604-1605).
8. **The self does not see itself; God does.** «רואה אתה את סתרי נפשי, אשר לא אראם אני» (Tzav VeZeruz 145). So finding oneself is never done by the self alone: it needs God's gaze, a confidant and the group (Hakhsharat 114, 137, 371).

**Put plainly, faithful to him:** people are looking for themselves. What they are really looking for is the soul under the ego, which is a spark of God's own "I". They find it by getting quiet, noticing, and reaching toward Him, and the self they find wants to give itself back, and comes back more itself.

## 6. Cautions and counterfeits

He warns in both directions, against false fire and against killing the fire. A guide must keep both.

- **Excitement without a vessel is nerves.** «לא יהי׳ בטוח שאין זה רק דמיון שוא וגרוי העצבים בלבד» (Chovat 324), foam on water.
- **Fervour made by movement.** Swaying hard, tallis over the face, squeezed-out words: after months of this even the ability to tell real from fake is damaged. The test: «נסה נא לחדול מן כל תנועתיך» (Hakhsharat 145). If everything goes quiet and you could chat, it was compulsion.
- **The main test is the traits.** «או רק התרגשות רמוי׳ היא הוי מסתכל במדותיך» (Chovat 231). And: «אם באמת מושל אני על עצמי עתה יותר מאשר לפנים» (Bnei Machshava Tova 252).
- **Imagination gone astray.** He fences it off: «לא דמיונות מדוחים, ולא הזיות הבל» (Hakhsharat 182); «לא שיראה האדם א"ע בדמיונות שוא» (Derekh HaMelekh 1493). The picture is a handrail and must fall away (Bnei Machshava Tova 90). Picturing yourself as nothing does not make you humble (Derekh HaMelekh 985).
- **Forcing the picture kills it**, because straining strengthens the ego-mind. «וככל שתרצה להתאמץ יותר לדמות אותם בדמיונך יותר תחדל תמונתם ממך לשעה זו» (Mevo 390)
- **Forcing feeling kills it.** «אל יתעקש להתעורר דוקא» (Hakhsharat 59), or «כאילו הכל בי כבה» (Hakhsharat 59). In hashkatah, force only wakes the ego (Derekh HaMelekh 1556).
- **A wish to wish.** «ורצון בלא תשוקה גם רצון אינו רק רצון לרצון» (Derekh HaMelekh 235).
- **Flame versus coal.** A flame from above may not stay; a coal set in your own self does. «השלהבת שמתלהב לפעמים אינו בטוח שכבר תישאר בו» (Derekh HaMelekh 735)
- **Borrowed arousal.** One can pray with feeling and stay just as low as before (Derekh HaMelekh 1249).
- **Regret that turns into pleasure at being rid of the regret** (Tzav VeZeruz 21); **brokenness that is only a missing desire** (Tzav VeZeruz 4).
- **Suppression is not cure.** What is only pushed down flares again later, stronger (Mevo 362-363); uproot it «מן נפשו לא מן מוחו ולבו בלבד» (Tzav VeZeruz 12).
- **Projection.** Righteous disgust at another's filth is your own craving in disguise: «דע לך כי כל המחשבות שאתה חושב עתה על זולתך שלך הן» (Hakhsharat 156).
- **Relying on technique.** «טעות נורא ומסוכן הוא טועה» (Hakhsharat 162). Devices help effort; they do not replace it, and they wear out.
- **Noise and show.** Real fervour is inside, not in shouting or clapping (Derekh HaMelekh 1343); soul-work opposes publicity (Bnei Machshava Tova 326).
- **The body first.** A heavy mood may be hunger, tiredness or an insult; check that before hearing it as a call (Derekh HaMelekh 937; Hakhsharat 313).
- **Drink.** Even in his own circle he limits it and says the coarse use it for sin (Hakhsharat 276); the bodily flame used "only for oneself" is not good: «כי אם חושב שהלהב הגופני לבד די לו ורק לצרכו ח"ו לא טוב» (Derekh HaMelekh 1179).

- **The dry investigators.** Some kill every stirring to test it. «כי בין עשרה התלהבויות רמיות תמצא אחת אמתית» (Bnei Machshava Tova 247): among ten false fervours you will find one true. He says the evil inclination is behind the over-scrupulousness (Bnei Machshava Tova 251).
- **Don't examine in the moment.** «ואסור לו לחקור בשעה זו אחר התרוממותו מה לו, כי החקירה בשכל מקלקלת את ההתרוממות» (Esh Kodesh 541). Examining pure thought while it happens «חקירה זו נפילה היא» (Bnei Machshava Tova 78).
- **Doubt is also a temptation.** «גם זה פתוי היצר הרע הוא להסתפק ולכפור במה שרואים ומרגישים בקודש» (Tzav VeZeruz 84), and «אבל את אשר הרגשתי הרגשתי» (Tzav VeZeruz 85).
- **"It's only imagination."** If it came by itself and you could not have produced it, that is the sign it is real (Mevo 127-128). Even an "imagined" stirring is real at the core: «אבל לעצם הישראליות שבו בשעה זו התעוררות אמיתית היא» (Mevo 144).
- **Sadness is not brokenness.** «שאין זאת שבירת ומרירת הלב רק מין העדר וטמטום» (Bnei Machshava Tova 46). «אבל עם כל זה בעצבות לא תשאר» (Hakhsharat 149).
- **Feeling worthless is not humility.** «כי מי שמרגיש עצמו לריק ומנוול מתנהג כריק ומנוול» (Esh Kodesh 541).
- **Feeling worse after a high is not failing**; a deeper longing woke and you see more (Mevo 211).
- **Motives: don't stop to fight them.** «והעיקר הוא שלא לחוש כלל להפניות שבאות במחשבתו בעת עבודתו ורק יעבוד» (Derekh HaMelekh 1550).

**How to tell real from false, in his terms:** arouse first, test afterwards, at a set time; judge by fruit, not glow (the traits soften, you rule yourself more, a trace of purity and longing stays after prayer, love and awe are added); what you could not produce on purpose you did not make up; and brokenness opens while sadness closes.

## 7. Against Chabad hisbonenus and Kuntres HaHitpa'alut

Hisbonenus, as `../Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md` sums it up: take one truth, unfold it in Binah with detail and analogy, hold it with Da'at for a long time until it reaches the heart, and bring it down into action; the mind rules the heart; "unsettled: settle first"; foreign thoughts: "return without commentary"; the subtlest obstacle is being aware of yourself in the state. Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (`KUNTRES-HAHITPAALUT.md`): the arousal must be "only Godly arousal, not the arousal of the life of flesh" (line 135); arousal that makes itself the point is "serving oneself" (167); "the deeper the arousal, the less it is felt" (556); the simple man's cry counts (662). (Hebrew for the Chabad side is not repeated here; see those files.)

- **Knowing must reach the heart.** His «הן גם מאז זאת ידעתי, אבל רק ידעתי» (Tzav VeZeruz 72) is the Chabad gap between understanding and Da'at exactly. Understanding with no effect on the heart is failure (Derekh HaMelekh 1319, 1514).
- **One truth, held and repeated.** "You surround me and fill my body" (Chovat 129), "everything is God except me" (Bnei Machshava Tova 182), "He alone exists", pictured and spread through mind and heart (Derekh HaMelekh 1308), "God, who has no end, loves me", thought again and again (Derekh HaMelekh 1186). These are hisbonenus objects, and he uses the word: "if you persist in contemplating" (Derekh HaMelekh 1591).
- **He describes the Chabad road fairly and does not rank it below his own.** «כך נוצר האיש שיהי׳ המוח שולט על הלב» (Mevo 195), set beside the Karlin road of plain forceful service, as "one way to the One God" (Mevo 204). He cites the Alter Rebbe's Siddur with approval twice (Chovat 295, 326).
- **Drawing down.** Clothe the stirring in a mitzvah (Hakhsharat 270); longings are good only if worked with (Tzav VeZeruz 65). That is Chabad's fifth stage.
- **The test is change, not intensity.** His traits test (Chovat 231) and self-rule test (Bnei Machshava Tova 252) match the Rashab's "has your dominant trait changed?", found independently.
- **Don't watch yourself in the state.** "Examining it is a fall" (Bnei Machshava Tova 78); "investigating spoils the elevation" (Esh Kodesh 541). This is Chabad's subtlest obstacle.
- **With Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, at the top.** At the peak he names not delight but «בטול העצמיות» (Chovat 344), "afraid even to fear, afraid to love" (Tzav VeZeruz 87), and fears "a trace of self stealing into my joy" (Tzav VeZeruz 144). That is the Mitteler Rebbe's "the deeper, the less felt". Both also defend the simple cry: "my voice, from my troubles, that is surely mine" (Esh Kodesh 265) beside Hitpa'alut 662; "a Jewish groan is very dear in heaven" (Derekh HaMelekh 753). Both refuse to forbid arousal (Hitpa'alut 743; Bnei Machshava Tova 247).
- **Suppression worsens it** (Mevo 362-363; the hisbonenus diagnostic).

**Where he differs (kept, not smoothed)**

1. **The door in.** Hisbonenus goes from the mind to the heart. He says, for most people of his time, the reverse: «יותר יקל לאיש לעורר את עצמו כשיתחיל לעורר את הנפש והרוח» (Chovat 291), because the intellect is more under the ego's control than the traits are (Chovat 292). He takes up the Alter Rebbe's own distinction to choose the lower faculty on purpose: *machshavah*, simple broad thought, widens the emotions, where revealed *mochin* quiet them (Hakhsharat 93). And in Derekh HaMelekh the intellect is the will's servant that invents excuses; a thorn is pulled out because it hurts, not because of a theory (Derekh HaMelekh 113-114).
2. **Imagination is an engine, not a risk.** "Strong thought and imagination are one kind" (Hakhsharat 99). «לכן המחשבה טהורה בעצמה בלא הדמיון אינה יכולה לעורר את המדות» (Derekh HaMelekh 475): pure thought without imagination *cannot* move the traits. The hisbonenus summary names worked analogy as a technique and does not name picturing at all. (The reports say Chabad is "wary of imagery"; the summary itself is silent on it. See section 9.)
3. **Empty first, then fill.** Chabad's diagnostic says "unsettled: settle first", and then fills the mind with a concept extended in length and breadth. Hashkatah settles by *emptying*: watch the thoughts until the head is hollow, then plant one verse softly (Derekh HaMelekh 1555-1556). And its rationale, that the working mind itself blocks what comes from above, has no Chabad counterpart as a method.
4. **Watch the intruder, don't return past it.** Chabad: "return without commentary" (and it is marked disputed). He: look straight at it, and it melts (Mevo 390); watch until it thins as with a broom (Hakhsharat 311); look ten times until you see its foolishness (Tzav VeZeruz 136). Elsewhere he also says: don't attend to them, just pray (Derekh HaMelekh 1551). Both reject fighting.
5. **Arousal from any source.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut's rule is "only Godly arousal, not the arousal of the life of flesh". His rule is "every arousal, even a bodily one, is a key to the soul" (Bnei Machshava Tova 100): a business worry, a sigh, a tune, dancing, even the chassidim's whiskey (Derekh HaMelekh 1178-1179). He concedes part of the Mitteler Rebbe's worry: arousal that climbs from the body keeps "the marks of the body" even when it rises, and arousal from above is purer (Derekh HaMelekh 1098). But the direction differs: Hitpa'alut filters arousal at the door; he lets it in and redirects it.
6. **Being moved is trained, and felt.** His target is to become "a person who is moved" at will (Hakhsharat 56-81), and to "look at the form of the feeling" (Bnei Machshava Tova 144). Hitpa'alut's deepest arousal is not felt as one's own. He meets it only at the peak; along the way he wants feeling felt.
7. **Analysis can kill the seeing.** «ואם רציתי, לחקור ולהבין, אז הכל חלף» (Tzav VeZeruz 89): when he tried to investigate and understand, everything passed. For Chabad, understanding in depth *is* the method.
8. **Seeing, not understanding.** The aim is a new sense, like smell, that sees the "You" (Bnei Machshava Tova 177-178); «כי אין זאת ראי׳ רק הבנה» (Hakhsharat 317) is his complaint against mere understanding. Contemplating the clouds with closed eyes "will not bring them down to him" (Bnei Machshava Tova 129).
9. **Melody, voice and the letters.** Niggun is a primary method (Hakhsharat 277-284; Bnei Machshava Tova 231-245); prayer is aloud with the whole body (Derekh HaMelekh 1046); the light of the letters is above the light of the intellect (Derekh HaMelekh 1213). Hisbonenus is silent and conceptual.
10. **The self as the object.** Chabad lists the soul as one object among five. For him it is usually *the* object: join every thought of God to "what am I doing before Him" (Derekh HaMelekh 193); know your soul first (Mevo 398); stand before your own heart as before a closed ark (Derekh HaMelekh 797).
11. **A positive self-feeling.** Chabad bitul distrusts any sense of self in the state. He requires a person to feel himself "a Jew, a chasid, a servant of God" (Esh Kodesh 541) and "an exalted person, only damaged now" (Derekh HaMelekh 542), and to reveal his unique likeness (Tzav VeZeruz 30). Both warn against I-ness; he makes room for an essential self that Chabad language does not usually name this way.
12. **Suddenness and gift.** His highest states come "suddenly" and cannot be summoned (Derekh HaMelekh 1322, 1396; Mevo 128). Chabad maps a path of cause and effect from contemplation to arousal.
13. **Social, not solitary.** The rebbe, the fellowship, the confidant, shared se'udah shelishis; solitude works "only for its moment" (Hakhsharat 345). Hisbonenus is an individual discipline.
14. **The broken mind.** Esh Kodesh writes for people with "no mind and no heart" (Esh Kodesh 93), "like a stone" (519). Its answer is not better contemplation but weeping with God, whispering from the depths and "He is God" (Esh Kodesh 261, 557, 585). No Chabad manual in the index addresses that state.

## 8. For the guide

Concrete moves, each faithful to him and sourced. The owner's rules stand over all of them: outside real danger, the guide never refers the person to a doctor or therapist; and **the guide never recommends drink (mashkeh)**. The Piaseczner used a little drink in a small supervised fellowship of chassidim and fenced it even there (Bnei Machshava Tova 320; Hakhsharat 276; Derekh HaMelekh 1179). That is his practice, reported, not something the guide passes on. When a source mentions it, the guide says nothing of it, or says plainly that it is not part of what it offers.

1. **Start from what is already moving.** "Before we try to feel anything, what is already there right now, a worry, a want, a sigh? We start there." (Hakhsharat 87; Bnei Machshava Tova 100; Derekh HaMelekh 828)
2. **Quiet first, by watching.** "For a minute or two, just watch your thoughts go by. Don't push them. Only ask: what am I thinking now?" If the head gets a little emptier, that is the point. (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
3. **One line, softly.** "Now say one short line, gently, not hard: *Hashem Elokim Emet*. Then tell Him simply what you want, like: I would so like to be close to You." Never force it; force wakes the ego. (Derekh HaMelekh 1556)
4. **Look at it, not in it.** When a thought or craving grips, "look at it for twenty seconds, as if from the side: so this is how much I want it." Looking dims it; dwelling feeds it. (Hakhsharat 293-294, 302)
5. **A heavy mood: since when?** "When did this start?" If the moment is found, the heart lightens. If the body is the cause (hunger, tiredness, a hurt), say so first. If nothing is found, it may be the soul crying. (Hakhsharat 313; Derekh HaMelekh 937)
6. **A picture as a handrail.** "Close your eyes, or turn to the wall. Picture yourself standing before Him like a child before a father who loves him." Offer it lightly and let it drop when it is not needed. If the person cannot picture it, come sideways through an ordinary memory. (Bnei Machshava Tova 90-91; Mevo 395)
7. **A hum is enough.** "If a tune comes, hum it, even in a whisper. Notice whether, after a while, it starts singing by itself." (Hakhsharat 279; Bnei Machshava Tova 245)
8. **Your own words.** "Tell Him what is actually weighing on you. Name it. Any language." Keep the words that burst out; they are sparks of the soul. (Tzav VeZeruz 15; Bnei Machshava Tova 274)
9. **Mouth before heart.** If nothing is felt, "say the words slowly anyway, and let the heart follow." Don't wait and don't squeeze. (Mevo 198; Esh Kodesh 504; Hakhsharat 59)
10. **Don't check in the middle.** "Don't ask now whether it's real. Afterwards, one question: are you a little more your own master? Is something left, a longing, a little purity?" (Esh Kodesh 541; Bnei Machshava Tova 252, 266)
11. **Put it into one act.** "What is one small thing you can do with this today?" A mitzvah, some learning, one resolution with a real safeguard, said once aloud. (Hakhsharat 270; Chovat 136; Tzav VeZeruz 65)
12. **Name the self rightly.** "You're not looking for something far away. You're uncovering the part of you that already sees Him. You are not bad at your core; you are a king's child with some dirt on you." And the ego is not that self: it is the one that has to step aside. (Bnei Machshava Tova 184; Derekh HaMelekh 1422, 1555)

**When the person is broken** (Esh Kodesh and the hospital pages of Tzav VeZeruz): do not push for feeling. Faith can be there unfelt (Esh Kodesh 578); they are not weeping alone (585); the One they lost track of is looking for them (82). Hope and surrender together: "may it get better soon; and He is God, He will do what is good in His eyes" (557).

**What the guide must not do, from him:** praise intensity; hunt images for a feeling that is already there; tell the person to fight thoughts; start with the soul's pains when a clearer or gladder door is open; offer technique as a substitute for the person's own effort; treat worthlessness as humility; or turn a high moment into a possession.

## A proposed constitution paragraph (not pasted; for the owner)

> **THE QUIET STEP IN**
> > Start where the person already is: what is moving in them now, even a worry or a sigh. Help them get
> quiet first. Watching the thoughts for a minute, without pushing, is enough. Then one short line,
> softly. If a picture helps, use it and let it go. If a tune comes, hum it. Let them talk to Him in their own
> words. Don't ask mid-way if it's real. Afterwards ask one thing: are you a little more your own master? What
> they are looking for isn't far. It is their own soul, and it already sees Him.
> > *(98 words.)*

Sources for each clause: Hakhsharat 87 and Bnei Machshava Tova 100; Derekh HaMelekh 1556 (watch, one verse, softly); Bnei Machshava Tova 90-91; Hakhsharat 279; Tzav VeZeruz 15; Esh Kodesh 541; Bnei Machshava Tova 252; Bnei Machshava Tova 184.

**How it sits with GUIDE-LAYER.md section 4.** That section ("THE STEP INWARD", from the Chabad service works) says: the bond is already there, ask what moved you, the deeper the quieter, the cry counts. This paragraph agrees on all four and adds the Piaseczner's two practical doors, quieting by watching and one soft line, plus picture, melody and one's own words. The one real tension: section 4 says "never make the feeling the goal", and the Piaseczner trains a person to be moved. The paragraph resolves it his way and theirs at once: it asks for no particular feeling and tests by the afterward.

## 9. Where the reports disagree or were unsure

1. **Where hashkatah is.** The brief assumed Bnei Machshava Tova or Tzav VeZeruz. Both reports say it is not there, and a count of the word across all seven files finds it only in Derekh HaMelekh 1553-1556. That text is a student's memory ("I do not remember the whole matter, only what I remember I write", line 1554), not the Rebbe's writing. So hashkatah should always be cited as a student's report. The Rebbe's own nearest words are "waiting an hour" (Derekh HaMelekh 89-90) and "calm yourself and watch" (Hakhsharat 310-311). The Derekh HaMelekh 1309-1620 report calls lines 1548-1552 both "Tzav veZiruz pieces" and "a talk to yeshiva students, 5681"; the file shows the 5681 talk, which cites the Tzav VeZeruz booklet at 1552.
2. **Does he start with the mind or with the body?** Bnei Machshava Tova says earlier generations began with the body, but this weak generation must begin with thought (lines 36-39). Chovat HaTalmidim says it is easier for most to begin with nefesh and ruach (body, senses, picture) than with reasoning (291), and Mevo HaShe'arim gives the Karlin "mouth first" road equal standing (198). They may not truly conflict: his "thought" in Bnei Machshava Tova is pictured strong thought, and Chovat calls picturing "a kind of thought" (291). The reports do not reconcile this, and neither does the text.
3. **Is he for or against "the mind rules the heart"?** The Bnei Machshava Tova report puts him close to the Chabad axiom; the Chovat HaTalmidim and Derekh HaMelekh 1-336 reports put him against it as a starting point; Mevo HaShe'arim has him describe it fairly and not rank it. Best reading: agreement on the goal, disagreement on the entry.
4. **Forcing.** Chovat HaTalmidim demands self-coercion ("stay away from the camp of the Shekhinah" if you think you can serve without it, line 241; prayer begins by force, 325). Hakhsharat forbids insisting on feeling (59, 61), hashkatah forbids saying things forcefully (1556), and Mevo shows that straining kills the image (390). The likely line is effort of service yes, forcing a feeling or an image no; the reports see the tension, and the Chovat report says outright that he "does not warn against forcing" there.
5. **Stray thoughts: watch or ignore?** Watch them until they thin (Hakhsharat 310-311; Tzav VeZeruz 136; Mevo 390), or pay them no attention and just pray (Derekh HaMelekh 1551; ulterior motives, 642). Both reject fighting. Which move fits which case is not settled in the texts.
6. **Looking at a feeling.** Hakhsharat says looking *at* a state dims it, so do not watch holy feeling, and do watch craving (the report calls this its own inference, from lines 148 and 293). Bnei Machshava Tova asks the person to "give birth to a form" of a feeling and look at it (142-144). Likely these are different acts (looking *into* a feeling to shape it, against stepping back to inspect it), but no text says so.
7. **Inner rest.** Derekh HaMelekh praises the servant's "calm, quiet, rest" (128) and teaches quieting (1555), yet says "the man with inner rest feels nothing" (517) and that prayer is a Garden of Eden of "no rest" (409). The reports note both; the text does not reconcile them.
8. **Imagination limits.** Bnei Machshava Tova permits a picture of the Throne as a crutch, leaning on the Raavad against the Rambam (87-90); Derekh HaMelekh 271 insists the soul's sketch is "not fantasy"; 985 says picturing yourself as nothing does not work. The reports agree he makes imagination central and fences it, but they draw the fence in different places.
9. **Chabad and imagery.** Several reports say Chabad hisbonenus is "wary of imagery". The hisbonenus summary does not say this; it simply does not name picturing as a technique. And this index's own MEDITATION.md builds each Chabad-style meditation with a step called "the picture". So the difference in section 7 is stated as "an engine for him, not a named technique there", not as "Chabad forbids it".
10. **Where he stands in the Chabad dispute.** The reports place him differently: near R. Aharon of Strashelye's defence of felt arousal (Bnei Machshava Tova, Tzav VeZeruz), or near "the Mitteler Rebbe's and R. Aharon's defence of being moved" together (Chovat). The hisbonenus summary itself says both affirm being moved and differ over sequence and trust. On sequence he is nearer R. Aharon; on trust he goes further than either.
11. **Dates.** File labels are narrower than contents: "Derekh HaMelekh (Warsaw, 1931)" holds talks from 1925 to 1938 and an appendix; "Esh Kodesh (1941)" runs 1939-1942 with 1943 glosses; Hakhsharat was corrected in the ghetto in 1941; Mevo HaShe'arim was written before 1937 with later additions. Bnei Machshava Tova's 1917-1923 is the file's own label. Section 2 follows the reports.
12. **Two books share text.** Bnei Machshava Tova and Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim share near-identical passages (the niggun at the wall, se'udah shelishis in the dark, the psalms at the wall, the fellowship rules). The reports quote both but do not say which is the earlier form.
13. **Readings the reports flagged as their own.** The Esh Kodesh 542 phrase is the Rambam's about God, applied by the Rebbe to the person; the Hakhsharat report's "one mechanism seen from two sides" (Chabad's self-watching obstacle and his looking-at technique) is marked as its inference; the Derekh HaMelekh 337-683 report cited Tanya ch. 16 from memory, unverified. None of these is relied on above as the text's own claim.
14. **Esh Kodesh and method.** It contains no picturing, quieting, melody or fellowship; it cannot settle any question about technique. Its value here is the inner life when method fails.
