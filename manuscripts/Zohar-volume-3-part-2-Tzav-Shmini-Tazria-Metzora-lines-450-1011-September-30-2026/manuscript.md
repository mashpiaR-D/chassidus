## The shape of the range

These four parashot are about fire, the priest, the Eighth Day, forbidden food, birth, and the leper. The Zohar turns each into a teaching about the soul and the Presence. Three threads run through it.

1. **The offering rises to Ein Sof, and the will is what rises.** The burnt offering (olah) is the Shechinah going up; the smoke is "the will of all" (450-474).
2. **The deed below wakes the deed above.** Holiness is drawn down by what is done and said below, even by a word said aloud over one's work (553-556, 672, 907-911, 941).
3. **The mouth and the Presence.** The leper is the one who spoke evil. The neshamah (higher soul) is the "speaking spirit"; evil speech silences it and it leaves (845-848). The Presence departs and returns with a person's return (966).

Around these: the Raya Mehemna's night-time offering of the animal soul (518) and its teaching on dying with "One" (588-593); the tefillin as the embrace (984-989); the soul leaving the body toward the Shechinah at death (958); and the teaching that wisdom is known by folly and light by dark (862-866).

## The unity of G-d

**Ein Sof: where the will goes, and where nothing stands.** Rabbi Elazar asks: the cleaving of the priests', Levites' and Israel's will in the offering, "how far does it rise?" Rabbi Shimon answers:

> עד אין סוף, דכל קשורא ויחודא ושלימו, לאצנעא בההוא צניעו, דלא אתדבק, ולא אתיידע, דרעוא דכל רעוין ביה (Zohar, Tzav 3:2; line 461)
> To Ein Sof. For every binding and unity and wholeness is to be hidden away in that hiddenness which is not grasped and not known, in which is the will of all wills.

Ein Sof "does not stand to be known, nor to make an end, nor to make a beginning". It is the "first nothing" (אין קדמאה) that brings out beginning and end. And:

> לאו רעותין, לאו נהורין, לאו בוצינין בההוא אין סוף (Tzav 3:3; line 462)
> There are no wills, no lights, no lamps in that Ein Sof.

All the lights "hang on it to exist, but do not stand to cleave to it". The one who knows "does not know". So the unity the offering reaches for is beyond all the unities it works with. When the highest point and the world-to-come rise, even they know only "the scent": the "pleasing scent" is the will of prayer, of song and of the priest become "one will" (כלהו אתעבידו רעותא חדא, line 463).

*Ours.* This is the Zohar's clearest statement, in this range, of what Chassidus calls the Essence above the lights (F05). It agrees closely with Chabad: Ein Sof is not one of the lights, not even a will; the lights hang on it. And it gives a practice: what reaches toward it is not an idea but the gathering of all wills into one.

**All holiness is His, and His is not theirs.** Hannah's "there is none holy like the Lord, for there is none beside You" is read carefully. Angels are holy, Israel are holy, "but not holy like the Lord":

> קדושה דקודשא בריך הוא בלתי קדושא דלהון, דהוא לא אצטריך לקדושה דלהון. אבל אינון, לאו אינון קדישין בלתך (Tazria 10:2; line 799)
> The holiness of the Holy One is without their holiness, for He does not need their holiness. But they are not holy without You.

This is the one-way dependence that the Tanya builds on: nothing stands on its own, He stands on nothing (F01, F06).

**Nothing below without its root above.** "There is no good and bad, holiness and impurity, that has no root and source above" (לית לך טב וביש, קדושא ומסאבותא, דלית ליה עקרא ושרשא לעילא, line 555). And the rule that follows: "by the deed below, the deed above is woken" (בעובדא דלתתא אתער עובדא לעילא, line 554); what hangs on a word is woken by a word (555-556). The same law is given the other way at line 856: "nothing is found below unless it is found above, and above it is found only when it is found below; all hangs this on that." And at 738: "the lower world depends on the upper, and the upper goes by the ways of the lower".

**The union made by the priest.** The wicked "separate this (זאת, the Shechinah) from this (זה, the Holy One)"; Aaron and his sons bring them close, "and all is found one without separation" (ואשתכח כלא חד בלא פרודא, line 548). When they are joined, "the female is called by the male's name", "and all is one" (551). Each sefirah rules its own holy day, "though all the sefirot are one" (ואף על גב דכל ספירן כלהו חד, line 504).

**The One who needs no defense, and is still the Watchman.** The world stands every moment on His kindness: "were it not that this El (the kindness of Abraham) wakes in the world, the world could not stand even one hour" (543-544). This is F01 in the Zohar's own terms: the world is kept up now, each day, against the judgments that would end it.

## States of consciousness

**The silence that is the smallest voice.** Elijah's "still small voice" is read as a real place:

> קול דא, קול בתראה, דהיא דממה, דלית לה מלה פרטא, אלא היא דממה מגרמה (Tzav 10:2; line 524)
> This voice is the last voice, which is silence, which has no particular word, only silence itself.

When all gather to it, "it is heard in all the worlds", and it is "fine" because it is "the smallest of all". The inner altar, where the incense of "the knot of faith" is offered, is called by this name (534, 536). This is close to S32 (quiet) and S57 (no self to watch), but it is not an emptying: it is the silence in which the knot is tied. *Proposed state: the fine silence.*

**Whispered and voiced.** Oil and wine come from one place. Oil is "in silence, always in a whisper", from the side of thought; wine "raises the voice and is never silent" (706-707). So the priest's work is "in silence", his intentions and joinings hidden (708). And yet Torah must be learned aloud: "the one who cleaves to Torah and its word is heard in his mouth, not in a whisper, but raises his voice" (712). *Ours:* two true modes of inner work, the silent intention and the voiced word, and the Zohar wants both, each in its place.

**The priest's shining face.** The priest must be seen "in joy and a shining face", "with no defect in his head or his clothes", and "judgment, anger and sadness are kept far from him" (696, 702). This is S53 (glad) given as a duty of the one who serves for others.

**Waking the will first.** A precise teaching on who moves first. When the Shechinah "wakes her will toward Him first and draws Him to her with much love and longing", all is filled from the right side, kindness. When He wakes first and she after, it goes to the left (821). And for us: "in everything a person must cleave his will above, to the Holy One" (822). *Ours:* the lower one's longing, sent up first, is what brings kindness down. This is S41-S43 as an act and not only a feeling.

**Knowing wisdom by folly, light by dark.** "Were there no folly in the world, wisdom would not be known" (862); Rav Hamnuna Sava would set a chapter of foolish things before the companions before the secrets (864); "one does not know the taste of sweet until he tastes bitter" (866). *Ours:* this belongs with C7 (darkness to light) and S68: the dark is not only overcome but is how the light is seen.

**Held when the Presence is near, left when it goes.** "While the holy soul cleaves to a person, he is beloved of his Master; many guardians guard him on every side ... and the holy Shechinah rests on him" (843). When he turns aside, she departs (844). And the return: "after he is purified and returns, what departed from him returns and rests on him" (ההוא דאסתלק מניה אהדר, ושארי עלוי, line 966). This is the Zohar's form of S84 (trust) and S77 (the lower return).

**Dying toward the Presence.** "At the hour of parting, the soul does not leave the body until the Shechinah is revealed to it, and the soul, out of joy and love for the Shechinah, goes out of the body to meet her" (958). *Ours:* the whole guide's work on fear of death can stand on this line.

> לא נפקא נפשא מן גופא, עד דאתגלי עליה שכינתא, ונפשא, מגו חדותא וחביבותא דשכינתא, נפקא מגופא לקבלהא (Metzora 3:3; line 958)
> The soul does not go out of the body until the Shechinah is revealed to it; and the soul, out of joy and love for the Shechinah, goes out of the body toward her.

## Meditation and practice

**The night-time offering of the animal soul (Raya Mehemna).** The fullest practical instruction in the range:

> וצריך בר נש, לקרבא בכל ליליא, קרבן נפשא ורוחא ונשמתא דבעירן, קדם יי'. ויתודה בכמה מיני ודויין, ויסלק לון במחשבתיה, קרבנא קדם יי' (Tzav 9:8; line 518)
> A person must offer every night the offering of the animal nefesh, ruach and neshamah before the Lord, and confess with many kinds of confession, and raise them in his thought as an offering before the Lord.

He brings them out "in the Shema, with one heart", and gives out "his spirit that beats in the veins of the heart", intending the four deaths of the court (518-519). *Ours:* this is the root of the bedtime Shema with its confession and mesirat nefesh (giving over the self). What it is for: to burn off the animal soul's hold before sleep. How: the confession, then the thought of giving it over, then the Shema. Guard: it is an inner act of intention and never touches the body.

**Dying with One, every day.** At death, one confesses and "intends to complete the Name", Shema Yisrael and Baruch Shem, to bring his soul as an offering (588-591). And because no one knows the day:

> שוב יום א' לפני מיתתך, דבכל יום ויום צריך בר נש לאהדרא בתיובתא, ולממסר רוחיה לגביה, דיפוק באחד, הדא הוא דכתיב … בידך אפקיד רוחי (Tzav 20:10; line 593)
> "Return one day before your death": every day a person must return, and give his spirit over to Him, that it go out in "One". As it is written, "Into Your hand I entrust my spirit."

*Proposed technique: the nightly handing over* (see the overview). It joins `cheshbon` (the evening look-back) to the Shema and the verse "into Your hand".

**Love in the Shema that never goes out.** The Raya Mehemna reads "a fire always burning on the altar, not to go out" as the love of the Shema: the offering "burns in the flames of the love of the unity in the reading of the Shema, which by night and day does not go out", and it asks the companions not to be still before Him (600). *Ours:* the Shema is not a sentence said once; it is a fire to be kept.

**Say what your work is for.** The women who spun for the Tabernacle "would say: this is for the Sanctuary, this is for the Tabernacle, this is for the curtain", so holiness would rest on the work (907). The builder should say with his mouth, as he begins, "I build this for the service of the Holy One" (911). Rabbi Elazar makes it general:

> בכל עובדוי דבר נש, לבעי ליה דלהוון כלהו לשמא קדישא. מאי לשמא קדישא. לאדכרא בפומיה שמא קדישא על כל מה דאיהו עביד, דכלא הוא לפולחניה (Tazria 34:12; line 941)
> In all a person's deeds, let them all be for the holy Name. What is "for the holy Name"? To mention with his mouth the holy Name over all that he does, that it is all for His service.

*Proposed technique: naming the purpose aloud.* It is the Zohar's form of the guide's `act` and `lips`: the word said over the plain task turns it toward Him.

**Burning the thought that rises.** "The olah (that which rises) is the bad thought that rises on a person's will to turn him from the true way; it must be burned in fire, so that it is given no place" (469, 521). *Ours:* the guide's `binding` (returning the wandering mind) with a Zohar image: the stray thought is put on the altar, not argued with.

**Tefillin as the embrace.** "When he lays the hand-tefillin, he must stretch out his left arm to receive the Assembly of Israel, and tie the knot with the right, to embrace her, to fulfill 'his left hand under my head and his right hand embraces me'" (989). Love "strong as death": the hardest thing is the parting of soul from body, and so the love of the Shechinah for the Holy One never parts (985).

**Speak to your heart.** David says "do not incline my heart to an evil thing": he was speaking to his own heart, to lead it. "And you shall return it to your heart" means "once, twice, three times, to turn it back and lead it and warn it" (851). *Ours:* this is `own_words` turned inward, and it fits the Tanya's "the mind rules the heart" (S28).

**Rebuke in love is hidden.** A man with a face full of sores is not suffering "afflictions of love", for those are hidden from people. The Holy One rebukes in love first inside the body, then under the clothes, and only if that fails, openly on the face (831-834). So one who rebukes a friend in love "must hide his words from people" (833). A guard for the guide: correction that shames is not from love.

**The mouth.** Evil speech drives out the neshamah, which is the "speaking spirit" that alone may speak before the King (845-847): "I was dumb with silence" is the soul made mute. And the other side: "as he is punished for an evil word, so for a good word that came to his hand and he could have said, and did not" (848).

**Midnight.** At midnight the Holy One enters Eden to delight with the righteous; the companions who learn then join the Shechinah in praise; "the Shechinah is joined to the King only through song and praise", and at dawn He holds out to her, "and to all who join her", the scepter of the thread of kindness (802, 820). Scent after Shabbat: when the extra soul leaves and the nefesh and ruach are "parted and sad", the scent brings them close and glad (636-638).

## The big human questions

**Why suffering.** Some suffering is love, and it is hidden (831-834). The Holy One "is exacting with the righteous, to add holiness to their holiness" (657). A tzaddik may be "lost in his righteousness" when the world's sins darken the moon, or be taken for not protesting the sins around him (838-840).

**The soul and its image.** With every spirit sent down comes a tzelem (image) that walks with a person all his days: "surely man walks in an image" (775-776, 782). Every spirit comes out male and female and they part; if a person is worthy they are joined again (783).

**Death and after.** The soul leaves toward the Shechinah (958). After judgment it is "whitened like iron whitened in fire", bathes in the waters and spices of the lower Eden, and rises step by step "until it is brought near like an offering on the altar" (959-960). The souls of the righteous come out to meet and save the living righteous, as Abraham and Isaac met Jacob (1010).

**Providence.** Blessing does not rest on an empty table (612). "When He gives quiet, who can condemn? When He hides His face, who can behold?" (660). He brings Israel to the land "out of His love, to rest His Shechinah among them" (906).

**Joy.** Wine begins in joy and ends in sadness; oil is joy from beginning to end (704-706). The one who clings to the place of teshuvah "does not cling after this world, not to its wealth nor its silver" (734).
