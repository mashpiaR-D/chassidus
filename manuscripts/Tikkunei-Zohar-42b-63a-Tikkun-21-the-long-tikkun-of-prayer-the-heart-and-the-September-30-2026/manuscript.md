## What this range is

Tikkun 21 is by far the longest tikkun, twenty folios. It is a book inside the book. Two old men and a mysterious boy (the son of Rav Hamnuna Saba, line 1424) meet Rabbi Shimon on the road. A herald cries that whoever kills the serpent will receive the King's daughter, who is prayer. The tikkun then walks through the cantillation marks as a map of how the Shechinah rises and falls; the shofar blasts as brain, heart and lungs; the liver, gall and spleen as the forces of the other side; Jonah's ship as the body; Shabbat, sukkah, lulav, tefillin, charity, the calendar, ritual slaughter; and more than forty names of the Shechinah, each a mitzvah (lines 1970-2150). It ends with the stone in David's sling, thrown so high that "the angels ask, where is the place of His glory?"

## The unity of G-d

**One and three and one.** The old men, who are the sefirot, say: הא אנן חד. ותלת הוינן. וכען אנן חד, "we are one; we were three; and now we are one" (line 1396). Multiplicity in the divine is a passing moment inside a oneness.

**Small below, endless above.** The Shechinah, the stone thrown by the faithful shepherd, rises until no one knows her place:

> ואע״ג דאיהי זעירא לתתא. לעילא לית לה סוף (TZ 61b:7, line 2208)
> *And though she is small below, above she has no end.*

This is the Tikkunim's formula for the Presence: the smallest point in the world, a point in the eye, the tip of the dalet in Echad (lines 1409, 2068), and at once without end. Our reading: the humblest place of contact with God is not a lesser God.

**One Name at the end.** When the King takes off His garments of darkness in the redemption, "the letters, which were in separation", are joined, and then:

> והיה יי׳ למלך על כל הארץ ביום ההוא יהיה יי׳ אחד ושמו אחד (TZ 60b:10, line 2166)
> *And the Lord will be King over all the earth; on that day the Lord will be one and His name one.*

Even the smallest gnat will praise Him (כלהו לעילא ולתתא מה דאתברי עד יתוש זעירא כלהו משבחין ליה, line 2168). In exile God is written one way and read another; in the world to come He will be read as He is written (lines 2180-2181). The unity is not yet fully spoken; it is waiting in the name.

**Seeing inside.** In exile, the King is clothed in darkness. Fools look at His garment, others at His body, "but a clear-eyed wise man looks within":

> ובני נשא דאינון שטיין מסתכלין בלבושא. ואחרנין הכי מסתכלין בגופא. ובגלות׳ כלהון שטיין. אבל פקחא חכימא אסתכל מלגאו (TZ 60b:5, line 2161)
> *Foolish people look at the garment; others look at the body. In exile they are all foolish. But the wise, clear-eyed one looks inside.*

This is the root of technique 11 (seeing the garment) and of face F03 (the hiding for the sake of revelation): the hiding is a garment, and the wise look through it.

## States of consciousness

**Held on the road, at the pillow, in poverty (ties S84 Trust, S63, being-held).** The Shechinah goes into every exile with her people: בכל אתר דגלו ישראל שכינתא עמהון, "wherever Israel went into exile, the Shechinah is with them" (line 1902). God Himself is poor when He is away from His place: וקב״ה כביכול איהו עני כד איהו בר מאתריה, "the Holy One, as it were, is poor when He is outside His place" (line 2078). And God cannot leave exile without her:

> בגין דקב״ה לית ליה רשותא לאפקא מן גלותא עד דאיהי נפקת עמיה (TZ 56a:2, line 1990)
> *For the Holy One has no leave to go out of exile until she goes out with Him.*

**Grief for Her, carried into prayer (ties Z-S04 from the previous report).** "Every person who has sorrow for the Shechinah, who is poor in exile and far from her husband, and calls to Him every day 'Hear O Israel' so that He come down to her":

> וכל בר נש דאית ליה ענוי משכינתא דאיהי עניא בגלותא רחיקא מן בעלה. וקרא ליה בכל יומא שמע ישראל דיהא נחית לגבה (TZ 48a:3, line 1652)

of him it is said "the voice of affliction I hear" (Ex. 32:18). And the hardest thing for her of all: וקשיא איהי לה מכלא אפרשותא דקב״ה מינה, "hardest of all for her is the Holy One's parting from her" (line 1550).

**Fallen, and only His right hand lifts (proposed Z-S08, "lifted by the right hand").** When the soul has sunk "beneath the feet":

> ווי ליה לבר נש דנשמתיה נחיתת תחות רגלוי (TZ 50a:2, line 1750)
> *Woe to the person whose soul has gone down beneath his feet.*

> ולית ליה עלוי וסלוק אלא בידא דקב״ה דאזיל שמא קדישא לימינא ואוקים לה (TZ 50a:2, line 1750)
> *He has no raising and lifting but by the hand of the Holy One, as the holy Name goes to the right and raises her.*

> דמאן דנפיל לא צריך לאקמא אלא בידא ימינא (TZ 50a:6, line 1754)
> *For one who has fallen should be raised only with the right hand.*

The right hand is the hand of kindness. From inside: I cannot get up; I am lifted, and lifted gently. The fallen prayer lies in the dust and prays like Ruth at Boaz's feet, "spread your wing over your handmaid, for you are a redeemer" (ואיהי מצלי לקב״ה דיוקים לה מעפרא, line 1777). Ties to S11 (the verdict: I'm my mistakes) as its answer and to S77 The lower return. And God's right arm "is stretched out to receive those who return; He will raise them from their fall and take them by the hand" (דרועא ימינא לקבל שבים. ויוקים לון מנפילו. דלהון ואחיד בידיהון, line 1949).

**Eyes down, heart up (proposed technique and state Z-T06, "the posture of the praying heart").** "My eyes and heart will be there all the days" (1 Kings 9:3):

> דצריך בר נש בצלותיה למהוי עינוי לתתא לגבי נשמתא דאיהי שכינתא דאיהי אסירא בגלותא ולביה לעילא בקב״ה (TZ 50b:12, line 1783)
> *In his prayer a person's eyes must be down, toward the soul, which is the Shechinah imprisoned in exile, and his heart up, toward the Holy One.*

From inside: looking down with pity at what is bound in me and in the world, and at the same time the heart lifted to the One above. Ties to S49 Weeping and joy in one heart. The Talmud (Yevamot 105b) has "eyes down, heart up"; the Tikkunim say what the eyes look at.

**Sadness is the great fish (a guard; ties S04 Low and heavy).** The fish that swallowed Jonah is poverty, and poverty's male and female are sadness and grief: דא עניותא. דאיהו עצב ונוקבא דיליה עצבון (line 1912). One who goes to see the Shechinah on the festivals "must guard himself from sadness" (ומאן דאזיל למחמי לה צריך לנטרא גרמיה מעציבו, line 2105), because sadness is a blemish that may not come near the altar. Chassidus's teaching that sadness blocks service (Tanya ch. 26-31) has here one of its sources.

**Many waters cannot quench it.** When the Shechinah's wings cover her children, the flood cannot touch them: מים רבים דמי טופנא לא יוכלו לכבות את האהבה דישראל באבוהון דבשמיא, "many waters, the waters of the flood, cannot quench the love of Israel for their Father in heaven" (line 1807). Ties to S41 The hidden love wakes (the love that no sin reaches).

## Meditation and practice

**1. Pray and know how to raise your will (ties technique 16, 20).**

> זכאה איהו מאן דמצלי וידע לסלקא רעותיה לעילא. דהא פימוי אפיק שמהן ואצבעוי כתבין רזין (TZ 44b:23, line 1502)
> *Fortunate is one who prays and knows how to raise his will on high, for his mouth brings out names and his fingers write secrets.*

And the lament of line 1518: woe to people "whose hearts and eyes are closed, who do not try to know the honor of their Master, to please Him through His Shechinah", and much more "to wake in Him love toward her" (line 1519), and "when they stand before Him they must stand in awe" (line 1520). If the Shechinah does not rise in a person's prayer, "a dog comes down to eat his offering" (line 1527).

**2. An hour before and after.** "A person must wait one hour before he prays and one hour after" (line 1511, the early pious ones of Berachot 32b). What it is for: to arrive and to linger. Ties to technique 2 (the threshold) and 15 (the quiet after).

**3. The poor man's prayer waits for no one; all prayers wait for it.** If even the last of ten is poor and his prayer cannot rise with the others, all the prayers are held back until it rises:

> כלהו צלותין מתעכבן לסלקא עד דההיא צלותא דעניא סליקת (TZ 42b:14, line 1405)
> *All the prayers wait to go up until that prayer of the poor one goes up.*

> ובגין דא תפלה לעני כי יעטוף. דכלהו מתעטפין בגינה עד דאיהי סלקא עמהון דלית פרודא בה מנייהו (TZ 42b:15, line 1406)
> *That is "a prayer of the poor when he is faint (wrapped)": they all wrap themselves (and wait) for her until she rises with them, for there is no separation between her and them.*

> וצלותא דעני איהו שכינתא (TZ 42b:16, line 1407)
> *And the prayer of the poor is the Shechinah.*

(Ours.) The weakest prayer in the room sets the pace. No one goes up alone. This is a strong teaching for the guide: a person who feels their prayer is too poor to count is told it is the one everyone waits for.

**4. Food from His hand, even bitter.** The dove brought Noah an olive leaf, and said: "Master of the worlds, let my food be given into Your hand and be bitter as an olive, and not sweet and given into the hand of a messenger" (רבון עלמין תהא מזונא דילי מסורה בידך ותהא מרירא כזית ולא תהא מתוק׳ ומסורה ביד שליח, line 1542). Ties to S84 Trust and technique 22. Our note: this is trust as a choice of whose hand, not of what taste.

**5. Everyone gets their own bread.** When the Shechinah comes down from prayer "like an eagle", she brings each one food as fits him: Torah to one as the food of the soul, bodily food to another, לכל חד כפום רעותי׳, "to each according to his will" (line 1531). She rises like a dove and descends like an eagle (line 1529).

**6. Stand in awe, and change on Shabbat.** The Shabbat is marked by change: clothes, food, place, and inside:

> ועוד שנוי מעשה. אם הוא עציב בחולא. דיהא חדי בשבתא. ואם אית ליה קטטה בחולא עם בר נש או עם אתתיה. דיהא ליה שלמא עמה בשבתא (TZ 57a:8, line 2039)
> *And a change of act: if he was sad on weekdays, let him be glad on Shabbat; if he had a quarrel on weekdays with a person or with his wife, let him have peace with her on Shabbat.*

And a man must win his wife over "with extra words of appeasement" on Shabbat, as God says to the Shechinah "open to me, my sister, my love" (line 2042). "If Israel kept one Shabbat as it should be kept, they would be redeemed at once" (line 2040), and "Shabbat weighs as the whole Torah" (line 2052). The weekday is made holy by what Shabbat leaves behind: ובהון אתעביד חול קדש, "and by them the weekday is made holy" (line 1556). Ties to S76 Shabbos: the soul returned.

**7. The shofar as the soul's three voices.** Tekiah is from the brain, shevarim from the heart, "a broken and crushed heart", teruah from the lungs (תקיעה מן מוחא. שברים מלבא, line 1710). Neshamah, nefesh and ruach sound in them (lines 1711-1714). Fortunate is one who joins neshamah and ruach with nefesh "in the voice of Torah" (line 1723), for one who does not learn has his soul weakened above and below (line 1723). Ties to technique 18 (saying it aloud).

**8. Song carries everything up.** אוריתא בנגונא שכינתא בנגונא. ישראל סלקין מגו גלותא בנגונא, "Torah in melody, the Shechinah in melody, Israel rise out of exile in melody" (line 1824). Ties to technique 19 (melody), S52.

**9. The whole mind raises all.** Of teshuvah: ועקרא לסלקא כלא במחשבה, "the main thing is to raise it all in thought" (line 2145). And the Shema said morning and evening counts as learning "day and night" (line 2153), because God does not make impossible demands of His creatures. Ties to technique 22 (carrying a line through the day).

**10. Overcoming the pull.** Whoever overcomes his inclination "is as if Gevurah overcame Samael in judgment for her children" (line 2146); the mighty one "conquers" his inclination like a dog kept under his hand (line 2147). Ties to S30 Holding back and C7 (darkness to light).

**11. Charity waters the garden.** Giving to the poor draws the river from Eden to water the garden (line 2080); fortunate is one who does merits to raise her to her place (line 2091). And returning a robbery, in teshuvah, means "returning the Shechinah to her place" (line 2140).

## The big questions

**What sin does to the body.** "In the place (another reading: limb) where a sin rests and a mitzvah is gone from it, that limb is blemished" (line 1862), and the Holy One does not rest there. The Jonah allegory (lines 1880-1899): the ship is the head and body, the sailors the limbs, the wind the ruach. When the limbs are not led by Torah, the spirit flies away, a storm rises, the dove goes down into the hold and lies in the dust, and the body is sick. Then the captain says: get up, call to your God; "look at your merits in the limbs that work in Torah and good deeds, and they will bring back upon you the upper Shechinah, which is teshuvah, and she will shield you" (line 1895).

**Suffering borne in prayer counts as an offering.** Those who come near in prayer "and bear many blows" are as if they brought whole offerings (line 2117); through prayer the Holy One redeems them "from the judgment of the beasts of the field" (line 2128). Moses gives himself for the middle people, "blot me out" (lines 1944-1946), and will be in the last exile, "wounded for our transgressions" (line 1947). God is long-suffering with the beinonim "and takes trouble for them" (line 1953).

**Where redemption hangs.** ופורקנא בלבא תליא דביה אתמר הלב רואה, "redemption hangs on the heart, of which it is said: the heart sees" (line 1787).

**Faith kept in exile.** "Fortunate is one who keeps this faith in his heart and his mouth" (line 2230), "and fortunate is one who is faithful with her in exile, who fears nothing above or below" (line 2231). Our reading: faithfulness to the Presence in exile is the Tikkunim's form of emunah; it is loyalty more than belief.

**Tzaddikim turn judgment to mercy** (line 1769).

## Chassidus and the Tikkunim here (ours)

- **Taken over whole:** "sadness is the great fish" (1912) and the guard against sadness in service (2105) run straight into the Tanya's chapters on joy. The "eyes down, heart up" of line 1783 is the posture of Tanya ch. 45 (pity for the divine spark, then love). "Many waters cannot quench" (1807) is the hidden love of Tanya ch. 18-19.
- **Built on:** "the wise look inside" (2161) becomes the whole Chassidic doctrine of looking through the garment of nature (Shaar HaYichud, ch. 1-3).
- **Where Chassidus goes further:** the Tikkunim's God cannot leave exile without the Shechinah (1990); the Rashab reads this as the Essence's will to be found precisely below (F07). The Tikkunim leave it as love's loyalty.
- **For the guide:** the poor man's prayer that everyone waits for (1405-1407); the right hand that lifts the fallen (1750-1754); food from His hand even if bitter (1542); change on Shabbat, sad to glad, quarrel to peace (2039). These are simple, felt, and usable.
