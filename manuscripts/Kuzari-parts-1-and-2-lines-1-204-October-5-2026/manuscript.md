## The shape of the range

The book is a talk between a king and a sage. The king of the Khazars keeps having the same dream. An angel tells him: your intention is pleasing to the Creator, but your deeds are not. He asks a philosopher, a Christian and a Muslim, and last, with little hope, a Jew, called "the Chaver" (the companion, the learned friend). Part 1 is the case for the Torah: why the Chaver starts from what happened, not from a proof. Part 2 turns to the Names of G-d, the Land, the offerings, Israel as the heart of the nations, the balanced life of the Torah, and the Hebrew language.

1. **The dream and the philosopher** (1-5). The deed matters, not only the intention. The philosopher's G-d does not know you.
2. **The G-d of history** (17-36). "The G-d of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, who brought Israel out of Egypt." The king of India parable: you know a king by what reaches you from him.
3. **Four levels and the divine matter** (37-53, 101). Nature, soul, mind, and above them a level that differs in kind: the inyan haElokı (the divine matter).
4. **Only by His command** (83-121). Human ways of drawing the divine are like a fool in a doctor's pharmacy. The golden calf. The promise is nearness now, not only after death.
5. **Names, heart, and balance** (124-204). Attributes are His acts or negations. The divine matter waits for a ready vessel. Israel is the heart of the nations. Joy on Shabbat is as near to G-d as a fast.

## The unity of G-d

**The One is known first by what He did, not by a proof.** The Chaver does not open with "I believe in the Creator of the world". He opens with history:

> אנחנו מאמינים באלהי אברהם יצחק ויעקב המוציא את בני ישראל ממצרים (Kuzari 1:17; line 17)
> We believe in the G-d of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, who brought Israel out of Egypt.

The king is let down: a "religion of reasoning", he thinks, would start with the Creator. The Chaver answers that such a religion is exactly the weak one:

> זה שאתה אומר היא הדת ההקשית המנהגית, מביא אליה העיון, ונכנסים בה ספקות רבות (Kuzari 1:19; line 19)
> What you describe is the religion of reasoning and good order. Thought leads to it, and many doubts come into it.

And his own opening, he says, is the proof, and needs no proof:

> אבל פתיחת דברי היא המופת, ועוד כי היא הראיה אין צריך עמה לא ראיה ולא מופת (Kuzari 1:21; line 21)
> But the opening of my words is the proof. It is the evidence itself, and needs neither evidence nor proof.

The parable of the king of India makes it plain (lines 25-31). If you only heard that India has a just king, you would owe him nothing. If his messengers came to you with gifts only found in his palace, and medicine that healed you, you would serve him. You would describe him "by the traits that became clear to me by sight" (בתארים אשר התבררו אצלי לעין, line 30). So Moses said to Pharaoh "the G-d of the Hebrews", not "the G-d of heaven and earth"; so the Ten Commandments open "I am the Lord your G-d who took you out of Egypt", not "I created the world" (line 31).

*Ours.* This is the Kuzari's first gift to the guide. The unity of G-d is not first a truth you prove and then feel. It is first a meeting, told and passed down, and only then thought about. Chassidus says the same in its own way when it puts faith (emunah) above understanding and asks understanding to serve it (F12, C6).

**The philosopher's One is far, and does not know you.** The philosopher in line 2 is a real voice, and the book gives him his best words. For him G-d has no will, "for intention shows a lack in the one who intends" (כי הכונה מורה על חסרון המכון, line 2). He does not know particulars, so "He does not know you, much less your intentions and deeds" (והוא אינו יודע אותך, line 2). The goal is to join the active intellect until "he and it are one thing" (line 2). And then:

> וכאשר תהיה על התכונה הזאת מן האמונה אל תחוש על איזה תורה תהיה ובאיזה דת ובאיזה מעשה (Kuzari 1:2; line 2)
> Once you are of this cast of mind, do not care which Torah you follow, which faith, which deed.

The whole book is an answer to this line. The king's dream already said the deed counts: "there is a deed that is pleasing in itself, not according to thoughts" (יש מעשה שהוא נרצה בעצמותו לא כפי המחשבות, line 3).

*Ours.* Chassidus later says it sharper: the mitzvah is G-d's inner will, and the deed in the hand reaches what no thought reaches (F10, S81). The Kuzari is its oldest support for that in the thought of the Rishonim.

**The Names say what He does, or what He is not.** Part 2 opens with the Names (line 125). All Names except the Tetragrammaton are of three kinds. Some come from His acts ("merciful", "jealous"). Some come from how people honor Him ("blessed", "holy"). Some only deny ("living", "one", "first"). "One" is said to deny many, not to give Him our kind of oneness:

> והעצם האלהי מרומם מהדביקה והפרידה, ונאמר אחד לשלל הרבוי (Kuzari 2:2; line 125)
> The divine Essence is above joining and parting. We say "One" to deny plurality.

He is "the essence of mind, and He is the mind, and mind is not a trait of His" (והוא השכל ואין השכל מדה לו, line 125). Mercy is not a feeling in Him. He is like a just judge whose one ruling makes one person rich and another poor "without any change in His Essence" (מבלי שישתנה בעצמותו, line 125). And will? Whatever it is that turns the heavens and shaped the air at Sinai into words, "call it will, or word, or what you like" (קרא אותו אתה: "חפץ" או "דבר" או כאשר תרצה, line 129).

*Ours.* Here the Kuzari sits close to the Rambam's negative attributes. Chassidus accepts that nothing we say reaches His Essence (F05, F12). But it adds what the Kuzari only hints at: the "negations" are also a ladder, and the Essence is found in the lowest place, not only above all (F07).

**The glory is a spark of His light where He is received.** The glory (kavod) is "a spark of divine light that helps His people and in His land" (ניצוץ אור אלהי מועיל אצל עמו ובארצו, line 131). It is like light on a cloudy day: people call it "sunlight", though bodies only shine because they face the sun (line 130).

**Everything was made for a vessel that can hold Him.** This is the Kuzari's answer to "why is there a world". The elements are for minerals, minerals for plants, plants for animals, animals for man, man for the chosen of men, and they for the chosen of the chosen, the prophets and the pious:

> והכל נהיה בעבור הסגלה ההיא להדבק בה הענין האלהי, והסגלה ההיא בעבור סגלת הסגלה, כמו הנביאים והחסידים (Kuzari 2:44; line 167)
> All of it came to be for that treasured one, so that the divine matter could cling to it; and that treasured one for the treasure of the treasure, such as the prophets and the pious.

*Ours.* This is the Kuzari's form of the "home below" (F07). The world exists so that the divine can rest on a created vessel. Chassidus says the same thing and moves the weight: the vessel is not only the rare prophet but every Jew in every deed, and the lowest place is the aim, not the path to the top. See KUZARI.md on where they part.

## States of consciousness

**The divine matter (הענין האלהי) as a level of its own.** The Chaver lists what makes things differ in kind. Plants have the "natural" level (feeding, growing). Animals add the "soul" level (movement, desire, senses). Man adds the "mind" level (order, city, law). And above mind there is a level that differs from man as man differs from beast (line 45). The king names it himself:

> אבל המעלה הזאת אלהית מלאכותית אם היא נמצאת, וזה מדין הענין האלהי לא מן השכלי ולא מן הנפשי ולא מן הטבעי (Kuzari 1:48; line 48)
> This level is divine, angelic, if it exists. It belongs to the divine matter, not to mind, not to soul, not to nature.

What is it like from inside? Adam had it: "the divine power after the mind, that is, the level by which he clings to G-d and the spiritual ones, and knows truths without learning, with a light thought" (line 101). It is a knowing that does not come by working things out. It is given, and it is sure.

*Ours.* This is the Kuzari's great contribution to a map of the soul. Chassidus calls something close to it the G-dly soul (nefesh haElokis) with its hidden love, and the essential bond (S41, S60). The Kuzari places it above intellect, as a different kind of faculty. That is the same step the Tanya takes when it puts the point of the soul above understanding (S37, S60).

**Meeting the one who has it.** "Anyone who meets a prophet at the time he meets him and hears his divine words, a new spirituality is born in him" (מתחדשת לו רוחניות, line 109). He is set apart from his kind "in purity of soul and longing for those levels and clinging to humility and purity". Nearness is caught from the near. A proposed state: **caught from the holy one** (see JSON in KUZARI.md). It is close to what Chassidus says of the tzaddik and of hearing a maamar from a Rebbe, and to S22 (hearing from afar).

**Not afraid of death.** Whoever reaches this level "will not fear death" (לא יירא מן המות, line 115). The king says of one who has seen such things: surely "he longs for his soul to stay in the parting from its senses and to go on delighting in that light" (line 114). The Kuzari is not dreamy about this. It is the fruit of a whole life of deeds, not an escape.

**The heart that is sick most and heals first.** Israel among the nations "is like the heart among the limbs: most sick of all, and most healthy of all" (line 159). The heart feels the least thing and throws it off at once; other limbs let a humor settle until it becomes a disease (lines 161-165). The same is said of the person: fine souls feel impurity that coarse ones do not notice (line 183).

> ישראל באמות כלב באברים, הוא רב חליים מכלם ורב בריאות מכלם (Kuzari 2:36; line 159)
> Israel among the nations is like the heart among the limbs: most sick of all, and most healthy of all.

*Ours.* As a state, this is **tender and quick to heal**. A person who is sensitive feels every wrong in himself sooner and more sharply. The Kuzari says this is not weakness. It is why he can be cleansed before the wrong takes root. It fits S05 (bitter with purpose) and S49, and it gives the sensitive person a way to see his sensitivity as a gift.

**The golden calf: wanting something to face.** The sin of the calf, in the Kuzari, was not a turn to another god. The people wanted a sensed thing "to face" while they waited, "as the other nations do", without denying the G-d who took them out (line 103). They built by their own reckoning what G-d had not commanded. This names a real state: **the hunger for something to hold** in the gap when G-d seems absent. It sits next to S18 (chasing the high) and S19.

## Meditation and practice

**Only the deed He gave brings near.** The king says it himself, and the whole book rests on it:

> כי אין האדם מגיע אל הענין האלהי אלא בדבר אלהי, רצוני לומר: במעשים שיצום האלהים (Kuzari 1:98; line 104)
> A person reaches the divine matter only by a divine thing, that is, by deeds that G-d commands.

The Chaver adds: "There is no reasoning, no deducing, and no clever thought in the service of G-d" (ואין בעבודת האל סברא ולא הקשה ולא התחכמות, line 105). The fool in the pharmacy (line 85) gives out medicines he does not understand and kills the people the doctor's medicines would have healed. People who invent ways to draw the divine down (star magic, talismans) are that fool. The real "helper in itself" is the divine matter, and "what harms in itself is its absence" (המועיל בעצמו הוא הענין האלהי, והמזיק בעצמו הוא העדרו, line 85).

*Ours.* For the guide this is a guard, and it is a strong one. A technique of the mind is a servant. It is not a way to force a result from above. The guide already says the deed closes the sitting (technique 21). The Kuzari says why: the deed is the one thing whose measure He set.

**Nearness is not the same as self-lowering.** The king asks why Jews do not have more hermits. The Chaver answers:

> לא יתכן להתקרב אל האלהים כי אם במעשים מצוים מאת האלהים, התחשב כי הקרבה היא השפלות והכניעה (Kuzari 2:46; line 169)
> One can only come near to G-d by deeds that G-d commands. Do you think that nearness is humbling and lowering oneself?

**The balanced life: give every power its share.** This is one of the key passages of the book:

> ותורת משה לא העבידה אותנו בפרישות, אך בדרך השוה ולתת לכל כח מכחות הנפש והגוף חלקו (Kuzari 2:50; line 173)
> The Torah of Moses did not make us serve by withdrawal, but by the even path, giving each power of soul and body its share.

Fasting is not service for one whose desires are weak and body thin; "better that he pamper his body". Poverty is no service if wealth comes lawfully. Then the line the guide should carry everywhere:

> וכללו של דבר, כי תורתנו נחלקת בין היראה והאהבה והשמחה, תתקרב אל אלהיך בכל אחת מהנה (Kuzari 2:50; line 173)
> In short, our Torah is shared among awe, love and joy. You may draw near to your G-d by each one of them.

> ואין כניעתך בימי התענית יותר קרובה אל האלהים משמחתך בימי השבתות והמועדים, כשתהיה שמחתך בכונה ולב שלם (Kuzari 2:50; line 173)
> Your humbling on fast days is no nearer to G-d than your joy on Shabbat and festivals, when your joy is with intention and a whole heart.

**Joy with intention.** Joy needs thought, as a plea does: "that you rejoice in the mitzvah itself, out of love for the One who commanded it, and recognize the good He did you by it". Then a picture: it is "as if you came into His inn, invited to His table and His good" (וכאלו אתה בא באכסניתו קרוא אל שלחנו וטובו, line 173). And if the joy moves into song and dance, "it is service and clinging to the divine matter" (היא עבודה ודבקות בענין האלהי, line 173).

*Ours.* A clear technique: **the guest at His table**. Before a Shabbat meal or a mitzvah, picture yourself a guest in His house, at His table, and let the thanks rise. It joins S53 (glad) and technique 19 (melody), and it gives joy a reason and a direction, which guards it from turning into lightness. See JSON (T-K1).

**Shabbat as a statement made by the body.** Keeping Shabbat "is itself the confession of G-d, but as if it were a confession in words of deed" (כאלו היא הודאה בדבור מעשי, line 173). One who rests because the work of creation ended there has confessed that the world was made, and so the Maker. "So keeping Shabbat brings one nearer to the Creator than withdrawal and abstinence" (line 173).

**"With everlasting love": the start is His, not ours.** The love in the blessing before Shema is placed there "so that we take to heart that the beginning is from Him, not from us" (שההתחלה ממנו לא ממנו אנחנו, line 173). As the living body did not make itself, so the people did not start its bond with G-d.

*Ours.* This is a quiet and deep instruction for prayer. Begin by remembering that He began. It is close to S65 (given right now) and S84 (trust).

**What real service looks like.** The marks of divine law are not "fine words, raised eyebrows and closed eyes in prayer and pleading" but pure thoughts whose proof is deeds that are hard on a person, done "with the utmost desire and love" and "the utmost joy and gladness" (line 179). This is technique 17 (telling real from counterfeit) in the Kuzari's own voice.

**Company matters.** "One who seeks to purify his thought in the proven sciences, or to purify his soul for pleading and prayer, will find harm" in the company of scoffers and in love songs and jokes (line 183). A guard for the one who sits in contemplation.

**Words said like a bird.** The king rebukes the Chaver: when you pray "bow to His holy mountain" and "who returns His Presence to Zion" without longing to go there, "it is only like the chirping of the starling" (כצפצוף הזרזיר, line 147). The Chaver agrees. Prayer said without meaning is noise.

## The big human questions

**Why do we exist?** For the divine to rest on a vessel ready for it (line 167). The ladder of creation runs up to the pious and the prophet.

**Is G-d waiting for us?** Yes, in the Kuzari's picture. As nature "waits" for a balanced mix to become a plant, and the soul waits for a formed body, and mind waits for a person whose nature and traits are even, so:

> והענין האלהי כמו צופה למי שראוי להדבק בו שיהיה לו לאלהים (Kuzari 2:14; line 137)
> And the divine matter is as if it looks out for whoever is fit to cling to it, to be his G-d.

And it comes in the measure of the readiness: "the divine matter rests on a person only according to his readiness for it, if little then little, if much then much" (אם מעט מעט ואם הרבה הרבה, line 147). The divine matter "does good and wants good for all", and when a thing is ordered to receive it, "it does not hold back from shining light and wisdom and knowledge on it" (line 149).

*Ours.* This is the Kuzari's word to anyone who feels far. The light is not held back. What is missing is the vessel, and the vessel is made by deeds, by order in the soul, by longing. It is a hopeful teaching, and it fits the Tanya's "the light is always there, the hiding is ours" (F03).

**Suffering.** Israel's sufferings are the heart's sickness: "He forgives the sins of His people, removing them one by one" and does not let them pile up to destroy us, as He let the Amorite's pile up (line 167). "The troubles that find us are a cause for setting right our faith, and for refining the pure ones among us and removing the dross" (line 167). The people is "not dead, but like a very sick man whom all doctors have given up, who hopes for healing by wonder" (line 157).

**The world to come.** The Kuzari's promise is nearness in this life. "Our promise is that we cling to the divine matter by prophecy and what is near it" (יעודינו הדבקנו בענין האלהי בנבואה, line 115). The Torah does not say "do this and I will bring you to gardens after death"; it says "you will be My people and I will be your G-d". One who has had nearness now has "the plain proof" of the world to come (line 109). The righteous of every nation are rewarded for their good deeds (line 117).

**Love and awe from Him first.** The bond starts from His side (line 173). The Chaver calls His giving of light "love" (ונקרא זה ממנו אהבה, line 173).

**Humility and exile.** The Chaver admits the king's sharpest point: if Jews bore exile willingly for G-d's sake, it would be their glory. Most bear it by force. Yet even one who could escape by "a word said without effort" and does not, earns merit (line 121).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 50-68 (the count of years, the week and the number ten held in common by all peoples, India's claims of antiquity), 187-189 (the sciences of the Sanhedrin, music), and 195-203 (the Hebrew language, the cantillation marks, vowels, swaying at reading) were read. They argue for the reliability of the tradition and the dignity of Hebrew. They support the frame (a living chain) but carry no unity teaching of their own, except that speech is "bringing what is in the speaker's soul into the soul of the hearer ... face to face" (line 195), which bears on why the guide prefers teaching from mouth to ear.
