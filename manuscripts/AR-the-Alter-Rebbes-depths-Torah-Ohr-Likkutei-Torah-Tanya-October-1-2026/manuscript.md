## What this slice adds to the full depth (one page)

1. The Alter Rebbe's maamarim go deeper by being honest about the self. Even the greatest lover of G-d is 'something, not nothing' (Tanya 35); even Rabbi Akiva's death on the word 'One' was a creature's yielding, 'still far from G-dliness' until a light from above took it in (TO Vayeshev); and yielding itself can turn into pride (LT Behar). So the bottom is not a feeling and not an achievement. It is a deed of His will, where no one stands apart, and a gift received, like Shabbos, that we only guard.

2. Under love there is a bond with no reason. The Torah gives love a reason, 'He is your life', and gives self-giving none, because self-giving binds to Him as He is, before He is anyone's Life: 'even had He not created the worlds, all would be the same before Him' (TO Esther). This point is equal in every Jew and surfaces even as a sinner's regret (LT Bamidbar). From His side the bond is a covenant He chose against reason (LT Nitzavim).

3. Knowing Him is sensing that He is, the way you sense your own soul without grasping what it is; feeling follows attention, so the command is to keep Him in mind as you keep yourself (LT Vaeschanan 6). The Essence was never hidden, only the light: 'even little children know there is a G-d' (TO Vayera).

4. The turn back is driven by compassion for one's own soul, for the distance itself, even without sin. If you feel nothing, the numbness is the sign of being cut off and a reason for greater mercy; we say 'Your great mercy' because only He knows how far it is (LT Shir HaShirim, Re'eh, Pinchas, Shabbos Shuva).

5. The low holds the high. Earth grows something from nothing, which the heavens cannot (Iggeres HaKodesh 20); the feet heal the head (TO Bereshis); hidden and revealed are equal before the Essence, so the highest light dresses in nature, like a groom sleeping in a tannery for his bride (TO Esther); a test holds G-d's power, like the top stone of a wall that falls farthest (LT Re'eh); the bare power of desire is holy at its root (LT Chukas); the beginner's ego is chaff that shields the kernel (TO Esther supplements).

6. For the guide: never demand that the depth be felt. Union does not depend on feeling ('I was like an animal with You, and I am always with You', Tanya 46; LT Vayikra 5). In the surrounding light reached by bowing, 'He sees no wrong' (TO Bereshis 1). The running and returning is His rhythm too; even His giving pulses, so that vessels can exist (TO Bereshis 2).

7. How these texts climb (stated markers): a kasha on a word or a clash of verses; 'the matter is'; a mashal from the person's own body or soul ('from my flesh I see G-d'); 'but in truth'; the mashal pushed until it breaks; 'higher still'; and then 'from this', down to the lips, the hand, the next mitzvah, the bow at Modim.

**How it was read.** SWEEP: every maamar in Torah Ohr (141 units) and Likkutei Torah (269 units) was ranked by density of depth terms (עצמות, מהות, ביטול היש, יחידה, נקודת הלב, מסירות נפש, כלא חשיב, רצוא ושוב, יש מאין, etc.); about forty of the densest and most central were then read in full (listed at the end), with the assigned Tanya chapters and Iggeres HaKodesh 20.

## AR-C01 · He made the worlds, so something must have changed. It did not.

1. **The world was made, yet nothing changed in Him. He is exactly who He was before there was a world.** (M1) How the text gets there: The text starts from the prayer 'You are He before the world was created, You are He after' and presses the word 'He': the very same, with no change at all. «פירוש – ״הוא״ ממש בלי שום שינוי» — *Meaning: 'He', truly, with no change at all.* · Tanya, line 278

2. **Look at yourself speaking. One word you say is nothing next to your power to speak, less next to your thinking, and nothing at all next to you.** (M9) How the text gets there: The proof is taken from the person's own soul: a word is tiny next to the power of speech that can say endless words, smaller next to thought, and nothing next to the soul's essence. «שדבור זה לבדו כלא ממש, אפילו לגבי כללות נפשו המדברת» — *This one word alone is truly as nothing, even next to the whole of the speaking soul.* · Tanya, line 281

3. **Go further in: before any words, there is just a wanting in the heart, with no letters in it at all.** (M8) How the text gets there: The ladder goes under thought: when a desire first falls into the heart it has no letters yet, only plain longing. «אין בה בחינת אותיות עדיין, רק חפץ פשוט וחשיקה בלב» — *It has no letters yet, only a simple wanting and longing in the heart.* · Tanya, line 283

4. **Here the comparison breaks. Your word leaves you and lives on its own. His word never leaves Him, because there is nothing outside Him for it to go to.** (M10) How the text gets there: The parable is pushed until it fails: human speech is felt as a separate thing; His is not, since 'there is nothing outside of Him'. «אבל הקדושברוךהוא אין דבורו מובדל ממנו יתברך חס ושלום, כי אין דבר חוץ ממנו» — *But the Holy One: His word is not separate from Him, G-d forbid, for there is nothing outside of Him.* · Tanya, line 286

5. **So why does the world feel separate? Because the hiding is there for us, so we can receive. To us it looks like something apart from Him.** (M11) How the text gets there: The contractions are named as 'hiding of the face', done so the lower ones can bear the light; the separateness is how it seems to them. «ולכן גם כן נדמה להם אור וחיות הדבור של מקום ברוךהוא המלובש בהם, כאלו הוא דבר מובדל ממהותו ועצמותו יתברך» — *And so it seems to them that the light and life of His word inside them is something separate from His very Being.* · Tanya, line 291

6. **And the hiding itself is not a wall between you and Him. It is like a snail's shell, grown from the snail. Darkness and light are the same to Him.** (M4) How the text gets there: 'But for Him' no contraction hides anything; the garments are not separate from Him, like the snail whose garment is from itself. «אך לגבי הקדושברוךהוא, אין שום צמצום והסתר והעלם מסתיר ומעלים לפניו, ו״כחשכה כאורה״» — *But for the Holy One, no contraction, hiding or covering hides anything before Him; 'darkness is as light'.* · Tanya, line 292

7. **That is why, before Him, everything is truly as nothing.** (M2) How the text gets there: The chapter's conclusion follows from the word that never left its speaker. «ולכן קמיה – כולא כלא חשיב ממש» — *Therefore, before Him, all is truly counted as nothing.* · Tanya, line 293

**Bottom.** Your own wanting-before-words, your word that is still inside you: that is how the world sits inside Him, never having left.

**Return.** Chapter 23 brings it down: a limb moves the instant you will it, with no order given; a mitzvah done by the body is that kind of limb for His will, and a thought of Torah is His will itself in your mind (lines 305, 309).

## AR-C02 · What is the root of being far from G-d?

1. **The root of all idol worship is not denying G-d. It is feeling yourself as a thing standing on your own.** (M2) How the text gets there: The text says idolaters even call Him 'G-d of gods'; their fault is counting themselves as a separate something. «כי עיקר ושרש עבודה זרה, הוא מה שנחשב לדבר בפני עצמו, נפרד מקדושתו של מקום» — *For the root of idol worship is being counted as a thing of its own, separate from G-d's holiness.* · Tanya, line 301

2. **Holiness only rests on what yields to Him. So the feeling 'I am my own' is the one thing that blocks Him.** (M2) How the text gets there: From the root just named: the Divine rests only where there is yielding. «כי, אין קדושה עליונה שורה אלא על מה שבטל לו יתברך» — *For the higher holiness rests only on what yields to Him.* · Tanya, line 301

3. **But even a plain, weak person would give up life rather than bow to an idol. Something in them refuses to be cut off. A 'spirit of folly' only covers it.** (M11) How the text gets there: The text takes the hardest case, a careless sinner, and shows the hidden love is there, covered by a spirit of folly. «לולי רוח שטות שבה – המכסה ומסתיר ומעלים את האהבה מסותרת שבנפשה האלהית» — *Were it not for the spirit of folly in her, which covers and hides the hidden love in her G-dly soul.* · Tanya, line 323

4. **Even while a person sins, the soul is still faithful to Him. It is in exile, not gone.** (M4) How the text gets there: After the sin, the text says, the soul 'returns'; and even during the sin it was with Him, only in exile inside the animal soul. «וגם בשעת החטא היתה באמנה אתו יתברך, רק שהיתה בבחינת גלות ממש» — *And even at the time of the sin it was faithful with Him, only it was truly in exile.* · Tanya, line 331

5. **This refusal to be cut off has no reason in it. It is not something you figured out. It is the nature of a G-dly soul.** (M5) How the text gets there: The text names the fear inside the love: not to be separated, even at the cost of life, 'without any reason or grasped thought'. «אפילו במסירת נפש ממש, בלי שום טעם ושכל מושג, אלא בטבע אלהי» — *Even to giving up one's life, without any reason or understanding, only by G-dly nature.* · Tanya, line 335

6. **It is above time. That is why no one will bow even for a moment and repent later: the light in the soul does not live in moments.** (M5) How the text gets there: The reason a person refuses even a brief act: the light of G-d in the soul is above time and rules over it. «מפני אור ה׳ המלובש בנפשם כנזכר לעיל, שאינו בבחינת זמן ושעה כלל, אלא למעלה מהזמן» — *Because of the light of G-d in their soul, which is not in time at all but above time.* · Tanya, line 338

7. **And it is in your hand, at every moment, to push away the folly and the forgetting and remember that love.** (M15) How the text gets there: Having reached a point above time, the text lands in 'every hour': it is near, in your mouth and heart. «שבכל עת ובכל שעה, בידו של אדם וברשותו הוא – להעביר רוח שטות והשכחה מקרבו» — *That at every time and hour it is in a person's hand and power to remove the spirit of folly and forgetting from within.* · Tanya, line 334

**Bottom.** A refusal to be cut off from Him, with no reason in it, above time, in every Jew.

**Return.** If you would accept death rather than be cut off for a moment, then the small daily things (resisting a desire, getting up to pray, giving charity) are much easier, and in them the inner will of G-d is fully present with nothing hiding it (lines 340-341). Remember this in the Shema every day (line 348).

## AR-C03 · Even a complete tzaddik who loves G-d: is he one with Him?

1. **The Zohar says the Divine light on a person's head needs oil, which is good deeds. Why isn't the soul enough? The soul is a piece of G-d.** (M1) How the text gets there: The kasha: the soul is 'a part of G-d above'; why should it need deeds, as a wick needs oil? «ולא די לו בנשמתו, שהיא חלק אלוה ממעל, להיות היא כשמן לפתילה» — *It is not enough for him that his soul, a part of G-d above, be the oil for the wick.* · Tanya, line 474

2. **Because even a perfect tzaddik who serves with love and delight is still someone: one who fears G-d and loves Him.** (M4) How the text gets there: The answer is surprising: the lover, however great, has not disappeared; he is a thing of his own that loves. «אינה בטילה במציאות לגמרי ליבטל וליכלל באור ה׳ ממש, להיות לאחדים ומיוחדים ביחוד גמור, רק הוא דבר בפני עצמו – ירא ה׳ ואוהבו» — *It is not wholly nullified, to be included in G-d's light and become one; it is a thing of its own, one who fears G-d and loves Him.* · Tanya, line 474

3. **G-d's presence rests only where something has completely yielded. Anything that is still 'someone' cannot hold it.** (M2) How the text gets there: Indwelling of the Shechinah is defined: the thing is included in His light and nullified. «אבל כל מה שלא בטל אליו במציאות לגמרי – אין אור ה׳ שורה ומתגלה בו» — *But whatever is not wholly nullified to Him, G-d's light does not rest and show itself in it.* · Tanya, line 475

4. **So the lover, who is 'something and not nothing', cannot grasp Him by his thought.** (M10) How the text gets there: The text draws the conclusion about the lover himself. «ואם כן, זה האוהב, שהוא יש ולא אפס – לית מחשבה דיליה תפיסא ביה כלל» — *And so this lover, who is something and not nothing: his thought does not grasp Him at all.* · Tanya, line 476

5. **A mitzvah is different. It is His own will, with no hiding at all. So the life in the deed is not a separate thing. It is one with Him.** (M6) How the text gets there: The commandments are the inner will itself; where there is no hiding, there is no separate thing. «אין החיות שבהם דבר נפרד בפני עצמו כלל, אלא הוא מיוחד ונכלל ברצונו יתברך» — *The life in them is not a separate thing at all; it is united and included in His will.* · Tanya, line 474

6. **And when the body does the mitzvah, the power of the body in that act is joined to Him completely.** (M15) How the text gets there: To bring the light even onto the body, one must do mitzvos done by the body itself. «שאז – כח הגוף ממש שבעשיה זו, נכלל באור ה׳ ורצונו ומיוחד בו ביחוד גמור» — *Then the very power of the body in this act is included in G-d's light and will and united with Him completely.* · Tanya, line 479

7. **Even the animal soul, which moves the hand, turns from bad to good in that moment.** (M11) How the text gets there: The animal soul is the go-between of soul and body; since it does the act, it is included in holiness. «נתהפך מרע לטוב ונכלל ממש בקדושה כנפש האלהית ממש» — *It is turned from bad to good and truly included in holiness like the G-dly soul.* · Tanya, line 480

**Bottom.** The deepest self-forgetting is not a feeling of love, which always has a lover in it. It is a deed of His will, where no one is standing apart.

**Return.** Do the mitzvah with the body: give with the hand, speak with the mouth, walk with the feet. The deed carries the oneness your love cannot (lines 479-482).

## AR-C04 · Knowing G-d is everywhere. Isn't knowing it enough?

1. **Knowing about G-d is not yet knowing G-d. Real knowing is fixing your mind on Him firmly, the way your mind holds a thing your eyes are looking at.** (M16) How the text gets there: The text separates hearing it 'from books' from deepening the mind until it is tied as to something seen. «אך עיקר הדעת, אינה הידיעה לבדה, שידעו גדולת ה׳ מפי סופרים ומפי ספרים» — *But the main thing in knowing is not the knowledge alone, knowing G-d's greatness from writers and books.* · Tanya, line 607

2. **It may be hard. Dig as for buried treasure; the awe is hidden in every heart.** (M3) How the text gets there: For a coarse soul the text lays down effort: 'I toiled and found'; the treasure is hidden in the understanding of the heart. «כך, צריך לחפור ביגיעה עצומה לגלות אוצר של יראת שמים, הצפון ומוסתר בבינת הלב של כל אדם מישראל» — *So one must dig with great toil to uncover the treasure of fear of Heaven, hidden in the heart's understanding of every Jew.* · Tanya, line 611

3. **How does He see you? Like you feel every part of your body, even heat in a toenail, from inside.** (M9) How the text gets there: The mashal is from one's own body: the brain knows every limb. «כמו אדם היודע ומרגיש בעצמו כל מה שנעשה ונפעל באחד מכל רמ״ח איבריו» — *Like a person who knows and feels in himself whatever happens in any of his limbs.* · Tanya, line 613

4. **But the comparison breaks: your soul is hurt by your body. He is not changed by anything in the world.** (M10) How the text gets there: The text says outright the parable is only to settle the ear. «רק שמשל זה, אינו אלא לשכך את האזן, אבל באמת, אין המשל דומה לנמשל כלל» — *This parable is only to quiet the ear; in truth it is not like the thing at all.* · Tanya, line 615

5. **You do not fear a king's body; asleep he frightens no one. You fear his life inside. So when your eyes see heaven and earth, see His life inside them.** (M7) How the text gets there: From outside to inside: we know the king's inner life only through his body and clothes; the world is His clothing. «שהרי כשישן – אין שום יראה ממנו» — *For when he is asleep, there is no fear of him at all.* · Tanya, line 619

6. **The main thing is habit: training the mind until it is fixed that everything you see is the King's outer garment. 'Faith' means training, like a craftsman training his hands.** (M16) How the text gets there: The step from knowing to near is not a new idea but repetition. «וזה נכלל גם כן בלשון ״אמונה״, שהוא לשון רגילות, שמרגיל האדם את עצמו, כמו אומן המאמן ידיו» — *This is also in the word 'faith', which means habit, training oneself like a craftsman trains his hands.* · Tanya, line 622

7. **Wisdom is seeing how each thing is born from nothing. And do not leave yourself out: your own body and soul are also nullified in His word.** (M9) How the text gets there: The higher fear: seeing the world nullified like sunlight in the sun, and then including oneself. «ואל יוציא אדם עצמו מהכלל, שגם גופו ונפשו ורוחו ונשמתו בטלים במציאות בדבר ה׳» — *And let a person not leave himself out: his body, soul and spirit are also nullified in G-d's word.* · Tanya, line 627

**Bottom.** You yourself, held in His word like a ray in the sun.

**Return.** If you think about this for a long while each day, the awe stays fixed for the whole day; then even a light reminder at any moment keeps you from what is wrong (line 617).

## AR-C05 · How do you wake love in a heart of stone?

1. **Love wakes love. As a face is reflected in water, a person who sees another truly loves him will love back.** (M9) How the text gets there: The text starts from plain human nature, 'as water reflects the face'. «״כמים הפנים לפנים, כן לב האדם אל האדם״» — *'As water reflects face to face, so the heart of man to man.'* · Tanya, line 658

2. **Now picture a great king who comes down with all his ministers to a lowly man lying on a dunghill, lifts him, and brings him into his innermost room. Even a heart of stone would melt.** (M3) How the text gets there: The mashal is built in full detail before it is applied. «ואף אם לבו כלב האבן – המס ימס והיה למים» — *And even if his heart is like a heart of stone, it will melt and turn to water.* · Tanya, line 659

3. **He did more. Every world and angel is nothing before Him, and He Himself came down into Egypt to lift us out.** (M6) How the text gets there: The nimshal is larger than the mashal: 'not by an angel', He in His glory and Himself. «אלא הקדושברוךהוא בכבודו ובעצמו ירד לשם» — *Rather, the Holy One in His glory and Himself went down there.* · Tanya, line 661

4. **And if you feel nothing? Asaf said: I am like an animal with You, I do not know; and I am always with You. And the little bit is very sweet to you, because you know how far you are.** (M16) How the text gets there: Even without present feeling, the past decision carries. «סומך לבו על שכבר גמר בלבו לעבוד ה' באמת זהו כמו באר מ"ח מפני שמאד ערב לו זאת המעט בעבודת ה' מפני שהתבונן שמאד מאד רחוק הוא מה'» — *He relies on having already decided in his heart to serve G-d truly; this is a well of living water, because this little service is very sweet to him, since he considered how very far he is.* · Torah Ohr, line 461

7. **The Arizal rejoiced greatly in doing mitzvos, not in his own service. Real humility is seeing that your friend is closer to G-d.** (M15) How the text gets there: The highest example lands on plain joy in the deed. «וזהו שהיה האריז"ל שמח מאד בעשיית המצות ולכך זכה לרה"ק ולא שמח בעבודתו» — *This is why the Arizal rejoiced greatly in doing mitzvos, and so merited the holy spirit, and did not rejoice in his own service.* · Torah Ohr, line 463

**Bottom.** A hungry soul to whom every bitter thing is sweet: the smallest deed tastes of Him.

**Return.** When you have forgotten, do the next mitzvah anyway, knowing you are far: that is the living water (lines 461-463).

## AR-C19 · Is there a love deeper than the love I understand?

*Likkutei Torah, Bamidbar 'Biur: Is ratzon ve-is ratzon' (lines 1144-1150)*

1. **One kind of will is born from understanding. The deeper you grasp, the more you want. The heart can hold it, because the mind rules the heart.** (M2) How the text gets there: The lower will is defined by its source. «רצון הנולד מן הדעת וההשכלה לפי העמקת שכלו» — *A will born from knowledge and understanding, according to the depth of his grasp.* · Likkutei Torah, line 1144

2. **So it is small or great as your mind is small or great.** (M10) How the text gets there: Its limit is named: it rises and falls with intellect. «והנה בתשוקת אהבה זו יש בה בחי׳ קטנות וגדלות לפי גדלות וקטנות השכל» — *This longing has smallness and greatness according to the greatness and smallness of the mind.* · Likkutei Torah, line 1145

3. **But there is a higher will at the root of the soul, shining below as the 'yechidah'. 5. **What stops it? 6. **That is why even the wicked are full of regrets: it comes from the yechidah.** (M9) How the text gets there: A proof from experience, quoted from the sages. «כי הרשעים מלאים חרטות מבחינת יחידה» — *For the wicked are full of regrets, from the yechidah.* · Likkutei Torah, line 1146

7. **Three wills: do His will as if it were yours; then His will in you; then cancel your will. The last is 'with all your might'.** (M13) How the text gets there: The levels are gathered into one ladder. «הג׳ בטל רצונך גלגלתא. בכל מאדך» — *The third: 'cancel your will', the skull; 'with all your might'.* · Likkutei Torah, line 1150

**Bottom.** A will at the root of the soul that the heart cannot feel and the body cannot hold, equal in all, surfacing even as a sinner's regret.

**Return.** Torah connects it to the powers that live in the body; hold it and do not let go until you bring it home (lines 1146-1148).

## AR-C20 · How can I 'know' G-d if no thought can grasp Him?

*Likkutei Torah, Vaeschanan 'Vidata hayom' (lines 2906-2929)*

1. **The verse commands us to know Him. But no thought grasps Him. How can that be?** (M1) How the text gets there: Two truths clash: 'know today' and 'no thought grasps Him'. «ולהבין איך יהיה בחי׳ סוכ״ע בבחי׳ הדעת וההרגשה והלא לית מחשבה תפיסא בי׳ כלל» — *To understand how the Surrounding can be in knowing and feeling, when no thought grasps Him at all.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2906

2. **Knowing here means sensing that something is, not grasping what it is.** (M2) How the text gets there: The text defines 'da'as' with the philosophers: knowing existence, not essence. «כי ענין הדעת הוא ידיעת והרגשת המציאות» — *For knowing means knowing and sensing existence.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2906

3. **You know this from yourself. You cannot grasp what your own soul is, yet you feel it is there, fully. You need no faith or proof for that.** (M9) How the text gets there: 'From my flesh I see G-d': the soul in the body is the model. «ומ״מ מציאותן יודע ומרגיש ממש אף שאינו משיג מהותן ואין צריך על זה אמונה וראיה. כ״א נרגש ממש» — *Still he knows and truly feels their existence though he does not grasp their essence; he needs no faith or proof for it; it is truly felt.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2910

4. **And notice: you feel your own matters strongly because you never take your attention off yourself. About a friend, even a dear one, you care less, because your mind leaves him.** (M9) How the text gets there: The strength of feeling follows the steadiness of attention. «משא״כ על אדם אחר אפילו על אוהבו וריעו לא איכפת ליה כ״כ כמו על עצמו» — *But about another person, even his friend and companion, he does not care so much as about himself.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2919

5. **Even natural feelings depend on attention. A soldier who stops thinking about his fear stops being afraid.** (M3) How the text gets there: The proof is laid down from a law of the Rambam. «הרי ראיה ברורה שאפילו ההתפעלות הטבעית שבאהבת עצמו היא תלויה ע״פ הדעת והמחשבה» — *A clear proof that even the natural feeling of self-love depends on knowing and thought.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2920

6. **So 'know the G-d of your father' means: do not take your mind off Him, as you never take it off yourself. Then love and awe follow on their own.** (M16) How the text gets there: The command is not to learn more but to keep attention. «כמ״כ ויתר מכן צ״ל שלא יסיח דעתו מחיי החיים א״ס ב״ה ועי״ז יומשכו מדותיו לאהוב את ה׳» — *So, and more, he must not take his mind off the Life of life, and through this his feelings will be drawn to love G-d.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2923

7. **Faith alone stays above, surrounding. A thief prays to G-d at the mouth of the tunnel, and still breaks in.** (M10) How the text gets there: Faith without knowing does not move the heart. «שהרי גנבא אפום מחתרתא רחמנא קריא הרי מאמין ביכולת רבש״ע ועכ״ז הוא עובר רצונו ית׳» — *For the thief at the mouth of the tunnel calls on G-d; he believes in the Master of the world's power, and still he transgresses His will.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2929

**Bottom.** Sensing that He is, the way you sense that you are: no grasp of what, no doubt of that.

**Return.** Learning Torah grasps the essence of His will dressed in physical things; it feeds faith and makes it knowing, as grain gives a child the mind to say 'Abba' (line 2939).

## AR-C21 · If I yield myself completely, have I arrived?

*Likkutei Torah, Behar 'Es shabsosai' (lines 996-1007) and Torah Ohr, Vayeshev (lines 654-660)*

1. **The purpose of every mitzvah is to turn the 'something' into 'nothing'. 2. **But this yielding is not yet the true one: he is still someone, nullifying himself.** (M4) How the text gets there: The text undercuts its own high point. «אמנם ביטול זה אינו אמיתי שהרי מ״מ יש הוא אלא שמבטל א״ע» — *But this nullification is not true, for still he is a something, only he nullifies himself.* · Likkutei Torah, line 997

3. **Even Rabbi Akiva, whose soul left him on the word 'One', was a creature giving himself, still far from G-dliness, until a light from the true One took it in.** (M5) How the text gets there: The extreme case is chosen to make the point. «ואפי׳ ר״ע שיצתה נשמתו באחד עכ״ז הרי מס״נ של הנברא רחוק עדיין מאלקות אלא שנמשך עליו הארה מבחי׳ אחד האמת» — *Even Rabbi Akiva, whose soul left on 'One': still a creature's self-giving is far from G-dliness, until a light from the true One is drawn onto it.* · Torah Ohr, line 660

6. **Guard especially that the yielding itself does not become a 'something': 'I nullified myself by my own power'. It is all from G-d.** (M11) How the text gets there: The subtlest trap: pride in one's humility. «שלא יהיה ביטול זה גופא בבחי׳ יש שהוא ביטל עצמו ע״י כחו» — *That this very nullification not be a 'something': that he nullified himself by his own strength.* · Likkutei Torah, line 1007

7. **And yet every Jew yields a little, as he can, by subduing or by transforming. That is his share. One who does not bow at Modim has none.** (M15) How the text gets there: The highest rung lands in the bow during the Amidah. «וביטול זה ישנו בכל א׳ מישראל. שהרי כל א׳ מבטל א״ע מעט לפי ערכו» — *This yielding is in every Jew, for each one nullifies himself a little, according to his measure.* · Likkutei Torah, line 997

**Bottom.** The true nothing is not an achievement but a gift; the creature's best yielding is only the vessel for it.

**Return.** Bow at Modim; do the small yielding of today; guard it from becoming pride (lines 997, 1007).

## AR-C22 · Why should I weep in prayer if I have not sinned?

*Likkutei Torah: Shir HaShirim 'L'reiach' (4368-4372), Re'eh 'Ani l'dodi' (3345), Pinchas (2532-2533), Shabbos Shuva (3808), Rosh Hashanah (3713-3714)*

2. **So even without sin, being in a body is a great distance from Him. Think: where did you come from, where are you going. Not feeling the pain is itself the sign of being cut off, and a reason for even greater mercy.** (M10) How the text gets there: The limb mashal is pushed to the case of numbness. «אך מה שאינו מרגיש בנפשו הכאב והפגם היינו מפני שפגם כ״כ עד שנפרד ונכרת משרשו לגמרי, ומזה אדרבה יעורר רחמים יותר גדולים» — *That he does not feel the pain in his soul is because he damaged so much that he was cut off from his root; and from this, on the contrary, let him arouse even greater mercy.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3345

7. **That is the weeping of Rosh Hashanah: it is for the soul, that came down from its place and became physical.** (M15) How the text gets there: The custom is explained by the chain. «אך ע״פ הנ״ל יובן שהבכיה היא לנשמתו דוקא שירדה ממקום כבודה ונתגשמה» — *From the above it is understood that the weeping is for his soul, that came down from its place of honor and became physical.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3714

8. **And one thought of return reconnects even what was cut off. A captive prince is still a prince; with one move he turns back.** (M12) How the text gets there: Cut off and still a prince: both are true. «כי אינו דומה מעלת השר עם היותו בשביה למעלת ההדיוט, כי עדיין ניכר וידוע מעלתו של השר ובתנועה א׳ יכול שיהפך מן ההיפך אל ההיפך לגמרי» — *A prince in captivity is not like a commoner; his rank is still known, and with one movement he can turn from one extreme to the other.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3345

**Bottom.** Compassion for your own soul, for the distance itself, measured by the One who alone knows its size.

**Return.** After the crying comes the joy and the bread: cry in the inner room, then 'put out the bread', learn, do (Behar 1, line 974).

## AR-C23 · My love in prayer is real, but it fades after. Is it true love?

**Return.** Pray first, then learn right after, so the Torah is baked into the soul (lines 969, 976).

## AR-C25 · Why would He stay bound to me when I am so far?

## AR-D1 · Even the lover is still someone

**Teaching.** The deepest love of G-d still has a lover in it: someone who loves and fears Him. That someone is 'something, not nothing', and the Presence rests only on what has fully yielded. The deed of a mitzvah is different: it is His will with nothing hiding it, so in the act itself there is no separate one. The descent does not end in a feeling of love; it ends in a deed where no one is standing apart. (stated: Tanya 35; ours: the placement after D2.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Feel the love you have for G-d, whatever it is.
2. Notice that there is someone feeling it: you, the lover.
3. Hear the text: that lover is 'something and not nothing'; his thought does not hold Him.
4. Now turn to a mitzvah you are about to do. It is His will itself, with no hiding.
5. In the doing, the power of your body in that act is joined to Him completely. Do it, and let the doing be the place where you are not apart.

**From inside.** A wick that needs oil: the soul alone does not keep the flame; deeds are the oil (Tanya 35, the Zohar's picture).

**Where it lands.** The hand giving a coin, the mouth saying a blessing, the feet walking to a mitzvah, now.

**Extends.** D2 and D12: sharpens D2 (the self cannot make itself vanish) by saying where it does vanish: in His will done by the body.

- «ואם כן, זה האוהב, שהוא יש ולא אפס – לית מחשבה דיליה תפיסא ביה כלל» — *This lover, who is something and not nothing: his thought does not grasp Him at all.* · Tanya, line 476 - «שאז – כח הגוף ממש שבעשיה זו, נכלל באור ה׳ ורצונו ומיוחד בו ביחוד גמור» — *Then the body's very power in this act is included in G-d's light and will, completely united.* · Tanya, line 479

## AR-D2 · Your yielding is not yet the nothing

**Teaching.** Turning the 'something' into 'nothing' is the aim of every mitzvah, but a creature's yielding is still a creature yielding: 'still he is a something, only he nullifies himself'. Even Rabbi Akiva's death on 'One' was 'still far from G-dliness' until a light from the true One took it in. The true nothing is a gift, like Shabbos, to be guarded, not made; and the worst trap is pride in one's own yielding. (stated: LT Behar; TO Vayeshev.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Yield as much as you can: in this moment you are His word being spoken.
2. Now look honestly: who is yielding? Someone is still there, doing it.
3. Do not try harder to vanish. The text says the true nothing is given, as Shabbos is given.
4. Guard only one thing: do not say 'I nullified myself'.
5. Bow, and let what comes come.

**From inside.** Being mute: like the sheaves, like a fetus that eats what its mother eats; you stop speaking and giving for a while (TO Vayeshev).

**Where it lands.** The bow at Modim; Shabbos received, not produced.

**Extends.** D2 (no one left to watch): confirms and sharpens it with the Akiva case and the warning that bittul can itself become a 'yesh'.

- «אמנם ביטול זה אינו אמיתי שהרי מ״מ יש הוא אלא שמבטל א״ע» — *This nullification is not true, for he is still a something, only he nullifies himself.* · Likkutei Torah, line 997 - «ואפי׳ ר״ע שיצתה נשמתו באחד עכ״ז הרי מס״נ של הנברא רחוק עדיין מאלקות» — *Even Rabbi Akiva, whose soul left on 'One': a creature's self-giving is still far from G-dliness.* · Torah Ohr, line 660 - «שלא יהיה ביטול זה גופא בבחי׳ יש שהוא ביטל עצמו ע״י כחו» — *That this very nullification not become a 'something': that he nullified himself by his own power.* · Likkutei Torah, line 1007

## AR-D3 · Bound to Him before He is your Life

**Teaching.** The Torah gives a reason for love, 'He is your life', and none for giving up life. Because 'He is your life' describes Him as Life-giver to the worlds, and 'that is not the main thing of G-dliness'; even had He created nothing, all would be the same to Him. Self-giving comes from refusing to be cut off from Him as He is, above being Life of life. That is why it has no reason, and why it is in every Jew, even the lightest. (stated: TO Esther 12; LT Vayikra 5.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. 'He is my life': feel how much you love being alive, and that He is the life in it.
2. Now go under it: He is not only the one who gives life. Even had He made no world, He would be exactly the same.
3. Ask what in you would refuse to be cut off from Him even if it cost your life.
4. Notice it has no reason. It is not 'because'. It is just 'not separate'.
5. Rest there: that is the bond under the love.

**From inside.** A candle in a torch: the soul's essence above the mind is nullified in His Essence (LT Vayikra 5, line 238).

**Where it lands.** The daily Shema 'with self-giving at One'; refusing one small separation today because it is a separation.

**Extends.** D8 (what you cannot let go): extends D8 by locating the bond above Him-as-Life-giver, not inside love.

- «ואין זה טעם על מסירת נפש כיון שהטעם הוא כי הוא חייך והרי מסירת נפש הוא להשליך חייו מנגד» — *This is no reason for self-sacrifice: the reason is 'He is your life', and self-sacrifice is casting life away.* · Torah Ohr, line 2631 - «וגם אילו לא היה בורא העולמות היה הכל שוה לפניו ית'» — *Even had He not created the worlds, all would be the same before Him.* · Torah Ohr, line 2632

## AR-D4 · The Essence was never hidden

**Teaching.** The great contraction hid the light, not the Luminary. 'In the Luminary, the Infinite Himself, contraction and hiding do not apply; on the contrary, it is revealed; so even children know there is a G-d', without grasping how. The space is not truly empty; He fills it. What a child knows simply is the deepest, not the least. (stated: TO Vayera.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Notice how you know that He is, before any argument.
2. That knowing does not explain 'how'. It is simple.
3. Hear: what is hidden is only the light, the 'how'. He Himself is not hidden.
4. So the simplest knowing touches the most essential.
5. Stay with the plain 'He is here'.

**From inside.** A child's certainty with no proof.

**Where it lands.** Saying 'Modeh ani' like a child, meaning it plainly.

**Extends.** D13 (plainness is deepest) and D7 (simple faith): supplies the reason plainness reaches: the Essence is revealed, the light is what is hidden.

- «ואדרבה המאור הוא בהתגלות ולכן אפילו תנוקות יודעים שיש שם אלוה מצוי» — *On the contrary, the Luminary is revealed; therefore even little children know there is a G-d.* · Torah Ohr, line 315 - «שהרי המאור ממלא את כל החלל» — *For the Luminary fills the whole void.* · Torah Ohr, line 317

## AR-D5 · Knowing He is, as you know you are

**Teaching.** Da'as is not grasping what He is; no thought does that. It is sensing that He is, the way you sense your own soul: you cannot say what it is, yet you feel it fully and need no faith or proof. And feeling follows attention: you are moved by your own affairs because you never take your mind off yourself. 'Know the G-d of your father' means: never take your mind off Him. Faith alone stays above, like the thief who prays at the tunnel and breaks in anyway. (stated: LT Vaeschanan 6.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Feel that you are here. You do not know what your soul is; you know it is.
2. That is the kind of knowing asked of you about Him.
3. Notice how your feelings follow your attention: what you keep in mind moves you.
4. Keep Him in mind the way you keep yourself in mind, without effort to define Him.
5. Let love and awe come on their own.

**From inside.** Not a vision, not a concept: the quiet certainty of being alive.

**Where it lands.** Shiviti: G-d before me always, through the day, as one carries oneself.

**Extends.** D5 (where knowing ends): corrects a possible reading of D5. The end of grasping is not the end of knowing; a sensing of existence remains and is commanded.

- «ומ״מ מציאותן יודע ומרגיש ממש אף שאינו משיג מהותן ואין צריך על זה אמונה וראיה. כ״א נרגש ממש» — *He knows and truly feels their existence though he does not grasp their essence; he needs no faith or proof; it is truly felt.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2910 - «כמ״כ ויתר מכן צ״ל שלא יסיח דעתו מחיי החיים א״ס ב״ה» — *So, and more, he must not take his mind off the Life of life.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2923

## AR-D6 · Compassion for your own soul

**Teaching.** Under the bottom there is a weeping that is not about sin. Even without sinning, being in a body is 'utter distance' from Him, and all paradise is one ray. The return is compassion for one's own soul. If you feel nothing, that numbness is the sign of being cut off, a reason for 'even greater mercy'; and since only He knows the size of the distance, we say 'in Your great mercy'. One thought of return reconnects; a captive prince is still a prince. (stated: LT Shir 4, Re'eh, Pinchas, Shabbos Shuva, Rosh Hashanah, Shir 41.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Think where your soul came from: above wisdom, above all worlds.
2. Think where it is: in a body, busy, coarse, even when nothing is wrong.
3. Let pity come, not guilt: pity for the soul in exile.
4. If you feel nothing, know that the numbness itself is the cut, and ask Him: in Your great mercy, because You know how great it is.
5. One thought turns it; you are still a prince.

**From inside.** Tears in the inner room; the shofar's sobbing; 'the lower waters weep: we want to be before the King'.

**Where it lands.** Rosh Hashanah weeping; 'ahavah rabbah... b'rachamecha harabim' every morning; after the tears, the bread of Torah.

**Extends.** New: between D8 (the bond) and D9 (the turn). The turn is driven by compassion, not by insight.

- «אף שלא חטא אעפ״כ הוא נתגשם ונפרד ממהותו ועצמותו בתכלית הריחוק» — *Even though he did not sin, he became physical and separated from His Essence in utter distance.* · Likkutei Torah, line 4369 - «והנה תשובה זו אינו דייקא על עבירות כ״א על הריחוק» — *This return is not specifically for sins, but for the distance.* · Likkutei Torah, line 5015 - «ומזה אדרבה יעורר רחמים יותר גדולים» — *And from this, on the contrary, let him arouse even greater mercy.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3345

## AR-D7 · Hidden and revealed are the same to Him

**Teaching.** Every world is hidden to the one below and revealed to the one above; that chain is the chain of worlds. Before His Essence there is no above and below. When the Essence showed itself at the Sea, hidden and revealed became equal and the sea became dry land. On Purim, when His Essence shines, Haman and Mordechai are equal, darkness like light; and the highest light, having nowhere to dress, dresses in the lowest: nature, a story with no Name, a groom in a tannery. (stated: TO Beshalach, Esther.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Notice what in your life is hidden and what is clear.
2. Hear: hidden and revealed are a scale for creatures.
3. Before Him, the hidden day is not further than the revealed one.
4. So the natural, nameless events of today carry the highest light, because it has nowhere else to go.
5. Look at an ordinary event and say: the groom came to the tannery.

**From inside.** The sea turning to dry land; the joy that goes past knowing.

**Where it lands.** Seeing Hashgacha in an ordinary day; the Purim meal's joy.

**Extends.** D4 (neither something nor nothing) and D10 (the world given back as His): adds that the hidden itself is His chosen dwelling for the highest.

- «שההעלם נעשה גילוי ע"י גילוי עצמותו שהעלם וגילוי שוין ממש» — *Hiddenness became revelation through the revelation of His Essence, for which hidden and revealed are truly equal.* · Torah Ohr, line 1662 - «מפני שמרוב מדרגתו הגבוה אין לו כלום במה להתלבש אלא במקום היותר נמוך» — *Because, from the height of its level, it has nothing to dress in except the lowest place.* · Torah Ohr, line 2638

## AR-D8 · The bare desire is holy at its root

**Teaching.** A desire has clothes (the object, the thoughts and words about getting it) and a body: the bare power of wanting. The power itself 'is not sin or evil'; it can want good as it now wants bad, and its root is in high holiness. Burn the clothes and keep the ash: then the same power turns to love Him like one's own life. G-d can be found even 'from there'. (stated: LT Chukas.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Feel a desire you have, as it is.
2. Separate the object from the wanting: what you want, and the wanting itself.
3. Do not think the object, do not speak it, do not do it: burn the clothes.
4. Feel the bare wanting that remains. It is not evil.
5. Turn it, as it is, toward Him: 'my soul longs for You'.

**From inside.** Ash: what is left when the smoke is gone is the wood's own earth.

**Where it lands.** Closing the eyes from a sight; turning the energy of craving into prayer.

**Extends.** D10 and D11 (the world and the self given back): the self returns with its desire included, not removed.

- «מ״מ אין מהות כח הזה חטא ורע מצד עצם עצמותו שהרי עצם מהותו הוא רק כח המתאוה והרי הוא יכול להיות כח המתאוה לטוב» — *The essence of this power is not sin or evil in itself; it is only the power to desire, and it can desire good.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2117 - «כי לית אתר פנוי מיניה ולכן גם מבחי׳ שם יוכלו למצוא את ה׳» — *There is no place empty of Him, so even from 'there' they can find G-d.* · Likkutei Torah, line 2120

## AR-D9 · Practice without breath is a womb

**Teaching.** A false bottom: the mitzvos done, the heart cold. The text calls it exile: like a fetus with every limb, fed through the navel, with no breath; 'a learned human command, physical doing alone and coldly'. A love that rises only in prayer and then passes is 'the lip of truth', the hem: the beginning of truth, and it leaves a mark. Neither is rejected; both are named as stages, and the way on is compassion, then joy, like a newborn who cries first and then gets milk. (stated: TO Vaera; LT Behar 1.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Notice where your practice is cold.
2. Do not despise it: it is a womb, and the limbs are all there.
3. Notice the love that comes in prayer and goes. It is the hem of truth.
4. Cry for the soul in its exile; anger at the bad, then pity, then joy.
5. Wait for breath; it comes with birth.

**From inside.** A fetus that eats what its mother eats; a newborn's first cry.

**Where it lands.** Keep the rote mitzvah; add the cry; after prayer, notice the trace that remains.

**Extends.** D12 (the deed in the hand): a caution. The deed is the deepest place, but the deed without breath is still a womb, not the bottom.

- «בבחי' עשיה גשמית בלבד ובקרירות כו'. וזהו עיקר הגלות ברוחניות» — *Physical doing alone and coldly; this is the main spiritual exile.* · Torah Ohr, line 1420 - «דהיינו התחלת האמת אבל אינו נקרא בשם אמת גמור לאמתו» — *That is the beginning of truth, but it is not called complete truth.* · Likkutei Torah, line 957

## AR-D10 · The test holds His power

**Teaching.** A test is a hiding of His face, and 'in the hiding itself there is G-dliness'. Shimi's curse came with G-d's breath in his mouth. When you stand firm you break the shell and find the fruit: a power of G-d from very high, because, as with a falling wall, the highest stone falls farthest. The beginner's ego, too, is a husk with a purpose: like chaff, it shields the kernel from heat and rot until it falls away. (stated: LT Re'eh 3; TO Esther supplements.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Name the thing that is testing you.
2. Hear: the hiding is also from Him; His breath is in it.
3. Ask what fruit is inside this shell.
4. Stand, without giving in.
5. Expect the fruit: a knowing of Him you could not get another way.

**From inside.** Breaking a shell; a stone falling from the top of a wall.

**Where it lands.** Not answering an insult; not giving in today.

**Extends.** D10 (the world given back as His) and M11: extends 'the world is His' to the obstacle itself.

- «הנה בחינת הנסיונות הם הסתר פנים ובהסתר עצמו יש אלהות» — *Tests are a hiding of the face, and in the hiding itself there is G-dliness.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3154 - «אך הענין הוא דבאמת סיבת הצמיחה באה ע"י המוץ» — *In truth the growing comes through the chaff.* · Torah Ohr, line 2954

## AR-D11 · Even His giving pulses

**Teaching.** Running and returning is not only ours. The heart beats, the breath goes out and in; the giving from the Infinite itself 'reaches and does not reach', spreading and withdrawing, or the vessels would dissolve. In His giving the spreading comes first; in ours the running comes first. 'If your heart runs, return to the One': 'against your will you live', to bring His life down. And the ash stays on the water: the mark of the running remains in the return. (stated: TO Bereshis 2; Tanya 50; LT Chukas.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Feel your heartbeat: out, back, out.
2. Hear: His light comes to the world in the same rhythm, so the world can exist.
3. When your love runs toward Him, let it, and then return.
4. Return into the body, to a deed, to speech.
5. Keep a trace of the running in the return.

**From inside.** A pulse; breath; a flame that rises and falls.

**Where it lands.** After intense prayer, going straight to learning and to a mitzvah; not staying in the high.

**Extends.** D14 (no bottom: begin again): gives D14 a body. The rhythm is His before it is ours.

- «כי מיד שנמשך ומתפשט צ"ל הסתלקות שאל"כ יתבטלו הכלים מהיות בחי' כלים» — *For as soon as it spreads there must be withdrawal, or the vessels would cease to be vessels.* · Torah Ohr, line 37 - «פירוש ״ואם רץ לבך״ – היא תשוקת הנפש שבלב» — *'If your heart runs': that is the longing of the soul in the heart.* · Tanya, line 708

## AR-D12 · He bound Himself to you

**Teaching.** From His side there is no likeness at all; a king of trees and stones is no king. Yet He 'compelled Himself, as it were, to bind our souls to Him', like friends who make a covenant because they fear the reason for love may end, so that love lasts even when reason says it should stop. From our side the answer is a voice without words, the shofar, and the bitterness of distance, which on a long scale gives the weight. (stated: LT Nitzavim.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Think how far you are from Him, in every way.
2. Hear: the bond does not rest on likeness or merit; He made a covenant.
3. A covenant holds even when reason says it should not.
4. Answer with a voice, not words: a sigh, a cry.
5. Let the distance become weight.

**From inside.** The shofar's plain blast; two friends passing between the halves.

**Where it lands.** Rosh Hashanah; any moment of feeling unworthy: answer it with the covenant.

**Extends.** D8 (what you cannot let go): adds the other side. The bond also holds from His side, by covenant beyond reason.

- «שאף שע״פ הטעם והדעת היה צריך לפסוק האהבה או לגרום איזה שנאה אעפ״כ מחמת הכריתות ברית מוכרח להיות אהבתם לעד קיימת» — *Even when by reason the love should stop or turn to hatred, because of the covenant their love must last forever.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3516 - «שהכריח את עצמו כביכול לקשר נפשותינו בו ית׳» — *He compelled Himself, as it were, to bind our souls to Him.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3525

## AR-D13 · The union you do not feel

**Teaching.** Union does not depend on feeling it. A plain person doing a mitzvah has G-d resting on him, 'only his soul does not feel it'. 'I was like an animal with You, and I am always with You.' One who learns a law for its own sake has his essence included in the Infinite 'even though he does not feel this inclusion at all', even if a little pride creeps in; 'though he does not see, his root sees'. (stated: Tanya 46; LT Vayikra 5.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Notice that you feel nothing much.
2. Hear: feeling is not the measure of being joined.
3. While you do this mitzvah, learn this law, He rests on you.
4. Your root sees what you do not.
5. Say: I am like an animal with You, and I am always with You.

**From inside.** Coarse matter as a screen over eyes that are still open behind it.

**Where it lands.** Standing up for anyone doing a mitzvah, even an unlearned person (Tanya 46).

**Extends.** D7 and D12: removes the demand that the depth be felt.

- «רק שאין נפשו מרגשת, מפני מסך החומר הגופני» — *Only his soul does not feel it, because of the screen of bodily matter.* · Tanya, line 666 - «ואע״פ שהאדם אינו מרגיש כלל התכללות זו בשעת עסקו בתורה» — *Even though the person does not feel this inclusion at all while he learns Torah.* · Likkutei Torah, line 241

## AR-D14 · No checking in the surrounding light

**Teaching.** In the inner light G-d 'tests hearts', checking if the longing is real. In the surrounding light, reached by bowing, 'He sees no wrong': only 'G-d his G-d is with him', drawn on its own, always. The one who has nothing, not even his own Torah and mitzvos, is the one He looks at; 'Blessed are You' means 'be blessed from Yourself'. (stated: TO Bereshis 1.)

**The hisbonenus (ours, on the text's steps).**

1. Notice the voice checking whether your prayer is sincere enough.
2. Hear: in the inner light that checking is real.
3. But bow: in the surrounding light there is no checking.
4. Come as one who has nothing of his own.
5. Say 'Baruch atah' and let Him be blessed from Himself.

**From inside.** Bowing to a king you cannot come near, so that he comes to you.

**Where it lands.** The bows of the Amidah; praying when you feel insincere.

**Extends.** D2 and D13: a relief for the self that keeps auditing its own depth.

- «משא"כ בבחינת מקיף לא הביט און וגו' רק ה' אלהיו עמו שנמשך מאליו תמיד» — *In the surrounding light 'He sees no wrong'; only 'G-d his G-d is with him', drawn on its own, always.* · Torah Ohr, line 14 - «אל עני ונכה רוח בחינת ביטול דלית ליה מגרמיה כלום אפילו תורה ומצות» — *To the poor and broken in spirit: yielding, having nothing of his own, not even Torah and mitzvos.* · Torah Ohr, line 13

## 3. Avodah: what the texts say to do

- **Each morning, give the soul back: say 'Elokai neshamah' meaning 'from now I hand it back to You'; do the same before learning, and each hour of long study.** «והנה, בהכנה זו של מסירת נפשו לה׳, יתחיל ברכות השחר» — *With this preparation of giving his soul to G-d, let him begin the morning blessings.* · Tanya, line 600
- **Before putting on tallis and tefillin, think at length that G-d stands over you and fills the earth, and accept His kingship.** «ויעמיק במחשבה זו ויאריך בה כפי יכולת השגת מוחו ומחשבתו, וכפי הפנאי שלו, לפני עסק התורה, או המצוה, כמו לפני לבישת טלית ותפילין» — *Let him deepen and lengthen this thought as his mind and time allow, before Torah or a mitzvah, as before putting on tallis and tefillin.* · Tanya, line 579
- **Set a long time once a day to contemplate that He fills everything and sees you; then a light reminder keeps you all day.** «כשיתבונן בזה שעה גדולה בכל יום» — *When he contemplates this for a long while every day.* · Tanya, line 617
- **Say the words of Torah and prayer aloud; thought alone does not bring the light down to the body.** «עד שיוציא בשפתיו» — *Until he brings it out with his lips.* · Tanya, line 701
- **Sing Pesukei d'Zimra as a melody, and test it: does it touch the point of your heart?** «משא"כ כשאומר הפסוקים ואינו נוגע לנקודת לבבו אין זה בחי' זמרה ונגון» — *But when he says the verses and it does not touch the point of his heart, that is not song.* · Torah Ohr, line 1611
- **A busy working person should not think he cannot pray as well as a scholar; let the day's worries feed the prayer.** «כי אדרבה נהפוך הוא שהם יכולים להתפלל יותר כי יתרון האור הוא מתוך החשך דוקא» — *On the contrary: they can pray more, for the advantage of light comes from darkness.* · Torah Ohr, line 202
- **Fix your study time right after prayer, so the Torah is 'baked' by prayer's fire.** «ולזאת העת קבוע ללמוד יהי׳ מיד אחר התפלה» — *Therefore the fixed time for learning should be right after prayer.* · Likkutei Torah, line 976
- **When the morning has faded, do the next mitzvah anyway, knowing how far you are; rely on your earlier decision to serve Him.** «סומך לבו על שכבר גמר בלבו לעבוד ה' באמת» — *He relies on having already decided in his heart to serve G-d truly.* · Torah Ohr, line 461
- **Do one thing beyond your habit: the hundred-and-first review, the extra coin of charity.** «וכן בצדקה שהפרוטה שהוא נותן יותר מרגילותו חשוב ביותר מן הרגילות» — *So in charity: the coin he gives beyond his habit is worth more than the habitual.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3010
- **Before Rosh Hashanah and in Elul, examine your past deeds where the love was lost, and let your heart become bitter and turn.** «שיפשפש במעשיו ודבוריו ומחשבותיו שעברו וחלפו מיום היותו אשר לא טובים» — *That he examine his past deeds, words and thoughts from his birth that were not good.* · Likkutei Torah, line 3343

## 4. Disagreements and limits kept

- Tanya 35 says even a complete tzaddik's love leaves him 'something'; TO Vaera says Moshe in Torah 'did not feel himself at all'. Ours: these are two levels, and the texts hold both. The guide should not tell a person the feeling of love is the end. - 'Bittul' is praised everywhere, and LT Behar (line 1007) warns that bittul can become a 'yesh'. Keep both: urge the yielding, never the pride in it. - Several maamarim say the highest rung 'cannot be awakened from below' (TO Beshalach 1666; LT Shir 13). That is not a reason to stop; the same texts give the human part (bitterness, compassion, the deed, the blessing before the mitzvah). - Teachings about the nations, angels, Kabbalistic worlds and specific Temple rites were read but not carried over unless they bear on the person's inner path. - Long maamarim whose last parts are mainly Kabbalistic cross-reference (LT Vayikra 5, Shir HaShirim 13 and 41, Re'eh 3, Shabbos Shuva 1, Shir HaShirim 1) were read through their argument and skimmed at the end.
