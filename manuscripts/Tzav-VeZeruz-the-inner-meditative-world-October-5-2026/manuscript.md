# Tzav VeZeruz: the inner meditative world

*Text read:* Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Tzav VeZeruz (Warsaw, 1930-1940).txt  
*Range:* whole file, lines 1-205

---
This is the Rebbe's private notebook, not a book he composed as one. He explains why he kept it in the first entry: a person should write down his thoughts, "not to make a composition of it, but to engrave himself on paper" (line 2, *רק לחרות את עצמו עלי נייר*). So the reader gets short commands to himself, dated confessions (turning forty, line 51; a Third Meal on Shabbos, line 44), prayers written out in full, the ecstasy of Simchat Torah 1932, and the pages he wrote at his son's hospital bed in 1935-36, with a postscript after the son and daughter-in-law were killed in September 1939. Much of what is taught systematically elsewhere (hashkatah, the bnei machshavah tovah) is not taught here. What is here is the same inner world lived through in the first person, which makes it the most direct witness we have of what his meditation was like from inside.

Line numbers refer to the text file. The file numbers entries as "Tzav VeZeruz N:M"; both are given.

## 1. What the range is: structure

- **1 (lines 1-2).** Why keep a notebook: life is too short to live the corrected life; writing yourself down lets your soul's rises and falls live on in the readers who "swallow" it.
- **2 (line 3).** Each year, picture the person you mean to be next year, "the imagined Reuven," and measure yourself against him.
- **3 (line 4).** When your heart feels broken, do not trust it yet; it may only be a lack of some desire. Hurry in while the heart is open.
- **4 (lines 5-19).** The long central entry: holding bad thoughts back from action, or even out of mind and heart, is not enough; they must be uprooted from the soul itself. Illustrated by the stomach ulcer that a quack stops from vomiting and a real doctor heals; by old men whose suppressed filth came back in old age; by the peasant tormented by thoughts to cross himself. The remedy is speech: pour out your heart to God alone, and he writes out a sample prayer.
- **5-6 (lines 20-21).** A sin "only against yourself" is still a sin. Regret that is not turned into actual change becomes a pleasure in having got rid of the regret.
- **7 (line 22).** The current of the generation's opinions sweeps everyone; the only way not to be carried is to keep moving upstream.
- **8 (lines 23-25).** Do not excuse yourself by your evil inclination, and do not boast of it either as proof you are important; the test is whether you fight back.
- **9 (line 26).** The soul needs to be moved; if its holy life gives it no feeling it will find feeling elsewhere or fall ill.
- **10 (lines 27-32).** Free choice belongs only to a real individual. Bring out your true self, your own "form and likeness," and then you become someone who chooses and serves God.
- **11-12 (lines 33-34).** Love and fear that find no holy place go into unholy ones; make your place in the world felt.
- **13 (lines 35-36).** You do not need proofs of God from the world; your soul already sees Him and speaks to Him as "You."
- **14 (lines 37-41).** Measure your growth by your will, not your wish ("blessing"): real will works, steps, and grows as you grow.
- **15 (line 42).** If you have no rules of your own for inner work, you are either not fighting or not noticing.
- **16 (line 43).** You must hate your inclination, not only decline it.
- **17 (lines 44-47).** Fear at the Third Meal: why fear to appear stained before God after death, and not now?
- **18 (lines 48-50).** Go out alone, to the forest if you can, and sing to God with the sun and the birds; prayer free of requests.
- **19 (lines 51-56).** Turning forty: "What am I lacking? Simply to be a Jew." A portrait with no soul.
- **20 (lines 57-62).** An old chassid who fell at sixty; become a *gibor*, master of yourself, so that nothing in you moves except by your own command.
- **21-25 (lines 63-69).** Good will is no comfort to a drowning man; only one who can throw off his worries for an hour can leap above himself; longings that are not worked turn into despair; when nothing moves you, picture yourself as a tzaddik; the living dead who hold their own "I" cheap.
- **26 (lines 70-76).** The Third Meal of Shabbos: face to face with God, shame, collapse, then "there is none like our God" and joy; at Havdalah "I find myself another man."
- **27 (lines 77-85).** The somersault he resolved to do at a Torah dedication: how an act invented for God becomes holy, the doubt that he fooled himself, and his answer.
- **28 (lines 86-87).** How to tell whether you only contemplated the order of worlds in your mind and imagination, or your soul was revealed and you glimpsed God beyond it.
- **29 (lines 88-92).** The world as a secret of God; to look at it as a secret is to see; to analyse it makes everything vanish.
- **30 (lines 93-114).** "A collection of some emotions from the festivals, especially the times of dancing, 5693 [1932]": a prayer-protest over Jewish tears, the dancing on Simchat Torah as the soul revealed, the dialogue between the man and his soul.
- **31 (lines 115-129).** "I will praise God with my life": a hymn of giving the whole self, body to *yechidah*, to song.
- **32-33 (lines 130-137).** One day of real service is harder than years of quantity; every person is mad in the area of his "fallen trait," and the remedy is constant watching of thoughts.
- **34 (lines 138-156).** "You are all my wealth": against every false "wealth" of imagination, the hidden search that every creature has for its Master.
- **35 (lines 157-205).** The son's illness in 1935-36 written as it happened (the dark hospital room, a prayer that will not come, the vision of the son at the Third Meal, the recovery) and the postscript on his death in 1939.

## 2. The method: concrete instructions for inner practice

The notebook gives fewer set exercises than his manuals, but every one it gives is concrete.

**Write yourself down.** Line 2: *טוב לו לאדם לרשום את כל מחשבותיו* ("it is good for a person to record all his thoughts"), not as literature but *רק לחרות את עצמו עלי נייר* ("only to engrave himself on paper"), keeping all the "wheelings of the soul, its falls and its rises."

**The yearly self-portrait.** Line 3: *בכל שנה עשה לך מטרה, צייר בעצמך* ("each year make yourself a goal, picture in yourself") what Reuven will be next year: his understanding, service, traits, "all his content." Then *והראובן הדמיוני יהי׳ לך למדה למדוד את עצמך בו* ("the imagined Reuven will be your measure"). If a year passes and you have not reached his ankles, count that year as not lived. This is picturing as a method, aimed at the self.

**Pouring out the heart (the main exercise here).** Line 15. The steps, in his order: 1. Place: *הסתר את עצמך בחדר מיוחד אם אפשר לך, ואם לאו הסב פניך אל הקיר* ("hide yourself in a separate room if you can; if not, turn your face to the wall"). 2. Picture: *וצייר במחשבתך שאתה עומד לפני כסא כבודו* ("picture in your mind that you stand before His Throne of Glory"). 3. Speak: *ושופך לפניו את לבך בשיח ותפילה כפי שעולה על לבך בכל לשון שאתה שומע* ("pour out your heart before Him in conversation and prayer as it rises in your heart, in any language you understand"). 4. Name the thing: his sample prayer (lines 17-19) leaves a blank to be filled, "and now there arose in me such-and-such a thought or desire [it is good to specify it]," and asks God to uproot it from the soul itself, not only from mind and heart. Why speech works (line 15): *הדבור בעצמו גדול כחו להוציא את החרצובות מקרב הנפש* ("speech itself has great power to draw the knots out from within the soul"), like telling a friend your worry and feeling "a stone rolled off your heart." The sample is only a model: *ולא לקבוע לך סדר תפילה, באתי* ("I did not come to fix a set prayer for you," line 16).

**Find your own counsel.** Line 14: the first of all remedies *שבעצמך תדרוש ותחפוש לטכס לך עצה* ("that you yourself seek and search out a plan for yourself"), like a soldier who does not rely only on orders. Line 42 adds that anyone really at war will find his own rules arising: "in this way and with this device I win, in that way I fail."

**Watching thoughts (hab'atah).** Lines 136-137. There is no cure that stops bad thoughts from arising; the remedy is *ריבוי ההבטה, להרבות הבט על כל מחשבה המתרחשת בקרבנו* ("much looking: to look often at every thought that happens within us"), especially in your "fallen trait." Nine times the look will be bent and you will think the thought clever; *בפעם העשירית נראה ישר כי שטות היא* ("the tenth time we will see straight that it is foolishness"). It needs two things together: *ושתיהן, ההבט״ה, וחיל״ל כבו״ד הדע״ת, נחיצות לנו* ("both are needed: the looking, and profaning the honour of one's own opinion"). As long as you revere your own thought, looking at it achieves nothing.

**Solitude in nature and song.** Lines 48-49. For someone who has never tasted prayer free of requests: *עזוב על שעה על שתים את העולם ושאונה* ("leave the world and its noise for an hour, for two"), *והתבודד יחידי. ואם אפשר לך לך היערה* ("be alone. If you can, go to the forest"), *והוי רואה את עצמך ליצור תם פשוט בין יצורי ד׳* ("see yourself as a simple, whole creature among God's creatures"), and with sun, moon, birds and trees sing before God. Concretely: *החל נא למשל, להכריז להם ולכל העולם, את אדון עולם* ("begin, for example, to proclaim to them and to the whole world 'Adon Olam'"), or "Yedid Nefesh." Duration: an hour or two. Words: a known hymn, sung aloud.

**Throw off worry for an hour.** Line 64: *רק מי שיכול להפקיר ולהשליך את דאגותיו על שעה עכ״פ* ("only one who can abandon and cast off his worries for at least an hour") can leap in spirit to a higher state; even if he falls back, returning is easier. This is the notebook's nearest thing to quieting: a deliberate, timed setting-aside.

**When nothing works, picture yourself a tzaddik.** Line 66, the plainest imagination exercise in the book: *צייר בעצמך שאתה צדיק, וראה בדמיונך את גודל נשמתך בשורשה* ("picture yourself a tzaddik, and see in your imagination the greatness of your soul in its root"), and its beauty when God and His holy ones come to walk with it in the Garden of Eden. Then hold it: *השקע והתמד במחשבה ודמיון זה נגד עיניך* ("sink in and persist in this thought and image before your eyes"). The result is promised: care not to soil it, *ותשוקה עד כלות הנפש לזכות לזה תתעורר בך* ("and a longing to the point of the soul's expiry will awaken in you").

**Measure by will, not wish.** Line 37: *הסתכל ברצונך, אבל רק ברצונך ולא בברכתך* ("look at your will, but only your will, not your blessing"). Compare what you actually worked toward a year or two ago with now. Real will *לא מדלג הוא רק צועד* ("does not leap, it steps," line 40).

**Make yourself the master.** Line 61: train so that nothing in you acts, speaks *or even thinks* on its own, *רק כל דבר בקרבו, מעצמו לא יתפעל* ("nothing within him should be moved of itself"), only at the command of his essential self.

**A self-invented holy act.** Lines 77-85: when longing outruns your service, find some concrete, costly act (his was a somersault before the Torah scroll) and dedicate it; he reports that once decided, merely thinking of it made his soul blaze.

**Tie the longing to work.** Line 65: *התשוקין והכסופין טובים רק אם עובדים בהם* ("longings are good only if one works with them").

## 3. The inner experience: what it is like inside

**The soul already sees God.** Line 35: when you say "You, God" in prayer or longing, it is *כיון שבאמת רואה היא אותו ית׳ לנגד עיני׳* ("because it truly sees Him before its eyes"); in pain "it is as if it holds the Throne of Glory." Line 36: *כי אם הי׳ לו לב אז את הכל מלבו ראה וידע* ("if he had a heart, he would see and know everything from his heart").

**Singing in the forest.** Line 49: *ותראה אם לא תתפרץ נפשך בתפארתה החוצה* ("see whether your soul will not burst outward in its splendour") toward its King, who has come near to listen, *ואש של מעלה תוקד בקרבך, אשר רק שטף של דמעות יצילוך וירגיעוך* ("and a fire from above will burn in you, from which only a flood of tears will save and calm you"). Line 48 describes the goal: that in speaking of God *דוחקת ויוצאת דרך לבך ומוחך גילוי אלקית* ("a revelation of Godliness presses out through your heart and mind") that you do not grasp.

**The Third Meal, in stages (lines 71-75).** This is the fullest stage-by-stage account in the notebook. - Exposure: *ועתה הנני נמצא לפניו יתברך פנים אל פנים* ("and now I find myself before Him face to face"), eyes from above looking through him "from side to side." - Wanting to hide: *רוצה אני להסתיר עצמי בין אנשים, תחת השלחן* ("I want to hide among people, under the table"). At "You are with me" his tongue sticks. - Knowing becomes real: *הן גם מאז זאת ידעתי, אבל רק ידעתי* ("I knew this before, but only knew it"); now His holiness "presses and tramples all of me," and even his best light turns to darkness. He is the worm who admired its tunnel until it saw a palace. - Out of nothing, a cry: the broken, foolish son who still calls "my father"; *לבי אחריך יהמה, ושמך נעים לי להזכיר* ("my heart moans after You, and Your name is sweet for me to mention"). - Turning: *לפעמים שברון לב לבד, ולפעמים התחזקות מתוך שבירת הלב* ("sometimes only brokenness, sometimes strength out of the brokenness"); at "Ein K'Elokeinu" he always leaps into joy and proclaims it to the grains of sand. - After: *וכשמביאים את הנרות מוצא אני את עצמי לאיש אחר* ("when the candles are brought [for Havdalah] I find myself another man"); brokenness and joy were *טהרה אחת וכביסה אחת היתה* ("one purification and one washing").

**The soul revealed versus mere contemplation (lines 86-87).** The inner sign: *תרגיש כאילו אתה נקרע כרגע מן עמידתך* ("you feel as if you are torn in an instant from where you stand") and raised to look upward, so nullified *עד שאף לירא יראת, ולאהב פחדת* ("that you were afraid even to fear, and afraid to love"). *זיעה תכסך ותמס* ("sweat covers you and you melt"). You are ashamed even to pray, and pray only because He wills it. Then *תדע שמגבול לבושיך והלאה עם נשמתך הבטת* ("know that you looked with your soul beyond the boundary of your garments").

**Seeing the world as a secret (line 89).** Looking at the world as God's secret, *לא את ההסתר ואת העדרו בלבד ראיתי, רק גם את האור וגם את ההסתר* ("I saw not only the hiddenness and absence, but the light and the hiddenness together"); his heart and soul filled with "grasp and sight." But *ואם רציתי, לחקור ולהבין, אז הכל חלף* ("if I wanted to investigate and understand, then everything passed"). The world is *חלון היכל המלך* ("the window of the King's palace," line 90). The silent creatures teach fear of God: *בשתיקתכם חרדת אלקים עלינו תפילו* ("in your silence you cast the trembling of God upon us," line 91).

**The dance (lines 100-105).** *בשמחתה לד׳ עצם נשמת ישראל מתגלה* ("in its joy before God the essence of the Jewish soul is revealed," line 100). Inside the dance: *הכל בטל עתה, אין עולם ולא דאגות* ("all is nullified now, no world and no worries," line 103). Then the soul itself objects, "what right have you to rejoice with God?", and the man answers that he is ready at every moment to give his life. The soul *מתמלאה הנפש במה שאינה משגת* ("is filled with what it does not grasp," line 105), and he suggests this joy is union *בשעה שהחומה של ברזל נופלת* ("when the iron wall falls," line 105). At the Yom Kippur tears: *הכל עתה מלא אור וקדושה ואת כל עצמי באור הזה הנני משליך* ("everything is now full of light and holiness, and I throw all of myself into this light," line 113).

**The imagined act that turned to an altar (line 81).** Once he resolved on the somersault, his soul ran ahead of his body's thought: *לא התהפכות פשוטה ראית כבר בדמיוני רק כעין מזבח הי׳ מבהיק לעיני* ("it was no simple somersault I saw in my imagination; it gleamed before my eyes like an altar"); blood seething, tears flowing. What he hoped for inside (line 84): *בעת אשר האנכיות שלי יתמוטט ועצמותי יתפורר* ("when my I-ness collapses and my self crumbles") and is replaced by a spark of God's radiance.

**The whole person as a single voice (lines 119-123).** He prays *עורי יחידתי במרום* ("awake, my yechidah, on high," line 119), and wants to be driven not by what he understands but *רק מגדלותך ית׳ אשר רוחי עד יחידתי דוחות אותי להכריז* ("only by Your greatness, which my spirit up to my yechidah drives me to proclaim," line 122). He pictures the whole world tearing off its garments and crying "the Lord is God" until *כל עצם יתבטל וכל יש לא יהי׳ עוד* ("every being is nullified and no 'something' remains," line 123).

**Presence and hiddenness as life and death (line 146).** *בהופעתה חיי מופיעים, ובהסתרתה גם חיי מוסתרים* ("when [the Shechinah] appears my life appears, and when it hides my life is hidden too"). God's embraces still come to us, but in hiding, *עד שגם אנו רק בבואותיו מרגישים* ("so that even we feel only His reflections," line 156).

**At the hospital bed (lines 174-194): the inner life under catastrophe.** He insists this is not rhetoric: *רק את הרגשיותי הבולטות אני מתאר ולא לסלסל במליצות* ("I describe only my prominent feelings, not to embellish," line 160). - Numbness: *אין כל רגש ולא כל מחשבה מתרחשים, רק אופל ועצב* ("no feeling, no thought stirs, only darkness and sadness," line 174). - Everything becomes one longing: *אין לי עולם, אף את עצמי לא אדע ולא ארגיש* ("I have no world; I do not even know or feel myself," line 175). - The blocked prayer: *לבי כמו נסתם מלהתפלל תפילה חזקה* ("my heart is as if sealed from praying a strong prayer," line 179). - Beyond human bounds: *את אשר אני עוד מכיר, לא כהכרת האיש הכרתי* ("what I still recognise, I do not recognise as a man recognises," line 181). - An image given, not made: suddenly he sees his son sitting beside him at the Third Meal, well; *לא מעצמי דמיתי* ("I did not imagine this of myself," line 188). And even within the vision he refuses to hold his son's hand before thanking God (line 189: *בדמיוני רוצה אני את יד בני להחזיק ולנשק*, "in my imagination I want to hold and kiss my son's hand"). - Beyond words: *נשמת שירה לאדון הנשמות שרתי* ("I sang a soul of song to the Master of souls," line 191) that neither his speech nor thought can now contain. - The breakthrough: *אף הוילון אשר בין מפתחי לבבי לבין שערי שמיך קרעת* ("You tore even the curtain between the openings of my heart and the gates of Your heaven," line 194).

## 4. The self and God

This notebook supports the owner's thesis strongly, and adds its own shape: the true self must first be *brought out* and *separated from the species*, and then that self turns out to be the one who serves, sees and longs for God.

- **The true self must be brought out.** Line 28: *והסתכל נא בנפשך האם הוצאת אתה את אמיתית עצמך* ("look into your soul: have you brought out your true self?"), or are you only "part of the species, like the plant and the animal." Line 30: it is not enough to think for yourself; *אך שגם עצמית אישיות ודיוקן מיוחד לעצמו יגלה מקרבו* ("he must also reveal from within himself an essential individuality and a likeness unique to himself"), as the Rambam's and Ramban's ways are each recognisable. Not only cedars: *גם כלנו אנחנו אזובי קיר* ("all of us too, hyssops on the wall," line 31).
- **And that self is the servant of God.** Line 32, the entry's conclusion: *העלה עצמך מן העולם, וגלה את אישיותך ממין האנושי, ותהי׳ לאיש בוחר ועובד את ד׳* ("raise yourself from the world, reveal your individuality out of the human species, and become a man who chooses and serves God"). Finding the self is not a detour from God; it is the condition of freely serving Him.
- **Sinning against the self is sinning.** Line 20: a sin *רק נגד עצמך צורתך ואנכיות של קדושה אשר הולדת בך* ("only against yourself, your form and the holy I-ness you have given birth to in yourself") is still to be grieved as a grave sin. The self here is something holy one has made.
- **Looking inward is where God is seen.** Line 35-36: no proofs from outside are needed because the soul already sees Him; one with a heart sees everything "from his heart."
- **Everyone is searching, and mistakes the object.** Line 149, the closest statement of the thesis: every creature has *תשוקה פנימית וחפישה בלתי נודעת זו לבקש את אדונכם תכלית מציאותכם* ("this inner longing and unknown search to seek your Master, the purpose of your existence"). Those who turned their hearts away invent a "wealth" to quiet the drive, like orphans hallucinating who *ואת העץ יחבקו לאמר, אבי אתה* ("embrace the tree and say, you are my father"). What people chase is really Him, unrecognised.
- **Finding Him within.** Line 143: *אם את הוד אבי ומלכי תשוקת חיי, בקרבי מצאתי* ("if I have found within myself the splendour of my Father and King, my life's longing") there is nothing more to ask. Line 152: the secret whispered in you from God's thoughts comes from none other than *השוכן עליך* ("He who dwells upon you"); even your trembling is the trembling of the bearers of His throne.
- **But the self does not know itself; God does.** Line 145: *רואה אתה את סתרי נפשי, אשר לא אראם אני* ("You see the hidden places of my soul that I do not see"), and he wishes *מי יתיר לי ואשמע את עצמי* ("who would permit me to hear myself"). The notebook is honest that self-knowledge is not guaranteed; he asks God for a sign whether he is coming closer.
- **Missing self and missing soul are the same lack.** Turning forty (line 56): *פשוט להיות יהודי, חסר לי* ("simply to be a Jew, that is what I lack"); *דומה אני בעיני כצורת אדם מצוירת* ("I look to myself like a painted human figure") with everything but the soul, and *פשוט רוצה אני מעתה להתגייר* ("I simply want from now on to convert").
- **The counter-current: the self must also give way.** He does not flatten the paradox. The hoped-for peak is when *האנכיות שלי יתמוטט* (line 84), "my I-ness collapses," and in the hospital *אין לי עולם, אף את עצמי לא אדע* (line 175). And the danger he sees in his own day (line 69) is not heresy but people for whom *האנכי שלהם הוזל והופקר* ("their 'I' has become cheap and abandoned"). So the order is: a cheapened self cannot find God; a revealed, individual, holy self can; and at the moment of meeting, that self is nullified into Him.

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

- **Brokenness that is only a missing desire.** Line 4: when you feel your heart softened, *עוד אל תבטח בך* ("do not trust yourself yet"); *יש שכל זה רק מן איזה תאוה אשר נחסרה לך* ("sometimes all this is only because some desire is lacking to you"), honour or another, and when you get it back the stone returns. Test: act on it now, while the heart is open.
- **Pushing the evil out of mind and heart into the soul.** Line 10: those old men *רק מן מוחו ולבו גרשם* ("only drove it from mind and heart"); it stayed in the soul and erupted in old age. His own prayer (line 17): *אז רק מן מוחי ולבי אני מגרשם, ובנפשי הם נשארים* ("I drive them only from my mind and heart, and in my soul they remain"). The real goal (line 12): uproot *מן נפשו לא מן מוחו ולבו בלבד* ("from his soul, not only from mind and heart"). Line 15 adds that suppressed worry and anger are the root of sudden causeless melancholy.
- **The pleasure of relief mistaken for purification.** Line 21: *כי טבע האיש שכל הרגשה אי אפשר לה שתאריך בקרבו* ("no feeling can last long in a person"); regret not turned into deed fades and is replaced by pleasure at being rid of it, and the fool *עוד יטעה לומר שתענוגו זה עונג נפשו* ("mistakes this pleasure for his soul's delight") in its repentance. *רשעים מלאים חרטות* ("the wicked are full of regrets").
- **Mistaking inner turmoil for spiritual greatness.** Line 24: do not boast that the raging of your desires proves God's angel is fighting you; *מי יערב לך ששליח ד׳ הוא אשר מתגרה עתה בך* ("who guarantees it is God's messenger provoking you") and not your own filth boiling?
- **Feeling is a need, and will be met somewhere.** Line 26: *נפש האדם אוהבת להתרגש* ("the human soul loves to be moved"), even to tears; only *רק האיש שמשלם חקה זה בעבודה ובהתרגשות התורה והתפלה שומר נפשו* ("only one who pays this due through emotion in Torah and prayer guards his soul"). Unfelt service drives the soul to sinful excitement or to illness. This is a caution *against* suppressing feeling, not only against false feeling.
- **Wish versus will.** Lines 38-40: "I want to be a tzaddik" without work is a blessing you give yourself, not will. Fake will can be enormous and leaps; real will is modest and steps.
- **Longing without work breeds despair.** Line 65: otherwise *נולד בקרב האיש, גם בלא ידיעתו, מין יאוש* ("a kind of despair is born in a person, even without his knowing").
- **Comforting oneself with good intentions.** Line 63: the drowning skull does not save itself by wanting to.
- **Knowing without being.** Line 61: not enough to behave as a chassid; one must *become* one.
- **Your own opinions are the least trustworthy.** Lines 133-137: in his "fallen trait" every clever man thinks like a madman and cannot see it; hence the constant looking and profaning the honour of one's own mind.
- **Has the contemplation reached the soul, or only the garments?** Lines 86-87 give the test (see section 3). If the signs are absent, *רק בתוך לבושיך ואפשר רק במחשבתך התבוננת. אבל גם מזה אל תתרחק וגם היא טובה* ("you contemplated only within your garments, perhaps only in your thought. But do not distance yourself from that either; it too is good").
- **Doubt as the counterfeit of humility.** This is the notebook's most distinctive point. After the somersault he fears *ואפשר רמיתי עצמי* ("perhaps I deceived myself," line 83), since the vision he hoped for did not come. He answers: *גם זה פתוי היצר הרע הוא להסתפק ולכפור במה שרואים ומרגישים בקודש* ("this too is a seduction of the evil inclination: to doubt and deny what one sees and feels in holiness," line 84), and concludes *אבל את אשר הרגשתי הרגשתי* ("but what I felt, I felt," line 85). He separates the two: the higher thing he hoped for he did not attain, and his hoping for it was some pride; the real feeling he had is not cancelled by that. Likewise in joy (line 144) he fears *אולי בשמחתי זו שמץ אנוכיות יתגנב* ("perhaps some trace of self steals into this joy of mine") and rejoices anyway.
- **Having to stir himself up.** Line 119: *ולמה זה יש זמנים שצריך אני עוד להפעיל ולהלהיב אותי* ("why are there times when I still need to work myself up and set myself aflame?"). He treats needing to force feeling as a sign of distance and is frightened by it, but he does not stop; he prays for the *yechidah* to wake. And line 124: if he forgets his intention, let the dedication itself not be broken.
- **Not everything that wanders in is a sign.** Line 179 is the honest counter-case: at his son's bed he wanted to pray until he felt answered, *ולא אוכל* ("and I could not"). Feeling cannot be commanded even by a master.

## 6. How it compares with the Chabad method of contemplation

**Where they agree.** - *Knowing is not yet knowing.* His Third Meal line *הן גם מאז זאת ידעתי, אבל רק ידעתי* (line 72) is exactly the Chabad gap between understanding and *daas* that reaches the heart. - *Mind and imagination alone are the lower level.* Lines 86-87 speak in Chabad terms: contemplating *ההשתלשלות* (the order of descent), causes and effects, "levels and screens," is valid but remains "within the garments." Chabad would agree that the aim is the Essence beyond the order of worlds. - *Bittul as the peak, and fear of self in the state.* His "afraid even to fear, afraid to love" (line 87) and his worry that "I-ness steals into the joy" (line 144) match the Chabad obstacle of being aware of yourself in the state. - *Feeling that is not brought down into action is dangerous.* Line 21 (regret without deed turns into pleasure) and line 65 (longing without work becomes despair) correspond to the Chabad fifth stage, drawing down: contemplation that stops at feeling has not finished. - *Test by change, not by experience.* His measure by will (lines 37-41) is close to the Rashab's test of whether the dominant trait has changed.

**Where he differs (kept as differences).** - *Picturing and imagination as the engine.* Chabad works by unfolding a concept in *binah* until it moves the heart; images serve as analogies. Here, when "you have tried everything," the prescribed move is to *picture* yourself a tzaddik and hold the image before your eyes (line 66); prayer begins by *picturing* yourself before the Throne (line 15); the year's work is measured against an *imagined* self (line 3). In Chabad's diagnostic table "grasp without heart" is met with duration on the same idea, not a picture. - *The self as the object.* Chabad's objects are mostly Godly truths (the light, unity, the order of descent), with the soul as one object among them. Here the recurring object is one's own soul in its root and one's own unique form (lines 28-32, 66). Revealing individuality is a precondition of choice and service, a theme Chabad hisbonenus does not stress. - *Speech rather than thought.* His chief purifying practice is spoken outpouring in your own words (line 15), because speech itself draws out the knots. Chabad contemplation is mainly silent and conceptual, before prayer. - *Song and nature.* Going to the forest and singing "Adon Olam" with the birds (lines 48-49), and dancing as the place where "the essence of the soul is revealed" (line 100), are central doors here. Chabad values the niggun but does not make singing in nature a method of contemplation. - *Feeling as a need, not a byproduct.* Line 26 says the soul must be moved and holy service must supply it, or the soul will seek it elsewhere. Chabad (at least the Mitteler Rebbe's warning) is more wary of arousal before the self has been worn down. His position sits closer to R. Aharon of Strashelye's defence of felt arousal, but his ground is psychological need, not *daas*. - *Analysis can kill the seeing.* Line 89: when he tried "to investigate and understand, everything passed." For Chabad, investigating and understanding in depth is the method itself. This is a real opposition, not a nuance: for this kind of seeing he says to hold the world as a secret and not take it apart. - *Watching thoughts rather than returning without comment.* For intrusive thoughts Chabad's table says return to the object without commentary. He prescribes the opposite for the "fallen trait": look at each thought many times until on the tenth you see its foolishness (lines 136-137). Both reject suppression (his line 12 insists on uprooting, not pushing down), but the move is different. - *Quieting.* The notebook has no formal hashkatah; its equivalent is throwing off your worries for an hour (line 64) and leaving the world's noise for solitude (line 48). This is an emptying for the sake of a leap, closer to *hisbodedus* than to Chabad's settling before study.

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before anything else, go somewhere alone, or just turn your face to the wall, and picture that you are standing before God." (line 15)
2. "Now tell Him, in your own words and your own language, exactly what is weighing on you. Name it. Speaking it out loosens what silence keeps buried." (lines 15, 17-19)
3. "For this hour, put your worries down on purpose. You can pick them up afterwards." (line 64)
4. "If nothing is moving in you, don't force it. Picture your soul as it is in its root, whole and beautiful, held by God, and stay with that picture." (line 66)
5. "Who is the person you are meant to be a year from now? Picture him, and let him be your measure, not the tzaddik you wish you could wake up as." (lines 3, 37-40)
6. "What you felt, you felt. Don't let doubt take it from you; just don't mistake it for more than it was." (lines 83-85)
7. "When a thought troubles you, look at it again, and again; you don't have to obey it, and you don't have to hide it." (lines 136-137, 12)
8. "Whatever you have been chasing, the hunger underneath it was for Him. Finding your own true self and finding Him are the same road." (lines 32, 149)
