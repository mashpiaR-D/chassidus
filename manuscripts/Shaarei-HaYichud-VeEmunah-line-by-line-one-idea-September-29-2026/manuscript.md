*Unity Index, the Strashelye part: finished 2026-09-29. R. Aharon HaLevi of Strashelye (1766-1828) was one of the Alter Rebbe's closest students. His "Gates of Unity and Faith" was published in 1820. Every line of the book (1,092 lines, each one a long paragraph) was read and asked one question: how does this line express the unity of God? The seven detailed reports are in `shaarei-hayichud/` (listed at the end). Every Hebrew line quoted below was checked word for word against the text with `find_he.py verify`, and checked again to sit on its own line.*

## The short answer

The Tanya comes back to the unity from many subjects. This book has no other subject. The author says he is explaining "root premises" he received from the Alter Rebbe, who set them out "a little" in the Tanya's Gate of Unity and Faith. So the whole book is one idea, taken through the whole of Kabbalah: **God is alone after creation just as He was before, and every level, screen and name in the Kabbalah is how He looks from our side. From His side, all of it is Him.** The point of knowing this is to become nothing before Him, and to let His oneness show in the most solid, separate-looking places.

The order is almost the reverse of the Tanya's. In the Tanya the practical face (Torah and mitzvah) and the soul lead. Here the Essence leads by far, and the soul is nearly absent. This book is about God and the worlds, not about the person's inner make-up. The high count for opposites joined is its own mark: "two opposites in one subject" is how the author explains almost everything.

## How the book builds it, gate by gate

**1. Why this has to be known (lines 1-74: the Preface).** Kabbalah exists, the author says, because the root of all service is to unite God and His presence in the world, and "that union comes only through knowing". He splits knowing in two. Knowing His unity in the worlds is a duty for every Jew, "each by his measure". The Names and mystical intentions are for the few. He sets three rules for any parable about God, and he defines knowledge as something felt "as real life" in the soul. Many lines here are biography and apology, which is where most of the book's untied lines are.

**2. Nothing may be said of Him (lines 75-96: the Introduction).** Nineteen short passages from the Zohar and the early Kabbalists all say one thing: no image, number or will may be said of the Infinite, not even "one", except as a denial. Then the author hands the reader the key: read all of it through the Alter Rebbe's Gate of Unity.

**3. The whole of the unity (lines 97-281: the First Gate).** He is simple, with no power besides Him. The ten sefirot (the Kabbalah's ten divine attributes) are vessels that hide Him, so that creatures can appear. Keter, the highest of them, is "the power that joins two opposites". The author separates "yachid" (alone, with no second) from "echad" (one made of parts). He gives a graded map of what may be asked. And he calls reading the tzimtzum (God's "contraction" to make room for the world) literally "a grave sin".

**4. Why ten, and whose side (lines 282-562: the Second Gate).** A long answer to "why exactly ten sefirot" turns into a rule for every word about God: "toward us" things divide, hide and contract; "toward Him" all is equal. Here come the book's boldest lines: "even the power of the yesh (the separate-seeming thing) is His Essence", and "as His power is without limit, so it is in limit". The gate ends on service: first unite the worlds through understanding, then be nullified above it.

**5. The faces of God (lines 563-778: the Third Gate).** The Zohar's pictures of the divine "faces" (skull, eyes, beard) are each read as a way His one power reaches creatures, and each ends: "from our side they differ". Old problems get answers from the unity: why God "hides His face", and how His knowing leaves us free. The central claim: the aim is that bitul (self-nullification) show "precisely in the yesh", in the solid thing itself.

**6. Lights and vessels (lines 779-931: the Fourth Gate).** Why are there vessels at all? To hide, so that limit can appear and then be joined back to its source. Even the power that contracts is His Essence, and the vessels outrank the light in their root. Four converts' confessions form a ladder of faith, up to Moshe's "there is no other". Each exile brings out one level of the unity. The deepest arousal comes not from understanding but from standing against the opposite.

**7. Small and grown (lines 932-1092: the Fifth Gate).** The Kabbalah's stages of "smallness" and "growth" become degrees of revealed unity. When the world runs by nature, the unity is hidden. When it grows, His intent shows through nature itself. Whether it grows depends on service. The book ends: without the unity that a person's service makes, "no worlds at all can be ascribed to Him".

## What it comes to, for a person

- **Nothing has changed since before the world.** He is alone now as He was then. What looks divided, hidden or far is how it looks from our side. That is not a flaw in the picture. It is the picture.
- **Know it until you feel it.** Understanding is required, and the author wants it detailed. But it only counts when it is felt as life. And even then, understanding leaves a "you" who understands. The last step is to let go of that too.
- **Before Him, everyone is equal.** How much people grasp differs. How nullified they can be does not: "the prince, the servant and the commoner" are nullified to the king alike.
- **The hard place is the right place.** The deepest bitul is not in a quiet, lit-up moment. It is in the solid, stubborn, separate-feeling world, and in holding firm against its pull. That is where "there is no other" is meant to show.

## Where the thread is thin (kept honest)

Some stretches carry little or no tie, and the index does not force one:

- **The frame of the book.** Fifteen of the 23 untied lines are in the Preface: the apology, praise and lament for his teachers, the history of the writings, advice to readers. The rest are a table of contents, cross-references, notes deferring details to the Etz Chaim, and the closing prayer.
- **Kabbalistic mechanics.** Much of the building-block count is maps of the divine "faces", joint by joint, the counting of worlds and screens, and the "anatomy" of creatures that opens the Second Gate. Where the text adds nothing about the unity, they carry no face.
- **The soul and the mitzvah.** "A part of God above" is almost never used (15 lines). Torah and mitzvot come in mostly as a formula, "Shema, prayer, Torah and mitzvot unite the worlds". The book never ties a particular mitzvah to the unity, as the Tanya does.
- **"A home below" in other words.** The book says "to reveal His perfection" or "His equality from the side of the worlds", and only rarely "a dwelling below". Counting these under that face is the index's naming; the idea is the text's own.
- **Repeats and slips.** Lines 340-342 repeat lines 334-337. A passage in line 874 comes back as lines 889-890. A few OCR slips are left as printed and never quoted.
- **Six lines are our reading only,** for example the last generations as the place the light shows most (line 17), and the souls in Adam's (lines 773-774).

## What it adds to the Tanya, and where it differs

The author builds on the Tanya and says so. He also goes further, and in places he goes another way. The range reports keep every such point. They are kept here without smoothing.

- **"Yachid" and "echad".** The index's first precision (from the later Rebbeim) is that God's unity is "yachid", alone, not "echad", one made of parts. This book draws the line exactly, in 1820. "Echad" is the union of two things, as in Atzilut, where light and vessel are joined: לכן בחינת יחוד זה אינו נקרא יחיד כי אם אחד שהוא לשון התאחדות שני מהותים (line 942). The higher face, where there is "no second at all", is "yachid" (lines 469, 722).
- **But even "yachid" is said only from our side.** Here the book differs from the index's own use. It gives "yachid" to Keter and the will, not to the Essence. Of the Essence itself: ואפילו בחינת יחיד אין לתארו, "not even 'yachid' can describe it" (line 447). It may be named only by denial (lines 225-227). So the precision holds, but for this author it describes a level below the Essence.
- **"From our side / from His side" as a rule for every word.** The Tanya gives the higher and lower unity as two teachings. This book turns them into a test for every Kabbalistic term: sefirot, vessels, Keter, the Names, even "unity", "knowledge" and "bitul" (line 324). His refrain: וכל ההבדלות הם בערכינו אבל לגביה יתברך אין צמצום מעלים לפניו, "all the differences are in our reckoning; toward Him, no contraction hides" (line 874). He guards the other side too. The levels are real, and "there is no aspect so small" that was not made by His Essence drawn into it on purpose (lines 329, 450). He holds both.
- **The tzimtzum is not literal.** The Tanya refutes the literal reading once. This book states it harder: "a grave sin" (line 251) and "no greater destruction" of faith (280.1). The contraction was "in the light, and not, God forbid, in His Essence" (line 787). He also places it: the main contraction is at the screen between Atzilut and the lower worlds (line 378). And he adds what the Tanya does not say: even the power that contracts is His Essence (line 782).
- **A graded rule for contemplation: the three Atiks.** The Zohar's three "ancient ones" become a map of what may be asked. Of the Essence, no question at all, and yet all contemplation is "to bind to Ein Sof" (line 233). Of the next level, asking without knowing: "the end of knowing is that we do not know" (line 247). Of the lowest, asking and knowing: here "Know today" is a real command (line 236). Speech is graded the same way: parable for Atzilut, borrowed names above it, silence higher still (lines 526-533).
- **Bitul above Da'at.** The Tanya's knowing is Da'at, binding the mind to the heart, with faith where grasp ends. This book makes that the first stage only. Understanding still leaves "someone who understands his own nullification" (line 491). The main bitul is להבטל למעלה מן הדעת ולהבין שהכל הוא לבדו יתברך, "to be nullified above Da'at, and understand that all is He alone" (line 558). And though grasp differs, bitul is equal for all (line 510).
- **Revelation "precisely in the yesh", through service.** The Tanya states the unity as a fact to contemplate. Here how much of it shows depends on how a person serves (lines 600, 751-756, 963). The aim is not that the solid thing vanish but that it be nullified while it stands (line 715), "on the earth below... in the very bottom of Asiyah" (line 887). The bitul that comes from inside the hiding shows more of the Essence than bitul through understanding (line 911).
- **Where it simply differs.** "Bears all opposites in one subject" is said of Keter, not the Essence; even "wonder" does not apply to Him (lines 407, 421). The soul-in-body analogy, which the Tanya refuses at the root, is used freely for Atzilut and refused only for the Essence (line 1076). The Tanya places great tzaddikim in Atzilut; this book says "no man ever grasped Atzilut", not even Moshe (line 875). The Tanya says the earth is not even called "od" ("something else"); this book says that in the soul-body kind of unity the created thing still counts as "od", and "ein od" is revealed only through turning the opposite (lines 1050-1051). The Tanya's arousal comes from knowledge brought to the heart; this book says understanding reaches only a radiance, and what reaches the Essence is bitul "not by grasp", made against the opposite (lines 897-898).

## Where it holds both sides

- **Knowing or believing?** "The whole purpose of knowing here is that we do not know" (line 50), and these teachings are "received mouth to mouth, without human reasoning" (line 562). Yet the first knowing is a duty for every Jew, today's souls need "long, detailed explanation" (line 22), and "the more you inquire, the more you understand His exaltation" (line 1079). The text leaves both standing.
- **Does service change anything above?** "If you are righteous, what do you give Him?" and "nothing can be added to Him" (lines 865, 1075). Yet the deeds below "give strength" to His drawing (line 864), "add power to the heavenly household" (line 1042), and in hidden places "My soul weeps" (lines 867-869). He calls it opposites held in one subject (line 866) and does not resolve it.
- **Which bitul is higher?** In service, the bitul above the worlds ranks highest (lines 753, 756). In purpose, the bitul within the yesh is the main intent and the delight (lines 715-717, 770). Both are kept.
- **Are the levels real?** He attacks those who think the division only seems so (line 329), and he says division is only "from the light, not the essence" (line 379). He holds both on purpose.
