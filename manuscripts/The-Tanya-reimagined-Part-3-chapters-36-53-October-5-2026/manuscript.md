This part is the book coming down from its summit. Chapter 33 asked you to see that He alone fills this very place. Now the book asks what that is *for*, and its answer is plain and startling: the purpose of everything is this lowest, darkest world, where light will shine out of darkness (36). From there it turns practical. Deeds lift the world, and charity lifts a whole life (37). Intention is the soul of the deed, and even a little of it carries a lifetime of plain service upward (38-40). Then comes a run of contemplations to use before prayer and study. Stand before the King who looks at you, and hand your soul back (41). Tie your mind to Him until everything you see is His clothing (42). See each thing being born from nothing (43). Wake the love you were born with (44). Feel mercy for the spark in exile (45). Let love wake love, like a face in water (46). Leave Egypt every morning in the Shema (47). Know that "surrounding" means *inside and not showing* (48), and that He hid His endless light out of love (49). Burn, and come back (50). Then the book explains how the Presence can "rest" in one place when He fills every place (51-52), and it ends on a candle: the wick burns and turns into fire (53). Each section retells the chapter plainly, names the unity in it, and gives a short sitting. Every source line was read in the Hebrew and checked against the source file (`Tanya — Hebrew text.txt`, line numbers as given). Where a chapter goes into lists or Kabbalah, the retelling compresses it and says so.

## Chapter 36 · The purpose is down here

The sages said something simple and strange. G-d made this world because He wanted a home in the lowest place.

But wait. For Him there is no high and low. He fills every world the same. Before the world was made, He alone was, filling the very space where the world now stands. It is still that way for Him now. What changed is only on our side. We get His light through many coverings, layer on layer, that hide it. "No one can see Me and live," He said. The sages add that even the angels can't.

That is what the whole chain of worlds is: coverings on coverings, each world one step further down, until you reach this physical world. Nothing is lower. Here the hiding is "doubled and redoubled darkness." It is full of things that seem to say, "I am, and there is nothing besides me."

So why all the steps down? Not for the high worlds. For them each step is a loss, a going away from His face. The purpose is this lowest world. It rose in His will that He would have joy when the dark side is pushed back and darkness itself turns into light. Then His light shines right in the place of the dark, stronger than it shines above. Up there it comes wrapped, so the worlds won't melt away. Light that comes out of darkness has something extra.

For that day He gave the Torah, which is called "strength". The sages say He gives the righteous strength to take their reward, so they won't dissolve when His light shows with no covering at all. "Your eyes will see your Teacher." "The Lord will be your light forever." The days of Mashiach, and above all the time when the dead live again, are why this world was made in the first place.

We already had a taste. At Sinai: "You were shown, to know, that the Lord is G-d; there is nothing else besides Him." Shown, really, with the eyes. "All the people saw the voices." They looked east and heard the word come from the east, and so from every side, and from above and below. There was no hiding then. At each word their souls flew out, and He brought them back with the dew that will one day raise the dead. Then came the sin, and people and world grew coarse again, until the end of days. Then the world will be made fine enough to hold the light. The light that shines on Israel will light up the darkness of the nations too: "Nations shall walk by your light." "All flesh shall see together."

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

He is the same here as before the world was. The hiding is only on our side. So the darkest, plainest place is not far from Him. It is where He wanted to live.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Think of the part of your life you'd call least holy: the commute, the bills, the argument at dinner. Let it be here. Don't push it away. - **The line, softly:** "The purpose is this lowest world." - **Think it through.** He fills this room as He filled it before anything was. What's different is only the coverings. Name a few: the walls, the noise, your own tiredness. Each is a layer He put on. Under all of them is the purpose: that light show here, in this. - **Where the picture breaks.** A "home" sounds like a place someone moves into from outside. He was never outside. The home is made when the hiding stops hiding Him. - **From inside.** The low parts of the day start to have weight. What you've been apologizing for is the job itself. - **The edge.** "You were shown": to see with open eyes that there is nothing else besides Him. You can't make that happen. Hold it as a promise. - **Into the day.** Do one good thing today in a place you think of as "not spiritual". This is why the world was made.

## Chapter 37 · What the hand lifts

That finished world, when His light is seen here with the eyes, depends on what we do now, all through the exile. Why? Because the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah itself. When you do one, you draw the Infinite's light down into a physical thing: the parchment of tefillin or a mezuzah, an etrog, a coin given to charity. Before, these things lived in a middle realm the Tanya calls *nogah*, a husk where light and dark are mixed. Now G-d's will is in them with no hiding, and their life rises and is taken into His light. (It has to be something allowed and honest. A stolen coin can't be lifted this way; the chapter is exact about that.)

Your own strength rises too. The force of the body's soul in your hand goes into the deed and goes up with it. It's the same with Torah, the Shema and prayer. Thinking the words isn't enough; you have to say them, and moving your lips counts as an act. The soul can't speak without the body, so the more strongly you speak, the more of your body's life goes into holy words. "All my bones shall say." And that life comes from your blood, which came from the bread and water you took in. So the food, too, turns from neutral to holy.

The chapter then lays out the plan in detail, and I compress it. The "do not" commandments keep the body's soul from feeding on what is wholly impure, which can't be lifted, only removed. The "do" commandments draw His light into every limb. Each soul has its own share of the world to lift: the food, the drink, the house and tools it uses in serving G-d. When all souls together have done this, the whole world's mixed life goes up, the impure falls away by itself, and "the glory of the Lord will fill all the earth."

Why did the soul come down at all? Not for itself. Even a perfect tzaddik down here can't reach the closeness he had before he was born, "not even a little of it." The soul came only to fix the body, the body's soul, and its share of the world. That is like the exile of the Presence itself: it goes down to gather sparks.

This is why charity is weighed against all the mitzvot. In most mitzvot only one power of your life is in the act. But you earned that money with your whole strength, hours of work. When you give it, your whole life goes up to G-d. And even if you didn't need it, you could have bought your life with it. So you are giving your life. That is why charity brings the redemption close.

Torah study is weighed against everything for another reason. It is thought and speech, the inner clothes of the soul, and when you study with real understanding, even the mind of the body's soul becomes holy. The feelings of an in-between person can't be changed that way; the bad is stronger there. And the mitzvot are the King's limbs, while Torah is His mind. Still, "the deed is the main thing." You stop learning for a mitzvah no one else can do, because "this is the whole of man": a home down here.

Last picture. Study is called "calling." When you learn, you call the Holy One to come to you, the way a person calls a friend, the way a small child calls his father to come and be with him, and not leave him alone. "The Lord is near to all who call Him in truth," and truth is Torah. Not only crying "Father! Father!" with nothing in your hands. From this a great awe can come while you learn.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

Nothing is too physical to hold His will. The coin, the bread, the tired hour of work all go up when they are used for Him, because He was hidden in them all along.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Think of the money you have with you, or in your account. Let it be what it is: hours of your life. - **The line, softly:** "When I give it to charity, my whole life goes up to G-d." - **Think it through.** The lunch that became blood, the blood that became strength, the strength that did the work, the work that became this money. Now picture giving some of it. All of that goes with it. - **Where the picture breaks.** "Goes up" sounds like moving from here to there. Nothing moves. The coin stays a coin. What changes is that His will is in it now with no hiding. - **From inside.** The day's tiredness has somewhere to go. Small acts start to feel heavy, in a good way. - **The edge.** The Infinite in a coin. The mind can't hold that. Let the hand hold it. - **Into the day.** Give some charity today and think: my hours go up with this. Or learn a few lines and call Him, like a child calling his father.

## Chapter 38 · Words with a soul in them

Here is a law that sounds backwards. If you say the Shema only in your heart, with all your intention, you haven't done it. Say it again, out loud. But if you said it out loud with no intention, you have done it (except for the first verse of the Shema and the first blessing of the Amidah). Why? The soul doesn't need fixing. It came down to fix the body and the body's soul, and it reaches them through the mouth and the hands.

Still, the sages said: prayer or a blessing without intention "is like a body without a soul." Here is the picture. Everything in this world lives from Him. Even stones and dust have His light and life in them, or they would go back to nothing. Yet the life in a body and the life in a soul can't be compared. The light is the same one, hidden in the same way, in the same clothing. What differs is how much shows. In a stone, almost nothing. In a plant, more. In an animal, more. In a speaking person, the most. Four levels, the chapter says, matching the four letters of His name.

So with a mitzvah. The deed itself, and words said without intention, are the body. The intention, to cling to Him by doing what He wants, is the soul. That doesn't mean thought is higher than the deed in itself; the book will say it isn't. It means He wants this too, and the light in the intention is as much greater than the light in the bare deed as a soul is greater than a body. One simple will is in both, "with no change at all." Only the showing is different.

Intention has two levels too, like an animal and a person. Someone who can think deeply about G-d's greatness, and from that give birth to awe in his mind and love in his heart, until his soul thirsts to cling to Him, and learns and prays with that in mind: his intention is like the soul of a speaking person, who chooses with understanding.

Someone who can't do that, but remembers and wakes the love hidden in his heart, at least in his mind, until he truly agrees to give his life for G-d's oneness so as to cling to Him, with a quiet fear of ever turning against Him inside it; and then learns and prays knowing what the words mean: his intention is like the soul of an animal. It is natural, not from understanding. It is an inheritance from our fathers. It is real, and it is alive.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

One light is in the stone, the plant, you, and your prayer, only more or less hidden. One will is in the deed and in the intention. Nothing lives on its own.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Pick words you say every day: a blessing, a prayer, a thank-you to G-d. Notice how fast they usually go by. - **The line, softly:** "A prayer without intention is like a body without a soul." - **Think it through.** A stone lives from Him. A plant a little more openly. A dog more. You most. Now say your words once, the usual way. Then say them again, slowly, knowing what each word means, wanting to be close to Him through them. - **Where the picture breaks.** A body without a soul is dead. But the chapter says words without intention still count and still hold His will. The picture is sharp on purpose. - **From inside.** The words start to have an inside. - **The edge.** One simple will, "with no change," in the deed and in the heart. You can't see how. You can say the words. - **Into the day.** Say one blessing today out loud, knowing what it means. If you don't have one, thank G-d out loud in your own words.

## Chapter 39 · Where your service goes

Angels are called "beasts" in the prophets, because they have no choice. Their love and fear come to them by nature. So the souls of the righteous stand higher. The chapter explains this with a map of the worlds, and I compress it. There is a world where G-d's feelings, His love and His awe, shine: angels live there and stand in awe or love day and night. And there is a higher world where His wisdom and understanding shine, the source of those feelings. That is the home of souls who served Him with love and awe born of understanding. The heart's longing they built becomes their clothing there.

Other souls, who served with the love that was simply hidden in them, go up there only on Shabbat and at the new moon, to delight in Him. A created mind can only enjoy what it understands. But why do they stand higher than angels at all, with only natural love? Because they could have chosen the bad, and they chose the good. They pushed back the dark side in their own bodies. That is light out of darkness.

That's where souls *live*. Their Torah and service go higher still, into the ten sefirot, which are G-dliness itself, and through them into the Infinite. The souls stand near and enjoy the radiance, and the radiance is their own Torah. The reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah.

Above all these is a world above created understanding. The greatest tzaddikim serve there, above love and awe from understanding. They are a chariot: given over to Him completely, with all they have, like the fathers, all their days. Someone whose root is smaller reaches there only at certain moments, like the bowing in the Amidah, when you count as nothing before Him. The chapter adds: "we have no business with hidden things." What is ours is the revealed part.

Then the hard part. Service with no stir of love or awe at all, even in the mind, where the hidden love stays hidden as it was, stays below, in "the world of separation." "Without love and awe it does not fly up." That is true even with no bad motive, only habit, "a command of men learned by rote," the way you were raised. You can't do something for someone truly unless you love or fear them. So it is with G-d.

Study for your own honor, to be known as a scholar, is different. That motive wraps the Torah, and the Torah sits in exile inside it until you return to G-d. When you return, your Torah returns with you. So "from not for His sake comes for His sake," surely, sooner or later, "for no one is cast away forever."

But plain study, neither for Him nor against Him, doesn't need that. The moment you learn that thing once more for His sake, everything you learned plainly joins it and flies up. It's the same with prayer said without intention.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

His will was in the plain service all along, waiting. One moment of meaning is enough to lift years. Nothing given to Him is lost.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Think of years of prayer, study or kindness done on autopilot. Don't judge them. Let them be there. - **The line, softly:** "As soon as I learn it again for His sake, what I learned plainly joins it and flies up." - **Think it through.** You don't do things for someone unless you love them or stand in awe of them. Wake a little of each: He is the King; He is my life. Then say one sentence you know by heart, a verse or a blessing, for Him. - **Where the picture breaks.** "Flying up" is a picture of places. These aren't places. They are how openly His will shows in what you did. - **From inside.** A quiet relief. Nothing was wasted. - **The edge.** The chariot, where a person is simply His. The chapter itself stops there: "we have no business with hidden things." Leave it. - **Into the day.** Before you learn or pray today, say once: "For Your sake."

## Chapter 40 · Standing in the river

Until you learn it again for His sake, plain study doesn't reach the sefirot at all. It rises only to the halls where angels stand. Rabbi Chaim Vital says that Torah without intention creates angels in one world, and mitzvot without intention create angels in a lower one. But Torah learned for your own honor doesn't rise at all. It stays down here. "What does a man gain from all his toil under the sun?" Even toil in Torah, if it's for his own glory. So the sages said, "Happy is the one who comes here with his learning in his hand": it didn't stay behind.

But the Torah and G-d are one, and He fills every world the same. So why the difference? It's in the ones who receive. The higher worlds get more light, with fewer screens. This world is lowest both ways. The light in it is squeezed small to the very edge, which is why it is physical. And it comes through many screens, down into *nogah*, the mixed husk that gives life to everything allowed here, including the speaking life in a person. When that life speaks idle words, the husk hides His holiness. When it speaks holy words of Torah and prayer, even without intention, the husk doesn't hide anything; it even turns to good. Still, the light in those words is squeezed as small as it gets, because a voice is a physical thing.

With intention it's different. The intention is the root of the words, the reason you say them. So it carries them up to where it lives, to a higher world or a higher still, depending on whether your love and awe are natural or come from understanding. There His will, which is in those words, shines without end, as it can't shine here at all until the end of days, when "the glory of the Lord will be revealed."

That's why love and awe are called *wings*. The Tikkunim says: those who learn with love and awe are called "children"; without them, "chicks" that can't fly. A bird's wings are not the bird. Its life doesn't depend on them. The head and body are the bird, and the wings let them fly. So the Torah and mitzvot are the main thing, the union itself. Love and awe carry them up to where His will is seen. (The chapter maps the bird onto the letters of G-d's name and onto Scripture, Mishnah and Talmud; I leave that out.)

Why call them wings, if loving and fearing G-d are commandments too? Because the goal of love is serving from love. Love without service is "love in delights," a taste of the World to Come. "Today to do them; tomorrow to be rewarded."

And someone who hasn't reached that taste, whose soul still thirsts for G-d all day long, and who doesn't drink from the Torah in front of him: he is like someone standing in a river, crying "Water! Water to drink!" "Ho, everyone who is thirsty, come to the water."

The Torah and He are one. The thirst and the water are in the same place. The longing is already standing in the river.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Let the longing be there, the "I want to be closer" that doesn't know where to go. - **The line, softly:** "Like one standing in a river, crying, 'Water!'" - **Think it through.** The bird: head and body are the deed, the words. The wings are love and awe. Now look at what's within reach: a book, a verse you know. That is the river. - **Where the picture breaks.** River water stays outside you until you drink. This water is His will, and it becomes your own thought as you learn it. And the thirst isn't wrong. It's a wing. - **From inside.** The longing turns into drinking. Something settles. - **The edge.** The light in those words shines fully only "at the end of days." You won't see it now. Drink anyway. - **Into the day.** Next time you long for closeness, open a book and learn a few lines. If you don't know where to start, read one Psalm slowly.

## Chapter 41 · Before the King, then handing yourself back

Always remember where serving G-d begins. Awe is the root of stepping away from the bad; love is the root of doing good. But love alone isn't enough. First wake at least the awe hidden in every heart: not to rebel against the King of kings. Wake it into your heart, or at least into your mind.

How? Think about the greatness of the Infinite, His kingship over every world. He fills them all and surrounds them all. "Do I not fill heaven and earth?" He sets aside the high worlds and the low ones and makes His kingship one with His people, and with you in particular, because a person must say, "The world was made for me." And you take Him on as your King, to serve Him the way a servant does.

"And see: the Lord stands over him, the whole earth is full of His glory, and He looks at him," and searches his heart, whether he serves as he should. So serve Him with awe, like someone standing before a king. Go deep in this and stay in it as long as your mind and your time allow, before learning or a mitzvah, before putting on tallit and tefillin.

Think, too, how the light of the Infinite, His will, is dressed in these letters of Torah, in these tzitzit and tefillin. When you read them or put them on, you draw His light onto the part of G-d inside your body, to be taken into it. (The chapter then says what each section of the tefillin and the tallit draws into the mind and heart. In short: not to use your mind for anything but Him, and to set a King over yourself.)

If, after all that, no awe falls on your heart, it's still whole service. You accepted His kingship in your thought and will, and that acceptance is true beyond doubt: it is the nature of every soul not to rebel against Him. The Zohar says: like an ox that is first given a yoke so it can do good in the world, a person must first take on the yoke of heaven. Everyone needs to be both a servant and a child.

Even someone who feels no awe or shame at all, if he means to serve the King, serves fully. And in fact he does have awe. At that moment, at least, he has the awe he'd feel if an ordinary person were watching him, the kind that stops you from doing something shameful. Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai blessed his students: "May your fear of heaven be like your fear of people. When a person sins, he says, 'May no one see me.'" That's the lower awe. The higher is a kind of shame. But without any awe, love alone doesn't fly. A bird can't fly on one wing. Awe alone is one wing too. Be a child as well: wake the hidden love, and remember in your thought that you love the One G-d and want to cling to Him.

"Don't separate yourself from the community." So aim higher than yourself. Aim to join the source of your soul and every soul, the Presence, His breath dwelling in every world, which gives you this very power to speak these words and do this deed. That is "for the sake of the union of the Holy One and His Presence." To want that purely takes great love, like a child who works for his parents more than for himself. But train yourself in it anyway. Some small part of you truly wants it. And your own soul joining His light, that every soul wants with all its heart.

Torah, mitzvot and prayer are a real giving of yourself, like the soul leaving the body at the end of seventy years. It stops thinking of the body's needs, and its thought is joined to the letters of Torah and prayer, which are His word and thought. That is what souls do in Gan Eden; only there, they feel it.

That's why the morning begins: "My G-d, the soul You gave me is pure. You breathed it into me, and You will one day take it from me." Meaning: since You breathed it in and will take it, from now I hand it over and give it back to You, to join it to Your oneness. "To You, Lord, I lift my soul." Do it by binding your thought to His thought and your speech to His speech, above all when you speak to Him directly: "Blessed are You."

Begin the morning blessings with this. Begin your learning with it. If you learn for hours, renew it at least every hour, because every hour a new flow of life comes down, and the last hour's life goes back up, carrying the Torah and good deeds done below. And let the whole point of handing yourself over be only to give Him joy, like a king's joy when his only son comes home from captivity. This intention is completely true in every soul, at every moment. Still, set times to think about His greatness, so love and awe can grow from understanding too.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

He fills this place and looks at you. Your soul is His breath. Handing it back is only saying out loud what was always true: it was His.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** This is for the first minute of the morning, before the phone. Whatever you woke up carrying, let it be there. - **The line, softly:** "From now I hand it over and give it back to You." - **Think it through.** The King fills this room. He stands over you and looks at you. He chose you: "the world was made for me." Take Him as King. Then: You breathed this soul into me. You will take it. So now, I give it. - **Where the picture breaks.** Giving something back means it was yours for a while. It was always His. And a king who "stands over you" is outside you; He is also inside the very soul you hand Him. - **From inside.** First you stand up straight inside. Then a letting go, like handing something precious back to the one it belongs to. - **The edge.** The chapter compares this to the soul leaving the body: a small dying into Him. You can't do it fully. Point toward it and let it be enough. - **Into the day.** Say "My G-d, the soul You gave me is pure" as a handing back. Or in your own words: "You gave me this soul. Today I give it back to You."

## Chapter 42 · Tie your mind to it

The Torah says, "What does G-d ask of you, except to fear Him?" The Talmud asks, "Is fear such a small thing?" and answers, "Yes, for Moshe it's small." But the verse says "of *you*," of everyone. The answer: there is something of Moshe in every soul. He is the faithful shepherd who brings *da'at*, knowing G-d, to every soul, each by its root. And in every generation sparks of his soul live in the teachers of the time, "the eyes of the community," to teach people to know G-d, because the heart's service follows knowing.

What is *da'at*? Not only knowing about G-d's greatness from teachers and books. It's taking your mind deep into His greatness and fastening your thought on Him with all the strength of heart and mind, until your thought is tied to Him in a strong knot, as tied as it is to a physical thing you see with your eyes and think hard about. *Da'at* means connection, as in "Adam knew Eve."

Every soul has this power, from Moshe. But in a body it takes hard work, twice over. The work of the flesh: to humble the body so it doesn't darken the soul's light, through turning back to G-d from the depth of the heart. And the work of the soul: not to find it too heavy to think deeply about G-d's greatness for a long, unbroken hour.

That hour isn't the same for everyone. Some souls are clear by nature. As soon as they think about Him, awe comes, as the Code of Law says: when a person thinks that the great King, whose glory fills the earth, stands over him and sees what he does, awe comes at once. Other souls are lower by nature and root, and find G-dliness in thought only with difficulty, especially if they are stained by the wrongs of youth. Still, if you push, with great effort and depth, for a long while, the lower awe will surely come. "I worked and I found: believe it." "If you seek it like silver and search for it like hidden treasure, then you will understand the fear of the Lord." Dig, the way someone digs for treasure deep in the ground, for the awe of heaven hidden in the understanding of every heart, a place above time. Then bring it up into real thought and stay with it long enough for it to act: to step away from the bad and do good, in thought, speech and deed, before the One who sees and hears and understands everything you do. "An eye that sees, an ear that hears."

He has no body. Rather, everything is open before Him, far more than to eyes and ears. A picture: a person feels in his brain everything that happens in his body, heat or cold, even a burn on a toenail. So G-d knows everything that happens in all creatures, because they all flow from Him. By knowing Himself, He knows them. But the chapter says this picture is only "to soothe the ear." A human soul is hurt by the body's pain. He isn't changed at all by the world. Wise people have written long books to understand this. But the believers, children of believers, need no study to say: "You were the same before the world was created, and You are the same since."

Anyone at all who thinks about this for a long while every day, how the Holy One truly fills the upper and lower worlds, heaven and earth, and watches and knows his heart and every word and step, will have awe settle in his heart for the whole day. And whenever he recalls it, even lightly, he'll step away from the bad.

Remember this too. With a human king, the awe is of his inner life, not his body. When he sleeps, nobody is afraid of him. You can't see that inner life with your eyes, only with your mind, by seeing his body and his clothes. So fear G-d by seeing heaven and earth, with the Infinite's light inside them, giving them life. (A note in the text: the sun and stars bow each day toward the west, where the Presence is. Someone who has never seen the king, walking into his court and seeing great ministers bow to one man, feels awe.) Many layers of clothing make no difference. A king in one robe or ten is the same king.

The main thing is habit. Train your mind until it is fixed in you that everything your eyes see, heaven and earth and all that fills them, is the outer clothing of the King. Then you'll always remember their inside and their life. That's the word *emunah*, faith: it means training, like a craftsman who trains his hands.

What you see is clothing. The life in it is His. Faith is the trained eye that stops forgetting who is inside.

## Chapter 43 · Two awes, two loves

About the lower awe, the sages said, "Without awe there is no wisdom." The lower awe can be small or large. The large kind comes from thinking about how vast He is: from the earth to the sky is a journey of five hundred years, and the same between one heaven and the next, and worlds above worlds without end. Even so, it's called outer, lower awe, because it comes from the worlds, which are only His clothing. It's the gate and the doorway to doing what He asks.

The higher awe is a kind of shame, an inner awe, and it comes from the G-dliness *inside* the worlds. About it they said, "Without wisdom there is no awe." Wisdom, *chochmah*, is read as *ko'ach mah*, "the power of what-is-it." "Wisdom comes from nothing." "Who is wise? One who sees what is being born": he sees each thing, how it is born and comes to be from nothing into something, by the word of G-d and the breath of His mouth. So heaven and earth and everything in them are nullified in His word. They count as truly nothing, like the sun's light inside the sun itself. And don't leave yourself out: your body and your soul too are nullified in His word, like one word next to the soul that speaks it. "The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom." But you reach this only by keeping the Torah through the lower awe first.

Love has two levels too. "Great love" is love in delights. It is a flame that rises by itself. It comes from above as a gift to someone who is whole in awe. You can't reach it without awe first. It comes from a world where nothing is cut off or separate.

"Love of the world" comes from understanding. You think about how He fills and surrounds all worlds, and how all counts as nothing before Him. From that thinking, the love in your soul takes off its clothes. It stops dressing itself in any pleasure, physical or spiritual. It wants nothing in the world but Him, the source of every pleasure, which are all nothing next to Him, as nothing is next to eternal life. "Whom do I have in heaven? And besides You I want nothing on earth." Even someone whose love isn't dressed in any pleasure can set his soul on fire this way, a flame rising to heaven.

Sometimes love comes before awe. That's why a person who has done wrong can come back through love, born in his heart when he remembers G-d. The awe is inside it, small and hidden. But that is a special gift for the hour, like the story of Rabbi Elazar ben Durdaya. The steady road, the one in a person's own hands, is to start with the lower awe, stepping away from the bad and doing good, and let the Torah light the soul. Then the light of love will shine on it.

Each thing is being born from nothing now, by His word. Seen from inside, the world is like sunlight inside the sun: there, but not a separate thing.

## Chapter 44 · The love you were born with

Each of these loves has endless levels. "Her husband is known in the gates": the Zohar reads it, G-d is known to each person "according to what he measures in his heart." That's why love and awe are "hidden things, for the Lord," while Torah and mitzvot are "revealed, for us and our children, to do." In deeds we all have one Torah. In love and awe, each is different, by his own mind and heart.

But there is one love that is the same in every soul. It holds all the levels. It's an inheritance from our fathers. The Zohar says it on the verse, "My soul, I longed for You at night": loving G-d the way the soul and spirit love, the way they cling to the body and the body loves them. "Because You, G-d, are my soul and my true life, I long for You." Like someone weak and in pain who longs for his strength to come back. Like someone falling asleep who wants his soul to return when he wakes. So I long for the light of the Infinite, the true Life of all life, to draw it into me by learning when I wake at night.

There is a greater love still, hidden in every soul too: the love of a child who works for his father and mother, loving them more than his own self, "because we all have one Father." Who would dare to reach even a thousandth of the love of Moshe? Still, a tiny glimmer of it shines in every generation.

It's hidden deep. To bring it out, "it is not far... it is very near, in your mouth and in your heart." Get used to saying it with your voice, so that your mouth wakes your heart and mind. Think deeply about the Life of life, that He is truly our Father and the source of our life, and wake love for Him like a child's love for a parent. Do it again and again, and habit becomes nature.

And if it seems to you like only imagination, don't worry. It is true in itself, a hidden love. You bring it out so it will lead to deeds: learning and doing mitzvot to give Him joy, like a child who serves his father. "G-d joins a good thought to the deed": the love becomes wings. The joy you give Him is like a king's joy when his son comes home from prison, or like His home below. And if you can't bring the love into your heart at all, you can still learn and do mitzvot for His sake by picturing this love in your mind.

These inherited loves, while they are only in the mind and the hidden heart, are called "natural." When they come out openly in the heart, the Zohar calls them "the heart's desire," and they reach higher. That takes *da'at*, fastening your thought hard, from the depth of the heart, on how He is our life and our true Father. These two loves are part of "great love," higher even than love from understanding. And still, work with your mind to reach love from understanding too, to grow the fire into flames that "many waters can't put out." It is gold next to silver. "This is the whole of man": to know the glory of G-d, each as much as he can.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

He is my life. Longing for Him is longing for my own life to come back to me. The homesickness is itself a being at home.

## Chapter 45 · Mercy for the spark

There is another straight road: the way of Jacob, whose trait is mercy. First wake in your thought great mercy before G-d for the spark of G-dliness that gives your soul its life. Think where it came from: the Life of all life, who fills and surrounds every world, before whom everything counts as nothing. And where it came to: dressed in "the snake's skin," as far as can be from the light of the King's face. This world is the thickest of the husks.

Especially when you remember all your deeds, words and thoughts, since the day you were born, that were not good. "The King is held captive in the tresses": in the running thoughts of the mind. "Jacob is the rope of His inheritance": like someone tugging a rope, a pull below pulls above. This is the secret of the exile of the Presence. "Let him return to the Lord, and He will have mercy on him": wake great mercy for the name of G-d that lives with us, "who dwells with them in the middle of their impurity."

"Jacob kissed Rachel, lifted up his voice, and wept." Rachel is the community of Israel, the source of all souls. Jacob, the trait of mercy, wakes great mercy for her. "He lifted up his voice": upward, to the source of mercy, "the Father of mercies." "And wept": to draw great mercy down from there onto all souls and their source, to lift them from their exile and join them to the light of the Infinite.

The kiss is spirit clinging to spirit: your speech joined to His word, which is the law you learn, and your thought to His thought. The embrace is deeds, above all charity and kindness: "His right hand embraces me." Through this a person can come to great love, openly in the heart.

## Chapter 46 · The King came down Himself

There is a straight road for every soul, very, very near, to wake the love hidden in the heart until it burns like fire, and you want to give Him your body, soul and all you have, especially during the Shema and its blessings.

"As water shows a face to a face, so is the heart of a person to a person." The face you show the water, the water shows back. When you truly love someone, your love wakes love in them, especially when they see it.

That's the way of people even between equals. Now picture a great king who shows his great love to a common, lowly, despised man lying in a garbage heap. The king comes down from his place of honor with all his ministers, lifts the man out of the dirt, brings him into his palace, room within room, where no servant or minister goes, and is alone with him there, close and true, embracing and kissing, spirit to spirit. Surely love would wake double and more in that man's heart for the king. "Even if his heart is like a stone, it will melt and turn to water," and his soul pour out in longing for the king.

All this, and infinitely more, our G-d did for us. His greatness has no end. He fills and surrounds all worlds, halls and worlds without number, and in each, angels without end. "Is there a number to His troops?" And all of them count as nothing before Him, like one word next to the soul. They all ask, "Where is the place of His glory?" And they answer, "The whole earth is full of His glory": His people below. He set aside the high worlds and the low ones and chose His people, and took them out of Egypt, a place of filth and impurity. Not by an angel, not by a messenger. The Holy One, in His glory and Himself, came down there. "I have come down to rescue them." He did it to bring them close in a true union. The kiss is speaking His word. Spirit to spirit is understanding His Torah, knowing His will and wisdom, which are all one with Him. And the embrace is doing the mitzvot with your limbs.

That's why we say "who sanctified us with His commandments." It's like a man betrothing a woman, to be one with her: "and they shall be one flesh." So, and infinitely more, is the union of the soul that learns and does mitzvot with the light of the Infinite. That's why Solomon wrote the Song of Songs as a bride and groom. "Holy" means set apart: the part of Him that is above the worlds and can't be dressed in them. Joined to Him, the soul reaches that holiness. "You shall be holy to Me, for I am holy."

And so it is with every soul while learning or doing a mitzvah. That's why the sages said to stand up for anyone doing a mitzvah, even a simple person who knows nothing: because of G-d, who is dwelling in his soul at that moment. His soul doesn't feel it. The thickness of the body darkens its eyes. "I was a fool and did not know; I was like a beast with You; and I am always with You." Even if I'm like a beast with You, and I don't feel this union, no awe, no burning love, still I am always with You. The body doesn't block the soul's union with the Infinite. "Even darkness is not dark to You." That's why Shabbat and Passover are as serious for the simplest person as for a tzaddik. The holiness shines in his soul too. "One Torah for all of us." ## Chapter 47 · Leaving Egypt this morning

"In every generation, and every day, a person must see himself as if he left Egypt today." That is the soul leaving the prison of the body, "the snake's skin," to be taken into the oneness of the Infinite. It happens through all Torah and mitzvot, and above all in the Shema, when you take His kingship on yourself and say His oneness out loud: "The Lord is our G-d, the Lord is One."

"Our G-d" is like "the G-d of Abraham." Abraham was given over completely to His oneness. But Abraham earned it by his deeds, going up in holiness step after step: "Abram journeyed, going on and on."

For us it's an inheritance and a gift. He gave us His Torah and dressed His will and wisdom in it, and they are perfectly one with Him. It's as though He gave us Himself, so to speak. The Zohar reads "Take for Me an offering" as "take *Me*."

So we say "You gave us, Lord our G-d, with love," and "by the light of Your face You gave us." Nothing holds the soul back from clinging to His oneness except wanting. If a person doesn't want it at all, then no. But the moment he wants it, and takes G-d on himself, and says "the Lord is One," his soul is taken into His oneness by itself. "Spirit brings spirit, and draws spirit." That is leaving Egypt. That's why the paragraph about leaving Egypt was set inside the Shema, though it's a separate commandment. They are truly one thing.

## Chapter 48 · Around and inside

A thinking person who looks at the greatness of the Infinite sees that it is just as the name says: without end. The light and life that spread from Him, by His simple will, are one with Him and have no end. If the worlds had come down from that light step by step, as cause and effect, with no contractions, this world could never have been made the way it is, with limits: five hundred years' journey from earth to sky, and so on. Even the World to Come and the highest Gan Eden, where the greatest souls live, are limited. They grasp only so much of His light, and enjoy only so much. They couldn't take in infinite joy without melting back into their source.

How the contractions work in detail, the chapter says, isn't for here. In general they hide the light, so that only a tiny bit shows to the lower ones, just enough to make them limited. That bit counts as nothing next to the endless. There's no ratio at all. One is a millionth of a million. But next to something with no number, a million millions isn't even one part. It counts as nothing.

That tiny light is dressed in the worlds to give them life. The rest, the hidden, endless light, isn't dressed in them openly. It "surrounds" them from above. It's called "surrounding all worlds."

But "surrounding from above" isn't about place. Place doesn't apply to spirit. It's about showing. A flow that shows in the worlds, that they grasp, is called "dressed" in them. A flow that is hidden, that they don't grasp, is called "surrounding."

Here's the picture. This physical earth. "The whole earth is full of His glory," the light of the Infinite. "Do I not fill heaven and earth?" Yet only a little life shows in it: the life of stone and plant. All the rest of the Infinite's light is called "surrounding" it, though it is truly inside it, because it doesn't show there. And the hidden is higher than the revealed.

Closer to the mind: when you picture something you saw, the whole thing, its back, its inside, the inside of its inside, is pictured in your mind. So your mind "surrounds" it. But only in imagination. "My thoughts are not your thoughts." His knowing of each creature surrounds it from top to bottom, inside and the inside of the inside, all for real. The whole globe of the earth, its thickness and everything in it down to the bottom: His knowing is its life, and brings it from nothing. It could only exist as it is, limited, with only stone and plant life, through great contractions. And His knowing is one with Him: "He is the knowing, the knower and the known. By knowing Himself He knows all creatures, not like a person's knowing, which is outside him... and a person cannot grasp this fully." So this knowing, being endless, isn't called dressed in the limited earth. It's called surrounding, though it holds every bit of it and brings it into being from nothing.

"Surrounding" means inside and not showing. All of Him is in the ground under you. Only a little of it shows.

## Chapter 49 · He set His light aside for you

There are countless ways His light is hidden on the way down to this world. In general, though, there are three great contractions for three worlds. The highest world, Atzilut, is G-dliness itself. To make the next world, where souls and high angels grasp His wisdom, there was first a great contraction. The tiny light in that world is still endless compared to the one below it, so another contraction was needed, and another below that. (The chapter says the details are explained elsewhere.)

The purpose of all of them is to make the physical human body, to push back the dark side, and to bring out the extra light that comes from darkness, when a person lifts his soul and his body's life and all his powers to G-d alone.

"As water shows a face to a face." The Holy One, so to speak, set aside His great, endless light, hid it in three contractions, all out of love for lowly man, to lift him up to Him. "Love presses on the flesh." How much more, then, is it right for a person to let go of everything he has, from soul to body, to cling to Him with longing, and let nothing hold him back, inside or out: not body, not soul, not money, not even wife and children.

That explains the two blessings before the morning Shema, which seem unrelated to it. The heart of the Shema is "with all your heart": with both your drives, standing against whatever keeps you from loving G-d. "Your heart" means the family your heart is tied to by nature; "your soul" and "your might" mean your life and your livelihood. Let go of all of it for the love of G-d. (This is about what the heart holds onto, not about walking away from anyone. The chapter is reading the Shema's "with all your heart.")

How can a physical person reach this? So first comes the blessing "Who forms light." It tells at length how the angels on high are all nullified to His light, calling out in awe, "Holy," meaning He is set apart and not openly dressed in them, and "the whole earth is full of His glory." Then the second blessing: "With an eternal love You loved us." In Hebrew, *ahavat olam*, "a love of the world": He contracted His endless light into the limits called "world," out of love for His people, to bring them close. "Great and extra compassion," more than for all the hosts above. "You chose us from every people and tongue": the physical body, which looks like any body. "And brought us close to thank You and to declare You One."

When a person takes this into the depth of heart and mind, then by itself, face to face as in water, his soul catches fire and a generous spirit comes over him, to let go of everything and only cling to Him, spirit to spirit. How? "These words shall be on your heart, and you shall speak them." Mind joined to mind is Torah study, and the mouth is where the breath comes out and shows: speaking the words of Torah. You haven't done it by thinking alone until you say it with your lips, to draw His light all the way down into the life in your blood, which came from stone, plant and animal, and lift all of it with the whole world. So His glory will fill this earth openly, "turning dark to light and bitter to sweet." That is the aim of a person's service: to draw His light down. But first you give Him your soul and all you have.

The hiding itself is love. He set His light aside so you could exist. And your letting go is only the face in the water, showing back.

## Chapter 50 · Run, and come back

All the loves so far come from the right side, from kindness. They're called "holy silver," *kesef*, from the words "you longed, longed for your father's house." But there is a love above them all, like gold above silver. It is love like "flashes of fire," and it comes from the upper strength of understanding.

This is how it comes. You think about the greatness of the Infinite, before whom everything counts as nothing, until your soul catches fire and blazes toward His glory, wanting to gaze at the King's glory. It's like a strong flame going up, wanting to break away from the wick and the wood it holds onto. That comes from the G-dly fire in the soul growing strong. From it comes thirst: "My soul thirsts for You." Then being "lovesick." Then the soul's pining away: "my soul longs, it even faints."

The Levites came from this root. Their service was lifting their voices in song and thanks, with melody, "running and returning," this fierce love like a flame from a flash of lightning. "It is impossible to explain this well in writing." Every wise-hearted person who binds his mind to G-d will find the good and the light hidden in his own soul, each by his measure, one moved one way and one another, after first stepping away from wrong completely, so nothing stands between.

And how do you serve, in Torah and mitzvot, from this love? Only by "returning." The Book of Formation says: "If your heart runs, return to the One." "If your heart runs" is the soul's longing in the heart, when it blazes so hard it is ready to pour itself out into the lap of its Father, the Life of life, and leave the prison of the body to cling to Him. Then tell your heart what the sages said: "Against your will you live." You live in this body to give it life, to draw the higher life down through the Torah of life, so that there will be a home below for His oneness, openly. "To be one in one": the hidden oneness, shown in the open world. That's what we sing: "Come, my beloved." And how can living here become your *will* and not only "against your will"? The chapter says this is explained elsewhere: "against your will you live," with the help of the Life of life.

The One you run toward is the One you return to. He is up there in the burning and down here in the task, and the home below is where "one in one" is shown.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Remember a moment, in prayer or song or somewhere else, when you wanted to stay up there and never come back down. - **The line, softly:** "If your heart runs, return to the One." - **Think it through.** A flame pulling up off its wick. Thirst, then lovesick, then pining. Then the return: you are alive in this body on purpose, to make a home for Him here. You come back to "the One," and He is down here too. - **Where the picture breaks.** A flame wants to leave its wick. But a flame lives only on its wick. The running needs the returning. - **From inside.** Burning, and then a willing coming back. - **The edge.** "It is impossible to explain this well in writing." Don't try to explain it to yourself either. - **Into the day.** After a prayer that moves you, go and do one concrete task, or one kindness, right away.

## Chapter 51 · As there, so here

To explain the child's saying from before (chapter 35), we first need to ask: what does it mean that the Presence "rested" in the Holy of Holies, or anywhere at all? Isn't the whole earth full of His glory? Isn't there "no place empty of Him"?

"From my own flesh I see G-d." A person's soul fills his whole body, head to foot. Still, it lives mainly in the brain, and from there it spreads to each limb, and each gets the power that fits it: the eye to see, the ear to hear, the mouth to speak, the feet to walk. And you feel in your brain whatever happens in any limb.

That difference isn't in the soul itself, as if it were split into pieces shaped like the body. The soul is one simple spiritual thing, with no shape and no place. You can't say it's more in the head than in the feet. All its powers are hidden in it, coming out to give life to the body. The main showing of them, their root, is in the brain. The brain gets its share first, and the life for every other limb is held in the brain too. From there a glow spreads out, like sunlight into room after room. (Even the heart gets from the brain.)

So, as a picture: the Infinite fills all worlds to give them life, endless worlds, endless angels and souls, higher above higher. His essence is the same above and below, like the soul in the body. The Tikkunim calls Him "hidden of all hidden": even in the hidden high worlds He is as hidden as He is here. No thought can grasp Him, even up there. So as He is found there, so He is truly found in the lower realms.

The difference between high and low worlds is only in the flow of life He shows out of hiding. (That's one reason it's called "light.") The higher worlds get a little more openly. The lower ones, even spiritual ones, get it through many clothes. And the clothes became so thick and heavy that He made this physical world through them, giving it life by a light so covered that no light or life shows at all. Only physical things, which seem dead. But inside them is light and life, bringing them from nothing at every moment, so they won't go back to nothing. And that light is from the Infinite, only dressed in many clothes. (The chapter traces the earth's life up through the worlds to the Infinite.)

His essence is equally here. Only the showing differs. The pen on your desk is no farther from Him than the highest angel.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Pick up one thing that "seems dead": a pen, a key, a stone. - **The line, softly:** "As He is found there, so He is truly found here." - **Think it through.** Your soul isn't more in your head than in your toes. It just shows more in your head. So He isn't more in heaven than in this key. Inside it, right now, light and life are bringing it from nothing. - **Where the picture breaks.** The soul at least shows in the brain. His essence isn't shown anywhere, not even above. It's hidden everywhere equally. - **From inside.** The ordinary stops looking dead. - **The edge.** "Hidden of all hidden." The highest angels don't see it either. That's the comfort: you aren't farther than they are. - **Into the day.** Touch one "dead" thing today and say: "Found here as there."

## Chapter 52 · The garment that lets us come close

In the soul, all its life shows in the brain, and the limbs get only a glow from it. So too, the main showing of life for all the worlds is dressed in His will and wisdom and understanding, which are called His "mind." And these are dressed in the Torah and its mitzvot. That showing is the source. Each world gets only a glow from it, like light from the sun.

This source has many names: "the revealed world," "the Queen," and *Shechinah*, the Presence, from "I will dwell among them." It's the first showing of the Infinite's light to the worlds. From it each thing gets the life that fits it, and it dwells inside them. So it's called "the mother of the children," the source of souls.

But the worlds can't bear the Presence itself without a covering. They would dissolve back into their source, like sunlight inside the sun, where you see no light, only the sun.

"What is the garment that can hide it and clothe it, and not dissolve in its light? It is His will and His wisdom," dressed in the Torah and its mitzvot, revealed "to us and our children." The Torah comes from the highest wisdom, far above the revealed world, "wise, but not with a wisdom that can be known." He and His wisdom are one. It came down step by step, until it was dressed in physical things: the mitzvot.

And as the Torah came down from world to world, the Presence came down with it and dressed itself in it in each world. That is the Holy of Holies of each world. The chapter maps this out, and I compress it. In one world the Presence is the source of the Talmud, the reasons behind the laws; in the next, of the Mishnah, the laws themselves. The laws come from His traits: kindness, which permits, so a thing can be lifted to G-d, and strictness, which forbids. All of it follows the highest wisdom. And so it goes down, world by world. From the Presence in each Holy of Holies, light and life spread to that world and everything in it, because all of it was made by the ten sayings of creation, "the word of G-d," which is called Presence.

The garment that hides Him is also what lets you come close. The plain law on the page is His wisdom, one with Him.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Open to one plain law or verse, something that feels dry. Let it be dry. - **The line, softly:** "What garment can hide it and not dissolve in its light? His will and His wisdom." - **Think it through.** Sunlight inside the sun can't be seen. A light that strong needs a covering, or nothing could stand near it. Here is the covering: this page. It is His wisdom. - **Where the picture breaks.** A garment is outside the one who wears it. This garment is His wisdom, one with Him. - **From inside.** Respect for the plain text. The dry page becomes a veil you can stand close to. - **The edge.** "Wise, but not with a wisdom that can be known." You hold the garment. You don't hold what's inside it. - **Into the day.** Learn one Mishnah today. Or read one verse of Psalms, as His garment.

## Chapter 53 · The wick and the flame

When the First Temple stood, with the Ark and the tablets in the Holy of Holies, the Presence rested there, dressed in the Ten Commandments, and it showed more strongly there than in the Holy of Holies of the upper worlds. Why? The Ten Commandments hold the whole Torah, and they come from the highest wisdom. To carve them on physical stone, they didn't come down step by step like the worlds. "The tablets were G-d's work, and the writing was G-d's writing," above nature.

The Second Temple had no Ark and no tablets, and the sages said the Presence didn't rest there. That means not at that level. It rested in the ordinary way, down through the worlds. Even so, no one could enter but the High Priest on Yom Kippur. "Since the Temple was destroyed, the Holy One has in His world only the four cubits of the law." "Even one person who sits and learns Torah, the Presence is with him," in the ordinary way, down through the worlds.

That's because almost all of the commandments are deeds. Even those of speech and thought need the lips, and moving the lips is a deed. The chapter adds that the 613 commandments with the seven of the sages add up to *keter*, "crown": the highest will, dressed in His wisdom and one with Him.

Now the child's saying from chapter 35 can be understood. "The upper light that burns over his head" is the Presence. It "needs oil": it needs to be dressed in wisdom, which is called holy oil. "And that is good deeds": the commandments, which flow from His wisdom. They hold the Presence's light on a wick. The wick is the body's soul, the life in you that wants and eats and gets tired.

As in a physical candle, the light shines by the burning of the wick, which turns into fire, so the light of the Presence rests on the soul by the burning of the animal soul, turning it from dark to light and from bitter to sweet. In the righteous, the animal soul itself turns. Or at least, in the in-between person, its clothes burn: thought, speech and deed, turned from the dark of the husks into the light of the Infinite, which is in the thought, speech and deed of the commandments. By this turning, something rises from below, and it draws the light of the Presence down onto the soul in the mind. And now we understand the verse: "For the Lord your G-d is a consuming fire."

Your ordinary nature is the wick. His fire needs something to burn. The struggle isn't in the way of the light; it is what the light shines by.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Name one pull of your nature today: impatience, a craving, not wanting to get up. Don't fight it now. Let it be there. - **The line, softly:** "The light shines by the burning of the wick, which turns into fire." - **Think it through.** No wick, no light. The wick disappears into the flame and becomes it. Even if the pull stays, the thought you turn, the word you don't say, the deed you do anyway: those are the wick burning. - **Where the picture breaks.** A wick gets used up. Here nothing is lost. It is turned into His light, "a consuming fire." And a candle doesn't choose to burn. You do. - **From inside.** The struggle starts to feel like fuel, not failure. - **The edge.** "A consuming fire." What burns in Him isn't gone. It's light. The mind can't follow that all the way. Let it burn. - **Into the day.** When your nature pulls today, say "wick" quietly, and do the good thing.
