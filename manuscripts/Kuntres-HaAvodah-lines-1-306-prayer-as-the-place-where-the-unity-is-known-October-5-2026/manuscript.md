## The shape of the range

This is a book about practice, not about cosmology. Its question is how a person at prayer comes to feel what the Tanya and Shaar HaYichud teach. So F12 (knowing the unity until the heart is moved) leads by a wide margin. F11 (refining the animal soul) comes next, because refining the animal soul is one of the two named aims of prayer. F08 (bitul) and F06 (the soul as part of God) follow. The cosmological faces F01-F05 appear mostly as the *content* of contemplation. The text says what one should think about in prayer (the life in each creature, memale and sovev, the kav, creation from nothing), but it rarely develops them for their own sake.

Many lines have no tie. Of the 90 *none* lines, most are front matter, 35 editor's footnotes, and two long stretches of practical guidance: guarding the eyes, ears, speech and thought in ch. 2, and order and honesty in one's stringencies in ch. 7. They are marked *none* and not forced.

## Front matter (lines 1-25)

The title page, the scan of the copyright page and the table of contents carry no tie. The Rayatz's covering letter (line 22) is mostly a polemic against "America is different" and against flattering the young. It ends by asking that the yeshiva produce a whole Jew in whom Israel, Torah and God are bound:

The heading of the tract names F12 by its own verse (lines 23-25): the service of the heart is to bind the soul to Godliness and "refine the animal soul" (OCR 'ולוכך את הנהיב'), "as it says, know today and return it to your heart" (OCR 'לבביר').

## Ch. 1 (26-64): prayer binds the soul's essence to the Essence

All the toil of prayer is to reach love (line 26). Prayer has two aims. The first is joining: the ladder by which nefesh, ruach and neshamah rise until the essence of the soul is bound to its source in the Essence of Ein Sof (F06 + F05):

> כמקור חוצבה בעצמית אוא"ם ב"ה (line 27)

The ladder is laid out level by level. The nefesh serves by acknowledgment. The general acknowledgment of Modeh ani gives one's whole existence to Godliness, and it is "very near to every Jew" (line 29). The higher unity, that all before Him is as nothing, is truly grasped only by lofty souls. But every Jew must have that bitul, at least as acknowledgment (line 32, F02 + F08). This matches the SPINE's F02 note on the Rashab. True knowledge of it is "the sight of the eye of the intellect" (line 32.2). Ruach contemplates what can be grasped: the divine life clothed in the world, "to bring the worlds into being and give them life" (line 38, F01 + F04). The Rashab says that this, and not some higher level, is the "service of the heart" of "know today", and the duty of every person:

> שעז"נ וידעת היום והשבות אל לבביך דוקא (line 38)

Neshamah grasps the divine matter stripped of its garments. The sign is that its natural senses fall away (line 40). The Rashab then marks the limit of this: "in truth he cannot grasp a divine matter with no garment at all" (line 46). Through memale one reaches sovev. "Within a thing" is memale, and "the thing" is sovev (line 51). That leads to the Godliness of the soul and its union with the Essence:

> נעשה בחי' יחוד ודביקות נפשו בעצמות אוא"ם ב"ה (line 51)

In the Amidah this is bitul in existence, "his head reaches heaven" (line 53). The second aim is refining the animal soul, prayer in place of a sacrifice (lines 55-56). The letters of Torah draw the revelation of Ein Sof downward (line 58, F10). The letters of prayer rise from below: the words and natural powers used all day are refined and become "a crown for their Maker" (line 60, F11).

## Ch. 2 (65-99): the yoke as the gate

This chapter is mostly practical, about guarding the senses and thoughts, and it is marked *none*. The ties come at a few points. The yoke is felt as the Master "who makes His kingship one over us" (line 76, F08). Before the light of divine kingship the klipot flee (line 89, F11). The divine soul is "like a polished thing that takes light into itself", and one's own crookedness covers it (line 92). The chapter's point for the unity is that the first act is to give oneself away:

That self-giving is "the gate to God" (line 96).

## Ch. 3 (100-127): awe is in the Essence, love in the lights

Awe comes from constant remembering, the Tanya 42 habit: all one sees is the King's garment, so one remembers "their inwardness and life" (line 101, F01). The editor notes at line 104 that printed Tanya reads slightly differently. Then the chapter's claim. Love comes from particular knowledge, so no one is born with it. Awe can be inborn, because it rests on a general, hidden sense of the Essence:

> והוא באמת הרגש בחי' עצמות אוא"ס ב"ה שלמעלה ממדריגות פרטיות (line 107)

Love is in His "lights and revelations". Awe, even lower awe, is in the Essence, so awe is bitul and higher than love (lines 107, 112, 113). Higher awe is shame before His greatness, the Zohar's "all before Him as nothing", bitul "like the sunlight in the sun" (line 113). It also comes from a contemplation that states F07's formula outright, with the OCR slip 'לסטה' for למטה:

> שהאוא"ס נמצא לסטה כמו למעלה (line 121)

The text explains this as Or Ein Sof "as it is in its essence, found below in real revelation", not His watching from above (line 121.2). Lower awe ranks below prayer's service, and higher awe far above it. Binding the soul, which is prayer's task, is done by love (lines 122-125).

## Ch. 4 (128-160): love like water, love like fire

Love like water is closeness born of contemplating the life in each creature. "You give life to them all." As a man feels the life in his own body, he sees "how the world lives from divine light" (line 129, F01 + F04):

> נרגש ונראה אצלו איך שהעולם חי מאור האלקי (line 129)

It grows into "my soul, I desire You", because You are my soul and my true life (line 129.2, F06). Then it becomes longing to draw Ein Sof in through Torah. What Torah draws in is Ein Sof above the worlds (line 134.1, F10). Beyond that is the love "like a son", from "You are truly our Father", from Havayah above Elokim (line 134.2). The contemplation of Godliness in the world has one advantage: even in physical things "the end of his will is the Godliness in them" (line 134.3).

Love like fire is where F11 becomes transformation. It comes from the greatness of Ein Sof, who fills and surrounds all, "all before Him as nothing" (line 144). In practice it comes from seeing one's own distance (line 147). The holy fire burns the fire of the other side, and the natural fire itself rises:

> הנה האש דקדושה שורף ומכלה האש דלעו"ז (line 149)

The prayer-sacrifice turns the animal soul "from the opposite to the opposite" (line 145, F09). The natural intellect must also come to see that the physical "is not worthy of the name of a thing by itself" (line 142). Lower teshuvah breaks the animal soul's selfhood. Only love raises it (line 150). The chapter closes by reading "levavcha" with two bets: the divine soul's closeness and the animal soul's refinement (line 157).

## Ch. 5 (161-199): the natural will and the yechidah

Love (ahavah) is from avah, will. The soul's natural will runs toward the Essence, because "it is a part of God above" (line 163, OCR 'אלק''). It is always one with it:

> והיא מיוחדת באמת תמיד בבחו' עצמות אוא"ם (line 163)

It runs at all only because souls come from the Essence "like a son from a father", one essence and yet a being of their own. That is how souls differ from Torah (line 163.2). The yechidah is "an essential bond with the Essence" and shows in self-sacrifice (lines 163-164). But the surrounding will does not change the animal soul's coarseness. The hidden love is like faith in the thief at the tunnel, who calls on God while he sins (line 184). The revealed yechidah does transform (lines 180, 182). A long passage on the Days of Awe (186-195) teaches that arousal changes nothing without year-round work. That passage is structural.

## Ch. 6 (200-250): only particular knowing is knowing

This is F12's hardest line. General contemplation, even of "He fills and surrounds all worlds", is surrounding only, a "false imagination" (line 204). Remembering is enough for awe, because bitul is natural to Jewish souls (line 204.2). It is not enough for love. Creation from nothing is beyond human grasp. Its power is the Essence's alone (line 201, F05 as the source of F01). The right contemplation is particular. It takes in the life drawn to each creature by its nature, and "how the power of Ein Sof is seen and revealed in the world" (line 211):

> ואיך שבזח נראה ונגלה כח הא"ם שבעולם (line 211)

Then comes the chain. Every blade of grass has its mazal above, "so the physical is nullified to the spiritual" (OCR 'בסל'). It goes up to the kav from Ein Sof. Contemplating the kav in Atzilut is the higher unity. Contemplating Beriah-Asiyah is the lower unity (line 221, F02). Along the way the Rashab corrects a reading of Shaar HaYichud 4 (line 216). Its two levels are not matter and then life. They are the light clothed in creatures, and the Essence of the divine ayin above all clothing:

> בחי' הארת האין המתלבש בהיש, ובחי' עצם האין שלמעלה מהתלבשות (line 216)

The chapter's reason for the toil is F07. The soul was bound to the body "to make everything one" (line 230). The true intent of the descent is "to raise the animal soul and all the matters of the world" (line 236):

> והו"ע קישור הנפש עם הגוף בכדי לעשות הכל בבחי' אחד (line 230)

## Ch. 7 (251-271): truth comes by stages

Most of the chapter is about a person who demands lofty stringencies of himself and cannot keep them. It is marked *none* or structural. Its one point for the unity is that the divine soul's excitement "does nothing to the animal soul" (line 255). So the refinement the tract asks for, the two souls joined, has not happened. The close gathers the book together. Contemplate memale at your own level (line 268). "Hear, O Israel" means understanding (line 269). Love with both inclinations, "so that the evil inclination too returns to love" (line 269.1). The desert generation saw Godliness from above and loved by itself. Those who came after were commanded to reach love by their own work (line 270).

## The Rayatz's letter (272-306)

The letter sorts love into ahavat olam (in two senses, concealment and eternity, "truly opposites" under one name, line 275), ahavah rabbah and love in delights. Ahavat olam in the concealment sense is "love, for He is your life", because "the life of the world is Godliness". Its opposite branch says the world's whole source is "a mere radiance that takes no place before Or Ein Sof" (line 276, F01 and F02). Eternal love is one where "cause and effect are one" (line 279). The springs of the deep make the earth moist just by dwelling in it (lines 280-282, G06). The letter's clearest general statement is providence: "for a Jew there is no chance". Every movement is for "Torah-light and the work of refinement" (line 287). The soul comes down to rise through mitzvot "below specifically" (line 300). Tzaddikim draw down supernal delight (line 302). Baalei teshuvah raise from below.

## What this book adds that the Tanya does not say, or says differently

- **Awe is in the Essence, love in the lights.** Tanya 41-43 makes awe the root and beginning, and ranks ahavah rabbah and love in delights above it. The Rashab says awe in general, even lower awe, rests on a feeling of the Essence, and is above love (lines 107, 112-113). He still makes love the whole work of prayer, because only love binds and refines (lines 123-125). He does not say that Tanya disagrees. We keep the two rankings side by side.
- **Knowing must be particular.** The Tanya asks for long contemplation (ch. 42). The Rashab calls general contemplation, even of memale and sovev, a "false imagination" (line 204). He requires the natural intellect of the animal soul to understand too (lines 142, 152). This is F12 at its strictest.
- **The animal soul can come to love.** The Tanya's beinoni never transforms the animal soul (ch. 12-13). The Rashab describes water-love that brings the natural mind to want Godliness (line 142), and fire-love that raises the natural fire into holiness (lines 149-150). Transformation comes through love above reason, and only after toil in reason (line 197). He then keeps the limit himself. The animal soul "by its nature does not want Godliness", and all its rising is the divine soul working on it (line 180, left as a "צ"ע"). The tension is in the text, and we leave it there.
- **The hidden love does not change anything by itself.** Tanya 18-19 grounds self-sacrifice in the hidden love that "cannot be separated from His oneness", and line 204.3 repeats it. The Rashab adds that this love is surrounding and changes no coarseness. The thief believes and still steals (line 184).
- **"Below as above" given to awe.** F07's formula appears in the contemplation that yields higher awe: Or Ein Sof present below as it is in its essence (line 121). The Tanya uses "as much below as above" for the lower unity (SH 7). Here it is tied to the Essence and to awe.
- **A reading of Shaar HaYichud 4** that the Rashab says goes against the "first impression" of the text (line 216).
- **The soul-body bond "to make everything one"** (line 230), a strong F07 statement about the person, not the world.

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- 90 line entries are *none*: the front matter and table of contents (1-21), 35 editor's footnotes, most of ch. 2 on guarding the senses (66-88), the Days of Awe teshuvah advice (191), most of ch. 7 on order in stringencies (252-265), and the public-Torah-class example in the letter (286).
- *Reading* is used six times. The crown "from nearness after distance" as opposites joined (60.2). The two meanings of "olam" as F09 (275). The springs parable as F06 and F09 (282). The body hiding the soul as F03 (292). The good body of Moses as F07 (293). Providence toward the Ravad's teshuvah as F03 (305). In each case the face is ours. - F03 is almost missing as a doctrine. Tzimtzum appears once, as the contraction that lets created minds know (line 48). Concealment otherwise means the person's own crookedness hiding the divine soul (line 92). The one place where concealment serves revelation is the aside on the root of the concealments, which is "above revelation" (line 180.2). - F09 is mostly the joining of the two souls and the two inclinations, not opposites joined in God.

## Disagreements kept

- **With the Tanya, on rank.** Tanya 43 puts love in delights above awe. The Rashab puts awe, as bitul from the Essence, above love (lines 107, 113), while love is the goal of prayer. The letter at the end (Rayatz) keeps the Tanya's order of loves. Neither side is smoothed.
- **With the Tanya, on transformation.** See above (lines 142, 149-150, 180, 197 against Tanya 12-13).
- **Within the book, on the animal soul.** Line 142 says the animal soul "too comes to want Godliness". Line 180 says it never does so by its own nature. The Rashab marks the question open.
- **With the SPINE's F12 dispute.** The SPINE sets the Alter Rebbe's "we have no business with hidden things" against the Rayatz's "to know". This book stands with knowing: nothing short of particular grasp counts (lines 204, 209-211). It also marks the limit, that no divine matter is grasped without some garment (line 46), and the fact that creation from nothing is beyond human mind (line 201).
