*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A daily calendar of about 600 very short entries, one per day of the year. The Rebbe compiled it (1942-43) from talks, letters and stories of the previous Rebbes. Each day gives a custom or point of liturgy, a date in Chabad history, and usually one saying: a practical, distilled piece of avodah (inner work). The aim is to put a short line of Chassidus in a person's hand every day and to shape his day, prayer, livelihood and relations with others. It mixes Hebrew with a lot of Yiddish.

**How much of it is medicine.** substantial: roughly half the entries are customs, liturgy, text corrections and history, but many of the rest are short, practical diagnoses and prescriptions for the inner life, including one explicit protocol for healing the soul.

**Its model of the person.** A person has a divine soul and an animal (natural) soul. The animal soul is individual to each person, cunning, and can even dress up as pious. Mind is cold and settled; the emotions (middos) are hot and agitated. Thought is a garment and servant of both, and it runs wild when it has nothing to serve. Under all of this lies an essence that stays undefiled. Change comes from honest self-knowledge, contemplation that moves from understanding to feeling, and concrete action rather than feeling alone. Steady habit, one's own effort and a supporting community (the farbrengen gathering, private audience with the Rebbe, friends) carry it.

**Its philosophy.** Divine providence reaches every detail: God renews the world every moment, and each soul is sent to a particular place and time to refine something there. The material world is meant to become a vessel for the divine. Each Jew is a part of the Essence, so his core cannot be lost or defiled. That is why repair is always possible, and why action in this world matters more than spiritual feeling cut off from it.

**What it adds.** It gives the diagnosis in distilled one-liners, meant as a daily dose. It includes an explicit soul-medicine protocol: locate the illness (body, faculties or habit), wish to heal, believe you can, then repair the weak and strengthen the healthy. It also offers a sharp critique of sighing instead of acting, the 'pious animal soul' test for self-deception, a comparison rule (look below materially, above spiritually), and many vivid Yiddish sayings on second chances and the undefiled core.

## Something is wrong with me inside and I don't know where to start

A general sense of sickness of soul: bad traits, bad habits and heaviness all at once, with no idea what the cause is or whether change is possible.

**What is really going on.** Illness of the soul works like illness of the body. It has a location: the coarseness of the body, faculties that lean toward pride or falsehood, or habits formed by poor upbringing or surroundings. It cannot be treated until the location is found, and healing needs both the wish to heal and the belief that one can.

**Program: The soul-healing intake.** (1) Treat the soul's illness as you would the body's: diagnose before you treat. (2) Find the source: a coarse body, faculties leaning toward pride or falsehood, or habits formed by upbringing or surroundings. (3) Until it is found, keep a straight regimen: mitzvos, fixed times for Torah study, acquiring good traits, and avoiding the bad. (4) Awaken the knowledge that you are ill, together with a longing to be healed. (5) Equally, awaken the knowledge that you can be healed, with hope and full trust. (6) Treat on two tracks: repair the sick faculty (teshuvah, return) and strengthen the healthy ones (good deeds) so they overpower it. *When:* Ongoing. The text gives no schedule, only an order: diagnose first, and keep the interim regimen while diagnosing. *For:* Someone ready to look at himself who senses general inner disorder. *Not for:* Someone in acute distress for whom 'know you are ill' would land as condemnation without the equal clause of hope. It is not a substitute for clinical care. `HY·Q01` · `HY·Q02` · `HY·Q03` · `HY·Q04`

**Reframe.** From *"Something is shamefully wrong with me."* to **The soul gets ill as the body does. The illness has a location and a cause, and knowing you can heal matters as much as knowing you are ill.** Diagnosis replaces blanket self-condemnation. The work makes hope and trust co-equal preconditions of healing, so despair counts as an obstacle to treatment and not as realism. `HY·Q01` · `HY·Q02` · `HY·Q03`

## I sigh and feel bad about myself, but nothing changes

Regret shows up as groaning and self-reproach, and it feels like doing something. Days pass with the same faults.

**What is really going on.** The sigh is only a key that opens the heart and eyes. Treating it as the work itself is how a person discharges his duty without acting.

**Program: Know yourself, then act instead of sighing.** (1) Take an honest measure of yourself: your real flaws and your real virtues. (2) Check for both distortions: over-strictness that invents faults, and leniency born of self-love. Both are false. (3) Let the sigh do its only job: open the heart and eyes. (4) Turn at once to one concrete act of repair, each person by his own means. (5) Remember that one act is worth more than a thousand sighs. *When:* Whenever regret arises; the text gives no fixed schedule. *For:* Someone who feels regret often but whose regret has become a substitute for change. *Not for:* Someone in grief or depression who needs room to feel; the text is aimed at sighs replacing action, not at feeling as such. `HY·Q08` · `HY·Q09` · `HY·Q10` · `HY·Q11`

**Reframe.** From *"Feeling terrible about it is my repentance."* to **A sigh is only a key that opens the heart. One act is worth more than a thousand sighs.** Feeling gets its proper role as an opening. Energy moves from self-reproach to concrete repair, the part the work says actually heals. `HY·Q09` · `HY·Q10` · `HY·Q08`

## I'm either too hard on myself or I let myself off the hook

Swinging between finding faults and sins everywhere and covering up real flaws out of self-love.

**What is really going on.** Both are departures from truth. Truth is the middle way: an honest knowledge of one's own flaws and one's own virtues.

**Program: Know yourself, then act instead of sighing.** (1) Take an honest measure of yourself: your real flaws and your real virtues. (2) Check for both distortions: over-strictness that invents faults, and leniency born of self-love. Both are false. (3) Let the sigh do its only job: open the heart and eyes. (4) Turn at once to one concrete act of repair, each person by his own means. (5) Remember that one act is worth more than a thousand sighs. *When:* Whenever regret arises; the text gives no fixed schedule. *For:* Someone who feels regret often but whose regret has become a substitute for change. *Not for:* Someone in grief or depression who needs room to feel; the text is aimed at sighs replacing action, not at feeling as such. `HY·Q08` · `HY·Q09` · `HY·Q10` · `HY·Q11`

**Program: The accounting, done like a businessman.** (1) Periodically go over everything that happened, as a merchant takes stock. (2) Identify the strengths in your inner work and reinforce them. (3) Identify the flaws in yourself and your work and fix them. *When:* Yearly, through the month of Elul. The work also asks for private reflection and repair on one's birthday. *For:* Anyone, both the full-time scholar and the working person. *Not for:* Someone prone to obsessive self-scrutiny, unless the strengths half is kept alongside the flaws. `HY·Q29`

## My prayer and spiritual life have gone dry and cold

Everything becomes dry and cold. Routine observance feels heavy, one rushes, and learning loses its taste.

**What is really going on.** Decline begins with the absence of inner work in prayer. The cold intellect and the agitated emotions are never joined, so understanding never becomes feeling.

**Program: The ladder of contemplation for dry prayer.** (1) Study a concept until it is clear and its idea shines for you. (2) Before prayer, contemplate it until you feel its vitality, not only its logic. (3) In prayer, contemplate it until you feel the divine within it. (4) Join the cold, settled mind to the hot, agitated heart, so that agitation becomes aspiration and mind becomes guide. (5) Aim for love felt in the body's heart, enough to forget what oppresses you and to wake inner hope. (6) A serious heaviness and self-examination before prayer can be what opens feeling in prayer. *When:* Daily, before and during morning prayer. *For:* Someone who learns and prays but feels nothing: cold, dry, rushed. *Not for:* The bitterness step is not for someone depressed or already weighed down; start with the steps of learning and feeling vitality. `HY·Q20` · `HY·Q19` · `HY·Q21` · `HY·Q28` · `HY·Q06`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm either cold and detached or overwhelmed and agitated."* to **Mind and excitement are two worlds to be joined. Then agitation becomes aspiration and mind becomes guide.** Neither pole is suppressed. Their union changes each: the heat gets direction and the cold gets life. `HY·Q19`

## I can't stop worrying about money

Anxiety about livelihood fills the mind and pushes out joy and inner life.

**What is really going on.** He takes his own effort to be the source of his livelihood. His part is to do what is needed honestly; the providing is God's part.

**Program: The livelihood-trust practice.** (1) Do what is actually needed in your work, honestly. (2) Leave the rest: livelihood is God's part. (3) Deliberately awaken trust in the One who provides for all. (4) Be glad and good-hearted, as if your livelihood were already set before you. (5) If worry stays, either put it out of mind or speak it to others who feel it with you. *When:* During working hours; the text addresses it to the businessman as part of his daily work. *For:* Someone working and anxious about income. *Not for:* Someone who would use it to avoid needed practical steps or to stay in a harmful situation. `HY·Q15` · `HY·Q16` · `HY·Q14`

**Reframe.** From *"My livelihood rests on my effort alone."* to **Do what you need to; God will do what He needs to. Be glad as if it is already provided.** Dividing responsibility releases the anxiety attached to the part that is not his. Joy is prescribed as part of the work, not as its reward. `HY·Q16` · `HY·Q15`

## Foolish or bad thoughts keep flooding my mind

Stray, empty or improper thoughts intrude, and he takes them as a verdict on who he is.

**What is really going on.** Thought is a servant of the intellect and emotions. When the head is empty it keeps working anyway, running wild. The cause is idleness of mind, not a corrupt self.

**Program: Give the mind work.** (1) Understand that stray thoughts come from an empty head, not from what you are. (2) Keep the intellect occupied with meaningful content, so thought has something to serve. (3) Memorize some texts (the work names Chumash, Tehillim, Mishnah and Tanya) to review while walking or working. *When:* Throughout the day, especially in idle moments in the street, at work or in transit. *For:* Someone troubled by idle, intrusive or improper thoughts. *Not for:* Clinical OCD intrusive thoughts, where effortful suppression and monitoring can make things worse. `HY·Q05`

**Reframe.** From *"These thoughts show who I really am."* to **Stray thoughts come from an empty head. Thought is a servant that runs wild without a job.** The thoughts are taken out of the realm of identity and treated as idle machinery, which gives a simple remedy: occupy the mind. `HY·Q05`

He feels he has missed his chance or is defiled at the core, even by his own choice, and cannot return.

## I'm weak and ordinary, with nothing special in me

He sees himself as lacking in intellect and in heart, and not capable of growth.

**What is really going on.** The weak person (enosh) holds every virtue of the great ones in hidden form. The springs are there in all ground; what they need is a patient digger.

**Program: Put every trait to use.** (1) Accept that even bad traits can serve good ends. (2) Learn from the thief: act modestly, take risks, value small things, toil, move quickly, trust and hope. (3) If you fail the first time, try again many times. (4) Dig patiently: the springs are in you; what matters is the digger's patience. *When:* No schedule given. *For:* Someone who feels deficient or defined by his flaws. *Not for:* `HY·Q24` · `HY·Q26` · `HY·Q25`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm just weak and ordinary."* to **The weak person already holds every virtue of the great; springs lie under all ground. It depends on patient digging.** Deficiency becomes hiddenness. Since the virtues are present, the task is uncovering them with patience, not acquiring something foreign. `HY·Q25` · `HY·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"My bad traits disqualify me."* to **Every trait, even a bad one, can be used in serving God; even a thief teaches seven lessons.** Traits are redirected instead of fought as enemies. The same drive becomes fuel, and persistence after failure is itself one of the lessons. `HY·Q24`

## My cravings pull me, and I think the answer is to punish my body

Caught by desire for permitted pleasures. He either gives in or turns to harsh self-denial.

**What is really going on.** Desire is broken by gently refusing a craved permitted thing, not by mortification. The body is to be refined, not broken.

**Program: Breaking a craving gently.** (1) Notice a permitted thing you strongly want. (2) Precisely because you crave it, choose not to do it. (3) Do not turn to fasting or mortification; the aim is to refine the body, not break it. (4) Keep body strong and spirit joyful so that vitality can go into your mitzvos. *When:* As cravings arise; the text gives no frequency. *For:* Someone driven by appetites for permitted things. *Not for:* Anyone with an eating disorder, scrupulosity or a history of self-punishment. `HY·Q23` · `HY·Q22` · `HY·Q31`

**Reframe.** From *"Holiness means denying and weakening the body."* to **One must be healthy, strong and joyful to bring vitality into the mitzvos. Refine the body; don't break it.** Strength and joy become the means of holiness instead of its opponents, which removes the motive for self-punishment. `HY·Q31` · `HY·Q22`

## My lofty plans keep stopping me from doing what's in front of me

A sudden strong wish to study deeper or contemplate more shows up exactly when a concrete duty is due, and the duty doesn't get done.

**What is really going on.** The animal soul is cunning and can wear the clothes of a pious, humble person. Anything that obstructs actual practice, however lofty, is its trick.

**Program: Unmasking the pious evasion.** (1) Notice a sudden strong urge toward a loftier pursuit just when a practical duty is due. (2) Apply the rule: whatever hinders actual practice, even something loftier, is a trick of the animal soul. (3) Do the concrete thing first. *When:* In the moment, whenever duty and an 'elevated' impulse compete. *For:* Idealistic people who procrastinate through spirituality. *Not for:* Someone who needs rest or healthy boundaries; do not use it to override them. `HY·Q12` · `HY·Q13`

**Reframe.** From *"This urge toward something higher must be good."* to **Whatever hinders actual practice, even something loftier, is the animal soul's trick. It can look like a humble, pious person.** It gives a clear test, whether something leads to action, that sees through self-deception wearing spiritual clothes. `HY·Q12` · `HY·Q13`

## I know I'm right and I can't let go of my view

Strong in his own opinion: 'this is what I say, this is what I hold'. He clashes with others.

**What is really going on.** Every 'I' and sense of self-existence is the source of bad and divides hearts. The strong-minded person has to leave his thoughts the way the wicked man has to leave his path.

**Reframe.** From *"This is how I say it; this is my position."* to **Every 'I' and self-assertion is a root of bad and divides hearts. The strong-minded must leave his thoughts.** It shows attachment to one's own view as the source of separation, which lets the person loosen it for the sake of connection. `HY·Q30`

## I compare myself to others and come out restless or complacent

He looks up at those who have more materially and down at those who have less spiritually, so he is both envious and self-satisfied.

**What is really going on.** The direction of comparison is inverted. Materially, look below and give thanks; spiritually, look above and ask to learn.

**Reframe.** From *"Others have more than me, and spiritually I'm doing fine."* to **In material things look at those below you and give thanks; in spiritual things look at those above and ask to learn.** Reversing the direction of comparison produces gratitude where there was envy, and aspiration where there was complacency. `HY·Q27`

Isolated with his worry, with no one who feels it with him.

**What is really going on.** Worry can be spoken to others who are one with him and feel what he is going through. The Chassidic path was founded so that neither Rebbe nor Chassid is alone.

**Program: The livelihood-trust practice.** (1) Do what is actually needed in your work, honestly. (2) Leave the rest: livelihood is God's part. ## Cautions

- 'Know that you are ill' can land as shame on a depressed or traumatized person. Keep the text's equal clause: know that you can be healed, with hope and trust. - 'One act beats a thousand sighs' can dismiss grief or depression that needs room; the target is sighing in place of action, not feeling itself. - The rule that any obstacle to practice, even a lofty one, is the animal soul could be misused to override rest, health or boundaries. - Refusing a permitted thing you crave is risky for eating disorders, scrupulosity or self-punishing personalities. - Bitterness and heaviness before prayer should not be prescribed to someone already low or depressed. - Livelihood lines ('cannot earn a cent beyond what is decreed') could be read as fatalism or a reason to stay in harm; the text also says do what is needed. - Collective lines on sufferings as 'birth pangs of Moshiach' calling for teshuvah must not be turned on an individual's suffering as blame. - Much is addressed to Jews and Chassidim and assumes Torah practice; a universal guide must translate it, not transplant it.
