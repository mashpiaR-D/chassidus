## The shape of the range

The addenda are loose pieces, but several of them go straight to the inner life: - a hungry child, the son of Rav Hamnuna the Elder, teaches the order of offering (1-17); - Shabbos is a king's day with his bride and his children (18, 21); - the soul that speaks, and what bad speech does to it (28-32); - Torah scholars live on a small taste of the world to come (35-40); - a midnight prayer and a vision of G-d's delight in the righteous (78-98); - the serpent, the man who repented, and the robbed man who blessed the judgment (103-122); - prayer as Jacob's ladder, and the morning order of prayer, ending with falling on the face (164-201); - Rabbi Shimon, sick, says he is judged by no court but G-d Himself (238-251).

## The unity of G-d

**1. "I am Hashem on every side."** The Shabbos teaching ends with a reason for "revere My sanctuary": "Guard yourselves from stray thoughts, for My sanctuary is holy. Why? For I am Hashem on every side" (כי אני יי בכל צד, line 18). *Ours:* a short line that holds the whole point of memale (filling). There is no side where He is not, and so no place for a careless thought.

**2. He alone holds Israel.** Balak thought Israel, like the nations, was given over to angels, and he sent Bilaam the names of the twelve angels of the chariot (136-139). But:

> וקודשא בריך הוא לא עביד הכי, דלא שביק לון למלאכים ולא לחילי שמיא. אלא לקודשא בריך הוא בלחודוי (Zohar, Addenda III 11:42; line 140)
> The Holy One did not do so. He did not leave them to angels or to the hosts of heaven, but to the Holy One alone.

He "took Israel and settled them in the strength of the body of the Tree" (line 140). Rabbi Shimon says the same of himself when he is sick: "I am not given over to an angel or to a judge above... The Holy One judges me Himself, and not His court" (238-239).

**3. His measures lean to merit.** A court above has some who lean to merit and some to guilt. "But one whom the High King judges... it is good, and a person can only come out of that judgment well." Why? "The measures of the High King lean always to merit," and "forgiveness is with You," not with another (line 241). *Ours:* the nearer to the One, the more mercy. This matches the Idra's "in the Ancient there is no left" (part 6).

**4. He comes down in the image of the one who prays.** Jacob's ladder is prayer. It is "set on the earth," which is the Shechinah, and "reaches heaven," which is the Holy One. The angels go up and down "on him," meaning on that praying person (164-165). The Holy One "comes down... and is made one in that prayer" (line 166). "A commandment without thought has no rising" (ומצוה בלא מחשבה לית לה סליקו, line 166).

## States of consciousness

- **The speaking soul.** When a person walks a true path, "and his mouth and tongue speak holy words in prayer and in Torah," the holy soul cleaves to him. He is "beloved of his Master," many guards keep him, and "the holy Shechinah rests on him":

> ודא היא דסלקא לעילא וממללא קמי מלכא קדישא ועיילא בכל תרעין ולית דימחי בידהא (Zohar, Addenda III 6:1; line 28)
> And this is the one who rises above and speaks before the Holy King, and enters every gate, and no one stops her hand.

Therefore she is called "the speaking spirit" (line 28). When a person speaks evil, "the Shechinah leaves him and the holy soul passes from him." She rises "in shame" and "is not given a place to speak as before" (29-30). "Who guards his mouth and tongue guards his soul from troubles": the speaking soul "was made silent because of bad speech" (line 32). He is also punished for a good word he could have said and did not (line 32). Maps to the `lips` work. *Proposed state: the speaking soul.* *Ours:* a clear picture of how speech either opens or closes one's own inner voice before G-d.

- **The one who made himself precious.** "Since you became precious in My eyes" is read as "you made yourself precious." A sinner is at first "despised before the Holy One." After he considers and turns back from his sin, "now he is precious of himself" (line 110). Then:

> דהא לית רחימו לקודשא בריך הוא בהדי בר נש בהאי עלמא, אלא במאן דתב מחוביה. ועל דא אני אהבתיך (Zohar, Addenda III 11:13; line 111)
> For the Holy One has no love with a person in this world except with one who returns from his sin. And so: "and I love you."

Maps to S77 and S78. *Ours:* strong words for someone who feels his past has made him worthless. The Zohar says the return itself makes him precious.

- **Justifying the judgment while weeping for others.** A Jew robbed and beaten sits under a tree and says:

> מאריה דעלמא, גלוי וידוע קמך דאנא לא חיישנא עלי ועל גופי ועל ממוני כלום, דהא בדינא אתעביד כל (מה) דאתעביד (Zohar, Addenda III 11:14; line 112)
> Master of the world, it is revealed and known before You that I do not worry for myself, for my body or for my money at all, for all that was done was done in justice.

He worries only for his old parents and for a poor man's purse meant for his daughter's wedding. "For this I weep, and not for my own" (112-114). Rabbi Elazar calls him "a true righteous man" (line 115). He bows in the dust and thanks G-d, and Rabbi Elazar prays and his wound heals (115-116). Maps to S84 and S86. *Proposed state: accepting for oneself, weeping for others.*

- **Seeing with the spirit.** Asked how he knew all this, Rabbi Elazar says: "It is not by a sign and not by wisdom. My spirit looked, and I saw as if I saw with my eye" (אלא רוחא דילי אסתכל ואנא חמינא כאילו בעינא חמינא, line 119). Near S57.

- **G-d's delight in those who renew Torah.** At midnight the Holy One enters the Garden. "All the effort they made all that day in new words of Torah, the Holy One longs for those righteous who renewed words in it, and delights in them" (line 81). Then He reveals Himself, "and they see the pleasantness of Hashem," their joy spreads, "and from that radiance... they make fruit for this world" (line 82). "There is no joy or delight for the Holy One except in the hour when the righteous there stand with Him" (line 84). Maps to S53.

- **The Presence not in one place.** An ancient book found in a cave describes the vision in Eden: "The Holy King is not revealed in a chamber, and not in one place." An embroidered sky "spreads over the Garden on four sides and fills with precious radiance, and He is seen there" (line 96). When the book flew from his hand, Rabbi Abba says: "I remained sad and wept" (line 97). Rabbi Elazar comforts him: perhaps the Holy One "wants His honor," that it not be revealed (line 98). *Ours:* longing for what was almost seen is itself part of the path.

- **Judged by the Merciful alone.** Sick, Rabbi Shimon is unseen for a time. His friends smell "the scent of the spices of the Garden of Eden." He tells them they were not fit "to see the face of the Ancient of Days" (238-245). Maps to S63.

## Meditation and practice

- **Falling on the face as giving the soul.** This is the addenda's fullest practice:

> מכאן ולהלאה אחזי גרמיה דמסר נפשיה למאריה ברחימו דכתיב … אליך ה' נפשי אשא (Zohar, Addenda III 14:11; line 193)
> From here on he shows himself as one who gives his soul to his Master in love, as it is written, "To You, Hashem, I lift my soul."

After prayer and confession, "since he entered all those upper levels... he must come down." Then "he sets himself, in the will of the heart, and gives Him his soul in love, and the Holy One counts it for him as if He took his soul from him" (line 195). Some sins are atoned only at death. So "that hour stands as atonement for all his sins" (line 195). Afterward she "rises between the two arms, in the embrace of love" (line 196). This confirms "daily giving-over" (part 5) and "confessing into His hands" (part 9). Ties to `heart_after` and `hold`.

- **The order of the morning.** The Addenda give a full order (183-191): 1. Read the Creation to "one day," "the praise of all." 2. Say the offerings "so all the stray thoughts of the night are atoned." 3. Say David's praises. 4. At "Who forms light," "the gates open," and the levels join "like pillars of smoke." 5. "With great love" binds to the right. 6. The Shema. 7. Rav Hamnuna: "whoever unifies this every day, joy is prepared for him from above," with the sign "I will rejoice in Hashem" (line 186). 8. The first blessing of the Amidah should be taken "into the heart, and bound like a flame in a coal, in love" (ואית לאכללא ליה בלבא ולאתקשרא כשלהובא בגחלתא ברחימו, line 189).

- **The King eats first, then speak your trouble.** This repeats Pinchas (part 10) and adds a comparison. After the King "eats" (the Amidah) and one falls on the face and gives his spirit, "let a person say the trouble of his heart: 'May Hashem answer you on the day of trouble,' the trouble of a woman in labor" (197-201). Ties to `own_words`.

- **Midnight words.** "Whoever wakes at night, at the hour the Holy One enters the Garden with the righteous, should say this verse with the will of the heart and aim his mind at it": "A river goes out from Eden to water the garden." Then "I thank You, for I am awesomely and wondrously made... and my soul knows it well." "This is the praise of the early pious ones when they woke at midnight" (line 78). Ties to the earlier "midnight rising." *Proposed technique: the river and the wonder (midnight verses).*

- **Going out wrapped.** "Whoever goes out of his doorway wrapped in a mitzvah, with tefillin on his head... the Shechinah meets him," and two angels walk him to the synagogue and bless him (line 22). Ties to `act` and `carry`.

- **Making the house ready for the bride.** At the start of Shabbos a Jewish home needs "a lit candle, a set table and a made bed... like a groom's home ready to receive the bride." If she comes and finds none of this, "the bride says: this house is not of Israel" (line 21). Ties to `act`.

- **Charity at every time.** "One who does charity at every time" wakes charity above "for all, upper and lower. So a person should set his heart and will and cling to the Holy One" (34). Ties to `act`.

## The big human questions

- **Why do young people die?** "A child from twelve years who did not reach twenty, or a young man who did not marry": the Holy One "uproots him and plants him afterward" where he will grow. "The Holy One sees in him that he will not succeed. If he hardened he would not be planted in a good planting. Now that he is tender and fit to be uprooted, He uproots and plants him" (line 214). There is a "rock of dispute" between Eden and Gehinnom, "because all the people of the world argue with their Master over that child" (line 214). *Ours:* the Zohar lets the protest stand, and names it.

- **What of those who were born again?** G-d "does not destroy the work of His hands." Even the earlier bodies are not lost (216-217).

- **How does healing work?** "All the healings of the world are in the hand of the Holy One." Some come through a messenger, "and sometimes they return." Those the Holy One heals Himself "never return": "Heal me, Hashem, and I will be healed" (line 121).

- **How do scholars live?** They wake food for the whole world, yet they are fed only from the world to come. What reaches them here is "a small contraction of its power, found in the sweetness of Torah. That is the joy of Torah scholars and their high food" (line 39).

- **Does G-d guard a person's honor even after he sins?** Adam asks Rabbi Shimon that his sin not be told to the whole world. "The Holy One spares his honor and did not want to publish his sin" beyond what the tree itself shows (247-248).

## For the guide (ours)

1. "I am Hashem on every side" (line 18) is a short, memorable line for the filling presence, and it comes with a clear duty: guard the mind.
2. "No love... except with one who returns" (line 111) and "you made yourself precious" (line 110) should be offered to people who think their past has closed the door.
3. The robbed man who accepts his own loss and weeps only for others (112-114) is a portrait of trust that is not numbness.
4. Falling on the face as a daily giving of the soul in love (192-196) is the addenda's fullest technique. It joins "daily giving-over" and "confessing into His hands" as one practice across the volume.
5. The speaking soul (28-32) gives a reason for guarding speech that is about one's own inner life, not only about harm to others.
