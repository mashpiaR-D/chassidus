## How to use this map

This is a description, not a checklist. The states below come in roughly this order for many people, but not for everyone, and not every sitting. Some come on the first day and some after years. Some never come in a way you can name, and the sources say that the deepest ones are the least felt.

So read it **before** or **after** a sitting, never during. During the sitting your job is the line (Part I). Examining a high moment while it happens spoils it [Esh Kodesh 541], and the Mitteler Rebbe's picture of the real thing is someone who "doesn't notice at all whether he is moved" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 219].

> **Source.** Esh Kodesh (line 541): «ואסור לו לחקור בשעה זו אחר התרוממותו מה לו, כי החקירה בשכל מקלקלת את ההתרוממות»

There is one thread through the whole map. You aren't producing something new. The bond between your soul and God is already there. It isn't made by effort; cleaving is "the life and bond of the soul with the Life of life" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 144, as the Unity Index's GUIDE-LAYER.md B01 cites it]. A sitting only lets what was always there show. That is why the states get quieter as they go deeper.

## 1. Scattered

**From inside.** You sit down and your head is a train station. The meeting, the bills, a song, what someone said. You can't hold one thought for three seconds. It feels like nothing holy could possibly happen in here.

**The source's image.** Sleep. The Mitteler Rebbe says the distraction of making a living "is called the exile of the Shechinah, and is called sleep" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 12]. Life in a sleeper isn't gone; it's hidden. The Piaseczner's image is sharper: the soul is crying, and we hear it the way a sleeper stung by a fly dreams that a crate fell on him [Hakhsharat 84]. We even mistake the soul's unease for hunger or thirst [Hakhsharat 85].

**What it means.** Scattered isn't failure. It's the starting place, and the unease inside it may already be the soul reaching out.

**What to do.** Arrive from what is already moving, then quiet (Part I, Stages 1-2).

> **Source.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 12): «כך טרדת הפרנסה שנקרא גלות השכינה נקרא שינה»
> Hakhsharat (line 84): «משל לאיש ישן אשר עקץ אותו זבוב במצחו»
> Hakhsharat (line 85): «ויחשוב שרעב הוא, צמא»

## 2. Quiet

**From inside.** After a minute or two of just watching the thoughts, they slow. There are small gaps. Your head feels roomier, a little emptier. You aren't asleep and you aren't blank; you're just not being dragged.

**The source's image.** "He will feel, slowly slowly, that his head is emptying and his thoughts have stopped their usual flood" [Derekh HaMelekh 1556, the student's record]. And what's left when the flood stops is not nothing: the Godly part "is seen without a garment" [Derekh HaMelekh 90]. On Shabbat the Piaseczner calls rest "the soul returning into itself" [Esh Kodesh 540].

**What it means.** Room has been made. It's not the goal; it's the table cleared for the line.

**What to do.** Say the line, softly (Part I, Stage 3). Don't stay in the quiet for its own sake.

> **Source.** Derekh HaMelekh (line 1556): «אז ירגיש לאט לאט שראשו מתרוקן»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 90): «ובאם היה האדם מעמיד ומפסיק על שעה את כל שטף מחשבותיו ורצוניו, היה חלק אלקות נראה לו בלא לבוש»
> Esh Kodesh (line 540): «השבת הנפש אל קרבו»

## 3. The line takes hold (and may go flat first)

**From inside.** You say the line and start thinking it through, one thing at a time. At first it's just words. Then one detail catches: *this cup is being said right now*. For a second it's not an idea, it's so. The room seems to fill with it. Then it slips, and you bring it back.

Often, just as you start on the details, it goes **flat**. Colder than a minute ago. This is common and it isn't a bad sign.

**The source's image.** Chabad's aim is that the truth becomes as real to you as a physical thing you're looking at with your eyes [Tanya ch. 42]. The Piaseczner describes the widening: a line turned over and over until "his room and all around him fill with this thought" [Chovat HaTalmidim 228]. And the Rashab names the cold: "when he begins to contemplate, which is the start of the real work, he goes cold" [Kuntres HaAvodah]; at first a person grasps "only the glimmer of the matter, not the matter itself" [Kuntres HaAvodah].

**What it means.** The line is becoming yours. The flat moment is where the work starts, not where it ends.

**What to do.** Stay with the same line. Don't switch to a better idea. Keep going into the details and down into your day (Part I, Stage 4).

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 42 (line 629): «כמו שהיא מקושרת בדבר גשמי שרואה בעיני בשר ומעמיק בו מחשבתו»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 228): «עד שגם חדרו אף כל סביבו מתמלאים מן מחשבה זו»
> Kuntres HaAvodah, OCR (line 371): «כאשר מתחיל להתבונן שזהו התחלת העבודה האמיתית מתקרר» … «הנה בהתחלת ההתבוננות ח"ה תופם רק בזהרורית הענין לא בהענין עצמו»

## 4. The heart answers

**From inside.** Something moves under the thinking. A warmth. A catch in the throat. A small "oh". Sometimes gladness, sometimes a longing, like homesickness, that turns out to be love. It usually comes by itself, when you weren't looking for it, and it's often small: a feather stirring, not a fire.

**The source's image.** "As water reflects a face to a face, so the heart of a person to a person" [Tanya ch. 46]: when you take in how He came close to you, your heart answers His. The Piaseczner's aim is small on purpose: to make "the tips of the soul's feathers" tremble [Hakhsharat 31]. The Mitteler Rebbe says the simple person is stirred because the Godly in his soul is drawn to God "as a spark is drawn after the flame" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 158]. The Tanya's own picture for the soul is a candle flame that always flickers upward, wanting to leave the wick and join its source [Tanya ch. 19].

For some people the heart answers late. The movement reaches it gradually, "with proper settledness" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 753].

**What it means.** The line has reached past the head. It doesn't mean you've arrived anywhere.

**What to do.** Nothing new. Don't grab it, don't push it higher, don't push it away. Go back to the line and keep thinking it through. Once the heart moves, the Mitteler Rebbe says, let the feeling "add its strength to your understanding, to widen and deepen it further" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 742].

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 46 (line 680): «כמים הפנים לפנים, כן לב האדם אל האדם»
> Hakhsharat (line 31): «לזעזע קצוות נוצותי׳ בבחירתו»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 158): «הנמשכת ונדבקת ממילא כאשר יומשך הניצוץ אחר השלהבת»
> Tanya ch. 19 (line 286): «נשמתם היא, למשל, כאור הנר שמתנענע תמיד למעלה בטבעו»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 753): «ולא יומשך ההתפעלות בלב עדיין עד אשר יביאו ללב בהדרגה וישוב נכון»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 742): «אדרבה התפעלות זו תוסיף תת כחה בכח בינתו להרחיב ולהעמיק לתכליתו יותר»

## 5. Less full of yourself

**From inside.** A lightness. For a moment you're not holding yourself up. Nothing to defend, nothing to prove. You don't feel small or crushed; you feel *less in the way*. It's quiet, not exciting. If you catch it and think "I'm very humble now", it's gone.

**The source's image.** *Bitul*, self-nullification. The Tanya's picture: the light inside the sun is "truly one thing with the body of the sun, and has no existence of its own at all" [Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 10]. The Mitteler Rebbe, in Imrei Binah: becoming still "like a silent stone, with no feeling of oneself" [Imrei Binah, line 572]. The Piaseczner's peak is the same: "Not delight and not joy do you feel at that moment, only the nullifying of the self" [Chovat HaTalmidim 344].

**What it means.** This is the person's side of the oneness: if there is nothing besides Him, being less full of yourself is simply seeing straight. The later Rebbeim say this, not excitement, is what reaches Him as He is (FOUR-SOURCES.md, "What reaches the Essence").

**What to do.** Don't try to hold it, and never chase it as a prize (see "Real and counterfeit", below). Go on to your own words and the lips (Part I, Stages 8-9).

> **Source.** Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 10 (line 862): «הזיו והאור שם – עצם אחד ממש עם גוף המאור המאיר, ואין לו שום מציאות כלל בפני עצמו»
> Imrei Binah (line 572): «בטל כאבן דומם בהעדר הרגשת עצמו»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 344): «לא עונג ולא שמחה אתה מרגיש בשעה זו רק בטול העצמיות»

## 6. Joy and bitul together

**From inside.** Two things at once that shouldn't fit. A gladness at what you've understood, and a bowing before what you can't. Sometimes it's weeping and joy together: sad at how far you've been, glad at how near He is, in the same breath.

**The source's image.** The Mitteler Rebbe's test for real contemplation of the unity: "As much joy, exactly that much bitul" [Gate of Unity ch. 5]. Either one alone isn't yet the real thing (claims A05). The Tanya quotes the Zohar: "Weeping is lodged in my heart on one side, and joy is lodged in my heart on the other" [Tanya ch. 34]. And Kuntres HaHitpa'alut says the bitter cry of the one who feels far and the joy of the one who feels near are "one" arousal, "only this one comes from the opposing side"; at the very same level the Godly soul rejoices and weeps over its distance from the One [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, lines 662, 666].

**What it means.** The unity shows here in you as a unity of opposites. Neither side cancels the other.

**What to do.** Don't trade one for the other. Say both to Him in your own words.

**Where the sources differ.** The Gate of Unity asks for joy and bitul in *equal* measure. The Piaseczner's peak is "not delight and not joy, only bitul" [Chovat HaTalmidim 344]. Imrei Binah puts bitul alone at the top (FOUR-SOURCES.md). We keep both: the balance is the test along the way; the quieter bitul may be what is left at the very deep end.

> **Source.** Gate of Unity ch. 5 (line 199): «ולפי ערך השמחה כך ערך הביטול ממש»
> Tanya ch. 34 (line 492): «בכיה תקיעא בלבאי מסטרא דא, וחדוה תקיעא בלבאי מסטרא דא»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 662): «ונמצא אחת היא רק שזה מצד המנגד»; (line 666): «ובאותה מדריגה עצמה תתמרמר הנפש האלהית ותבכה מצד ריחוקה מאחד»

## 7. The melody that sings itself: deeper, and less felt

**From inside.** This is hard to describe because you aren't really there to watch it. It's like a tune that started humming in you without your deciding to. It's clearly felt, and yet you aren't feeling *yourself* feel it. Afterward you might not be able to say what happened, only that something did. Sometimes it comes suddenly, a lifting you didn't make.

**The source's image.** The niggun. The Mitteler Rebbe asks how a feeling can be felt in the heart and yet not felt, "two opposites in one subject", and answers with song: its arousal is sudden, "without choice or deliberate will at all", it comes "by itself, without his knowing", felt, and yet he doesn't feel himself in it [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, lines 207, 213, 214]. Then the great rule: "the deeper the arousal of the essence, as in the most inward love, will and delight, the less he feels it" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, lines 555-556]. The Piaseczner meets it from his side: "little by little you will feel that your soul has begun to sing by itself" [Hakhsharat 279]. He also says a person may not know what his soul saw, or whether it saw at all [Derekh HaMelekh 553], and that the highest moments come suddenly: "suddenly he feels lifted above the world" [Derekh HaMelekh 1396].

**What it means.** The deeper it goes, the less there is of "me having an experience". Quiet and hardly-felt may be the deepest moment of all (GUIDE-LAYER.md B06).

**What to do.** Nothing. Don't examine it. When it passes, go on to the lips and the act.

> **Source.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 207): «שזה ב׳ הפכים בנושא א׳»
> (line 213): «ותחלה יש להבין מהות התפעלות הניגון שהוא בחינת התפעלות פתאומית דוקא בלתי בחירה ורצון שכלי כלל»
> (line 214): «כי אם ממילא ומאליו בלתי נודע לו»
> (line 555): «אף על פי שההתפעלות מורגשת מאד בלבו לא נקרא מורגש בעצמו אדרבה העדר הרגשה בעצמית בהתפעלות זו»
> (line 556): «וזהו הכלל גדול דכל שההתפעלות העצמות עמוקה יותר כמו באהבה ורצון ותענוג היותר פנימית, יותר לא ירגיש בה»
> Hakhsharat (line 279): «מעט מעט תרגיש שנפשך התחילה כבר לנגן מעצמה»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 553): «ואף שהוא לא ידע מה ראתה נפשו אף לא ידע אם בכלל ראתה»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 1396): «פתאום מרגיש שהונף והורם מעל לעולם אל היכלי עליון»

## 8. The aftermath

**From inside.** You get up. If it was real, you may see it later rather than now: you're a bit quieter, a bit less touchy. Someone's sharp word doesn't sting the way it would have. There's a little longing left, or a little purity. Sometimes you feel like a different person for the day.

And then, some days later, it's gone. The heart feels shut. This is part of the map too.

**The source's image.** The Piaseczner: whatever the soul saw, "awe and love are added to a person after ascents like these" [Derekh HaMelekh 553]. After the lights are brought in on Friday night, "I find myself another man" [Tzav VeZeruz 75]. The Mitteler Rebbe's sign of the real thing: afterward, "true lowliness" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 828], and an insult doesn't move him, "because he has nothing of his own" [line 829].

And the fall is built in: "a person must always be going up and coming down" [Derekh HaMelekh 704]. "Whoever thinks he never falls from his level, that is a sign he never rose" [Derekh HaMelekh 1549]. When the heart seems sealed, "in truth it is full of holiness even now" [Derekh HaMelekh 1240].

**What it means.** The aftermath is where you find out what the sitting was (see the test, below).

**What to do.** The one act, the sentence to carry, the check later (Part I, "After"). When the fall comes, come back to the same line the next day.

> **Source.** Derekh HaMelekh (line 553): «אבל יראה ואהבה נתוספות לאיש אחרי עליות נשמה כאלו»
> Tzav VeZeruz (line 75): «וכשמביאים את הנרות מוצא אני את עצמי לאיש אחר»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 828): «וסימנו שאחר כך יבוא לכלל שפלות אמיתית»; (line 829): «ואינו מרגיש זה כלל כי נבזה בעיניו נמאס באמת מפני שאין לו מצד עצמו כלום»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 704): «כי תמיד מוכרח האיש להיות בעולה ויורד»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 1549): «ומי שחושב בדעתו שאינו נופל ממדרגתו, סימן הוא שמעולם לא עלה למצב התרוממות»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 1240): «ונדמה לו שלבו אטום. ובאמת גם עתה מלא קדושה הוא»

## Real and counterfeit

Something moved. Was it Him, or was it me? The sources care a great deal about this, and they warn in **both** directions: against false fire, and against killing the fire by doubting every spark.

## The one rule

The Alter Rebbe's whole aim, his son says, was that the chassidim's arousal be "only Godly arousal, and not the arousal of the life of flesh" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 135].

In plain terms the question is not *"Am I moved?"* but **"What moved me?"** If the thing itself moved you (the line, the truth that this cup is being said right now), it's His. If the wish to be moved moved you, it's you (KUNTRES-HAHITPAALUT.md, "What it comes to, for a person").

> **Source.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 135): «שיהיה התפעלות נפשם רק התפעלות אלקות דוקא ולא התפעלות חיי בשר»

## Signs of self-arousal (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut)

1. **You wanted the feeling more than Him.** The lowest kind is where "all he wants is to be moved"; "he doesn't want or aim at Godliness at all" [lines 226, 228]. Its name: "this is called serving oneself" [line 167]. The error is making the arousal the main thing, "and not the Godliness at all" [line 548].
2. **"Aha, I'm warm, I've seen light."** Excited, and straight back to business, without going on to fix it in length, breadth and depth [line 675].
3. **Afterward, you feel more yourself.** The sign of a false "deep" moment: "as soon as it passes, he comes to feel himself, from that very experience" [line 808].
4. **An insult stings more.** The proof: when someone slights him, he is hurt even more [line 813].
5. **You chased bitul as a prize.** The young man wanted self-nullification "because it was the one thing he lacked", and so "it came out the opposite: even at that moment he was mistaken" [line 814].

The good news inside the bad: even the lowest kind has something good hidden in it, a hidden love that wants God under a strange garment [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, lines 229-230]. Nobody is written off.

> **Source.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 228): «כי אינו רוצה ומכוין לבחינת אלקות כלל»
> (line 167): «וזהו הנקרא עובד את עצמו»
> (line 548): «עד שיעשה עיקר מן ההתפעלות ולא מן האלהות כלל»
> (line 675): «יתפעל ויאמר האח חמותי ראיתי אור»
> (line 808): «וסימן לזה שמיד שחלף זה ועבר, בא לכלל הרגשת העצמיות מזה עצמו דוקא»
> (line 813): «והמופת לזה כשיבטשוהו בבזיון (הנקרא שאר) מתפעל ביותר»
> (line 814): «הרי יצא דבר והיפוכו, כי גם באותה השעה מוטעה היה, והיה להיפוך שרצה להגיע למדריגות הביטול כי לא יחסר לו רק זה»

## The Piaseczner: false fire

- **Nerves, not fire.** Excitement with no vessel under it may be "only empty imagination and irritation of the nerves" [Chovat HaTalmidim 324].
- **Fervour made by movement.** Swaying hard, squeezing out words. His test: "Try stopping all your movements" [Hakhsharat 145]. If it all goes quiet and you could chat, it was compulsion.
- **Look at your traits.** "Or is it only a deceptive stirring? Look at your traits" [Chovat HaTalmidim 231].
- **Flame, not coal.** A flame that flares now and then "is not sure to stay"; a coal set in your own self does [Derekh HaMelekh 735].
- **Forcing kills it.** Forcing the picture makes it leave [Mevo HaShe'arim 390]; forcing feeling makes the heart go dead [Hakhsharat 59].

> **Source.** Chovat HaTalmidim (line 324): «לא יהי׳ בטוח שאין זה רק דמיון שוא וגרוי העצבים בלבד»
> Hakhsharat (line 145): «נסה נא לחדול מן כל תנועתיך»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 231): «או רק התרגשות רמוי׳ היא הוי מסתכל במדותיך»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 735): «השלהבת שמתלהב לפעמים אינו בטוח שכבר תישאר בו»

## The Piaseczner and the Tanya: don't kill the fire

- **One in ten.** Some people kill every stirring to test it. "Among ten deceptive fervours you will find one true one" [Bnei Machshava Tova 247].
- **Don't investigate in the moment.** "It is forbidden to investigate his elevation at that moment, because investigating with the intellect spoils the elevation" [Esh Kodesh 541].
- **Doubt is also a temptation.** "This too is the evil inclination's seduction: to doubt and deny what one sees and feels in holiness" [Tzav VeZeruz 84]. "What I felt, I felt" [Tzav VeZeruz 85].
- **Even an "imagined" stirring is real at the core.** For the Jewishness in him, "at that moment it is a true awakening" [Mevo HaShe'arim 144].
- **The Tanya:** even if it seems to him to be imagination, "let him not worry" [Tanya ch. 44].
- **R. Aharon of Strashelye:** let him not worry that his service is mixed with something; "though mixed, it is whole" [Shaarei Avodah, introduction].
- **Feeling worthless is not humility.** "Whoever feels himself empty and vile behaves empty and vile" [Esh Kodesh 541].
- **And the Mitteler Rebbe himself:** "let no one err and forbid or disqualify this arousal" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 743].

> **Source.** Bnei Machshava Tova (line 247): «כי בין עשרה התלהבויות רמיות תמצא אחת אמתית»
> Esh Kodesh (line 541): «ואסור לו לחקור בשעה זו אחר התרוממותו מה לו, כי החקירה בשכל מקלקלת את ההתרוממות»
> Tzav VeZeruz (line 84): «גם זה פתוי היצר הרע הוא להסתפק ולכפור במה שרואים ומרגישים בקודש»; (line 85): «אבל את אשר הרגשתי הרגשתי»
> Mevo HaShe'arim (line 144): «אבל לעצם הישראליות שבו בשעה זו התעוררות אמיתית היא»
> Tanya ch. 44 (line 665): «ואף אם נדמה לו לכאורה, שהוא כח דמיוני – לא יחוש»
> Shaarei Avodah, introduction (line 22): «בל יחוש לעבודתו שהיא מאונה באיזה תערובת כי הגם שהיא מאונה אף על פי כן היא תמימה»
> Esh Kodesh (line 541): «כי מי שמרגיש עצמו לריק ומנוול מתנהג כריק ומנוול»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 743): «ולא יטעה הטועה לאסור ולפסול התפעלות זו»

## The test: the aftermath

Here all the sources meet. **Arouse first; test later; judge by the fruit, not the glow.**

- **Are you a little more your own master?** "Whether I truly rule myself now more than before" [Bnei Machshava Tova 252].
- **Are you the same person?** After an hour of prayer or study, is he still the man he was after idle hours? [Esh Kodesh 525].
- **Did love and awe grow?** "Awe and love are added to a person after ascents like these" [Derekh HaMelekh 553].
- **Lowliness, and an insult that doesn't sting.** The real thing leaves "true lowliness", and he isn't moved by a slight, "because he has nothing of his own" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, lines 828-829]. The false thing leaves him fuller of himself [line 808].
- **Did it reach a deed?** Light holds only in a vessel [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 769]. Fervour that never goes into Torah can turn into strictness, irritation and anger [Likkutei Torah, Metzora].
- **Test only sometimes**, never in the middle of prayer [Hakhsharat 148].

## Where they differ, and when each fits

- **Filter at the door, or let it in and redirect?** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut allows only Godly arousal [line 135]. The Piaseczner says every stirring, even a bodily one, is a key to the soul [Bnei Machshava Tova 100]. He concedes part of the worry: arousal that climbs from the body keeps its marks (PIASECZNO.md §7.5).
- **Warn the person, or reassure them?** The Mitteler Rebbe calls taking imagination for Godly arousal "the error found most among our people" (the craft file, C6.3). The Tanya and R. Aharon say "don't worry" [Tanya ch. 44; Shaarei Avodah]. The Tanya consoles the one who cannot feel; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut warns him (KUNTRES-HAHITPAALUT.md, "Where it differs").
- **When each fits:**
- *During the sitting:* no checking at all, either way. Go back to the line. (All agree.)
- *If you are anxious, scrupulous, or sure you're a fake:* the reassuring voices are for you (one in ten; though mixed, it is whole; don't worry).
- *If you find yourself chasing highs, measuring sittings by intensity, or feeling a bit special:* the Mitteler Rebbe's signs are for you (what moved me; the afterward; the insult).
- *Everyone, later:* the aftermath test.

> **Source.** Bnei Machshava Tova (line 100): «כל התעוררות אפלו אם גופנית, היא מפתח לנפש»

## One sentence to keep

The deeper it goes, the quieter it gets, and the less of "me" is in it; so don't measure a sitting by how much you felt, but by whether, afterward, you are a little more your own master and a little less full of yourself [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 556; Bnei Machshava Tova 252].
