## What this slice adds (one page)

The Mitteler Rebbe writes for people who do not feel. His books say so. Derech Chaim was written because of 'the great poverty and pressure of the hour,' for people who 'cannot free even one hour,' so that 'every man will find rest for his soul according to his place.' Kuntres HaHitpa'alut is a long diagnosis of real versus imitation arousal. What his core works add to 'Go deeper' is a map of the person who is numb, busy, sad, or far, and of the exact ways the texts walk that person down to the point that does not change.

(1) Numbness is sleep, not death (MR1-C01). The rope's top end is tied above. Yom Kippur and Ne'ilah show it: even 'the lightest of the light' weep with no preparation. (2) Sadness and bitterness are different things (MR1-C02, MR1-E2). Sadness 'is not from G-dliness at all, only that his soul is not whole in some completeness that would honor his name.' The 'no hope' is the evil inclination's plan. (3) The root of falling is casting off the yoke, not desire. The fix is one push at a time, and each push counts as teshuvah in deed (C04). (4) Under all earned feeling is a Jewishness 'like physical nature.' A child of six won't break Shabbos, and simple people are often more careful than scholars. Here the highest and lowest souls are equal (C05, C15, E13). (5) The last generation, 'unable to budge the body,' is honestly named. Its plain deed under the yoke is 'what gives it.' Mashiach comes 'only from the power of the deed' (C06, E5). (6) Love with no limit is born from what you cannot bear: the drowning friend, the soldier surrounded. 'Pharaoh brought them close.' Trouble 'is only a cause' that uncovers what was always there (C08, E3). (7) Feeling is the wing, not the flight. Seeking the feeling is a common mistake, and so is banning it (C09). (8) Silence before the King: noise measures distance, and the Amidah is whispered by one too near to speak (C10). The shofar is the stages of grief (C11). (9) Darkness comes before light, and only a source beyond light can turn darkness itself (C12, E6). The candle needs the oil of 'nishtkeit,' or the flame jumps off (C13). (10) Before love there is a 'want of the want,' and it is in every Jew (C14, E1). (11) A father's essential mercy is not used up and doesn't wait to be called (C22, E8). The dirt under the palace floor was in the King's first will (C23, E12). (12) Tears are bread: pain and delight weigh the same, and every tear adds up. Even one mishnah guards you all day (C18, E10).

How to use it with DESCENT (ours). E3 sharpens D8 (what you cannot let go): the unbounded love shows exactly at the point of what you cannot bear. E5 extends D12 (deed): the poorest generation's plain deed is the cornerstone. E7 extends D5 (where knowing ends): acknowledging beyond understanding, where the simple man's thanks to the scholar holds more bittul. E9 extends D13 (plainness): broken vessels are His dwelling. E12 extends D1: the smallest detail is equally willed. E13 extends D7: in simple will every Jew is 'one man.' E1, E2, E4, E6, E8, E10, E11 are new: the want of the want, sadness versus bitterness, the image that stays, darkness that turns, mercy that is Himself, pain and delight as one weight, joy at completion.

Guards (ours, from the texts' own balances). Despair is named as the inclination's tactic, never as humility. Pain over thoughts or missing good 'has no advantage at all.' Feeling is neither the goal nor forbidden. Lowliness is 'nishtkeit' that lets you receive, not 'who am I,' which the texts call false humility. Mesirus nefesh is described as a hidden nature that shows in a test, not as something to seek.

## MR1-C01 · Derech Chaim 95, 144-147; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 10-19; Sha'arei Orah 195; Ateres Rosh 127

**Start (ours).** "I pray and feel nothing. I haven't done anything terrible, but inside I'm empty."

1. **You don't need big sins to feel dead inside. The text says plainly that a person with no grave sins can be as empty as someone who sinned a lot. His prayer comes from his lips while his heart is somewhere else.** (M1) *How:* Text: he pushes back on the man who thinks he is fine because he avoids grave sins. He holds that emptiness is its own kind of distance and needs its own return. «כי גם תפלתו בפיו מהשפה ולחוץ ולבו רחוק לגמרי» — *for even his prayer is in his mouth, from the lips outward, and his heart is wholly far away* · Derech Chaim 145 (OCR)

2. **Usually it comes slowly, from being worn down. Hard work and money worries wear on you until you feel nothing, as if you had never tasted fear of G-d.** (M2) *How:* Text: he names the slow fall that comes from being overloaded. It is not rebellion. The text's own Yiddish word for it is deadness (טוטקייט). «מצד קוצר רוח ועבודה קשה ומרה שמררו חייו» — *from shortness of breath and hard, bitter work that embittered his life* · Derech Chaim 95 (OCR)

3. **But nothing in your real self has died. It is like sleep. A sleeping man isn't dead, and when you wake him he is alive at once, just as before.** (M4) *How:* Text: the sleep parable. Being busy is a covering, not a change in what the soul is. «שהרי כשיקיצנו אותו מיד נעור וחי כמקדם ממש» — *for when one wakes him he is immediately awake and alive exactly as before* · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 10

4. **Your soul is like a rope. The top end is tied above, and the bottom end is in your body. When the top end is pulled, the bottom moves by itself. That is why a person sometimes suddenly feels like coming back, without having prepared at all.** (M3) *How:* Text: the rope mashal (Yaakov chevel nachalaso). It explains why sudden returns happen with no preparation, and why effort from below moves the top end too. «כמשל המניע לחבל בראשו ינוע קצהו התחתון ממילא מאיליו» — *like one who moves a rope at its head — its lower end moves by itself* · Derech Chaim 56 (OCR)

5. **That is why, even after long numbness, a trace is usually left. When a time of favor comes, it wakes.** (M12) *How:* Text: the outcome depends on whether some trace (רושם) is left. He does not promise. He says it can happen, and that it comes from above at a time of favor. «אך יכול להיות שאם רושם יראה נשאר בלבו אז בעת רצון למעלה יעוררוהו ויתעורר» — *but it may be that if a trace of fear remained in his heart, then at a time of favor above they will awaken him and he will awaken* · Derech Chaim 95 (OCR)

6. **And look at what actually happens. On Yom Kippur even the lightest of the light cry, with no preparation and no connection to what they've done all year.** (M15) *How:* Text: Yom Kippur proves the hidden point is there in everyone. The crying does not come from preparation or deserving. «וגם הקלי קלים ההולכים בשרירות לבבם רק רע באין פחד אלקים בלבם כלל יתעוררו בעי"ת בבכי' רבה שלא ע"י הכנה ולא לפי ערך מעשיהם» — *even the lightest of the light, who walk after their hearts' whim, only evil, with no fear of G-d at all — they awaken on Yom Kippur with great weeping, not through preparation and not in proportion to their deeds* · Sha'arei Orah 195

7. **At Ne'ilah, the closing prayer, even someone who has thrown off the whole yoke and is lost in every grave sin weeps and truly returns. It happens at that hour.** (M15) *How:* Text (Ateres Rosh): this is 'seen with the senses.' The great shofar of Ne'ilah reaches even the 'lost and banished.' «שגם הקל שבקלים שנקי אובד ונדת שאיבד א"ע בפר"ע לגמרי» — *that even the lightest of the light, called 'lost and banished,' who destroyed himself entirely by casting off the yoke* · Ateres Rosh 127 (OCR)

8. **So never say it is hopeless. It is the very nature of your G-dly soul to be moved again, each person by his own measure.** (M16) *How:* Text: the instruction that closes the opening section of Hitpa'alut. «לא יאמר נואש לנפשו כי עצם מהות טבעיות נשמתו ורוחו האלקית להתפעל תמיד» — *let him not say 'it is hopeless' to himself, for the very essence and nature of his soul and G-dly spirit is to be moved always* · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 19

**Bottom.** The numbness is sleep, not death. The rope's top end is still tied.

**Return (ours, from the text's instruction).** Keep doing the deeds and don't give up on yourself. Expect to be woken: at Yom Kippur, at a time of trouble, or with no warning at all.

## MR1-C02 · Derech Chaim 58-59, 89-90, 380-381

**Start (ours).** "Every time I look honestly at myself I fall into a pit. I see I'm full of bad, I feel hopeless, and then I get worse."

1. **This is a known cycle. A man sees himself full of bad. Now and then it stirs him and he falls into gloom. He sees no hope, so he goes back to doing worse.** (M1) *How:* Text: he describes the cycle step by step, as something 'many are confused about.' «ויפול בעוצב לפי שעה ואחר רואו שאין לו תקוה חוזר להתקלקל ביותר» — *he falls into sadness for the moment, and after seeing he has no hope he goes back to ruining himself more* · Derech Chaim 89 (OCR)

2. **The text names where it comes from. The voice saying 'no hope' is the evil inclination's plan to knock you down completely.** (M11) *How:* Text: the despair is not humility or honesty. It is a strategy. «ובאמת זהו רק עצת היצר שכונתו להפילו לגמרי» — *in truth this is only the counsel of the evil inclination, whose intent is to fell him entirely* · Derech Chaim 89 (OCR)

3. **Here's the line he draws. Pain over a bad deed you actually did is accepted. Extra pain over thoughts, or over the good you lack, adds nothing. That sadness comes from the body.** (M10) *How:* Text: the comparison between 'more pain is better' and real teshuvah breaks at thoughts and missing good. There, more pain is not more holiness. «אין יתרון הצער ועוצב זה מעלה כלל» — *this pain and sadness has no advantage at all* · Derech Chaim 90 (OCR)

4. **Look at what the sadness is really about. It isn't about G-d. It's that you aren't the complete person you want to be honored as.** (M2) *How:* Text: in the image of the altar's ash, the 'leftover ash' is taken outside the camp. That leftover is sadness born of a hurt ego. «ועצבות זו אינו מצד אלקות כלל רק מאשר אין נפשו שלם באיזה שלימות שיכבד שמו» — *and this sadness is not from G-dliness at all, only that his soul is not whole in some completeness that would honor his name* · Derech Chaim 381 (OCR)

5. **Follow it and you can see where it goes. 'Why bother, I can't do it anyway' turns into coldness, then into giving in on what's permitted, then into what's forbidden. Sadness is the doorstep into the rooms of the other side.** (M11) *How:* Text: he traces the slope step by step. «כי יאמר לנפשו למה עבודה זאת לו אחר שלא יוכל לקבל» — *for he says to himself: why this service for him, since he cannot receive* · Derech Chaim 381 (OCR)

6. **Real bitterness is different. It comes from feeling a lot of G-d, and it makes you hate even the small wrongs you never noticed before. Like fire in a refiner's pot: the more fire, the finer the silver.** (M4) *How:* Text: the 'ash kept beside the altar' (G-dly bitterness) versus the 'leftover ash' (sadness). The mashal is the refiner. «כמו המצרף שנותנים בו אש לצרף הכסף מן הסיגים» — *like the crucible in which fire is put to refine silver from dross* · Derech Chaim 380 (OCR)

7. **Real return holds both. Bitterness at having been far, and great joy at coming back. If there is no joy, it isn't real teshuvah yet. It only lasts until the next test.** (M12) *How:* Text: the two returns ('from evil' and 'to G-d') weigh the same. The joy is the sign that the return is real. «ובאמת כאשר אין שמחה בלבו מאשר כבר נפטר מן הרע... אינו בגדר התשובה אמיתית כלל» — *in truth, when there is no joy in his heart that he is already free of the bad... it is not in the category of true teshuvah at all* · Derech Chaim 58 (OCR)

**Bottom.** Sadness is about me and my honor. Bitterness is about distance from Him.

**Return (ours, from the text's instruction).** Once you notice the pain, go straight back to strengthening yourself. Take on something for the future, and let the joy of coming back carry it.

## MR1-C03 · Derech Chaim 59-66, 126-127

**Start (ours).** "How can I cry over what I did and be happy at the same time? Isn't that two opposite things?"

1. **He asks the question himself: how can the deepest bitterness and the greatest joy go together?** (M1) *How:* Text: he names the problem before he solves it. «איך יתכן שיחוברו ב' הפכים כאלה תכלית המרירות עם תכלית השמחה» — *how can two such opposites join — utmost bitterness with utmost joy* · Derech Chaim 59 (OCR)

2. **Picture a spark and a big torch. Far from the torch, the spark slowly goes out. As it comes closer it grows brighter, until it joins the flame.** (M6) *How:* Text: the spark and torch mashal. Coming closer and lighting up are one movement. «הניצוץ רחוק מאד מן האבוקה יכבה אורו מעט מעט» — *the spark, very far from the torch, its light goes out little by little* · Derech Chaim 63 (OCR)

3. **So every step you walk away from where you were is also a step closer. That's why the verse says he 'walks along weeping.' The walking and the crying are the same thing.** (M7) *How:* Text: 'הלוך ילך ובכה' — every step of leaving is in tears, and every step is nearer. «דמשמע שהליכתו זו בכל שעה היא בבכיה דוקא» — *which means his walking, every moment, is with weeping* · Derech Chaim 64 (OCR)

4. **It is like a son who was far from his father for a long time. He walks crying to find him. When he sees him, he cries and is glad at once.** (M9) *How:* Text: the father-son mashal is the answer to the question. Both feelings belong to one love. «כמשל הבן שנתרחק מאד מאביו בודאי הלך ילך ובכה לבקש את אביו אך בראותו ישמח ויבכה כא'» — *like a son who went very far from his father — surely he walks weeping to seek his father, but on seeing him he rejoices and weeps together* · Derech Chaim 65 (OCR)

5. **And G-d isn't waiting at the top. 'You will find Him' means He finds you where you are, at the bottom step.** (M16) *How:* Text: the torch comes down to the spark. 'שלום שלום לרחוק ולקרוב' means the far one becomes near. «שימצא לך באשר אתה שם בתחתית המדריג'» — *that He will be found for you where you are, at the bottom of the levels* · Derech Chaim 64 (OCR)

6. **Even someone in the lowest pit is taken back while still unclean, if he turns with a broken heart. Menashe was answered through a tunnel dug in heaven.** (M15) *How:* Text: 'Your right hand is stretched out to receive those who return.' «וימינך פשוטה לקבל שבים אע"פ שהן בשאול תחתית» — *and Your right hand is stretched out to receive those who return, though they are in the lowest Sheol* · Derech Chaim 66 (OCR)

7. **In the end the poorest rejoice the most. The joy of someone who was far is bigger, so big his heart can't hold it and he cries for joy, like Yosef when he saw Binyamin.** (M5) *How:* Text: 'ואביוני אדם בקדוש ישראל יגילו.' The joy-crying is measured by the earlier bitter crying. «אך הבע"ת ישמחו בו הרבה יותר אע"פ שמעורבת שמחתם בבכי» — *but the returnees will rejoice in Him much more, though their joy is mixed with weeping* · Derech Chaim 127 (OCR)

**Bottom.** Crying and gladness are one walk toward one Father.

**Return (ours, from the text's instruction).** Walk, even if you cry as you go. The joy is the sign you are getting closer. Don't wait to feel clean before you come.

## MR1-C04 · Derech Chaim 67-83

**Start (ours).** "I keep regretting, fasting, promising — and falling again in the same place."

1. **If you keep regretting and falling back, you haven't touched the root. The verse mocks 'fools who fast over their sins' while still living however they please. Constant regret doesn't fix that.** (M1) *How:* Text: he reads 'מעונותיהם יתענו' as a gentle mockery of fasting without a root. «וע"ז א' רשעים מלאים חרטו' שמתחרט כמה פעמים לפי שתמיד חוזר לקלקולו» — *about this it says 'the wicked are full of regrets' — he regrets many times because he always returns to his corruption* · Derech Chaim 70 (OCR)

2. **The root is casting off the yoke: going after whatever your heart wants, as a matter of course, without stopping to ask if it is against His will.** (M11) *How:* Text: 'פריקת עול' is the root of every sin, and it is worse than the desire itself. «והעיקר על פריקת עמ"ש שהוא שרש לכל החטאים» — *and mainly over casting off the yoke of Heaven, which is the root of all sins* · Derech Chaim 67 (OCR)

3. **Taking the yoke on is very concrete. When something bad comes into your heart, you push it away right then, because you decided that firmly.** (M2) *How:* Text: the yoke here is not a feeling. It is a firm decision to push away the thought at once. «מיד שיבוא בלבו איזה דבר רע ידחנו ויסירנו ע"פ הסכם חזק שבלבו» — *as soon as some bad thing comes into his heart he pushes it away and removes it, by the strong resolve in his heart* · Derech Chaim 70 (OCR)

4. **And each push counts. Every time you push a thought away, that is teshuvah in actual deed, even though all you did was not follow your heart.** (M4) *How:* Text: he reads 'ולא תתורו אחרי לבבכם' as an act. Not going after your heart is the teshuvah of action. He adds a Yiddish line: 'he doesn't let himself turn after what the heart craves.' «ובכל דחי' ודחי' שדוחה מלבו נק' תשובה בפו"מ» — *and every single pushing-away that he pushes from his heart is called teshuvah in actual deed* · Derech Chaim 83 (OCR)

5. **Don't measure it by how you feel. A flare-up of feeling changes from hour to hour. Someone suddenly on fire with teshuvah falls the other way soon after. Plain wholeheartedness doesn't flare, but it is true.** (M10) *How:* Text: here feeling and wholeness part ways. The Yiddish word is 'ערינשטקייט' (earnestness, arousal). «כמו שאנו רואים בכל המתעוררים פתאום וילהב לבבו בתשובה הרי יפול במעט זמן להיפוכו» — *as we see in all who are suddenly aroused and their heart flames in teshuvah — he falls in a short time to the opposite* · Derech Chaim 72 (OCR)

6. **That's why turning in deed has a higher root than turning in will or thought. The end is wedged into the beginning.** (M5) *How:* Text: the order rises from deed, to speech, to will. Deed is highest in root because the end is wedged in the beginning. «שמה שישוב ויעזוב החטא בפו"מ שלא ילך בדרך זו עוד גדול מכולן» — *that he turns and leaves the sin in actual deed, not to walk this way any more — is greatest of all* · Derech Chaim 82 (OCR)

7. **There's a sign it's working. Your heart is in your own hands, and you can drop a thought the moment it comes. 'Let the willful not rule me, then I will be whole.'** (M3) *How:* Text: 'מזדים חשוך עבדך אל ימשלו בי אז איתם.' «כאשר אין הזדים מושלי' בו דהיינו שלבו ברשותו להסיר מחשבתו ולבבו מכל רע תכף» — *when the willful do not rule him — that is, his heart is in his power to remove his thought and heart from all bad immediately* · Derech Chaim 71 (OCR)

**Bottom.** 'Tam' is simple wholeness, a point of will beyond reason. It doesn't flicker.

**Return (ours, from the text's instruction).** Push one thought away at a time, and count each push as a deed of teshuvah. Fasting helps only after the root is fixed.

## MR1-C05 · Derech Chaim 91-98; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 445-478; Sha'arei Orah 148

**Start (ours).** "In prayer I feel real fear of G-d, and an hour later I'm angry, stingy, cutting corners in business. Is my fear fake?"

1. **You aren't the only one. Everyone sees these two opposites in himself every day. In prayer, love and fear come. A moment later you're empty and can't stand up to one bad trait.** (M1) *How:* Text: he states the contradiction as something everyone can see in himself. Then he asks how the fear could be real. «וכל אדם יראה בנפשו ב' הפכים האלה בכל יום ממש» — *and every person will see in his soul these two opposites every single day* · Derech Chaim 92 (OCR)

2. **There are two kinds of fear. The first is earned by effort and contemplation. It rises and falls with your days. The second is like nature, in every Jew, and it never changes.** (M2) *How:* Text: earned fear versus natural fear. Once you see they are two, the contradiction goes away. «הוא אשר כמו טבע בנפש כל א' מישראל ביראת אלקים וביראת חטא בלי שינוי כלל וכלל לעולם ממש» — *that which is like nature in the soul of every one of Israel, in fear of G-d and fear of sin, with no change at all, forever* · Derech Chaim 96 (OCR)

3. **Proof: tell a child of six or seven to break Shabbos or eat something unkosher and he'll risk his life. An orphan with no one to teach him keeps it. Women guard it even more.** (M15) *How:* Text: the proof is that it is not taught. Children, orphans, and women show it is nature, not training. «כמו גם בטף כשיאמרו לתינוק בן ו' וז' שנה שיחלל שבת או שיאכל דבר טמא יסכן נפשו ולא יעבור» — *as even in little ones — when they tell a child of six or seven to desecrate Shabbos or eat something impure, he will risk his life and not transgress* · Derech Chaim 97 (OCR)

4. **Many simple people who are whole in what they do are much more careful than people of learning and Torah.** (M15) *How:* Text: he says it outright, as something 'known.' «ובפרט יש הרבה אנשים פשוטים תמימים במעשיהם שזהירים הרבה יותר מן בעלי דעת ובעלי תורה» — *and especially there are many simple people, whole in their deeds, who are far more careful than people of understanding and people of Torah* · Derech Chaim 97 (OCR)

## MR1-E1 · The want of the want (extends: new)

**Teaching (stated).** Before any love or fear can light, there has to be a small longing to have them, a 'will for a will.' The text says this longing is in every Jew, whoever he is. Without it even calm love goes cold and dead. It is something you can ask for: 'Unite our heart.'

## MR1-E2 · Sadness is about me (extends: new)

**Teaching (stated).** The text separates two things that feel alike. G-dly bitterness comes from feeling a lot of G-d, and it makes a person refuse even small wrongs. Sadness (atzvus) comes from 'his soul not being whole in some completeness that would honor his name.' The first is the ash kept by the altar. The second is the leftover ash carried outside the camp. Sadness leads step by step to 'why bother,' to coldness, to the permitted, to the forbidden. Its message of 'no hope' is the evil inclination's plan.

**Hisbonenus (ours, built on the text's steps).** 1. Take the last time you fell into gloom about yourself. 2. Ask honestly: was the pain about being far from Him, or about not being who I want to be seen as? 3. If it was about my image, the text says it 'is not from G-dliness at all.' 4. Watch where it was leading: 'why this service for me?' 5. Turn at once to strengthening yourself and a decision about tomorrow. That is the instruction.

**Inside (ours).** A quiet sorting: this ache is His, that one is mine. **Lands (ours).** The despair loses its authority. You can feel bitterness without falling.

- «ועצבות זו אינו מצד אלקות כלל רק מאשר אין נפשו שלם באיזה שלימות שיכבד שמו» — *this sadness is not from G-dliness at all, only that his soul is not whole in some completeness that would honor his name* · Derech Chaim 381 (OCR) - «ובאמת זהו רק עצת היצר שכונתו להפילו לגמרי» — *in truth this is only the counsel of the evil inclination, whose intent is to fell him entirely* · Derech Chaim 89 (OCR)

## MR1-E3 · Love born from what you cannot bear (extends: D8)

**Teaching (stated).** There is a love with no limit that doesn't come from reasons. It comes from the opposite, from something you can't bear: a friend drowning, being surrounded with no way out. 'Pharaoh brought them close.' The text says hardship 'is only a cause' that uncovers a love that was always in the soul by nature. Once a person gives up on his own escape, the narrowness turns over, and he walks on calmly, without grief. Like a circle, the lowest point touches the highest.

**Hisbonenus (ours, built on the text's steps).** 1. Recall a narrow place where you had no way out. 2. Notice what rose in you there that you didn't have before. 3. Hear the text: this wasn't created by the trouble, only uncovered. 4. Picture the moment after giving up on your own rescue. Is there a strange calm? 5. **Inside (ours).** Fear turning into steadiness. A love bigger than its occasion. **Lands (ours).** Suffering is not proof He left. It may be the place where the hidden love surfaces.

## MR1-E4 · The image that stays (extends: new)

**Teaching (stated).** All day the soul's powers 'go here and there' after the body's needs and making a living, and still its image stays engraved above, like lovers whose images are cut into each other's hearts. ## MR1-E5 · The deed of a generation with nothing left (extends: D12)

**Teaching (stated).** The last generation is honestly described: learners no better than plain folk, prayers that move no one, souls that can't budge the body. And yet, 'that is exactly what gives it.' What's left is the bare deed under the yoke, and that is 'higher than the beginning of thought.' Mashiach comes only from the strength of that deed. The choice that comes from feeling changes. The yoke doesn't, 'as if it were natural and essential.'

## MR1-E6 · Darkness that turns (extends: new)

**Teaching (stated).** Light pushes darkness away but can't turn it into light. Only a source beyond light can do that, 'He made darkness His hiding place.' So there are two kinds of repair. One overpowers bitterness with sweetness, and the bitterness as such stays bad. The other makes the bad itself, as it is, into good: 'willful sins become merits — the willful sins as they are.' That second repair belongs to teshuvah from the depth, and the prosecutor becomes the defender.

**Hisbonenus (ours, built on the text's steps).** 1. Pick one dark thing in your past you have only pushed away. 2. Hear: pushing away is light's work. It doesn't change the dark. 3. Ask: what would it mean for this very thing to become a reason I come close? 4. Bring it to Him from the depth, not to fix it but to hand it over. 5. Say 'G-d will light up my darkness.' Light it up, not take it away.

**Inside (ours).** The dark thing stops being only a stain. It becomes a door. **Lands (ours).** You no longer need to hide your past from your service.

- «היינו שנדחה החשך מפני האור אבל לא שיהפך החשך עצמו לאור» — *meaning the darkness is pushed away before the light, but not that the darkness itself turns into light* · Sha'arei Orah 397 - «זדונות נעשין לו כזכיות. זדונות כמו שהן» — *willful sins become for him like merits — willful sins as they are* · Sha'arei Orah 383

## MR1-E7 · Acknowledging what you cannot understand (extends: D5)

**Teaching (stated).** Blessing draws down what can be known. Acknowledging (hoda'ah) reaches the Essence that can't be known: 'Your Name is good, and to You it is fitting to give thanks.' It's like admitting your friend is right against your own view, or seeing a miracle with your own eyes and having to admit it. The Jews of Purim are called 'Yehudim,' from hoda'ah, because their self-sacrifice was above reason. **Inside (ours).** A bow without a reason, and quiet. **Lands (ours).** Not understanding becomes a way of being with Him, not a wall.

## MR1-E8 · Mercy that is Himself (extends: new)

**Teaching (stated).** Measured mercy can be used up. Essential mercy is a father's toward a darling child, 'My insides stir for him.' Even if the son sins until he is loathed, it prevails, 'as one always has mercy on himself.' Above patience, which is strength holding anger back and can run out, is 'He does not stir up all His wrath' to begin with. Such a father doesn't wait to be called: 'before they call I will answer,' because 'in his distress he is distressed.'

**Hisbonenus (ours, built on the text's steps).** 1. Think of the worst thing you've done. 2. Picture a father who has every reason to be angry and isn't, because the son is himself. 3. **Inside (ours).** Being held without having earned it. **Lands (ours).** You stop calculating whether you've used up your chances.

- «דגם אם ירבה לפשוע עד שימאס וישנאנו בתכלית יגברו רחמים העצמיי'» — *even if he sins until He loathes and utterly hates him, the essential mercy will prevail* · Imrei Binah 498 - «כי בצרתו לו צר» — *for in his distress He is distressed* · Imrei Binah 127

## MR1-E9 · Broken vessels are His home (extends: D13)

## MR1-E10 · Pain and delight weigh the same (extends: new)

## MR1-E12 · The dirt under the floor was wanted (extends: D1)

## MR1-E13 · One man in the simple will (extends: D7)

**Teaching (stated).** In understanding no two Jews are alike. In the simple point of will they are 'as one man,' like soldiers going to war. That's why at Sinai and on Purim the verbs are singular. ## 3. Avodah

- **Push away one bad thought the moment it comes, and count each push as an act of teshuvah.** «ובכל דחי' ודחי' שדוחה מלבו נק' תשובה בפו"מ» — *every pushing-away from his heart is called teshuvah in actual deed* · Derech Chaim 83 (OCR)
- **When gloom about yourself comes, don't feed it. Turn at once to strengthening yourself and one decision for the future.** «אלא צריך שמיד שיצר לבו יחזור להתחזק בלבו» — *rather, as soon as his heart is pained he must return to strengthen himself in his heart* · Derech Chaim 90 (OCR)
- **Make a strong, specific resolve and trust that G-d sees it and helps. Teshuvah can start without feeling.** «וה' בוחן לב וכליות ודאי יודע היטב מהסכם החזק הטוב עוזר לו» — *G-d, who tests hearts, surely knows the strong good resolve and helps him* · Derech Chaim 134 (OCR)
- **At bedtime Shema, confess specifically and say 'Into Your hand I entrust my spirit.'** «ולזה אומרי' אחר וידוי דק"ש שעהמ"ט פסוק בידך אפקיד רוחי» — *therefore after the confession of bedtime Shema we say the verse 'Into Your hand I entrust my spirit'* · Derech Chaim 137 (OCR)
- **Learn at least one chapter, one mishnah, or one verse every day. It guards you all day.** «אפי' פרק א' ומשנה א' או פסוק א' דק"ש» — *even one chapter, one mishnah, or one verse of Shema* · Derech Chaim 120 (OCR)
- **Give a coin to a poor person before praying.** «וכרבא דיהיב פרוטה לעני והדר מצלי» — *like Rava, who would give a penny to a poor man and then pray* · Sha'arei Orah 61
- **Each day, for a minute, look at the sky or the ground with your eyes and contemplate them coming from nothing.** «כמו שכתוב שאו מרום עיניכם, בראיה גשמיית» — *'lift your eyes on high' — with physical sight* · Gate of Unity 331
- **Whisper the Amidah, standing still. Bow with your body at Modim.** «ולכך כל המשמיע קולו בש"ע ה"ז מקטני אמנה» — *whoever makes his voice heard in the Shemoneh Esrei is of little faith* · Imrei Binah 602
- **Ask for the wanting itself: say 'unite our heart' as a real request.** «וזהו שאנו מבקשים ויחד לבבינו לאהבה וליראה את שמך» — *this is what we ask: unite our heart to love and fear Your Name* · Sha'arei Orah 197
- **After prayer, go to Torah to quench the thirst the prayer woke. Don't rest on the feeling.** «והוא ע"י מים דתורה אחר התפלה» — *and that is through the waters of Torah after prayer* · Derech Chaim 600 (OCR)
- **Do what you are by nature. Don't imitate someone else's level or style.** «רק כל אחד יעשה כפי מה שהוא בעצם (נאטירליך ניט נאך גימאכט)» — *each should do according to what he is in essence — natural, not imitated* · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 893
- **Don't look down on the plain, sudden cry of teshuvah, yours or another's.** «ובזה יש רבים הטועים שמשפילים הצעקה כזאת» — *many err, looking down on such a cry* · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 663
- **Read Chassidus slowly and take it to heart. Don't read it just to say you read it.** «ולא יקרא כקורא בספר לצאת י"ח» — *and he should not read like one reading a book to fulfill an obligation* · Derech Chaim 53 (OCR)
