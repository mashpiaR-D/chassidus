## What a sitting is

A sitting is simple. You take one line of teaching about God's oneness. You think it through slowly, piece by piece, until it comes down into your own kitchen and your own day. You keep bringing your mind back to it. You let your heart answer if it wants to, and you don't push it. Then you say it with your lips, you talk to Him a little in your own words, and you get up and do one thing with it.

That's all. It isn't breathing, it isn't relaxing, and it isn't emptying your head for its own sake. It's the Jewish way of meditating that Chabad calls *hisbonenus*, "looking closely": a strong, steady look at one idea, staying with it a long time until you know it through and through [Mitteler Rebbe, Gate of Unity, opening chapters].

> **Source.** Gate of Unity, opening chapters (line 26): «מהות ההתבוננות בעצם הוא ענין ההסתכלות החזקה בעמקות הענין ולעמוד עליו הרבה»

Why bother? Because the oneness of God can't be grasped the way a fact is grasped. It can only be glimpsed, and the glimpse comes when the mind is tied to it and stays [Tanya ch. 3]. Without that tie, the Tanya says, even a wise person who understands all of it ends up with nothing but daydreams [Tanya ch. 3].

> **Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 3 (line 113): «הנה אם לא יקשר דעתו ויתקע מחשבתו בחוזק ובהתמדה – לא יוליד בנפשו יראה ואהבה אמיתית, כי אם דמיונות שוא»

The sitting has three parts: **before**, **during** and **after**. The "after" matters as much as the "during". A sitting that ends in a good feeling and nothing else hasn't finished [Likkutei Torah, Metzora].

The minutes are **ours**. The sources never count minutes. They say "as long as your mind can and your time allows" and "a long, unbroken time", and that the right length is different for every soul [Tanya ch. 41; ch. 42]. A short sitting that truly stays is better than a long one that only recites (ours, following the Hisbonenus craft file, C2.2).

## 1. Pick a set time

Choose a time and keep it. If you can't be on fire all day, at least set times when God is revealed inside you [Piaseczner, Derekh HaMelekh 1379]. The Alter Rebbe names such times: prayer, "or other times set aside to be alone with your Maker" [Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 12]. Someone busy earning a living has to make free times too [Derekh HaMelekh 688].

The best time the sources know is just before prayer [Kuntres HaTefillah §11; Derekh HaMelekh 83]. If morning prayer isn't your practice, any fixed time works: before you open your phone, at lunch, before bed.

> **Source.** Derekh HaMelekh (line 1379): «עכ"פ יקבע לו זמנים שיתגלה ד' בקרבו»
> Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 12 (line 1162): «או שאר עתים מזומנים להתבודד עם קונו»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 688): «גם כשהוא כבר מוטרד בפרנסה צריך הוא לעשות לו זמנים פנויים»

## 2. Pick a place

A room of your own if you can. If you can't, turn your face to the wall [Piaseczner, Tzav VeZeruz 15]. Stay in one place, by the wall or with your eyes shut [Bnei Machshava Tova 275]. A corner of the kitchen with your face to the wall is enough.

> **Source.** Tzav VeZeruz (line 15): «הסתר את עצמך בחדר מיוחד אם אפשר לך, ואם לאו הסב פניך אל הקיר»
> Bnei Machshava Tova (line 275): «בתפלתך תעמד במקום אחד, אצל הקיר או בסגירת עינים, ותתפלל בקול»

## 3. Pick how long

Choose one of three lengths before you start, and don't feel bad about the short one.

- **3 minutes**: the thread. One line, one detail of it brought into your day, a verse, one act. For a day with no room.
- **10 minutes**: the ordinary sitting. All the stages, lightly.
- **20 minutes** or more: a free morning. Room for the thinking-through to widen and go deep.

Go as deep as your mind can and as long as your time allows [Tanya ch. 41]. How long it takes is not the same for every soul [Tanya ch. 42]. Every moment you stay longer is "like buying life for your soul for the whole day" [Kuntres HaTefillah §16, the Rashab passing on the Mitteler Rebbe's advice to working men]. The Rashab's own standard for experienced people is "about an hour or more"; that is not a demand on a beginner (ours, following the craft file, C2.2).

> **Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 41 (line 601): «ויעמיק במחשבה זו ויאריך בה כפי יכולת השגת מוחו ומחשבתו, וכפי הפנאי שלו»
> Tanya ch. 42 (line 631): «להעמיק ולהתבונן בגדולת ה׳ שעה גדולה רצופה. כי שיעור שעה זו – אינו שוה בכל נפש»
> Kuntres HaTefillah §16, OCR (line 212): «וכל מה שיאריך בתפלה אפילו רגץ יותר הרי זה כקונה חיים לנפשו על כל היום»

## 4. Choose the one line

A sitting has **one** line. Not a topic, not a lesson, one sentence. Knowing, the Mitteler Rebbe says, is "gathering all the power of your mind to bind itself to this idea only" [Gate of Unity, opening chapters].

- **Take it from something you learned**, not something you make up on the spot. The contemplation before prayer is "feeling the life of what you learned" [Hayom Yom]. The Rashab advises thinking over some teaching of Chassidus before prayer [Kuntres HaTefillah §11]. Part II of this manual gives one line for each rung of the ladder; `MEDITATION.md` in the Unity Index gives one for each of the twelve faces ("The word that made this is saying it right now", "I am not a thing on my own", "He is here").
- **Choose it for what you need**, not for which idea sounds deepest. Each person has a particular trait that is theirs to fix [Kuntres HaAvodah]. If you're anxious about money, a line about being given this moment fits better than a line about the worlds above.
- **Keep the same line for days** (see "After", below).
- **Don't switch in the middle.** Halfway through, a better idea will offer itself. That urge, however lofty, is a trick to pull you off the work [Hayom Yom, the Rebbe Maharash to the Rashab].

> **Source.** Gate of Unity, opening chapters (line 68): «שהוא הקיבוץ והאסיפה מכל כח שכלו להתקשר רק במושכל זה»
> Hayom Yom (line 415): «שקודם התפלה, ענינה הרגש חיות הענין שלמד ולא הרגש השכלי»
> Kuntres HaTefillah §11, OCR (line 164): «אחר שלובשים חטו"ת טוב לחשוב קודם התפלה איזה ענין בדא"ח כחצי שעה או יותר»
> Kuntres HaAvodah, OCR (line 388): «ובעבודתו צריך לידץ המדה הפרטית. שטניע לחלקו לתקן»
> Hayom Yom (line 368): «ויש אשר מתעורר חשק גדול ללמוד חסידות או להתבונן היטב באיזה השכלה, ובאמת הנה זהו רק עצת היצר»

## 5. Prepare: a coin, a few words of Torah, and gathering your strength

- **A coin for charity.** "Rabbi Elazar would give a coin to a poor man and then pray, as it says, 'In righteousness I will see Your face'." The Alter Rebbe explains that charity opens the flow of knowing, "to contemplate the greatness of God" [Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 12]. Put a coin in a box, or set it aside, before you sit.
- **A few words of Torah.** Read the line in its setting: a paragraph of Tanya, the page in Part II. The Rashab puts a teaching of Chassidus before prayer [Kuntres HaTefillah §11].
- **Step out of the day.** Before prayer a person must separate from the world and think: I am about to speak with God [Derekh HaMelekh 83].
- **Put your worries down for the hour.** Only someone who can throw off his worries for an hour, at least, can leap above himself [Tzav VeZeruz 64]. They will be there when you get up.
- **Gather your strength.** The Piaseczner's words, to say before you begin: "I gird my loins, and with all my strength I work to strengthen my love, my awe and my thought in God," so the flood of thoughts won't sweep them away [Chovat HaTalmidim 245]. Thoughts are hooked one to another and flow like water, and a good thought that comes without effort gets washed away; that is why he says it (ours, from the Chovat HaTalmidim report on the same line).

> **Source.** Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 12 (line 1162): «רבי אלעזר יהיב פרוטה לעני והדר מצלי, דכתיב: אני בצדק אחזה פניך» … «היא בחינת גילוי הארה והשפעת הדעת והתבונה, להתבונן בגדולת ה׳»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 83): «וצריך להפריש עצמו קודם התפלה מן העולם ולכוון שהוא מתקרב עתה לדבר עם ד'»
> Tzav VeZeruz (line 64): «רק מי שיכול להפקיר ולהשליך את דאגותיו על שעה עכ״פ»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 245): «הפסק קודם כל תפלה ועבודה וחשוב, הנני משנס את מתני ובכל כחי הנני מיגע לחזק את אהבתי יראתי ואת מחשבתי בד׳»

## Stage 1. Arrive from what is already moving

Don't start by trying to feel something. Start from what is already there right now: a worry, a want, a sigh, a small joy. Everything is already in you. Your job is only to get to know yourself and what is happening in you [Piaseczner, Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 87]. Any stirring at all, even a bodily one, is a key to the soul [Bnei Machshava Tova 100]. Take hold of whatever corner of the soul is awake now, even a prayer for your own needs, and don't wait for a holier feeling [Derekh HaMelekh 828].

Start with the clearer, gladder feelings if you can, not with the soul's pains [Hakhsharat 89]. You can borrow a love you already have: think for a moment of how you love your children, or even your money, and turn that love toward Him [Esh Kodesh 213].

*In practice:* sit down, and in one sentence tell yourself where you are. "I'm tired and worried about Thursday." That sentence is the door. You won't solve it now; you'll bring the line into it.

> **Source.** Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim (line 87): «ועליך להשתדל רק להכיר אותך ואת אשר בך מתרחש»
> Bnei Machshava Tova (line 100): «כל התעוררות אפלו אם גופנית, היא מפתח לנפש»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 828): «מוטב שיאחז בקצה הזה של הנפש שמצא עתה»
> Hakhsharat (line 89): «ומוטב שלא תתחיל בהתרגשויות כאבי הנפש»
> Esh Kodesh (line 213): «יזכיר לו אהבתו לבניו אף לממונו»

## Stage 2. Quiet: calmly watch the thoughts, don't push

Now, for a minute or two, just watch your thoughts go by. Ask yourself one question: *what am I thinking now?* Don't fight them and don't follow them. Little by little the stream slows down [Derekh HaMelekh 1556, the student's record of the Piaseczner's *hashkatah*].

Why? The Piaseczner's reason is that a person's own busy self-ness is what blocks what comes from above [Derekh HaMelekh 1555]. The first chassidim, he says, would stop the whole flood of their thoughts and plans for an hour before praying, and then the Godly part in them showed itself without a garment [Derekh HaMelekh 90].

How to watch: stop, and if you can sit, sit; if not, lean on something. Watch "with a restful heart, not with agitation and not with too much effort". At first the thoughts dim, and every moment they thin out more, "until it is as if you swept your mind clean with a broom" [Hakhsharat 310-311].

**This is not the goal.** A blank head is not what we want. The quiet only makes room for the line, and it lasts a minute or two. The Piaseczner's own steps go straight from the quiet to one verse [Derekh HaMelekh 1556]. (See "Where the sources disagree", no. 4.)

## Stage 3. The one line, said softly

Say your line once, softly. The Piaseczner's example is a single verse, *Hashem Elokim Emet*, "the Lord God is truth". Say it gently, **never forcefully**, because force wakes up the ego. Say it as a yes, not a no: "I want to be close to You", not "I must stop being far" [Derekh HaMelekh 1556]. His own Yiddish for it: "I would so much want to be close to the holy Creator."

Say it calmly, and repeat it only while it is still widening. Think of God, not of the words [Hakhsharat 110]. If the line has "You" in it, lean on the word "You", and picture that you are saying it to God who is right in front of you [Chovat HaTalmidim 134].

> **Source.** Derekh HaMelekh (line 1556): «ואז יתחיל לאמר פסוק אחד כגון ד' אלקים אמת» … «אבל לא שיאמר זאת בחזקה» … «אבל לא באופן שלילי רק באופן חיובי»
> Derekh HaMelekh (line 1556): «איך וואלט געוואלט זיין אזוי נאהנט צום הייליגען באשעפער»
> Hakhsharat (line 110): «ובנחת חשוב או אמור זאת, וכפול אותם אם תרגיש שמוסיפים ומרחיבים את יראתך לד׳» … «ובלבד שלא תחשוב את התיבות בלבד שאתה אומר, רק את ד׳»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 134): «תדייק את ה-״אתה״-, ותצייר בדעתך שלד׳ אשר לנגדך אתה אומר»

## Stage 4. Think it through: breadth, length, depth, and down into your life

This is the heart of the sitting, and it should take the most time. The Mitteler Rebbe names three moves [Gate of Unity, opening chapters]:

- **Breadth.** Take the line to every side, in many details, "like a wide river". Say it one way, then another. If the line is "The word that made this is saying it right now", go through things one at a time: the table, the window, the street, your own hand. Each is being said this second.
- **Length.** Bring it down, clothe it in pictures "until a small child could grasp it", until it is "very near to your mind". Bring it into this room and into the thing you named in Stage 1. Not "the world is created every moment" but "Thursday's meeting is being given being right now, and so am I".
- **Depth.** Only after length and breadth does depth come. Depth is the idea's own point, "as it is", what's left when you let the pictures go. Say it in the fewest words, and then be still with it.

Then make it particular: not "everything" but **you, here, now**. The Tanya's contemplation narrows from "His kingship over His people in general" to "over him in particular" [Tanya ch. 41]. The Rashab puts it plainly: "He fills all worlds" said in general only hovers over you, and the excitement it gives is "a false imagination" [Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 6]. Take one real thing. Bring the matter close to yourself [Kuntres HaTefillah §4].

Go slowly. "Reading-through" flows fast without stopping; looking closely is "the pausing, the opposite of speed" [Gate of Unity, opening chapters]. The aim is that the truth becomes as real to you as a thing you're looking at with your eyes [Tanya ch. 42].

Simple and broad beats clever and deep, the Piaseczner says, at least to begin with [Hakhsharat 94].

**It may feel flatter now.** Many people find that the moment they start into the details, it goes cold. That's where the real work starts; don't change course [Kuntres HaAvodah; see Part V].

> (line 34): «ואורך הוא עוצם ירידות ההשכלה להלביש את המושכל במשלים שונים עד להביאו בהשגות התינוק קטן»

## Stage 5. The picture, and where it breaks

Most lines come with a picture (Part II gives one for each rung): the ray inside the sun, the candle flame reaching up, the king who moves into a plain man's house. Use one, or two at most. Let it land. Then **say where it breaks**, every time. The Alter Rebbe does this himself: a parable "only settles the ear; in truth it doesn't resemble the thing at all" [Tanya ch. 42]. A picture you never break becomes a picture of God with a body.

The Piaseczner allows a picture of God for beginners, even though God has no image: stand before His throne "like a son pleading with his father" [Bnei Machshava Tova 91]. It's a handrail, and once your thought is strong it falls away by itself [Bnei Machshava Tova 90].

Two cautions from him. Don't strain for the picture: the harder you try to picture a thing, the more it leaves you [Mevo HaShe'arim 390]. If you can't picture it head-on, come at it sideways, through an ordinary memory you don't care much about, and let the holy thing appear inside it [Mevo HaShe'arim 395].

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 42 (line 637): «רק שמשל זה, אינו אלא לשכך את האזן, אבל באמת, אין המשל דומה לנמשל כלל»
> Bnei Machshava Tova (line 91): «ציר לך שאתה עומד לפני כסא כבודו ואתה מתפלל ומבקש ממנו ית' פשוט כבן שעומד ומתחנן מאביו»
> Bnei Machshava Tova (line 90): «אז התמונה הציור גופני הזה ממילא יתבטלו»
> Mevo HaShe'arim (line 390): «וככל שתרצה להתאמץ יותר לדמות אותם בדמיונך יותר תחדל תמונתם ממך לשעה זו»
> Mevo HaShe'arim (line 395): «רק יתחיל לחשוב מדבר שאין רצונו בו»

## Stage 6. Binding the mind: coming back without arguing

All through the sitting, your mind will wander. That's normal. When you catch it, **come back to the line.** Don't argue with the stray thought, don't answer it, don't even ask why it came. Act as if you didn't hear it, and put a little more strength into what you were doing [Tanya ch. 28]. Don't get sad about it either; a stray thought is not a sign the sitting failed [Tanya ch. 28].

This is what the Tanya means by knowing (*da'at*): binding your mind to the idea "with a strong, firm bond", fixing your thought there and not letting it wander off [Tanya ch. 3].

The Piaseczner agrees you should not fight: the more you try to push thoughts away, the stronger they get [Derekh HaMelekh 1551], and you can't chase away darkness except with light [Chovat 249]. But he offers a second way, for a thought that keeps coming back or a craving that grips you: **look at it instead of in it.** Ask for twenty seconds, "so this is what I'm thinking", as if from the side [Hakhsharat 302]. A thought you look at straight on melts [Mevo HaShe'arim 390]. (See "Where the sources disagree", no. 3, for when to use which.)

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 28 (line 401): «אל ישיב מאומה ושום טענה ומענה נגד המחשבה זרה … רק יעשה עצמו כלא יודע ולא שומע ההרהורים שנפלו לו, ויסירם מדעתו, ויוסיף אומץ בכח כוונתו»

## Stage 7. What it is like from inside: described, never demanded

Somewhere in the sitting your heart may answer. It may be a quiet gladness, a lightness, a homesickness that is really love, a sudden sense of being held. Or nothing may move at all. **Both are fine.**

Don't ask for a feeling and don't check for one. The Mitteler Rebbe's picture of the real thing is a person who "doesn't notice at all whether he is moved, because the aim is not to be moved; the movement happens by itself" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 219]. Forcing yourself to feel gives you, in the Rashab's words, "only empty imagination" [Kuntres HaTefillah §2]. The Piaseczner agrees: don't insist on being stirred [Hakhsharat 59].

If nothing moves, the thinking still counts. Your mind is in your own control, and when you contemplate, love is born by itself, "in the mind, at least" [Tanya ch. 17].

If something does move, don't push it away, and don't make a big thing of it either [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, lines 741, 743]. Just go back to the line and keep thinking it through (Stage 4). Part IV describes these states one by one, in the order they tend to come.

> **Source.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 219): «שאז לא ירגיש כלל אם הוא מתפעל, כי אין הכוונה להתפעל רק שההתפעלות מאליו נעשית»
> Kuntres HaTefillah §2, OCR (line 92): «כ"א שמכריח א"ע להתפעל גם בלבו ואז הוא מתפעל בלב. אבל הוא רק דמיון שוא»
> Hakhsharat (line 59): «אל יתעקש להתעורר דוקא»
> Tanya ch. 17 (line 265): «כי מוחו ברשותו ויכול להתבונן בו ככל אשר יחפוץ, וכשיתבונן בו בגדולת איןסוף ברוךהוא, ממילא יוליד, במוחו על כל פנים»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 741): «יראה שלא יעשה יש ודבר מהתפעלות לבו ולא ירגישנו כלל» and (line 743): «ולא יטעה הטועה לאסור ולפסול התפעלות זו»

## Stage 8. Your own words to Him

Now talk to Him, in whatever language you actually speak. Tell Him what is really weighing on you, and name it. Pour out your heart "as it comes up in your heart, in any language you understand" [Tzav VeZeruz 15]. Speech itself has great power to loosen what is knotted inside [Tzav VeZeruz 15].

If words burst out of you, keep them and use them again another day; they are sparks of your own soul [Bnei Machshava Tova 274]. R. Nachman of Breslov goes further: you can't come to self-nullification except by being alone and pouring out your words between you and your Maker [Likutei Moharan 52].

A minute is enough. Bring the line into it: "You're saying me right now. Then You know about Thursday."

> **Source.** Tzav VeZeruz (line 15): «ושופך לפניו את לבך בשיח ותפילה כפי שעולה על לבך בכל לשון שאתה שומע» … «הדבור בעצמו גדול כחו להוציא את החרצובות מקרב הנפש»
> Bnei Machshava Tova (line 274): «כי דבורים אלו ניצוצי נפשך הם, ממנה נחצבו»
> Likutei Moharan 52 (line 2696): «ואי אפשר לבוא לידי בטול, כי אם על ידי התבודדות»

## Stage 9. The lips: a verse, or the Shema

Thought isn't the end. The vessel that holds what you felt is **speech in words of Torah** [Likkutei Torah, Pekudei]. So say one line out loud, or under your breath: your line's own verse, or "There is nothing besides Him", or "I have set God before me always".

For a Jew, the deepest form is the first verse of the Shema, said the way the Alter Rebbe teaches it [Shulchan Aruch HaRav, Orach Chaim 61]:

1. **Cover your eyes** with your hand, so you don't look at anything that pulls you away.
2. **Say it out loud**, to wake your intention. *Shema Yisrael, Hashem Elokeinu, Hashem Echad.*
3. **Draw out "Echad", "One".** Hold the **ches** a little, long enough to crown Him in the heavens and the earth (the seven heavens and the earth make eight, the number of *ches*). Hold the **dalet** longer, long enough to think that He alone is in His world and rules all four directions.
4. Some tilt the head with the thought: up, down, and to the four sides.
5. Then, quietly, "Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever", and let the world come back, full of Him.

If you feel nothing, say the words anyway. Put yourself into the words and the fervour comes by itself [Mevo HaShe'arim 198]. Even if you speak with your mouth alone, the heart wakes up through it [Esh Kodesh 504].

> **Source.** Likkutei Torah, Pekudei (line 119): «והנה בחי׳ הכלי לאור דרצוא ושוב הוא הדבור בד״ת דוקא»
> Shulchan Aruch HaRav OC 61:5 (line 771): «נוהגין לקרות פסוק ראשון בקול רם כדי לעורר הכונה . ונוהגין לתן ידיהם על פניהם בקריאת פסוק ראשון»
> OC 61:6 (line 772): «וגם לא יחטף בחי"ת אלא יאריך בה קצת, כדי שימליך הקדוש ברוך הוא בשמים ובארץ» … «אבל בדלי"ת צריך להאריך יותר, כדי שעור שיחשב שהקדוש ברוך הוא יחיד בעולמו ומושל בד' רוחות העולם»
> Mevo HaShe'arim (line 198): «האיש יכניס עצמו בהדיבורים ובעבדות, וההתלהבות תבא ממילא»
> Esh Kodesh (line 504): «מ״מ ידבר בפיו בלבד, ועי״ז גם לבו יתעורר»

## Stage 10. A tune, if one comes

This one is optional. If a tune comes, a wordless *niggun* or a line of a psalm, let it. Turn to the wall or close your eyes. At first you sing to wake the soul, and "little by little you will feel that your soul has begun to sing by itself" [Hakhsharat 279]. You don't need a new tune or a good voice; a hum in a whisper is enough, and it is heard on high [Bnei Machshava Tova 245].

Don't make a tune the point. If it isn't there, skip this stage.

> **Source.** Hakhsharat (line 279): «קח לך איזו תנועה של ניגון, תסב את פניך אל הקיר, או רק תסגור את עיניך» … «מעט מעט תרגיש שנפשך התחילה כבר לנגן מעצמה»
> Bnei Machshava Tova (line 245): «כי יש מי שמנגן רק המיה בלחש וקולו נשמע במרום»

## One act

Get up and do one thing with it, today. The running toward God is only a beginning, to wake the soul; the main thing is drawing it down into Torah and mitzvot [Likkutei Torah, Metzora]. When a good thought comes to you, do a mitzvah with it or learn with it, and that way you clothe it in holiness [Hakhsharat 270]. The Alter Rebbe's own close to a deep contemplation is: "I will make Him a dwelling", Torah study "as my time allows" [Tanya ch. 34].

Make it **one** thing, small, with a when: a coin before breakfast, one blessing said slowly, a phone call you've been putting off, ten minutes of learning. The Piaseczner adds: in the high moment, say your resolution out loud, from the heart, because the soul bows to words that come out strongly from the heart [Chovat HaTalmidim 136].

Without a vessel, the light doesn't hold. The Mitteler Rebbe's image for arousal that never reaches a deed is a flame in the air with nothing to burn on [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, lines 769, 778].

> **Source.** Likkutei Torah, Metzora (line 664): «שהרצוא אינו אלא התחלה לעורר הנפש אבל העיקר הוא ההמשכה בכלים רבים דאותיות התורה ובמצות»
> Hakhsharat (line 270): «שכשבא איזה הרהור טוב באיש יעשה בו מצוה או ילמוד בו, ובזה ילבישו בקדושה»
> Tanya ch. 34 (line 487): «אעשה לו משכן ומכון לשבתו, הוא העסק בתלמוד תורה כפי הפנאי שלי»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 136): «כי זאת חקת הנפש, נכנעת היא לפני דבורים היוצאים בחזקה מן הלב»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 769): «להיות ידוע שאין לאור אחיזה ותפיסה רק בכלי»; (line 778): «ואם יאיר אור התפעלות בלב הרי זה כשלהבת הפורח באויר שאין לו קיום»

## Carry one sentence into the day

Take a short piece of the line with you, six words or less. When you think of it during the day, even for a second, the sitting comes back. The Tanya says that when you contemplate at length once a day, a "light contemplation" at any moment afterward brings it back [Tanya ch. 42]. Use the same sentence every time (ours: the craft file, C8.5, C9.6).

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 42 (line 639): «כשיחזור ויתבונן בזה אפילו בהתבוננות קלה, בכל עת ובכל שעה»

## The check, later

Not during the sitting. Later, at a set time, ask one plain question:

**"Am I a little more my own master than before?"** [Bnei Machshava Tova 252]

Test yourself only sometimes, never in the middle of prayer [Hakhsharat 148]. After an hour of prayer or study, the Piaseczner asks: am I the same person I was after the idle hours? [Esh Kodesh 525]. The Rashab's version, a generation earlier, asks whether your particular dominant trait has changed (the Hisbonenus ontology). By the end of a week, ask: did one reaction I would normally have had, not happen? (ours, the craft file, C9.5).

Don't ask it while you're sitting. Examining a high moment while it happens spoils it [Esh Kodesh 541].

> **Source.** Bnei Machshava Tova (line 252): «אם באמת מושל אני על עצמי עתה יותר מאשר לפנים»
> Hakhsharat (line 148): «נסה את הנסיונות לפעמים ורק לפעמים»
> Esh Kodesh (line 525): «אינו אותו האיש של אחר שעות הבטלה»
> Esh Kodesh (line 541): «ואסור לו לחקור בשעה זו אחר התרוממותו מה לו, כי החקירה בשכל מקלקלת את ההתרוממות»

## Repeat the same line across days

Keep the same line for a week. The main thing is habit, getting the mind used to it [Tanya ch. 42], and "habit becomes nature" [Tanya ch. 44]. But repeating is not the same as going deeper. If you just remember the general idea by saying it every day, there is "no life of Godly light in it" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 395]. So each day, go one step further into the same line: a new detail, a new place in your day, a little less explaining and a little more staying (ours, the craft file, C9.2-9.3).

One caution from the Piaseczner: a special device used every single day stops working in the end. He gives his fiery Shema only three times a week [Hakhsharat 436]. The line stays; the special effort can rest.

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 42 (line 644): «אלא העיקר הוא ההרגל, להרגיל דעתו ומחשבתו תמיד»
> Tanya ch. 44 (line 664): «וכשירגיל עצמו כן תמיד הרי ההרגל נעשה טבע»
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (line 395): «וגם אם יזכור הכלל על ידי חזרתו ורגילתו בה כל הימים עם כל זה אין בזה חיות אור אלהי»
> Hakhsharat (line 436): «נכון שלא לאמור זה בכל יום, כנ״ל מהמאו״ש שהתחבולה שמשמשים בה בכל יום לסוף אינה פועלת»

## The body: posture, eyes, voice

The sources give the body a part, in their own ways. Here is what they actually say.

- **Standing or sitting.** Stand in one place [Bnei Machshava Tova 275]. When stray thoughts are wild, stop, and sit if you may, or lean on something [Hakhsharat 310]. Either is fine for a sitting at home.
- **Eyes closed, or face the wall.** By the wall or with eyes shut [Bnei Machshava Tova 275]; face the wall if you have no room of your own [Tzav VeZeruz 15].
- **Or eyes open, on the world.** The Tanya's way of knowing goes through "seeing with eyes of flesh the heavens and the earth and all their hosts", as the King's garments [Tanya ch. 42]. The Piaseczner: "Look at the sky and contemplate; strengthen your thought" [Chovat HaTalmidim 132]. For a line about creation, eyes open on a real thing can be better than eyes shut.
- **Eyes covered for the Shema**, with the hand [Shulchan Aruch HaRav 61:5].
- **Heart above, eyes below.** In prayer, the heart should be turned upward and the eyes downward [Sefer HaArachim, vol. 9].
- **Voice.** The first verse of the Shema is said out loud [Shulchan Aruch HaRav 61:5]. The Piaseczner prays aloud [Bnei Machshava Tova 275], and says the one line softly, never forcefully [Derekh HaMelekh 1556]. A whispered hum is enough [Bnei Machshava Tova 245]. The Chabad thinking-through itself goes on in thought [Tanya ch. 41: "let him go deep in this thought"].
- **The hands.** They put the coin in the box, cover the eyes for the Shema, and do the one act.
- **Effort of the body, when the mind is scattered.** The Piaseczner: picture yourself pushing through a big crowd toward the place where God is, and strain your body a little with it, but keep your mind on "toward God", not on the straining [Chovat HaTalmidim 250]. This is his; Chabad has no such step. (See "Where the sources disagree", no. 2.)

**What is not here.** No watching the breath, no scanning the body, no relaxing, no "noticing sensations", no light you picture flowing through you. The object of a Jewish sitting is always a line of teaching, never a sensation [Gate of Unity, opening chapters: "a strong gaze into the depth of the matter"]. That the sources would rule out mindfulness is our inference from what they say contemplation is (the craft file, N2); the owner's rule for this project makes it firm (claims D16).

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 42 (line 641): «על ידי ראיית עיני בשר בשמים וארץ וכל צבאם»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 132): «הבט נא השמימה והתבונן, אמץ את מחשבתך וחשוב»
> Sefer HaArachim vol. 9, OCR (line 3293): «שבתפלה צריך להיות לבו למעלה ועיניו למטה»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 250): «צייר בדמיונך שרוצה אתה להדחק דרך המון עם רב אל המקום אשר שם האלהים» … «ובלבד שלא תשמש את מוחך להתאמצות והתכווצות גופך»

## Three sample sittings

These use the line of the first rung, "The word that made this is saying it right now" (the Unity Index, MEDITATION.md F01; Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 1). Swap in any line from Part II.

## The 3-minute thread

- Name where you are, in one sentence. - Say the line softly. - Take one thing in front of you, your cup, and think: this is being said into being right now. If the word stopped, it wouldn't fall. It just wouldn't be. And so with me. - Cover your eyes: *Shema Yisrael...*, drawing out *Echad*. (Or: "There is nothing besides Him.") - One act: a coin in the box before you leave. Carry: "being said right now."

## The 10-minute sitting

- **Before:** the coin; read the line in its place; "I gird my loins..."
- **Arrive (30 sec):** "I'm worried about money."
- **Quiet (1 min):** what am I thinking now? Watch; don't push.
- **Line (20 sec):** "The word that made this is saying it right now."
- **Think it through (4 min):** the table; the window; the street; my hand. Each being said this second. Not "was made"; "is being made". Now the money worry: the account, the bills, me sitting here worrying about them, all being said right now. It may go flat here; stay.
- **Picture (1 min):** His word never leaves Him the way my words leave me, so I am never outside Him (Tanya ch. 21). Only a picture; His speech is nothing like ours.
- **Come back** each time you drift, without arguing.
- **Own words (1 min):** "You're saying me now. You're saying the bills too."
- **Lips (1 min):** the Shema, eyes covered, *ches* and *dalet* drawn out.
- **After:** one act (pay one bill today with Him in mind); carry "being said right now"; tonight, the check.

## The 20-minute sitting

The same stages, with more room: two minutes to arrive and quiet; the thinking-through in all three moves (breadth in four or five things, length down into the worry, depth in one short sentence and then a long stillness); two pictures at most, each broken; a longer talk with Him; the Shema; a tune if one comes. After the high point, if there is one, go back once more into a detail before you finish. The Mitteler Rebbe calls cutting it short at the first warmth "the bad sickness that loses everything" [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 737; see Part V].

## For a person of another faith, or unsure about God

The oneness of God is true about everyone and everything, so this sitting is for everyone. The method stays the same: one line, thought through, the mind brought back, one act. Only the clothing changes (the guide's constitution, FOR ANYONE; claims D17).

**A person of another faith.** Use the name for God you use. The truths are His oneness, His presence in everything, and that every person is made in the image of God [claims U01, Toras Shmuel]. The practices are the ones given to all people: faith in the one God, setting Him before you with a verse of Psalms, talking to Him in your own words, kindness, honesty, charity, thanks on waking [claims U02, J01, J04]. Swap the Jewish-only steps:

| Instead of | Use |
| --- | --- |
| The Shema with *Echad* drawn out | "I have set God before me always" (Psalm 16:8), or your own words |
| A mitzvah as the one act | A kindness, or a coin given |
| Modeh Ani | Thanks on waking, in your own words |
| The mezuzah | The line, said at the door |
| "The soul is a part of God above" (said to a Jew) | "You are made in the image of God" |

Setting God before you is a practice the Alter Rebbe calls "a great principle of the Torah": a person doesn't sit and move and act alone at home the way he does before a great king [Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1:5].

**Someone unsure about God, or not believing.** Treat the line as something to think through, not something to sign. Start with what you can check: this moment isn't holding itself in being, and nothing you can see is either. Reason as far as it goes. Where it stops, the sources say, knowing ends and faith begins, and we say plainly that from there on it is an invitation [claims F44]. The practice is the same thinking-through, a kind act, and, if you're willing, a few honest words to Him. If your answer is no, that is your answer.

We never ask anyone to leave their faith or take on what isn't theirs [claims U03].

> **Source.** Shulchan Aruch HaRav OC 1:5 (line 71): «כי אין ישיבת האדם ותנועותיו ועסקיו והוא לבדו בביתו כישיבתו ותנועותיו ועסקיו והוא לפני מלך גדול»

## Where the sources disagree (kept, not smoothed)

**1. Start with the mind, or with the body and feeling?** - *Chabad:* mind first. The line is understood, thought through, and held until it reaches the heart (the Hisbonenus ontology; Tanya ch. 3, 42). - *The Piaseczner, one way:* for most people it's easier to begin by waking the lower soul (feeling, senses, picture) than with reasoning [Chovat HaTalmidim 291]. Mouth first, heart after [Mevo HaShe'arim 198]. - *The Piaseczner, the other way:* the true order is from the body up, but in this weak generation "we must begin the work with thought" [Bnei Machshava Tova 36, 39]. His "thought" there is strong, pictured thought (PIASECZNO.md §9.2). - *When each fits:* if the line opens up when you think about it, go the Chabad way. If you can think it but it stays dry for days, start from what is already moving (Stage 1), use a picture, say the words aloud, and let the thinking follow.

> **Source.** Chovat HaTalmidim (line 291): «יותר יקל לאיש לעורר את עצמו כשיתחיל לעורר את הנפש והרוח»
> Bnei Machshava Tova (line 36): «עבודת המחשבה צריכה להתחיל מלמטה למעלה, מתחלה יטהר את גופו» … (line 39): «לכן צריכים להתחיל את העבודה במחשבה»

**2. Force, or never force?** - *Never force a feeling or a picture:* forced feeling is empty imagination [Kuntres HaTefillah §2]; don't insist on being stirred [Hakhsharat 59]; don't say the line forcefully [Derekh HaMelekh 1556]; straining kills the picture [Mevo HaShe'arim 390]. - *But effort, yes:* some souls find God in their thought "only with difficulty and force" [Tanya ch. 42]. The Piaseczner girds his loins [Chovat 245], pushes through the crowd with his body [Chovat 250], and prays loudly when the heart is shut, "like someone trying to roll the stone off the well of his heart" [Chovat 294]. - *When each fits:* effort goes into staying, coming back, and doing. Never into squeezing out an emotion. If your mind is scattered and dull, more effort (voice, standing, the crowd). If you catch yourself trying to *make* a feeling, less.

> **Source.** Tanya ch. 42 (line 632): «ולא תוכל למצוא במחשבתה האלהות, כי אם בקושי ובחזקה»
> Chovat HaTalmidim (line 294): «כמי שרוצה לגול בחוזק את האבן ממקור לבו ונפשו»

**3. Watch the stray thoughts, or ignore them?** - *Ignore:* act as if you didn't hear, and return [Tanya ch. 28]; the Rebbe adds that even thinking about why the thought came counts as arguing with it (the craft file, C7.2). The Piaseczner too: don't pay attention to ulterior motives, just keep working [Derekh HaMelekh 1550, 1551]. - *Watch:* stop, calm yourself, and look at the thoughts until they thin [Hakhsharat 310-311]; look at a thought and it melts [Mevo 390]. - *When each fits:* ignore the passing ones. Returning is the answer. If one thought keeps coming back, or a craving grips you, stop and look at it for twenty seconds, then return. Both reject fighting.

**4. Get quiet by emptying, or by filling?** - *Chabad:* settle first, then fill the mind with one idea taken in length and breadth (the Hisbonenus diagnostics: "Unsettled: settle first"). - *The Piaseczner's hashkatah:* watch until the head empties, then plant one verse softly [Derekh HaMelekh 1556]. - *This project's rule* bans "emptying the mind" as a technique (the guide's constitution). - *How this manual holds it:* the quiet is only a minute or two, it is done by watching, not by trying to be blank, and it ends in the line. Emptiness is never the goal. (This way of holding them together is ours.)

**5. Silent thought, or aloud?** - Chabad thinking-through is in the mind [Tanya ch. 41]. The Shema's first verse is aloud [Shulchan Aruch HaRav 61:5]. The Piaseczner prays aloud, with the whole body, and hums [Bnei Machshava Tova 245, 275]. - *When each fits:* think the line through silently; say the line, the verse and your own words out loud, even softly.

**6. Is being moved the aim?** - R. Aharon of Strashelye: the main point of contemplation is to be moved in the heart [Shaarei Avodah, introduction]. The Mitteler Rebbe: the aim is not to be moved; it happens by itself [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 219]. Both say never suppress it [Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, line 743]. This manual never asks for a feeling and never pushes one away, and leaves the rest to Part IV.

> **Source.** Shaarei Avodah, introduction (line 21): «שיהיה עיקר ההתבוננות בעבודה בכדי להתפעל בלב דייקא באהבה ויראה»

**7. Is a picture an engine or a handrail?** - The Piaseczner: pure thought without imagination *cannot* move the traits [Derekh HaMelekh 475]. - The Tanya: a parable only settles the ear [Tanya ch. 42], and the Piaseczner himself calls the picture of the Throne a handrail that falls away [Bnei Machshava Tova 90]. - *When each fits:* use a picture when the line stays in the head; break it every time; drop it when the line holds without it. And don't hunt for pictures once a feeling is already there [Hakhsharat 184].

> **Source.** Derekh HaMelekh (line 475): «לכן המחשבה טהורה בעצמה בלא הדמיון אינה יכולה לעורר את המדות»
> Hakhsharat (line 184): «לא תחפש דמיונות ומשלים רק תתעכב ותתבונן בהתרגשותך הקדושה»

## Never

- **No breath-watching, body scan, relaxing or mindfulness framing.** The object is a line of teaching, not a sensation (above).
- **No drink.** The Piaseczner's small, supervised fellowship of chassidim took a little, and he fenced it even there (PIASECZNO.md §3, step 9; §8). This manual does not pass it on, ever.
- **No demand for a feeling, and no promise of one.** Not a state, not a rank, not an experience.
- **No turning a hard time into material.** If someone is in real danger (self-harm, not wanting to be here, abuse, fear of hurting someone, not eating or functioning at all, or grand, sleepless speech about a special mission), the sitting stops and the guide follows its crisis protocol, nothing else (the guide's constitution, SAFETY). Outside real danger, the guide does not send people to a doctor or therapist; it stays with them and brings what Chassidus has (the owner's rule).
