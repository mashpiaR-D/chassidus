# Esh Kodesh: the inner meditative world

**Only One research** · October 5, 2026 · early draft 0.1 · Result [025](../../CONTENTS.md#025) · manuscript 9 of 12 · [Cite](CITATION.bib) · [Quote check](../../verification/catalogue.md#esh-kodesh-the-inner-meditative-world-october-5-2026)

*Text read:* Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Esh Kodesh (Warsaw Ghetto, 1941).txt  
*Range:* whole file, lines 1-620

---
These are the Sabbath and festival talks R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira gave in the Warsaw Ghetto from Rosh Hashanah 1939 to the summer of 1942. He wrote them down, added glosses in 1942-43, and buried them in a milk can with the Ringelblum archive. They were found after the war. It is not a meditation manual. It is a record of what happens to the inner life when a person, and a whole community, is being crushed. It also records what the Rebbe told them to do inside themselves to stay alive as souls. Most talks begin from a question on a verse or a Rashi and move to "what this hints to us in our situation" (לפי מצבנו).

Line numbers refer to the source file. The Hebrew is quoted as it stands in the file, with abbreviations left in.

**What this file does not contain.** Hashkatah (quieting), a worked picturing or imagination exercise, a melody technique, and the group of "bnei machshavah tovah" do not appear. A search of the whole file finds no דמיון, ציור, השקט or ניגון. Melody comes up only as the Talmudic "bring me a musician" (קחו לי מנגן), which he uses to explain that joy has to be aroused (lines 373, 531, 612). Picturing comes up once, in a quotation from the Pardes: a thought you have given a form is stronger, "like a man looking at a drawn picture" (line 379). The front letter of 1943 says his method books (Hachsharat HaAvreichim, Mevo HaShe'arim, Tzav V'Ziruz) were buried with this one (lines 34-45). For the technique, go to those books. Esh Kodesh shows the same inner teaching under catastrophe: what is left of the self, what is left of feeling, and where God is found then.

## 1. What the range is: structure

**Front matter (lines 1-56).** Title page, the 1960 editors' account of finding the manuscript in Warsaw, and the Rebbe's Yiddish and Hebrew notice asking the finder to send his writings to his brother in Tel Aviv. Then his letter of 26 Tevet 5703 (January 1943) asking that his books be printed and studied, and memorial dedications to his mother, wife, son, daughter-in-law, and to his daughter Rechil Yehudis, "snatched from me" in Elul 1942 (line 42).

**5700 (1939-40), lines 57-262.** The first year, during the siege and the first months of occupation. - *Rosh Hashanah (57-68).* The king's son among tormentors cries louder when he senses the king near. Crying out on Rosh Hashanah is a sign that the soul feels God revealed. He also warns that crying can be mere habit. - *Shabbat Teshuvah (69-74).* Return to God, not only from the sin. Those killed for God's name feel no pain because their senses rise into thought. To the degree a sufferer deepens and binds himself to the thought "this cleanses me to come close," the pain lightens. - *Chayei Sarah (75-78).* Too much suffering "salts the meat" beyond use. Sarah died of the shock of the Akedah to show God what Israel cannot bear. - *Toldot (79-82).* God gives before we deserve. There are the "scattered" and the "lost": the lost no longer know themselves. God is the owner of the lost thing and goes looking for us. - *Vayetzei (83-86).* "I" (אני) becomes "I" (אנכי), a word that is written and not only spoken. Engrave on the heart the humility that suffering brings, so it stays after rescue. - *Vayishlach (87-90).* Jacob asks for a blessing beyond mere escape. "You prevailed": even in great trouble you were a prince (שר) inside. - *Vayeshev (91-95).* Silence of the one who has too much to say, and the muteness of the one broken past speech. The mute can still hint. When it gets worse, he forces himself to cry out. - *Miketz (96-99).* "El Shaddai" who says "enough" to the slow chain of natural causes. - *Vayigash (100-102).* The yoke of commandments falls off in destruction. Jacob answered with the Shema, meaning self-sacrifice. - *Vayechi (103-105).* When Torah itself is "closed," the end is near. - *Va'era (106-109).* Grace, then kindness, then mercy. Out of suffering, the service is done "without the breath of life." - *Bo (110-113).* God rejoices when Egypt is struck and Israel is not. - *Beshalach (114-118).* God walks "before them," to their measure. In good times serve with joy. In trouble, serve with a broken heart and pouring out the soul. Rabbi Yose heard the heavenly voice in a ruin. - *Yitro (119-123).* Written "in hiding." Torah was given on the road so that it binds everywhere. - *Mishpatim (124-126).* A covenant to save us whether we are worthy or not. - *Ki Tisa (127-130).* Moses gave his life for sinners too. That is love of the King's son. - *Vayakhel (131-134).* Holiness reaches even the letters' number values and the workdays. - *Pekudei/Shekalim (135-142).* Real revelation happens in doing, in the concealed lower world. God wants to dwell "within each one." - *Vayikra (143-147).* All the sufferings of Israel are offerings, and the call to Moses is a call of love. The angels learned their loud "holy" from Israel's cry out of pain. - *Zachor (148-152).* Amalek "cooled" Israel through admiration for worldly wisdom. See now what that wisdom is. - *Purim (153-154).* Joy on Purim is a law: bring at least a spark of joy into a broken heart. - *HaChodesh (155-160).* One who is commanded draws light beyond his own reach. - *Shabbat HaGadol (161-165).* God is close even when we have no merit. What is part of your essence you understand the way you understand yourself. Let Torah not be foreign to you but your own essence. - *Pesach (166-173).* The teachers stopped when they saw their students shining. Four sons. Every limb and bone will praise. - *Chol HaMoed Pesach (174-176).* God's glory is to take back the sinner, because a Jew is a part of God. "Engraved on the palms": even in hiding you are seen, and "your soul feels" it. - *Seventh day and Isru Chag (177-184).* The souls above suffer and sing with us. Doing a mitzvah pulls the salvation down into the world of action. - *Metzora (185-187).* The plague on the house hid treasure. Say only "something *like* a plague." - *Kedoshim (188-193).* God can grant the lower fear. The higher fear you must climb to yourself. Under suffering, arouse longing to rise. - *Emor (194-200).* Rabbinic decrees become mitzvot in their own right. Kavanot a person makes from his own heart are good kavanot. The more he is nullified, the more his self becomes godly. - *Behar and Bechukotai (201-209).* "I am my beloved's": give yourself, again and again, until His longing wakes for you. Then you grow beyond yourself. Fix the longing of this time in memory for life. - *Nasso (210-215).* The Maggid's method of waking love (see 2.1). A crushed person's essence does not act. Blessing has to reach the body too. - *Shavuot (216-227).* At Sinai God spoke *with* Israel, not only to Himself, so His essence was revealed. We are far from the fathers' deeds, and God joins far things. - *Beha'alotcha (228-232).* In exile there is fear but no room to serve in it. The "reversed nun" is bowing out of suffering. - *Sh'lach (233-234).* Faith that does not look for a natural way out. - *Balak (235-239).* Man was made "by hand," so holiness reaches his body. A Jew falls only to lying down, not to the ground. - *Pinchas (240-248).* The knees give way from brooding speculation (חקירות). Keep the young from "hegyon." The shepherd must enter each one's inner self. - *Ki Teitzei (249-252).* Saying "You" to God in prayer. First the one who prays has to be found in his own words (see 4.2). - *Ki Tavo (253-258).* When suffering has no natural cause, strength and salvation also go beyond nature. - *Nitzavim (259-262).* Forbidden to gather and cry aloud, we pray "from the depths," in a whisper.

**5701 (1940-41), lines 263-434.** The ghetto is sealed (November 1940). - *Rosh Hashanah (263-274).* Even if our return is only from pain, "my voice" is surely mine. Truth is what God wills. Ask God to guarantee the "do good" part we cannot promise. - *Shabbat Teshuvah (275-280).* Giving your strength and soul into study and prayer is the real offering. - *Second day of Sukkot (281-292).* On his son's first yahrzeit. The murdered are the completion of the Akedah. We must return, for their sake too. - *Shemini Atzeret (293-298).* Seen from above, the prophets' rebukes were all song. God unites with Israel in a "small meal" too. - *Bereshit (299-304).* One whose essence has not become Torah is "a box with books in it." Fixed hours of study hold you when you forget. - *Noach (305-313).* Stiff-neckedness as steadiness. Noah needed support; Abraham stood by himself. - *Toldot (314-321).* The Shechinah suffers for the individual, not only the whole. - *Vayishlach (322-325).* When the suffering is past human strength, the load is on God. "Take the load off Me." - *Vayeshev (326-334).* Suffering is a "decree" (chok) beyond understanding. Do not let faith be damaged if it lasts another week. - *HaChodesh (335-344).* God cares more for Israel's honor than His own. The souls leaving in suffering are offered above. - *Shabbat HaGadol (345-354).* Longing draws down light. When a person grows, God is "called great." - *Pesach (355-374).* The individual is a branch of the one soul of Israel. Only by rising into it can he rise to God. Knowing above reason (the "chochmah" point). A student's question draws from the teacher's source. - *Seventh day of Pesach (375-384).* The face is the soul's image. Each time you review, go deeper. Suffering is also a lesson: first of all, faith. - *Beha'alotcha (385-386).* Aaron did not change even when it was bitter. - *Chukat (387-398).* Serve not for reward. That means shrinking your "I" (see 2.6). Suffering sinks a person back into himself. Do not grow used to others' pain. - *Masei (399-404).* Birth pangs of Messiah. Something must be nullified before new light is born. - *Nachamu (405-412).* There is no comfort for the dead except resurrection. - *Eikev (413-422).* Wanting plus pain at the lack draws holiness. His 1943 gloss says there are no longer words even to lament (line 421). - *Re'eh, Shoftim, Ki Tavo (423-434).* Seeing judgment as just removes the hiding. Be whole (תמים) with God and do not chase the future. We no longer feel each blow, "the dead flesh does not feel the knife."

**5702 (1941-42), lines 435-620.** Hunger, typhus, and from July 1942 the deportations. - *Rosh Hashanah (435-451).* Prayer once rose wrapped in the voice of Torah; now it comes from far away. Repentance is a new creation. We cry for bread, and we cry that we must cry for bread. - *Shabbat Shuvah (452-461).* One life outweighs the river's obedience. Each person's return is his own. He observes that this year's prayers had less fire, and says why. "Out of the depths," depth below depth. - *Sukkot, Hoshana Rabbah, Shemini Atzeret (462-473).* Abraham called God "Lord" in the dark. Suffering is an offering. Make God a "small meal" in our smallness. - *Bereshit (474-476).* Guidance through fear sees "with one eye"; through love, with two. - *Toldot (477-484).* After his own recovery from typhus. A person dares not rejoice at his rescue. Give your whole life in thanks, and then you may rejoice. - *Vayishlach (485-492).* Abraham saw the cloud because the mitzvah brought him near God's thought. Spelling out one's needs in prayer brings the kindness down into speech. - *Hanukkah (493-501).* Faith as a light seen within. It must reach self-sacrifice. Doubt comes mostly from fear for oneself. - *Vayigash (502-505).* The inner movement of prayer, from "He" to "You" (see 2.2 and 3.1). - *Shemot (506-513).* Constant fear of sin gives uplift and joy. "I am nothing now; from now on I will be." - *Va'era (514-521).* The ash of offerings. We learn what we had only when it is gone. Heaviness and cold faith (Amalek), and how distance wakes longing and song. - *Bo (522-528).* Each person knows God "by what he measures in his heart." Look inside after every hour of study and prayer (see 2.5). - *Beshalach (529-534).* To sing you need joy from inside yourself. When the person is shattered, "no personality is left" to strengthen. - *Yitro (535-543).* Shabbat is "the soul returning into itself." Do not analyze moments of elevation. Feel yourself a chasid, not trash. "Knowledge of God is in knowledge of oneself." - *Mishpatim-Shekalim (544-552).* God's infinite grief is hidden in the inner chambers. Through Torah one enters there. The man who has risen hears the voice of Torah in birdsong and lowing cattle. - *Zachor (553-563).* Old words of comfort wear out. Hope alone is fragile: add "He is God, He will do what is good in His eyes." The new task is to overpower one's own collapse. - *Purim and Parah (564-575).* Pour out your whole self. Enter God's will as one enters a mikveh, with all of you. - *HaChodesh (576-588).* Faith is "the soul's seeing." Inherited faith and love are unknown to you until you add to them by choice. In the inner chambers God weeps, and one who weeps *with* Him is strengthened. - *Rosh Chodesh Nisan (589-594).* On his father's yahrzeit. The tzaddikim in their graves grieve for Israel's bodily suffering too. - *Chukat (595-606).* Miriam's service welled up from herself. "There is nothing but Him." The children's cry "ratevet!" is the cry of all our souls to God. - *Matot (607-616).* "What do you know?" at the end of all attainment. The holy spirit cannot come in sadness. God suffers with us, and some suffering we bear *for* Him. - *Shabbat Chazon (617-620).* Hearing about suffering is nothing like seeing it. God "saw," and so He "knew the pains," not the good they would bring.

## 2. The method: every concrete instruction for inner practice

There is no graded exercise here. What there is: short, repeated instructions for what to do inside, many of them addressed to people who were starving. In order of the inner process:

**2.1 Waking love before prayer (Nasso, line 213).** Quoting the Maggid through the Seer of Lublin. Before praying, remind yourself of your love for your children, "even for your money." Once that love is stirring, recall God's greatness and His good to you, and the love turns toward Him. > שכשרוצה האיש לעורר בקרבו אהבה לד׳ קודם התפילה יזכיר לו אהבתו לבניו אף לממונו (line 213)

Order: an ordinary love first, then the thought of God, then the transfer. It is a technique of borrowing a real feeling you already have.

**2.2 Approaching prayer (Vayigash, lines 504-505).** Do not pray by chance or by habit. Before praying, "give heart" that prayer is cleaving to Him, the way one approaches a king. > רק גש ומתקרב אליו, וקודם התפלה נותן לב שהתפלה היא התדבקות אליו ית׳ (line 505)

If the heart is not there, start with the mouth: "first in your mouth and then in your heart." > כתיב מקודם בפיך ואח״כ בלבבך (line 504) > מ״מ ידבר בפיו בלבד, ועי״ז גם לבו יתעורר (line 504)

**2.3 Put yourself into the words (Ki Teitzei, line 251).** Before prayer can make God "You," the one praying must be revealed in his own words, "his self found in the words of his prayer." Then, as water mirrors a face, God becomes present. This holds even for prayer "from the walls of the heart" out of distress. > צריך האיש המדבר בתפילה להתגלה מקודם בדיבורי תפילתו, היינו שעצמותו ימצא בהדיבורים של תפילתו, ואז כמים הפנים אל פנים פועל גם בחי׳ התגלותו ית׳ שיהא נוכחו (line 251)

**2.4 Spell out the details (Vayishlach 5702, lines 490-492).** In prayer, name your specific needs (healing, livelihood) with the heart's will. That pulls the hidden kindness down into speech and then into action. Jacob named every harm Esau could do.

**2.5 Look inside after every hour (Bo 5702, line 525).** After every session of study or prayer, check whether you came a little closer to the "secret," your own personal perception of God. Besides the check every few months or years, check every hour: are you not the same man you are after idle hours? > צריך להביט בקרבו אם התקרב מעט עכ״פ אל בחי׳ הסוד (line 525) > אינו אותו האיש של אחר שעות הבטלה (line 525) The failure he names is not feeling whether one came closer: שלא הרגישו אם נתקרבו אליו ית׳ על ידה (line 525).

**2.6 Shrink the "I," for hours and days at a time (Chukat 5701, line 390).** Whoever wants to rise must lessen his feeling of himself. Train yourself so that even your feelings in service are for God's sake. It depends on how much you shrink your "anochiyut," your I-ness. Everyone can train at this, "if not always, then for hours and days." The main means is Torah study aloud and doing mitzvot with the body, so that "the body becomes Torah." > צריך להקטין את הרגשת עצמותו (line 390) > ותלוי׳ זאת במדה שמקטין את האנכיות שלו (line 390) > ואם לא על תמיד עכ"פ לשעות ולימים יכול לעבוד ולהרגיל א״ע בעבודה טהורה זו (line 390)

**2.7 Deepen and bind yourself to one thought (Shabbat Teshuvah 5700, lines 72-73).** This is the closest thing in the book to a contemplation. The martyr lifts all his senses into the world of thought until he is "clothed entirely in thought," and feels only delight. The ordinary sufferer can take a share of this. Hold the thought that the suffering cleanses you so you can come near God. To the degree you deepen and bind yourself to that thought, the pain lightens, "until our feelings are nullified." > הוא מעלה את כל חושיו וכר׳ אל עולם המחשבה עד שמתלבש כולו במחשבה (line 72) > אז במדה שמתעמק ומתקשר יותר במחשבה זו, יותר קושי היסורים נעשים קלים (line 73) > עד שהרגשותינו שלנו מתבטלות (line 73)

**2.8 Engrave it (lines 86, 203-204).** What a person feels in the crisis (humility, fear of God, longing to serve Him) must be deliberately engraved and fixed "for all the days of our lives," the way maror was eaten in Egypt to be remembered later. > צריכים לחקוק את הדברים בלבנו שתשאר ההכנעה והיראה מלפניו ית׳ (line 86)

**2.9 Give yourself again and again (Behar, line 202).** Even if you do not yet long to give yourself to God, give and give again until the longing wakes. > מ״מ יתן ויחזור ויתן עד שגם תשוקתו תתעורר ליתן א״ע אליו ית׳ (line 202)

**2.10 Joy as a law (Purim 5700, line 153; Beshalach 5702, lines 531-532).** Even crushed, "bring at least a spark of joy into the heart." > חוק הוא שצריך עכ״פ איזה ניצוץ של שמחה להכניס אל לבו (line 153) Song must come from inside: לזמר צריכים שמתוך נפשו ולבו יזמר מעצמו (line 531). To get there, Elisha "called for a musician" (line 531). In suffering, David found something to sing about: it could have been worse. "A great rule for us": rejoice in that (line 532). The same line admits this fails when the whole person is shattered.

**2.11 Pray in a whisper, from the depths (Nitzavim 5700, lines 260-261).** Normally one prays aloud, since "voice awakens intention" (line 260). When forbidden to cry out, cry inside: "only from the depths of my heart and not in a loud voice" (רק ממעמקי לבי ולא בקול גדול, line 261). Offer the pain of not being allowed to pray as its own plea.

**2.12 Keep to set hours (Bereshit 5701, lines 302-303).** Fixed times for study hold a person even when he forgets or is blocked: הזמן מעוררו (line 302). Now that there is more idle time, add more sessions.

**2.13 Go deeper at each review (line 381).** When you repeat your learning, go deeper each time and learn something new, so that God is teaching you now too. > לכן גם כשהאיש חוזר על למודו צריך שבכל פעם יתעמק יותר וילמוד בכל פעם חדשות מהתורה (line 381)

**2.14 Stop brooding (Pinchas, line 245; Shoftim 5701, line 432).** The main work is to strengthen faith "and push away the speculations and the thoughts" about how long and whether we can bear it. > לכן בעיקר צריכים לחזק את האמונה ולרחק את החקירות ואת המחשבות (line 245) Be whole with God and do not chase the future (lines 427-432).

**2.15 Hope and surrender together (Zachor 5702, line 557).** Hope for rescue at every moment, and also say Eli's words, "He is God; He will do what is good in His eyes." Hope alone does not soften the bitterness. Bowing the head does. > אז את ההרגשה המרה של רעת וכאבי היסורים, הוא מפיג ומחליש (line 557)

**2.16 Do not let your compassion get used to it (Chukat 5701, line 393).** "We must not grow used to Israel's troubles." Wake compassion, and let it be felt "in all his essence," in heart and body, not only known in the mind. > צריכים שלא להתרגל בצרות ישראל (line 393)

**2.17 Overpower yourself, now over your own collapse (Zachor 5702, line 561).** There was always the work of overpowering desire. Now there is a new work, overpowering "our fall and broken spirit."

## 3. The inner experience: what it is like inside

**3.1 The contraction toward Him (line 504).** This is the single most direct description of the felt state in the file. At certain places in prayer a person feels "as if he has contracted, all of him, to one place toward Him, and is drawn there entirely now." Or he feels only a will to be drawn, and out of that longing feels himself drawn. "That too is cleaving, at least for that moment." > רק באיזה מקומות בתפילה יש שמרגיש איך כאילו נתכווץ כלו אל מקום אחד אליו ית׳ ולשם נמש כלו עתה (line 504) > אז גם זו היא דבקות עכ״פ ברגע זו (line 504)

**3.2 The rhythm of "You" and "He" (line 505).** Judah's speech shifts between third and second person, and so does prayer. Sometimes one cannot say "You" at the start. In the middle "come moments of revelation and nearness, 'You,'" then it hides again, then "You" again. In the end one arrives at "You." > גם אז כשמתפלל באים לו באמצע תפילתו רגעים של התגלות והתקרבות בחי׳ נוכח (line 505)

**3.3 Elevation you cannot name (line 541).** "Every Jew sometimes feels an elevation," in prayer, on Shabbat, at other exalted times, "and in his intellect does not know what is with him now." It is a spark of the light that rests above him, entering him. > וכל איש ישראלי מרגיש לפעמים התרוממות (line 541)

**3.4 Knowing above reason (lines 364-365).** Sometimes knowledge comes to a person "without reason, only that it seems to him that it is so." Later he finds the reasons, or never finds them and still knows. That is the chochmah point, the "flash of the soul" (כח־מה הארת נפשנו, line 364), which the Maggid calls "the ancient root of the mind." > לכן יש שבאה איזה ידיעה לאיש בלא שכל רק שנראה לו שכך הוא (line 365)

**3.5 Faith as the soul seeing (lines 577-578; 495).** Faith is not believing because one does not know. It is "the knowing and seeing of the soul": the soul sees a little of His greatness, and so a person is surer of it than of anything reasoned. It is like a prophet's sight, but inward and unseen. > אך הוא ענין ידיעת וראית הנפש (line 577) When sad or broken one may not feel it, "yet he believes then too, only he does not feel it." > מאמין הוא גם אז רק שאינו מרגישה (line 578)

**3.6 What is inherited is unfelt until you add to it (lines 579-586).** His image is the body. You do not know your stomach, heart and lungs, which work without your will. You know your hands because you move them. So too the love and faith inherited from the fathers work in the soul unfelt. They are revealed and felt only when you add to them by your own choice. > וכשמוסיף עליהן מתגלות לו ומרגיש אותן (line 582)

**3.7 Each knows God by the measure of his heart (lines 524, 526).** From the Zohar: God "is known and cleaved to according to what one measures in his heart." A person's fear, love and faith are themselves kinds of revelation. He does not see visions like a prophet, but "feels what his soul was shown," like Daniel's companions who did not see and yet trembled. > דאיהו אתידע ואתדבק לפום מה דמשער בלבי' (line 524) > רק היראה ממנו והאהבה והאמונה בו ית׳ ממראות האלקים שנתגלה לנפשו מרגיש (line 524) Each person's share of perception is a "secret" that cannot be taught to another. > וכ"א מה שמשיג מאלקותו ית׳ ואינו יכול (להסביר) להבין לזולתו נקרא סוד (line 251)

**3.8 The uplift after fear (line 511).** Constant fear of sinning feels, while it lasts, like worry. "Yet after such times of thought he feels an uplift and also joy." He calls it "the sweetness of Your fear." > מ״מ אחרי זמני מחשבה כאלו מרגיש התרוממות וגם שמחה (line 511)

**3.9 Rest as return inward (line 540).** Shabbat rest, "in human terms," is "the soul returning into itself." On weekdays holiness rests on a person from outside; on Shabbat it is inside him. This is the nearest the book comes to quieting. > ומה היא מנוחה במשל האדם, השבת הנפש אל קרבו (line 540)

**3.10 Hearing the one voice (line 551).** One who rises and unites with the oneness of God's voice in the Torah hears it in everything, "from the chirping of birds and the lowing of cows and the noise of people." > מציפצופי העופות ומגעיות הפרות ומקולות ורעש בני האדם, מכלם שומע קול ד׳ שבתורה (line 551)

**3.11 Distance wakes song (line 520).** In the Song of Songs the beloved withdraws and the soul goes out after him. Distance wakes a greater longing and song, as a father and son love more when apart. > היינו שע״י ההתרחקות נתעוררה השירה שמתעוררת בתשוקה יתירה (line 520)

**3.12 Weeping with God in the inner rooms (lines 584-585).** God weeps "in the inner chambers." A man wonders at himself: I am broken, always weeping, how can I learn and even say new Torah? Is it hard-heartedness? No. Whoever pushes in through Torah "weeps there with God and also learns Torah with Him." Weeping alone breaks a person. Weeping together with God holds him up. > ושם בוכה ומילל עמו כביכול יחד (line 585)

**3.13 What catastrophe does to the inner life.** These are the states he describes most exactly. - *Losing oneself.* "The man himself loses himself" (he adds in Yiddish, *er ferlirt zikh*). He does not recognize himself, cannot remember how he felt before prayer a year ago, and does not feel whether he is a Jew, a man or a beast. > עד שגם האיש בעצמו אובד א״ע [ער פערלירט זיך] וא״ע אינו מכיר (line 81) - *Muteness.* Not the silence of one who has much to say but the muteness of one with "no mind and no heart with which to grasp or feel" (אין זאת חרישה רק אלמות כאלם שאין לו הדיבור, line 93). - *Numbness.* "Only trampling do we feel in all our being; the world is dark for us." Dead flesh does not feel the knife. > רק רמיסה בכל עצמותנו מרגישים אנו, העולם חשוך בעדנו (line 434) - *Stone.* Cold faith starts as a heaviness in which "he becomes entirely like a stone, in dullness of heart and mind" (וכלו נעשה כאבן בטמטום הלב והמוח, line 519). - *No one left to rejoice.* When a person is broken to pieces, even if he believes, "there is no longer a man who can rejoice" (אין כבר איש אשר ישמח, line 430). "No personality remains" to be strengthened (ולא נשאר ממנו אישיות אשר יתחזק, line 532). - *Worn-out words.* The comforter too grows tired. "The words have grown old," and they no longer work on him or on his listeners (line 556). - *1943.* In his gloss: now there are "no words with which to lament," "no heart to wake" (line 421).

**3.14 The heart that stands anyway.** Against this, the other pole. "Even then you were a prince inside" (היית בקרבך שר, line 90). "Depth within depth, still I strengthen myself and call You again" (line 461). "Your soul feels that even then you are engraved and seen" on God's palms (line 176).

## 4. The self and God

The owner's thesis, that in finding one's true self one finds God, is stated here almost word for word, and then made more complicated. The Rebbe holds two things at once, and both should be kept.

**4.1 He says it outright (Yitro 5702, line 542).** "It is known from the Rambam, and the kabbalists cite it, that the knowledge of God is in knowledge of self" (the Hebrew can be read as His self or one's own). His point is about the one who feels he is a chasid: that very knowledge is a part of God's knowledge in him. > ונודע מהרמב״ם ז״ל ומביאים אותו המקובלים שידיעת ד׳ היא בידיעת עצמו (line 542) > שגם הדעת עצמו, שבו מרגיש שהוא חסיד חלק דעת אלקים הוא (line 542) Strictly, the Rambam's phrase is about God knowing everything by knowing Himself. The Rebbe applies it the other way, to the inner knowing by which a person knows himself as God's. So a person's true self-knowledge is a share of God's knowing. The suffering, he says in the next line, is there "to pound out the human knowing" so that "the knowledge of God can be revealed in the inwardness of each one" (line 543).

**4.2 The self must first be found in the words (line 251).** In prayer the order is explicit. First the person is revealed, "his self is found in the words of his prayer." Then, "as water reflects the face," God becomes "You." Finding yourself comes first, and it is what makes God present. > היינו שעצמותו ימצא בהדיבורים של תפילתו, ואז כמים הפנים אל פנים פועל גם בחי׳ התגלותו ית׳ שיהא נוכחו (line 251)

**4.3 Religion must become your essence, not foreign to you (lines 164-165).** "A man's intellect is bound up with his essence; each understands according to what he is in essence." Things foreign to you, you understand by reasons. "Things that are a man's essence he grasps simply, as he grasps himself." So: let the service of God "not be foreign to you but your own essence." > והדברים שהם עצם האיש מבין ומשיג האיש אותם בפשטות כמו שמשיג את עצמו (line 165) > שלא תהא עבודת ד׳ התורה ומצוות זרה לכם רק עצם שלכם תהי' (line 165) Again: one who studies and whose essence is not changed "is only a box with books in it" (line 301). "The God that is mine, that I will serve with my very self" (האל שלי שגם בעצמותי אעבדהו, line 213).

**4.4 Your own feeling of who you are matters (line 541).** Pride is bad, but a person must not feel himself "empty and vile," for one who does "behaves as empty and vile." He must feel himself "a Jew, a chasid, a servant of God." That self-feeling is itself a spark of the light above him. Even struck, he is "a captive king's son." > כי מי שמרגיש עצמו לריק ומנוול מתנהג כריק ומנוול (line 541) > צריך האיש להרגיש עצמו לישראל, לחסיד, ולעובד ד' (line 541)

**4.5 He grows beyond himself when God longs for him (line 207).** When a person gives himself again and again, God's longing for him wakes. "I grow more than my own self, because He longs for me with a longing greater than I, and I am made greater above my own essence." > רק גם אני מתגדל יותר מעצמותי, כיון שאותי הוא ית׳ משתוקק בתשוקה הגדולה ממני, ונתגדל אני למעלה מהעצם שלי (line 207) The movement also runs the other way. When the person grows, God "is called great" (line 351).

**4.6 The more nullified, the more godly the self (line 199).** A person who cannot do Lurianic kavanot, "who aims from his heart and soul to God as he knows and wills — those are good kavanot." And "if he is more nullified to Him, his self becomes more godly." > כשמכוון מלבו ונפשו לד׳ כפי דעתו ורצונו כוונות טובות הן (line 199) > אם נתבטל אליו ית׳ יותר אז נעשה עצמותו יותר אלוקית (line 199)

**4.7 The counterweight: shrink the "I."** The same book says, just as firmly, that the feeling of self is the obstacle. "Shrink the feeling of your self," "shrink your I-ness" (line 390). The one who "thinks himself something, in his own mind, is outside" the mikveh of God (line 571). The more a person loves himself, the less he loves Israel (line 400). Doubt in the ghetto came mostly from "his self and his sufferings touching him more," and the cure is "to give over his soul, his self and his self-interest" (line 498).

**4.8 How the two fit.** In this book the "self" (עצמות) is double. There is the self as I-ness, the self-feeling that sinks into "this hurts me, that I lack" (line 391). There is the self as essence, the soul whose own feeling is "a Jew, a chasid," which prays "from its inwardness" and whose words must carry it. The first has to be made small. The second has to be found, and when it is found it turns out to be already God's. "The inwardness of his soul does and speaks to God" even when he seems to be asking only for bread (פנימיות נפשו לד׳ עושה ומדברת, line 604). The children crying "ratevet!" in the street are "the cry of their soul and the cry of all our souls to God" (line 604). There is one more step. The individual self is a branch of the one soul of Israel, and "until he rises from the separateness of his self to the soul of Israel he cannot rise further to God" (line 358). So finding oneself, for him, runs past oneself into the community and into God. The owner's thesis holds if "self" means essence and not I-ness. The Rebbe insists on that distinction, and a guide should keep it.

**4.9 God looks for the lost self (lines 81-82).** When a person has lost himself so badly that he does not know himself, God is "the owner of the lost thing" who goes out to find him. At the point where a person cannot find himself, the book says God is the one searching. > בעל האבדה יחזור עלינו למצוא אותנו (line 82)

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

**5.1 Do not analyze the elevation (line 541).** When you feel uplifted and do not know why, "it is forbidden to investigate at that moment what is with him, for investigating with the intellect spoils the elevation." > ואסור לו לחקור בשעה זו אחר התרוממותו מה לו, כי החקירה בשכל מקלקלת את ההתרוממות (line 541)

**5.2 Habit counterfeits prayer (lines 63, 67).** The accusers say our crying and repentance are "all only habit": we are used to asking, used to saying words of repentance, without really recognizing God's kingship. The test: whether the prayer comes "with extra arousal and with noise" (line 63), and whether a person who looks inside himself makes every small sin large (line 61). > אבל הכל רק רגילות, נתרגלנו שצריכים לבקש דברי תחנונים (line 67)

**5.3 Crying from pain versus crying from recognition (lines 62-65, 264-265).** The king's son cries "because it hurts." That is not yet recognizing the father. Yet the Rebbe also defends pain-crying: even if our repentance comes only from pain, "my voice, from my many troubles, that is surely mine" (line 265). He does not dismiss the lower cry. He asks for it to rise: "we cry from the narrow place," and we pray that God will "answer in the wide place," that we may pray higher prayers (lines 450-451).

**5.4 Self-sunk service (line 391).** Suffering gives atonement, "but here is the trouble": a person gets used to feeling himself more. All day he is sunk in "this hurts me and that I lack," and his service becomes service for himself again. > כי בכל היום שקוע בעצמו זה כואב לו וזה חסר לו (line 391) It is not a sin but a sinking. He names it so it can be seen.

**5.5 Brooding dressed as thought (lines 245-247).** The knees buckle not from the suffering but from the speculations about it: how long, can we bear it. Keep the young from "hegyon," this kind of reasoning about the end.

**5.6 Hope alone (line 557).** Leaning everything on one expected rescue, or on some promise or natural sign, breaks a person when it does not come ("hope deferred sickens the heart"). The genuine thing is hope together with "He is God."

**5.7 Doubt that calls itself compassion (line 498).** A man says his faith is shaken because he sees others tortured. "The truth is" that he does grieve for them, "but in his innermost self" the extra shock is fear that he will come to the same state. The cure is to hand over one's self.

**5.8 Complaint as prayer versus complaint as a question (line 496).** "Why have You abandoned us?" said "in the language of prayer and pleading, pouring out the heart before God — it is good." The same words as a challenge, or as a hidden crack in faith, are dangerous. > והנה אם מדבר איש הישראלי זאת בלשון תפילה ותחנונים, בשפיכת לבו לפני ד׳, טובה היא (line 496)

**5.9 Is my strength just hard-heartedness? (line 585).** The one who can still learn while weeping suspects himself: האין זאת רק אבירת הלב בקרבי (line 585). The test he gives is whether you weep alone, which breaks you, or with God, which holds you up (see 3.12).

**5.10 Feeling worthless is not humility (line 541).** See 4.4. One who feels himself trash thinks only gross sins are forbidden, and so grows proud when he finds none. One who feels himself a chasid finds every speck of dust bad.

**5.11 The cold (lines 518-519).** Some bad inclinations can be turned to good. Heat for sin can become heat for a mitzvah. The inclination of coldness (Amalek), of heaviness and laziness, cannot. It damages faith by weighing the heart down until a person becomes like a stone. Admiring worldly wisdom is one form of this cooling (lines 149-151).

**5.12 Not knowing is part of the real thing (line 609).** After all his service and grasp, a person knows he has not served and not grasped: "what do you know, what have you seen, all is hidden as before." For him that is the repair, not a failure.

**5.13 Joy is required, and he admits when it is impossible (lines 612-613, 532).** The holy spirit and even "saying a derasha on the troubles" are impossible in broken-heartedness. "Sometimes one cannot even force oneself to speak." He does not pretend otherwise. The fallback is prayer, trust, and knowing "we are not alone in our troubles, He suffers with us."

## 6. Comparison with Chabad hisbonenus

Hisbonenus (per `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`): take one truth, extend it slowly in binah until daas holds it and it reaches the heart. Duration is the main technique, and the enemy is self-awareness in the state.

**Where they agree.** - *Mind leads.* His nearest thing to contemplation (2.7) is exactly "deepening and binding oneself to a thought" until the feelings are overridden. Going deeper at each review (2.13) is extension in binah. - *Self-awareness is the danger.* His "shrink the I-ness" and "feel your fear not for yourself" (line 390) match the Chabad warning against being aware of oneself in the state. - *Draw it down.* Chabad's last stage is bringing contemplation into action. His teaching that revelation happens only in doing (lines 138-141), that the body must "become Torah" (line 390), and that thought must go down into speech (Akiva lingering in "One" and not going on to "and you shall love," line 491) is the same concern. - *Do not stop at feeling.* He wants the fear felt in crisis engraved permanently (2.8), which is a version of Chabad's test that the trait has actually changed.

**Where he differs (not smoothed).** - *Where it starts.* Hisbonenus starts from an intellectual concept (the order of worlds, unity) and works toward the heart. Esh Kodesh almost never gives a concept to contemplate. It starts from what the person already feels: love for his children (2.1), pain, the cry, longing. It works on and lifts that. The one "contemplation" (2.7) is a thought about one's own suffering, not about God's greatness in the abstract. - *Mouth before heart.* He explicitly allows starting with the mouth alone and letting the heart follow (line 504). Chabad hisbonenus puts the thinking before prayer so the heart is moved from understanding. - *Felt moments over sustained duration.* He values "moments" of "You" (lines 504-505), a flash of contraction toward God, "cleaving at least for that moment." Chabad stresses long, patient dwelling. He also forbids analyzing the elevation (line 541), while hisbonenus is analysis carried down into feeling. - *Knowledge above reason.* He gives real weight to knowing "without reason" (line 365) and to faith as the soul's seeing (line 577). In hisbonenus, daas is reached through binah. - *Self-feeling as positive.* Chabad bittul distrusts any sense of self in the state. He requires the person to feel himself "a chasid" (line 541) and to put his self into the words of prayer (line 251). Both sides warn against I-ness, but he makes a place for the essential self that Chabad language usually does not name this way. - *Joy as a precondition, and melody as its trigger.* "Bring me a musician" (lines 531, 612) and "joy is a law" (line 153) make joy something you set up in advance. In hisbonenus, joy is more a result. In this book melody appears only as that Talmudic example, not as a technique. - *Picturing and quieting.* Absent here in both directions. There is one line on a pictured thought being stronger (line 379) and one on rest as the soul returning inward (line 540). This book cannot settle where he differs from Chabad on imagination and hashkatah. That has to come from Hachsharat HaAvreichim and B'nei Machshavah Tovah. - *Contemplation when the mind is broken.* This is where he goes past any Chabad manual. He writes for people with "no mind and no heart" (line 93), "like a stone" (line 519). His answer is not better contemplation. It is weeping with God (line 585), whispering from the depths (line 261), and "He is God" (line 557).

## 7. For the guide

1. Start where the person already feels something, a love, a pain, a longing. Say: "Before you turn to God, remember someone you love, feel that, and then turn it toward Him" (line 213).
2. If the heart is closed, let them begin with the mouth. Say the words slowly and wait for the heart to follow. "First in your mouth, then in your heart" (line 504).
3. Ask them to put themselves into the words, to mean themselves when they say "I," before trying to feel God as "You." He becomes present the way a face appears in water (line 251).
4. When a moment of nearness comes, the feeling of being drawn in "to one place," tell them not to stop and examine it. Examining it spoils it (lines 504, 541).
5. Afterwards, ask one question: "Are you the same person you were before this hour?" (line 525).
6. If they feel worthless, gently correct it. Feeling like nothing is not humility. Let them feel they are a person who belongs to God, a "captive king's son," even when struck (line 541).
7. If they are in real suffering and cannot feel anything, do not push for feeling. Tell them faith can be present without being felt (line 578), that they are not weeping alone, and that the One they have lost track of is looking for them (lines 82, 585).
8. Close with both hope and surrender: "May it get better soon. And He is God, He will do what is good in His eyes" (line 557).
