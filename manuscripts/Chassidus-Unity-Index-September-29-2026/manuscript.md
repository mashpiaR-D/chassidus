## 1. The thesis and the faces

**The thesis.** Chassidus is one point: the Essence of God (Ein Sof, atzmus), drawn down and revealed as it is present in everything. Put as unity, the point reads 'there is nothing besides Him', true even in the lowest earth, and that is His dwelling below. It is not one subject among others. It is separate from every subject and so present in every subject down to the smallest, and it shows most fully at the lowest point: the animal soul, the physical world, the last generation. (SPINE.md §1)

- **Unity means yachid, not echad.** 'Echad' unifies parts; 'yachid' is a single point with no relation to parts, not even as their source (the Rebbe, Inyanah 5:5; the Rayatz on 'Havayah echad'). The index uses 'the unity of God' only in that sense. (precision)
- **Whose claim it is.** The Rebbe states it for the whole of Chassidus in 1965: אשר תורת החסידות היא נקודה אחת שאינה מורכבת מפרטים שונים (Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus 2:1, line 9). The spine took its warrant from the Mitteler Rebbe, who says it of his Shaar HaYichud, and from the Rayatz, who calls that book 'the key to the teaching of Chassidus'. The works pass found an earlier statement for a whole body of teaching. The Mitteler Rebbe writes that everything his father taught, every Shabbos, in public and in private, was הכל היה לקבוע אחדות ה' הפשוטה, שהוא בחי' עצמות אא"ס ב"ה במוח ולב כל אחד כפי אשר יוכל שאת (Igros Kodesh, letter 36, line 838; the letter is his preface to Imrei Binah, 1821, and the same sentence opens that book, line 3). That is some 140 years before Inyanah. (works/group-06.md, works/group-07.md)
- **Each Rebbe names it from his own side:** the unity in every detail (Mitteler Rebbe); the true unity that is His dwelling below (Alter Rebbe); 'Chassidus is chayus' (Tzemach Tzedek); Anochi, the Essence beyond a mere radiance (Maharash); knowing God so as to love and fear Him (Rashab); joining form and matter (Rayatz); the Ein Sof point that permeates everything (the Rebbe). The Rebbe folds the earlier definitions in as branches of the point. That reconciliation is his reading, recorded rather than assumed. (How each Rebbe states it)
- **Its limits.** It is a Chabad claim about Chabad Chassidus. Of the 220 works, 96 come from other schools. The idea is present there, strongly in the founding circle, but it is often not the organizing center: Breslov's faith, Izhbitz's will of God and the Polish doctrine of the tzaddik have their own. The Rashab's warning against those who deal only in 'the deep matters' names the index's own risk, so the grammar works subject by subject and does not repeat a slogan. (Limits of the thesis; §5 below)

**The twelve faces.** The one idea takes twelve recurring forms across the corpus, each named from the sources. The counts show how much of the index each face carries.

**The grammar.** These are the fourteen moves by which the sources themselves show a subject to be a face of the one idea (SPINE.md §3):

G01 trace the thing to its source in divine speech · G02 hold both knowledges: nothing before Him, something to itself · G03 show that its concealment serves revelation · G04 join the particular to the whole, and the whole to what is beyond whole and part · G05 find the highest in the lowest · G06 explain by analogy from the soul, and mark where the analogy fails · G07 add life, not a fifth interpretation · G08 show the human work in it as bitul · G09 show its purpose as the home below, or as its own purpose · G10 show opposites joined in Him · G11 begin from the effect; end in action · G12 read every event as instruction and providence · G13 take it through force, light and life · G14 show Havayah is Elokim in it.

For the five-step way to apply them to a subject not yet in the index, see Applying the grammar to a new subject. The spine also maps the 22 pillars of *Chassidus Concepts* onto the faces (§4) and keeps nine disagreements between the Rebbeim and the schools (§6).

## F01 · The world said into being now

> Every created thing, stone and dust included, exists only because the letters of God's speech stand in it at this moment and continuously bring it from nothing into being. If they left for an instant it would return to absolute nothing. Providence is this same continuous creation, seen in each particular.

Spine entry · 18 subjects filed here (42 touch it) · 36 works (11 Chabad, 25 other) · 191 Tanya lines · signature terms in 90 Chabad works (1.5/10k) and 74 others (0.58/10k).

*Thin or forced?* None. This face is stated in so many words by the Besht, the Maggid, the Alter Rebbe, the Rayatz and the Rebbe.

- S009 · Continuous re-creation (K01): Continuous re-creation is the unity of God as dependence without pause: the world is only while the creating word stands in it.
- S010 · Particular providence (K01): Particular providence is the unity of God as continuous creation seen in each particular: every movement is His doing, and each one bears on the whole purpose.
- S043 · The sparks (K04): The sparks are the unity of God as the fallen letters of His word, scattered in every thing and waiting to be gathered from separation back into oneness.
- S063 · Man as a small world (K05): Man as a small world is the unity of God seen 'from my flesh': the person who contains all creation, from whom one learns that a hidden life sustains the world, and who fixes the world by fixing himself.
- S073 · Speech (K06): Speech is the unity of God as the analogy of creation and the place where the analogy fails: one word is nothing before the soul that speaks, as the world is nothing before Him, yet man's speech leaves him and His does not; every human word is hewn from the Shechinah's speech, and speaking is going out of oneself.
- S077 · The letters (K07): The letters are the unity of God as the substance of every created thing: His speech contracted into combinations that are each thing's name and life, and rooted in 'essential letters' within the Ein Sof itself.
- S078 · The ten utterances (K07): The Ten Utterances are the unity of God as creation spoken now: His speech, one with Him, standing in each thing and bringing it from nothing at every moment.
- S108 · Blessings (K09): A blessing is the unity of God as 'baruch', a drawing down: the Havayah hidden in each thing's creative word is woken and becomes 'You', present and addressed, in Elokim itself.
- S111 · Psalms (K09): Psalms is the unity of God as the words of a king whose mouth was only the vessel of His speech. Its verse 'Your word stands in the heavens' grounds continuous creation, and simple Jews' recital of it breaks every partition. *(partly thin)*
- S132 · Anger (K11): Anger is the unity of God forgotten in the moment: faith in providence leaves, and a person sees the other as the source of his hurt instead of the word of God that sustains the one who hurts him.
- S141 · Suffering (K12): Suffering is the unity of God as hidden good: the same continuous creation from the Ayin, too great to be grasped, which faith includes and raises into the good it already is.
- S144 · Trust (K12): Trust is the unity of God leaned on: having no support in the world besides Him, and so living as if the good He brings into being each moment were already here. *(partly thin)*
- S145 · Doubt and heresy (K12): Heresy is the unity of God denied in thought, and doubt is its cooling first step: the world imagined as able to stand without the word that makes it, and the self as able to stand apart from His oneness. *(partly thin)*
- S153 · The new moon and the months (K13): The month is the unity of God as renewal: vitality drawn again from nothing, the moon's shrinking to nothing and refilling showing that the tzimtzum is only for an instant, and renewal given to Israel because 'everything is His vitality'.
- S154 · Rosh Hashanah and Elul (K13): Rosh Hashanah is the unity of God as creation renewed each year: the year's light withdraws to its root, His will to reign is drawn anew by the simple cry of the shofar, and the new, higher light waits to be revealed by the deeds of those below.
- S187 · Eating (K16): Eating is the unity of God as nourishment: what feeds the soul is the word of God that stands in the bread and brings it into being, so that eating joins the word in the food to the word in the eater and raises it.
- S194 · Vitality in all things (K16): Vitality is the unity of God as continuous creation: the life in every thing, stones included, is the divine word bringing it from nothing now, so that 'You give life to all' means 'You bring all into being'.
- S195 · Mineral, plant, animal, speaker (K16): Mineral, plant, animal and speaker are the unity of God as a ladder with its top at the bottom: each kingdom lives by the word within it, the sparks fell into all four for man to raise, and the lowest kingdom is rooted highest.

## F02 · The higher and the lower unity

> From His side ('before Him', from above downward) the world is nothing at all, not even 'another' thing beside Him. That is the higher unity of Shema. From the world's side (from below upward) the world is a real, separate thing that He fills and rules in time and space through Malchus. That is the lower unity of Baruch Shem. Both are true, and the second exists for the sake of the first.

Spine entry · 24 subjects filed here (73 touch it) · 41 works (22 Chabad, 19 other) · 120 Tanya lines · signature terms in 80 Chabad works (0.56/10k) and 40 others (0.19/10k).

*Thin or forced?* None for Chabad. Outside Chabad the formal two-unity vocabulary is rarer: about a third of the rate (see coverage).

- S001 · The unity of God (K01): The unity of God is a simple singularity that creation leaves unchanged and that every detail of creation shows.
- S005 · Nothing besides Him (K01): Nothing besides Him is the unity of God as exclusivity: there is no other god, and beyond that nothing at all that stands on its own before Him, even in the lowest earth.
- S006 · Higher and lower unity (K01): Higher and lower unity is the unity of God said twice: once from His side, where the world is nothing, and once from below, where He fills a real world in time and place.
- S016 · Light and luminary (K02): Light and luminary are the unity of God as the ray that has no existence apart from its source: the sefirot, and all that is emanated, are one with Him as sunlight is one with the sun. *(partly thin)*
- S024 · Atzilus and the screen (K02): Atzilus and the screen are the unity of God as the border where 'He and His vessels are one' ends and real hiding begins: a screen that hides only toward the created, and that the light of Malchus breaks through.
- S030 · Upper and lower knowledge (K03): Upper and lower knowledge are the unity of God as known from two sides: from above, the world is nothing and He is the true Yesh; from below, the world is yesh and its source is 'nothing'. *(partly thin)*
- S035 · The union of the Holy One and His Shechinah (K03): The union of the Holy One and His Shechinah is the unity of God as the encompassing and the filling light made one by a person's act.
- S036 · The thirteen attributes of mercy (K03): The thirteen attributes of mercy are the unity of God as a mercy that comes from where all is nothing before Him, measured so that the world, sins included, can receive it.
- S037 · The divine names (K03): The divine names are the unity of God as one Being called by many names, which are His attributes and one with Him.
- S056 · The five levels of the soul (K05): The five levels of the soul are the unity of God as the two unities inside a person: four levels that live in worlds and feel them as real, and a fifth, above worlds, that feels them as nothing. *(partly thin)*
- S079 · Written and Oral Torah (K07): The Written and Oral Torah are the unity of God as concealment and birth: the will held whole and hidden in Chochmah (the 'father'), and brought out into revealed, detailed limbs by Malchus (the 'mother'), where He and His word are one. *(partly thin)*
- S104 · The Shema (K09): The Shema is the unity of God declared in both its forms, the higher ('Havayah is One') and the lower ('Blessed be the Name…'), and the soul's inclusion in that unity by accepting His kingship.
- S106 · The order of prayer as a ladder (K09): The order of prayer is the unity of God as a ladder set on the earth: the soul climbs from acknowledgment through creation and the two unities to bitul, and the light comes down the same rungs.
- S114 · Great love and eternal love (K10): Great love and eternal love are the unity of God felt in two directions: eternal love rises from below, from contemplating that all worlds are as nothing before Him; great love comes from above, from Atzilut 'where there is no separation'.
- S115 · Awe (K10): Awe is the unity of God as it overwhelms the self: lower awe sees Him hidden inside the worlds as their life; higher awe sees the worlds, and oneself, nullified in existence before Him.
- S124 · Truth (K10): Truth is the unity of God as what is really so: He alone is, and every creaturely truth is as nothing before Him, so to be true is to concede, and in the end to see, that there is nothing besides Him. *(partly thin)*
- S126 · Higher and lower teshuvah (K11): Higher and lower teshuvah are the unity of God as the two letters heh of His Name returned to their place: the lower heh brought back from exile among the husks, and the upper heh where the soul cleaves to Him spirit to spirit.
- S138 · Joy (K12): Joy is the unity of God as it is felt: the heart's answer to 'there is nothing besides Him, even below', and a share in His own joy in His dwelling here.
- S140 · Bitterness, not sadness (K12): Bitterness is the unity of God as it measures distance: the pain of a living heart that sees the worlds as mere radiance before Him and itself far, and so is emptied to receive Him.
- S150 · Time (K13): Time is the unity of God as a creature: brought from nothing at every moment through His Malchus, while His Name Havayah is 'was, is and will be in one instant', above it and yet present within it.
- S152 · Shabbos (K13): Shabbos is the unity of God as return: the worlds and the powers clothed in speech rising back to their source and to the Essence, while He goes on creating, and the higher and lower unity joined.
- S182 · Jerusalem (K15): Jerusalem is the unity of God as complete awe: the lower and the higher awe, two unifications, joined in one place the whole Land will become. Yet before Ein Sof even upper Jerusalem is a limit to be left behind. *(partly thin)*
- S190 · Work and commerce (K16): Work and commerce are the unity of God as testimony: whoever separates his earthly business from the One bears false witness to the Shema, while business done to give life to others makes the form like its Maker and turns the working day into His dwelling.
- S197 · Place and space (K16): Place is the unity of God as containment reversed: He is the place of the world and the world is not His place; space is brought into being by Malchus and is nothing before Him, yet He is found within it, and where He is revealed the place itself is no measure.

## F03 · The hiding for the sake of revelation

> The world's apparent independence is a concealment worked by God's own power (gevurah, the Name Elokim, 'nature'). It conceals only toward the creatures and is itself a kindness. Its purpose is revelation: there is no king without a people who stand apart from him. Once one knows He is hiding there, it is no longer a hiding.

Spine entry · 24 subjects filed here (71 touch it) · 36 works (7 Chabad, 29 other) · 226 Tanya lines · signature terms in 97 Chabad works (4.36/10k) and 79 others (2.35/10k).

*Thin or forced?* None. The concealment vocabulary is common to all schools (coverage F03). Only the reason given for it varies: love (Maggid), kingship (SH), revelation (Rashab, Rebbe).

- S014 · Tzimtzum (K02): Tzimtzum is the unity of God as a concealment He works on His own light, so that finite worlds can exist and receive Him; it hides only toward the creatures, never before Him.
- S015 · Line and impression (K02): The line and the impression are the unity of God as the trace He leaves inside His own concealment: the reshimu holds all of Him in hint within the 'empty place', and the line draws Him, measured, into every world down to the end of Asiyah. *(partly thin)*
- S021 · The chain of descent (K02): The chain of descent is the unity of God as one will descending through garments that conceal, whose purpose lies not in the higher worlds but in the lowest one.
- S023 · The four worlds (K02): The four worlds are the unity of God as degrees of one concealment: the same ten sefirot of Atzilus, Godliness itself, hidden further at each screen, while to Him first and last are the same.
- S025 · Concealment and revelation (K02): Concealment and revelation are the unity of God as one power seen from two sides: the gevurah that hides Him in the creature is His own, and once one knows He is hiding there, it is no hiding.

## F04 · Filling and surrounding

> He is present in two ways that are not the same relation. As 'filling' (memale), He is the vitality clothed inside each creature, contracted to its measure. The very 'somethingness' of matter comes from the surrounding light, not from the filling light.

## K01 · God: Unity and Essence

subjects/K01.md · 13 subjects · 100 passages · no adversarial re-check recorded

## K02 · Tzimtzum, Light and the Chain of Worlds

subjects/K02.md · 13 subjects · 97 passages · no adversarial re-check recorded

The part of Chassidus most easily misread as a map of God's distance: withdrawal, void, ladder of worlds, screens. The sources read each element as the unity seen from below. The contraction hides only toward the creatures (SH 6); the trace in the void holds all of Him in hint; the line reaches every creature (IH 20); the vessel is His own power to limit; the whole chain exists for the lowest world (Tanya 36). ## 4. The Tanya as one argument

The Tanya is the one book read at the level of the sentence. Every line (1,660 lines, 1,639 with text) was asked one question, how it expresses the unity, and given a JSONL entry naming its faces, the tie in one sentence and a kind: **stated** (the text makes the tie), **structural** (a building block: a definition or step the teaching needs later), **reading** (the tie is ours) or **none**. Where one line holds more than one tie, it was split into sentence sub-entries, giving 1,831 entries in all. Every Hebrew phrase quoted in the six reports was checked at its line with `find_he.py verify`, and at assembly all 1,816 non-empty opening phrases in the JSONL were checked again against their lines and found. The overview in plain English is TANYA.md; the six range reports are in tanya/.

*Line numbers here are those of the Chabad Library text (`_drive/.../Tanya — Hebrew text.txt`). They are 22 to 34 higher than the line numbers of the Chassidus-txt copy that the spine and the subject entries cite, so the spine's Tanya 33:6 at line 455 is line 477 here.*

Read in order, the parts make one argument:

1. **Who you are** (Likkutei Amarim 1-17). The book starts with anthropology, not doctrine: two souls, the faculties and garments, kelipah and nogah, the beinoni. Most lines are building blocks. The unity enters at a few load-bearing points, and the leading face is already F11: the inner struggle is framed from the start as darkness to be turned.
2. **The idea said plainly** (18-25). Faith is above knowledge because 'the One God' lives in the soul's chochmah. Then the definition: משום דכולא קמיה כלא חשיב, וכאין ואפס ממש (line 301). The hiding is His own garment (ch. 21), and the root of idolatry is כי עיקר ושרש עבודה זרה, הוא מה שנחשב לדבר בפני עצמו (line 323). Sin is measured as separation from the One (ch. 24).
3. **The ordinary life turned toward it** (26-34). Joy, foreign thoughts, subduing and transforming, love of Israel from 'one Father' (ch. 32), and then the sentence the whole index rests on: אשר אפילו בארץ מתחת – אין עוד מלבדו, וזו היא דירתו בתחתונים (line 477).
4. **The purpose** (35-40). The Shechinah rests only on what is nullified, and a mitzvah, His will with no hiding, is the oil for the light (ch. 35). The goal of all the worlds is stated as אלא התכלית הוא עולם הזה התחתון (line 510), and the dwelling is made by deeds throughout the exile (ch. 37).
5. **From knowledge to love** (41-53 and Chinuch Katan). How to contemplate so that it moves the heart: before the act, through da'at as a bond, through the two awes. Sovev is defined as not spatial (ch. 48), every contraction is for the material body (ch. 49), and the Shechinah is carried into the four cubits of halacha (chs. 51-53). Chinuch Katan names faith in His unity as the root of love.
6. **The doctrine, step by step** (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, the most stated part at 81%). The world is spoken into being this moment (SH 1-2); before Him it is nothing (3); the hiding is His own power, and Havayah is Elokim (4-6); He fills the earth as the lower unity, and a literal tzimtzum is rejected (7). The unity of His Essence and attributes is רק להאמין באמונה, שהיא למעלה מהשכל ומהשגה (line 834). The divine speech is then traced down to the stone (11-12).
7. **Sin and return** (Iggeret HaTeshuvah). The letters of the Name are read through the soul: אין שום דבר גשמי ורוחני חוצץ לפניו יתברך (line 941), except sin, which cuts the rope; return ties it again, stronger at the knot.
8. **The point at work** (Iggeret HaKodesh). Charity, prayer, suffering, anger and study are each argued from the unity. He is found 'equally, truly' below as above: שכמו שהוא יתברך מצוי בעליונים – כך הוא ממש בשוה בתחתונים (line 1090). Suffering: אבל באמת אין רע יורד מלמעלה, והכל טוב (line 1144), and sadness is והרי זה ככופר חס ושלום (line 1146). Only the Essence makes something from nothing: ולכן הוא לבדו בכחו ויכלתו לברוא יש מאין ואפס המוחלט ממש (line 1295). Anger: כי אילו היה מאמין שמאת ה׳ היתה זאת לו – לא היה בכעס כלל (line 1376), in a letter written to explain a saying of the Baal Shem Tov (Tzava'at HaRivash 120:4).
9. **The deed holds the Essence** (Kuntres Acharon). Contemplation איננו דבק אפילו במדות העליונות אלא במציאותן, ולא במהותן (line 1566), while the hand that takes the etrog הרי זה תופס ממש חיותו המלובש בו מנוקבא דאצילות, המיוחדת באור אין סוף המאציל (line 1574). The purpose of the whole chain is stated twice: וזהו תכלית ההשתלשלות, להתגלות אור עליון למטה, ולא לעלות התחתון למעלה (line 1589), and כי זהו תכלית ההשתלשלות שירד העליון למטה ויהיה לו דירה בתחתונים (line 1608). The last line of the book traces the Shabbat rest from speech back to the Ten Utterances: כמו ששבת ה׳ מיו״ד מאמרות שנבראו בהם שמים וארץ גשמיים (line 1660).

The most abstract faces, F02 (the two unities, 120), F04 (filling and surrounding, 121) and F09 (opposites, 85), are the rarest. The book's weight falls on the deed that brings the infinite down, and its last word (Kuntres Acharon 4) says so outright.
- **The most doctrinal stretches** are Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah (81% stated) and Likkutei Amarim 18-25 (80%). The opening chapters, Likkutei Amarim 1-17, are the least stated long stretch of body text (31%), because they lay down the terms the rest will use.
- **Where each face gathers.** F10 is densest in the chapters on Torah as His will (Likkutei Amarim 4, 5 and 23, where 85-93% of lines carry it). F01 gathers in Shaar HaYichud 1 and 11-12 and in Iggeret HaKodesh 14 and 25. F05 gathers in Iggeret HaKodesh 20 (26 of its 37 lines) and Shaar HaYichud 7. F04 gathers in Likkutei Amarim 48 and 51, and F07 in Likkutei Amarim 33-36 and Iggeret HaKodesh 23 and 32. The anthropology of Likkutei Amarim 1-17 runs on F11, F10 and F12.

## Two of the spine's disagreements are already inside the Tanya

The line-by-line reading found both sides of disagreements that the spine keeps between different Rebbeim within the Alter Rebbe's own book, sometimes within a single letter.

- **A radiance, or the Essence?** Iggeret HaKodesh 7 says both in one letter. He Himself fills heaven and earth, אני ממש, דהיינו מהותו ועצמותו כביכול ולא כבודו לבד (line 1101); and in the next line creatures cannot receive their life from His Essence alone, only from the spreading of His life, 'a radiance of His Name' (line 1102). Iggeret HaKodesh 20 divides the work between them: the power to make something from nothing is the Essence's alone (line 1295), while the life in creatures is 'a radiance of a radiance of a radiance' (line 1305). The spine keeps this as a dispute between the Maharash ('creation from nothing is only a mere radiance') and IH 20. The Tanya already holds both formulations; whether IH 20's division reconciles them is our reading. (lines 841-1120, 1121-1400)
- **Knowing, or believing?** Shaar HaYichud 9 says 'we have no business with hidden things' (line 861), and Iggeret HaKodesh 25 calls His presence in everything a matter of the revealed things, להאמין אמונה שלימה במקרא מלא (line 1411). Yet Kuntres Acharon 4 says that knowing the order of the worlds ואדרבה – עולה על כולנה (line 1577), and Kuntres Acharon 8 makes contemplation before the Shema והיא חובה של תורה ממש, למביני מדע (line 1644). The passages differ in audience and in object, and the text does not reconcile them. The spine's F12 dispute between the Alter Rebbe and the Rayatz therefore has a version inside the Tanya itself. (lines 1401-1660)
- **Kept as well:** a ranking of services that the letters do not settle (charity 'the main service' in the footsteps of Moshiach, IH 9, against 'no holiness like Torah's', IH 23); transformation reserved to tzaddikim while the beinoni subdues, with full transformation placed in the future (lines 386-387, 395, 462); and letters 27-28 on the passing of the tzaddik, which give him a power the rest of the book does not stress and which the index records without folding into the one idea.

## The claim and its measures

- **It is a Chabad claim.** The index does not say the other schools are organized around this idea. Where their texts state it, the entries say so; where they are organized around faith, the will of God or the tzaddik, the entries say that too (below).
- **Counts are emphasis, not proof.** The face coverage and the work counts are plain letters-only substring counts. They miss vocalized texts and count incidental senses: 'atzmus' as bones, 'bitul' as bitul Torah or halachic nullification, 'yichud' as 'especially' or halachic seclusion, 'tzimtzum' as precision.
- **Where 'reading' lives.** Only 2 of the spine's 175 anchors are marked reading. The looser claims sit in the connective tissue. In the subject entries, 172 of 1,299 derivation steps are reading and 8 are partly stated. Of the 145 works that carry the idea, 20 have a signature that is our reading resting on stated passages. Even where a work's passages are stated, the claim that the whole work is one expression of the idea is usually ours, and many entries say so in their signature line.
- **Primary is a judgment.** A work's primary faces were weighed from its counts and from reading, and a subject's first face is the one its derivation starts from. Both are editorial judgments recorded in the entries, not measurements.

- **One book at sentence level.** Only the Tanya has been read line by line. Every other work is represented by counts, a weighed set of faces, a signature and a handful of verified passages. README.md explains how to extend the sentence-level pass.
- **Hebrew and Yiddish.** The fixed term set is Hebrew. Yiddish carries much of the God-language in the Rebbe's and the Rayatz's talks ('der Eibershter'), and non-Chabad authors write השי"ת, חיות, נקודה, so their counts undercount. Several group files add supplementary terms per work for this reason.

*A research index that tests one claim across the Chassidus shelf: that Chassidus is not 220 books but one idea said 220 ways. Start with INDEX.md.*

## What this is

The claim is the Lubavitcher Rebbe's. In *Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus* (1965) he says that Chassidus is "one point, not composed of distinct details": the Essence of God, drawn down and present in everything. Put as unity, that point reads "there is nothing besides Him", true even in the lowest earth, and that is His dwelling below. The Mitteler Rebbe says the same of his father's whole teaching, about 1821.

The index takes the claim seriously enough to test it. It does not collect proof-texts. It asks of each subject, each book and, for one book, each line: *how, exactly, is this the one idea, and does the text say so, or do we?*

- **Subject by subject.** 197 subjects of Chassidus, from the unity of God to sleep, money and the Land of Israel. Each is traced to the idea in numbered steps, and every step is marked as stated by a text or as our reading.
- **Work by work.** All 220 works of the Chassidus shelf, Chabad (124) and other schools (96). Each work gets term counts, the faces of the idea it carries, a one-paragraph *signature* saying how it expresses the idea, and verified passages. Each entry also says whether the work carries the idea at all.
- **Line by line.** The Tanya, all 1,660 lines. Every line was read and asked the question, and its answer is recorded in a JSONL entry.

Throughout, the index keeps three things apart: what the texts **state**, what is our **reading**, and where the tie is **thin**. It keeps the disagreements between the Rebbeim and the schools instead of smoothing them.

## Where to start

1. **INDEX.md**, the master index. It gives the thesis and the twelve faces in brief, every subject and work filed by face, every subject by cluster, the Tanya as one argument, and the honest limits.
2. **SPINE.md**, the thesis in full: how each Rebbe states it, the twelve faces with their sources, the fourteen moves of the grammar, the 22 pillars mapped to the faces, and the disagreements kept.
3. **A cluster** in `subjects/` for a subject you care about, or **TANYA.md** for the Tanya in plain English.
4. **MEDITATION.md** and **meditations/INDEX.md** for the meditation behind each of the twelve faces and behind each of the 197 subjects: the unity can't be understood, only glimpsed in contemplation, so this is where the index is meant to be used.

## How it was made

All of it was built on 2026-09-28 and 2026-09-29.

1. **The spine.** The Rebbeim's own definitions of Chassidus were read in context: the Mitteler Rebbe's Shaar HaYichud, the Rayatz's *Kuntres Toras HaChassidus* and *Kuntres Limud HaChassidus*, the Rashab's *Eitz HaChaim* and *Kuntres HaTefillah*, the Maharash's *Toras Shmuel*, the Tzemach Tzedek's *Derekh Mitzvotekha*, the Tanya, *Keter Shem Tov*, *Maggid Devarav leYaakov*, Hayom Yom and the Rebbe's *Inyanah*. From them came the thesis, twelve faces named in the sources' own words, and fourteen moves the sources themselves make when they show a subject to be the one idea (the grammar). For each face, its signature terms were counted in every work, Chabad against the other schools. Every one of the 175 anchors was read with at least five lines of context and returned FOUND from `find_he.py verify` at its line.
2. **The subject list.** Subjects were drawn from the 22 pillars of *Chassidus Concepts* (each README and architecture), the concept map, the atlas, the library-by-concept list and the four themes of the *Sefer HaArachim* synthesis. The list was checked against the books' own contents: the chapters of the Tanya, the parsha sections of Likkutei Torah and Torah Ohr, the mitzvos of Derech Mitzvosecha, the maamar headings of Hemshech Ayin Beis and the subject index of Likkutei Sichos. Search terms are unvocalized and matched letters-only; each was counted over the 160 non-OCR works.
3. **The subject entries.** Each subject was derived from the spine through its faces and the grammar, step by step, with each step marked stated or reading. The Rebbeim were taken one by one, then the other schools. Passages were read in context and verified at their lines, disagreements were kept, and a thin note says where the tie is weak. Seven of the sixteen clusters then had a dated adversarial re-check, and an eighth records the same kind of revision; all eight cut back overreach (INDEX.md §5).
4. **The works.** The 220 works were read in twelve groups. For each: the twelve fixed unity terms (אין עוד מלבדו, יחוד, אחדות, ממלא כל עלמין, סובב כל עלמין, צמצום, ביטול, דירה בתחתונים, עצמות, אין סוף, אלקות, השגחה פרטית) were counted with `find_he.py count` as lines containing the term, per 10,000 words, plus supplementary terms where the fixed set misses a school's vocabulary. Passages were read in context and, in long works, sampled across the work's length. The faces were then weighted, and the signature, structure, passages, disagreements and thin note were written.
5. **The Tanya, line by line.** The drive text was read in six ranges. Each line got a JSONL entry, each range got a report, and TANYA.md sums them up (see the protocol below, which follows this pass).
6. **The assembly.** INDEX.md was built from the JSON and JSONL files: every count in it is computed from them, and every link was checked against the headings of its target file. The Hebrew quoted in INDEX.md was verified again at its line, and all 1,816 non-empty opening phrases in the Tanya JSONL were checked again against their lines.

**The verification rule.** No Hebrew enters the index unless `find_he.py verify FILE "..." --line N` finds it at the cited line. Matching is letters-only: nikud, punctuation, quotation marks and spacing are ignored, so ה' and ה׳ match. Pieces separated by … must each occur within two lines of N. OCR text is quoted exactly with its errors, and the intended reading is given in the note. All 2,275 passage citations in the index (1,895 distinct passages in 212 files) passed.

## How to read it

- **stated**: a text in the corpus makes the tie to the unity itself.
- **reading**: the tie is ours. It may be sound, but no text says it.
- **thin**: the tie is weak or would have to be forced, so it is not forced. **partly thin** means part of a subject or work is tied and part is not; the entry says which part. - In the Tanya pass, **structural** marks a building block (a definition, or a step the teaching needs later), and **none** marks a line with no tie: a header, a marker, a legal detail, an appeal.
- *OCR* marks uncorrected machine transcription. - "Not located in this pass" means only that this pass did not find it. "Not re-verified" marks a citation carried over from earlier work without being re-read.

- **F01-F12** are the twelve faces and **G01-G14** the moves of the grammar. Among the spine's anchors, T-numbers support the thesis, F-numbers with a letter (F03k) support a face, G15-G21 are further anchors used in the pillar map and the disagreements, and P-numbers support the pillars.
- **S001-S197** are subjects and **K01-K16** their clusters. A subject is filed under the first face its entry lists, the face its derivation starts from.
- **group-01 to group-12** are the works files. A work's faces are weighted *primary*, *secondary* or *minor*, and INDEX.md files a work under each primary face. INDEX.md §5 sorts the works into five tiers: those that carry the idea as their stated subject, those that state it but are organized around another center, those that carry it in part, those that are thin as a whole, and those that cannot be assessed.

**References and line numbers.** A ref is the library file's own ref string: Sefaria numbering for clean texts, scan page and paragraph for OCR scans, and the printed page for *Kuntres Limud HaChassidus*. "line" is always the line in the cited file. The Tanya has two numberings. The spine and the subject entries cite the Chassidus-txt copy, and the Tanya pass cites the Chabad Library text, which runs 22 to 34 lines higher, so Tanya 33:6 is line 455 in the first and line 477 in the second.

**Counts are emphasis, not proof.** They are plain letters-only substring counts. They miss vocalized texts and count incidental senses (atzmus as bones, bitul as bitul Torah, yichud as "especially"). Yiddish and non-Chabad vocabulary are undercounted.

**Disagreements are kept.** Where the Rebbeim or the schools differ (on whether the tzimtzum is literal, whether creation is from a radiance or from the Essence, knowledge or faith, prayer or action), both sides are recorded with their passages, and nothing is harmonized unless a text does it. Where the index proposes a reconciliation, the proposal is marked reading.

## How to extend the sentence-level pass to another book

The Tanya pass is the model. Doing the same for another book takes ten steps.

**1. Choose the book and fix the file.** Prefer a clean, non-OCR text, and decide which file's line numbers you will cite. Record the choice in every report header. If a second copy carries the refs (the Sefaria numbering in `Chassidus-txt`), align each line to it by its opening letters and give the ref in `where`. The Tanya pass cited the drive text and aligned it to Chassidus-txt. Look at the line lengths first. Some files hold a whole paragraph or essay on one line (*Maggid Devarav leYaakov* has 277 lines for 57,000 words), and there most entries will need sentence sub-entries.

**2. Split the book into ranges.** Use about 280 lines per range, one reading session each. Name them `<book>/lines-A-B.jsonl` and `<book>/lines-A-B.md`, and keep a book's files in a folder of its own, as `tanya/` does.

**3. Read every line in context.** Use `find_he.py read FILE A B` (or `skim` to orient first). Read in full, not by search.

**4. Write one JSONL object per non-empty line.**

```json {"line": 477, "sentence": null, "where": "Tanya Part I, Likkutei Amarim 33:6", "opens": "ועל זה תיקנו ליתן שבח והודיה", "faces": ["F07", "F02"], "how": "The morning 'how good is our portion, how fair our inheritance': our inheritance is 'the true unity of God, that even in the earth below there is nothing besides Him, and that is His dwelling below' (the spine's anchor sentence).", "kind": "stated"} ```

- `line` is the line in the cited file.
- `sentence` is `null` for the whole-line entry. When one line holds more than one tie, add sub-entries with `sentence` set to the clause number (a clause ends at a full stop or colon). The sub-entry's `opens` gives the first few words from where that clause starts.
- `where` is the book, part and chapter:paragraph, aligned to the ref copy.
- `opens` is the line's opening words (for a sub-entry, the clause's), exactly as they stand, so anyone can find the line again.
- `faces` lists the spine's faces only (F01-F12, as defined in SPINE.md). Give the ones the line itself carries, usually one to three; the Tanya averaged 1.6 on lines with a face. A structural line may carry faces or none.
- `how` is one sentence, in plain English, saying what the line does for the idea. Quote the Hebrew in the report, not in `how`.
- `kind` is one of `stated`, `structural`, `reading` or `none` (see "How to read it"). Never force a tie: halachic detail, appeals, salutations and markers are `none`.

**5. Verify.** Check every `opens` string, and every Hebrew phrase you will quote, at its line with `find_he.py verify FILE "..." --line N`. One-word lines may be too short for the tool, so note them. Keep verification in the same session as the reading.

**6. Write the range report.** Give it a header (source file, range, entry counts by kind, faces by count, the line numbering used and how it maps to the ref copy, the verification statement). Then write "How the range builds the idea", chapter by chapter, with each key line quoted in Hebrew and its line number. Close with "Where the tie is thin", the disagreements kept (inside the book, and against the spine's §6), and relation to earlier work. Say where a face is ours and not the text's.

**7. Count from the JSONL, not by hand.**

```python import json, glob, collections rows = [json.loads(l) for f in glob.glob('BOOK/*.jsonl') for l in open(f, encoding='utf-8') if l.strip()] lines = [r for r in rows if r['sentence'] is None] print(len(rows), 'entries;', len(lines), 'lines') print(collections.Counter(r['kind'] for r in lines)) print(sorted(collections.Counter(x for r in lines for x in r['faces']).items())) none = sum(r['kind'] == 'none' for r in lines) print(f"none: {none} of {len(lines)} ({100 * none / len(lines):.1f}%)") ```

**8. Check adversarially.** Re-read a sample of your own entries against the text and look for three failures. A `stated` entry whose line states no tie should become `reading`. A face added by association, not by what the line says, should be removed. A quotation attributed to the wrong voice (a gloss, a later editor, the compiler) should be corrected. Record the check and what it changed at the top of the reports, as the subject clusters do.

**9. Sum up.** Write `BOOK.md` on the pattern of TANYA.md: the short answer, the counts by kind and by face, how the book builds the idea step by step, a handful of lines to carry, where the thread is thin, and the disagreements the book holds inside itself.

**10. Update the index.** Add the book to INDEX.md: a section beside §4, the counts in the header, and its place in §5's limits. If the pass changes the work's signature or faces, update its entry in `works/group-XX.md` and `.json` and say so in the entry.

**Candidates.** The Mitteler Rebbe's *The Gate of Unity* (2,712 segment lines in Chassidus-txt; the book the Rayatz calls "the key to the teaching of Chassidus", and the source of 13 spine anchors) is the natural next book. *Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus* (156 lines) is the thesis itself and fits in one range. *Tzava'at HaRivash* (312 lines), *Keter Shem Tov* (519) and *Maggid Devarav leYaakov* (277 long lines) would test the idea in the founding circle, before Chabad's vocabulary. A book the index files as organized around another center, such as *Likutei Moharan* or *Mei HaShiloach*, would measure how much of such a book carries the idea at the level of the sentence, which is the question §5 of INDEX.md leaves open.
