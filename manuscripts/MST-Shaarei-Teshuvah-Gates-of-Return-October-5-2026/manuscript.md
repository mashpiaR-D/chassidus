*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A manual in two parts for "people pressed by their business who long to bring their hearts close to their Father in Heaven." It teaches how teshuvah of the heart (bitterness, a broken heart) and prayer of the heart (joy, love, contemplation) depend on each other. Teshuvah comes before prayer as its foundation. The work names the ways the heart is blocked (dullness, desire, worry about livelihood, stray thoughts, coldness) and gives the matching work for each. Its main teaching is set inside long Kabbalistic maamarim on the Gates of Teshuvah, Choice and Prayer, with a part 2 and a section on education (Chinuch).

**How much of it is medicine.** substantial: The introduction and many sections speak directly to the heart's condition in teshuvah and prayer, but most of the text is Kabbalistic cosmology (malchus, kav ha-midah, sefirot, angels, the blessings and Shema).

**Its model of the person.** A godly soul is "sown" in a natural or animal soul and body. The "adam" level (mind, heart, action) sits below a simple essential will, the yechidah or "point of the heart." Will spreads into many wants, which scatters the soul, and thoughts follow wherever will was invested. Change comes by uprooting will at the depth of its pleasure through bitterness, by moving the heart through contemplation (the sword), and by a cry from the point of the heart (arrows). Lowliness comes before every ascent, and the body inherited from one's parents shapes how soft or hard the heart is.

**Its philosophy.** Divine light rests only in lowliness and a broken heart. Teshuvah reaches the source of life, which is above the life that comes through the mitzvot, so it heals the soul's faculties. Essential love in the yechidah cannot be put out by "many waters." The soul's descent into the body is for the sake of ascent, like a seed that rots before it grows. Choice is the first point of will in the heart, and what comes from above only strengthens the direction already chosen.

**What it adds.** It calibrates bitterness and joy against each other and fixes teshuvah before prayer every day. It sorts types of inner dullness and blames some of it on inherited temperament. It says closeness and distance from the body are one motion and that one should not check oneself. It treats stray thoughts as what is left of past will, 'scattering of the soul.' It gives two tools, contemplation (sword) and the cry from the point of the heart (arrows), and places choice in the first point of will.

## I understand it in my head, but my heart feels nothing when I pray

Contemplation moves the mind while the heart stays stone. Sometimes it is the reverse: the heart is moved and the mind is blank. The person concludes his understanding was fake.

**What is really going on.** The dullness sits in the faculty where he indulged, even in permitted things, or it comes from the grossness of the natural soul he received from his parents. Mild dullness is like stale air and opens easily. Only a heart of stone resists.

**Program: Opening a dulled heart by contemplation (the sword).** (1) Do not conclude that the mind's arousal is false. (2) Locate the dullness, mind or heart, by where you indulged. (3) Stir teshuvah first. (4) Labor at contemplation in Pesukei d'Zimra and Shema; this is the 'sword' that opens a heart that is not stone. (5) Where the dullness is shallow, a little fresh air (bitterness or joy) is enough. *When:* In daily morning prayer, during Pesukei d'Zimra and Shema. *For:* One whose heart is closed by attachment to permitted worldly things, not by a heart of stone. *Not for:* Deep-rooted evil beyond the reach of contemplation; that needs G05. `MST·Q07` · `MST·Q08` · `MST·Q24` · `MST·Q28`

## I can be joyful before God or broken before Him, never both

*Its own term:* תשובה במרירות / שמחה וטוב לבב

Some people skip the heart's teshuvah and go straight to joy, then collapse. Others dwell in bitterness and never reach joy. Both are confused.

**What is really going on.** Joy without teshuvah has no vessel and falls at once. Bitterness without joy is 'vanity.' The depth of joy in prayer is measured by the depth of bitterness before it.

**Program: Teshuvah of the heart before prayer, then joy.** (1) In a moment of respite from business, before prayer, turn the heart to teshuvah. (2) Add daas: contemplate until you clearly discern what harms your soul's life. (3) Let bitterness over the heart's distance come from that discernment; this broken heart is the 'heaviness of head' before prayer. (4) Then move straight into joy, love and closeness in Pesukei d'Zimra, the blessings and Shema, contemplating the Creator's greatness. (5) Do not lengthen one and cut the other short: the depth of joy is measured by the depth of bitterness. *When:* Daily, just before prayer; written for busy people 'whenever they find rest from the burden of the times.' *For:* People absorbed in work who long to draw near and want to know how to arouse the heart. *Not for:* Someone who already sinks into sadness; the text itself calls lengthening teshuvah at joy's expense 'vanity.' `MST·Q01` · `MST·Q02` · `MST·Q03` · `MST·Q04` · `MST·Q05` · `MST·Q06`

**Reframe.** From *"Real service is joy, and bitterness is just sadness. Or: only brokenness is real."* to **Bitterness and joy are one pair. Right after a broken heart comes living joy, and joy lasts only on that foundation.** Divine light rests only where there is lowliness. Joy without it has no vessel and collapses, and bitterness left without joy never turns into life. `MST·Q01` · `MST·Q02` · `MST·Q03`

## This desire is so deep in me I'll never be free of it

A desire that reaches the essence of the self, even in permitted matters, returns again and again and dulls the mind and heart.

**What is really going on.** Will and pleasure are bound to it at depth. If he chases the desire deliberately ('I'll follow my heart'), there is no hope. If it overtakes him at its time, it can be uprooted.

**Program: Uprooting a deep desire: bitterness to its depth, then confession.** (1) Ask honestly whether you pursue the desire deliberately or it overtakes you at its time; in the second case there is hope. (2) Let the pain reach as deep as the pleasure and will you had in it; this uproots the will and its life. (3) Confess in speech, naming the sin privately, to undo the 'body' of what was done. (4) Keep up a daily thought of teshuvah and spoken confession. *When:* Daily thought of teshuvah and confession, with the bedtime Shema confession as the text's example. *For:* One overtaken by a deep desire he does not want to follow. *Not for:* Anyone at risk of self-harm: the text's models of bitterness include loathing life and souls departing in weeping. `MST·Q09` · `MST·Q10` · `MST·Q11`

**Program: The cry from the point of the heart in silent prayer (the arrows).** (1) When contemplation cannot reach the deep evil, stop relying on intellectual intention. (2) In silent prayer, pour out the soul like a poor man over what he lacks. (3) Let words come from the point of the heart, a simple essential feeling beyond intellect. (4) Know that lowliness this deep is what comes before the higher closeness. *When:* The silent Amidah, especially in real distress. *For:* One whose distress is real and whose mind and heart no longer respond. *Not for:* Manufacturing a crisis or lowliness on purpose; the text describes this state arising from real extremity. `MST·Q19` · `MST·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"This desire is in my bones; I'll never be rid of it."* to **If it overtakes you rather than you chasing it, there is hope. Pain that reaches the depth of the pleasure uproots it.** The desire lives in will and pleasure at a certain depth. Pain that reaches the same depth takes the will away, and with it the thought's life. `MST·Q09` · `MST·Q10`

## Stray thoughts flood me, and worst of all when I pray

*Its own term:* מחשבות זרות / הרהורים / פיזור הנפש

Unwanted thoughts of lust, money or honor push in, most of all during prayer, and he cannot turn his mind away.

**What is really going on.** Prayer is a battle, and the animal soul fights hardest at close range. Thoughts come only in areas where he once invested will, because they are what is left of that will. His self is scattered into many wants.

**Program: Gathering a scattered will against stray thoughts.** (1) Know that the thoughts rise in prayer because the godly soul is fighting at close range. (2) Tell apart thoughts from a will you once invested and passing 'chaff' you never wanted, which has no life. (3) Treat the root: uproot the will behind the thought through teshuvah. (4) In Shema, give over the simple will ('whom have I in heaven but You'); this gathers the scattered wants back to their source. *When:* During prayer, especially at Shema. *For:* One troubled by recurrent lustful, money or honor thoughts tied to things he once wanted. *Not for:* Obsessive-compulsive sufferers who would turn the 'battle' into constant self-checking. `MST·Q20` · `MST·Q30` · `MST·Q31` · `MST·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"The thoughts in my prayer prove I'm corrupt and my prayer is worthless."* to **They surge because you are fighting at close range. Thoughts in things you never wanted are lifeless chaff.** Effort shifts from wrestling each thought to uprooting the will behind it. Thoughts with no will behind them are shown to be harmless. `MST·Q20` · `MST·Q31`

## I've worked on myself for years and feel nothing has changed

He studies and contemplates, feels no loosening of his bond to the body, and despairs of himself.

**What is really going on.** He looks for change as something visible. The drawing close in understanding is itself the distancing from the body, and that cannot be perceived.

**Program: Keep walking, don't check yourself.** (1) Keep laboring at closeness through contemplation. (2) Do not search yourself for proof that you have moved away from the body. (3) Seek closeness to God for itself, not to feel good. (4) Expect each deeper stage to make the earlier one feel like distance, and expect a fall before an ascent. *When:* Ongoing, across the years of one's service. *For:* Those who study and contemplate and despair because they feel no change. *Not for:* One who is not laboring at all, since the answer assumes real effort at closeness. `MST·Q16` · `MST·Q17` · `MST·Q18` · `MST·Q19`

**Reframe.** From *"Nothing in me has changed."* to **Every step of closeness is the same step of distance from the body, only it cannot be seen. Keep walking and don't check.** Distance and closeness are one motion, as in walking. Since the result cannot be felt, self-inspection only breeds despair, while labor produces the change. `MST·Q16` · `MST·Q17` · `MST·Q18`

## Cautions

- Its models of bitterness include loathing one's life and souls departing in weeping; never offer them to someone depressed or suicidal. - 'Joy without bitterness falls at once' is aimed at the complacent; for a depressed person, begin with joy and worth. - The text ties sadness and melancholy to coldness and spiritual failure; clinical depression is not a moral lapse. - 'Soft as a reed, trampled like dust' must not be used to justify submitting to abuse or silencing oneself. - 'Prayer is a battle' can feed obsessive self-monitoring; for OCD, lead with the text's point that unwanted thoughts are lifeless chaff. - Lowliness before every ascent should not be manufactured by crushing oneself. - Hard-heartedness from one's parents and nogah language can shame; use it to lift blame, not to label. - Do not use the story of illegitimate children dying at the moment of teshuvah.
