## The shape of the range

This gate is not a book that sometimes reaches the unity. The unity is its only subject. The author, R. Aharon HaLevi of Strashelye, a leading student of the Alter Rebbe, says he is explaining "root premises received from the light of our holy Rebbe's Torah", which the Rebbe set out "a little in his holy book, in Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah" (line 98). So nearly every line is *stated*. No line is *none*: even the Chagigah quotation, the Zohar's palanquin and the hyle of egg and chick are there to carry the argument, so they are *structural*.

The faces fall in a different order from the Tanya. **F05 (the Essence beyond both) leads by far.** The gate's first rule is that nothing, not even "hidden within hiddenness", may be thought of Ein Sof. **F09 (the unity of opposites)** is the engine of the whole gate. The phrase "two opposites in one subject" (שני הפכים בנושא אחד) comes back again and again, and Keter is defined as the power that joins them. **F12** is dense because the gate keeps asking what may be asked, what may be known and what may only be believed. The cosmology of continuous creation (F01) and of filling and surrounding (F04) is present but is not the subject. **F06 (the soul as part of God) never appears.** F10, F11 and F07 appear only at the edges, mostly in glosses, where the author promises a later part on service.

## Ch. 1 (97-105): simple and composite

The gate opens with its rule. No name, sefirah or image may be said or thought of Ein Sof if it implies any division, "even in potential and hiddenness". What remains to a person is faith and an act: > רק להאמין שהכל מאתו יתברך ולייחד כל הנמצאים אליו יתברך (line 97)

Lines 99-104 build the definition step by step (*structural*). "Composite" means two essences joined, like a horse and its rider. Everything created is a compound of the four elements. Even water, the "simple" element, is simple only relative to compounds, since it can be turned into stone. The soul is one power, and its many forms show only through the body. Then line 105 applies this to God. The root of faith is that there is no power besides Him: > אשר אין כח זולתו והוא לבדו מבלי התערבות כח אחר חס ושלום ואפס בלעדו (line 105)

To place created powers in Him even in hiding "makes Him a master of many powers, and there is no greater sin".

## Ch. 2-3 (106-128): vessels that hide, a drawing that is equal

The question is set in line 106. If He is simple and nothing is outside Him, where does difference come from, and "how is there limit from the limitless?" The Kabbalists' answer is the ten sefirot. They are vessels whose work is to hide: > והמה נקראים כלים להסתיר אור עצמותו יתברך בכל הנמצאים באופניהם (line 107)

The soul in the body is the parable (lines 108-109). The same life is in the brain and the feet, which is why the feet obey at once. A flaw in a limb does not flaw the soul. From this comes the gate's version of "below as above": > ומצד עצמותו שוה התמשכותו יתברך בשמים ובארץ ובכל חילוקי דרגין בהשואה גמורה (line 110)

Line 111 states F03 in its classic form. He hides His light in the vessels "so that it is revealed in creatures as yesh, in order to reveal His light". Line 112 adds the key word: "He Himself" is what is drawn into them, and the difference is only in revelation.

Chapter 3 then fences in the word "unity" itself. The sefirot are united with Him, and before Him they are "nullified like sunlight in the sun", as in the Alter Rebbe's Shaar HaYichud (line 118). Moshe "beheld the image of Havayah": in every creature he saw its unity, not its separateness (line 120). But even this belongs only to "the side where He descends to reign": > כי לשון יחוד נופל על מה שאנו רואים התחלקות הדרגין (line 121)

And of His Essence: > ואינו נופל בו יתברך לשון יחוד כמאמר ולפני אחד מה אתה סופר (line 122)

Lines 122-128 add the Zohar's "Ein Sof has no trace at all" (לית ביה רשימו). Line 123 marks the analogy where it fails, which is G06. A man's wisdom leaves a trace in him because he is composite and needs to reach outside himself. Ein Sof needs nothing.

## Ch. 4-5 (129-153): new from nothing; what may be asked; the higher and the lower unity

The sefirot are "a wholly new existence", as new as creation from nothing (lines 129-131). Line 132 records an oral teaching: the author heard "explicitly from our holy Rebbe" that even the Ramak's parable of fire in a flint fits only the hidden brain of Keter, not Ein Sof. Lines 134-140 take up "when it rose in His simple will". Will implies change and lack, yet the world is not by chance. Reason cannot hold "a thing and its opposite in one subject" (line 138), but with Him: > כל רעיון יכול להיות כל הדברים מעצם ונושא אחד (line 139)

Then the rules of contemplation. One does not inquire into the wondrous. One contemplates what is permitted, and the permitted contemplation is itself an act of uniting (line 141). Binah asks "Who created these?", but the purpose of the question is bitul, not information: > והשאלה הוא בכדי לבטלם לאין סוף יתברך (line 142)

Line 147, a long gloss on the Zohar's "ad", holds the gate's deepest teaching on the two unities. In the higher unity (Zeir Anpin), levels come from Him and are nullified, which is one wonder. In Malchut, the lower unity, His power is revealed as levels that are *not* nullified, so they appear as separate things and yet live only by Him. That is "two wonders", and it is further from reason: > ודבר זה הוא רחוק מאד מן השכל יותר מבחינת היחוד הנמשך בזעיר אנפין שהם בחינות יחודא עילאה (line 147)

In the same gloss: one sees "with the senses" His Essence revealed *precisely* in creatures (147.6). In the future Malchut will stand above Binah, and "precisely in yesh Ein Sof will be revealed" (147.12). A gloss on the gloss explains exile. Service now is in hiding, through Torah, practical mitzvot and itkafia. A son nullified to his father while the father hides his face shows more bitul than one nullified while the father shows his love (147.8-9). Also inside line 147: Chochmah is "the very bitul of the worlds to their source", and in Keter itself even bitul does not apply, "for on whom would it fall?" (147.1, 147.4).

Line 148 names the work that draws the higher unity into the lower: > על ידי עבודת התחתונים בקריאת שמע ותפלה ותורה ומצות (line 148)

Lines 149-153 close the chapter. The heart of the contemplation is "that there is no change in Him at all from before the world was created to after" (line 151). Two philosophical errors are both called heresy. One says the emanation became other than Him, and this error takes providence away from creatures (line 152). The other puts all powers hidden in Him, like hyle.

## Ch. 6-8 (154-174): R. Azriel's perfection; tzimtzum, kav, breaking and repair

R. Azriel's answer (line 155) is that a perfection without power in limit would be lacking. So He must be: > יש לנו לומר בהכרח שהוא בגבול מבלי גבול (line 155)

The parable is the perfect sage (lines 156-158). He is perfect only if he can "bear a thing and its opposite" and teach the smallest child. To do so he must contract his great wisdom. The drop in the ocean (line 159) is then called only "to soothe the ear" (line 160). Tzimtzum is set out: to reveal perfection in limit, He hid the infinite revelation and left a fine line (line 162). "All the tzimtzumim are in Or Ein Sof": in His light, not His Essence. Even the kav is infinite like Him (line 163). The breaking comes because vessel and light are "two opposites really" (line 166). The breaking and the repair happen only for the receivers (line 167). The repair lets the separate vessel receive the light and unite with it, because "in truth the receivers too are not outside Him" (line 168). Line 174 makes the sefirot the creatures themselves: > והם הם עשר ספירות דאצילות והם הם הנבראים ממש (line 174)

## Ch. 9-12 (175-200): where the parables fail; Keter as the joiner of opposites

Every parable is marked where it fails. The sage is composite and changes as he contracts; Ein Sof does not (line 175). "I fill heaven and earth" holds even in the levels that hide Him (line 176). The Ra'avad's rule is that the simple brings forth only the simple. Since there is none besides Him, "all that is drawn from Him is not other" (line 179). Keter is then set out as the hyle. At the instant an egg stops being an egg it both is and is not, and that is "ayin", like something sought and not found. The Kabbalists call this power Keter: > ובלשון המקובלים נקרא בחינה זו בשם כתר דהיינו כח המחבר שני הפכים ביחד ומאחדם באחדות נפלאה (line 186)

Unless He hides His boundless power, the limit in it cannot appear (line 187, F03). Line 189 marks where the egg fails as a parable: His will does not come to be or pass away, "but in truth all is He alone". The soul holds the body's powers hidden, but no "soul-power" may be placed in the Cause of causes (lines 191-194). Line 194 gives the gate's definition of the God of Israel: > כי אלה"י ישראל הוא היחוד העצום אשר אין זולתו יתברך כלל (line 194)

Creation from nothing means "the yesh is not in His power even hidden at all", and the author cites the Alter Rebbe's "He and His life are one" for this (line 196). The Idra's lamp: come near to the rays "and you find only the lamp" (line 198). Line 200 closes the chapter: "only to believe, with faith above intellect, that all is drawn from Him".

## Ch. 13-16 (201-224): Keter, Havayah, and yachid against echad

Keter is "ayin", but His Essence may not even be called ayin. He is the source of all sources (line 201). Keter is "black before the Cause of causes", and the darkness is the hiding (line 202). No wisdom forces the unity to be the way it is; all is made "in order to reveal His unity" (line 205). Havayah is the Name that brings all into being, yet "I, Havayah, have not changed" (line 213). The flame bound to a coal fails as a parable, because a flame, once caught in wood, leaves its coal, while the sefirot never leave Him (line 215). Line 216 gives the gate's inner practice. The heart runs to grasp the division and must return: > אם רץ לבך להשיג איך הוא התחלקותם שוב למקום שהיית בו דהיינו שאין זולתו (line 216)

"Atzilut" has two meanings, "set apart" and "beside Him" (etzlo). Then comes the distinction the SPINE also makes: > כנודע החילוק שבין יחיד לאחד כי תואר יחיד הוא דבר שהוא יחיד לבד ואין זולתו (line 223)

## Ch. 17-21 (225-251): negation; the three Atiks; knowing and not knowing

Not even "yachid" may be said of the Essence, only negation. It is called "without end", not "without beginning" (line 225). The author stresses this "because this knowledge is the root and essence of understanding His unity" (line 227). He is in every detail with particular providence: > והוא יתברך נמצא בכל פרט מריש כל דרגין עד שילשול קטן שבים בהשגחה פרטיות על כל דבר ודבר (line 231)

The Zohar's three Atiks become a map of contemplation. The first, Ein Sof Himself, may not be asked about at all. Yet: > כי אדרבה כל עיקר ההתבוננות ועבודתינו הכל הוא לקשר לאין סוף יתברך (line 233)

The second, Arich, may be asked about but not known: one seeks so as not to find, "the end of knowledge is that we do not know" (line 247). The third, Zeir Anpin, may be asked about and known. There F12's own verse is read as a command to know: > ועל זה נאמר וידעת היום כו' כי הוי"ה הוא האלהי"ם בשמים ממעל ועל הארץ מתחת אין עוד (line 236)

"We have no business with hidden things" is kept for the Essence apart from His extension (line 249). Line 251, another long line, defends R. Chaim Vital for writing nothing above Malchut of Adam Kadmon. It names the purpose of the tzimtzum and the kav: that the levels be revealed as separate "so that through this His unity will be revealed in heaven above and on earth below" (251.3). It calls reading the tzimtzum literally "a grave sin", excused only as a failure to understand: > ולהבין הצמצום כפשוטו שכביכול צמצם עצמו וזה עון פלילי (line 251)

## Ch. 22-28 (252-281): tzimtzum only for us; the place of the world; binding as the whole service

Every name, title and epithet comes from His revelation in worlds (line 252). There is no tzimtzum in His Essence. "Before Him darkness is as light" (line 254). All tzimtzumim belong to Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah, and the author cites the Alter Rebbe for this (line 262). The soul-body analogy is refused at its root: > אבל הקדוש ברוך הוא אין שייך בו לומר שנתפשט בתוך העולמות כמו הנשמה בגוף (line 263)

Instead, "He grasps all worlds and none grasps Him" (line 264). With the Rambam's knower and known, "the known, that is the vessels, are also His Essence" (line 265). But the word "knowledge" applies only in Atzilut, and the author cites the gloss in Shaar HaYichud ch. 9 for this (line 267). Chochmah is bitul without comprehension, and Binah is bitul through comprehension (line 269). "Cleave to His attributes" and "to Him and not to His attributes" are reconciled: the cleaving goes through His middot, but the call is to Ein Sof, who is united in them (line 273). The grades of unity the Kabbalists describe are our measuring, not changes in Him (line 279). The last gloss names the error the gate was written against (280.1): reading "before Him" as "before the tzimtzum", as if something changed after it. The gate ends on the limit of the person's power, and on what follows from that limit: > כי אין בידינו כח כי אם לקשר כל הדרגין אליו יתברך ביחוד גמור (line 281) > ממילא יעלה היחוד הזה בבחינת אין סוף בעצמותו יתברך (line 281)

## What this book adds, or says differently from the Tanya

- **"From our side" as a rule for every term.** The Tanya (SH 7) refutes the literal tzimtzum once. This gate makes "from our side" (מסטרא דילן) the rule for every Kabbalistic word: sefirot, vessels, Atzilut, Keter, tzimtzum, the Names, even "unity" and "knowledge". The tzimtzumim are "only in Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah" (line 262), and the author says he has this from the Alter Rebbe.
- **F09 carries the argument.** In the Tanya range F09 was a secondary face. Here "two opposites in one subject" is how the gate explains everything, and Keter is defined as the power that joins opposites (lines 184, 186).
- **The lower unity is the greater wonder.** The Tanya range presents SH 7's lower unity as the lower rung. This gate says it is "further from reason" than the higher: two wonders against one (147.7). It also says that in the future Malchut will stand above Binah and Ein Sof will be revealed "precisely in yesh" (147.12). This is close to what the SPINE calls F07 in the Rebbe's form (the yesh itself as the place of the Essence). The word "dwelling" does not appear, so the F07 tag there is carried by the idea, not the word.
- **A map of what may be asked.** The three Atiks give a graded rule (lines 233-249). At the Essence, no asking and no thought. At Arich, asking without knowing, seeking so as not to find. At Zeir Anpin, asking and knowing, "Know today". The question itself serves bitul (lines 142, 144).
- **The limits of bitul.** Bitul does not apply at Keter, "for on whom would it fall?" (147.4), because there is no second there. The Tanya gives nothing like this.
- **Service in exile as hidden service** (147.8-9). The son who stays nullified while his father hides his face shows more bitul than the son whose father shows his love.

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- **F06 is absent.** The gate never speaks of the soul as a part of God. Its soul analogies concern how any soul relates to its body, not the Jewish soul's root.
- **F10 and F11 appear only at the edges:** line 148 (Shema, prayer, Torah and mitzvot draw the higher unity down), line 150 (Torah and service with self-sacrifice), line 194 (the Torah's purpose is to unite all creatures), and the glosses 147.8, 251.10-11 and 280.3. The author says service will be explained "in the part on service". Its absence here is a matter of plan, not doctrine.
- **F07 is never named.** Its six tags are the three statements that creatures are revealed "as yesh" or "in heaven above and on earth below" by intent (147.6, 147.12, 251.3, 280.3) and two *reading* sub-entries (110.4, 178.1).
- **F01 is present but secondary** (continuous giving of being, providence at 152 and 231). Line 100.1, the stone that would perish without its "soul", is *reading*: the text means a natural life-force, not divine speech. - The kabbalistic mechanics are *structural*: Akudim (165), golem and form (190), the crowns of Zeir and Nukva inside 147, the palanquin (239), the grades of unity (278).

## Disagreements kept, not smoothed

- **Yachid, and for what.** The SPINE, with the Rebbe and the Rayatz, makes "yachid" the name for the Essence's unity. This gate agrees on what yachid and echad mean (line 223), but gives "yachid" only to Keter, His extension. It says that even "yachid" cannot be said of the Essence except as negation (lines 225, 227). The two usages stand. The SPINE's precision point should not be read into this book unchanged.
- **Knower and known.** In the Tanya range, SH 8 (Tanya line 849) states that His Essence and knowledge are one simple unity, with the Rambam. The SH 9 gloss (Tanya line 860) places "knower and known" after the tzimtzum, in the vessels. This gate reads SH 8 through that gloss: "knowledge" applies only in Atzilut, and of Him in Himself "the word knowledge does not apply at all" (line 267). It cites the gloss for this. Both sides are in the Tanya. This gate chooses one.
- **F12, knowing against believing.** The gate holds both sides the SPINE records, and gives each a place. "We have no business with hidden things" is applied to the Essence apart from His extension (line 249), which is narrower than a general limit on inquiry. "Know today" is a real command to know, at Zeir Anpin (line 236). But the knowing is itself for the sake of bitul, and above it lies "the end of knowledge is that we do not know" (line 247). This is closer to the Alter Rebbe's reserve in the Tanya than to the Rayatz's "toil to understand", and it is not the same as either.
- **Literal tzimtzum.** The gate states the Alter Rebbe's side as the SPINE's F03 records it, and states it harder: the literal reading is "a grave sin" (line 251) and "no greater destruction" of the faith (280.1). It excuses those who held it as erring "from lack of understanding". The other side (Tzava'at HaRivash, per the SPINE) is not in this range.
- **The soul analogy.** Like Iggeret HaKodesh 7 in the Tanya, the gate refuses "as a soul in a body" (line 263). Yet it uses the soul parable more than any other, from line 100 to line 279. It says each time where the parable holds (the soul is known only by its acts, it is equal in all limbs, the limbs do not change it) and where it fails (the soul is affected by the body, and the body is a separate thing).
