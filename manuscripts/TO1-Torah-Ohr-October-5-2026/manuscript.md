*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Maamarim on the weekly portions, delivered by the Alter Rebbe and edited by the Tzemach Tzedek. Each one starts from a verse or a saying of the Sages, builds a Kabbalistic account of the worlds (sefirot, Tohu and Tikun, the surrounding and indwelling light), and then often turns briefly to avodah: prayer, the Shema, Torah study, bitterness and joy, refining one's traits. Its aim is to show that the inner life of a Jew in prayer and Torah mirrors, and brings about, the divine dynamics it describes. Most of the text is cosmology. The therapeutic material is concentrated in short, practical turns, usually introduced with "the advice for this" (עצה היעוצה) or "let a person take to heart" (ישים האדם אל לבו).

**How much of it is medicine.** some. Most of each maamar is Kabbalistic cosmology, but the turns to avodah are dense and practical: prayer that fades, mind without heart, stray thoughts, worry about a livelihood, bitterness and joy, refining one's traits.

**Its model of the person.** A divine spark (the divine soul) is clothed in a body and in an animal, vital soul that hide it. The person is mind (understanding) and heart (love and awe). Feelings born of understanding are stable. Feelings cut off from it flare up and pass. Between mind and heart is the "narrow throat": worldly pleasures, thoughts and schemes that block understanding from reaching the heart. The voice joins them. A love inherited from the forefathers lies hidden like wine in grapes and comes out through pressing: humility and a broken heart. Temperament mixes into even good traits. Change is toil against habit and nature. It goes top-down (contemplation leads to feeling, then to deeds) or bottom-up (Torah and deeds first).

**Its philosophy.** All worlds, even the highest paradise, are only a radiance of God, and they are as nothing compared with His essence. The soul came down into a body and into the worries of this world so that it can rise higher than before: light that comes out of darkness shines more. The animal soul comes from a higher root (Tohu), so when it is turned toward God it brings a love without limit. Torah and mitzvot carry the Infinite light itself. Exile is a pregnancy whose birth is revelation and joy.

**What it adds.** Among early Chabad works it gives unusually concrete diagnoses of prayer that fails: love that fades because it is cut off from the mind; the \"narrow throat\" blocking mind from heart; the error of the working man who thinks he cannot pray. It offers graded remedies: the path from below for those who feel nothing, the voice, the red wine of bitterness before the white wine of joy, and compassion for the spark rather than for oneself. It gives an explicit dignity to people absorbed in making a living.

## My heart opens in prayer, and an hour later it's as if I never prayed

In prayer he is moved and loving. Afterwards the love is gone, and he turns to his own way, from one extreme to the other, without noticing. Sometimes the light disappears so completely that it feels as if he never prayed at all.

**What is really going on.** The feelings were never joined to the mind, so they stand alone and pass. Also his intention was for himself: he was satisfied with the experience in prayer and did not store the light in vessels of Torah study, so what he felt was dreamlike and not real.

**Program: The two wines: bitterness first, then joy.** (1) Become lowly in spirit on your own, not through others humbling you: "let my soul be as dust to all". (2) Take an honest account of the thoughts, words and deeds that were not for God. (3) Feel bitter over how far you have fallen, and cry out to God until you step out of the mindset you were bound to all day. (4) Direct compassion not at yourself but at the divine spark in you, how far it has come down. (5) Contemplate that the mitzvot are the Infinite light itself, and rejoice in doing them. (6) Keep the joy of the soul separate from the sadness of the body. (7) Afterwards store the light in vessels of Torah study, for God's delight and not your own. *When:* Before and during prayer. The bitter part is also placed at the bedtime Shema. The bitterness is a prelude to joy, not a state to stay in. *For:* Someone whose prayer is blocked by guilt, stray thoughts or a heart that feels like stone. *Not for:* The text itself warns against mixing joy with sadness. Not for someone depressed or overwhelmed by shame without guidance. `TO1·Q32` · `TO1·Q28` · `TO1·Q09` · `TO1·Q17` · `TO1·Q19` · `TO1·Q14` · `TO1·Q16` · `TO1·Q10`

**Program: Rise above the disturbances.** (1) Do not fight the disturbing forces on their own level. (2) Rise above them through Torah study, which is the source of their life, so they fade by themselves. (3) Be like dust: bear and absorb. (4) Delight in the Torah as God's own delight. That joy removes every obstacle within and without. *When:* Morning and evening ("rise early and stay late over them"). *For:* Someone beset by obstacles and distractions. *Not for:* No exclusion in the text. `TO1·Q13`

Joined to understanding, and kept in Torah for God's sake, it lasts.** Emotions born of the intellect are inward and stable. Light that is not held in vessels withdraws. `TO1·Q01` · `TO1·Q10` · `TO1·Q11`

## I'm too busy earning a living to be spiritual; prayer is for scholars, not me

All day he is swamped by thoughts and worries about making a living. He assumes people who sit and study can pray properly and that he cannot.

**What is really going on.** An error. The soul came down into worry about livelihood for the sake of an ascent. Love born out of that darkness is stronger and without limit, so the working man can pray more than the scholar.

**Program: Turning the workday into prayer.** (1) Acknowledge that the day was spent in the press of earning a living (the "many waters"). (2) In prayer, contemplate how every blade of grass has its star above, which receives from angels, and so on up to God's kingship. (3) Contemplate that all of this is only a radiance, nothing compared with His essence. (4) Let a longing arise to leave the darkness and cleave to Him alone: "whom have I in heaven". (5) Enter the words of prayer as Noah entered the ark, and let them lift you. *When:* Daily prayer, especially after being absorbed in work. Contemplated "in the depth of knowledge". *For:* Working people who feel second-class spiritually. *Not for:* No exclusion in the text. Not a substitute for practical help for someone in real financial crisis. `TO1·Q02` · `TO1·Q03` · `TO1·Q04`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm stuck in business all day; real prayer belongs to people who sit and learn."* to **Your immersion in worldly worry is the very darkness out of which a greater light and a boundless love can come. You can pray more than the scholar, not less.** Light out of darkness surpasses light alone. The animal soul's root in Tohu is higher than the divine soul's, so when it is turned toward God it brings a love that knows no limit. `TO1·Q02` · `TO1·Q03`

## I understand it all but I don't feel anything

He knows and understands the ideas of Godliness, but nothing stirs. His joy stays in his head and never reaches his heart.

**What is really going on.** The world's pleasures, thoughts and affairs sit in the "narrow throat" between mind and heart and block the passage. Some souls are simply unable to give birth to feeling from contemplation alone.

**Program: The path from below: do first, feel later.** (1) Recognize that you are not yet able to give birth to love and awe from contemplation alone. (2) Begin with regular Torah study (the six orders of the Mishnah are named) and with doing the mitzvot. (3) Let the Torah give strength to the divine soul and wear down the animal soul. (4) Through this you receive the power to come to love and awe. *When:* Ongoing and daily. The text calls this the normal path for most souls. *For:* Anyone who understands but feels nothing. *Not for:* The text reserves the reverse order, contemplation first, for great souls. `TO1·Q06` · `TO1·Q07`

**Program: Use the voice.** (1) If your mind is too small to stir love through thought alone, cry out to God aloud in prayer. (2) The voice arouses intention and brings feeling into the heart. (3) Read Torah aloud, so the voice calls the light out of the mind into the heart. (4) Pray among others: hearing their prayers wakes love even in someone who has none of his own. *When:* During prayer and Torah study. *For:* Someone whose contemplation stays in the head. *Not for:* No exclusion in the text. `TO1·Q18` · `TO1·Q26` · `TO1·Q20`

**Program: Birth pangs: feel the blockage, then the joy.** (1) Identify what blocks mind from heart: this world's pleasures, thoughts, affairs and schemes. (2) Feel the pain and bitterness that God's light is held back from your heart by them. (3) Contemplate the Infinite light in Torah and mitzvot until it touches the point of the heart. (4) Let the joy come; the obstacles fall away before it as darkness does before light. *When:* Daily, morning and evening, alongside remembering the Exodus. *For:* Someone whose understanding never becomes feeling. *Not for:* The pain is a birth pang, not an end in itself. `TO1·Q24` · `TO1·Q25` · `TO1·Q23` · `TO1·Q31`

**Reframe.** From *"I understand but feel nothing; I'm just not a spiritual person."* to **Knowing without being nourished by it is a named condition, and there is a path for it: start with study and deeds, which give you the power to feel.** Study and mitzvot strengthen the divine soul and weaken the animal soul, and this makes feeling possible from below upward. `TO1·Q06` · `TO1·Q07`

**Reframe.** From *"The blocks in my heart are too solid to move."* to **When joy in God is born in the heart, the obstacles turn out to be nothing and take up no place.** Joy is revelation. The obstacles have no substance of their own and fall away as darkness does before light. `TO1·Q31` · `TO1·Q23`

## Is my devotion real, or is it just my personality?

He loves learning and is devoted, but suspects his passion comes from his temperament rather than from God.

**What is really going on.** Natural temperament really can mix into the soul's traits, even ones that look wholly good. The mixture belongs to the outer layer of the heart. Knowledge (daat) draws out its inner layer, which is pure.

**Reframe.** From *"My passion for holy things is surely pure."* to **Even a trait that looks wholly good may carry some mixture of temperament. Seek the inner heart through knowledge.** Mixture belongs to the outer layer of the heart. Knowledge draws out the inner layer, where the mixture does not reach. `TO1·Q05`

## I get angry, jealous, and need to be above people

Anger, envy and the urge to lord it over others keep coming back. Changing them feels impossibly heavy.

**What is really going on.** The traits must be "tanned" until they are for God alone. That means changing ingrained habit and nature, and it is heavy precisely because it goes against one's nature.

**Program: Refining traits: changing nature, soft speech.** (1) Accept that refining your love, awe and interpersonal traits means changing habit and nature, and that this is heavy toil. (2) Name the target traits: anger, envy, arrogance, lording over others. (3) Answer softly. A soft answer quiets not only the other person's wrath but also your own. *When:* Continuous, in daily dealings with people. *For:* Someone struggling with anger, jealousy or a need to dominate. *Not for:* Not a call to swallow legitimate protest or stay in harm. `TO1·Q21` · `TO1·Q22`

**Reframe.** From *"I can't master myself; something is wrong with me."* to **Souls in this late era lack native strength, so toil is expected and not a failure, and God lights the darkness from above.** It lifts the verdict on oneself, puts effort in place of self-blame, and opens to help from above. `TO1·Q27` · `TO1·Q30` · `TO1·Q21`

## Why is all this suffering coming on me?

Hardships pile up and he reads them as proof that he is being punished or does not deserve good.

**What is really going on.** The maamar says suffering is not because he is unworthy of this world's good. It comes to break coarseness of spirit, so that a lowered self can reveal what is hidden.

**Reframe.** From *"This suffering means I'm being punished or don't deserve good."* to **The maamar says it is not unworthiness. Suffering comes to soften coarseness of spirit.** A self lowered like dust uncovers the hidden love and fear. Coarseness is what makes a person desire that his own will be done. `TO1·Q12` · `TO1·Q32`

## Cautions

- The 'red wine' of bitterness, crying out "until he leaves his mind", and accepting the four death penalties at the bedtime Shema could deepen depression or scrupulosity. The text insists it lead into joy and not be mixed with sadness. - "Sufferings come to break coarseness" should never be told to someone who is suffering as an explanation of illness, loss or abuse; it risks blaming the victim. - "Let my soul be as dust to all" can harm someone with low self-worth or in an abusive relationship. Lowliness here serves uncovering love, not self-erasure. - "One who blemished cannot rejoice in prayer" can reinforce shame. Give it only together with its remedy: compassion for the spark, then joy. - The humoral theory of temperament (black and white bile) is outdated medicine and must not be used to diagnose anyone. - "Later generations lack strength" should not become learned helplessness. The text pairs it with toil and with light from above. - "A soft answer" is for one's own anger and must not be pressed on someone who needs to set a boundary or leave danger.
