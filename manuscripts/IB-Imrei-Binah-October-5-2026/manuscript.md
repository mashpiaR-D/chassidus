*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** The Mitteler Rebbe gathers his father's teachings on the Shema and adds his own explanations. The work is organized as gates: Kerias Shema, Tzitzis and Tefillin. The author states its aim himself: in hard times, give ordinary people a way to settle the unity of God, the higher unity (Havaya) and the lower unity (Elokim), into mind and heart, so their souls can find rest. The introduction and the opening gate press that deep contemplation is required of everyone, each by his capacity. After that, most of the book is Kabbalistic exposition of the letters of the Shema, the sefirot and the worlds. Guidance for inner life comes up only here and there.

**How much of it is medicine.** some: the introduction, Pesach HaShaar sections 9-10 and 24, and a few Tzitzis passages give real psychology and method, but most of the range is dense Kabbalistic cosmology of letters, sefirot and worlds.

**Its model of the person.** The divine soul has mind (ChaBaD) and emotions. The emotions come in two kinds: those in the intellect and those in the heart. Knowing (daas) is a felt connection: thought held in depth until the thing is sensed like sight, as against thought that just passes through. Some emotions are innate. Once an emotion is born in the heart it can outgrow the mind, and the mind is a lamp showing which way to choose, not a ruler. Love comes from distance, awe from nearness. The soul's essence surrounds the body and is untouched by what happens to it. Change comes from contemplation that is deepened and kept in mind. Then a turning away from bad follows by itself (mimeila).

**Its philosophy.** God's unity has two sides. Havaya surrounds all worlds and lies beyond knowing, reachable only by faith. Elokim fills the worlds and can be known, as a soul is known in its body. The Shema commands bringing faith that is above knowledge down into knowing and feeling. A law built into creation says darkness comes before light, and every rise is preceded by a fall that matches it. The divine rests only where the self is set aside, never in grandness.

**What it adds.** This work insists that the Shema's commandment is felt knowing (daas as deepened thought, against hirhur), required of every simple Jew. It condemns both false humility and the collecting of Kabbalistic kavanos as ego. It spells out the law that darkness precedes light in everyday terms: money, children, learning, livelihood. It pairs love from distance with awe from nearness. It holds that a desire once born can outrun the mind, which is a lamp and not a ruler.

## Who am I to contemplate God deeply? I'm too small, too simple, too busy

He sees his own lowness and how busy he is, and concludes that deep contemplation belongs to great people. So he leaves it altogether and feels humble for doing so.

**What is really going on.** This humility is false. It is the evil inclination's advice to push him away from God's light. Every person, even one who can only read a book, must work at this by his own capacity, because the life of his soul depends on it.

**Program: Deepened contemplation of God's unity at the Shema, kept in mind all day.** (1) Take a little time away from business; don't excuse yourself by pleading lowness or busyness. (2) Keep one intention only: to bind the soul to God Himself, not to gather Kabbalistic intentions. (3) Take a known truth, e.g. as the soul gives life to the body, God gives life to all worlds (Elokim, memale). (4) Hold the thought in depth (not a passing thought) until it is felt, like seeing. (5) Bring faith in what is beyond knowing (Havaya, sovev) into that felt knowing. (6) Through the day, don't let it slip; constant remembrance is enough, and the feeling comes by itself. *When:* A short period taken from work, at the Shema and prayer, daily; remembrance carried through the day. *For:* Everyone according to his capacity, including someone who is only able to read a book. *Not for:* Not a vehicle for status or accumulating esoteric kavanos; the text says such seeking cannot taste the light. `IB·Q01` · `IB·Q03` · `IB·Q05` · `IB·Q06` · `IB·Q08` · `IB·Q14` · `IB·Q15` · `IB·Q16` · `IB·Q17`

**Program: Beginner's foundation: simple contemplation of creation from nothing.** (1) Start with simple contemplation: heaven, earth and all their hosts coming into being from nothing. ## Everything I work for is falling apart; I'm close to giving up

In his livelihood, his learning or his hopes, he has worked hard, suffered losses and comes close to despair.

**What is really going on.** Darkness always comes before light. Every rise, in money, standing, children or wisdom, is preceded by a fall and pain that match it. The near-despair is the evening before the morning.

**Reframe.** From *"I've lost so much and I'm near despair; it means I'm failing."* to **Every rise is preceded by a fall that matches it. Darkness comes before light in money, children, learning and livelihood. This is the evening before the morning.** Pain becomes the known first stage of a law of creation instead of a verdict, so the person can keep working through it instead of quitting. `IB·Q11` · `IB·Q12` · `IB·Q13`

## My heart is heavy and bitter when I come to pray

He feels his coarseness and his distance from God, and takes the heaviness to mean prayer is lost.

**What is really going on.** There is no love or joy without bitterness and submission first. The deeper the bitterness, the more joy lights up afterward through contemplating God's greatness.

**Program: Bitterness and submission before prayer, then joy through God's greatness.** (1) Before praying, take on seriousness (koved rosh): humble the heart before God. (2) Feel the darkness of the body, bound to physical desires and far from the King's light. (3) Let the heart be bitter over this and submit as deeply as it can. (4) Then contemplate God's greatness; the joy that comes lights up the bitterness that came before. *When:* At the start of prayer, each time one prays; the bitterness is a prelude, not the goal. *For:* Someone whose prayer is flat or self-satisfied and who has inner stability to spare. *Not for:* The depressed, the self-hating, or someone already crushed; for them the bitterness would never turn to joy. `IB·Q09` · `IB·Q10`

**Reframe.** From *"Bitterness and heaviness are the opposite of the joy I'm supposed to have."* to **There is no love or joy without submission first. The depth of bitterness is the darkness the coming joy will light.** Light only appears against the darkness before it (evening before morning). Bitterness that is gone through and aimed at God's greatness is the very thing joy lights up. `IB·Q09` · `IB·Q10`

## I feel so far from God

He feels exiled, distant and cut off, and reads this as proof that he is not close.

**What is really going on.** Love is born from distance, as a father longs most for a son who is far away. Nearness produces awe. The feeling of distance is the fuel of love.

**Reframe.** From *"I feel so far from God; I must be cut off."* to **Love is born from distance. The farther the beloved, the more the longing burns. Your distance is the fuel of love.** In the work's psychology love rises from distance and awe from nearness. So a sense of distance gets turned into longing instead of shame. `IB·Q21` · `IB·Q22`

## I pray with my lips, but all day my heart chases what it wants, and I don't even see a contradiction

He prays, learns and does mitzvos, and meanwhile the center of his heart goes to his cravings. He thinks he is fine because he avoids serious sins.

**What is really going on.** Without seeing divine light he cannot sense that he is in darkness, so he takes darkness for light. The divided heart does not notice itself.

**Program: Seeing the tekheles: contemplating the awe of His kingship until light shows the dark.** (1) Contemplate the greatness and awe of His kingship with clear recognition in heart and mind; accept His yoke fully. (2) Let that light show you the coarse darkness you took for light. (3) Set the whole heart to one Father in heaven, with no other master beside Him. (4) Turning away from empty pleasures then follows by itself (lo sasuru, mimeila). *When:* With the mitzvah of tzitzis and acceptance of the yoke of Heaven, daily. *For:* Someone living a double life who does not notice it, or who feels 'fine' because he avoids major sins. *Not for:* The scrupulous or those prone to shame; the text's harlot imagery and 'seeing oneself as truly bad' can wound. `IB·Q24` · `IB·Q25` · `IB·Q26` · `IB·Q29`

**Program: The floor for the busy: one master in the heart.** (1) Accept that constant devotion belongs to great tzaddikim, while this floor is in everyone's power. (2) While busy, trust God for your livelihood and cast your burden on Him; don't credit nature or luck. (3) Don't willfully bring yourself to impure fantasies. (4) When a bad thought falls in, push it away, be pained by it, and return (teshuvah). *When:* All day, in the middle of work and ordinary thoughts. *For:* Ordinary people swamped by work and making a living. *Not for:* Intrusive-thought sufferers (OCD) should not be told to fight or be pained by each thought; this could intensify it. `IB·Q27` · `IB·Q28` · `IB·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm basically fine; I keep away from serious sins."* to **Someone who has never seen light takes darkness for light. Feeling fine may simply mean the light is missing.** Self-knowledge depends on light. Once divine light is seen, the darkness is recognized, and turning away follows without a separate fight. `IB·Q24` · `IB·Q25` · `IB·Q29`

## I'm swamped with making a living and full of stray thoughts; I can't be spiritual

Most of his mind is taken by work and worldly thoughts. He can't live like the great tzaddikim, so he feels there is no point.

**What is really going on.** Constant devotion belongs to great tzaddikim. But every person can keep his heart from belonging to another master: trust God for his living, and don't willfully cultivate impure thoughts.

**Program: The floor for the busy: one master in the heart.** (1) Accept that constant devotion belongs to great tzaddikim, while this floor is in everyone's power. (2) While busy, trust God for your livelihood and cast your burden on Him; don't credit nature or luck. (3) Don't willfully bring yourself to impure fantasies. (4) When a bad thought falls in, push it away, be pained by it, and return (teshuvah). *When:* All day, in the middle of work and ordinary thoughts. *For:* Ordinary people swamped by work and making a living. *Not for:* Intrusive-thought sufferers (OCD) should not be told to fight or be pained by each thought; this could intensify it. `IB·Q27` · `IB·Q28` · `IB·Q29`

Keeping the heart given to one master is what counts, and that is in everyone's power. `IB·Q27` · `IB·Q28`

## Others feel joy in prayer; I feel bitterness. Something is wrong with me

*Its own term:* יש מתפעל כך ויש מתפעל כך

He compares his emotional response in prayer with other people's and finds his own lacking.

**What is really going on.** Hearts differ because minds are shaped differently. The same content moves one person to joy and another to bitterness.

**Reframe.** From *"Others feel joy in prayer and I feel bitterness; something is wrong with me."* to **Hearts differ. The same content moves one person to joy and another to bitterness, according to the shape of each mind.** Differences in emotion come from differences in how each mind takes things in. So one's own response is a legitimate path, not a failure. `IB·Q23`

## I know it's not good for me, but I can't make my heart stop wanting it

His reason has decided against something, and the desire keeps its full force.

**What is really going on.** Once a heart-emotion is aroused it can grow beyond the thought that produced it. Then even a contrary verdict of the mind cannot overturn it. The mind is a light for choosing, not a switch.

**Reframe.** From *"If I truly understood it was bad, I'd stop wanting it."* to **Emotions have their own existence. Once a heart-desire is born it can outgrow the thought behind it. The mind is a lamp for choosing, not a switch.** Blaming oneself for failing to 'think' a desire away stops. Understanding is seen as light that guides the choice, while the heart's force is recognized as real. `IB·Q31` · `IB·Q32`

## Cautions

- Bitterness before joy (G03) is harmful for a depressed or self-hating person; the text means bitterness only as a doorway to joy through God's greatness. - 'Near despair precedes success' could romanticize suffering or keep someone in ruinous losses; the text even praises learning pain 'until he sickens in body'. - The introduction ties hardship in making a living to 'poverty of daas'. Never tell a poor or suffering person that his troubles come from not contemplating. - Tzitzis passages use harlot imagery and 'seeing oneself as truly bad'. For the scrupulous or shame-prone this deepens self-condemnation. - 'Push away a bad thought and be pained' can worsen intrusive thoughts (OCD); intrusive thoughts need a gentler, non-combative response. - Rebuking 'false humility' can shame someone whose limits are real (illness, cognitive load). Offer it as an invitation, not a charge. - Awe as becoming 'like a stone, without feeling oneself' should not be taught to someone with dissociation or a fragile sense of self.

<a id="ib-q12"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">כמו שאנו רואי' גם בעסק פרנסה שלא יצליח שום אדם בלי יגיעת הנפש וצער רב בתחלה עד שיאמר נואש בנפשו כו' אז ימצא ריוח וברכה רבה</div>

As we see in making a living too: no one succeeds without toil of soul and much pain at first, until he says in his heart that it is hopeless; then he finds profit and great blessing. *Imrei Binah, Pesach HaShaar sec. 10 · line 52 · `IB·Q12` ✓*

<a id="ib-q13"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">משום דכל עלי' צ"ל ירידה ושפלות תחלה לפ"ע העלי' דוקא שהוא החשך שקודם לאור</div>

Because every ascent must be preceded by a descent and lowliness proportionate to that ascent, which is the darkness that precedes the light. *Imrei Binah, Pesach HaShaar sec. 10 · line 51 · `IB·Q13` ✓*

<a id="ib-q14"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">וענין ההרגשה היינו העמקת המחשבה כשמעמיק בידועה ולא כשעולה במח' בלתי עיון והעמקה שנק' בשם הרהור</div>

Feeling means deepening thought, when one goes deep into what one knows, not when it arises in thought without study and deepening, which is called a passing thought (hirhur). *Imrei Binah, Pesach HaShaar sec. 24 · line 101 · `IB·Q14` ✓*

<a id="ib-q15"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">אבל הדעת הוא ההתקשרות בעומק עד שירגיש בחוש אותו הדבר</div>

But daas is connecting in depth until one senses the matter tangibly. *Imrei Binah, Pesach HaShaar sec. 24 · line 101 · `IB·Q15` ✓*

<a id="ib-q16"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">אבל כשהוא מעמיק דעתו ולא יסיחנה ממילא ירגיש וא"צ אלא זכרון תמיד ושלא ישכח ויסיח דעתו</div>

But when he deepens his mind and does not divert it, he will feel it by itself; all that is needed is constant remembrance, not forgetting and not letting the mind slip. *Imrei Binah, Pesach HaShaar sec. 24 · line 101 · `IB·Q16` ✓*

<a id="ib-q17"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">כי הרי יוכל להרגיש בנפשו בהעמקת מחשבתו איך שהוא י"ת מחי' את העולמות כדמיון חיות הנפש בגוף</div>

For he can feel in his soul, by deepening his thought, how God gives life to the worlds, like the life of the soul in the body. *Imrei Binah, Pesach HaShaar sec. 24 · line 101 · `IB·Q17` ✓*

<a id="ib-q18"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">ומה שהוא מעצמות הנפש שאינה נתפסת ומתלבשת כלל בגוף הנה לא תתפעל כלל ממאורעות הגוף</div>

What belongs to the essence of the soul, which is not grasped or enclothed in the body at all, is not affected at all by the body's events. *Imrei Binah, Pesach HaShaar sec. 24 · line 101 · `IB·Q18` ✓*

<a id="ib-q19"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">לזה הטעם יש לאדם להרגיל א"ע להעמיק דעתו בתחלה רק בהתבוננות פשוטה בבריאת שמי' וארץ וכל צבאם מן הרוחניות אל הגשמיות מאין ליש</div>

For this reason a person should train himself to deepen his mind first only in simple contemplation of the creation of heaven and earth and all their hosts, from spiritual to physical, from nothing to something. *Imrei Binah, Shaar Kerias Shema sec. 26 · line 211 · `IB·Q19` ✓*

<a id="ib-q20"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">כל יגיעו בהתבוננו' בפרטית ההשתלשלו' דע"ס דאצילות באו"כ ולמעלה מעלה לא יוקבע בנפשו כלום</div>

All his toil in contemplating the details of the chain of the ten sefirot of Atzilus, lights and vessels and higher still, will fix nothing in his soul. *Imrei Binah, Shaar Kerias Shema sec. 26 · line 212 · `IB·Q20` ✓*

<a id="ib-q21"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">וכמ"כ בעבודה שבלב בתפלה התפעלות האהבה באה רק מהתבוננות הריחוק מאלקות</div>

So too in the service of the heart in prayer: the arousal of love comes only from contemplating one's distance from Godliness. *Imrei Binah, Shaar Kerias Shema sec. 74 · line 387 · `IB·Q21` ✓*

<a id="ib-q22"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">שכל שיתרחק ממנו בזמן ומקום יותר יותר תכלה נפשו אליו באה"ר ותתעורר האהבה והתשוקה אליו ביותר</div>

The farther he is from him in time and place, the more his soul pines for him with great love, and love and longing for him are aroused all the more. *Imrei Binah, Shaar Kerias Shema sec. 74 · line 387 · `IB·Q22` ✓*

<a id="ib-q23"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">יש מתפעל בחדוה ויש מתפעל במרירות וגם שהתפעלות שניהם מדבר א'</div>

One is moved to joy and another is moved to bitterness, even though both are moved by the same thing. *Imrei Binah, Shaar Kerias Shema sec. 68 · line 366 · `IB·Q23` ✓*

<a id="ib-q24"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">לא ירגיש בעצמו שהוא בחשך ורע ולא יתן לבבו לזה כלל ונדמה לו שאינו בחשך כלל ושם חשך לאור</div>

He will not feel that he is in darkness and bad, will pay it no attention at all, and it will seem to him that he is not in darkness at all, taking darkness for light. *Imrei Binah, Shaar HaTzitzis sec. 17 · line 544 · `IB·Q24` ✓*

<a id="ib-q25"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">וזהו עיקר התערובת קשה שבפיו ולבו בתפלה ותו"מ לה' וכל היום הולך אחר כל תאות לבו רק רע ואינם סותרים זא"ז כלל</div>

This is the main harsh mixture: with mouth and heart in prayer, Torah and mitzvos he is for God, and all day he follows every desire of his heart, only bad, and to him these do not contradict each other at all. *Imrei Binah, Shaar HaTzitzis sec. 17 · line 544 · `IB·Q25` ✓*

<a id="ib-q26"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">אבל כל שיכיר באור האלקי יותר יותר ירגיש בנפשו אפי' עון שדש בעקביו</div>

But the more he recognizes the divine light, the more he will feel in his soul even a sin one tramples with one's heels. *Imrei Binah, Shaar HaTzitzis sec. 17 · line 544 · `IB·Q26` ✓*

<a id="ib-q27"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">וגם אם טרוד בעסקיו וחושב מחשבות רבות שלא לה' אבל הכל יבטח בה' וישליך על ה' יהבו</div>

Even if he is busy with his affairs and thinks many thoughts not for God, in everything he should trust in God and cast his burden on God. *Imrei Binah, Shaar HaTzitzis sec. 17 · line 544 · `IB·Q27` ✓*

<a id="ib-q28"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">אדרבה בנפול לו איזה מחשבה רעה ידחנה ויצר לו מזה ויעשה תשובה ועכ"פ בזה אינו נקרא זונה</div>

On the contrary, when some bad thought falls into his mind he should push it away, be pained by it and return; in this at least he is not called unfaithful. *Imrei Binah, Shaar HaTzitzis sec. 17 · line 544 · `IB·Q28` ✓*

<a id="ib-q29"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">וזה יראה כל אדם בנפשו אם לא ירצה להטעו' א"ע שלבבו חלק מיעוטו להש בפיו ושפתיו יכבד</div>

Every person will see this in his own soul if he does not want to fool himself: his heart is divided, the smaller part for God, with his mouth and lips he honors Him. *Imrei Binah, Shaar HaTzitzis sec. 17 · line 543 · `IB·Q29` ✓*

<a id="ib-q30"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">למי שכל ימיו הי' בחושך ולא ראה אור כלל כשיראה אור יתפעל ביותר ויכיר יותר בהפלאת יתרון מעלת האור על החושך</div>

One who was in darkness all his days and never saw light, when he sees light he is moved all the more and recognizes more how far light surpasses darkness. *Imrei Binah, Shaar HaTzitzis sec. 16 · line 541 · `IB·Q30` ✓*
