## The shape of the range

The first range (lines 1-759) asked why there are worlds at all. This range asks how a person reaches the Essence, and what reaches it. The answer runs through all six maamarim. Light, even the highest light, runs back to its source. Only the Essence, which has no source to run to, can come down and stay. So the person who most truly reaches the Essence is the one who comes back down: into Torah and mitzvot, into the animal soul, into eating and business. The range's plainest statement of this is:

> דבחי' השוב באמת נטוע ומושרש בעצמות א"ס ב"ה (line 2008)

"Return (shov) is truly planted and rooted in the Essence of Ein Sof."

F05 (the Essence) still leads the count. But F08 (bitul), F11 (darkness turned to light) and F06 (the soul) come right behind it, and F12 (knowing) is far more frequent than in lines 1-759. The range moves from cosmology into service. Most of its statements about the unity are statements about what a person does. The purely cosmological faces F01 to F04 come back in force in only two places. The first is *Yehuda Ata* (1728-1766), on the light clothed in worlds and sovev. The second is *Vayelech Ish* (1856-1895), which is the Rashab's reworking of Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2-3.

## Vayishlach Yaakov, second half (1422-1509): the intermediary that does not divide

The maamar was building a theory of the intermediary (memutza). The interpreter passes on only what he takes from the teacher; the teacher's own mind stays apart from him (1422). So Keter holds Atik, which is "wholly removed", and Arich, whose lights reach the worlds. Atzilus as a whole is an intermediary in which the Essence comes down, and so "two opposites can be united", the infinite Emanator and the created worlds (1429, F09):

> וע"י אצי' יוכלו להתייחד ב' הפכיים (line 1429)

Then comes the range's first key distinction. There are three ways for upper and lower to join: the upper comes down, the lower goes up, or both meet in the middle. The covenant is the meeting in the middle: souls rise to Arich, and Or Ein Sof lowers Himself into Atik (1427). Torah is also an intermediary between the Essence and the souls. But it is a different kind (1438, F10 + F06 + F05):

> אבל בתורה אין הממוצע מפסיק, ומתחברים נש"י בבחי' עצמות אוא"ס ב"ה כו (line 1438)

In the worlds the intermediary divides, and only a glow comes through. In Torah it does not divide, and souls are joined to the Essence itself.

The rest of the maamar ranks what reaches the Essence. Torah's hidden wisdom carries the Essence's own pleasure, and in the future souls will feel it as the reasons of Torah and mitzvot (1440). Above that is the pleasure hidden in the Essence itself, which is never felt (1450, 1459). Souls can rise even there, because they are rooted higher than Torah's wisdom (1452):

> כי להיות דנש"י מושרשים בבחי' עצמיות ממש שלמעלה מבחי' חכ' דתורה (line 1452)

Only teshuvah from the depth of the heart reaches this level. The ba'al teshuvah's pain touches "the essential point of his Jewishness" (1492.1). Narrowness produces force out of all proportion (1489), and so teshuvah is a leap rather than a graded climb (1492.2). "He answered me with expanse" is the expanse of the Essence as it is in itself, because anything drawn down is by that no longer true expanse (1492.3). Then comes the practical turn. Teshuvah draws new light into Torah and mitzvot (1496-1497). Esau's evil can be refined only by teshuvah, "which turns even evil to good", and only after one has refined the good (1507, F11).

## Ner Chanukah (1510-1618): fire, breath, and why the mitzvah comes first

The maamar asks why "a mitzvah is a lamp" comes before "Torah is light". It builds a psychology of fire (neshamah, the mind) and breath (ruach, the emotions and the will). Fire only withdraws upward. Breath rises and also comes down, because it relates to an other (1531, 1556). The mind cleaves upward "in bitul and real union" (1549). The emotions give life to the practical mitzvah.

Line 1556 describes the mind with an image the Rebbe later uses for the Essence and for Chassidus (SPINE, Inyanah 7-8): "like oil, which mixes with nothing, and though it permeates everything, wherever it is it is set apart". Here it is said of the intellect. We mark it *reading*, not stated.

The unity comes in at two places. First, the Shema is the contemplation that rouses the soul's fire (1581.2, F12 + F02 + F05):

> ובחי' הוי', שזהו בחי עצמות אוא"ס, הוא עצמו ית' הוא אלקינו (line 1581)

For Havayah, past, present and future are equal, and "all before Him counts as nothing". And this very Essence is "our God", the source of life of the souls, while other creatures draw life from Malchus. Second, ratzo alone is Tohu. The purpose is shov, drawing light into vessels (1595, F07):

> שזהו רצון העצמי בבחי' פנימיות ועצמות א"ס, להמשיך גילוי אלקות למטה דוקא (line 1595)

The mitzvot are rooted in the supernal will above Torah's wisdom, and "all the light of Torah depends on the mitzvot" (1609, 1612). Chanukah follows from this. The Greeks defiled the oil of holy wisdom. Torah's light can "light up the darkness", even outside in the public domain, but it shines only after the mitzvah is done in deed and with self-sacrifice: mezuzah on the right, then the lamp on the left (1614, F11).

## Vayigash (1619-1717): who rouses the soul, and what counts as service

A spark does not rise just because it comes from the fire. The pull toward a source needs a felt source, and creatures, like vessels, do not feel theirs (1623, 1630). The soul at its root is "in utmost cleaving and unity with Godliness" (1630). Once it has come down, it is a "something", and its ratzo comes only now and then. The ratzo comes from above: from the inner Essence ("toward the face of the menorah", 1632), through the soul's ruach, which is its will (1667). The divine life that creates all worlds also runs and returns (1642, F01). And the true will of the Essence is in shov (1667, F07):

> ואמיתית הרצון דעצמות א"ס הוא בבחי' השוב (line 1667)

Then the maamar turns on its own teaching. If the rousing comes from above, it is not service. The sudden morning rousing is only a preface and quickly passes. The service is the toil it makes possible: contemplation until "the divine idea shines in the soul", and making the animal soul itself understand and love (1674, 1679, F12 + F11). Aaron's lighting is different, because the higher love it draws is itself a service (1697).

## Yehuda Ata (1718-1837): light is not Essence, and the two knowledges

This maamar holds the range's clearest cosmology. The light clothed in the worlds is only an outer, contracted glow (1729, F04 + F03 + F05):

> דהנה, עצמות אור א"ס אינו בערך העולמות כלל (line 1729)

The Essence, revealed, would leave no room for worlds, so there came the tzimtzum and the kav (1729.1). The light is then graded. The essence of the kav shines above Atzilus. In Atzilus shines the "essence of the radiance". In BYA only the "existence" of Godliness shines (1729.2). The lowest creatures do not even feel that, and so they can be opposed to Godliness (1729.3, F03):

> מ"מ, אינו מרגיש שהחיות הוא אלקות, עד שיכול להיות ח"ו היפוך מאלקות (line 1729)

Sovev is not "above". It is inside every creature and is its main life, but it is not felt, and so it raises no world to its own level (1736-1737, F04, citing Tanya 48). Mitzvot draw the surrounding light, but only as a makif (1740). On Shabbat the makif is felt inwardly. In Torah it "becomes truly inner light" (1766). That is Moses against Aaron. Human arousal reaches only the chain of worlds. The Or Ein Sof above the chain "takes no account of the creatures' service", and it is given as a gift, though only when one's own work is complete (1774).

Then comes the range's sharpest statement on knowing (1810, F08 + F12 + F02). Lower knowledge is "bitul hayesh only": the self is nullified yet still a something. That leaves room for a knowledge of the other side, which can choose evil. Higher knowledge knows the Essence of Or Ein Sof. It is recognition and feeling, not intellectual knowing, and it leaves no such room:

> להיות דד"ע הוא ענין בחי' הביטול במציאות (line 1810)

The Shema's contemplation is spelled out (1826.2, F02):

> שאין שום דבר מעלים ומסתיר על אוא"ס, וה"ה מאיר בגילוי על העולמות גם לאחר שנתהוו (line 1826)

In that fire the animal soul too is "burned and turned to light" (1826.3). Pesukei deZimra is the contemplation of creation renewed daily, "and truly every moment" (1832, F01). Judah (the Amidah) is bitul in existence, reached only through Reuben, Simeon and Levi (1836).

## Vayelech Ish (1838-1968): the Rashab's Shaar HaYichud 2-3

This maamar takes up the craftsman and the sun's ray, the parables of Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2-3, and presses them further. A vessel stands apart from its maker, and even completes him with a use he lacks. The ray needs the sun always, and within the sun it is nullified (1857). Then the Rashab goes past the Tanya in three steps:

1. The ray is of the sun's own kind, something from something. Creatures come from absolute nothing, and "no creature compares to the Creator" (1871.2).
2. Yet the radiance that sustains them is "their whole reality": > שבאמת כל מציאות הנבראים הוא האין האלקי המהווה אותם ומחי' אותם (line 1871)
3. Their being-something is "only in their own feeling" (1863.1). They are also present in their source, as the ten sefirot hidden in the Essence, so He lacks none of their perfection (1863.2, 1881). This is why "He who planted the ear" hears: providence follows from the hidden sefirot.

He names two nullifications that are opposites (1879, F02 + F09). Needing His light always is closeness and relation. Adding nothing to Him is distance. He adds a third, which the ray lacks: their very existence is beyond comparison with their source (1884). The angels see all this in Malchus of Atzilus, and it is what makes them cry "holy" (1894).

The rest of the maamar is prayer as offering. The fire below is Pesukei deZimra. The fire from above belonging to the animal soul is the angels' love (1841, 1897). The fire of Havayah is the souls' flame in the Shema, from the Essence, in which "the animal soul is burned entirely, bitul in existence" (1915). Eating, the table that "atones like the altar" (1917), and honest business also refine the animal soul. On Shabbat the soul itself is bound to Or Ein Sof, like incense (1931). It rises only through the work of refining the animal soul (1936, F11):

> והיינו שע"י העבודה בבירור הנה"ב, עי"ז דוקא הוא העלי' דנה"א (line 1936)

## Ki Ka'asher HaShamayim (1969-2034): shov is rooted in the Essence

This is the range's peak. Shov is a person's own work even more than ratzo is (1975-1977). Shov is higher in its root because it is the purpose, and it draws on the inner side of Or Ein Sof (1976). In Chochmah, seeing the essence brings shov: one is "nullified from his existence" and can only make vessels below (1985, F08 + F07). Knowing the Essence has a route. First comes grasping the negation. Then comes knowing the wondrousness. Then comes recognition, and only then seeing (1996, F12).

The core argument follows. Light runs back to its source because it is only a glow. The Essence has no source to run to, and it does not settle into vessels like light. So it is drawn through the practical mitzvot, which are "outside the category of vessel" (2005, F05 + F10):

> ולכן המשכת בחי' העצמות הוא במצות מעשיות דוקא, שאינו בגדר כלי (line 2005)

Shov is planted in the Essence as the desire for a dwelling below, "for which there is no reason at all" (2008). In the soul, shov is its essential bitul: it does not expire into the Essence but returns below. So a person finds no rest except by drawing light into speech and deed, "being nothing in essence" (2010).

Then comes the range's most important sentence about the unity (2019, F02 + F07 + F11). It is easier to expire than to live rightly below. The higher unity alone says that the world has no existence at all, as before it was created (2019.1). The union of the higher and the lower unity is higher:

> אבל ענין יחוד יחו"ע ויחו"ת הוא, שגם כמו שהעולם הוא במציאות, הוא בבחי' ביטול ויחוד בתכלית (line 2019)

> והיינו שהוא ע'"י עבודתו מהפך את הדברים הגשמיים שנעשים אלקות (line 2019)

Accepting the yoke is one's own act, like a people crowning a king (2026, 2029). Yet it comes from the soul's essence being "bound and united with the truth of His Essence" (2029). In the future, run and return will come "from the Essence of Or Ein Sof", and today's service will be raised into it (2031).

## HaChodesh HaZeh (2035-2062), opening only

God's greatness is known from nature, whose unceasing order shows "the power of the infinite", and still more from what is above nature (2036, F01 + F09 + F03). The range ends as the maamar asks why service must be one's own when the love from above is higher. It begins to answer with sons and servants, knowing Him "in general" or "in detail" (2055).

## What this range adds that the Tanya does not say (or says differently)

- **The sun-ray parable is corrected, not only used.** Shaar HaYichud 3 compares creation to the ray nullified in the sun. The Rashab says the ray is something from something, so creatures are nullified *more* than the ray. They are beyond comparison, and their being-something is "only in their feeling" (1863, 1871, 1884). He also adds a point the Tanya does not make: their perfection is in Him as the hidden sefirot, so He lacks nothing (1863.2, 1881).
- **Newness, not cleaving, is why creatures depend.** A ray cannot exist without the sun because it is light cleaving to its source. Creatures cannot exist without their source mainly because they are new (1860). F01's "every moment" becomes the reason for F02.
- **The union of higher and lower unity ranks above the higher unity.** Shaar HaYichud 7 treats the Shema's higher unity as the height of the teaching. The Rashab puts above it the physical world, kept as it is and made Godliness through a person's service (2019.2). The SPINE's F02 names both unities. This range ranks them.
- **Why the practical mitzvah.** Tanya 35-37 says the mitzvah draws Or Ein Sof into the physical. The Rashab gives the mechanism: the Essence neither runs back nor settles into a vessel, and the practical mitzvah is "outside the category of vessel" (2005). He also gives the order: without the mitzvah even Torah's light does not shine in a defiled soul (1612, 1614).
- **Shov above ratzo.** Tanya 50 praises love like flashes of fire. Here the flame is the easy part (2016). Return rooted in the Essence is the higher part (1976, 2008, 2025).
- **The mind's route to the Essence.** Negation, wondrousness, recognition, then seeing (1996). Seeing, once reached, produces shov (1985). The Tanya does not map this route.
- **Torah as an intermediary that does not divide** (1438), unlike the intermediaries in the worlds.

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- 335 lines are editor's footnotes, and all are *none*. Some carry real content: the Rebbe's notes at 1457, 1463 and 2057, and the Rashab's own glosses at 1809 and 1908. At 1876 the manuscript has a sentence the Rashab struck out. It said creatures themselves cannot be said to be included in Him, only their life. The printed text at 1863 says otherwise (their existence is there as the hidden sefirot). We note this hesitation and do not tag it. - The physical parables are *structural* with no face: fire, air and wick (1530-1531, 1569-1579), the craftsman (1857 is tagged only for the ray side), the child's schooling and the house-dedication (1686), and the vow (2017). - Kabbalistic mapping is structural unless the text itself draws the unity from it: Joseph and Judah as Yesod and Malchus (1620, 1708-1709), the forty cubits of the Ulam (1792), and the Names MaH and BaN (1905, 1917).
- *Reading* is used twice. At 1540 the mind's silence before a new idea is heard as bitul. At 1556 the oil image is said of intellect, and we connect it to the Rebbe's later use for the Essence. - F03 (the hiding) is rare, with 5 tags. The range speaks of concealment mostly as the animal soul's (1674) or the sitra achra's in a question of Torah (1514), and only once as a cosmic tzimtzum (1729). - F09 (the unity of opposites) appears at only 7 places. It is tagged where the text itself names opposites joined: Atzilus uniting two opposites (1429), the two opposite nullifications (1879), and the physical made Godliness (2019.2).

## Disagreements with the Tanya, kept

- **Does the soul rise by nature?** Tanya 19 says the soul, like a flame, by nature wants to leave the body and be included in its source. Line 1623 says a pull toward the source needs one to feel the source. It says the soul's ratzo comes from a rousing from above, through the ruach, not from nature alone. The Rashab reconciles this at 1568: the Tanya is speaking of Chochmah in the soul, while ruach by nature binds itself to an other. The emphasis still differs, and we keep it.
- **Which unity is higher?** In Shaar HaYichud 1-7 the higher unity (the world is nothing before Him) is the higher rung. At 2019 the Rashab says the union of both unities is "far above" the higher unity. One can read Tanya 33 and 36 (a dwelling in the lowest place) as saying the same. But the Tanya never ranks the two in these words. The Rashab does.
- **Knowing the Essence.** The Alter Rebbe (SH 7) leaves the unity of His Essence to faith above intellect. The Rashab gives a route by which the mind reaches "seeing the essence", through negation and recognition (1996). He also calls higher knowledge "knowledge of the Essence of Or Ein Sof" that is recognition, not intellect (1810). This is the same tension recorded for lines 1-759, and it is sharper here.
- **The sun-ray parable.** The Tanya (SH 3) uses the ray as the model of nullification. The Rashab says the model understates it (1871, 1892). The Tanya's point stands, but the Rashab calls it insufficient.
