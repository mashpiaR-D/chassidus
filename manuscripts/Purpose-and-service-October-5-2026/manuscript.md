<a id="c40"></a> ## C40 · My ordinary life feels spiritually empty

*6 findings from Tanya, Likkutei Torah (part 3 of 3), Hayom Yom, Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, Likkutei Sichos (part 1 of 3), Chanah Ariel*

> Only prayer and study seem to count; my work day is dead time. I'm alive but not living. I'd rather soar in lofty spiritual heights than deal with what's in front of me.

**What is really going on.** The condition has two faces, and the works answer both. On the empty ordinary: Tanya says the world exists because God wanted a dwelling in the lowest place, so the working day is the very site of the purpose. Charity given with joy from hard-earned money redeems the heart's inner point that was caught up in business. Likkutei Sichos: the few holy hours define who you are in quality, even if they are few in quantity, and the rest can feel secondary to that core. Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus: for a king's child, life means connection with the Father. Modeh Ani on waking should stretch across every moment, and every morning you are a new creation. On escaping to the heights: Likkutei Torah calls running upward alone a kind of pride; every run must return into practical deeds. Hayom Yom: a lofty urge that shows up just when a practical duty is due is the animal soul's trick. Chanah Ariel: raw fire is holy, but once mature it must be ordered with judgment.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"Only prayer and study count; my working day is dead time."* → The purpose of creation is a dwelling in this lowest world, and your working day is its site. Charity from your toil, given with joy, redeems the heart's inner point. `TAN·Q16` · `TAN·Q31`
- *"Most of my time is mundane, so I'm mostly mundane."* → That is quantity. In quality, the hours of prayer, study and good deeds determine your true being. `LS1·Q08` · `LS1·Q09`
- *"Today is just another day in the same old world."* → Every morning a person becomes a new creation, because all of reality is renewed at every moment. `INY·Q10` · `INY·Q24`
- *"Real spirituality means rising above the world."* → Running upward alone is pride. There must be running and returning, into study, charity, kindness and prayer. `LT3·Q36`
- *"This urge toward something higher must be good, even if it stops me doing my duty."* → Whatever blocks actual practice, even something loftier, is the animal soul's trick. It can dress as a humble tzaddik. `HY·Q12` · `HY·Q13`
- *"My fire for God frees me from ordinary order and judgment."* → Once the fire has matured into understanding, you must order your words and ways with judgment. `CA·Q40`

1. **Wake into connection, and carry it through the day** · *from Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus (On the Essence of Chassidus)* (1) On waking, before anything else, say Modeh Ani and thank God for returning your soul. (2) Reflect at once before Whom you are lying. (3) Let the thanks be for life as connection, not just for being alive in a body. (4) Sense that this morning you are a new creation. (5) Remember that whatever else is lacking, this thanks comes from a point that stays whole. (6) Get up with energy and stretch that moment across the day, thinking the same way in every task. *When:* Seconds on waking, then held through every moment of the day. *For:* Anyone, especially someone who starts the day heavy or flat. *Not for:* It doesn't replace care for depression. Never use its line that a life without Godliness is no life, or that one would loathe such a life, with someone who already hates their life. `INY·Q06` · `INY·Q24` · `INY·Q07` · `INY·Q10` · `INY·Q04`
2. **Raise the ordinary: charity from your own toil, given with joy** · *from Tanya* (1) Hold the purpose: your ordinary day is where the dwelling is built. (2) From what you earn by your own work, give with joy and a good heart. (3) Know that this redeems the inner point of your heart that was caught in business. *When:* As earnings come in, and regularly. *For:* Those who feel their working life is spiritually worthless. *Not for:* Not a call to give beyond one's means under pressure. `TAN·Q16` · `TAN·Q31`
3. **Weigh your life by quality** · *from Likkutei Sichos* (1) Notice the melancholy thought: 'how can I be holy when most of my time is mundane?' (2) Tell quantity apart from quality. (3) Recognise that your holy hours determine who you really are. (4) Carry that into work, so the mundane feels secondary to your core. *When:* Whenever the thought arises. *For:* Working people who feel their spiritual life is too small to count. *Not for:* Not a license to neglect the fixed times for study and prayer that the teaching assumes. `LS1·Q08` · `LS1·Q09`
4. **Bring the height down into the deed** · *from Likkutei Torah, Hayom Yom, Chanah Ariel* (1) When love and longing rise, let them rise. (2) Then return: bring it into practical study, charity, kindness and prayer. (3) When a lofty urge appears just as a concrete duty is due, recognise the trick and do the concrete thing first. (4) Once the fire has matured, order your words and ways with judgment. *When:* Whenever inspiration or a 'higher' impulse competes with what is in front of you. *For:* Idealists prone to spiritual highs that escape life, and people who procrastinate through spirituality. *Not for:* Not to override needed rest or healthy limits. `LT3·Q36` · `HY·Q13` · `CA·Q40`

- From *"My working day is dead time; only prayer and study count."* to **The world exists so God can have a dwelling in the lowest place. What you give with joy from your own toil redeems your heart's inner point.** The ordinary becomes the site of the purpose, not a distraction from it. *(Tanya)* `TAN·Q16` · `TAN·Q31` - From *"Most of my life is mundane, so I'm mostly mundane."* to **You are defined by the quality of your holy hours, not their number.** The core colours the periphery, and the sense of being small no longer defines him. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS1·Q09` - From *"I'm alive and I get through the days; isn't that enough?"* to **For a king's child, life means connection with the Father. Days lived without it are not yet fully living.** What counts as living changes from bodily existence to connection. *(Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus (On the Essence of Chassidus))* `INY·Q08` · `INY·Q09` · `INY·Q20` - From *"Today is just another day in the same old world."* to **Every morning you are a new creation, and all of reality is renewed at every moment.** Renewal becomes something felt, not only known. The reader notes that applying it to a personal fresh start is an inference. *(Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus (On the Essence of Chassidus))* `INY·Q10` - From *"Real spirituality is rising above the world."* to **Running upward alone is pride. The height lies in returning to make a dwelling below.** It points the soul's longing toward concrete deeds. *(Likkutei Torah)* `LT3·Q36` - From *"This urge toward something higher must be good."* to **Whatever blocks actual practice, even something loftier, is a trick dressed as piety.** A clear test, whether it leads to action, sees through self-deception in spiritual clothes. *(Hayom Yom)* `HY·Q12` · `HY·Q13` - From *"My fire exempts me from order and judgment."* to **Raw fire is holy, but once mature it must be ordered with judgment.** It honors the fire while folding it into mind and ordered feeling. *(Chanah Ariel)* `CA·Q40`

**Where the works agree.** The ordinary is not waste. It is where the meaning is meant to live, and a spirituality that flees the concrete is suspect. Every lift is meant to come back down into a deed.

**Where they differ.** Likkutei Sichos consoles: a few holy hours define you, and the rest can stay secondary. Tanya makes the mundane itself the site of purpose, through charity from toil. Inyanah is the most demanding: every moment should carry the waking awareness, and days without it do not count as life. So the works range from reassurance to challenge.

**Order and timing.** On waking, Modeh Ani. Through the working day, hold the awareness, and when a lofty urge competes with a duty, do the duty first. As earnings come, give with joy. When a spiritual high comes, return it to a deed. When the day feels small, weigh it by quality.

- 'Alive but not living' can be depression. Refer to care (see C14). Inyanah's line that such a life is to be loathed must never reach someone who already hates their life. - Charity must not be pressed beyond means. - 'A lofty urge is a trick' must not override needed rest or boundaries. - A sudden burning fire that ignores all norms and sleep can be a sign of mania and needs medical attention, not only the counsel to order it with judgment. - Burnout from overwork is not spiritual emptiness and needs rest, not more meaning-making.

<a id="c41"></a> ## C41 · Trying to help others, and it isn't working

*3 findings from Likkutei Sichos (part 1 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3), Pelach HaRimon*

> I'm not good enough to guide anyone until I fix myself. When I try, my fervor overwhelms them. People let me down, and I wonder what the point of all my effort is.

**What is really going on.** The evidence here is thin: three passages from three works. Likkutei Sichos calls it an error to wait until one is complete before influencing others. The decision to bring others close is itself what reveals a person's true self and helps his own work, even if he sees himself at the very bottom. A Likkutei Sichos letter to a disheartened educator answers others' failures with 'Abraham was one': one person's work carries weight regardless of numbers. Pelach HaRimon says guidance only lands when it is measured to what the listener can hold and given calmly, and that fire and a loud voice cannot be taken in.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"I have to fix myself before I can help anyone."* → This is called an error. Deciding to bring others close is what reveals your true self and raises you, even if you see yourself at the very bottom. `LS1·Q13` · `LS1·Q12` · `LS1·Q11`
- *"Others failed, so my effort is worthless."* → One person can inherit the land: Abraham was one. `LS2·Q20`
- *"If I speak with more fire, they'll be moved more."* → Guidance given in fiery excitement and loudly cannot be taken in properly. Only calm speech is received. `PR·Q30`

1. **Rise by turning toward others** · *from Likkutei Sichos* (1) Even if you see yourself at the very bottom, don't make your own completeness a precondition. (2) Decide to give a real part of your life to bringing others toward good. (3) Know that this decision itself reveals your true self and helps your own work. (4) Take each chance to help as it comes, without long calculations. *When:* A life decision, acted on at every opportunity. *For:* Someone who holds back from doing good because they feel unworthy. *Not for:* Someone using service to others to avoid needed personal help, or giving to the point of burnout. `LS1·Q11` · `LS1·Q12` · `LS1·Q13`
2. **Guiding calmly and to measure** · *from Pelach HaRimon* (1) Hold back your own fire and loudness when teaching or guiding. (2) Give only as much as the other person can hold. (3) Speak calmly, since calm words are the ones that are heard. *When:* Whenever teaching or guiding. *For:* Mentors, teachers, parents, anyone trying to help another. *Not for:* The text sets no limit. `PR·Q30`

- From *"I have to fix myself before I can help anyone."* to **Choosing to help others is itself what raises and repairs you.** The decision reveals the person's true self and draws help into his own work, so he is 'adorned first' by the very act he thought had to wait. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS1·Q12` · `LS1·Q13` - From *"Others failed, so my work is worthless."* to **One person can tip the world; Abraham was one.** Moving the measure from numbers and other people's conduct to the weight of a single deed restores meaning and effort. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS2·Q20` - From *"More passion will reach them better."* to **Help lands when it is measured to what they can hold and given calmly.** The receiver's vessel sets how much can be taken in. Calm speech fits the vessel, while fire overwhelms it. *(Pelach HaRimon)* `PR·Q30`

**Where the works agree.** The evidence is thin. The works take the measure away from the helper's own state, intensity and results, and put it on the act of turning toward the other person and on what that person can receive.

**Where they differ.** They are complementary rather than in conflict. Likkutei Sichos pushes outward even when one feels unworthy. Pelach HaRimon restrains the helper's fire.

**Order and timing.** Decide to help without waiting to be complete. When you help, give calmly and to measure. When results disappoint, weigh the single deed, not the numbers.

- Helping others must not become a way to avoid one's own needed care. - Caregivers and teachers at risk of burnout should not hear 'take every chance without calculation' as a demand to give without limit. - 'Influencing others' must never override their consent or boundaries. - Someone in an abusive relationship should not be encouraged to keep 'helping' the abuser.

<a id="c18"></a> ## C18 · Work and making a living have swallowed my whole life

*6 findings from Torah Ohr (part 1 of 2), Torah Ohr (part 2 of 2), Kuntres HaHispaalus, Imrei Binah, Kuntres HaTefillah, Kuntres Umaayan*

> Business takes all my mind and heart. I have no time to pray or learn, and prayer is for scholars, not for someone like me. I've drifted so far I'm starting to write myself off.

**What is really going on.** The works treat this as a matter of where the heart's depth goes, not of hours alone. Torah Ohr calls it an error to think business people can't pray like scholars. The soul came down into worry about livelihood for the sake of an ascent, and love born from that darkness can be greater. Work itself is to be done coolly, as if compelled, to provide for the household. Kuntres Umaayan: the business is only a vessel for blessing; treating it as the source is a kind of false worship; pray and learn first. Kuntres HaTefillah names the enslavement 'drunkenness', and holds that even a few more slow moments of morning prayer leave an imprint that guards the day. Imrei Binah sets a floor anyone can reach: trust God for your living and push away bad thoughts. Kuntres HaHispaalus: the drifted person is asleep, not dead; the real cause of falling is giving the depth of the heart to business; group review and private guidance wake him.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"Real prayer belongs to people who sit and learn, not to someone in business."* → That is the error of business people. They can pray more, because light out of darkness is greater. `TO1·Q02` · `TO1·Q03`
- *"If I stop to pray or learn, I'll lose money."* → The business is only a vessel for blessing. If the blessing isn't there, more hours won't help; if it is, a little work suffices. `KU·Q26` · `KU·Q28`
- *"My heart has to be fully in my work."* → Business should be done coolly, as if compelled, to provide for the household. The depth of the heart is not given over to it. `TO2·Q32` · `KH·Q30`
- *"I can't be constantly devoted, so what's the point?"* → Constant devotion belongs to great tzaddikim. Everyone can trust God for his living and push away bad thoughts. `IB·Q27` · `IB·Q28`
- *"A few rushed minutes of prayer don't matter."* → The imprint of even a little real connection guards him through the busy day. `KT·Q32` · `KT·Q31`
- *"I've drifted so far it's hopeless."* → He is asleep, not dead. The soul's nature to be moved has not changed, and it wakes at a touch. `KH·Q01` · `KH·Q02`

1. **Turning the workday into prayer** · *from Torah Ohr* (1) Acknowledge that the day went to the press of earning a living. (2) In prayer, contemplate how every blade of grass has its source above, and so on up to God's kingship. (3) Contemplate that all of this is only a radiance, nothing next to His essence. (4) Let a longing arise to leave the darkness and cleave to Him alone. (5) Enter the words of prayer and let them lift you. *When:* Daily prayer, especially after being absorbed in work. *For:* Working people who feel second-class spiritually. *Not for:* Not a substitute for practical help for someone in real financial crisis. `TO1·Q02` · `TO1·Q03` · `TO1·Q04`
2. **Business as vessel, done coolly** · *from Kuntres Umaayan, Torah Ohr* (1) Settle in your mind that the blessing comes from God and the business is only a vessel for it. (2) Pray on time, slowly, with at least the simple meaning, and ask for your needs. (3) Keep fixed times for Torah without hindrance. (4) Then work, coolly, to sustain yourself and your household, without giving it the depth of your heart. (5) Give yourself over to God as the one who feeds you. *When:* Daily: prayer and fixed Torah times first, then work. *For:* Business people and workers whose work crowds out everything else. *Not for:* Someone in real financial danger. It is not licence to neglect honest work or obligations to family. `KU·Q26` · `KU·Q27` · `KU·Q28` · `TO2·Q32` · `TO2·Q11`
3. **The busy person's morning** · *from Kuntres HaTefillah* (1) Each morning, see how business consumes mind and heart, and let the heart be moved. (2) Don't talk business before prayer. (3) Learn a little before praying, as a fixed rule. (4) Pray slowly, attending to the words. (5) Know that every extra moment leaves an imprint that guards you through the day. *When:* Every weekday morning; even one moment longer counts. *For:* People in business who cannot pray at length on weekdays. *Not for:* Not a replacement for longer contemplation for those who have the time. `KT·Q31` · `KT·Q32`
4. **The floor for the busy: one master in the heart** · *from Imrei Binah* (1) Accept that constant devotion belongs to great tzaddikim, and that this floor is in your power. (2) While busy, trust God for your livelihood and cast your burden on Him. (3) Don't willfully cultivate impure fantasies. (4) When a bad thought falls in, push it away and return. *When:* All day, in the middle of work. *For:* Ordinary people swamped by work. *Not for:* People with intrusive thoughts (OCD) should not be told to fight each thought or be pained by it. `IB·Q27` · `IB·Q28` · `IB·Q29`
5. **Set times of awakening, review with a friend, and private guidance** · *from Kuntres HaHispaalus* (1) Now and then, turn your heart to waking your soul. (2) Fix a free time with others and meet as a group. (3) Go over a teaching aloud with a friend many times, each strengthening the other. **Where the works agree.** Work is legitimate, but it must not own the depth of the heart. The working person is not spiritually second-class. Small fixed anchors (a slow morning prayer, a little learning, trust through the day) are within reach and carry weight. Drifting is dormancy, not death.

**Where they differ.** Torah Ohr says the working person can pray more than the scholar. Kuntres HaTefillah concedes he cannot pray at length on weekdays and settles for a few extra moments. Kuntres Umaayan makes the strongest claim, that a little work suffices if blessing is there, and frames reliance on business as false worship. Imrei Binah sets the floor lowest. Kuntres HaHispaalus puts the remedy in community and guidance rather than individual discipline.

**Order and timing.** Morning: no business talk, a little learning, slow prayer. Work coolly through the day, trusting and pushing away stray thoughts. In prayer, lift the day's darkness into longing. Through the year, set times for group review and private guidance to wake what has drifted.

- 'A little work suffices if the blessing is there' must never reach someone in real financial crisis as advice to work less, or imply that poverty means lack of faith. - Not a licence to neglect family obligations or honest work. - People with intrusive thoughts should not be taught to fight or be pained by each stray thought. - Burnout and workaholism may need clinical and practical help, not only reframing. - Don't tell someone who has lost a job that it came from lack of trust.
