**What it is.** Derushim on the weekly parsha and the festivals by a leading mashpia who studied under the Alter Rebbe and the Mitteler Rebbe. Most of it is dense Kabbalah: partzufim, Tohu and Tikkun, nogah, the unifications. Scattered through it, often as "and so it is in avodas Hashem", are sharp practical passages. They cover how contemplation matures into feeling, how to hold bitterness and joy together before prayer, what anger is, the blocked mind, pride hiding in religious zeal, the weight of lapses, and when to afflict oneself. They lean on Yiddish phrases and parables from town life.

**How much of it is medicine.** some — roughly a tenth of the text speaks to a person's inner condition, embedded as brief applications inside long cosmological and Kabbalistic derushim.

**Its model of the person.** A person holds a divine soul, which never sins and always rises to be absorbed in God. Beside it sit an intellectual/animal soul and natural feelings of nogah, which can fall into evil. Change comes through contemplation, which matures in stages like a fetus, a nursing infant and an adult. First the mind makes a person act, but only while he is thinking. Then the heart draws life from the mind and yearns on its own. At last the heart itself sees what the mind sees. The mind rules the heart only in this "gadlus". Raw fire (Tohu) has to be settled into ordered mind and feeling (Tikkun). Zeal carried out through the body brings a sense of self with it.

**Its philosophy.** God alone truly exists ("the faithful God", the one who endures). The world is concealment, and it lets things feel separate. The work below, done with understanding and inner consent, turns that separateness into unity, and this is what "service is a need on high" means. The divine spark in a person is "the truth of God" and cannot be stained. Sin belongs to the soul that fell. So a person can always separate himself from his fault and rejoice in his portion.

**What it adds.** A mashpia's clinical distinctions: a blocked mind versus Tanya's heavy heart; a clean 'Jewish emptiness' versus a desert of pride and anger, each with its own remedy; being held by anger versus holding it; lapses from weakness versus resentful service. It also gives graded remedies by severity, three stages for contemplation reaching the heart, pride hidden in zeal, and the image of a soul that feels alive only through bitterness.

## My resolutions only last while I'm thinking about it; then I'm my old self

While he contemplates, he acts. When the thought fades, nothing is left, and nature pulls back as if nothing had happened.

**What is really going on.** Contemplation is still at the stage of 'ibbur': the mind drives behavior but has not yet entered the heart's own feelings. Nature is strong and does not move without it. The contemplation has to mature until the heart yearns by itself.

**Program: Contemplation that grows from the head into the heart (ibbur, yenikah, gadlus).** (1) Receive knowledge of God, ideally from a teacher before whom you are self-effacing, rather than from books alone. (2) Contemplate it in depth, for example that God fills and surrounds all worlds, chose us and gave us the mitzvos. (3) Let the contemplation decide concrete practice, and do all you can; at first it holds only while you are thinking. (4) Keep at it until the heart's natural feelings are moved to thirst and longing, and yearn by themselves even after the thinking stops. (5) Aim for maturity, where the heart itself sees what the mind sees. *When:* The text gives no schedule. It is a developmental process across stages, and it naturally belongs to prayer-time contemplation. *For:* Someone whose good behavior depends on thinking about it, or whose emptiness has no active poison in it. *Not for:* Someone in the 'desert of the nations' (pride, anger, lust), who per G04 must first go through remorse. `CA·Q01` · `CA·Q02` · `CA·Q03` · `CA·Q16`

**Reframe.** From *"I keep the mitzvos out of habit, and that's all service is."* to **Doing things out of Jewish nature without a settled mind is carried by the King. Real service is the transformation of your own will and understanding.** It points the person toward contemplation that reaches his own will and heart, the process of G01. `CA·Q33` · `CA·Q01`

## I feel so bad about what I've done that I can't pray with any joy

He knows he should come to prayer broken, and he should also serve with joy. The two seem to cancel each other out, and the guilt swallows the prayer.

**What is really going on.** He has taken the sinning part of himself for all of himself. What sinned was the animal soul that fell. The divine soul is always rising and has no sin, so once he makes the separation, joy is possible. Sadness also drives the Presence away.

**Program: Bitterness, then separation, then joyful prayer.** (1) Before prayer, make an honest reckoning of your lowness and distance from God, each according to his own measure. (2) Let yourself taste real bitterness in proportion to that reckoning. (3) Then tell your soul: the one that sinned is the intellectual/animal soul that fell; my divine soul always rises and has no sin. (4) Separate holy from mundane so they do not mix. (5) Rejoice in your portion, a part of God above, and pray with joy as if you had never blemished your soul. *When:* Just before prayer, each time. *For:* Someone whose guilt blocks joyful prayer, or who confuses brokenness with sadness. *Not for:* Someone depressed or crushed by shame: the bitterness step can deepen despair. The text does not address that case. `CA·Q04` · `CA·Q05` · `CA·Q06`

**Reframe.** From *"I've sinned, so I'm stained; joy in prayer would be hypocrisy."* to **The part of you that sinned is the fallen animal soul. Your divine soul always rises and has no sin. Separate them and rejoice in your portion as if you had never blemished it.** Once the two souls are separated they stop feeding off each other. Bitterness is kept to the animal soul, and joy becomes truthful rather than denial. The Presence rests only where there is joy, not sadness. `CA·Q05` · `CA·Q06` · `CA·Q34`

## The only time I feel my soul is when I'm miserable about not being good

He can't manage to do good. He goes through the day distracted and never calls on his soul's strength. The one sign of spiritual life he has is an occasional mourning over not serving God at all.

**What is really going on.** His soul is in 'Egypt': it can feel its life only through bitterness. He is like a sick man who tastes sweet as bitter and feels alive only through sharpness. Healing means feeling life without sorrow.

**Reframe.** From *"Pain over my failures is the only proof my soul is alive."* to **Feeling alive only through bitterness is a sickness, like a patient who tastes sweet as bitter. Healing means feeling life without sorrow and grief.** Naming the misery as the soul's exile rather than its authentic voice frees him to look for life through revelation and the light of Torah, instead of producing more bitterness. `CA·Q08` · `CA·Q09` · `CA·Q10`

## When I get angry it takes me over and I do things I'd never do

In the moment, anger rules him and his judgment leaves him.

**What is really going on.** He is caught inside the anger like a prisoner in jail, and his mind withdraws. The alternative is to hold the anger in one's hand, and to see that the anger won't serve even his own purpose.

**Program: Holding the anger instead of being held by it.** (1) Notice when the anger has hold of you and your judgment is leaving. (2) Ask whether being angry at the one who wronged you will help your actual aim at all. (3) If it won't, cover the recoil and dress it in kindness: speak calmly, not once or twice, and in several ways, until the purpose is reached. (4) Let whatever sharpness remains be something you hold in your hand, not something that holds you. *When:* In the moment of provocation. *For:* Someone whose anger overrides his judgment. *Not for:* It is not a license to intimidate a household or students; see cautions. `CA·Q11` · `CA·Q12` · `CA·Q37`

**Reframe.** From *"Anger just happens to me; it takes over."* to **There is a way to hold the anger in your hand instead of being held in it like a prisoner.** His judgment withdraws only when he is inside the anger. Seeing it as a tool held from outside keeps the mind present, and the question 'will this help?' lets the recoil be dressed in calm. `CA·Q11` · `CA·Q12` · `CA·Q37`

## I feel nothing when I pray; my heart is dead to anything holy

*Its own term:* מדבר יהודה / אידעשע פוסטקייט

Prayer and song don't move him. He fears this means he is corrupt.

**What is really going on.** The work tells two kinds of desert apart. One is a 'desert of the nations': pride quick to anger, burning lusts, separateness. It kills the capacity to be moved until he returns. The other is a 'desert of Judah', a Jewish emptiness with no poison in it, and contemplation alone can bring song from there.

**Program: Diagnosing the dead heart: desert of Judah or desert of snakes.** (1) Ask whether the numbness comes with pride quick to anger, burning desires, or attachment to the world, or whether it is simply empty. (2) If the poison is present, first 'scald' it with the fire of bitterness and sorrow over having walked in darkness, and return to God. (3) If it is plain Jewish emptiness, contemplate 'God, You are my God': deepen the awareness that God is my own strength, the divine spark in me. (4) Let that thought settle well in mind and heart until it turns into song. *When:* Before and during prayer. *For:* Someone who is numb in prayer. *Not for:* The scalding step is not for a person who is already depressed. `CA·Q14` · `CA·Q15` · `CA·Q16`

## I want to be close to God, but my mind feels shut and I can't connect at all

His heart doesn't turn to evil. He actually longs for holiness, but he cannot cleave. The light of wisdom in him feels closed off.

**What is really going on.** This is a blocked mind, which is different from Tanya's heaviness of heart. When it comes alone it is a fall sent from above, and no evil descends from above. It comes only to lift him to a more complete teshuvah.

**Program: Graded remedies for the blocked mind.** (1) Take the blockage as a call to a higher teshuvah, not as a sign of evil. (2) Mild case: say words of Torah even without understanding them (Zohar), immerse, give charity. (3) Middle case: 'quarantine': reduce business and add Torah study. (4) Severe case: an examination of oneself that judges oneself strictly, with sorrow, fasting and weeping until purified. (5) Afterward, revive the soul with new life and prepare over seven days so the eyes open to holy feelings. *When:* As needed. The severe stage lasts until purified, and the preparation period is seven days. *For:* Someone who longs for holiness but cannot connect, without a heart pulled toward evil. *Not for:* The severe stage is never for anyone depressed, eating-disordered or medically fragile. `CA·Q28` · `CA·Q29` · `CA·Q30` · `CA·Q31`

**Reframe.** From *"I can't connect at all; something must be deeply wrong with me."* to **A blocked mind by itself is a fall sent from above, and no evil comes down from above. It comes to lift you to a more complete teshuvah.** The blockage stops being a verdict and becomes a summons. That opens the graded remedies instead of despair. `CA·Q28` · `CA·Q29`

## I'm worn out by God's hiddenness, and any inspiration I get vanishes at once

The concealment is so great that he can't taste any delight. His soul is weary of carrying it and wants to run away. Flashes of feeling come and fade immediately.

**What is really going on.** The text counts the weary longing to escape darkness as a real level of avodah (the 'earth'). A flash that fades right away still belongs to the one piece of the menorah.

**Reframe.** From *"If an inspiration fades at once, it was worthless."* to **Even a flash that sparkles and withers at once is still 'of one piece' with the menorah. And the weary wish to flee the darkness is itself a level of service.** It honors the smallest and most fleeting movement of the soul, so he keeps turning toward the light instead of writing himself off. `CA·Q17` · `CA·Q18`

## I keep slipping through laziness or desire; maybe I don't really love God

After a lapse he concludes that his love is fake.

**What is really going on.** A lapse that comes from the body's laziness or from desire overpowering him doesn't touch the love. It is like the loyal man in the parable, who sometimes spoils even his own business out of laziness. What separates love from hatred is aim and resentment, not the absence of failure.

**Reframe.** From *"My lapses prove I don't really love God."* to **A lapse out of laziness or overwhelming desire doesn't touch the love. The loyal man sometimes ruins even his own affairs that way.** Love is measured by what one wants and wishes for the Other, not by never failing. The real opposite of love is resentful, grudging service. `CA·Q20` · `CA·Q21`

## Being so devoted makes me feel important

His energetic, disciplined service carries a quiet sense of 'I am a servant of God.' Even his Torah learning makes him wise in his own eyes.

**What is really going on.** Service through the body's zeal is by nature a service of the self, and self-importance can feed on it. Humility comes from being absorbed in God's unity. Awe of His majesty then has to guard the zeal.

**Program: Humility first, then awe to guard zeal.** (1) Begin prayer by contemplating God's unity, that nothing exists apart from Him: this is humility, with no sense of self. (2) When that light fades as you turn to needs or study, keep its trace. (3) Rouse awe of God's majesty, which moves you to put all your powers into His service. (4) Carry both the humility and the awe together into zealous, bodily service. *When:* The order within daily prayer, then carried through the day. *For:* Diligent, disciplined people prone to feeling self-important about their piety. *Not for:* Anxious or scrupulous people, for whom the 'fear and dread' step may feed anxiety. `CA·Q24` · `CA·Q25` · `CA·Q27`

**Reframe.** From *"My zeal and discipline prove I'm a true servant of God."* to **Zeal through the body is service of the self and can breed self-importance. Humility comes only from being absorbed in God's unity.** Seeing where ego hides lets him pair zeal with humility and awe, so the vitality stays but the self-regard goes. `CA·Q24` · `CA·Q25`

## Should I fast and afflict myself to become holy?

He wonders whether severity is the path.

**What is really going on.** It depends on the person. One who has tasted a glimpse of the world to come should widen his mind and not afflict himself, so as not to diminish his service. One whose soul only thirsts may sanctify himself in what is permitted.

**Program: Calibrating self-denial to where one stands.** (1) Ask honestly whether you have tasted a glimpse of the spiritual, or only thirst for it. (2) If you have tasted it: what the Torah forbids is enough; widen your mind and do not afflict yourself, so your service is not diminished. (3) If you only thirst: sanctify yourself in some permitted matter, as a vow made for heaven's sake. *When:* Not specified. *For:* Someone drawn to asceticism. *Not for:* Scrupulous people. Vows carry halachic weight and need guidance. `CA·Q32`

## I'm all fire for God and don't care about norms or other people's opinion

A soul burns for God without study, ignores honor and manners, and does odd things.

**What is really going on.** This is a soul from Tohu. Its fire is holy while it is still above understanding. Once it has matured into understanding, it is obliged to order its words and ways with judgment. Otherwise it damages its holiness.

**Reframe.** From *"My fire for God exempts me from ordinary order and judgment."* to **Raw fire is holy while it is still above understanding, but once matured you must order your words and ways with judgment.** It honors the fire while requiring it to be integrated into mind and ordered feelings, which prevents the fire from damaging itself. `CA·Q40`

## Why am I being put through this test?

He meets a trial and feels it as a burden or a punishment.

**What is really going on.** Before the test, his power to overcome was hidden inside his body's nature. Standing up to it brings that power out and subdues nature under the divine soul.

**Reframe.** From *"This trial is a burden or punishment laid on me."* to **Before the test, your power to overcome lay hidden in your nature. Standing up to it brings that power out.** The test becomes the way a latent strength is actualized and nature is subdued, which gives it meaning and a goal. `CA·Q36`

## Cautions

- G02's bitterness step and G04's 'scalding' can deepen depression or shame; with a depressed person, begin from the divine soul's innocence (F01). - G05's severe stage (fasting, weeping, mourning) must never go to someone depressed, eating-disordered or medically fragile. - Q12's 'holding anger' comes from a context of casting fear on household and students; it must not be used to justify intimidating family or dependents. - Q27's awe and dread used to drive diligence can feed scrupulosity or anxiety. - Line 279 ranks Torah study among ways of healing the body; never present that as a substitute for medical care. - Q40's Tohu souls must not be used to romanticize erratic behavior that may be mania or another illness needing care. - G07's vows carry halachic weight; don't recommend them to scrupulous people without rabbinic guidance. - Line 512's 'haters of God' parable should not be used to label struggling or religiously wounded people.

<a id="ca-q24"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">ויש בחי' עובדי ה' בגופם הוא ענין הזריזות והותיקות להכניס כל כחותיו בעבודת ה' שזהו בבחי' ישות עצמו דוקא. ובבחי' זו יוכל להיות יניקת החיצונים ח"ו בישות וחשיבות לעצמו שהוא עובד ה'</div>

There are those who serve God with their body: zeal and diligence, putting all their powers into serving God. This is specifically a matter of one's own selfhood, and here the outside forces can feed, God forbid, on self-importance, that 'I am a servant of God.' *Chanah Ariel, Eikev · line 514 · `CA·Q24` ✓*

This is called humility, for there is no sense of self in it, since he is absorbed and occupied in grasping God's unity, that there is nothing besides Him. *Chanah Ariel, Eikev · line 514 · `CA·Q25` ✓*

<a id="ca-q27"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">ולזאת צריך לעורר בחי' יראת הרוממות שמחמת רוממות האל ב"ה המצוה על עבודתו תפול על האדם אימה ופחד המכריחו להכניס כל מיצוי הנפש וכחותי' בעבודתו ית'</div>

Therefore he must rouse awe of God's majesty: because of the exaltedness of God who commands His service, awe and dread fall upon the person, compelling him to put the whole of his soul and powers into His service. *Chanah Ariel, Eikev · line 514 · `CA·Q27` ✓*

<a id="ca-q28"></a> <div dir="rtl" lang="he">והענין ברוחניות שלא ישט לבו לדבר רע כלל. ואדרבא הוא מתאוה להתקרב אל הקדושה האלקי' אבל אין יכול לידבק כלל. נק' טמטום המוח</div>
