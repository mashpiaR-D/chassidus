## 0. The short answer

The modern books that work all share one **format**, and the format is separable from their content. Every stage gets:

1. **A name** you can remember and say about yourself ("I'm in Stage 4").
2. **A one-line goal.**
3. **What it feels like from inside**, described plainly, so you recognize it.
4. **The skill** being trained (one, not five).
5. **The practice**: how long, which steps, what to do when.
6. **The obstacles**, each named, each with its fix.
7. **Signs of mastery**: how you know you can move on.
8. **Honest warnings**: what can go wrong, including the hard parts no one advertises.
9. **A way to always know where you are**: a map, a self-check, and permission to go back.

Chassidus already has nearly every piece of this, scattered over two hundred years of maamarim: named stages of contemplation (breadth, length, depth), named levels of soul and of bitul, named obstacles (timtum, atzvus, machshavos zaros, the counterfeit hispa'alus), named remedies, and blunt warnings. What it never had is the **single readable book** that lays them out stage by stage for a beginner. That is the gap.

What we must **not** borrow is the content these maps measure (breath, sensations, insight into impermanence, jhanas), their promise of a guaranteed end state, and their habit of measuring progress by experiences. Chassidus measures by fruit, not by states, and says outright that the deepest things are given, not earned. Section 7 lays out the adaptation.

## 1. Rav Doniel Katz · The Elevation Project

**What it is.** An Australian-born baal teshuvah who left a film and theatre career, Rav Doniel Katz founded the Elevation Project to bring "Torah's model of human psychology and consciousness" (his phrase) back into lived practice: meditation, breathwork, emotional work and inner refinement, sourced, in his framing, from "Kabbalah Chassidus" and open to seekers of every background. He teaches in live seminars, retreats, an app (guided meditations, courses, live events) and online academies.

**How it is laid out (public view).** The public material shows the *architecture* more than the *stages*: - **A foundation course of "nine mechanisms of consciousness"**: a nine-part video series mapping "keys" for self-mastery, emotional transformation and spiritual experience. Teaching the machinery of the inner world first, before the climb. - **A teacher training** that trains "ten simple and powerful Jewish meditation techniques" over five months, with weekly exams and practice teaching. Techniques as a toolkit, learned one at a time. - **A retreat arc in four movements**: set the field (intention, the map of consciousness); ascend beyond the ordinary (expanded states); turn darkness into light (fear, trauma and old patterns worked "from the top down"); bring heaven down to earth (integration into daily life). That arc is, almost exactly, the shape of the Chabad ladder: up, the dark turned, and down into the deed. - **One named technique in public articles**: the Baal Shem Tov's ha'alas hamachshavos (elevating a thought): notice the negative trait, subdue it, then turn its energy toward the good. Awareness first, then a "tool kit" to steer the mind.

**His language and pedagogy.** Experiential and direct: the words of prayer as "a launchpad to an experience"; the greatest lack today is an *emotional* connection with the Creator; the aim is "not simply to believe" in a higher reality but to experience it. He insists the results be credited to Torah, not to secular methods, and frames the work as recovering tools the tradition "buried out of fear, trauma, and exile". Warm, inclusive, visibly unafraid of feeling.

**What we could not verify.** A full public stage-by-stage syllabus (the nine mechanisms by name, the ten techniques by name) is behind course walls. A search summary named several book titles; we could not confirm them from a publisher, so we do not list them. This section describes only what is publicly visible.

**What to borrow.** - **The promise of experience, kept honest.** People come for something felt. Chassidus agrees that knowing is not yet knowing until it is felt ("I knew this before too, but I only knew it"). The book should say plainly that this path is meant to be *lived*. - **Machinery before the climb.** Teach the parts of the inner world (two souls, mind/heart/deed, the garments) early, so every later stage has words. - **The four-movement arc.** Up, the darkness turned, and down into life. It matches our ladder and makes the descent feel like a destination, not an afterthought. - **The Besht's three-step turn** (notice, subdue, turn): the same as the Tanya's iskafya and is'hapcha. - **Inclusion.** Open to "spiritual seekers everywhere", which is the owner's audience too.

**What not to borrow, and why.** - **Breathwork and body-states as the object.** The Chabad sitting has a *line of teaching* as its object, not a sensation. The manual's rule: no breath-watching, body scan or relaxation framing. Breath can calm the arrival; it is never the meditation. - **"Rapid healing", "breakthrough", "extraordinary experiences" as the sales line.** Chassidus is wary of exactly this: wanting arousal "so as to be a something" is not even counted as a level (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut), and the deepest arousal is the one "least felt". A book that promises states trains people to chase them. - **Top-down trauma work in a book.** Real harm and grief belong with a person. The book can comfort and steady; it does not process trauma.

**Fit with Chassidus.** Very close in spirit and in arc. The difference is the *object* (a line of the unity, thought through, versus techniques and states) and the *measure* (fruit in the day versus experiences in the session). Our book can be the Chabad-specific, source-cited, fruit-measured cousin of what Elevation does in seminars.

Sources: [Elevation Foundation Series](https://elevationacademy.teachable.com/courses/138592) · [Elevation Mentor Training](https://elevationacademy.teachable.com/p/elevation-mentor-training) · [TRANSCEND retreat](https://elevationretreat.com/) · [Elevation Project app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/elevation-project/id6572282764) · [Mishpacha profile](https://mishpacha.com/?p=16515) · [Yiboneh workshop page](https://www.yiboneh.com/rosh-hashanah-meditation-workshop-with-rabbi-doniel-katz.html) · [Times of Israel author page](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/author/doniel-katz/) · [The New Year Meditation](https://unity.elevationproject.com/)

## 2. Culadasa (John Yates) · *The Mind Illuminated*

**Structure.** The clearest modern example of the format. Ten stages of training attention, grouped into four parts (roughly: novice, skilled, transition, adept), with four milestones between them. Every stage has the same four headings: **goals, obstacles, skills, mastery**. Stage One is simply *establishing a daily practice*; its mastery sign is "never missing a day". Later stages each name one main obstacle (mind-wandering, forgetting, gross distraction, strong dullness, subtle dullness, subtle distraction) and the one skill that beats it (labeling, checking in, connecting, and so on). "Interludes" between chapters explain the theory (a model of mind) just when the reader needs it. The rule: master each stage before the next; you cannot skip, but you will move back and forth.

**What makes it work.** - **One obstacle, one skill, per stage.** The reader always knows what to work on. - **Mastery defined as a behavior you can check**, not a feeling. - **Stage One is the habit.** No one is told to transcend before they can sit daily. - **Theory in small doses**, placed where it is needed. - **Permission to be anywhere.** Different days, different stages.

**What to borrow.** The four headings (we expand them). A "Stage One: a set time" chapter. Milestones that mark fruit, not peaks. Interludes: short theory pages (the two souls; mind, heart and deed; what bitul is) between parts.

**What not to borrow, and why.** The object (the breath at the nose) and the goal (a unified, pliant mind and meditative joy, the jhanas) are not ours. The Chabad object is a truth about God's oneness; the goal is that it be known *and lived*. Also the implied guarantee that diligent practice yields the top stages: Chassidus says the higher bitul "comes of itself", given from above, and that "a person must always be going up and coming down". Finally, a lesson from the book's own history: its author was removed from teaching in 2019 after misconduct allegations. Borrow the format, not anyone's authority; our book keeps every claim tied to a source.

**Fit with Chassidus.** The format fits beautifully. Chassidus already lists obstacles in this "name it, fix it" way (Tanya chapters 25 to 31 are almost a troubleshooting manual). Culadasa's "dullness" maps onto timtum; his "distraction" onto machshavos zaros; his "establishing a practice" onto the Piaseczner's "at least set times".

Sources: [Shortform summary](https://shortform.com/pdf/the-mind-illuminated-culadasa-matthew-immergut-and-jeremy-graves) · [Stage theory notes (TMI)](https://dds-course-rufuspollock.flowershow.me/Stage%20theory%20in%20meditation%20(Mind%20Illuminated)) · [Reader notes, Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/32332072-the-mind-illuminated/7256638-ian-pitchford?page=1)

## 3. Daniel Ingram · *Mastering the Core Teachings of the Buddha*

**Structure.** "An unusually hardcore dharma book", free online. Its center is a detailed retelling of the Theravada "progress of insight", sixteen stages: three preliminary ones (mind and body, cause and effect, the three characteristics); the Arising and Passing Away (a bright, exciting peak that people mistake for the goal); then the "Dark Night" (dissolution, fear, misery, disgust, desire for deliverance, re-observation), a run of unpleasant stages produced by the practice itself; then equanimity, and the stages of path and fruition. He also maps the concentration states separately, and insists the maps are "strangely predictable" across people.

**What makes it work.** - **Radical honesty about the hard parts.** He names the Dark Night, says it can spill into daily life, and tells people what helps. Many readers say this was the first book that told them the truth. - **Peaks named as peaks.** The A&P "high" is described so the reader does not mistake it for arrival. - **Plain language about states**: what you'll actually experience, no mystification. - **The map as a tool for self-location.** You read the descriptions and recognize where you are.

**What to borrow.** The honesty: a chapter that says "here is where it goes cold, here is where it hurts, here is what to do". The naming of the counterfeit peak (Chassidus has its own, sharply: "Aha, I'm warm, I've seen light", Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). The descriptive voice: "here is what people commonly notice", never "you should feel this".

**What not to borrow, and why.** - **The content of the Dark Night.** In insight practice the misery is produced by seeing sensations dissolve. Chassidus has no such stage, and we should not import one. Its dips are different: going cold "just as the real work begins" (Kuntres HaAvodah), up and down as a law of life (the Piaseczner), and the *chosen*, short, set-hour bitterness (merirus) that the Tanya separates sharply from sadness. We describe those, and only those. - **Attainment talk.** Ingram openly claims high attainments and encourages others to. Chassidus forbids examining one's elevation and teaches that the real kind "does not feel itself". No ranks, no claims, no "I got stage 11". - **Intensity as a virtue.** Hardcore retreat practice has well-documented casualties (see the Varieties of Contemplative Experience study below). Our book stays moderate: minutes a day, a deed after, and a firm stop line.

**Fit with Chassidus.** The honesty fits; the map does not. Use his candor, not his stages.

Sources: [MCTB, The Progress of Insight (free online)](https://www.mctb.org/mctb2/table-of-contents/part-iv-insight/30-the-progress-of-insight/) · [MCTB overview, updevelopment](https://updevelopment.org/mastering-the-core-teachings-of-the-buddha/) · [Varieties of Contemplative Experience study, Cheetah House](https://cheetahhouse.org/vce) · [Brown Alumni Magazine on meditation side effects](https://www.brownalumnimagazine.com/articles/2025-01-16/side-effects-of-meditation)

## 4. Shinzen Young · Unified Mindfulness

**Structure.** Less a ladder of stages, more a *skill system*. Mindfulness is defined as three skills working together: **concentration power, sensory clarity, equanimity**. Practice is a menu of "ways" (focus in, focus out, focus on rest, focus on flow, nurture the positive), each with precise labeled techniques ("See, Hear, Feel"). Experience is "untangled" into components so it can be met one piece at a time. He also speaks frankly of "purification": that stored pain can surface during practice, and that equanimity lets it pass.

**What makes it work.** - **Precise, teachable definitions.** You know exactly what skill you are training. - **A menu matched to the person and the day**: active or restful, inner or outer, depending on what you need. - **Divide and conquer.** A big, tangled experience is broken into pieces small enough to hold.

**What to borrow.** Naming the *three skills* our path trains, in Chabad words: **binding** the mind (kesher hada'as, which is concentration); **detail** (hisbonenus pratis, contemplation in particulars, which is clarity); and **evenness** (the Besht's reading of *shiviti*, "all is equal to him", which is the fruit, not the method). Also the menu: on a heavy day the sources give a different door (joy, a tune, the mouth) rather than more effort. And "divide and conquer": the Tanya's "bring it down into details" is the same move.

**What not to borrow, and why.** The object (sensory events) and the metaphysics (no-self through sensory deconstruction). In Chassidus the self is not dissolved into sensations; it is given up *to Him* and then "given back" as one who can love and choose. Equanimity is not the goal, either; joy and bitul together are.

**Fit with Chassidus.** Good for clarity of teaching. The Chabad equivalent of his three skills already exists; we only need to name them as clearly.

Sources: [See Hear Feel introduction (Shinzen, PDF)](https://www.shinzen.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/SeeHearFeelIntroduction_ver1.8.pdf) · [Five Ways to Know Yourself (PDF)](https://www.shinzen.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/FiveWaystoKnowYourself_ver1.6.pdf) · [Sounds True: algorithmic approach](https://www.soundstrue.com/a/resources/?p=27192)

## 5. Ken Wilber · states and stages (briefly)

**The one idea worth having.** *States* (temporary experiences: a moment of awe, a glimpse of oneness) are not *stages* (lasting development, built slowly, which cannot be skipped). Anyone at any stage can have a high state; a high state does not make anyone more developed. His grid plots experiences as "a state at a stage".

**Why it matters for us.** This is almost exactly the Mitteler Rebbe's warning in Kuntres HaHitpa'alut: the natural soul's ladder of arousals and the Godly soul's levels are two different ladders, and one does not reach the second by climbing the first. It is also Sefer HaArachim's rule that the higher bitul "comes of itself" while the lower comes through contemplation. So our book is built in two layers: - **Stages of skill and character** (the practice, the habit, the binding, the deed, the fruit in the day). These you build, in order. - **Rungs of the unity** (being said now, before Him nothing, alone, the simple will, the plainest thing). These are truths to contemplate, and the deep ones are glimpsed as states, given, not earned. You can glimpse rung ten on day three; it does not mean you are "at stage ten".

**What not to borrow.** The rest of the integral apparatus (quadrants, color-coded worldview levels). It adds jargon and nothing the sources need.

Sources: [Integral World, the Wilber-Combs Lattice](https://www.integralworld.net/visser770.html) · [Sloww, states and stages](https://www.sloww.co/states-stages-ken-wilber/) · [Interaction Institute, states and stages](https://interactioninstitute.org/states-and-stages/)

## 6. Rabbi Aryeh Kaplan · *Jewish Meditation: A Practical Guide*

**Structure.** A short, practical introduction (1985): why Judaism has a meditative tradition and how it went underground; then techniques one at a time: mantra-like repetition of a phrase, contemplation of an object or idea, visualization (including of Hebrew letters), hisbodedus (talking to God in your own words), the meditation of "nothingness", and using the Shema and the Amidah as meditations.

**What makes it work.** Authority plus simplicity. It proved, to a whole generation, that Jewish meditation is real and traditional. Each technique is short and doable. Prayer itself is shown as the main meditative arena.

**What to borrow.** Prayer as the home of the practice (the Rashab's "half hour before" prayer is the same idea); hisbodedus as a daily element; the Shema as the summit-sentence of the sitting.

**What not to borrow, and why.** Kaplan surveys many schools (Abulafia's letter permutations, prophetic techniques). The Chabad path is narrower and safer: *hisbonenus*, a thought-through truth, and the index's technology file already explains what the letter methods add and what the guide keeps out. He also offers no developmental map: techniques, not stages. That is what our book adds.

**Fit with Chassidus.** Kaplan is the door that made Jewish meditation thinkable; our book walks through it with the Chabad method specifically.

Sources: [Penguin Random House page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/89694/jewish-meditation-by-aryeh-kaplan) · [Partners in Torah library summary](https://partnersintorah.org/library_view/314-jewish-meditation)

## 7. The format, adapted: what our book does differently

| Modern format | Our adaptation | Why (the source) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stage = a level of attention or insight | Stage = a skill and a rung of the unity, side by side | Knowing the unity *is* contemplating it (ONE-PROCESS §1) |
| "What you'll experience" | "What people often notice", described, never demanded | Wanting the feeling is "serving oneself" (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut) |
| Mastery = a reliable state | Mastery = fruit in the day: quicker return, the line comes back by itself, one reaction that didn't happen, a deed done | "Am I more my own master than before?" (the Piaseczner); its sign is lowliness afterward (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut) |
| Linear progress to an end | A spiral: up, a turn, down, and round again one rung deeper | "The return is the main thing" (Imrei Binah); "always going up and coming down" (the Piaseczner) |
| Dark Night as a stage | Three honest dips: going cold when real work starts; up and down; the chosen, short bitterness, kept apart from sadness | Kuntres HaAvodah; Derekh HaMelekh; Tanya 31 |
| Obstacles named in a new vocabulary | Obstacles named with the sources' own words: timtum (the stone heart), atzvus, machshavos zaros, hispa'alus that serves itself, the sleeper | Tanya 26 to 31; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut; Kuntres HaTefillah |
| The top is the goal | The top is the turn; the goal is the deed below | "a dwelling in the lowest realm" (Tanya 36) |
| Higher stages earned by practice | The higher bitul is given; practice makes the vessel and the thirst | Sefer HaArachim; Ayin Beis |
| Teacher-certified attainment | No ranks, no claims; the guide never grades | Esh Kodesh: do not examine your elevation |
| Breath or sensation as object | A line of teaching as the object; mouth, tune and deed as aids | The manual's "Never" list |
| Intensity | Minutes a day; a firm stop line for danger | The guide's SAFETY rule |

**The one-sentence design.** *A Mind Illuminated in format, a Tanya in content, and a Piaseczner in tone: named stages, one skill each, obstacles with their Chassidic names and remedies, signs measured by fruit, honest about the dips, and always ending in the hand.*
