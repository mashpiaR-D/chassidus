## 1. The short answer

The corpus holds **39 distinct techniques** (not counting letter permutation, which ABULAFIA.md covers), in **seven families**, plus one recorded absence: no breathing technique exists in these sources (B6). Chabad hisbonenus is one of them, the constructive family's centre, and the earlier research covers it well. The rest of the 'technology' is older and wider: the Besht's and the Maggid's work inside the letters and the voice, the Arizal's Names, yichudim and grave-prostration, Sha'arei Kedusha's solitary 'as if dead' retreat, the Merkavah ascent as background, Breslov's speech, Lizhensk's imagined fire and declared intention, Piaseczno's quieting and picturing, and the bodily practices (melody, swaying, prostration, midnight). Under all of them runs one design (section 4): hold a vessel that carries God, so that the self is not needed; climb from body to voice to letter to thought to nothing; use the image and then drop it; pass through ayin; come back into the deed.

**How the branches connect (the main hybrids).** The families are not sealed; the strongest techniques sit where two meet.

- **Letter -> Emptying.** The Maggid's letter path (L1) ends in ayin (E1). The letter is the bridge that runs both ways.
- **Image + Emptying.** Sha'arei Kedusha (I8) first empties ('as if dead'), then pictures the ascent. The fire (I3) and the Shema (L5) use an image to reach a handing-over.
- **Constructive -> Body.** Hisbonenus (C1) is carried by melody (B1) and must end in the deed (A5); the Mitteler Rebbe's discernment (C6) audits it.
- **Speech -> Emptying.** Breslov: bitul only through hisbodedus (S1 -> E1).
- **Name -> Person.** The Arizal's yichudim (I7) are done on a tzaddik's grave, soul bound to soul (S5), in prostration (B3).
- **Emptying -> Action.** Raising the fallen desire to its root (E3) is applied to seeing (I4) and eating (A2).
- **Old -> new.** Heikhalot descent (I5) -> the Besht's inward ascent and palaces (I6) -> the Arizal's retreat (I8) -> Breslov's spoken solitude (S1).

## C. Constructive / intellectual

*The mind builds something (an idea, an account, a truth) and holds it until the heart answers.*

## C1. Hisbonenus: extended contemplation of the greatness of God (התבוננות)

- **School and source:** Chabad (Alter Rebbe, Mitteler Rebbe, Rashab); roots in the Besht's 'think of His greatness'.
- **Object:** An idea: one teaching about God (the Infinite light filling and surrounding, the order of descent, 'all before Him is as nothing', the soul's root). Object type: idea.
- **Method:** Chosen before prayer or in a set sitting. Take one idea, unfold it in length and breadth with detail and analogy, then deepen it (daas) by holding it until it is as vivid as a thing seen with the eyes. Duration is the instruction most repeated. It ends in the heart answering and is then brought down into an act. Seated or standing, eyes usually closed, often at length before and during prayer; the voice and a tune may carry it (see B1). The full stage map and the diagnostics are already written in the files named under 'Prior coverage'.
- **State, and from inside:** From inside: first the idea is only words; then it opens and has room; then it is simply true, the way a physical object is true, and love or awe rises without being pushed. The goal is not the feeling but bitul: the self shrinks in the presence of what is understood.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Daas means binding (as in 'Adam knew'): the mind held on one point with force binds the soul to it, and the mind rules the heart, so what the mind truly holds, the heart eventually answers. The idea is itself Godliness clothed in wisdom, so dwelling in it is dwelling in Him.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Contemplation that stops at understanding, or at feeling, has not finished; it must reach the deed (A5). The counterfeit is arousal from the wish to be aroused (C6). Needs a settled mind first; do not contemplate while scattered (see E2).
- **Relations:** C6 (its discernment tool); E1 (its goal, bitul); B1 (the melody that carries it); A5 (where it must end).
- **Prior coverage:** Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md and paths/CRAFT.md; ONE-PROCESS.md (the fourteen steps); manual/part1-how-to-sit.md stages 3-7.
- **Verified lines:**

## C2. The entrance thought: 'before Whom I stand' (לפני מי)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash), Alter Rebbe (Shulchan Arukh HaRav OC 1, Tanya 41).
- **Object:** A presence: the King standing over you and looking at you. Object type: idea held as presence.
- **Method:** A short act at a threshold: on waking, before prayer, before study. Stop and say in the heart to Whom you are about to cleave: the One who by His word brings all worlds into being. Let awe come; awe is the gate. Only then begin. The Alter Rebbe puts it at the first moment of waking: think before Whom you are lying.
- **State, and from inside:** A small sobering and straightening, like entering a king's room. Short. It is the door, not the room.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Awe is 'the gate to enter before Him' (Besht); reverence opens the vessel before love fills it. It works by changing the frame before the content: the same words said 'before Him' are a different act.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** If it does not arrive, deepen, and repent of what blocks it (see C3's rule 'if it does not reach him at once'). Not a substitute for the main contemplation.
- **Relations:** C3 (the same presence kept all day); C1 (the room it opens into).
- **Prior coverage:** Brief mentions in manual/part1-how-to-sit.md (preparation); not treated as its own technique before.
- **Verified lines:**
- *מתחלה כשרוצה להתפלל יהי' ביראה שהוא השער לכנוס לפניו* (at first, when he wants to pray, let him be in awe, which is the gate to enter before Him) · Tzava'at HaRivash 66:1, `Tzava'at HaRivash (Medzhibozh and Mezritch, c. 1740-1770).txt` line 121
- *יחשב בלבו לפני מי הוא שוכב* (let him think in his heart before Whom he is lying) · Shulchan Arukh HaRav, Orach Chayim, Second Edition 1:4, `Shulchan Arukh HaRav (Liozna, c. 1770-1805).txt` line 70
- *והנה ה׳ נצב עליו, ו״מלא כל הארץ כבודו״, ומביט עליו* (and behold, God stands over him, 'the whole earth is full of His glory', and looks at him) · Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 41:3, `Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt` line 578

## C3. Shiviti as awareness: the King stands over you (שויתי ה' לנגדי תמיד)

- **School and source:** Alter Rebbe (Shulchan Arukh HaRav OC 1:5); Besht (mutual gaze; evenness).
- **Object:** A presence, held as being seen. Object type: idea/presence (not a picture; contrast I1).
- **Method:** Take to heart that the great King stands over you and sees what you do, the way you would sit and speak differently in a king's presence. If awe does not come at once, go deep into the thought until it does, and repent of what blocks it. The Besht's form: see the Creator with the mind's eye as you would look at a person, and think that He is looking at you. Kept through the day.
- **State, and from inside:** A steady quiet reverence and shame before Him; for the Besht also an evenness: praise and insult, delicacies and plain food, become the same, since everything is from Him.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Behaviour and inner life change in the presence of one who sees; the technique installs that presence permanently. The Besht reads shiviti as hishtavut (evenness): once the self is not the centre, events stop pulling it.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** The Shulchan Arukh HaRav wording is awareness of being seen, not visualisation of the letters; the letter form is I1. Evenness is the fruit of long devekus, not a pose.
- **Relations:** I1 (the same verse done as a picture of the Name); A1 (kept alive through the day as the small thought).
- **Prior coverage:** Not catalogued as a separate method in the earlier files.
- **Verified lines:**
- *כל שכן כשישים האדם אל לבו, שהמלך הגדול, מלך מלכי המלכים הקדושברוךהוא, עומד עליו ורואה במעשיו* (all the more when a person takes to heart that the great King, the King of kings, stands over him and sees his deeds) · Shulchan Arukh HaRav, Orach Chayim, Second Edition 1:5, `Shulchan Arukh HaRav (Liozna, c. 1770-1805).txt` line 71

## C4. Ein od milvado: fixing the one reality until other powers lose their hold (אין עוד מלבדו)

- **School and source:** R. Chaim of Volozhin (Nefesh HaChayim III:12); the same move in Piaseczno ('the Creator is the only existent') and the Besht ('all is as nothing before Him').
- **Object:** An idea held as fact: there is no power but Him. Object type: idea.
- **Method:** Fix it in the heart and say it: God is the true God and there is no other power at all; the world is full only of His simple oneness. Then actively pay no attention to any other power or will (a threat, a fear, a person's intent) and bind the mind to Him alone. Piaseczno turns the same sentence into a quieting formula said several times softly ('I believe with complete faith that the Creator is the only existent...').
- **State, and from inside:** The fear or the pull goes slack; what threatened is felt as having no power of its own. Calm, not excitement.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** In their terms: other powers have no force of their own, only what He gives them; when a person truly nullifies them in his heart and cleaves to Him alone, 'of itself' they cannot act on him (the story of R. Chanina and the sorceress). Judgments are sweetened in their root.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Nefesh HaChayim forbids speculating on 'how' the world is filled by Him (the tzimtzum question); the practice is the fact held, not a theory worked out. Piaseczno: never forcefully.
- **Relations:** E1 (it is bitul from the side of the world); C1 (a single-sentence hisbonenus); E2 (used as the verse after quieting).
- **Prior coverage:** Piaseczno's version in PIASECZNO.md step 5; Nefesh HaChayim not treated before.
- **Verified lines:**
- *כשהאדם קובע בלבו לאמר הלא ה' הוא האלקים האמתי ואין עוד מלבדו יתברך שום כח בעולם* (when a person fixes in his heart to say: God is the true God and there is no other power in the world besides Him) · Nefesh HaChayim, Gate III 12:8, `Nefesh HaChayim.txt` line 728

## C5. Cheshbon hanefesh: the accounting (חשבון הנפש)

- **School and source:** Alter Rebbe (Tanya 29); Breslov (judging oneself in hisbodedus); Piaseczno (a day's thoughts looked at); Besht (the check against self-seeking).
- **Object:** Oneself: one's thoughts, words and deeds. Object type: self.
- **Method:** At a set time (bedtime in the classic form, or inside the daily hisbodedus) go over thoughts, words and deeds: were they on the side of holiness or 'not for God'. Breslov makes it a trial: sit as your own judge on every affair. Piaseczno: gather a day's thoughts and look at them; for an unnamed mood ask 'since when?'.
- **State, and from inside:** Sobering, then relief: Breslov says judging yourself removes the fears above you. Done right it ends in resolve and joy, not in sadness.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** What is looked at loses its grip; judging yourself below removes judgment from above; a heart made contrite is the vessel (Tanya 29 uses it to break the coarse heart before contemplation).
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** The Besht's strongest warning: sadness is the enemy's aim; over-scrupulous reckoning is his trick (Tzava'at HaRivash 44-46). Keep it short and end in joy.
- **Relations:** S1 (Breslov folds it into hisbodedus); E2 (Piaseczno's watching of thoughts is its moment-by-moment form); B4 (the night hour it often lives in).
- **Prior coverage:** darkei-hachassidus/sources-d-wider-chassidus.md 2.9; PIASECZNO.md step 3 and step 12.
- **Verified lines:**
- *לעשות חשבון עם נפשו, מכל המחשבות והדיבורים והמעשים שחלפו ועברו* (to make an accounting with his soul of all the thoughts, words and deeds that have passed) · Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 29:15, `Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt` line 395
- *והוא בעצמו ידין וישפט את עצמו על כל עסקיו ומעשיו* (and he himself will judge himself on all his affairs and deeds) · Likkutei Etzot, Hitbodedut 1, `Likkutei Etzot (Bratslav, Ukraine, 1790-1810).txt` line 326

## C6. Discerning the arousal: the Mitteler Rebbe's levels of hitpa'alut (התפעלות)

- **School and source:** Mitteler Rebbe (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, 1813).
- **Object:** The arousal itself, examined for its source. Object type: one's own state (a second-order technique).
- **Method:** Not a separate sitting but a discipline laid on every sitting: ask where the movement came from. If the idea moved you, it is Godly; if the wish to be moved moved you, it is 'serving oneself'. The ladder runs from hearing from afar, through the natural soul's responses, to the Godly soul's own levels up to the yechidah. Test afterwards: false bitul leaves you feeling yourself 'from that very experience'.
- **State, and from inside:** The truest arousal is felt and yet not felt as one's own, 'two opposites in one subject', like a song that sings itself. The deeper, the less felt.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** In holiness the vessel is in bitul, so the less self in the feeling, the more light; self-feeling is the mark of the counterfeit.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Do not chase bitul as a prize; do not despise the simple cry (it is the same arousal from the other side). Arousal must reach the deed or it is a flame in the air (A5).
- **Relations:** C1 (the contemplation it audits); B1 (the niggun is its model of felt-yet-unfelt); A5 (its closing rule).
- **Prior coverage:** KUNTRES-HAHITPAALUT.md and kuntres-hahitpaalut/; manual/part4-inside.md.
- **Verified lines:**
- *שיהיה התפעלות נפשם רק התפעלות אלקות דוקא ולא התפעלות חיי בשר* (that the arousal of their souls be only Godly arousal and not the arousal of the life of flesh) · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 7:3, `Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (Lubavitch, 1813).txt` line 135

## L1. Entering the letters of prayer and Torah (כניסה באותיות)

- **School and source:** Besht and the Maggid (Tzava'at HaRivash 75, 108, 118; Maggid Devarav leYaakov 27:4).
- **Object:** The letters and words being said. Object type: letter/sound.
- **Method:** Pray (or learn) aloud with all your strength in the words. Think that God clothes Himself in the letters and that each letter holds worlds, souls and Godliness. Put your soul into each letter ('come, you and your whole body, into the word'). Go from letter to letter until the body is forgotten and you feel the letters joining one another. Then the letters move into thought and you no longer hear what you say. Then comes ayin, where bodily powers are nullified. The Maggid maps the stages on the worlds: joining letters is Yetzirah, letters of thought are Beriah, ayin is Atzilut.
- **State, and from inside:** Delight 'without measure' as the letters join; then hearing drops away; then no one there. The Besht: said with great binding, every word already contains all the kabbalistic intentions 'of themselves'.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Speech is the garment of thought and God's power is in the letter, so the one who is wholly in the letter is one with Him: 'his strength is in the letter and in the letter the Holy One dwells'. The letters are the bridge that runs both ways: God descends into them, the soul ascends through them.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Begin slowly (L3); if a foreign thought 'rides the words' it is worse than silence (L3 mechanism). Needs faith: without it one 'separates the Friend'. The Besht puts this binding above the memorised kavanot (I7).
- **Relations:** L2 (its listening side); E1 (where it lands); I7 (the Arizal's kavanot it simplifies); L3 (the voice craft that carries it); A1 (the Torah letters as the all-day handle).
- **Prior coverage:** Mentioned in ONE-PROCESS.md and sources-d 1.1-1.5 as integration; not laid out as a method.

## L2. Hearing the world of speech speak: 'not I am speaking' (עולם הדיבור מדבר)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 75:3, 127:2).
- **Object:** The voice itself, heard as the Shechinah's speaking. Object type: sound.
- **Method:** While praying, listen to each word you say, and hear that the Shechinah, the world of speech, is speaking it. In ordinary speech the same: do not think you are the speaker; the life in you, the Creator, speaks in you and raises the word to its root. A word from the heart enters the upper heart 'by the breath'.
- **State, and from inside:** Speaking without being the author: the voice goes on and you are its listener. Humility without effort; the same with other people, whose speech is also from Him.

- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Needs 'great faith'. Not a license for passivity; the words must be said with all strength (L1).
- **Relations:** L1 (the entering side of the same act); E1 (agency dissolved into ayin); A1 (carried into ordinary conversation).

## L3. The craft of the voice: whisper-cry, full-strength word, lengthened word, slow start (קול ודיבור)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 32-34, 58, 70); Chabad custom (first verse aloud).
- **Object:** The word as sound. Object type: sound.
- **Method:** Start the prayer slowly and gather force toward the middle. Put all your strength in each word: each word is 'a complete stature', and a half-said word is like a body missing a limb. Learn to cry out in a whisper: the cry that comes from devekus should be quiet. When a word holds you, lengthen it, not wanting to part from it. When you fall, say the word with 'small intention', cleave first to the body of the word and then put the soul in. At a low level pray from the siddur, seeing the letters; at a high level close the eyes. The first verse of Shema is said aloud to arouse intention.
- **State, and from inside:** The voice becomes the handle of the attention; at the top the words 'come out of the mouth by themselves' and may even go fast.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** The sound is the vessel: 'the voice arouses the intention' (Shulchan Arukh HaRav; the Rashab says the same of melody). Strength in the outer letter lets the inner light shine; the body of the word first, then its soul.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Don't spend all your strength at the start (Tzava'at HaRivash 32, 38). Not loud for show; the devekus cry is in a whisper.
- **Relations:** L1 (the letters it carries); B1 (melody is the voice without words); B2 (the body-first rule is the same rule); S2 (the silent scream is its inward limit).

## L4. Verses as a counter-sound: interrupting a desire or a trait with holy words (אמירת פסוקים)

- **Relations:** E3 (the deeper form: bringing the trait to its root); L1 (same premise: letters carry holiness).

## L5. The Shema of Echad: ches, dalet, covered eyes, and the soul handed over (קריאת שמע)

- **School and source:** Shulchan Arukh HaRav OC 61; Arizal (Sha'ar HaKavanot, Shema 6:3); Tanya 41; Lizhensk; Piaseczno ('the leap').
- **Object:** The verse, and within it one word, Echad, taken letter by letter. Object type: letter/sound, with an image (the four directions) and an act of will (giving the soul).
- **Method:** Say the first verse as if it were a new decree you never heard. Say it aloud to arouse intention, hands over the face so the eyes see nothing else. On Echad: do not rush the ches, lengthen it a little, making Him king in heaven and earth (seven heavens and earth = 8); lengthen more on the dalet, long enough to think that He is alone in His world and rules the four directions. The lengthening is in thought, not a drawn-out sound; some tilt the head with the thought. Then pause before 'Baruch shem'. The Arizal adds: at the dalet of Echad give your soul over for the sanctification of the Name, accepting the four deaths of the court. Lizhensk: picture it happening and hold firm. The Tanya reframes it: prayer and Torah are themselves mesirus nefesh, the mind clothed in the letters, the soul returned to Him to unite with His oneness.
- **State, and from inside:** A short total act: for the length of a letter nothing is held back. Piaseczno calls it a leap.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Covering the eyes cuts the world out; the voice raises intention; the letter shapes (the ches's roof, the dalet's four corners) carry the image of kingship over all space. Giving the soul is what lets the lower Presence rise ('she has no strength to rise except by your giving over', Sha'ar HaKavanot). In Tanya's terms, the soul's hidden love refuses separation from the One, and the Shema wakes it.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Halakhic limits: the dalet is not to be drawn out in sound or mispronounced (SAH 61:7). The martyrdom image is to be held truthfully (Lizhensk: 'speak truth in your heart').

## I1. The Name before the eyes: picturing Havayah (ציור שם הוי"ה)

- **School and source:** Arizal (Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh 3:12, 3:20; Sha'ar HaKavanot; Pri Etz Chaim); Reshit Chokhmah; Sefer Charedim; Lizhensk.
- **Object:** The four letters of the Name, written in square (Assyrian) script, sometimes vocalised, sometimes interleaved with Adnut. Object type: name/image.
- **Method:** Picture the four letters of Havayah between the eyes, in Assyrian script, as 'I have set God before me always'. Hold them. Notice what can be pictured and what cannot: a letter that will not form marks a flaw; the colour tells the world you cleave to (black ink: Asiyah; red: Yetzirah; white: Beriah; white and shining: Atzilut). The truest seeing is when the Name looks like a flame of fire. You may picture it in each limb. Variations: Reshit Chokhmah, be alone part of the day and think on the greatness of the Name's letters; Sefer Charedim, intend the vav of the Name in Torah and the heh in a mitzvah; Lizhensk, when walking and meeting a woman, picture Adnut before the eyes; at bedtime, set YAHDVNHY before the eyes, and hold the Name vocalised with segol until you sink into sleep.
- **State, and from inside:** Gladness and a polished, shining soul (Reshit Chokhmah: whoever wants to gladden his soul...). Steadiness: the eyes have one place to rest. Also, in the Arizal's form, a self-diagnosis.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** The soul is hewn from the Name, so thinking on it makes the soul shine. 'As water face to face': engraving the Name in your heart engraves your soul above. The Name is a fixed point that blocks the eyes' wandering (Lizhensk uses it against looking).
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** The Arizal's diagnostic reading (colours, missing letters, death decreed for a practised one who cannot picture it) is not for ordinary use and can frighten; treat it as report, not instruction. A related image, not a separate technique: the Tanya's 'candle that always flickers upward' (the soul's nature to leave the wick and cleave to its root) and the Arizal's flame-Name share the picture of fire; no source here tells one to gaze at a candle.
- **Relations:** C3 (the same verse as awareness rather than picture); I7 (the yichudim are built from Names); L5 (the bedtime Shema form); E1 (the Name held till sleep: a gentle loss of self).
- **Prior coverage:** Not treated before (Chabad files use Shiviti as awareness).

## I2. Picturing: the Throne, the scene 'as if you were there', the self in its root (ציור)

- **School and source:** Piaseczno (Bnei Machshava Tova, Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim, Tzav VeZeruz).
- **Object:** A picture: standing before the Throne as a son before a father; a holy scene; one's own soul in its root. Object type: image.
- **Method:** Picture yourself standing before the Throne of Glory, praying simply like a son pleading with his father. Or take part in a holy event as if you were there. When nothing moves, picture yourself a tzaddik and see your soul in its root. Simple and broad, not deep and clever. Once the feeling is there, stop hunting for images and stay in the feeling.
- **State, and from inside:** Warmth, nearness, the child's plea; then the picture falls away by itself.

- **Prerequisites and cautions:** God has no image; the picture is a handrail that drops away. Do not force the image; come at it sideways from something you do not care about.
- **Relations:** E2 (the same law run backwards: looking at an image dims it); S1 (the Throne is where the speech goes); I1 (picture of letters versus picture of scene).

## I3. The fire of Kiddush Hashem: imagined martyrdom (ונקדשתי)

- **School and source:** Lizhensk (Tzetl Katan 1-3); Arizal (four deaths at Echad, bedtime Shema, nefilat apayim); Besht (ready to die in prayer).
- **Object:** An image of a great fire reaching to heaven, and oneself choosing it. Object type: image + act of will.
- **Method:** In any free moment (sitting idle alone, lying awake), picture a great and awesome fire burning before you up to the heart of heaven, and yourself, for the sanctity of the Name, breaking your nature and throwing yourself into it. - **State, and from inside:** Everything small drops away; there is no room left for vanity ('why do I need any self-regard, if I may die after two or three words?').

## I4. Histaklus: seeing the life inside things (הסתכלות)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 90; 22); Maggid (the good eye, MDL 43); Alter Rebbe (Tanya 42).
- **Object:** Whatever is in front of the eyes: a face, a vessel, food, the sky. Object type: world.
- **Method:** When you see beauty, ask where it comes from: a dead body has no such face, so the beauty is a divine power spreading in it; why be drawn after the part when you can cleave to the root of all beauty. The same with a vessel (the form is its spirit, the matter its dross) and with food (the sweetness is the upper life). The Maggid: when you look at a thing, know that it is as nothing before Him; this 'good eye' draws more life into it, and the admiring eye that makes it a thing by itself cuts it from its root. The Tanya: train the mind so that all it sees, heaven and earth, are the King's outer garments, and so remember their inner life. Whatever you see, remember the Holy One.
- **State, and from inside:** The world stays, but thin, like a garment with someone inside; desire turns into longing for the root. Done all day it reaches the dreams, where the spiritual is seen 'naked, without garment'.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Looking makes a vessel (Maggid): the way you look decides whether the thing is joined to its root or cut off. Seeing the part as part sends the pull back to the whole. Habit, like a craftsman's hands, makes it the mind's default.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** The Besht also warns not to gaze at worldly things for their own sake (Tzava'at HaRivash 5, 90:1); histaklus is an answer to a glance, not a licence to look. Not to be practised as an exercise on forbidden sights.
- **Relations:** E3 (the pull it meets is raised to its root); C3 (the looking is mutual in Shiviti); A2 (applied to eating).
- **Prior coverage:** sources-d 1.5 (Maggid's opening) as integration; not as a method.

## I6. Ascent in thought: katnus and gadlus, palace to palace (עליה במחשבה)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 67, 87, 135-137; the letter on his ascent of the soul).
- **Object:** The upper worlds as places the mind can be in. Object type: image/world.
- **Method:** Two modes. Katnus (smallness): do not go up at all; only think that the whole earth is full of His glory and He is near, and serve in great devekus below. Gadlus (greatness): strengthen yourself and rise in thought, splitting all the firmaments at once, above angels and thrones. Order: first cleave below properly, then rise. Outside prayer too: think that you are above the dome of the sky. In prayer, walk palace to palace; a foreign thought means you were judged and pushed out, so return in shame and lowliness. Come down several times a day to rest.
- **State, and from inside:** 'Where a person thinks, there he is': the Besht says that in gadlus he is in the upper worlds. Katnus is quieter but still great devekus.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Thought is a complete stature and goes where it thinks (the Maggid: wherever he thinks, he draws himself there). The descent is for ascent.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Do not force gadlus; most of the time a person can only serve in katnus, and that is also service. Descending to rest is required, since constant delight is no delight.
- **Relations:** I5 (the older ascent it internalises); I8 (the Arizal's fuller retreat protocol); E1 (at the top the thinker disappears).
- **Prior coverage:** Not treated before.

## I7. Kavanot and yichudim of the Arizal (including at the graves of tzaddikim) (כוונות ויחודים)

- **School and source:** Arizal (Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh, Sha'ar HaKavanot, Pri Etz Chaim); received and simplified by the Besht.
- **Object:** Divine Names and their numerical and letter combinations, joined in the mind; at a grave, also the tzaddik's soul. Object type: name + person.
- **Method:** First mend the soul (tikkunim for sins, purity). Then unify: hold the Names and join them in the prescribed order (e.g. Havayah with Adnut; the yichud 'you must always set before your eyes'). At home, only after midnight; at a grave, on the eve of Rosh Chodesh or the fifteenth, not on Shabbat or festivals. At the grave: stretch out on it, intend that your stretching out causes the tzaddik's soul to stretch out into his bones so he is as if alive, and raise your soul and his bound together, yours included in his, from your place in the shared root in Adam up to its head, as 'mayin nukvin'. The Besht: when you do yichudim, think at that moment of His greatness.
- **State, and from inside:** The Arizal describes the souls of tzaddikim cleaving to the one who unifies, and revelation (ruach hakodesh, the tzaddik's teaching) following.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Names are the channels of the worlds; uniting them in the mind below causes union above ('arousal from below'). At the grave, soul joins soul 'spirit to spirit', sharing a root.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Sha'arei Kedusha: the 'straight way' is holiness that brings inspiration by itself; forcing it through adjurations and yichudim risks mixed, external results if one errs at all. The Besht: great binding in the plain word includes all the kavanot by itself. The mechanics of the numbers are for specialists and are not unpacked here.
- **Relations:** I1 (its elementary form, the single Name); I8 (its retreat setting); B3 (the prostration it uses); S5 (cleaving to a tzaddik without Names).
- **Prior coverage:** Not treated before.

## I8. The prophetic retreat of Sha'arei Kedusha: as if the soul has left (התבודדות לרוח הקודש)

- **School and source:** R. Chaim Vital (Sha'arei Kedusha III:8).
- **Object:** First nothing (the world stripped away), then the upper worlds and the soul's roots. Object type: nothing, then image.
- **Method:** After long purification (repentance, all the positive commandments, rising at midnight, love of every Jew, good traits acquired as habit): immerse, enter a house alone where no human voice or bird will disturb, preferably after midnight. Close the eyes. Strip the mind of this world as if the soul had left, like a dead man who feels nothing. Then with strong desire think of the upper world and cleave there to the roots of your soul; imagine your soul has risen and picture the upper worlds as if you stand in them. If you did a yichud, draw light down through all worlds and intend to receive your share last. Remain alone in thought as if the spirit rested on you. If nothing comes, you are not yet ready: return to service and try again after some days.
- **State, and from inside:** At first the spirit comes rarely, with light matters and few words; it grows. The goal named: uprooting the imaginative faculty and cleaving above 'in the utmost cleaving'.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Emptying the mind (imagined death) removes the self that blocks; imagination then aims the emptied mind upward; purity of vessel decides what comes.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Test what comes: if its words are false, or mixed, or about worldly vanities, or not according to Torah, push it away; Ben Azzai and Ben Zoma prove the danger. Part four (the practical ways) was deliberately not printed. The Besht: never be alone in hisbodedus without a companion in the room.
- **Relations:** E1 (its first half is ayin); I6 (its second half is ascent); S1 (Breslov keeps the solitude but turns it into speech); I7 (yichudim may be done inside it).

## E. Emptying / ayin

*Attention lets go of its content and of the self that holds it, and stands in the nothing between states.*

## E1. Ayin and bitul: thinking oneself nothing; standing in the nothing between states (אין / ביטול)

- **School and source:** Maggid of Mezritch (MDL 27, 68); Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 53, 62, 101); Breslov (LM 52); Chabad (bitul as the aim).
- **Object:** Nothing: the self set aside; or the gap between one state and the next. Object type: nothing.
- **Method:** Think of yourself as nothing and forget yourself completely; ask everything in prayer for the Shechinah's sake, not your own needs. In prayer be as if stripped of physicality, not feeling your own existence ('if I am not for myself, who is for me': when I am not, no foreign thought comes near). Make yourself as one who is not. Even in marital union, be ayin. The Maggid's model of change: between egg and chicken there is a moment that is neither, which no one can pin down; that is ayin, and every change passes through it. So at the turn of a thought, a mood or a trait, stand in that neither-nor.
- **State, and from inside:** Above time: 'the world of thought where all is equal, life and death, sea and dry land'. No fear of foreign thoughts, since there is no one for them to trouble.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** The Infinite has no vessel in a 'something' asking for itself; it can clothe itself only in one who thinks himself nothing. Change requires passing through ayin, because the new comes only from nothing (wisdom is 'ayin'). Breslov: the road to bitul is hisbodedus.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Bitul sought as a prize is its opposite (KHH). It is reached, not posed; the Chabad test is the afterwards (C6). Not dissociation: the Maggid pairs it with prayer and service.
- **Relations:** L1 (the letter path ends here); E2 (Piaseczno's quieting is a gentler entrance); E3 (every raised desire passes through ayin); S1 (Breslov's road to it); C1 (Chabad's road to it).
- **Prior coverage:** ONE-PROCESS.md step 7 ('before Him, nothing'); sources-d 1.4; manual/part4-inside.md state 5.

## E2. Hashkatah: quieting by watching the thoughts (השקטה)

- **School and source:** Piaseczno (Derekh HaMelekh 'Mind Quieting Advice'; Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim); older form 'waiting an hour'.
- **Object:** The stream of thoughts, looked at from outside. Object type: nothing / the mind itself.
- **Method:** Sit and look at your thoughts for a few minutes: 'what am I thinking?'. Little by little the head empties and the thoughts stop their usual flood. Then say one verse, such as 'Hashem Elokim Emet', to tie the emptied head to one holy thought. Then ask for what you need, softly, never forcefully, and in positive terms. Alternative entrance: gaze at the small hand of a clock. Close with a verse in a melody. Practise for weeks.
- **State, and from inside:** The head emptying; a sleep-like quiet while awake, into which inspiration can come.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** The self's busyness (yeshut) is what opposes inspiration from above; in sleep it gets in because the mind is still. Looking at a thought dims it; dwelling in it feeds it (the law of the relative's image).
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Force wakes the ego. Do not fight thoughts; they grow stronger. Piaseczno uses it as a door, not a destination: the emptied mind is tied at once to a holy verse.
- **Relations:** E1 (a modern entrance to ayin); C4 (the verse it ends on); I2 (the same law of images); B1 (closes with melody).

## E3. Raising the fallen love or fear to its root (העלאת מחשבות ומדות לשרשן)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 87, 90, 120, 127); Maggid.
- **Object:** The arriving desire, fear, pride or pleasure itself. Object type: one's own movement of heart.
- **Method:** If you are praying without fervour, push the foreign thought out and pray with force. If you are in fervour, look at the thought: what is it? If a bad love, bring it to its root, the love of God: say in your heart, what have I done, taking a piece of the world of thought to a filthy place; be humbled to the dust; bring the thought to ayin; and then love returns to the world: if I love this, how much more should I love Him. The same with fear (why fear a man? God is clothed in him), pride, victory, gratitude, bonding. With pleasure: this is a part of the world of pleasure; set yourself wholly into it and you are, sitting here eating, in the world of pleasure.
- **State, and from inside:** The energy of the pull is not lost; it turns into love, awe or delight in God. Often with a moment of shame, then warmth.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** There are seven 'thoughts' (the seven traits), each fallen at the breaking; every love and fear is a spark of His, in exile. Taking it back to its root (through ayin) raises the spark and sweetens the judgment in its root.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** The Besht's condition: only when in fervour; otherwise push the thought away. The Tanya (ch. 28) limits raising foreign thoughts to tzaddikim and tells the ordinary person to act as if he did not hear them and strengthen his prayer. This is a real disagreement; the hisbonenus diagnostics mark foreign thoughts as a disputed state ('Intruded on': return without commentary).
- **Relations:** E1 (the passage through ayin); L4 (the quick counter-sound form); I4 (the same move on what is seen); A2 (the same move on eating).
- **Prior coverage:** The dispute over foreign thoughts is noted in Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md (disputed states).

## E4. Stillness and silence: prayer without motion, the hidden hisbodedus (שתיקה ומנוחה)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 59, 105, 133).
- **Object:** Nothing outward; one thought. Object type: nothing / one idea.
- **Method:** When cleaving, make no movement, not even of the body, so as not to break it. Pray with love, awe and great fervour without any movement, so that to another it looks like words without devekus. Prefer silence to talk, even talk of Torah, since in silence one can think more of His greatness. Lie in bed looking asleep while alone with the Creator. Keep 'only one thought' in His service.
- **State, and from inside:** Hidden, inward fervour; nothing shows. The Besht rates it higher and faster than the prayer seen on the limbs, and 'the husk has no hold on it'.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Stillness keeps the thread of devekus unbroken; what is wholly inward has no outside for the other side to grasp.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Comes after the body has been roused (B2); first body, then thought alone. Not for the beginner's cold day.
- **Relations:** B2 (its opposite and prerequisite: rousing by movement); E1 (its inner content); S1 (the lying-in-bed form is a silent hisbodedus).

## S. Speech / relationship

*Attention turns into address: talking to God, or joining to another soul (a tzaddik, a friend, every Jew).*

## S1. Hisbodedus: private speech with God (התבודדות)

- **School and source:** Breslov (LM 52; Likkutei Etzot 'Hitbodedut'); Sefer Charedim; Besht; Piaseczno.
- **Object:** God as 'You', and one's own heart laid open. Object type: person (the divine You) and self.
- **Method:** Set a fixed time daily (Likkutei Etzot: 'some hours in the day'; the later Breslov custom is an hour), best at night when the world is free, in a special place outside the town where no one goes, or in a field among grasses and trees (they come into the prayer). Speak in your own language, as to a teacher or a friend, with arguments, excuses, pleading. Pour out what you want to leave and what you long for. Judge yourself. Make prayers out of the teachings you learn. If words will not come, say one word, even only 'Master of the world'; even speaking without heart is very good, since speech wakes the heart. Do not wait for tears. Sefer Charedim's older form: one day a week (or one in fifteen, or one a month) withdraw from people, bind the mind to Him as if already standing before Him on the day of judgment, and speak softly as a servant to his master and a son to his father.
- **State, and from inside:** Slow: a trait at a time is cancelled, then the self; one is included in His unity. From inside it alternates between a stone heart and sudden openness; Breslov says the Shechinah pours out her own talk in answer.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Nullification comes only through hisbodedus: speaking before Him lets one cancel the desires one by one and then the whole self. Speech has great power; the words God sends into the mouth of one who tries are ruach hakodesh. Sefer Charedim: it helps the soul sevenfold more than study.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Heartbreak is not sadness; keep it from turning into depression. The Besht: have another person in the room when alone (TzH 63). Note: Sefer Charedim's text in this corpus says one day a week and 'sevenfold', not 'one seventh of the day'.
- **Relations:** E1 (its goal); C5 (the accounting inside it); S2 (its silent form); I8 (the older silent retreat it turns into speech); L1 (Torah turned into prayer).

## B1. Niggun: melody as a vessel above words (ניגון)

- **School and source:** Chabad (Alter Rebbe 5566 maamar; Rashab 5668; Rayatz); Breslov (LM 54, 64); Piaseczno; Mitteler Rebbe (the song that sings itself).
- **Object:** A melody, often wordless; sometimes only thought. Object type: sound without letters.
- **Method:** Put the contemplation into a tune: the same contemplation sung aloud moves the soul far more than without melody (Rashab). A Chabad niggun may map the path itself: the Rayatz describes his father praying in a three-part tune (first, thinking an abstract idea and going down into it; second, being moved by it; third, longing for breadth and height without end). ## B3. Prostration: nefilat apayim and stretching out on the grave (נפילת אפים / השתטחות)

- **School and source:** Arizal (Pri Etz Chaim; Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh).

## B4. Tikkun Chatzos: rising at midnight to mourn and learn (תקון חצות)

- **School and source:** Arizal (Pri Etz Chaim 'Gate of the Midnight Prayer'); Alter Rebbe (SAH OC 1:2-3); Besht; Breslov (LM 149).
- **Object:** The exile of the Presence and one's own sins; then Torah. Object type: idea + body in a set posture and hour.
- **Method:** Rise at midnight (or at least in the last third of the night). Sit on the ground by the door near the mezuzah, shoes off, head wrapped like a mourner, weep as much as you can (the Arizal adds ash on the forehead). Lament a little with 'By the rivers of Babylon' and the like; better a little with intention than much without. Then learn Torah until morning. The Besht: if sleep falls on you, walk about the house and sing loudly.
- **State, and from inside:** Grief that turns: the night is a 'time of favour', and Breslov calls midnight a redemption that sweetens judgments.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** At midnight the Holy One Himself laments, and 'the Shechinah is opposite' whoever learns at night. Mourning with Him joins the person to His grief; the hour's stillness makes the heart open.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Not to become sadness: it passes into learning and joy. The Besht pairs rising at midnight with sleeping some hours by day, so health is kept.
- **Relations:** C5 (the accounting at night); S1 (Breslov's night hisbodedus); I7 (yichudim at home only after midnight).
- **Prior coverage:** Only mentioned in passing earlier.
- **Verified lines:**

## B5. Mikveh and the contemplative fast (טבילה ותענית בכוונה)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 15, 43, 77); Sha'arei Kedusha (immersion before the retreat).
- **Object:** The body's purification or restraint, with a set inner script. Object type: body + idea.
- **Method:** Immerse often, with the intention that belongs to the mikveh. On a fast, say inwardly the Besht's long script (Tzava'at HaRivash 43): woe is me for my desires; I afflict myself to humble them, to bring union above, to ease the Shechinah's sorrow; I trust in His kindness for strength; all is from Him. Sleep a little at the start, move about, learn in thought alone to ease the pain.
- **State, and from inside:** Lightness and joy in the suffering; the Besht insists on joy.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Affliction accepted for His sake humbles the desires below and 'causes above' the removal of husks and union. Immersion changes the body's state and, with intention, the soul's.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** The Besht's stronger rule: serving in joy without self-afflictions is better, since afflictions cause sadness; do not fast to reach a level; do not be proud of it; guard health. The guide should not recommend fasting; the mikveh and the inner script are the transferable parts.
- **Relations:** C5 (the accounting it embodies); I8 (immersion before the retreat).
- **Prior coverage:** Not treated before.
- **Verified lines:**

## B6. Breath (a gap, recorded as such) (נשימה / הבל)

- **School and source:** Maggid (MDL 99:2); Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 71).
- **Object:** The breath, as carrier of speech and life. Object type: body.
- **Method:** No breathing exercise appears in these sources. What appears is theory: 'for each breath, praise God' read as the life going into the body and at once returning upward ('this is the breath'), carrying what one thought, said and did; and the word from the heart entering the upper heart 'by the breath'.
- **State, and from inside:** Not described.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** Breath is the pulse of reaching-and-not-reaching (mati velo mati) of the life-force; it is the vehicle of speech, so the practice lives in L1-L3.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Do not import breath-counting techniques under this tradition's name; the sources give the idea, not a drill.
- **Relations:** L3 (where breath is actually worked: the voice); A1 (the rhythm of running and returning).
- **Prior coverage:** Not treated before.
- **Verified lines:**
- *על כל נשימה ונשימה תהלל יה* (for each and every breath, praise God) · Maggid Devarav leYaakov 99:2, `Maggid Devarav leYaakov (Mezritch, Volhynia, 1760-1780).txt` line 215

## A1. Devekus in all activity: the small thought kept lit (דביקות / מחשבה קטנה)

- **School and source:** Besht (Keter Shem Tov; Tzava'at HaRivash 81, 84, 94, 111).
- **Object:** God, held lightly behind whatever one is doing; often through the letters of Torah. Object type: idea/presence, or letters.
- **Method:** Keep 'I have set God before me always' even going about business. Treat the upper world as your home: when you must go out into worldly talk, go like a man who leaves his house meaning to return at once, and come straight back to the first devekus. Train it: while in devekus, deliberately ask for some household need or say something, so as to learn to stay attached while doing physical things. When the fire goes (it must: fervour is 'reaching and not reaching'), hold on to His garments, the letters of Torah, which one can think of always, even while talking with people.
- **State, and from inside:** Not ecstasy: a low steady glow, a live coal from which the fire can be blown up again.
- **Mechanism (their terms):** 'Know Him in all your ways': knowing is joining. The physical is a throne for the spiritual. The coal must not go out; if even the small thought goes out, the soul goes out. The letters are the permanent handle because God is clothed in them.
- **Prerequisites and cautions:** Do not rely on devekus to excuse idle talk (Tzava'at HaRivash 30:2). Coming down to rest is part of it (I6).
- **Relations:** C3 (the same verse as awareness); L1 (the letters as the handle); A2 (eating and using); I6 (the home above).

## A2. Eating and using with elevation (birurim) (בירורים)

- **School and source:** Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 87:10, 109, 127:3).

## A3. Imitatio Dei: making oneself like the Maker in deeds (התדמות לקונו)

- **School and source:** R. Moshe Cordovero (Tomer Devorah); taken up widely in Chassidus.

## 4. How meditation works in this tradition (our synthesis)

*This section is ours: a reading across the catalogue, in plain English. The lines under each point are the sources' own words that the reading rests on.*

**1. Attach the mind to a vessel that already carries Him.** Almost no technique aims the mind at God directly. The Besht says why: one cannot cleave to Him, for He is a consuming fire, so cleave to His garments, the letters. Every family picks a garment: the letter and the word (L1-L3), the Name (I1), a verse (L4, C4), a melody (B1), a tzaddik (S5), a mitzvah or a meal (A2), a thing seen (I4). The mind holds the vessel with full force and the vessel does the carrying. The Mitteler Rebbe states the rule from the other end: light holds only in a vessel. Seen in: L1, L2, L3, I1, B1, S5, A1, A2. - *כי זה אפשר להיות תמיד במחשבתו לחשוב באותיות התורה והתורה הוא לבושו יתב'* (for this is possible always: to think in his mind of the letters of the Torah, and the Torah is His garment) · Tzava'at HaRivash 111:3, line 229 - *כי בזה נעשה אחדות עמו ית' כי כחו בהאות ובהאות שורה הקב"ה* (for by this he becomes one with Him: his strength is in the letter, and in the letter the Holy One dwells) · Tzava'at HaRivash 108:1, line 221 - *שאין לאור אחיזה ותפיסה רק בכלי* (that light has no hold except in a vessel) · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 38:2, line 769

**2. The obstacle is the self-image, and each family loosens it a different way.** The Maggid puts the problem in one line: while a person thinks of himself as a 'something' asking for his needs, the Infinite cannot clothe itself in him. Piaseczno says the same: the self's busyness opposes inspiration. The families are different ways of getting the self out of the way. Letters overload it until the body is forgotten (L1). Quieting starves it (E2). Hearing the Shechinah speak takes away its authorship (L2). The fire, the Shema and the 'as if dead' of Sha'arei Kedusha confront it with its end (I3, L5, I8). Hisbodedus talks it down, trait by trait (S1). Discernment catches it hiding inside the experience itself (C6). None of them attacks the self head-on; each gives attention something else to do until the self is not needed. Seen in: E1, E2, L1, L2, I3, I8, S1, C6. - *וכן כשחושב א"ע ליש ומבקש צרכיו אז אין הקב"ה יכול להתלבש בו* (when he thinks of himself as a 'something' and asks for his needs, the Holy One cannot clothe Himself in him) · Maggid Devarav leYaakov 68:6, line 156 - *וזהו הנקרא עובד את עצמו* (and this is called serving oneself) · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 8:8, line 167 - *צריך בתפלה להיות כמו מופשט מגשמיות ואיננו מרגיש מציאותו בעוה"ז* (in prayer one must be as if stripped of physicality, not feeling his own existence in this world) · Tzava'at HaRivash 62:1, line 115

**3. One ladder runs under many techniques: body, voice, letters, thought, nothing.** The Maggid gives the ladder most exactly: go letter to letter with all your strength until you forget the body (Yetzirah), then into the letters of thought where you no longer hear yourself (Beriah), then ayin (Atzilut). The same order recurs everywhere. The Besht: rouse the body first, then serve in thought alone; cleave to the body of the word, then put in its soul; pray from the siddur when small, close the eyes when high. The Shulchan Arukh HaRav: the first verse aloud to arouse intention. The Rashab: the voice arouses the intention of the heart. Piaseczno: for most people it is easier to start from the lower soul. So the tradition's first rule of method is to start low and outward and let the practice climb inward. Nobody is told to begin in silence. Seen in: L1, L3, B2, E4, B1, L5. - *בתפלה צריך לשום כל כחו בהדבורים וילך כך מאות לאות עד שישכח מגופניות* (in prayer one must put all his strength into the words and go so from letter to letter until he forgets his bodiliness) · Maggid Devarav leYaakov 27:4, line 84 - *ואח"כ יבא לאותיות המחשבה ולא ישמיע מה שהוא מדבר* (then he comes to the letters of thought and does not hear what he is saying) · Maggid Devarav leYaakov 27:4, line 84 - *ואח"כ הוא בא למדת אי"ן שנתבטל אצלו כל כחותיו הגשמיים* (then he comes to the attribute of ayin, where all his bodily powers are nullified) · Maggid Devarav leYaakov 27:4, line 84 - *וצריך לעורר עצמו בתחלה בגוף שלו בכל כחו כדי שתאיר בו כח הנשמה* (first he must rouse himself in his body with all his strength so the power of the soul will shine in him) · Tzava'at HaRivash 58:3, line 111 - *שע"י הניגון דוקא נעשה התפעלות הנפש דקול מעורר הכוונה שבלב* (through melody specifically the soul is moved, for the voice arouses the intention in the heart) · Sefer HaMaamarim (scans, OCR), 5668, scan p. 104 ¶1, line 27679

**4. Imagination is used, then dropped.** The image techniques (I1-I8, and the Throne of I2) are generous with pictures: letters in fire, the Throne, the burning pyre, the palaces, the upper worlds. But every source that uses the picture expects it to fall away. Piaseczno: the bodily picture cancels itself. Sha'arei Kedusha: the aim is to uproot the imaginative faculty entirely. Breslov: melody subdues the imagination. The picture is scaffolding that aims an emptied mind, and it has to come down. That is why emptying (E) and image (I) are paired so often in one protocol (I8 is both). Seen in: I1, I2, I8, B1, E2. - *וידמה בעצמו כאלו יצאה נפשו ועלתה למעלה, ויצייר עולמות העליונים כאלו עומד בהם* (and imagine that his soul has left and risen above, and picture the upper worlds as if he stands in them) · Sha'arei Kedusha, Part 3 8:8, line 452 - *ועקירת הרהורי המחשבה וכח המדמה שבו לגמרי, ויתדבק בעליונים בתכלית הדבוק* (and uprooting the musings of thought and the imaginative faculty in him entirely, and cleaving above with the utmost cleaving) · Sha'arei Kedusha, Part 3 8:11, line 455 - *עלידי נגון ביד הנ"ל, עלידיזה נכנע המדמה* (through melody played by hand, the imagination is subdued) · Likutei Moharan 54:7:18, line 2810

**5. Change passes through ayin.** The Maggid's egg and chicken: there is a moment that is neither, and every change passes through it. The Besht's way with a bad love uses the same hinge: shame, then 'bring the thought to ayin', and then love returns turned toward God. The Shema's handing-over at the dalet, the prostration, the midnight lament and the 'as if dead' are all short planned passages through nothing. The sitting is built to go through a moment of no-self and come back different, not to stay there. Seen in: E1, E3, L5, B3, B4, I8. - *ויש שעה שאינו לא ביצה ולא תרנגל ואין שום אדם יכול לכוין את השעה כי אז היא בחינת אי"ן* (there is a moment when it is neither egg nor chicken, and no one can pin down that moment, for then it is ayin) · Maggid Devarav leYaakov 27:2, line 82 - *ויביא זאת המחשבה למדת אין* (and bring this thought to the attribute of ayin) · Tzava'at HaRivash 87:8, line 166 - *ולכן אנו צריכין למסור נפשינו על קדוש השם באות ד' דאחד* (therefore we must give our souls over for the sanctification of the Name at the dalet of Echad) · Sha'ar HaKavanot, Sermons on Intentions of the Shema Recitation 6:3:5, line 301

**6. It is a relationship even when it is silent.** The sources keep describing a mirror. Reshit Chokhmah: engrave the Name in your heart and your soul is engraved above, as water reflects a face. The Besht: you look at the Creator and the Creator looks at you. Breslov: when you pour out your talk, the Shechinah pours out hers. Arizal: your stretching out on the grave makes the tzaddik's soul stretch out. So even the letter and image techniques are addressed; they are not exercises on an object. This is why speech (S1) sits so easily inside all the others. Seen in: I1, C3, S1, I7, S5. - *כן יגרום שתחקק ציור נשמתו למעלה* (so he causes the image of his soul to be engraved above) · Reshit Chokhmah, Gate of Love 1:43, line 929 - *והוא מסתכל על הבורא ית'. והבורא ית' מסתכל בו* (he looks at the Creator, and the Creator looks at him) · Tzava'at HaRivash 137:5, line 288 - *אזי גם השכינה כנגדו מפרשת לפניו שיחתה וצערה* (then the Shechinah opposite him also pours out before him her talk and her sorrow) · Likkutei Etzot, Hitbodedut 11, line 336

**7. A pulse, not a plateau.** Fervour is 'reaching and not reaching': constant delight is no delight. So the design is two-part. There are peaks at set times (prayer, the night, the weekly retreat), and a thread through the rest of the day (the small thought, the letters of Torah as handle, the declared intention). One must come down to rest several times a day. What must never go out is the coal: if even the small thought goes out, the soul goes out. Seen in: A1, A4, I6, S1, B4. - *כן ההתלהבות הוא במטי ולא מטי כי תענוג תמידי אינו תענוג* (so fervour is 'reaching and not reaching', for constant delight is no delight) · Tzava'at HaRivash 111:2, line 228 - *וצריך לירד למטה כמה פעמים ביום לנוח עצמו ממחשבתו מעט* (and he must come down several times a day to rest a little from his thought) · Tzava'at HaRivash 135:2, line 281 - *כך לא יהיה דבוק במחשבה קטנה תמיד בו ית' יכבה נשמתו לגמרי* (so, if he is not always attached to Him by a small thought, his soul will go out entirely) · Keter Shem Tov 1:185:2, line 257

**8. The practice ends in the deed.** Arousal that does not reach action is a flame flying in the air. The tradition ends its sittings in an act (A5), carries the state into eating and using (A2) and into conduct shaped on the divine attributes (A3), and treats every act as a chance to renew the intention (A4). The body is not left behind at the top; the Besht calls it a throne for the spirit. Seen in: A1, A2, A3, A4, A5. - *הרי זה כשלהבת הפורח באויר שאין לו קיום* (it is like a flame flying in the air, which has no endurance) · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 38:11, line 778 - *מאחר שהגשמי הוא כסא לרוחני* (since the physical is a throne for the spiritual) · Keter Shem Tov 2:47:2, line 456 - *האדם ראוי שיתדמה לקונו* (a person should make himself like his Maker) · Tomer Devorah 1:1, line 1

**9. The tradition builds in its own quality control.** Almost every technique carries a test, and the tests agree. Sha'arei Kedusha checks the content of what comes (true, mixed, vain). The Mitteler Rebbe checks the afterwards (do you feel yourself 'from that very experience'?). The Besht checks the motive (not to reach a level) and the mood (sadness is the enemy's aim). Lizhensk checks sincerity (do not fool the Mind above). The Besht adds a companion in the room. The stronger the technique, the stronger the warning attached to it. Seen in: I8, C6, B5, A4, S6. - *אם כל דבריו אמת או שקר ואמת מעורבין* (whether all its words are true, or falsehood and truth mixed) · Sha'arei Kedusha, Part 3 8:10, line 454 - *בא לכלל הרגשת העצמיות מזה עצמו דוקא* (he comes to feel himself, from that very experience) · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39:11, line 808 - *ולא יכוין בעבודתו רק לעשות נחת רוח להבורא ית' לבד ולא שיבא למדרגה* (let him intend in his service only to give pleasure to the Creator, and not to attain a level) · Tzava'at HaRivash 47:1, line 94 - *אך יזהר שיהיה דובר באמת בלבבו* (but let him take care to speak truth in his heart) · Noam Elimelekh, Introduction, The Tzetel Katan 3, line 25

## 5. Corrections and disagreements kept

- **Sefer Charedim is not 'one seventh of the day'.** The text in this corpus says: one day a week (or one in fifteen, or one a month) withdraw and be alone with God, and 'this helps the soul sevenfold more than study' (Sefer Charedim, line 937). The seven is a multiplier, not a fraction.
- **Shulchan Arukh HaRav OC 1:5 is Shiviti as awareness, not visualisation.** It asks the person to take to heart that the King stands over him and sees (line 71). Picturing the four letters is the Arizal's (Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh line 24), Reshit Chokhmah's and Lizhensk's. They are two techniques (C3 and I1).
- **Echad is lengthened in thought, not in sound.** SAH 61:7 (line 773) forbids drawing out the dalet in pronunciation.
- **Foreign thoughts: raise them or ignore them?** The Besht raises them to their root when in fervour (E3); the Tanya (ch. 28, line 373) says this was said only for tzaddikim and tells others to act as if they did not hear (line 379). Kept, not resolved.
- **Kavanot versus binding.** The Arizal prescribes exact Names; the Besht says great binding in the word includes all the kavanot by itself (line 239); Sha'arei Kedusha itself prefers the 'straight way' of holiness to forcing through yichudim (line 443).
- **The candle flame.** The Tanya's candle (line 264) and the Arizal's Name 'like a flame of fire' (line 24) are images of the soul's nature and of true seeing; no source in the corpus instructs gazing at a candle.
- **The head between the knees** (the classic Merkavah posture) is not in the Heikhalot Rabbati text here; it comes from a Geonic responsum outside this corpus.

## 6. Gaps

- **Abulafia and letter permutation**: left to the separate study by design.
- **Sha'arei Kedusha part four** (the practical ways to ruach hakodesh) was withheld by the printer; the corpus has only its outline (line 461).
- **The Arizal's yichudim in detail**: the numerical mechanics of each yichud in Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh (lines 165-300) were not unpacked; only the frame, timing, prerequisites and the grave practice.
- **Breath**: no breathing technique exists in these sources; only theory (B6). Nothing was imported.
- **Karlin, Berditchev, Kotzk, Izhbitz, Ger (Sfas Emes), Ropshitz, Komarno**: not mined here for techniques beyond what `darkei-hachassidus/sources-d-wider-chassidus.md` already has (Karlin's shouting, Berditchev's address).
- **Chabad hiskashrus to the Rebbe** (picturing the Rebbe, learning his maamar before prayer) is not catalogued; the Rayatz's sichos are the place to look.
- **Rashab's Kuntres HaTefillah and Kuntres HaAvodah** are uncorrected OCR and were not re-read here; they are the fullest Chabad manuals on prayer technique.
- **Zohar, Tikkunei Zohar, Pardes Rimmonim, Or Ne'erav, Ramchal (Derekh Hashem on prophecy), Maggid Meisharim** were not mined; the Ramak and Ramchal especially may hold more method.
- **The Besht's ascent of the soul** is attested (Mekor Mayim Chayim line 392) but its method is not described in these texts.
