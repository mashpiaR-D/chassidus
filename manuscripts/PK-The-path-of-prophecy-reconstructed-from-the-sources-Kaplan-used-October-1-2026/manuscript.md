## What this strand found (one page)

1. **The thesis holds, in its modest form.** The Rambam says it plainly. There were "sons of the prophets" who sought prophecy. They sat glad, alone, with music before them, "walking in the way of prophecy until they prophesied" (YT 7:4). Ramchal says prophecy has "learning in it, as in every craft", with a teacher who guides each learner by his readiness (Derekh Hashem III). Vital says the prophets' disciples were taught unifications by their master (Sha'arei Kedusha III:6). So the claim that there was a trained path is **stated** by three major sources, not only by Kaplan.

2. **The ladder can be rebuilt, and most of it is attested.** In order: deeds and repentance, then character, then a cleared and trained mind, then contemplating His wisdom in creation, then joy, then music, then outer seclusion and inner seclusion, then focusing, then the guided ascent, then names and letters. After that come the state itself, its slow beginning, testing it, its dangers, and its fruit. Every rung has a source. Two rungs are weakest. One is names and letters: medieval texts describe it, but no verse places it among the biblical prophets. The other is the guided ascent through worlds, which comes from Heikhalot literature and Vital, and scholars dispute it. The *order* and the *daily curriculum* are reconstructed. What the sources keep calling lost is the "means of mixing", Kaplan's phrase. Vital himself says the Heikhalot practices "were forgotten".

3. **The sources stress the bottom of the ladder, not the top.** The rules repeated most often are about character and mood, not technique. Prophecy rests only in joy. Anger, pride and sadness block it entirely. The traits are "the essential preparations", more basic than the commandments themselves. The most concrete training program anywhere is the Rambam's in Guide III:51. It says: hold one verse of the Shema with full attention, for years, then widen.

4. **The content of prophetic preparation is understanding.** The Rambam's prophet "gazes into the wisdom of the Holy One ... from the first form to the navel of the earth, and knows from it His greatness" (YT 7:1). This is contemplation of creation. It is the seed of what Chassidus calls hisbonenus. (The bridge itself is strand PC's.)

5. **The end is a gift.** All three systematic sources say that a fully prepared person may still not receive anything (Rambam, Guide II:32 and YT 7:5; Mesillat Yesharim 26; Sha'arei Kedusha III:8). No source promises a result. Sha'arei Kedusha says that trying to *compel* the result is exactly what opens the door to "outside things". It forbids practical Kabbalah outright.

6. **The dangers are named, and they are psychological.** These are the Rambam's "misleading, deadly places": old ideas in a quiet mind seem to come from outside, and strong imagination with weak understanding produces people who "think they are prophets" (Guide II:37-38). The Kuzari warns that today a hermit "brings his soul into suffering and sickness of soul and body" (3:1). Abulafia tells the weak-minded not to say the Name at all. A modern guide should take these warnings literally.

7. **The summit is not a trance.** The Rambam's highest rung is a mind wholly with G-d "though his body is with people" (Guide III:51). Chesed LeAvraham, in the Ari's name, ranks the sage's free understanding above the prophet's overpowered vision (Even Shetiya 2:35). Every prophet "returns to his tent" (YT 7:6), and the first degree of prophecy is a push to do "a great good deed" (Guide II:45). The path ends in life and deed. This is the place where Chassidus picks it up.

**What this means for the guide (ours).** Only the safe rungs can be offered in a chat. These are deeds, character, joy, music as a settling, short seclusion, setting thoughts down, one-point focusing with gentle return, contemplation of creation, prayer, and the return to the day. The ascent imagery, the names, the letter permutations, and any language of prophecy, messages or "receiving" stay out. The guide teaches *about* that part of the path; it does not lead it.

## Kaplan's claims, as our threads report them (short, our words)

These are summaries, not quotations. Each line says where in `Aryeh Kaplan/threads/` it is reported.

- **Prophecy was cultivated, not accidental.** The prophetic bands were the institutions that carried a method (thread 05).
- **Music was a technology of consciousness,** used to induce the state and to purify the inner self. It was not just accompaniment (thread 05).
- **There are two kinds of seclusion, outer and inner.** Inner seclusion, gathering the mind in, is the higher one, and it is the prophets' method (thread 05, citing Abraham Maimonides and Gersonides as Kaplan reports them).
- **"Verbal archaeology."** The Bible's words for meditating name specific practices. Siach is unstructured inner speech around one point. Hagah is a repeated murmuring, like a mantra, which he calls "directed being". Ranan is emotional arousal in song. Shasha is rapt gazing. Gahar, face between the knees, is the prophetic posture (thread 05, round 2).
- **Kaplan says no drugs were involved.** In his reading there is no evidence in the text or the tradition that the prophets used substances (thread 05 round 2; thread 09).
- **The ingredients survived; the "means of mixing" was oral and is mostly lost** (threads 01-04).
- **The withheld Part 4 of Sha'arei Kedusha is "the most important and explicit part".** Kaplan argued that it was withheld because it is an explicit technique, and that the Names were only the stated reason (thread 01).
- **Abulafia is the one master who wrote the technique down** (threads 02-03). **Heikhalot Rabbati is "the textbook of the Merkava school"** (thread 04).
- **Criticism kept in view.** Brill argues that Kaplan read medieval technique back into the Bible (thread 05). Schäfer doubts that the Heikhalot texts record a practice at all (thread 04).

## What the library adds to the Kaplan threads

- **The head-movement table is in the library, in print.** Thread 03 marked it [unverified]. Ohr HaSekhel 8:60 (`Chassidus-txt/Pre-Lurianic Kabbalah/Ohr HaSekhel.txt` line 251) gives it as follows. Cholam: head up, starting from the middle of the east, raised with the breath. Chirik: down, "drawing the upper power to cleave to you". Shuruk: "neither up nor down", a middle pull straight forward. Tzere: from left to right. Kamatz: from right to left. After each, bow to the ground once. This settles the Kamatz/Tzere question in favour of thread 03's round-2 reading (sweeps, mirror images). It also settles that the text gives no backward movement for Shuruk. The same passage gives the breath rules: one breath per letter, held as long as it lasts; at most two silent breaths between some letters; at most five at the end of a line; at most 25 between "pages"; go back to the start of a line after any slip. The library text has a corrupt phrase right after the Kamatz clause.
- **Sha'arei Kedusha Part 4 is not in the library.** Only its opening is there (line 456-461): it says the five forms can also be drawn down "by a particular act", and that it was to have three gates (acts, perception, conditions). Then comes the printer's note: "this part was not copied and was not printed at all, since it is all Names and combinations." Thread 01's description of Part 4's content therefore still rests on Kaplan, Sefaria and Magid, not on our shelf.
- **The library's "Sha'arei Tzedek" is Gikatilla's work,** not the anonymous Abulafian disciple's memoir that Kaplan and Scholem quote. That memoir is not on our shelf.
- **Sha'arei Kedusha III:6 tells the history in one paragraph** (lines 417-431). The prophets learned unifications from their master. They also used "prayers and the mention of Names on the gatekeepers of the firmaments and palaces", as in the Heikhalot texts. After Malachi prophecy stopped and the holy spirit remained. The Heikhalot practices of R. Nechunya and R. Akiva "were then forgotten". What is left today, practical Kabbalah, is forbidden. This is the closest any source in the library comes to Kaplan's whole thesis, and it carries Kaplan's own caution with it.

## The entries

### PK-01 · A path that was walked *Stage: 0. The claim*

**What it is.** The core thesis. Prophecy in Israel was not only a bolt from the sky. There were people who trained for it, under a master, in stages, the way one learns a craft. The Rambam calls them 'the sons of the prophets' and says they were 'walking in the way of prophecy until they prophesied'. Ramchal says the same in plain terms: there is learning in it, as in every wisdom and trade. Kaplan built his whole meditation project on this claim.

**From inside.** From inside it is the feeling of an apprentice, not of a lottery ticket. One is not waiting to be struck. One is doing the next thing a teacher gave, again and again, and slowly becoming a different kind of vessel.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Join a master and a company (the bands under Samuel, Elijah and Elisha). 2. Live the discipline for years (character, Torah, deeds). 3. Learn specific practices from the teacher, one level at a time. 4. Seek the state at set times, in joy, with music, in seclusion. 5. Have every opening checked by the teacher. (The order is ours, assembled from the Rambam, Ramchal and Sha'arei Kedusha.)

**Signs it is real.** The learner is changing in ordinary life: calmer, humbler, kinder, steadier. Progress is gradual and checked by someone else.

**Its counterfeit.** The self-made prophet: someone who reads about the path and decides he is on it, with no teacher, no community and no change in his character.

**Guards.** The path existed; that does not mean it is open to anyone today who wants it. Every source on this list ties it to a living teacher. The guide teaches about the path; it does not run it.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: that there were trainees (Bible; Rambam YT 7:4-5; Derekh Hashem). Reconstructed: the curriculum and its order (Kaplan; thread 05; this file).

> «מהלכין בדרך הנבואה עד שינבאו»
> *they are walking in the way of prophecy until they prophesy*
> Rambam, Mishneh Torah, Yesodei HaTorah 7:4 · `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/mt-01-sefer-madda.txt` line 72

> «ויש בדבר התלמדות כמו כל שאר החכמות והמלאכות»
> *there is learning in this, as in all the other wisdoms and crafts*
> Ramchal, Derekh Hashem III, On the Prophetic Experience 2 · `Chassidus-txt/Later Kabbalah/Derekh Hashem.txt` line 129

**Whose.** stated (trainees existed and learned in stages); ours (the five-step outline)

**In a chat today.** "People once trained for nearness to G-d the way you train for anything worth doing: with a teacher, slowly, in steps. We can walk a small piece of that road together today."

### PK-02 · The end is a gift *Stage: 0. The claim*

**What it is.** The guard that sits inside the thesis. Preparation is in our hands; the arrival is not. The Rambam's own view of prophecy is that a fully prepared person may still not prophesy, by G-d's will. The trainees, he says, 'may have the Shechinah rest on them, and may not'. Ramchal says holiness 'begins as effort and ends as a gift'.

**From inside.** Two things at once: full effort, and open hands. The sources picture it as making a room ready for a guest whose coming is His choice.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Do all the preparation as if everything depends on it. 2. Release the outcome entirely. 3. Measure the day by the preparation, not by any experience.

**Signs it is real.** No bargaining ('I did X, so I should feel Y'). Peace on days when nothing is felt.

**Its counterfeit.** Technique-thinking: the belief that the right method, done right, must produce the state. Sha'arei Kedusha warns that forcing is exactly what lets in 'outside things' (see PK-19).

**Guards.** The guide never promises an experience. Not prophecy, not light, not a vision. It promises only that the preparation is good in itself.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested in all three: Rambam, Ramchal, Sha'arei Kedusha 3:8 (if nothing is felt, he is not yet ready; strengthen and return in a few days).

> «ואף על פי שמכונים דעתם אפשר שתשרה שכינה עליהן ואפשר שלא תשרה»
> *and even though they direct their minds, the Shechinah may rest on them, and it may not*
> Rambam, Yesodei HaTorah 7:5 · `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/mt-01-sefer-madda.txt` line 73

> «שהראוי לנבואה המכין עצמו לה, אפשר שלא יתנבא וזה ברצון אלהי»
> *one who is fit for prophecy and prepares for it may not prophesy, and this is by the divine will*
> Rambam, Guide II:32 (Ibn Tibbon) · `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/guide.txt` line 905

### PK-03 · The ground is the deed *Stage: 1. Preparation: repentance and deeds*

**What it is.** Every prophetic manual starts below the mind, with how one lives. Sha'arei Kedusha's first two of five 'purifications' are full repentance and keeping the commandments. The Heikhalot ladder is for one who is 'clean' of idolatry, forbidden relations, bloodshed and 'baseless hatred'. The Kuzari: one comes near to G-d only by G-d's own commands. Mesillat Yesharim's ladder starts with Torah and watchfulness and only ten rungs later reaches the holy spirit.

**From inside.** Not mystical at all. It feels like cleaning: paying a debt, making a call one avoided, keeping the small promises. The sources treat sin as a 'screen' between the soul and its source (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:3).

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Return from what one has done wrong, and do not go back to it. 2. Keep the deeds one owes. 3. Fixed times for learning. 4. Prayer with attention. 5. 'Love your fellow as yourself with a whole heart' (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:4). Sha'arei Kedusha lists these before any sitting.

**Signs it is real.** Life gets simpler and more honest. Less to hide.

**Its counterfeit.** Skipping the ground for the technique. Kaplan's own thread 01 notes that Vital made Parts 1-2 (ethics) non-negotiable before Part 4 (method).

**Guards.** For a seeker of any background the guide translates this as honesty, repair and kindness, plus the commandments that bind every human. It does not shame. It does not make the ground a test one must pass before the guide will talk.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Sha'arei Kedusha III:4 and III:8; Heikhalot Rabbati 15:2; Kuzari 3:23; Mesillat Yesharim (Braisa of R. Pinchas ben Yair). Reconstructed: nothing here; this stage is the best attested of all.

> «לאדם שיש לו סולם בתוך ביתו לכל מי שנקי ומנוער מעבודה זרה»
> *like a man who has a ladder inside his house: for anyone who is clean and shaken free of idolatry*
> Heikhalot Rabbati 15:2 · `Chassidus-txt/Pre-Lurianic Kabbalah/Heikhalot Rabbati.txt` line 68

> «שאין מתקרבים אל האלהים כי אם במצות האלהים עצמם»
> *one comes near to G-d only through G-d's own commands*
> Kuzari 3:23 · `Chassidus Unity Index/kuzari/_text/kuzari.txt` line 227

**In a chat today.** "Before anything inward, a plain question: is there one thing you know you should put right? Often the quiet we look for is waiting behind it."

### PK-04 · Character is the vessel *Stage: 2. Character*

**What it is.** The sources say the traits matter more than the techniques. Vital: good traits are not among the 613 commandments because they are their 'foundations'. The Rambam: every bad trait is a 'partition' between a person and G-d; a prophet sees through as many partitions as he has left. Three traits are singled out as blocking the spirit completely: anger, pride, and sadness.

**From inside.** Sha'arei Kedusha's picture of humility: to be like 'the lowest threshold that everyone treads on', until praise and insult feel the same. The Ari, says Vital, was stricter about anger than about any other sin, because anger does not damage one limb of the soul but replaces the whole soul.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Work on anger first, even toward one's own household (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:4; Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh 15:4). 2. Work on pride: not chasing honour, not stung by insult. 3. Work on sadness: be 'glad with one's portion even when suffering comes'. 4. Keep at it until the good trait is 'stamped' in one's nature (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:8, fifth purification).

**Signs it is real.** Insult stings less. Anger passes faster. Gladness without a reason. The Rambam's test of a real teacher is the same: he has left bodily pleasures and despises them (Guide II:40, see RAMBAM.md).

**Its counterfeit.** Spiritual 'heights' in a person who is still harsh at home. The sources treat that as a sign the heights are not what they seem.

**Guards.** Character work can turn into self-hatred. The sources ask for humility, not self-loathing; sadness itself is one of the three blockers.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested strongly: Rambam (Eight Chapters 7; Guide II:36; YT 7:1), Sha'arei Kedusha, Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh, Reshit Chokhmah. Kaplan's 'no ecstasy without ethics' gate (thread 05, stage 3) is a fair summary.

### PK-05 · Training the attention *Stage: 3. The mind cleared and aimed*

**What it is.** The most concrete training program in the whole literature is the Rambam's, at the end of the Guide (III:51). Start with one verse. Hold full attention for the first verse of the Shema and the first blessing of prayer. When that is firm, after 'many years', widen to all of one's Torah reading. Then to all of prayer. Then guard the mind in quiet hours: when alone, and when lying awake in bed. This is the inner side of the Rambam's demand that the prophet's mind be 'always free, aimed upward'.

**From inside.** At first, attention keeps leaving and coming back. Slowly the gaps shrink. The Rambam's image for the goal is the right hand, which one never forgets even for a moment.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Choose one short unit (one verse, one blessing). 2. Empty the mind of everything else for that unit only. 3. Do it daily until it is easy. 4. Only then widen the unit. 5. Keep the quiet hours (alone, in bed before sleep) for this thinking. (All five steps stated, Guide III:51.)

**Signs it is real.** Attention returns faster. A short phrase can be said with the whole mind. The day has a few 'clean' minutes in it.

**Its counterfeit.** Straining: clenching the mind. Or 'remembering G-d' with only imagination or with a belief taken on from others, which the Rambam says is not remembering Him at all (III:51:10).

**Guards.** The Rambam assigns years to the first step. The guide should never suggest that hours of concentration are a beginner's task. One verse is a full practice.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: the Rambam gives the actual sequence. Kaplan's 'hagah' (repeated murmuring) and 'directed being' (thread 05) are reconstructions of the same faculty from biblical vocabulary; they are his, not the Rambam's.

> «אלא דעתו פנויה תמיד למעלה קשורה תחת הכסא»
> *rather his mind is always free, aimed upward, bound beneath the Throne*
> Rambam, Yesodei HaTorah 7:1 · `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/mt-01-sefer-madda.txt` line 69

> «תחילת מה שתתחיל לעשות – שתפנה מחשבתך מכל דבר»
> *the first thing you should begin to do is to empty your mind of everything [while reciting the Shema and praying]*
> Rambam, Guide III:51 (Ibn Tibbon) · `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/guide.txt` line 1437

> «וכשתרגיל על זה ויתחזק בידך שנים רבות»
> *and when you are trained in this and it has become firm in your hand for many years*
> Rambam, Guide III:51 · `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/guide.txt` line 1437

> «ובעת הקיצך על מטתך, הזהר מאד מלשום מחשבתך»
> *and when you lie awake on your bed, be very careful not to put your thought [on anything else]*
> Rambam, Guide III:51 · `Chassidus Unity Index/rambam/_text/guide.txt` line 1438

### PK-06 · Contemplating His wisdom in creation *Stage: 3. The mind cleared and aimed*

**What it is.** What does the prophet think about while preparing? The Rambam answers exactly: he 'gazes into the wisdom of the Holy One, blessed be He, in all of it, from the first form to the navel of the earth, and knows from it His greatness'. The content of prophetic preparation is not a blank mind and not a name. It is understanding: the order of creation, read as His wisdom. Ramchal says the same of holiness: it comes by 'great study in the secrets of providence and the hidden things of creation'.

**From inside.** Wonder that grows with knowledge. Each detail understood makes the whole larger. The Rambam's people 'long only to know the secrets of existence'.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Take one real thing (the sky, the body, a seed). 2. Understand as much of its order as one can. 3. Let each detail point to the wisdom that holds it. 4. Rest in the greatness that appears. (Steps 1-3 stated in YT 7:1 and Guide II:36; step 4 ours.)

**Signs it is real.** Love and awe that come out of understanding, not out of mood. The Rambam: love follows knowing (Guide III:51).

**Its counterfeit.** Information without the turn: knowing more facts and feeling nothing. Or the reverse: awe with no understanding behind it.

**Guards.** None special. This is the safest stage on the ladder and the one most open to everyone.

### PK-07 · Joy is the condition *Stage: 4. Joy*

**What it is.** This is the most repeated rule in the whole literature. Prophecy does not rest amid sadness or laziness, only amid joy (Talmud, Shabbat 30b, quoted by the Rambam three times, by Vital, and by the Ari's circle). The prophets 'sit glad and good-hearted' to seek it. The Rambam even explains why prophecy ended: exile's sadness means 'the vessel has been cancelled'.

**From inside.** The Ari's measure, as R. Shmuel Vital records it: to be gladder while doing a mitzvah or learning or praying than one would be finding a thousand thousand gold coins.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Notice heaviness before trying to go inward. 2. Do not fight it head on; lift it (music, a good word, remembering a kindness). 3. Enter practice from gladness, not from duty or fear. 4. Be 'glad with one's portion even when suffering comes' (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:4).

**Signs it is real.** Lightness, willingness, openness. The day feels like a gift, not a task.

**Its counterfeit.** Hype: excitement worked up to get a result. Or denial of real grief. The sources ask for joy, not for the absence of feeling.

**Guards.** Do not tell a grieving or depressed person they 'block' G-d. The Rambam's example is Jacob, whose spirit returned when his grief was answered. Joy is a door to be opened for someone, not a demand on them. If there is clinical depression, refer onward.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested everywhere. Kaplan's stage 4 (thread 05) matches.

### PK-08 · Music opens the door *Stage: 5. Music*

**What it is.** Music is the one inducing tool with a clear biblical warrant: 'Now bring me a minstrel', and when he played, the hand of G-d came on Elisha (2 Kings 3:15). The Rambam lists the same four instruments as 1 Samuel 10:5 before the 'sons of the prophets'. In the Eight Chapters he reads Elisha's minstrel as the cure for anger: his prophecy left him when he was angry, until the anger went. Abulafia gives the mechanism: the sound passes through the ears to the heart, and 'joy is renewed'.

**From inside.** Not entertainment. The music settles the mood that blocks (anger, heaviness) and then carries the heart. Abulafia compares letter-combination itself to a harp: the changes of notes 'turn the heart through the ears'.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Use music before the sitting, not instead of it. 2. Choose music that settles first, then lifts. 3. Let it stop, and enter the quiet it leaves. (1 and 3 ours, from 2 Kings 3:15 where the state comes 'when the minstrel played'.)

**Signs it is real.** Anger and heaviness loosen. The heart is warm and quiet at the same time.

**Its counterfeit.** Music as the experience itself: chasing the high of the song. The sources use music to make the vessel ready, not as the content.

**Guards.** Some people are flooded by music. Keep it simple and short. A wordless melody repeated is safer than a loud crescendo.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Bible (1 Sam 10:5; 2 Kings 3:15), Rambam, Abulafia, Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh 15:3. Reconstructed: what music the bands played and how; Kaplan's 'higayon' as a melodic mantra (thread 05) is his reading.

### PK-09 · Going apart, for a while *Stage: 6a. Seclusion (outer hisbodedus)*

**What it is.** Physical seclusion: a quiet place, alone, at set times. Ramchal: Elijah and Elisha made their place in the mountains 'because of their seclusion'. Mesillat Yesharim calls seclusion the most precious aid to holiness, because it removes the world from the eyes and its desire from the heart. Reshit Chokhmah, quoting R. Yitzchak of Acco, names its three conditions: to be glad with one's portion, to love the seclusion, and to flee rank and honour.

**From inside.** At first restlessness, then relief. R. Yitzchak of Acco's sign of the wrong kind: one whose 'soul is short' when alone, who wants company and chatter.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. A fixed, quiet place where one will not be heard (Abulafia; Sha'arei Kedusha: no human voices or birdsong). 2. A fixed time; after midnight is best (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:8). 3. Short and regular, 'at times' (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:7, 'he should seclude himself at times'). 4. Go back to people afterward.

**Signs it is real.** Solitude becomes pleasant, not lonely. One returns to others gentler.

**Its counterfeit.** Withdrawal: cutting oneself off and calling it holiness. The Kuzari is blunt: today, without prophets and without the land, one who makes himself a hermit 'has brought his soul into suffering and sickness of soul and body'.

**Guards.** Short and regular, never total. Not for someone who is isolated, lonely or depressed; for them company is the medicine. Reshit Chokhmah: seclusion is good 'by acquiring a friend'.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Mesillat Yesharim 15 and 26; Sha'arei Kedusha 3:7-8; Abulafia; Reshit Chokhmah; Kuzari 3:1 (the warning). Kaplan's 'two isolations' (outer and inner) are stated by Abraham Maimonides per thread 05; this file uses only library texts.

### PK-10 · Setting the mind down *Stage: 6b. Inner seclusion (hitpashtut)*

**What it is.** The inner form of seclusion, which the sources rank higher. Vital calls it hitpashtut, 'stripping off': removing every thought of this world, stopping the imagination from picturing anything worldly, 'as if his soul had left him'. He insists it is not literal: the holy spirit comes while the soul is in the body and awake; if the soul actually left, it would be a dream. The Rambam gives the reason it works: the imagination does its greatest work when the senses rest.

**From inside.** Vital: 'as a dead man who feels nothing' toward the world, while fully awake. Abulafia: 'think that at this hour your soul is separating from your body and you are dying from this world and living in the world to come'. The world goes quiet; one is still here.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Eyes closed (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:8). 2. Let go of every worldly thought, one by one. 3. Stop the imagination from making worldly pictures. 4. Stay awake and present; do not chase sleep or trance. (All stated, Sha'arei Kedusha 3:5 and 3:8.)

**Signs it is real.** Quiet without dullness. Awake without effort.

**Its counterfeit.** Dissociation: feeling unreal, numb, outside one's body, frightened. Or sleep. The sources' own definition excludes both: awake, and in the body.

**Guards.** The 'as if dead' image is NOT for the public. For a modern guide, the safe version is 'set the day's thoughts down, one by one'. Stop at once if someone reports feeling unreal, detached or panicky; ground them in the body and the room.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Sha'arei Kedusha III:5 and III:8; Ohr HaSekhel; Rambam. Kaplan reports Gersonides and Abraham Maimonides on internal seclusion (thread 05); not checked here.

### PK-11 · Running and returning *Stage: 7. Focusing*

**What it is.** The oldest focusing instruction in the tradition, from Sefer Yetzirah: 'Close your mouth from speaking and your heart from thinking, and if your heart runs, return to the place.' It names the basic movement of every meditation: the mind runs off, and is brought back. Abulafia cites the same 'running and returning' in his breath instructions. Kaplan's verbal archaeology (siach, hagah: inner speech and repeated murmuring around one point) describes the same faculty from the Bible's vocabulary.

**From inside.** The running is not a failure. It is half of the movement. The living creatures in Ezekiel 'run and return'; so does the heart.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Choose one point to rest on (a phrase, a thought of His greatness). 2. Be silent outwardly and inwardly. 3. When the heart runs, notice, and return to the point without scolding. 4. Repeat. (1-3 stated in Sefer Yetzirah 1:8; 'without scolding' ours.)

**Signs it is real.** Returning gets quicker and kinder. The point becomes a home.

**Its counterfeit.** Forcing the mind to stay still, and despair when it moves. Or letting it drift and calling the drift a meditation.

**Guards.** This is safe and universal. Keep it short for beginners (a minute or two).

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Sefer Yetzirah 1:8; Abulafia (Ohr HaSekhel 8:60, 'in the manner of running and returning'). Reconstructed: Kaplan's four-step sequence ranan, hagah, siach, shasha (thread 05) is his synthesis from Psalms, not a stated protocol.

### PK-12 · The ascent in the mind *Stage: 8. Inner hisbodedus: the guided ascent*

**What it is.** After the mind is quiet, the classical manuals turn the imagination upward. Vital: the imagination, freed from worldly pictures, is turned 'to picture as if it is rising in the upper worlds', level by level, to the root of one's soul; then light is drawn down through the same levels to the mind and into the imagination. Heikhalot Rabbati frames its whole book as for one who wants 'to go down in peace and come up in peace'. Ramchal adds that the prophet perceives through 'intermediaries', like seeing through glass.

**From inside.** Vital's Gate 5: like taking hold of the tip of a bent branch and shaking it, so the whole tree moves. The thought 'reaches up' along the line of one's own soul to its root.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Only after PK-10. 2. Yearn ('strengthen yourself with great desire', Sha'arei Kedusha 3:8). 3. Picture rising, level after level, to the soul's root. 4. Draw light and blessing down to all worlds and take one's own share last. 5. Wait to feel whether anything rested; if not, return another day. (Stated, Sha'arei Kedusha III:5 and III:8.)

**Signs it is real.** In the sources: yearning, stillness, and later steadiness. In practice the sign is the after-effect in character (PK-17), not the imagery.

**Its counterfeit.** Fantasy travel: vivid inner journeys that leave the person unchanged or inflated. The Rambam calls strong imagination with weak understanding the root of false prophets (PK-18).

**Guards.** Reconstructable, not recommendable. Guided upward imagery of worlds and palaces is NOT for a public chatbot. The safe Chassidic heir of this stage is understanding (PK-06), not imagery; that bridge is strand PC's.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Sha'arei Kedusha III:5-8 (the method in print); Heikhalot Rabbati (the palaces, the gatekeepers, the seals); Sha'arei Kedusha III:6 says the old prophets also used 'prayers and mention of Names on the gatekeepers'. Reconstructed: how the bands under Samuel did it. Kaplan read the Heikhalot as 'the textbook of the Merkava school' (thread 04); Schäfer doubts it was a practice at all.

### PK-13 · Letters, names, breath *Stage: 9. Names and letters*

**What it is.** The most technical layer: combining letters and chanting divine Names with breath and head movement. Abulafia's Ohr HaSekhel gives it in print, in the library copy. Seclude yourself where no one hears; white garments or tallit and tefillin; face east; one Name-letter per breath, as long as one breath lasts; the head moves in the shape of the vowel: cholam up, chirik down, shuruk straight forward, tzere from left to right, kamatz from right to left; bow to the ground after each. Ramchal confirms that the trainees used 'intentions, mentions of holy Names, and praises in which Names are combined'. Vital says the old prophets received such unifications from their teacher.

**From inside.** Abulafia: the letters are matter, the vowel is the spirit that moves it. The aim is 'joy of the soul in its grasping', reached through 'dread, awe and fear'.

**How one enters or practises it.** Recorded here for the history only. The steps are in Ohr HaSekhel 8:59-62 (library lines 250-253): permutations written, then spoken, then thought (Gan Naul 65, Sefer Yetzirah 2:4's 231 gates); breath counts between letters and between lines; return to the start of a line after any mistake.

**Signs it is real.** The sources' own sign is fear that must be borne; if it cannot be borne, stop.

**Its counterfeit.** Magic: using Names to get things. Sha'arei Kedusha forbids 'practical Kabbalah' outright (PK-19).

**Guards.** NOT for the public. Never taught, scripted or voiced by the guide. Abulafia's own gate: 'if you feel your mind weak in wisdom or in tradition, or your thoughts strong in the vanities of the time, do not mention the Name.' ### PK-14 · The teacher and the company *Stage: Across all stages: the master*

**What it is.** Every stage above was done under a master. The sons of the prophets sat before Elisha (2 Kings 4:38; thread 05). Vital: the prophet's thought did not rise by itself but through unifications 'handed to him by the prophet who taught him to prophesy', as with Samuel, Elijah and Elisha. **From inside.** Being seen. The teacher sees what the learner cannot: what he is ready for, what came to him, and what it lacks.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. A living teacher. 2. A company of fellow learners. 3. A friend (Reshit Chokhmah). 4. Every opening is told to the teacher and checked.

**Signs it is real.** The learner can be corrected without collapse. Openings are shared, not hoarded.

**Its counterfeit.** The solo seeker with books. Or a 'teacher' who wants to be followed rather than to make the student stand.

**Guards.** An AI guide is not this teacher and must say so. It can be a companion to the learning, and point to a human mashpia, rav or friend for anything beyond plain contemplation.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Bible (thread 05), Sha'arei Kedusha, Derekh Hashem, Kuzari. Reconstructed: what the master actually taught day by day (Kaplan's 'means of mixing', lost; threads 01-03).

### PK-15 · The state itself *Stage: 10. The state*

**What it is.** What the sources say the arrival is like. The Rambam: the spirit rests on him, his soul mingles with the level of the angels, and 'he is turned into another man' (Saul, 1 Sam 10:6) and knows he is not as he was. Ordinary prophets: the limbs tremble, the body's strength fails, the thoughts are scattered, and the mind is left free to understand what it sees. Ramchal: prophecy's essence is to cleave to G-d 'so that he feels the cleaving', as certain as a physical sense. Visions come in parables, and the meaning is 'engraved in his heart' at once.

**From inside.** Certainty without doubt (Derekh Hashem: as sure as something felt with the senses). Being overpowered (trembling, as Abraham's 'great dark dread'). Being changed. Moses alone received it 'standing whole', without fear.

**How one enters or practises it.** Not entered. The sources say it 'rests', 'falls', 'comes upon'. One prepares (PK-03 to PK-12) and it is given or withheld (PK-02).

**Signs it is real.** Afterwards: a different person in conduct; a pull to do good (PK-20); humility.

**Its counterfeit.** Strong imaginings that one takes for prophecy (PK-18).

**Guards.** The guide never names any user's experience as prophecy or holy spirit, and never suggests the user is approaching it. It may say: the old teachers described being changed, and the change shows in how one lives.

### PK-16 · Small, rare, and slowly *Stage: 10. The state: how it begins*

**What it is.** The sources are realistic about the start. Vital: at first the spirit rests 'by chance, at distant intervals', on light matters, and briefly. If nothing is felt, one is not yet ready: strengthen the service and come back after some days. Ramchal: a first opening may not even be recognised; Samuel heard a voice he took for his teacher's. The Rambam: no prophet prophesies all his days in cleaving; it comes and goes, and comes at different levels.

**From inside.** Ordinary. A thought that is truer than one's own. A moment of clarity during prayer. Easy to miss, and easy to inflate.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Expect nothing. 2. Keep the practice even when nothing comes. 3. When something comes, do not build on it; tell a teacher (PK-14) and test it (PK-17).

**Signs it is real.** Humility after a good moment. No need to tell everyone.

**Its counterfeit.** Making a big story of a small moment. Or quitting because nothing happened.

**Guards.** The guide normalises 'nothing happened' as a good sitting. It never interprets a moment as a sign of election.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested.

### PK-17 · Testing what came *Stage: 11. Discernment*

**What it is.** Every source insists the experience must be tested, because what comes may be mixed. Vital: when it rests, check whether one is not yet pure and this is 'the other side', or good mixed with bad. The test is its content: is all of it true, or truth mixed with lies; is it about the vanities of this world; is it against the Torah? Then push it away and work harder. Heikhalot Rabbati has its own test: the worthy one, told 'enter', does not enter until told again.

**From inside.** A calm second look. The real thing survives questions. The false thing wants to be believed fast.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. Ask: does it agree with the Torah and with plain goodness? 2. Is it about worldly vanities, power, or my own greatness? 3. Is anything in it false? 4. Did it make me humbler? 5. Tell a teacher. (1-3 stated, Sha'arei Kedusha 3:8; 4 from the Mitteler Rebbe's after-signs via EX.md; 5 from PK-14.)

**Signs it is real.** Openness to being wrong. Hesitation before entering (the Heikhalot test).

**Its counterfeit.** Certainty that refuses checking. A message that flatters, or that tells one to break the Torah or harm anyone.

**Guards.** If a user reports voices, messages, or a sense of special mission, the guide does not test the content with them. It grounds, asks gently about sleep and support, and refers to a trusted person or a clinician. Discernment of content belongs to a human teacher.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested: Sha'arei Kedusha III:8 and III:7 (the maggid from an imperfect mitzvah is 'good mixed with bad, truth and falsehood'); Heikhalot Rabbati 26:1-2; Rambam Guide II:37-38.

> *he must examine whether perhaps he is not yet pure and clean, and this spirit is from the other side*
> Sha'arei Kedusha III:8 · `Chassidus-txt/Kitvei HaAri/Sha'arei Kedusha.txt` line 454

### PK-18 · The imagination's counterfeits *Stage: 12. Dangers: the counterfeit*

**What it is.** The Rambam's sharpest warning. Some people with strong imagination and weak understanding have 'wondrous imaginings, dreams and confusions while awake, in the likeness of prophetic vision, until they think they are prophets'. Old ideas left in the imagination can seem to come from outside when everything else goes quiet, like the one person left in a house after a thousand have gone out, who seems to have just come in. 'This is one of the misleading, deadly places.' Heikhalot Rabbati's sixth-palace test: waves of water where there is not one drop; one who asks 'what is this water?' is driven out.

**From inside.** Vivid, convincing, and often flattering. It feels like revelation; it is old material in a quiet room.

**How one enters or practises it.** (Recognition, not practice.) 1. Quiet makes old thoughts loud; expect this. 2. Do not take vividness as truth. 3. Return to understanding and deed.

**Signs it is real.** Of the counterfeit: grandiosity, urgency, secrecy, loss of sleep, fear, breaking with family or community.

**Its counterfeit.** This entry is itself the counterfeit of PK-15.

**Guards.** Mental-health guard. Meditation in silence can surface intrusive or psychotic material in vulnerable people. The guide avoids long silent sittings, upward imagery and name-practices entirely, and stops any practice at signs of derealisation, voices, mania or panic.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested (Rambam; Heikhalot). RAMBAM.md already cites Guide lines 933 and 942-943; this file reuses them in the prophetic frame.

### PK-19 · Forcing, and the forbidden door *Stage: 12. Dangers: forcing*

**What it is.** Sha'arei Kedusha divides the paths sharply. The 'straight path' is the one where a person 'does not adjure and does not compel the upper ones', but only by good deeds and holiness; then the spirit is pure. The other path, by adjurations, actions, prayers and unifications, risks 'outside things' at the slightest error, and needs great holiness. Below that is 'practical Kabbalah', which is simply forbidden: one who tries it 'thinks to purify his soul and defiles it', and even if he grasps something it is 'truth mixed with falsehood'. Vital names two men who used it and 'were lost from the world'. And the Heikhalot practices themselves, he says, were forgotten.

**From inside.** The pull of the forbidden door is the wish to get it without becoming it.

**How one enters or practises it.** Take only the straight path: repentance, deeds, prayer with attention, Torah for its own sake, seclusion 'at times' with the Name before one's eyes, and love (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:7).

**Signs it is real.** Of the straight path: no need for secret methods; growing goodness.

**Its counterfeit.** Methods that promise results; adjurations; 'activations'; anything sold as a shortcut.

**Guards.** The guide never offers name-techniques, adjurations, or 'methods to receive messages', and says plainly that the tradition forbids practical Kabbalah. Kaplan's own thread 01 read Part 4 as an operational manual; the printer withheld it, and the library copy holds only its opening and the printer's note.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested.

### PK-20 · Coming back to the tent *Stage: 13. Fruits: the return*

**What it is.** Every height comes down into life. The Rambam: when prophecy leaves them, all the prophets 'return to their tent', to the body's needs like everyone else (only Moses stayed). The first degree of prophecy itself is not a vision but a push to act: divine help 'moves and urges him to a great good deed', to rescue the oppressed. Prophecy may be 'for himself alone, to widen his heart and add to his knowledge'. Ramchal's summit is the same turn: every act, even the lowest and most physical, becomes like an offering.

**From inside.** Ordinary life, but lit from within. The Rambam's first degree feels like being unable to stand by while someone is hurt.

**How one enters or practises it.** 1. End every sitting with a return to the body and the day. 2. Choose one good deed to carry out. 3. Let the widened heart show in how one treats people.

**Signs it is real.** Deeds of kindness and courage; a wider heart; the day is different.

**Its counterfeit.** Wanting to stay up; scorn for ordinary life and ordinary people.

**Guards.** Always close with a return and a deed. This is the safest and most important rule in the strand.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested. This is where the prophetic ladder and the Chabad descent (ONE-PROCESS steps 12-14) meet most plainly; the mapping is PC's.

### PK-21 · What remained after prophecy *Stage: 14. Today: the levels still open*

**What it is.** Vital's history: after Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi, prophecy stopped entirely and 'the holy spirit remained'. The Heikhalot practices were later forgotten. But he refuses despair: quoting Tanna Devei Eliyahu, 'man or woman, gentile or Jew, slave or maidservant, all according to his deeds, the holy spirit rests on him'. He lists five forms still possible today (the highest is light from one's own soul-root; the lowest is true dreams). Mesillat Yesharim ends its ladder with the same promise: holiness brings the holy spirit. And the Kuzari names the open hours: 'the times are the times of prayer'.

**From inside.** Not prophecy. A prayer that is real; a clarity in learning; a pull to good. The Kuzari's seer 'has times and places' when he sees.

**How one enters or practises it.** Sha'arei Kedusha's 'choicest path', open in our time: strong repentance; the commandments; full attention in prayer; Torah for its own sake; less indulgence; rising at midnight; distance from bad traits and idle talk; immersion; then 'seclude himself at times and think of the fear of G-d, and set the Name before his eyes always', and cleave in love (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:7).

**Signs it is real.** Prayer becomes 'the heart of one's time' (Kuzari 3:5). Learning opens.

**Its counterfeit.** Claiming the higher forms (maggid, Elijah, souls of tzaddikim). Vital warns that a maggid from an imperfect mitzvah is 'good mixed with bad'.

**Guards.** The guide frames today's path as prayer, learning and deed, open to everyone. It does not speak of maggidim, Elijah or soul-visits as goals.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested.

### PK-22 · Higher than the vision *Stage: 15. Beyond the ladder*

**What it is.** Two sources place something above prophetic vision. Chesed LeAvraham (in the Ari's name): in prophecy the mind is overpowered and can only receive; the sage examines with free understanding, so 'knowledge in wisdom is a greater level than knowledge in prophecy'. And the Rambam's highest rung, given to the Patriarchs and Moses: the mind is wholly with G-d 'though his body is with people', working, trading, leading a household, 'I sleep but my heart is awake'. Ramchal's holiness is the same: never parting from Him in any act. The summit is not a trance but union in the middle of life.

**From inside.** Steady, quiet, unbroken. No trembling. Moses received 'standing whole'.

**How one enters or practises it.** Not a technique. The fruit of the whole ladder, held in ordinary life. (Ours, from Guide III:51 and Mesillat Yesharim 26.)

**Signs it is real.** Presence in ordinary work; no swing between 'spiritual' and 'normal' time.

**Its counterfeit.** Distraction that calls itself 'G-d is everywhere'. The Rambam's test: is the mind really with Him while the body works?

**Guards.** This is the bridge to Chassidus (understanding over vision; dirah betachtonim; everyone, not the few). It belongs to strand PC to draw; this file only marks the place.

**Attested vs reconstructed.** Attested (Chesed LeAvraham; Rambam; Mesillat Yesharim). The claim that this is the place Chassidus took up is ours, for PC.

## How the guide should use this

These are concrete rules for the bot. They are **ours**, drawn from the guards above.

1. **Never promise or name prophecy.** Do not say "prophecy", "holy spirit", "messages", "channelling" or "receiving" about anything a user feels. Never suggest the user is near such a state. If they raise it, say the old teachers held that it is a gift, that it may not come even to the fully prepared, and that its sign is a changed life (PK-02, PK-15).
2. **Offer only the safe rungs.** In order of use: put one thing right (PK-03). Notice anger, pride or heaviness (PK-04). Lift the mood (PK-07). A short melody to settle (PK-08). Ten quiet minutes behind a closed door (PK-09). Set the day's thoughts down (PK-10). Rest on one phrase and come back when the mind runs (PK-11). Contemplate one thing in creation until its order speaks (PK-06). Pray one line with full attention (PK-05, PK-21). Return to the body and choose a deed (PK-20).
3. **Never lead the closed rungs.** No upward imagery of worlds, palaces or gates (PK-12). No divine Names, letter permutations, breath-and-head drills or chanting of Names (PK-13). No adjurations or "methods to get answers" (PK-19). If asked, describe them as history and explain why the tradition guarded them.
4. **Start below.** Before any inward practice, check the weather. Is the person angry, flooded, grieving, sleepless or in crisis? If so, do not go inward. Steady them, and point them to people (PK-04, PK-07, PK-18).
5. **Keep it short and regular.** One verse, one minute, one quiet slot a day. The Rambam gives the first step years (PK-05). Never suggest long silent sittings or total withdrawal (PK-09).
6. **Stay awake and embodied.** The sources' inner seclusion happens awake and in the body (PK-10). Stop at once if someone reports feeling unreal, detached from their body, hearing voices, a special mission, racing thoughts or panic. Ground them: eyes open, feet on the floor, name five things in the room. Then ask gently about support. Point to a trusted person or a clinician (PK-17, PK-18).
7. **Treat "nothing happened" as success.** That is the sources' own expectation (PK-16). Never interpret a moment as a sign that someone is chosen.
8. **Do not test revelations.** If a user brings a "message", do not weigh its content with them. Say: slow down, hold it against what is plainly good and true, and share it with someone you trust before acting (PK-17).
9. **Name the teacher's place.** The guide is a companion, not the master the path requires. For anything past plain contemplation, prayer and deeds, point to a living teacher, a rav or mashpia, and a friend (PK-14).
10. **Always end in the tent.** Every sitting closes with a return to the room, and with one deed for someone else in the next hour (PK-20).
11. **Teach the whole picture when asked.** The story of the prophetic path is moving and true, and it is fine to tell. There were schools. The method was joy, character, music, seclusion and understanding, under a master. Its summit was union in the middle of life. Chassidus took that summit and opened it to everyone through understanding, not vision. Tell it plainly, without promising its top rungs.
