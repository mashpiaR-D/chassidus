**What it is.** A collection of R. Hillel of Paritch's explanations of maamarim, arranged by the parsha cycle from Vayikra to Behaalosecha. Much of it is his notes of talks by the Mitteler Rebbe and the Tzemach Tzedek, with his own commentary added. Its aim is to lay out the Kabbalistic structure of the festivals and mitzvos (Pesach, the Omer, Shavuos, Yom Kippur) and to trace each one into avodah, the inner work of prayer, teshuvah and bittul. Scattered through it are passages that stop and diagnose a person's inner state and prescribe a remedy. One whole treatise (440-483) does this systematically.

**How much of it is medicine.** some. Most of the text is Kabbalistic cosmology of the festivals and sefirot. A few dense passages (440-483, 396-399, 682-684, 1194-1198) are real diagnosis and prescription for the soul.

**Its model of the person.** A person has ten powers that mirror the sefirot. The mind is cool, like "light that shines for itself". The heart is hot, like "light that shines for another", and only the heart moves the limbs. Above mind and heart lie simple will and simple delight, hidden in the soul's essence. Deepest is the yechidah, the essence-point, which is always pure and outside free choice. A divine soul and an animal soul share the body. The core of the animal soul is only the power of desire, dressed in bad garments. Change works by layers: damage at one level is healed by waking the level above it (bina gives birth to new emotions, simple will repairs the mind, the essence repairs the will). Prayer purifies and Torah rebuilds the vessels.

**Its philosophy.** The powers of the soul correspond to the sefirot above, so arousing a level below arouses the same level above. Reward and punishment run through the ordered will clothed in wisdom. Above that is a simple will, a "will of all wills", where darkness and light are equal and forgiveness can flow. People reach it only by arousing their own simple will and essence through self-surrender. The divine essence is untouched by a person's failures, so nothing stands before teshuvah.

**What it adds.** It offers graded triage. It matches the depth of the damage (heart, mind, simple will, essence) to the deeper faculty that must be woken: contemplation, then surrender beyond reason (the 'lot'), then the essence-point. It warns that bitterness cannot be sustained. It diagnoses 'lovesickness' as loss of taste and pairs purging in prayer with rebuilding by Torah, like medicine and then food. The divine soul 'explains' itself to the animal soul with compassion for both. It also advises making one's pain known to others, and teaching calmly.

## I know what I should do, but I feel nothing and can't get myself to do it

The mind agrees that learning, praying and doing good are right, but there is no desire in the heart, so nothing moves. The person avoids outright wrongdoing, yet is sluggish and indifferent toward the good.

**What is really going on.** The mind is intact, since he still keeps the boundaries, but it stays aloof and has not clothed itself in the heart. Neglecting positive action has left the divine soul's emotions unlit, so a general knowledge no longer pulls the heart after it.

**Program: Long contemplation to give birth to new feeling, while acting now.** (1) Recognize the state: the mind knows, but the heart does not move, because the mind has not entered the heart. (2) Do not expect to flip the heart end to end in one hour; the text calls that hard. (3) Contemplate at length the exaltation of the Infinite, beyond the source of worlds, until all the life of the worlds counts as nothing before Him. (4) Let the mind clear the blindness of imagination: what looks like life (worldly existence) is passing, and true life is the divine. (5) Meanwhile do the good deeds with alacrity, even with no vitality in them yet (dig the spoiled well deeper). (6) Feeling will be born by itself: bina is the 'mother' that gives birth to emotions. *When:* A 'long hour' of effortful contemplation, implicitly around prayer; repeated until the emotions are reborn. *For:* Someone whose mind is clear and whose conduct holds, but whose heart is dull and slow to do good. *Not for:* Someone whose mind itself is foggy or flooded with intrusive thoughts (P02); the text says such a person cannot hold a long contemplation. `PR·Q01` · `PR·Q02` · `PR·Q03` · `PR·Q05` · `PR·Q06`

**Reframe.** From *"If I don't feel it, doing it would be hollow; I'll wait until I'm moved."* to **Act with alacrity now, even with no vitality in it yet, and meanwhile go deeper; feeling is born afterwards.** Bina 'gives birth' to emotions, and a spoiled well is fixed by digging deeper, not by waiting. Deep contemplation plus action lets the heart follow the mind by itself. `PR·Q05` · `PR·Q06` · `PR·Q03`

## My mind is foggy and invaded; when I think of God it's cold, like someone else's business

He cannot hold a deep thought for long, because intrusive thoughts crowd in and trouble him. Even when he pushes them away, the truth does not stick. It stays a cold thought about something that does not concern him.

**What is really going on.** The mind has lost its rule over the heart (in the text, through crossing prohibitions). Contemplation (bina) alone can begin the repair but cannot finish it. A level above intellect has to be woken.

**Program: The 'lot': surrender beyond reason (accepting the yoke).** (1) Begin with contemplation, knowing it will open the repair but not complete it. (2) Arouse the simple will above reason and emotion, as when casting a lot, where both outcomes are equal to you and you bind yourself to whatever comes. (3) Accept the yoke of Heaven before the yoke of mitzvos: set aside your whole self, even your own will, before the Source of will. (4) Hold this as self-sacrifice in potential; it wakes the will above, from which forgiveness flows. *When:* Yom Kippur is its day, but the text says every person has in daily service something like Yom Kippur: accepting the yoke. *For:* Someone whose mind has lost command and cannot sustain deep thought, but whose basic will to belong is intact. *Not for:* Someone whose simple will is itself broken (P03), for whom the text says this is not enough; and people whose sense of self is already eroded. `PR·Q07` · `PR·Q10` · `PR·Q11`

**Reframe.** From *"If I have no feeling or understanding, my prayer and learning are worthless."* to **When mind and heart are dull, the letters themselves (words of Torah, prayer and praise said humbly) are what forge a new will.** The soul is 'full of letters'. The lowest faculty, the letters, reaches the essence, as a crown is made of stone, so speech can work where intellect and feeling cannot. `PR·Q15` · `PR·Q16`

## I've gone so far there's nothing good left in me and no way back

*Its own term:* פריקת עול / פגם ברצון הפשוט

He has thrown off the yoke entirely, and his mind and heart feel damaged beyond use. There is no love or awe in prayer and no understanding in Torah.

**What is really going on.** Even the simple will is damaged. Yet the essence of the soul is pure and beyond choice, and nothing stands before teshuvah; it only needs a deeper arousal from the essence-point.

**Program: Return from the far edge: search, essence, bitterness, polishing, letters.** (1) Search every power of the soul for one that was never used in wrongdoing (R. Elazar b. Durdaya); holding one edge of the unity holds all of it. (2) If none is found, conclude 'it depends only on me': the essence-point that is always pure. (3) Let pain at the distance, which you cannot bear, reach the essence. (4) Taste the bitterness of the wrong, knowing it cannot hold steady and a fall can follow. (5) Keep polishing afterwards in humility until the heart softens into a vessel for steady Torah and service. (6) Meanwhile hold to the letters of Torah, prayer and praise, said in lowliness like the worm whose strength is in its mouth; they form a new will even without feeling. *When:* 'Tamid': both continuous and at fixed times, in Torah and prayer; the polishing is long. *For:* Someone who feels he has thrown off everything and that his mind and heart are spent. *Not for:* Anyone depressed or prone to despair: the bitterness step is dangerous, and the text itself warns it cannot be sustained and may lead to a fall. `PR·Q10` · `PR·Q11` · `PR·Q12` · `PR·Q13` · `PR·Q14` · `PR·Q15` · `PR·Q16`

**Reframe.** From *"I've ruined myself completely; there is nothing left to return with."* to **The essence of the soul is pure, damage cannot touch it, and it lies beyond your choice. Nothing stands before teshuvah; it only needs a deeper place.** Choice governs only the soul's expressions. Finding even one untouched point grasps the whole, so the search for what is unblemished replaces the inventory of damage. `PR·Q12` · `PR·Q14` · `PR·Q11`

## My prayer and good deeds are rote and cold; nothing holy has any taste

*Its own term:* חולת אהבה / מצות אנשים מלומדה בקרירות

He goes through the motions coldly and from the outside, with no joy in a mitzvah. Like a sick man who tastes bitter as sweet, he finds his pleasure only in himself.

**What is really going on.** A foreign fire of worldly cravings has grown strong and consumed the divine fire. Delight in the divine is felt only through thirst, and the thirst has gone out. Beneath the coldness a concealed delight remains.

**Program: From coldness to thirst: contemplating one's distance, then Torah as nourishment.** (1) Contemplate the divine as the source of all delight; even the highest Garden of Eden is only its ray. (2) See your own distance: no taste, no joy, rote performance. (3) Let the heart grow bitter from this; it purifies the foreign fire and awakens thirst, and delight follows the thirst. (4) Know that your own bitterness does not finish the purging; the main removal comes from above, so ask ('heal me'). (5) In prayer, teshuvah purifies. All day, Torah rebuilds the vessels and makes one soft as a reed, like medicine first and then good food. *When:* Purification at prayer time; rebuilding through Torah 'all day'. *For:* Someone functioning outwardly but cold and tasteless inside. *Not for:* Someone already sunk in self-reproach or depression, for whom 'becoming bitter' deepens the illness. `PR·Q20` · `PR·Q21` · `PR·Q22` · `PR·Q23` · `PR·Q24`

**Reframe.** From *"My coldness proves I don't really care."* to **Doing it anyway, even flatly and stubbornly, is a will above reason, and a hidden delight lies inside it.** Every will has an inner delight at its source. Coldness means the essence is hidden, not absent, so the task is uncovering, not creating from nothing. `PR·Q31` · `PR·Q21`

**Reframe.** From *"I have to scour myself clean with my own remorse."* to **Your own bitterness does not remove the foreign fire completely; the main removal comes from above. Prayer purifies and Torah then rebuilds, like medicine and then food.** It relieves the self-punishing effort, sends the person to ask for help, and adds a constructive phase (rebuilding the vessels) after the purging. `PR·Q23` · `PR·Q24`

## I can't even feel bad about how far I've fallen

He feels numb toward his own distance, with no bitterness and no longing. Only a bare point of identity remains.

**What is really going on.** Some failings (the text names neglect of Torah and baseless hatred) weaken the powers of the divine spark itself, so it no longer has the strength to be stirred by distance.

**Program: Cry out, and make your pain known to others.** (1) Admit that you do not even feel bitterness at the absence. (2) Cry out over 'He is not here', the absence itself. (3) Make your pain known to others, so that many ask for mercy on you (like Rachel 'making others weep'). (4) The outcry gathers the scattered powers of the soul into peace, undoing baseless hatred. *When:* Not specified; tied to the absence felt now, after the Temple's destruction. *For:* Someone numb, whose own spark no longer stirs. *Not for:* Someone who would expose himself to shame or to unsafe listeners; choose trusted people. `PR·Q17` · `PR·Q18` · `PR·Q19`

**Reframe.** From *"What I love and want shows who I really am."* to **What you cannot bear the opposite of reaches your essence. Pain at distance goes deeper than love.** Desire is an outward spread of the soul and can hold opposites. Unbearable absence touches the essence and wakes it. `PR·Q13` · `PR·Q19`

## My desires feel like an enemy living inside me

He has two opposed wills, one pulling up and one pulling down, and he experiences his own drives as darkness.

**What is really going on.** The inner core of the animal soul is not evil. It is only the power of desire, dressed in soiled garments, and its root is even higher than the divine soul's.

**Program: Raising the animal soul: explain, pity both, unite the wills, refine each trait.** (1) Let the divine soul 'explain' to the animal soul that its root is very high; it fell far, like a stone thrown from a height. (2) Out of that, great compassion arises for both souls, the one fallen and the one clothed in a body. (3) A great outcry comes from a broken heart, and a strong longing is born to rise to the root. (4) The two opposed wills become one simple will toward the Father in Heaven. (5) Then refine each of the seven emotional traits one by one (the 49 days of the Omer). *When:* Framed on the Omer period (Pesach to Shavuos); the detailed refinement takes 49 days. *For:* Someone at war with his own drives. *Not for:* Not for an unsteady person asked to 'break' himself; the broken heart here grows out of compassion, not self-attack. `PR·Q25` · `PR·Q26` · `PR·Q27`

**Reframe.** From *"My desires are evil; my animal side is my enemy."* to **The core of the animal soul is only the power of desire, which can itself turn toward God; its root is higher than the divine soul's.** Compassion takes the place of war. Pity for both souls unites two opposed wills into one, and that unity is what lifts the drive. `PR·Q25` · `PR·Q26` · `PR·Q27`

## After spiritual highs I crash into pride, anger or cravings

*Its own term:* רצוא בלי שוב / יניקת החיצונים

A burst of fiery enthusiasm in prayer is followed by arrogance, cravings, or anger at those who oppose the holy.

**What is really going on.** The fervor never settled into bittul and humility (shuv). Emotion that is felt as self-feeling throws off waste, such as zeal turning to anger.

**Program: Settling fervor into bittul.** (1) After fiery enthusiasm, come to 'shuv': lowliness of essence, until the flame itself subsides. (2) The sign that it has settled: you rejoice with the poor and share insight with those poor in understanding. (3) Prefer a contemplation of His transcendence ('all is as nothing before Him'), which stays around the heart and throws off no waste such as zealous anger. *When:* Immediately after moments of spiritual excitement. *For:* Someone whose spiritual highs turn into pride, anger or cravings. *Not for:* Someone with little feeling at all (P01, P04), who needs kindling, not damping. `PR·Q28` · `PR·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"My anger at those against the holy is righteous passion."* to **Anger that flows from spiritual excitement is waste, an overflow of felt emotion.** Seeing the zeal as a by-product shows the person that his fervor lacks settling (bittul), and directs him to a deeper, quieter contemplation. `PR·Q28` · `PR·Q29`

## When I try to guide someone, my fervor overwhelms them

He speaks with fire and in a loud voice, and the listener cannot take it in.

**What is really going on.** Real influence has to be measured to the listener's vessel and delivered calmly. Fervor is suited only to what is not meant to be taken in inwardly.

**Program: Guiding another calmly and to measure.** (1) Restrain your own fire and loudness when teaching or giving moral guidance. (2) Give only as much as the receiver's vessel holds, no more and no less. (3) Speak calmly, since the words of the wise are heard when spoken calmly. *When:* Whenever teaching or guiding. *For:* Mentors, teachers, anyone trying to help another. *Not for:* No limit given by the text. `PR·Q30`

## Cautions

- The bitterness and 'sufferings' step (G03, G04) harms a depressed person; the text itself says bitterness cannot hold steady and may lead to a fall. - Do not tell someone their numbness or fog is punishment for particular sins; the text ties dullness of mind and heart to specific transgressions. - The text reduces heresy to casting off the yoke; do not apply that to a modern person's sincere doubt. - 'Make your pain known to many' should mean trusted people, never public exposure or shame. - Nullifying one's will ('the lot', the yoke) is dangerous for someone in a coercive relationship or with an already eroded self. - Long contemplation should not be forced on someone flooded by intrusive thoughts; the text itself says such a person cannot sustain it. - Claims about soul-roots (e.g. trust depending on how 'high' a soul is, line 1076) must never be used to rank a person. - The text addresses Jews and Jewish ritual (Yom Kippur, Omer); carrying it to anyone needs care, not literal transfer.
