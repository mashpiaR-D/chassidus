## What this file is

The map (`consciousness-map/MAP.md`) says where a person can be. The ladder (`ONE-PROCESS.md`) says how the rungs follow one another. This file says how a person **gets in**: the practical doors the sources name, each with its own key. **(ours)** A door is a concrete act, at a concrete time, that the sources say opens the experience of "nothing but Him, and He is fully here".

Three things hold across all the doors **(ours, from the sources below)**:

1. **Every door has two halves: in and back out.** The Shema is the run up; the Amidah begins the return; then Torah, then the deed (Torah Ohr, DR-05). A door that only goes up is not yet a door.
2. **The mind opens most doors; the deed and the yoke open the deepest.** Contemplation (DR-01) feeds prayer, learning, joy and nature. But the Tanya puts the mitzvah's union above even a tzaddik's love (DR-07), and kabbalas ol and mesirus nefesh need no feeling at all (DR-08, DR-09).
3. **The heavy doors come with timers.** Bitterness and tears only at set times, in private, then joy (DR-11). The sources themselves set these limits; the guide keeps them stricter still.

## DR-01 · The set hour of contemplation

**Who it suits.** Anyone who can sit still and think for some minutes; the main road of Chabad. Ours: a beginner starts with a few minutes. Stated: the sources ask for about an hour, its length differing by soul.

**When.** Before morning prayer above all; also a fixed hour on another part of the day to train the mind.

**What it is.** Hisbonenus (התבוננות): taking one true thing about Him (for example, that He makes everything new each moment) and thinking it through slowly, in detail, until it is felt, not only known. It is the door the other doors lean on.

**From inside.** The Rashab: when the light shines inside the mind, the heart follows by itself; the heart 'hears' and 'feels' the Godly matter the mind grasped. The Rebbe (Hayom Yom) names three rungs of a 'ladder of feeling': in study one feels the idea; before prayer one feels the life in it; in prayer one feels the G-dliness in it. Sometimes, he adds, one feels G-dliness with no work at all, as a gift from the essence of the soul.

**The steps.** Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah, five preparations): (1) clear every worry from the mind; do not rush in from outside; stand in one place, do not pace. (2) After putting on tallis and tefillin, think a matter of Chassidus for about half an hour or more, before prayer, sometimes again before Yotzer. (3) Lower the self by an honest reckoning (only at set times; see DR-11). (4) If that did not open the heart, wake great mercy on your own soul, mainly at the blessing Ahavas Olam. (5) Before the Amidah, a short but bound contemplation: I stand before the King. The contemplation itself: about an hour or more, widening the matter into many details, binding the mind to the thing itself, until it 'settles' in the mind. Tanya ch. 42: 'a long, unbroken hour', its length differing by soul. To make this easy at prayer, set aside an hour or two at another time of day to think deeply in Chassidus (Rashab). Ours: for the guide, the same shape in miniature: one line, a few details, staying, then a sentence to carry.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the heart moves 'by itself', without being pushed; it lasts; it reaches the deed and changes the natural traits (Kuntres HaTefillah). Ours: afterward the person is a little steadier in an ordinary moment.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated: 'general' contemplation, the matter only in summary, is 'no contemplation at all'; any feeling from it is 'a false imagination' that vanishes. Pacing while thinking gives only an outer 'breadth of spirit'. Inventing new clever readings of the prayer words gives a short lift that lasts hours, not a change. Feeling only the 'conclusion' (G-d is good and wonderful) after the reasoning has gone is a small, outer arousal.

**Guards.** Ours: not for someone in acute distress, panic or spacey states (send to grounding first; MAP S20, S21). Do not demand a feeling (Tanya ch. 42: the hour differs per soul). For a person not Jewish, offer the sitting without tallis, tefillin or Jewish prayer. Stated: the young and unlearned should not build on their own inventions.

**Whose.** Steps, length and counterfeits: stated (Rashab, Tanya, Hayom Yom). The miniature for chat and the guards for non-Jews and distress: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Pick one true line and give it ten unhurried minutes: not more ideas, more detail on the same one. Then see what's left when you stand up."

## DR-02 · Drawing out the word One

**Who it suits.** Anyone who says the Shema; the single most concentrated door in the day. For someone not Jewish, the idea (He is one in all directions) without the liturgy.

**When.** Twice daily at the Shema; the heart of morning prayer.

**What it is.** At 'Hashem Echad' (G-d is One) one lingers: the ches (numerical eight) for the seven heavens and the earth, the dalet (four) for the four directions, all held as one. Then 'Baruch Shem' (Blessed be the name of the glory of His kingdom), said quietly, is the 'lower unity': He who is above place and time is also here, filling place and time.

**From inside.** The Tanya: the Shema verse is the 'higher unity' (all is nothing before Him); Baruch Shem is the 'lower unity' (He fills the heavens, the earth and the four sides 'equally, truly'). The Rashab: the seven heavens, earth and four directions are 'nullified to the aleph', the Infinite; from the large dalet, the last level, comes the 'name' that brings worlds into being; and through this one comes to 'You shall love', wanting only Him.

**The steps.** Stated (Shulchan Aruch HaRav 61): do not swallow the ches; draw it out a little, to crown Him in heaven and earth. The dalet is not prolonged in sound; rather, during the dalet and after it, before 'Baruch Shem', draw it out in thought, crowning Him in the four directions. Stated (Rashab): the whole of Pesukei deZimra and the Shema's blessings are arranged to widen the praise so that by the Shema it is fixed in the heart; contemplate daily 'and draw out the Echad', until it settles, and one comes to 'seeing'. Tanya ch. 25: read the Shema with the readiness to give oneself over for His unity, and remember it all day.

**Signs it is real.** Stated (Rashab): the faith moves from hidden to revealed, 'as if seeing'. Ours: the word 'One' stops being a number and becomes a place one stands.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: a long, theatrical dalet with an empty mind (the Alter Rebbe warns against over-stressing the sound itself). Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah): a summary thought here, felt briefly, is 'surrounding' only.

**Guards.** Ours: never teach liturgy as obligation to someone not Jewish; offer 'He is one above, below and on every side' as a thought. Not for a person who is frightened of 'dissolving' (MAP S20); keep the lower unity in front: He is here, in this room, in this body.

**Whose.** The halachah of the ches and dalet, the higher and lower unity, the Rashab's reading: stated. 'A place one stands' and the guards: ours.

**In a chat today.** "When you say 'One', stay on it a breath longer. Up, down, and the four directions around you: all of it, one. Then quietly: and He's right here too."

## DR-03 · Prayer's climb: the ladder

**Who it suits.** Someone who prays the morning service; for others, the shape (thanks for the world, then the ones who bow, then the One) can be walked in their own words.

**What it is.** The order of prayer is a ladder 'set on the earth whose top reaches heaven' (Zohar, cited by the Alter Rebbe). Each section has its contemplation: Pesukei deZimra on His greatness in making the world; the Yotzer blessings on the angels who say 'Holy' and 'Blessed' and are wholly nullified; the Shema on His oneness 'from the side of the worlds'; Baruch Shem on all worlds coming from the mere 'name' of His kingship.

**From inside.** Torah Ohr: through prayer the soul rises 'level after level' by the cry of the heart, to be included in the Infinite. The Shema is the running up (ratzo).

**The steps.** Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah 10): in Pesukei deZimra contemplate His greatness in bringing the world into being; also the praise and nullification of the outer worlds; in the Yotzer blessings, the song of the angels; in the Shema, how He is one in the seven heavens, earth and four directions; at Baruch Shem, how all worlds come from a glimmer of a glimmer. In each, contemplate at length, in detail, binding the mind, until the heart wakes in love and longing 'to want nothing else'. Stated (Rashab 5651): the sages arranged all of Pesukei deZimra from Baruch She'amar to the Shema to widen the praise so that it is fixed in the heart.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the heart wakes 'by itself' into love and longing (ratzo), and 'he will want no foreign thing'. Ours: the rung one stands on at the end is higher than the one at the start.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah): praying 'by habit like everyone', or with a short general thought, is 'no service at all', and makes even the learning of Chassidus go to waste.

**Guards.** Ours: the ascent is not the goal; DR-04 and DR-05 must follow. Never press a person not Jewish into Jewish prayer.

**Whose.** The ladder and the contemplation for each section: stated. 'The rung at the end is higher': ours.

**In a chat today.** "Start with thanks for one thing that exists this morning. Then think of everything that bows. Then let it all come down to one word."

## DR-04 · Standing before Him: the Amidah

**Who it suits.** Anyone who prays the Amidah; in its essence, anyone who can stand and speak to Him as 'You'.

**When.** The silent standing prayer, after the Shema; also any moment one turns to say 'You'.

**What it is.** The Amidah is not more climbing. One stands before the King and speaks to Him face to face, 'Blessed are You'. Its work is bittul (self-giving, being nothing of one's own) before Him. The Alter Rebbe: here the return downward (shov) begins.

**From inside.** Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah): great awe falls, and he is in an inner bittul; this is the main thing of the Amidah. Torah Ohr: in the eighteen blessings the revealed light comes down, 'from Blessed to You', face to face. Tanya ch. 42: the bows of the Amidah are the yoke taken on again 'in deed', after it was taken on in speech in the Shema.

**The steps.** Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah, fifth preparation): before the Amidah, a short contemplation, but bound: I stand before the King of kings and speak to Him directly: 'Blessed are You', 'You are mighty', 'You are holy', 'You grace', 'Return us, our Father'. Think how all the highest lights darken before Him, and how the Essence is here in every place, and before Him I stand.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: inner bittul, not excitement; awe. Ours: words become simple and few; the 'I' who was achieving something goes quiet.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: carrying the excitement of the Shema into the Amidah as performance. The sources set the Shema's work (running) and the Amidah's (bittul) apart.

**Guards.** Ours: for a person with a harsh image of G-d, stress 'Our Father' and nearness, not fear. Not when the person is flooded with shame (MAP S11); start them at DR-11's mercy instead.

**Whose.** The preparation, inner bittul and 'the return begins here': stated. Guards: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Now stop thinking about Him and talk to Him: 'You'. Short and plain. You're standing in front of Someone."

## DR-05 · Coming down: from prayer into the day

**Who it suits.** Everyone; above all the person who had a good sitting and wants to keep it.

**When.** The end of prayer and the hours after it: learning, work, eating, people.

**What it is.** The climb is for the sake of coming down. Torah Ohr gives the order: the Shema is the run up; the Amidah begins the return; after the Amidah the light is drawn into Torah study; then 'even more' into the deed of the mitzvos. 'Against your will you live', in a body, so the light can be below.

**From inside.** Stated (Torah Ohr): the return through Torah and mitzvos is like the heavens around the earth; the run up is a mustard seed beside it. Ours: the day becomes the place where what was seen in prayer is held.

**The steps.** Stated (Torah Ohr 37-38): after the Amidah, learn (the light flows into Torah); then do mitzvos (the light enters a vessel). Tanya ch. 41: begin the fixed study right after prayer with the same readiness of giving oneself over. Hayom Yom: every knowing, even the deepest, must come into avodah, the refining of traits. Ours: carry one sentence from the sitting into the first task. Gap: we did not find a source in the library on Aleinu specifically as the descent; we do not claim one.

**Signs it is real.** Stated (ONE-PROCESS section 3): the test lives down here: no sting from an insult, love and awe added afterward. Ours: the first conversation after prayer is gentler.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated (Mitteler Rebbe, cited in ONE-PROCESS): arousal that never reaches deed is 'a flame flying in the air'.

**Guards.** Ours: do not make the person feel the day is a fall; the sources call the return the main thing.

**Whose.** The order (Shema, Amidah, Torah, mitzvos): stated. The 'one sentence' practice: ours. The Aleinu question: open.

**In a chat today.** "Before the day takes you, pick the first thing you'll do and do it as if He asked for it. That's where the quiet goes next."

## DR-06 · Learning Chassidus with the heart

**Who it suits.** A person who likes ideas; the person who says 'I understand it but don't feel it' (MAP S16).

**When.** A fixed time of study, best right before prayer; and in any free hour.

**What it is.** Learning Chassidus is not information. The Tanya: when the mind grasps a teaching of Torah, the mind holds it and is held by it, 'a wondrous union with nothing like it'. The Rebbe Rashab adds the warning: learning that is not carried into prayer and felt there is lost, and can even swell the ego.

**From inside.** Stated: the Mitteler Rebbe: 'the soul hears words of Chassidus', and that hearing drips down into the life that animates the body. The Frierdiker Rebbe: Chassidus 'stands a chassid face to face with the Essence'.

**The steps.** Stated (Hayom Yom 415): three contemplations: (a) in learning, after understanding the matter well, think into its depth until the idea shines; (b) before prayer, feel the life of what you learned; (c) in prayer, feel the G-dliness in it. Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah): learn to understand, not to invent; be a 'vessel to receive'; then bring the matter into prayer at length. Ours: learn one short passage, then ask: what is this saying about Him, here?

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the learning becomes 'established in the soul' and changes the traits; a desire to learn more. Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah 12): the sign of a non-vessel is no wish to learn deeply, only to hear.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated: learning only the 'high' topics (above Atzilus, before the tzimtzum) for the thrill of depth; distorting them by making them physical; learning much and then acting the opposite the moment one stops.

**Guards.** Stated: without prayer, learning Chassidus 'can make him the very opposite'. Ours: not a door for someone who uses ideas to stay out of their own life; pair with DR-07.

**Whose.** The union in learning, the three contemplations, the warnings: stated. The one-question practice: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Read just one paragraph. Then close it and ask one thing: what is this telling me about Him, right here?"

## DR-07 · The deed as union with His will

**Who it suits.** Doers; the person whose mind wanders but whose hands can act; people who feel nothing (the deed does not depend on feeling).

**When.** Any mitzvah; before tefillin, tzitzis, Torah study; for a person not Jewish, any good act done because He wants it (ours).

**What it is.** Tanya ch. 35-37: even a tzaddik's love is still 'something in itself', but the mitzvah is His inner will with no concealment at all. In doing it, the body's very strength is included in His light, 'one in a complete union'. This is how He has a home below.

**From inside.** Stated (Tanya ch. 35): the Shechinah rests only on what is nullified to Him; the mitzvah is the oil that lets the light hold the wick. Ch. 46: when a person takes in how He set aside His great light for us, 'as water reflects a face', the soul flames to leave everything and cleave to Him, 'to be included in His light'.

**The steps.** Stated (Tanya ch. 41): before the mitzvah or study, contemplate His greatness and that He stands over you, as long as your mind and time allow; then think how the Infinite Light, His will, is clothed in this Torah, these tzitzis, these tefillin, and by putting them on you draw His light onto the Godly spark in you, 'to be included and nullified in His light'. Ch. 46: let the love that He showed us wake love back. Ours: one breath before the act: 'He wants this, now.'

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the act is done for His sake 'only to give Him pleasure' (ch. 41). Ours: the act feels plain and whole, not dramatic.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated (ch. 41): doing it from love alone, to cleave, without the yoke, is not yet 'the service of a servant'. Ours: doing it to feel spiritual.

**Guards.** Ours: never present Jewish commandments as obligations to a person not Jewish; offer the universal frame (a good act because He wants it). Not as a guilt list.

**Whose.** The union through the deed and the steps before it: stated. 'He wants this, now' and the universal frame: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Before you do it, one breath: He wants this, now. Then do it plainly. The doing is the meeting, whether or not you feel anything."

## DR-08 · Accepting the yoke each morning

**Who it suits.** Everyone, especially the person who 'feels nothing', or is tired of chasing feelings. The Tzemach Tzedek: it changes the person's very being.

**When.** First thing in the day; before Torah and mitzvos; at the Shema and at the bows of the Amidah.

**What it is.** Kabbalas ol: taking Him on as King simply, before and beneath understanding. Tanya ch. 41 calls it the beginning, essence and root of the service. It does not depend on feeling: 'this acceptance is true beyond any doubt', because it is the nature of every Jewish soul not to rebel.

**From inside.** Stated (Hayom Yom): through the yoke of a simple servant, 'even while he sleeps the yoke is noticeable'. Ours: a floor under the feet; relief at not having to produce a state.

**The steps.** Stated (Tanya ch. 41): think, at least briefly, of the greatness of the Infinite and His kingship over all worlds; that He sets aside upper and lower and makes His kingship one over you in particular; and you accept Him as King, to serve Him as a servant. 'He stands over him', sees, and searches the heart. Lengthen this as your mind and time allow. Even if no awe is felt, the acceptance in the mind and will is real.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the learning and mitzvos done from it are 'a complete service'. Ours: steadiness that does not swing with mood.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: grim self-coercion or fear of punishment dressed as kabbalas ol; or saying the words with no turning of the will.

**Guards.** Ours: for someone harmed by authority or religious coercion, start with being held (being-held/) and DR-07's love before the language of King. For a person not Jewish, frame it as handing the day to Him.

**Whose.** Stated (Tanya, Tzemach Tzedek). The guards and the non-Jewish frame: ours.

**In a chat today.** "You don't have to feel anything to start. Just say it plainly: today is Yours. That's real even when it's quiet."

## DR-09 · Giving oneself over: the essential bond

**Who it suits.** Anyone; it is the deepest and also the simplest door. Tanya: even 'the lightest of the light' can give his soul for G-d's holiness.

**When.** At the Shema (twice daily); before blessings, prayer and study; and in trials, when holding on costs something.

**What it is.** Mesirus nefesh: the readiness not to be cut off from His oneness at any price, which sits in every Jewish soul 'with no reason or grasped understanding, by a Godly nature'. The Rebbe (Hayom Yom): in trials the yechidah, the soul's single point, is revealed, and then even the animal soul's own core turns toward G-dliness.

**From inside.** Stated (Tanya ch. 41): the soul says 'You breathed it into me... from now I hand it over and return it to You, to unite it in Your oneness'. Torah, mitzvos and prayer are themselves this giving-over: thought joined to the letters of His word. Hayom Yom: 'great pleasure in serving G-d', and the world's yes and no 'are equal to him'.

**The steps.** Stated (Tanya ch. 25): read the Shema accepting His kingship with mesirus nefesh, and remember it all day. Ch. 41: with this readiness begin the morning blessings 'Baruch Atah', the fixed study after prayer, and study in the middle of the day; especially in saying 'You' to Him directly. Ours: the question that opens it: 'What in me would never let go of Him, no matter what?'

**Signs it is real.** Stated (Hayom Yom): firmness in keeping Torah and mitzvos with inner life; joy; indifference to worldly yes and no. Ours: calm under pressure.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: dramatic talk of martyrdom; self-harm or risk dressed as devotion (refuse at once); grandiosity.

**Guards.** Ours: never invite literal danger; danger overrides everything (MAP S21). Do not use with a person who is suicidal or self-erasing (MAP S04, S11); with them speak of being held and wanted.

**Whose.** Stated (Tanya, Hayom Yom). The opening question and the guards: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Under all the noise, there's a place in you that would never let go of Him. You don't have to make it. Just notice it's there."

## DR-10 · Joy from the unity itself

**Who it suits.** The person who is low but not in crisis; anyone who needs light before prayer or work.

**When.** Tanya ch. 33: 'at set times, when he sees his soul needs to be cleansed and lit with gladness of heart'; also right after a time of contrition (DR-11).

**What it is.** Joy here is not mood-lifting. It is what happens when a person thinks deeply about the true unity: He fills all worlds and this very earth, He alone, as before creation. The joy is because this is 'closeness to G-d itself', and the purpose: a home for Him here.

**From inside.** Stated (Tanya ch. 33): the heart rejoices and the soul exults 'with gladness and song, with all heart, soul and might'; like a simple person whose king has come to live in his house, and infinitely more.

**The steps.** Stated (Tanya ch. 33): deepen the thought and picture in the mind the true unity: He fills all worlds above and below, the whole earth is His glory, all is as nothing before Him, He alone is here as He was before the six days, 'even now, without any change'; like the sun's light in the sun. 'When he deepens this much' the heart rejoices.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the joy carries into love of every Jew (ch. 32) and readiness in service. Ours: lightness that does not need the circumstances to change.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated (ch. 31): joy that is really the body's satisfaction. Ours: forced cheer that covers grief.

**Guards.** Ours: not as a cure for depression or grief (MAP S04, S14); meet the pain first. Never 'you should be happy'.

**Whose.** Stated (Tanya ch. 33). Guards: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Try this slowly: He's not far off. He fills this room, this street, the ground under you, the way He did before anything existed. Let that be good news."

## DR-11 · Tears and the broken heart

**Who it suits.** The person with a real reckoning to make, or whose heart is already heavy for heavenly reasons; not for the fragile.

**When.** Stated: only at times, at a fitting hour; generally at Tikkun Chatzos (midnight lament), in private; or when one is sad anyway over worldly things (then turn the sadness to its proper place). Never during prayer and Torah study themselves.

**What it is.** Bitterness over one's distance, a broken heart, sometimes tears. It is a door because it empties the vessel: 'I dwell with the crushed'. The Mitteler Rebbe: contemplating 'Hashem is One' and how far one's soul is, the heart turns bitter in teshuvah and weeping, to a sudden cry. It is always followed by joy; the Zohar's 'weeping lodged in my heart on this side, joy lodged in my heart on that side'.

**From inside.** Stated (Rayatz): at a farbrengen a niggun can bring 'not a weeping of pain but a weeping of the soul's longing'. Stated (Tanya ch. 34): weeping and joy lodged together in one heart (MAP S49).

**The steps.** Stated (Kuntres HaTefillah, third and fourth preparations): make an honest reckoning of thought, speech and deed that were not for G-d, until the heart breaks truly; if that does not move it, wake great mercy on your soul: how it came down from a high roof to a deep pit; mainly at Ahavas Olam. Then gladden the soul with Tanya ch. 31 and 33 and learn Chassidus, so as to pray with joy. Tanya ch. 31: one may stay an hour or two in lowliness; even if it turns into great sadness, do not worry, then turn to joy.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the heart becomes a vessel; prayer after it is moved (Hayom Yom: through the heaviness before prayer the traits are moved in prayer). Ours: relief, softness toward others.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated (Tanya ch. 26, 31): sadness is from kelipah; worry over sins belongs only at confession, not at prayer or study. Ours: wallowing, self-hatred, the verdict 'I'm my mistakes' (MAP S11).

**Guards.** Stated: only at set times, not daily for most people, and in private. Ours: never with depression, grief, trauma, or self-harm risk; never induce tears; always end in joy and mercy.

**Whose.** Times, steps and the turn to joy: stated. The safety exclusions: ours.

**In a chat today.** "It's alright that this hurts. The ache is part of you reaching for Him. Let it be, a little, and then let Him hold it."

## DR-12 · The higher return

**Who it suits.** Anyone after a lower teshuvah (regret and change); also the person with no great sins who still feels far.

**When.** In prayer, above all the Shema and its blessings; in Torah study with love and awe; on Shabbos (Tanya: 'Shabbos is the higher teshuvah').

**What it is.** Teshuvah is not only fixing wrongs. The higher return is the soul going back to where it was before it was breathed out: united with Him 'in the utmost unity', like breath still inside the one who breathes. The Maggid (Hayom Yom): return 'until Havayah', the One above the worlds, becomes 'your G-d', i.e. nature itself.

**From inside.** Stated (Iggeres HaTeshuvah): the soul rises to its source and cleaves to Him 'in a wondrous union'; 'spirit cleaving to spirit'.

**The steps.** Stated (Iggeres HaTeshuvah): first the lower teshuvah: humility, contrition, mercy (Tikkun Chatzos, at least once a week before Shabbos); then the higher one, in prayer with intention, especially the Shema, saying 'with all your heart' truly, so that G-d's word is truly in one's mouth; and in Torah with awe and love. Joy and the humility together. Hayom Yom gives five paths of teshuvah by the letters of the word: wholeheartedness; 'I set Hashem before me always' (the world's being given each moment); love of one's fellow; 'know Him in all your ways' (seeing providence); walking humbly.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: joy and humility together, with trust that He forgives at once (Iggeres HaTeshuvah). Ours: a sense of coming home rather than of passing a test.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: endless guilt loops presented as teshuvah; or a 'spiritual high' that skips the lower return.

**Guards.** Ours: never imply someone's suffering is punishment. For a person not Jewish, 'return' as coming back to Him, without Jewish legal frames.

**Whose.** Stated (Tanya, Hayom Yom). 'Coming home': ours.

**In a chat today.** "Coming back isn't only fixing what went wrong. It's going back to where you were before you were ever apart from Him. That place is still there."

## DR-13 · Seeing His hand in today

**Who it suits.** Busy people, business people: the Mitteler Rebbe said those in business can have an advantage in 'seeing G-dliness sensorially'. Anyone who lives in events more than in ideas.

**When.** Through the day; at evening, looking back.

**What it is.** Hashgacha pratis (personal providence): every movement of every creature is His providence, and that providence is its very life and being. More: each small movement bears on the whole purpose of creation.

**From inside.** Stated (Hayom Yom): the person who sets heart and mind on all that happens to him and around him 'sees G-dliness sensorially'.

**The steps.** Stated (Hayom Yom): 'accustom yourself to see' providence, how He renews the world and the creatures every moment. Set heart and mind on what happens to you and around you. Ours: at night name three small things in the day that were arranged, and ask what each was for.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: it brings teshuvah (Hayom Yom names it one of the five paths). Ours: less resentment, more curiosity; 'why did this come to me?' becomes 'what is this for?'.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: reading signs and omens, superstition, 'the universe is telling me'; using providence to justify someone's suffering or to blame them.

**Guards.** Ours: never explain a person's pain or loss as providence's 'reason'; with grief, stay with the grief. Not a fortune-telling tool.

**Whose.** Stated (Hayom Yom). The evening practice and guards: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Tonight, think back to three small things today that lined up just so. Don't explain them. Just notice Someone was arranging."

## DR-14 · Lifting the eyes to creation

**Who it suits.** Nature-lovers; people who feel G-d most outdoors; people not Jewish (it needs no practice).

**When.** Any time one sees the sky, the earth, a living thing.

**What it is.** 'Lift your eyes on high and see who created these' (Isaiah 40:26). The Alter Rebbe: as I know my soul is in my body because I see the body alive, so I 'see' Him in the worlds, which are like a great body. And this knowing is felt 'as if seeing'; hence it is called da'as, recognition and feeling, and not only faith. Tanya ch. 42: all one sees, heaven and earth, are the King's outer garments.

**From inside.** Stated (Hayom Yom, the Alter Rebbe): Chassidus is 'Shema': lift your eyes 'on high', higher and higher, beyond the mind; and that very thing to be grasped by the mind.

**The steps.** Stated (Tanya ch. 42): train yourself constantly, so it is fixed in heart and mind, that everything you see is the King's outer garment, and so remember always their inner life. This is 'emunah', from 'training', like a craftsman training his hands. Stated (Likkutei Torah): keep it in mind without distraction; a passing thought does nothing. Ours: look at one thing; ask what is keeping it in being right now.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: it becomes recognition and feeling, 'as if seeing'. Ours: the world stops looking flat (MAP S12).

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: nature-worship; the beauty itself taken as G-d (the garment for the King).

**Guards.** Ours: few risks; for someone spacey or derealized (MAP S20), keep it concrete and bodily.

**Whose.** Stated (Likkutei Torah, Tanya, Hayom Yom). The one-thing practice: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Look at one thing outside, a tree or the sky. It isn't holding itself up. Something is giving it being, right now. Stay with that."

## DR-15 · Eating and the physical

**Who it suits.** Everyone; especially the person who feels the body is the enemy of the spirit.

**When.** Meals; Shabbos and festival meals; any use of the physical world.

**What it is.** Birurim (sorting and raising): the life in food, when eaten to have strength and a wide mind for Him, rises 'like an offering'. Torah Ohr: by eating, a person gains strength to pray and give his soul over in 'Echad', and so the food itself is raised into bittul. On weekdays eating is sorting; on Shabbos it is a drawing-down from above.

**From inside.** Stated (Hayom Yom, the Tzemach Tzedek to his daughter-in-law): 'better to eat for the sake of praying than to pray for the sake of eating'. The Frierdiker Rebbe: make the physical 'a vessel for the spiritual'.

**The steps.** Stated (Tanya ch. 7): eat and drink 'to widen his mind for G-d and His Torah', or for the joy of Shabbos and festivals. Hayom Yom: be healthy and strong, and joyful, to put life into mitzvos. Ours: a blessing said slowly; one bite tasted as given.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: strength for prayer; the food 'rises'. Ours: eating calmly, without guilt or greed.

**Its counterfeit.** Stated (Tanya ch. 7): eating for appetite alone, even of permitted food, does not rise. Ours: asceticism and fasting as the path (the Tzemach Tzedek told his daughter-in-law to eat first).

**Guards.** Ours: never touch eating-disorder territory with 'eat less' or 'eat for G-d' pressure; a person with such a struggle goes to care first. For a person not Jewish, no kashrus frame.

**Whose.** Stated (Tanya, Torah Ohr, Hayom Yom). Guards: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Before you eat, say thanks slowly, and eat to have strength for what matters. The food becomes part of the reaching."

## DR-16 · Speech: saying it aloud

**Who it suits.** People whose mind races (speech anchors it); people who pray alone.

**When.** Blessings; prayer; Torah said by heart in the street, the store, the subway.

**What it is.** Speaking holy words joins one's speech to His word. Tanya ch. 41: the soul is given over 'through binding my thought to Your thought and my speech to Your speech, in the letters of Torah and prayer, and especially in saying to G-d directly, Blessed are You'. The higher teshuvah: 'that G-d's word truly be in his mouth'.

**From inside.** Stated (Hayom Yom): saying letters of Torah in the store, the street, the subway 'cleans the air'. Ours: the words steady the person who says them.

**The steps.** Stated: say blessings and prayer with the mind in the words; know some Torah by heart (Chumash, Tehillim, Mishnah, Tanya) so as to say it anywhere (Hayom Yom). Ours: say one verse aloud slowly, or one plain sentence to Him in your own words (MAP S55).

**Signs it is real.** Ours: the person speaks to Him, not about Him.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: rapid mouthing with the mind elsewhere; words as a spell.

**Guards.** Ours: for a person not Jewish, their own words to Him, not Jewish liturgy.

**Whose.** Stated (Tanya, Hayom Yom). The own-words practice: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Say one sentence to Him out loud, in your own words. Short. 'You're here.' Hearing your own voice say it does something."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 41, line 621: «ובפרט באמירה לה׳ לנכח, כמו ״ברוך אתה״», *and especially in saying to G-d directly, like 'Blessed are You'*<br>
> Tanya, Iggeres HaTeshuvah, line 991: «להיות דבר ה׳ בפיו באמת», *that the word of G-d be truly in his mouth*<br>
> Hayom Yom, line 57: «ווען מ'פאהרט אין סאבוויי און מען זאגט אותיות שבתורה, מאכט מען מיט דעם ריין דעם אויר», *when one rides the subway and says letters of Torah, one cleans the air*<br>
> </small>

## DR-17 · Niggun: the soul's door

**Who it suits.** People for whom words are too much; the numb (MAP S03); groups.

**When.** Before prayer; at a farbrengen; in a low moment; at the end of a sitting.

**What it is.** The Frierdiker Rebbe: the work of a chassid is to know oneself, and that is 'through a niggun'. A niggun shows a person where he is, where he must be, and where he can be, because it is the revelation of the essence of the soul. 'A niggun is an opening and a gate.' Singing means 'singing oneself out'.

**From inside.** Stated (Hayom Yom): a chassidic niggun strengthens hope and trust, brings joy, and sets the house in a ray of light. Stated (Rayatz): a niggun one prayed with can bring a weeping of longing.

**The steps.** Stated (Hayom Yom, on koshering meat as avodah): soaking = immersing in the Rebbe's words; salting = yechidus (private audience); rinsing = niggun. Ours: hum a slow wordless tune; let it repeat; stop and sit in the quiet after.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: hope, trust, joy. Ours: the person ends quieter than they began.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: music as a mood-drug, chasing the high (MAP S18); performance.

**Guards.** Ours: few risks; do not push someone grieving to sing joyful tunes.

**Whose.** Stated (Rayatz, Hayom Yom). The humming practice: ours.

**In a chat today.** "No words for a minute. Hum something slow, again and again, and let it carry what you can't say. Then just be still."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz), line 6527: «ניגון איז א פתח ושער וואס עי"ז קען מען צוקומען צו דעם וואו מ'דארף זיין», *a niggun is an opening and a gate through which one can come to where one must be*<br>
> Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz), line 6527: «א ניגון ווייזט אים וואו ער איז, ווי ער דארף זיין און וואו ער קען זיין», *a niggun shows him where he is, where he must be and where he can be*<br>
> Hayom Yom, line 418: «א חסידעשער ניגון שטארקט די תקוה ובטחון בריינגט שמחה», *a chassidic niggun strengthens hope and trust and brings joy*<br>
> Hayom Yom, line 517: «שרי' - ווייקען זיך אין דברי הרב, מליחה - יחידות, הדחה - ניגון», *soaking: steeping oneself in the Rebbe's words; salting: yechidus; rinsing: niggun*<br>
> </small>

## DR-18 · The farbrengen: together

**Who it suits.** The lonely; the person stuck alone in their head; anyone who needs to hear it from another person.

**When.** Shabbos afternoon or Motzaei Shabbos; festive dates; any gathering of friends around a word of Chassidus.

**What it is.** The chassidic gathering: a story, a word explained, a niggun, sometimes a l'chaim, and honest talk about one's ways. Hayom Yom: one of the foundations of the chassidic path, an opening to love of one's fellow. The Mitteler Rebbe: it is the four walls of a spiritual mikveh.

**From inside.** Stated (Rayatz): an inward word explained, an inward niggun, a niggun one prayed with, and a weeping of longing from 'the feeling of the soul's inner essence'.

**The steps.** Stated (Rayatz): the order was a story, then the 'therefore' (bechen), how it must work in the powers of mind, heart and action; 'and kabbalas ol one must get by oneself'. Stated (Hayom Yom): the speakers ask the listeners to improve their ways and fix times to learn. Ours: for the guide, the warmth of a friend telling one true story and one plain 'so what do we do'.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: good resolutions kept; love among friends. Ours: the person leaves less alone.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: drinking without the word; a social evening with no turning inward.

**Guards.** Ours: alcohol never for someone with addiction or the young; the guide never encourages drinking. The guide is not a substitute for a real community; point to one.

**Whose.** Stated (Hayom Yom, Rayatz, citing the Mitteler Rebbe). The guide's adaptation and guards: ours.

**In a chat today.** "This is better with people. Is there someone you could sit with this week over a story and a l'chaim of tea, and just talk about what matters?"

> <small>**Sources.**
> Hayom Yom, line 577: «ההתוועדות - פארבריינגען - ברבים בכלל, ובשבת או במוצאי שבת בפרט, היא אחת היסודות בדרכי החסידים והחסידות», *the farbrengen in public, in general, and on Shabbos or Motzaei Shabbos in particular, is one of the foundations of the ways of chassidim and Chassidus*<br>
> Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz), line 9720: «אז א חסידות'ער פארבריינגען איז די ד' דפנות פון דער רוחניות'דיקער מקוה», *a chassidic farbrengen is the four walls of the spiritual mikveh*<br>
> Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz), line 6415: «און קבלת עול מלכות שמים דארף מען אליין קריגן», *and kabbalas ol one must get by oneself*<br>
> </small>

## DR-19 · Shabbos: the soul returned

**Who it suits.** Shomer Shabbos people; for others (ours), a set hour of stopping with Him.

**When.** From Wednesday (preparation begins), Friday night, Shabbos day, its meals and its prayers.

**What it is.** On Shabbos the worlds rise to their source; Tanya calls Shabbos 'the higher teshuvah'. Torah Ohr: the will above reason shown on Shabbos lights the weekday prayer; weekday prayer is the ladder joining Shabbos to the six days. Eating on Shabbos is not sorting but drawing-down.

**From inside.** See MAP S76 (Piaseczner: 'when the candles are brought in, I find myself another man'). Ours: the sorting stops; nothing has to be achieved.

**The steps.** Stated (Hayom Yom): three days before Shabbos are preparation for it. Stated (Tanya): the lower teshuvah at least once a week before Shabbos, so that Shabbos and its prayers can be the higher return. Stated (Torah Ohr): draw the Shabbos light into each weekday prayer.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: the week is blessed from it ('all days are blessed from it', Zohar cited in Hayom Yom). Ours: the person carries some of its stillness into Sunday.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours (with MAP S76): just a day off; worry carried in.

**Guards.** Stated (MAP S76): never impose Jewish practice on someone not Jewish.

**Whose.** Stated (Tanya, Torah Ohr, Hayom Yom). The non-Jewish set hour: ours.

**In a chat today.** "Give yourself one stretch of time this week where nothing needs fixing. Not a break from Him; time with Him, with nothing to do."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Tanya, Iggeres HaTeshuvah, line 995: «שהשבת היא בחינת תשובה עילאה», *Shabbos is the higher teshuvah*<br>
> Torah Ohr, line 2396: «וגילוי הארה זו היא בזמן התפלה דחול כשמתעורר באהבה וחפץ לה' ית' שהוא בחי' הארה מקדושת שבת המאיר בימי החול», *the revelation of this light is at the time of weekday prayer, when one wakes with love and desire for Him; it is a ray of Shabbos's holiness shining in the weekdays*<br>
> Torah Ohr, line 377: «אבל האכילה שבשבת היא המשכה מלמעלה לשובע נפשו», *but eating on Shabbos is a drawing from above to satisfy his soul*<br>
> Hayom Yom, line 28: «ג' ימים קודם השבת הוא הכנה לשבת», *three days before Shabbos are preparation for Shabbos*<br>
> </small>

## DR-20 · The festivals' two doors

**Who it suits.** Anyone keeping the Jewish year.

**When.** Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkos, Shemini Atzeres and Simchas Torah; each holiday as it comes.

**What it is.** The Rebbe (Hayom Yom): Rosh Hashanah and Shemini Atzeres share the same inner intentions, but Rosh Hashanah is the ascent, worked through supplication, humility and great bitterness, and Shemini Atzeres is the drawing-down, worked through joy. The year itself repeats the ladder: up through brokenness, down through joy.

**From inside.** Ours: the same unity, met from two sides: as awe on the Day of Judgment, as joy when dancing with the Torah.

**The steps.** Stated: on Rosh Hashanah, supplication with humility and bitterness; on Shemini Atzeres, joy. Ours: before a festival, ask which side of the ladder it is.

**Signs it is real.** Ours: after the festival, a change one can name.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: the holidays as only family and food.

**Guards.** Stated (MAP S76): never impose Jewish practice on someone not Jewish. Ours: bitterness on Rosh Hashanah is not for the depressed; joy at Simchas Torah never requires alcohol.

**Whose.** The Rosh Hashanah / Shemini Atzeres pair: stated. The year as ladder: ours.

**In a chat today.** "This holiday is one side of the climb. You don't have to do all of it. One real moment of the day's feeling is enough."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Hayom Yom, line 574: «בר"ה העבודה בתחנונים בהכנעה ובמרירות עצומה, ובשמע"צ היא בקו השמחה», *on Rosh Hashanah the work is in supplication, with humility and great bitterness; on Shemini Atzeres it is in the line of joy*<br>
> </small>

## DR-21 · Seeing the unity in today's world

**Who it suits.** Modern people; those who think in systems, science, business; the person unsettled by fast change.

**When.** Any time; reading news, doing accounts, watching how things depend on one another.

**What it is.** The Rebbe: the unity of 'there is nothing besides Him' does not mean the worlds are absent; the worlds themselves, as they exist, defined by time and place, are united with His essence. And in all creatures 'unity is noticeable', for one sees sensorially that no creature can fulfil its role alone; all fill one role together, to reveal 'Hashem is One'. It is hidden, and Jews reveal it by Torah and love of one another. He adds: in every change, every time and place, find the point of unity, revealed when one looks deep, 'and especially in recent times', to see the providence.

**From inside.** Stated (the Rebbe, telling of R. Binyamin Kletzker): after adding up his business accounts correctly, he wrote at the total 'There is nothing besides Him'; in the physical thing itself it was felt that its reality is G-dliness.

**The steps.** Stated (Toras Menachem): do not be shaken by changes around you; in each one look deep until you see the Divine power that created the place, the time and the people. Ours: take one system (a body, a city, an ecosystem, a market) and see how nothing in it stands alone; then ask who makes it one.

**Signs it is real.** Stated: not being shaken by change. Ours: the news becomes less frightening; interdependence is felt as His oneness, not as chaos.

**Its counterfeit.** Ours: pantheism (the world is G-d) or vague 'all is one' that drops Him; reading every event as a message; messianic excitement used to deny real suffering.

**Guards.** Ours: never use this to explain away tragedy; with a person in grief or danger, stay with them first.

**Whose.** Stated (the Rebbe). 'Take one system' and guards: ours. We did not find in the library a single sicha that says in these words 'today the unity can be seen'; the Rebbe's statements above are the closest we verified, and 'especially in recent times' is his.

**In a chat today.** "Look at how nothing in your day works alone: your body, your street, the network on your phone. It all hangs together as one. Ask who's holding it as one."

> <small>**Sources.**
> Inyanah shel Toras HaChassidus (the Rebbe), line 165: «שגם העולמות עצמם כמו שהם נמצאים במציאותם (ומוגדרים בגדר זמן ומקום) מיוחדים הם בתכלית היחוד בעצמות אוא"ס ב"ה», *even the worlds themselves as they exist in their reality (and defined by time and place) are united in the utmost unity with the Essence of the Infinite Light*<br>
> Toras Menachem (the Rebbe), line 18239: «בכל הנבראים שברא הקב"ה בעולם ניכר שיש בהם ענין האחדות», *in all the creatures G-d created in the world, the matter of unity is noticeable*<br>
> Toras Menachem (the Rebbe), line 98416: «למצוא בכל שינוי, בכל זמן ומקום, את נקודת האחדות שמתגלה וניכרת כאשר מתעמקים», *to find in every change, in every time and place, the point of unity, revealed and noticeable when one looks deep*<br>
> Toras Menachem (the Rebbe), line 98417: «ובפרט בזמנים האחרונים – לראות בהם את ההשגחה הפרטית», *and especially in recent times, to see in them personal providence*<br>

## What is stated, what is ours, what is open

- **Stated** (a source says it): every step, time, length, sign and counterfeit attributed above to a named work, with its verified line.
- **Ours**: the idea of 'doors' as a set; the picking table; the plain-English chat lines; the universal versions for people not Jewish; all safety exclusions beyond what the sources themselves say (grief, depression, eating disorders, danger, alcohol).
