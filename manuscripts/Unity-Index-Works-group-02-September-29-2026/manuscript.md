## Signature (stated (Basi LeGani 5711; Simchas Torah 5722; Vayeshev 5732) / reading (that the whole collection is one expression of it))

Toras Menachem is the one idea set as the programme of a leadership. Its first maamar, Basi LeGani 5711, states the whole of it (stated). The purpose of the chain of worlds is not the upper worlds, whose business is revelation. It is this lowest world, which 'feels that its existence is from itself' precisely because it is rooted in the Essence, whose existence is from itself (3545). A dwelling is where a person lives 'with his whole essence', so the dwelling below means 'not only revelations but that the Essence of Ein Sof is found in them' (3548). The seventh generation's task is to draw the Shechinah 'down, literally' by turning the animal soul's folly into holy folly, and to proclaim 'El olam', not 'El ha-olam': Godliness and world are 'kula chad' (3556-3559). What the work adds over the length of 23 years is twofold. First, a demand about knowledge: the Alter Rebbe 'demands' that faith be not enough, 'ein od' must penetrate every faculty, and Chassidus must be spread because it is where 'ein od' is best explained (54584-54587, stated). The Rebbe here takes the 'know' side of the F12 disagreement and attributes it to the Alter Rebbe. Second, a precision about which unity is meant: 'echad' as against 'yachid', Havayah hu ha-Elokim as a union of two things, and above both 'ein od milvado', 'no other existence at all', revealed at Sinai while the world stayed world (107701-107702, stated). That every occasion-bound talk in the 6.5 million words carries this is our reading. The concept vocabulary is dense (עצמות 4.45/10k), but many talks are about the calendar, education or public events, and there the tie is made by the Rebbe only sometimes.

## Structure

Chronological volumes (5710-5733), each a sequence of farbrengens, with maamarim marked by ד"ה headings interleaved among the sichos. The first volumes open with the mourning talks and the acceptance of leadership, and Basi LeGani 5711 (vol. ב, 3538-3565) lays down the doctrine the rest applies: purpose = dwelling for the Essence below, method = itkafya/is'hapcha, task of the generation = drawing the Shechinah into the physical. The middle volumes (5718-5727) carry the densest concept vocabulary (עצמות, אלקות) and the explicit statements on knowing 'ein od' (5722). From 5728 the register turns to campaigns (מבצע) and redemption, where 'ein od' and the dwelling are given as the stated end (e.g. 106126, 107521). The structure thus moves from principle to application, as G11 says it should: begin from the effect and end in action.

## Signature (stated in chapters 7 and 9; thin elsewhere)

Mostly thin. It is a life of a Chassid with his collected papers. Most of it (the biography, the halachic chapter, the grammar chapter) does not carry the one idea. Where his own short Chassidus essays appear, they apply it faithfully in the Chabad manner (stated). He reads 'with all your heart' through the two knowledges: the lower knowledge is the bitul of yesh to ayin, and the higher is that 'above is the true yesh and what is below is ayin' (665). He then sorts service by them. Knowing and contemplating the unity is open to the beinoni and yields itkafya. That the heart should see 'ein od mamash milvado' belongs to the tzaddik, whose service is is'hapcha (666). He also restates the dwelling below as Godliness revealed 'in our knowledge' below as above (751). What it adds is only a Chassid's gloss, useful as a witness that the two-knowledge scheme (F02) was the working frame of an ordinary Chabad Chassid's learning.

## Structure

Introduction and archive description (1-35); biography in five chapters (36-495); writings in four chapters: halacha (496-573), Kabbalah and Chassidus (574-668), language and grammar (669-726), outlook and service (727-886). Only the Kabbalah/Chassidus and outlook chapters serve the idea.

## Signature (stated (980, 981, 1068, 1147); the relation of its two centers is reading)

Derekh HaMelekh expresses the one idea through the Piaseczner's own theme: the revealed soul. The unity is seen in the world only as far as the seer's soul is revealed (stated). Every part of the world is 'a letter of the letters of Elokim', and every Jew must see in the world these combinations, memale. Whoever does not see them fails because 'his soul is hidden'. ## Signature (stated (150, 201, 284); the placing in the F12 disagreement is reading)

Chanah Ariel is a Chabad Chassid's commentary in which nearly every subject is taken back to the relation of yesh and the unity (stated throughout). His particular angle is faith as the faculty that sees the unity directly. Contemplation's work is to nullify the intellect's attachment to the feeling that the world exists. 'But when the spirit of faith itself rests on him, he needs no reason or explanation at all', for he comes to recognize Godliness itself, that there is nothing besides Him, 'and so the world and its yesh do not contradict the unity of the Creator at all' (201). That places him on the 'believe' side of the kept F12 disagreement, closer to the Alter Rebbe's 'we have no business with hidden things' than to the Rayatz's 'know'; the alignment is our reading. He keeps two reasons for creation side by side and does not reduce one to the other: the dwelling below, and 'no king without a people' (284). He then distinguishes an outer dwelling, which existed even in the two thousand years of chaos since He alone governs, from the true one, 'that the Essence be revealed in the worlds' through Sinai and the Temple, in Israel (284, stated). He makes Elokim the shield that conceals Havayah down to 'complete yesh', and the mitzvah the means, given only to those who received the Torah, by which Havayah is drawn into the physical object (150, stated). What he adds is the faith-centered epistemology and a careful two-level account of the dwelling.

## Signature (stated (40, 811, 1188); its relation to F02 and to Chabad is reading)

Mei HaShiloach is not organized around the unity vocabulary; its center is the will of God and its depth (the counts show this plainly). Where it touches the one idea it gives a radical form of it (stated). The rabbinic 'all is in the hands of Heaven except the fear of Heaven' holds only 'according to the limit of human understanding'. 'In truth all is in the hands of Heaven, even the fear of Heaven, only in this world God hid His way', and 'even all the sins of Israel are in God's providence' (40). The Izhbitzer applies 'ein od' to the human will itself: a person should not imagine he has 'any power of his own', he has only longing, while 'the earth is the Lord's and there is nothing besides Him' (811). In the spine's terms this is one of the schools 'organized around other centers' (the will of God); the unity here is present and forceful, but it is not the book's center.

Tiferet Yosef carries the Izhbitz-Radzin form of the one idea, more gently than Mei HaShiloach and with more unity language (stated). Man's task is to break through the partition by words of Torah and see his own grasp united with God's light (164). The hiding itself turns out to be chesed: God hid His kindness so that there be room for man's labor (164). That is F02 and F03 together, in the form the spine calls 'once one knows He hides there, it is no hiding'. What the book adds is the unusual non-Chabad use of the dwelling below. It is always drawn from the Midrash on 'yom echad': 'from the hour He was alone in His world He desired a dwelling below' (84). It is read as God showing a person, after his service, that his service has a place. That this is the same F07 as Tanya 36's is our reading. The source is the same midrash, but the emphasis differs: the place given to human service, not the Essence at home.

## Faces

- **F08 Bitul** (primary): 68: 'let him have no importance at all, but be at the level of ayin'; 11-12: all worlds are as nothing before Him, so why be drawn after desires.
- **F02/F03 Higher unity / tzimtzum** (secondary): 11: 'all, the world of the spheres, of the angels and of the Throne, is as nothing before Him, for they are all within the space of the tzimtzum that He contracted Himself, and all was made by one word'.

## Signature (stated (10, 11, 68); the tzimtzum-wording link is reading)

Darkhei Yesharim turns the one idea directly into conduct (stated). Because 'every thing in the world is full of the Creator' and even the most trivial event is 'from His providence', a person should make no distinction between what goes as he wished and what does not (10). Because all worlds are 'as nothing before Him', made 'by one word', it is folly to cling to a leaf when one can hold the tree (11-12). The human counterpart is bitul: 'let him be at the level of ayin' (68). What it adds is the shortest path from F01 and F02 to equanimity, devekut and humility, which is the spine's G12 and G08 in a few lines. One wording should be marked: 'the space of the tzimtzum that He contracted Himself' (11) is spatial, like the wording of Tzava'at HaRivash that the spine sets against SH 7. Whether it is meant literally is not stated, so linking it to that dispute is our reading.

## Structure

Numbered rules of conduct, beginning with the thought that everything is from the Creator and full of Him (10-12), then devekut during speech and study, joy, fasting and humility; then a Chassid's ethical will (68-118) and a few Maggid sayings (119-123). The doctrinal lines come first and the rules follow from them.
