Here the Shelah opens his second great book, the "Ten Utterances" (Aseret HaDibrot). He builds it on ten pillars (line 2668): Torah, service, kindness, justice, truth, peace, return, prayer, charity, and faith, which holds them all. Each pillar gets a season and a tractate. Weekdays (*Chullin*, "ordinary things") are the pillar of truth; Shabbat is the pillar of faith. Each tractate has three chapters: *Ner Mitzvah* (the law), *Torah Ohr* (the inner reasons), and *Derech Chayim* (the path, ethics).

## 1.1 He alone, and all from Him

The Torah Ohr on Shabbat opens with the strongest unity line in this range:

> והוא היווה את כל, וממציאות הווייתו נתהווה כל הויה, כי הוא לבדו ואין זולתו (line 3268)

"He brought all into being, and from the reality of His being every being came to be, for He alone is, and there is none besides Him." And:

> וכל מה שיש בנמצא, הן גשמי הן רוחני, הכל הוא ממציאותו (line 3268)

"All that exists, bodily or spiritual, all is from His reality." Shabbat is the sign of this, because Shabbat says the world was made new. The same line reads the evening and morning blessings as a lesson in oneness: the day is held in the night and the night in the day, "to show their oneness, for one G-d created them" (כלול מדת יום בלילה, ומדת לילה ביום, להורות על אחדותם כי אל אחד בראם, line 3268).

## 1.2 He makes it new, still today

> ובאמת השם יתברך מחדש בכל יום תמיד (line 3272)

"In truth G-d renews every day, always." The blessing over the havdalah flame says *borei*, which the Shelah reads as "He created before and creates still today" (ובורא פירושו, ברא כבר ובורא עוד היום, line 3287). The weekdays are the same six days of creation going out and coming back: "the branches go out from Shabbat and return to Shabbat, out and back, and they are renewed" (line 2880). Quoting Avodat HaKodesh: "there is only ever one day" (ולעולם לא נמצא כי אם יום אחד בלבד, line 2874). Shabbat is the point; the six days are "the edges going out from the point and returning to it" (line 3272).

This is the ground of the Chassidic "He renews in His goodness each day, always" (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud 1-2), stated without the Alter Rebbe's step that the thing would fall back to nothing if the renewal stopped. The Shelah's picture is a wheel turning around a still point.

## 1.3 Hidden, not gone

On the weekdays the upper light is "closed". The Shelah (from Pardes) says what this does not mean:

> אין הכוונה שאור האצילות נסתם מכל וכל, שזה מורה סילוק ההשגחה. אלא האור העליון מתעלם מבפנים, והוא כמושב המלך שהוא בהיכלו בפנים (line 2863)

"It does not mean the light is shut off entirely, which would mean His care is gone; rather the upper light hides within, like the king seated inside his palace." So "whatever is done, all of it is by the king's word" (line 2863). This is a tzimtzum teaching in small: hiding is not leaving. It is close to the Tanya's reading of tzimtzum as concealment, not removal (Shaar HaYichud 7).

## 1.4 The mashal of fire

G-d "lights the whole world with His glory, and from that side He is revealed; yet no grasp of mind can grasp Him" (line 3275). The worlds are a great bonfire, a torch lit from it, a candle lit from the torch, and the candle shining "in the dark house, the world of doing, and all by the power above" (והכל בכח העליון יתברך, line 3275). The first light of creation was not new: "only now it was revealed, like a flame from the coal" (line 3274).

## 1.5 No exile above

The weekdays, he says with the Ari's school, are when the Presence dresses in the husks "to keep her children", as "in all their trouble He is troubled" and "I am with him in trouble" (line 3340). But the upper lights are untouched: "no impurity touches there" (line 3341), and "above, heaven forbid, there is no exile and nothing bad" (כי למעלה חס ושלום אין גלות ולא שום דבר רע, line 3297). Exile is a word for our side only.

## 1.6 One power in all; one face to all

"Since the sefirot are a complete oneness, one power is in all of them and all are in one power" (line 2927). And the face of G-d turns to us straight or backward by our deeds, not by luck, "as water shows face to face"; the holy face "shows itself to each thing alike, bad or good; in their oneness they are even to each thing and its opposite" (line 2929). The One does not change. What we do changes what we see.

## 1.7 Torah as His names

"The whole Torah is names of the Holy One; from them and in them all the worlds unfold, and they hold up all of creation" (line 3199). This is the Ramban's teaching that the Shelah uses to make the Torah reading a meeting with the King: hear it "like the king's decree newly come to us" (line 3200).

## 1.8 The unity at the doorpost and in the tefillin

The mezuzah "holds the oneness of the Holy One, so each time he comes and goes he will remember His oneness" (line 2735). Sunday is the day "to fix His oneness in the heart" (line 2946).

## 3.1 The prayer before work (proposed: `work_as_means`)

"When he goes to trade let him say: for the unity of the Holy One and His Presence I go to this work, and I trust that G-d will send me a living through it, and this work is only a means" (line 2771). With profit: thanks, and do not be proud. With loss: "thank and praise G-d, for surely it is for his good" (line 2772). *Tie:* `arrive`, `carry`, S84. *Ours:* the guide keeps the words plain and says them in any language.

## 3.2 The tithe of days (proposed: `tithe_of_days`)

"He must count all the days of his life, the days G-d feeds and keeps him... so G-d's watching care never leaves his eyes, and through this he serves Him in love" (line 2780). The Shelah makes every tenth day a day of return and fasting, counted from Rosh Hashanah (lines 2778-2789); on Shabbat it stays a day of return without the fast (line 2787). *Ours:* the guide keeps the count and the review, and does not prescribe the fast.

## 3.3 The day in thirds and the day's plan

Eight hours for Torah and prayer, eight for work, eight for sleep (line 2770). The day's order (lines 3099-3100): prayer, then study "from strength to strength", honest work, holy eating, "some act of kindness every day", at evening think of return, and before sleep finish it. *Tie:* `cheshbon`, `act`.

## 3.4 Hands at work, mind above

The story of the hermit who became a shul caretaker and spilled the oil beside the lamps from his cleaving (line 2909): the wise man of the town said he should be the rabbi. The Shelah's reading: the sages did their work "by their outer limbs" while "their thought and soul cleaved" (line 2909). *Tie:* `hold`, `carry`, S82. *Ours:* start with simple work (washing, walking); bring the mind back without force.

## 3.5 The day-of-week themes (proposed: `day_theme`)

"Sunday points to His oneness, to fix His oneness in the heart... Monday return; Tuesday prayer; Wednesday Torah; Thursday charity" (line 2946), and Friday: remember that man was made in the image. *Ours:* a simple weekly rhythm the guide can offer to anyone, with Sunday as the day of the unity.

## 3.6 Ride the hour's light (proposed: `hour_light`)

From Tomer Devorah (quoted at lines 3101-3104): "how a person binds himself to the upper holiness... never parting from the upper quality... to know which quality rules the hour and bind himself to it." Night is kingship: accept the yoke at bedtime. Morning is kindness: go to prayer with love. Daytime is Torah. Afternoon is judgment: pray with awe. Evening returns to kingship. The meal is a kindness to the body. "Always cleaving to the light that rules" (line 3104). *Tie:* `binding`, S83. *Ours:* a plain version: at each turn of the day, name the quality of the hour and meet it.

## 3.7 Bless as you move

The morning blessings follow the body's waking step by step: covered, eyes opened, stretching, standing, feet on the floor, shoes, walking, belt, head covered, face washed (line 3067). The Shelah asks that one do each act with intent so each blessing is earned (line 3085). *Tie:* `details`, `act`. *Ours:* a waking practice of thanks, one move at a time.

## 3.8 Join evening to morning; wake in love

"All this shows the joining and the oneness, so a person should keep a fixed rule to tie the coming in to the going out" (line 2949): bridge night and day with study or service. On waking, "set love of the Creator in his heart, who gave back his soul, for many lay down in their beds and did not rise" (line 2981). *Tie:* S65, `heart_after`.

## 3.9 Say it before you do it (proposed: `say_before`)

Before a mitzvah say aloud: "here I am about to do this mitzvah, whose law is such and such; I will do it awake and glad" (line 3352), then do it, then think of its inner sense. Deed, speech and thought are joined. The same move in food: "this I buy or cook for the honor of Shabbat", "and then holiness spreads into this food" (line 3169). *Tie:* `lips`, `act`.

## 3.10 Shabbat: rest from work, speech, worry, anger

- **Prepare with your own hands**: "his dignity is that he honors Shabbat" (line 3112).
- **Rest from speech**: "just as one must rest from work, so one must rest from speech" (line 3243), since G-d made the world by speech and rested from it. R. Shimon told his talkative mother, "Mother, it is Shabbat", and she was quiet.
- **Rest from worry**: "let all your work be in your eyes as if it were done" (line 3248).
- **No anger**: "a double wrong" (line 3171). "Better a dinner of greens with love than a fatted ox with hate" (line 3173).
- **Delight for the guest, not for yourself**: "whoever delights the Shabbat", not "himself on Shabbat" (line 3181); eat light and glad, not stuffed (line 3182).

*Tie:* `quieting`, S84, S15. *Ours:* the guide keeps these four rests as one practice; it does not carry the wine at the table or at havdalah (the guide never drinks).

## 3.11 The Shabbat resolve (proposed: `shabbat_resolve`)

The week is a menorah: three days before Shabbat to prepare, three after that still hold its mark (line 3349). "Let him look in each day at the holiness in it, a taste of the holiness of Shabbat", and "on each Shabbat let him make a holy resolve how he will live in holiness in the days to come" (line 3349). And each day, "some hour" set apart to cleave to Him makes the six days "ordinary things made in the purity of the holy" (line 2892). *Tie:* `chazarah`, `cheshbon`.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Money and fear.** You cannot have more or less than what is set (line 2760); honest work is the channel. Loss is for good (line 2772).
- **Work and holiness.** Trade is holy too; prayer is "holy of holies" and trade "holy" (line 2680). Hands can work while the mind stays above (line 2909).
- **Time.** There is one day; return today (lines 2874, 2916). Days count by being counted (line 2780).
- **Rest.** Real rest is of the heart: work seen as done (line 3248).
- **Home and peace.** A plain meal with love beats a feast with a fight (line 3173); the Presence says "this one is mine" of a home with light and peace (line 3172).
- **Being together.** "Though the bodies are apart, at their root they are joined" (line 2845): why we walk a guest out.

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **Renewal.** The Shelah's "He renews every day, always" (line 3272) and "creates still today" (line 3287) are the seed of Shaar HaYichud ch. 1-2. The Alter Rebbe presses it further: the renewal is from nothing, each moment, and without it the thing is nothing. The Shelah has the wheel and the point; Chabad has the word that never stops.
- **Tzimtzum.** "Hidden within, like the king in his palace" (line 2863) matches the Tanya's reading that tzimtzum is hiding, not removal (Shaar HaYichud 7); the Shelah does not argue the point but assumes it.
- **Negative attributes.** "No grasp of mind can grasp Him" while "He lights the whole world with His glory" (line 3275) is the Rambam's two sides: known by His works, not in Himself. The Shelah does not use the Rambam's language here.
- **The Ramak.** The Shelah cites the Ramak on the reading of the Torah portion as a union (line 3300) and quotes Tomer Devorah at length (lines 3101-3104). "All that exists is from His reality" (line 3268) is close to the Ramak's "He is all that exists", with the Ramak's guard kept: "no impurity touches there" (line 3341), so not all that exists is He.
- **Business in faith.** Chabad's teaching that the workday is where the dwelling below is built (Tanya ch. 36) meets the Shelah's "trade is holy" (line 2680) and the prayer before work (line 2771).
- **Not carried.** The line that the six days are holy only for Israel (line 2892), the fast of every tenth day, the wine at the Shabbat table and havdalah, the marital timing (lines 3194-3196), the nail-cutting secrets, and the many laws of muktzeh, eruv and candles are read and not carried into the guide.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **He alone, all from Him** (line 3268) and **He renews every day** (line 3272), with the hidden king (line 2863).
2. **The prayer before work** and **loss is for good** (lines 2771-2772).
3. **Hands here, mind above** (line 2909).
4. **The day-of-week themes**, Sunday for the unity (line 2946).
5. **Ride the hour's light** (lines 3101-3104), in a plain form.
6. **The four rests of Shabbat**: work, speech, worry, anger (lines 3171, 3243, 3248).
7. **The Shabbat resolve** and **an hour a day set apart** (lines 2892, 3349).
8. **Say it before you do it** (line 3352).
