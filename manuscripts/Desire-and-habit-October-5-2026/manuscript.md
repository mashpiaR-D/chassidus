<a id="c10"></a> ## C10 · I feel like a fraud because I still want what I've renounced

*6 findings from Tanya, Torah Ohr (part 2 of 2), Imrei Binah, Toras Chaim (part 3 of 4), Derech Mitzvosecha, Biurei Tanya*

> Everyone thinks I'm good, but inside the cravings are as strong as ever. I'm only forcing myself. I pray with real feeling, then walk out and want the world. I must be a hypocrite.

**What is really going on.** Tanya gives the core: the in-between person (beinoni) keeps the evil in him at full strength. What he rules is thought, speech and deed, and this rank is open to anyone at any hour. He shouldn't believe praise that calls him a tzaddik, but grieving that he isn't one comes from arrogance, and every craving not acted on is a mitzvah kept. Biurei Tanya: the mind rules the garments, not the heart's essence; the real choice is at the first moment; if the pull has already taken hold, remember 'I don't want to be separated from God'; habit becomes second nature, and real change shows in what you want. Torah Ohr: forcing the heart is a real level with its own worth. Imrei Binah: a desire, once born, can outgrow the thought that made it; the mind is a lamp for choosing. Toras Chaim: the prayer and the craving are each true from their own root, fused like a dream. Derech Mitzvosecha: traits stay as they were born while the will refuses, and the distance itself can power a greater love.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"If I still want it, I'm a hypocrite."* → The mind rules action, speech and thought, not the heart's essence. Traits stay as you were born with them, and what counts is that your will refuses to follow. This rank is open to anyone at any time. `BT·Q11` · `DM·Q31` · `TAN·Q03`
- *"Everyone thinks I'm good, so I'm deceiving them."* → Don't believe them when they rank you as a tzaddik; consider yourself in-between. That is an accurate self-assessment, not fraud. Grief that you aren't a tzaddik comes from arrogance. `TAN·Q01` · `TAN·Q09`
- *"If my prayer were real, I wouldn't fall right after it."* → The feeling in prayer cannot have been false. Each side is true from its own root, and their being fused together is the exile. `TC3·Q07` · `TC3·Q08` · `TC3·Q09`
- *"If I truly understood it was bad, I'd stop wanting it."* → Once a desire is born it can outgrow the thought behind it. A heart coarsened by indulgence won't turn by contemplation alone, and the mind is a lamp for choosing, not a switch. `IB·Q31` · `IB·Q32` · `BT·Q12`
- *"Forcing myself is fake. Only real transformation counts."* → Even those who only force the heart have 'only One in their heart', and a sin not committed is rewarded like a mitzvah done. `TO2·Q28` · `TAN·Q08`
- *"My worldly wants disqualify me from closeness."* → The concealment the body brings is exactly what can release a love without limit, like water bursting through a dam. `DM·Q17` · `DM·Q18`

1. **Govern the garments: count the turning, not the craving** · *from Tanya, Biurei Tanya (Esterman, Kaidaner, Simchovitz)* (1) Rename the state: desire at full strength is the in-between person's normal condition, not proof of fraud. (2) Choose at the very first moment of enticement: don't give it your heart at all. (3) If it has already taken hold, don't argue with it through contemplation; remember that you don't want to be cut off from God. (4) Turn your mind away and keep action, speech and thought in hand, even while the heart still wants. (5) Count each turning as a mitzvah kept, and be glad of it. *When:* In the moment of temptation, as often as it comes, and when praise lands and shame rises. *For:* Anyone who feels like a fraud or despairs of changing. *Not for:* Not a promise that cravings will vanish, which the texts call a gift. Not to be used for intrusive thoughts in OCD, where pushing thoughts away can intensify them. `TAN·Q03` · `TAN·Q08` · `BT·Q06` · `BT·Q07` · `BT·Q11`
2. **In prayer: bend the heart and fill both chambers** · *from Torah Ohr, Derech Mitzvosecha* (1) Contemplate the One God as far as your soul can bear, and through it bend your heart toward love. (2) Let one side of the heart hold bitterness over how far your aims have drifted into material things. (3) Let the other side hold joy in the commandments as God's will. (4) Let the distance itself drive the love, like water breaking through a dam. (5) When desire rises suddenly, let the bitterness be about the arousal while the will stays set against following it. *When:* Daily prayer, especially at the Shema. *For:* The sincere in-between person still drawn to worldly wants. *Not for:* Depressed people must never be given the bitterness without the joy. Not a warrant for crushing ordinary feelings or for self-punishment. `TO2·Q29` · `TO2·Q28` · `DM·Q14` · `DM·Q15` · `DM·Q16` · `DM·Q18` · `DM·Q30`
3. **Habit and a renewed will** · *from Biurei Tanya (Esterman, Kaidaner, Simchovitz)* (1) Practise the right response even while it feels imaginary; in the end habit becomes second nature. (2) Notice when the practice turns routine, and wake a new will beyond habit. (3) Judge progress by whether what you want has changed over time, not by feeling in prayer. *When:* Ongoing, at fixed times set for the work. *For:* Someone whose feeling in prayer never carries into life, or whose practice runs on routine. *Not for:* Someone expecting fast transformation; the texts say hating evil is ultimately a gift. `BT·Q13` · `BT·Q09` · `BT·Q10`
4. **Mercy to another lifts your own soul** · *from Derech Mitzvosecha* (1) Give to someone poor from your own toil. (2) The mercy you show him arouses mercy on your own soul, lifting it from its lowliness. *When:* Ongoing; no schedule is given. *For:* Someone whose traits feel heavy and unchanging. *Not for:* Not a transaction for forgiveness, and not beyond one's means. `DM·Q32`

**Where the works agree.** Still wanting does not make you a fraud. The pull is expected, and what is in your hands is action, speech, thought and will. The feeling in prayer was real. The work is steady governing, not self-condemnation, and change in the heart itself is slow and partly given.

**Where they differ.** On transformation: Tanya and Biurei Tanya call hating evil a gift, not your job, while Biurei Tanya (quoting the Rashab) says real service is when your wants actually change, and Torah Ohr treats forcing as the road to transformation. On mood: Tanya says rejoice in each turning, while Derech Mitzvosecha asks for bitterness over the arousal alongside joy. On explanation: Tanya speaks of the in-between person, Toras Chaim of an exile-dream fusing two true things.

**Order and timing.** Choose at the first moment. If it has taken hold, turn away and remember you don't want to be cut off. Afterward, count the turning and be glad. Daily in prayer, bend the heart, with bitterness always joined to joy. Over time, build habit and renew the will. Judge progress over years by what you want, not by what you feel in prayer.

- Addiction and compulsive behavior need treatment. 'Govern the garments' alone may not be enough. - For intrusive thoughts in OCD, 'turn your mind away at once' can become a struggle with thoughts that makes them worse. Get clinical guidance. - Depressed people should never be given the bitterness half without the joy. - Ordinary human desires must not be framed as evil to someone outside the religious frame. Translate this as the gap between wanting and choosing. - 'Don't believe the world's praise' must not feed impostor feelings. It means not ranking yourself a tzaddik, not believing you are bad. - Urges to harm anyone need protection and professional help, not only self-governance.

**Truths for the guide** *(candidates, each checked against its passages; see CHECKS.md)*

- **What rises, and what you do** · You are not answerable for what rises in you, only for what you do, say and dwell on. Each time you turn away is a win, not proof that you are weak. *Check: yes.*
- **Both are true** · The you that is moved in prayer and the you that wants the world an hour later are both real. The split is the human condition, not a lie. *Check: yes.*

**What the core works do not cover.** None of these works deals with addiction or compulsion. Impostor feelings about social or work image, apart from spiritual rank, are not addressed. There is no guidance for secular people on telling harmful desires from ordinary ones. The craving part of TC1:P10 (in C01) belongs here.

<a id="c32"></a> ## C32 · My desires make me bad at the core

*5 findings from Likkutei Torah (part 1 of 3), Likkutei Torah (part 3 of 3), Kuntres Umaayan, Sha'arei Avodah, Pelach HaRimon*

> My cravings pull me toward physical pleasure and feel like an enemy living inside me. My feelings seem to drag me away from God. People like me must be worse than animals.

**What is really going on.** The works separate the power of desire from the deed. Likkutei Torah (Alter Rebbe): the desiring power is the same whether aimed at the permitted or the forbidden; its root is in supernal holiness and it fell through the breaking of the vessels; only the deed is evil. Turned, it becomes love of God 'with both inclinations', and an evil angel turned to good surpasses the good. Love is an act of will, and will governs the emotions. Pelach HaRimon (R. Hillel of Paritch): the animal soul's core is only desire, with a root higher than the divine soul's; seeing this brings compassion for both souls and unites two wills. Sha'arei Avodah: every feeling has an inner side that is God's power. Kuntres Umaayan: bad traits come from the body; the soul in itself is supremely high, and the highest soul is given the coarsest matter to refine. The methods are mainly prayer-based.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"My strong desires prove I'm evil at the root."* → The desiring power in itself is rooted in supernal holiness; it came to be drawn toward sin only through a fall. The inner core of the animal soul is not evil, only the power of desire. `LT3·Q02` · `PR·Q25`
- *"My cravings are the enemy, and I have to destroy them."* → The craving itself can be turned to love of God, and once turned it surpasses what was always good, like the advantage of light out of darkness. `LT1·Q23` · `LT1·Q24` · `PR·Q26`
- *"Love of God is a feeling, and I just don't have it."* → The emotions depend on the will, and the love is clothed and hidden inside the very craving for physical things. `LT1·Q30` · `LT1·Q31`
- *"My sadness, fear and pleasures pull me away from God."* → Every feeling has an inner side; outward fear can become awe, sadness a broken and humble heart or joy, anger turned at the evil itself. `SA·Q20` · `SA·Q21`
- *"I've sunk so low; I'm worse than an animal."* → The soul in itself is supremely high and can rise step after step. The highest soul is given the coarsest body because it can refine it. `KU·Q16` · `KU·Q17`

1. **Turn the desire itself in prayer** · *from Likkutei Torah* (1) In the morning prayer, contemplate God's greatness in depth through the psalms of praise. (2) Continue through the blessings of Shema to 'One' and 'love with all your heart', meaning with both inclinations. (3) Know that love is an act of will, and will governs the emotions. (4) Let the love take up the craving until the craving itself turns to want God, the source of all delight. *When:* Daily morning prayer. *For:* Someone fighting physical cravings. *Not for:* Not a promise that desire vanishes quickly; the text speaks of lifelong refining. Not a substitute for treatment of sexual compulsion or addiction. `LT1·Q23` · `LT1·Q30` · `LT1·Q31`
2. **Compassion instead of war** · *from Pelach HaRimon, Likkutei Torah* (1) Know the root: the power of desire is holy at its source (Pelach HaRimon: higher even than the divine soul's) and fell far, like a stone thrown from a height. (2) Let compassion arise for both sides of you, the fallen drive and the soul clothed in a body, and for the part of God in you that fell. (3) If a cry comes from the broken heart, let it; it is born of pity, not self-attack. (4) Let the two opposed wills become one simple will toward the Father in Heaven. (5) Then refine the emotional traits one by one. *When:* The Omer period (Pesach to Shavuos) for the trait-by-trait work; the Days of Repentance and daily prayer for the compassion. *For:* Someone at war with his own drives who needs a way that is not self-hatred. *Not for:* Someone unsteady asked to 'break' himself; someone in whom compassion for the soul slides into despair; anyone pressed to weep. `PR·Q25` · `PR·Q26` · `PR·Q27` · `LT3·Q02` · `LT3·Q11` · `LT3·Q16`
3. **Look at the inside of every feeling** · *from Sha'arei Avodah* (1) In eating and pleasure, look past the outside to the inner taste, and delight in God. (2) In joy, rejoice in God. (3) In sadness or outward fear, turn to awe by seeing that all is Him alone, or turn the sadness to joy. (4) Aim anger and hatred at the evil itself. (5) Do this through a general awareness of unity, not special intentions for every act. *When:* Throughout the day, in ordinary activity. *For:* Anyone who feels daily emotions as rivals to devotion. *Not for:* Not a way to bypass grief or clinical depression. Anger aimed at 'the evil' must never become hostility toward people or oneself. `SA·Q20` · `SA·Q21`

- From *"My strong desires prove I'm evil at the root."* to **The power to desire is holy at its root. Only the act is bad, and the same power can become love of God.** The drive is lifted back to its root and redirected rather than destroyed. *(Likkutei Torah, Pelach HaRimon)* `LT3·Q02` · `PR·Q25` - From *"My cravings are the enemy, and love of God is a feeling I just don't have."* to **Love is a matter of will, and will rules the emotions. The craving itself, turned toward God, becomes 'very good'.** Because the desire has a holy root it can be transformed, not only defeated, and redirecting the will moves the heart. *(Likkutei Torah)* `LT1·Q30` · `LT1·Q31` · `LT1·Q23` - From *"At best I can hold the craving down."* to **A drive turned toward good surpasses what was always good, like light out of darkness.** The strength of the drive becomes the measure of what it can become. *(Likkutei Torah)* `LT1·Q24` - From *"My animal side is my enemy, and I have to fight it."* to **Pity both sides; compassion joins two opposed wills into one.** Compassion takes the place of war, and the unity it produces is what lifts the drive. *(Pelach HaRimon)* `PR·Q26` · `PR·Q27` - From *"My feelings are the enemy of my devotion."* to **Every feeling has an inside that is His power. The same energy can turn toward Him.** Looking at the inner side turns delight, joy, fear, sadness and anger into their holy counterparts instead of fighting them. *(Sha'arei Avodah)* `SA·Q20` · `SA·Q21` - From *"I've fallen so far I must be worse than others."* to **The higher the soul, the further it falls. The highest soul is given the coarsest matter so it can refine it.** The depth of the fall becomes evidence of height, and the soul can climb step after step. *(Kuntres Umaayan)* `KU·Q16` · `KU·Q17`

**Where the works agree.** All separate the drive from the deed and the self from its cravings: desire is holy or neutral at root, and the aim is to turn it, not destroy it. Likkutei Torah and Pelach HaRimon agree on a holy root and on compassion rather than war; Sha'arei Avodah and Likkutei Torah agree that a feeling's energy can be redirected toward God.

**Where they differ.** On the root: Likkutei Torah places desire's root in supernal holiness; Pelach HaRimon puts the animal soul's root above the divine soul's; Kuntres Umaayan instead locates bad traits in the body and exalts the soul. On method: contemplation in prayer until love takes up the craving (Likkutei Torah); compassion uniting two wills, then trait by trait (Pelach HaRimon); moment-to-moment inward looking in daily life (Sha'arei Avodah).

**Order and timing.** First the reframe: the drive is not the self and is holy at its root. Then daily prayer that turns the will; compassion for both sides rather than attack; the trait-by-trait work over weeks. Through the day, look at the inside of each feeling. The deed stays under restraint throughout.

- Not permission to act on desires; the texts keep the deed as what can be evil. - Sexual compulsion, addiction and eating disorders need clinical care; 'turn the desire' does not replace it. - Desires to harm others are not material for reframing; they need firm boundaries and professional help. - Grief and depression: turning sadness into 'broken heart and lowliness', or into joy, is not treatment and can dismiss real pain. - 'Hate and be angry at the evil' must not be turned against people or against oneself. - The 'body of coarse matter' language can harm someone with body shame or an eating disorder. - 'The deeper the fall, the higher the soul' must not romanticize self-destruction.

Regret that reaches as deep as the pleasure uproots it.** The desire lives in will and pleasure at a certain depth, and pain at the same depth takes the will away. *(Sha'arei Teshuvah (Mitteler Rebbe))* `MST·Q09` · `MST·Q10` - From *"I'm trapped, it's my fault, so there's no way out."* to **Some pull is an involuntary human residue, a light matter. Even the fully entrenched have a remedy.** Separating residue from cultivated habit eases blanket guilt, and a sure remedy restores agency. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS3·Q26` · `LS3·Q24` - From *"It feels good, so it's good for me."* to **Is this my good at the level of my soul? What I bind myself to, I become.** Seeing the pull as a descent rather than a gain removes its claim to be good. *(Kuntres Umaayan)* `KU·Q06` · `KU·Q07` - From *"A glance means nothing to me."* to **The pull shows where your hidden delight is attached, and the glance leaves a mark even if you feel nothing.** Delight is stirred through sight at a level where there is no felt reaction, so the coolness hides how deep it goes. *(Kuntres HaAvodah)* `KA·Q02` - From *"It came back again, so I've failed."* to **The pull renews every day, so each day needs fresh strength, and the pressure itself can wake a firmer resolve.** The daily return is the shape of the work, not a verdict, and opposition is what strengthens the power to win. *(Likkutei Torah, Toras Chaim (Shemos))* `LT2·Q31` · `TC3·Q29` · `TC3·Q30`

**Where the works agree.** All agree that reasoning alone does not win in the moment of pull. There is a point in the self the craving cannot cover: the point that doesn't want to be cut off, the soul's essence, what is above will. Action can be governed even when the heart can't. Indulgence strengthens the pull, and steady opposite action weakens it over time.

**Where they differ.** They differ on where the strength comes from. For Kuntres Umaayan it is the person's own essential stubbornness. For Sha'arei Avodah it is mercy from above, because the captive will cannot free itself. For Sha'arei Teshuvah it is regret reaching the depth of the pleasure. For Biurei Tanya it is remembering the love, not contemplating. Kuntres Umaayan and Kuntres HaAvodah are rigorous ('do the opposite'). Likkutei Sichos softens this with the idea of an involuntary residue.

**Order and timing.** Guard the senses and choose at the first moment. If the pull takes hold, don't argue: act the opposite and remember the point beneath. If even the will is captive, cry out. Afterward, daily return and fresh fire in morning prayer. For those not yet sunk, the honest question.

- None of this replaces addiction treatment, medical care or support groups, especially for substances, gambling or anything dangerous. - 'Where the inclination says yes, say no' and 'whatever you want by nature, don't do' can harm people with OCD, eating disorders or scrupulosity. - The deep-bitterness practice (regret as deep as the pleasure, models of loathing life) is not for anyone depressed or at risk of self-harm. - Intrusive, unwanted thoughts are not cravings. They should not be met with confession or self-accusation. - Guarding the eyes must not become shame about one's body or constant anxious self-monitoring. - Relapse must not be read as proof of being hopelessly bad. The texts say the pull renews daily and that even the entrenched have a remedy.
