This is the second half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Vayikra: Yom Kippur, the forbidden foods and unions, Kedoshim, the laws of the kohanim, the festivals, the menorah and the showbread, shemitah and yovel, the blessings and the curses, and the vows of value. Much is law, and a large part is left out under D28. But some of the Maharal's most useful teachings for daily life are here.

Six teachings matter most for the guide:

- **A blessing opens the flow.** Why must one bless before eating? "כי במה שהארץ וכל אשר בה לה', שם קודש חל עליו" (589): "since the earth and all in it are G-d's, the name of holiness rests on it." The blessing "עולה למדריגה אשר שם השפע והברכה להשפיע" (589): "rises to the level where the flow and the blessing pour out." "כי בברכה הוא יתברך נותן הארץ לבני אדם, על ידי ברכתו והשפעתו" (589): "through the blessing He gives the earth to people."
- **Holiness is setting apart, and it lifts above the world.** "שכל קדוש פורש עצמו מדבר שאינו ראוי" (530): "whoever is holy separates himself from what is not fit." And one who gives himself for His Name "הוא קדוש, ובשביל שהוא קדוש הוא נבדל מן העולם" (680): "is holy, and because he is holy he is set apart from the world." So a miracle is done for him, "שהוא למעלה מן העולם" (680): "which is above the world."
- **Everything returns to its first standing.** "כי היובל ויום הכפורים שניהם דבר אחד" (746): "the yovel and Yom Kippur are one thing." On Yom Kippur "שהוא יתברך מכפר להם, וחוזרים לחזקתם הראשונה" (746): "He atones for them, and they return to their first standing."
- **He is joined to us even in exile.** "I will bring them into the land of their enemies." Why "I"? "וכיון שהוא יביאם בעצמו, אם כן הוא יתברך דבוק בהם" (843): "since He brings them Himself, He is joined to them."
- **His care is in everything.** Those who deny His care say He does not know details. The Torah shows "כי השגחתו בכל דבר" (583): "that His care is in everything."
- **Giving to the poor is an offering.** One who leaves the gleanings for the poor is as if he built the Temple and brought an offering. "הרי נתינתו לעני נתינה להשם יתברך, וזהו כאילו הקריב קרבן" (704): "his giving to the poor is giving to G-d, and it is as if he brought an offering."

## 2.1 The earth is His; the blessing opens the flow

This is the fullest passage in the range. The sages say whoever enjoys this world without a blessing has misused what is holy: "כל הנהנה מן העולם בלא ברכה מעל" (589). Why? "כי במה שהארץ וכל אשר בה לה', שם קודש חל עליו" (589). Everything is His, so everything carries His Name, like a thing given to the Temple. The blessing does not take the holiness away. It lifts the one who blesses: "נמצא כי המברך על הפרי עולה למדריגה אשר שם השפע והברכה להשפיע" (589). At that level He is the Giver, and the thing is given. "כי בברכה הוא יתברך נותן הארץ לבני אדם, על ידי ברכתו והשפעתו" (589). And "כי אז יש ברכה המשפיעה בלי שיעור" (589): "then there is blessing that pours out without measure."

So one fruit is seen from two sides. From the side of "the earth is G-d's," it is His and holy. From the side of His giving, it is given to us. The blessing moves the person from the first side to the second.

## 2.2 His care is in everything

"כי המכחישין מכחישין בהשגחת השם יתברך ובידיעתו, שאין לו ידיעה בפרטים כלל" (583): "the deniers deny His care and knowledge, saying He has no knowledge of details at all." They say details are by chance, or too low for Him. The Torah answers by choosing the most particular and lowest example it can, "להודיע כי השגחתו בכל דבר" (583): "to make known that His care is in everything." (The example itself is left out under D28; only the rule is used.)

## 2.3 Holiness is being set apart

"שכל קדוש פורש עצמו מדבר שאינו ראוי" (530). "כי הפרישה מדברים אשר הם פחותים והם אינם אנושים נקרא זה קדושה" (591): "separating from things that are low and not human is called holiness." In the Maharal's thought, G-d is the one who is set apart (Shemot 65, 381). So a person who sets himself apart becomes like Him in this.

## 2.4 Set apart, so above nature

"כלל הדבר, שהאדם אשר אין שוקל חיות העולם נגד קדושת שמו יתברך" (680): "a person who does not weigh the life of this world against the holiness of His Name." "הוא קדוש, ובשביל שהוא קדוש הוא נבדל מן העולם" (680). Since he is set apart from the world, the world's order does not bind him: "ועושה לו נס, שהוא למעלה מן העולם" (680). "כי הנס הוא גם כן קדושה לשמו יתברך" (680): "the miracle too is holiness for His Name."

## 2.5 Everything returns to its first standing

The yovel shofar is sounded on Yom Kippur. "כי היובל ויום הכפורים שניהם דבר אחד, כי היובל הוא חזרת הכל לחזקתו הראשונה להיות כבראשונה" (746): "the yovel is everything going back to its first standing, to be as at first." "וכן יום הכפורים הכל חוזר לחזקתו הראשון, שהוא יתברך מכפר להם, וחוזרים לחזקתם הראשונה" (746). And "אבל שופר של יום הכפורים הוא להשמיע שופר גאולה לקרוא דרור" (746): "the shofar of Yom Kippur is a shofar of redemption, to call out freedom." The land goes back to its owner; the person goes back to G-d; the soul goes back to how it was.

## 2.6 He is joined to us in exile

"I will bring them into the land of their enemies." The Maharal reads "I" as the good measure: He does not send them off by another. "וכיון שהוא יביאם בעצמו, אם כן הוא יתברך דבוק בהם, ולא יניח [את] ישראל להפרד מהם" (843): "since He brings them Himself, He is joined to them, and He will not leave Israel to be parted from them." Even exile is His own act, so He is in it with them.

## 2.7 Shabbat holds the festivals

"שהמועדים הם חלקי השביתה, והשבת הוא כולל כל השביתה" (682): "the festivals are parts of the rest, and Shabbat includes all the rest." The seven festival days stand under the seventh day. And Shabbat is a stop that starts a new count: "כי השבת היא שביתה ותכלית, וראוי לגרום התחלת חשבון" (689): "Shabbat is rest and completion, and it is fit to begin a count."

## 2.8 Shabbat testifies

"שזהו כבודו לקיים שבת, המעיד על שהוא ברא העולם" (538): "keeping Shabbat is His honor, since it testifies that He created the world." So a father may not tell his son to break Shabbat: both are bound to His honor.

- **MH-S58 "Back to the first standing" (746).** "וכן יום הכפורים הכל חוזר לחזקתו הראשון, שהוא יתברך מכפר להם, וחוזרים לחזקתם הראשונה" (746). Inside: after return, I am not a patched-up version of myself. I am back to how I was at first, as land goes back to its owner in the yovel. How to enter: at the close of Yom Kippur, or after any real return, say: "I am back in my first place with Him." It differs from S48 Returning, which is the turning. This is the arrival.
- **MH-S59 "He is with us in exile" (843).** "וכיון שהוא יביאם בעצמו, אם כן הוא יתברך דבוק בהם" (843). Inside: the hard place I am in is not a place He sent me away to. He brought me there Himself, so He is there. How to enter: in a far or hard place, say: "You brought me here Yourself, so You are here with me." It differs from MH-S48 Close in weakness, which is about the sick. This is about being far from home.
- **MH-S60 "All is His, then given" (589).** "כי בברכה הוא יתברך נותן הארץ לבני אדם, על ידי ברכתו והשפעתו" (589). Inside: before I eat, I see the food as His, holy. When I bless, I see Him giving it to me now. How to enter: hold the food a moment before the blessing and think: this is His. Then say the blessing slowly and think: now He gives it to me. It differs from S65 Given right now, which sees all as given at each moment. Here the blessing is the turn from "His" to "given."

## 4.1 Bless, and receive (`mh_bless_then_receive`, new)

This is the practice for MH-S60 (589). Before eating or drinking water or enjoying any good thing, stop. Say: "This is Yours." Then say the blessing, and think: "Now You give it to me." Eat as one who has been given a gift.

## 4.2 Urge yourself where you are slow (`mh_urge_where_slow`, new)

The Torah puts fear of mother before father, since people fear the mother less. "שלעולם בכל מצוה שאין האדם זריז בה מעצמו, מלמד לך הכתוב שצריך האדם לזרז בה" (535): "in every mitzvah where a person is not quick by himself, he must urge himself on." Practice: find the mitzvah you are slowest in. That is where to put your effort this week. The ones you do by nature need less.

## 4.3 Hold him before he falls (`mh_hold_before_fall`, new)

"אל תניחהו שירד" (784): "do not let him go down." "שיחזיק אותו שלא יפול" (784): "hold him up so he does not fall." Practice: when you see someone start to slip, in money or spirit, help now, before they fall. A small help now is worth more than a big help later.

## 4.4 Giving as an offering (`mh_gift_as_offering`, new)

"הרי נתינתו לעני נתינה להשם יתברך, וזהו כאילו הקריב קרבן" (704). Why is a commanded gift so great? "כי המצווה ממלא רצון השם יתברך" (704): "the one commanded fills G-d's will." The Maharal's parable: one builds the king a house, the other plants him a garden; the house is what the king needs. Practice: when you give tzedakah, think: "I am doing His will, and this goes to Him." Give as one brings an offering.

## 4.5 Walk deeper (`mh_walk_deeper`, new)

"כן יהיה עמל בתורה ולהעמיק בה תמיד יותר ממה שהעמיק בה קודם. וזה נקרא הליכה בתורה" (798): "he will toil in Torah and go deeper always, more than before; this is called walking in Torah." And for the statutes: "אלא שאין יכול להבין אותם לגמרי, אבל יכול להבין מהם מעט" (798): "he cannot understand them fully, but he can understand a little." Practice: return to one thing you have learned before. Look for one layer you did not see last time. Do not wait to understand it all.

## 4.6 Learn in order to do (`mh_learn_to_do`, new)

"שהלמוד על מנת לעשות הרי הוא שומר הלמוד בתוך לבו כדי לקיים אותו במעשה" (799): "learning in order to do keeps the learning in his heart." Practice: at the end of each learning, pick one thing to do from it today.

## 4.7 Stop, then begin the count (`mh_stop_then_count`, new)

"שאחר כל הפסק" (689) comes "התחלת מנין" (689): "after every stop comes the start of a count." Practice: at the end of each Shabbat, begin the week as a new count. Ask: what will I count toward this week? Let the rest of Shabbat be the start, not just a break.

## 4.8 Pay, and ask forgiveness too (`mh_ask_forgiveness_too`, new)

One who wounds another pays, and more: "ויבקש ממנו מחילה וימחול לו" (729): "he must ask his forgiveness, and the other forgives him." Practice: when you have hurt someone, making it good in money or deeds is not the end. Go and ask them to forgive you.

## 4.9 It is not yours (`mh_not_yours`, new)

At the yovel the field goes back. "אל תרע עינך בה שאינה שלך" (764): "do not begrudge it, for it is not yours." Practice: when you must give back or let go of something, say: "It was never mine. It is His." Let go without a bad eye.

## 4.10 Holy for His Name (`mh_count_as_nothing`, extended)

"כלל הדבר, שהאדם אשר אין שוקל חיות העולם נגד קדושת שמו יתברך" (680). Add to the practice: the one who does not weigh this world against His Name is set apart from the world, and what is above the world comes to him. This is for a person's own choices of holiness in daily life. It is never a reason to seek danger.

## 4.11 Suffering as a call back, for oneself only (`mh_return_today`, extended)

"ואם יעשה תשובה" (636), "מרפא אותו, שלא הכה אותו רק בשביל שיחזור מחטאו" (636): "He heals him, for He struck him only so that he would turn from his sin." And "לא יביא עליהם יותר ממה שחטאו" (825): "He will not bring more than they sinned." Add to the practice: when something hard comes to you, ask: is there something I should return from? Then return, and trust that the One who struck heals. Guard: this is only for oneself. It is never said to another person about their suffering.

## 5. The big human questions

**Is my food mine?** It is His. Through the blessing He gives it to you (589).

**Does G-d care about small things?** His care is in everything (583).

**What is holiness?** Being set apart from what is not fit and not human (530, 591). The one set apart for His Name is above the world's order (680).

**Can I really start over?** On Yom Kippur all returns to its first standing, as the land returns in the yovel (746).

**Where is G-d when I am far away?** He brought you there Himself, so He is joined to you there (843).

**Why do I suffer?** For oneself only: He strikes only to bring back, never more than the sin, and He heals the one who returns (636, 825, 830).

**How do I learn Torah?** Walk in it: go deeper each time (798). Learn in order to do (799).

**What does giving mean?** Giving to the poor is giving to G-d, like an offering (704).

**Who owns what I have?** The land is not yours but His (764).

**What holds a home and a people together?** Peace holds everything up (804).

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **The blessing** (589) is close to Tanya's teaching that every thing's life is the word of G-d in it, and that eating with a blessing lifts the spark in the food (Tanya ch. 7-8; Shaar HaYichud ch. 1). The Maharal speaks of two sides, "His" and "given," and the blessing moves the person from one to the other. Chabad speaks of the spark lifted. Both say the blessing changes how the thing stands before G-d.
- **His care in everything** (583) is the Baal Shem Tov's particular providence, which Chabad teaches for every leaf (Shaar HaYichud ch. 2, Keter Shem Tov). The Maharal adds that the deniers' two reasons, chance and lowness, are both answered.
- **Tzimtzum.** "The earth is G-d's" before the blessing and "given to people" after (589) is close to Chabad's two views: from His side all is Him; from our side the world stands apart (Tanya ch. 48; Shaar HaYichud ch. 7). The Maharal does not say the world is hidden. He says it is holy and His, and the blessing opens His giving.
- **The Rambam's negative attributes.** "Holiness is being set apart" (530, 680) is the Maharal's constant theme: G-d is the one set apart (Shemot 65). The Rambam's G-d is known by what He is not. The Maharal makes this a path: the holy person is set apart from the world and so is like Him.
- **The Ramak's formula** ("He is all, and all is not He"). "The earth and all in it are G-d's" (589) is the first half. The one set apart from the world (680) points to the second: holiness is not the world.
- **Back to the first standing** (746) fits Tanya's Iggeret HaTeshuva ch. 4 and 7: return reaches the soul's root, which was never damaged. The Maharal gives the image of the yovel.
- **He is joined to us in exile** (843) is the teaching that the Shechina goes into exile with Israel (Megillah 29a), which Tanya uses (ch. 17 and Iggeret HaKodesh 25). Both say the exile is not His absence.
- **Walk deeper** (798) fits the Chabad way of study: depth (ha'amakah) and returning again and again (Tanya ch. 42 and the Rebbe's call to learn with depth).
