"The Ten Utterances" is the first of the three parts the Shelah promised in Toldot Adam. Where Toldot Adam built the theory (man in the image, the Torah as the Name, service as a need on high), this part is a **ladder of the inner life**. He lists ten "utterances", "all included in one utterance: He is the true One" (line 838), and then climbs them:

"The tenth gate, holy to G-d: the Presence in the heart, as it says I have set G-d before me always; its end is fixed in its beginning" (line 865). The ladder starts with "Hashem is One" and ends with the One in the heart.

## 1.1 "There is no other existence besides His"

The Shelah's own statement of the unity in this range (line 878) is stronger than the one in Toldot Adam. He contrasts a common view with "the truth of faith as I see it". The common view: G-d made everything from nothing, then gave the heavens power to run the world, "and as it were His hand moved away from them", until He chooses to intervene (line 877). The truth:

> הש"י מחדש בטובו בכל יום תמיד מעשה בראשית בכוונה מכוונת שופע שפעו, ואלו היה מונע רגע אחד היה הכל כלא היה בטל המציאות (line 878)

"G-d in His goodness renews the work of creation every day, always, pouring His flow on purpose; if He held it back one moment, all would be as if it never was." And he reads "there is none else" (Deuteronomy 4:39) through it:

> שאין עוד מציאות בעולם זולת מציאותו יתברך, כי בהסתרו יאבד הכל (line 878)

"There is no other existence in the world besides His existence, for if He hid Himself all would be lost." The stars have only "power" in the sense of readiness, "as the eye is ready to see when sight flows into it" (line 878). The conclusion: "there is no power but His power; power is His alone" (line 884).

This is almost word for word what the Tanya's Gate of Unity says in chapters 1-3 (the word of G-d must stand in each thing at every moment, or it would return to nothing) and in chapter 6 on "ein od". The Shelah even argues from the same verse. Chabad takes it one step further (the creature is "nothing" before Him even while it exists); the Shelah stops at "no existence besides His" held by constant renewal. For the guide, this is the plainest pre-Chassidic statement of F01 and F02 we have.

## 1.2 The Essence pierces all

In the meditation on greatness the Shelah copies the Pardes:

> כי עצמותו נוקב ויורד דרך הספירות ותוך הספירות ובין הספירות (line 1049)

"His Essence pierces and descends through the sefirot, within the sefirot and between the sefirot", and on through chariots, angels, spheres and elements "to the last point in the depths". "He is the life of the sefirot, the life of the chariots, the life of the angels and the life of all creation" (line 1048). This is memale (F04, filling) said with the word atzmut (Essence). The Shelah's own line just before it (1048) turns it into a practice (see 3.1).

## 1.3 Nothing separates but sin

"There is no separation between Him and us; only sin separates" (line 1007). "The great cleaving, He to them and they to Him, brings that providence with no go-between" (line 968). In the dance at the end of time no angel stands between (line 996).

## 1.4 Knowing it in the heart

The first utterance is "know today and return it to your heart" (F12). The Shelah insists on a knowing that is proven and held in the heart, "beyond what was received from one's fathers" (line 868). He reads "this is my G-d and I will glorify Him" (ואנוהו) as *ani vaHu*, "I and He":

> אני והו דבוקים ביחד כביכול, כי הידיעה נתפסת בלב (line 868)

"I and He, as it were, cleaving together, for the knowing is held in the heart." G-d known only by tradition stays "high above him and I am far from Him in the depth of the heart" (line 868). Knowing brings closeness; received faith alone leaves distance.

## 3.1 Up and down the worlds (the Shelah's own contemplation)

The clearest contemplative instruction in the range (lines 1029-1049, 1142). First go **up**: the earth and its wonders (minerals, plants, "there is no painter like our G-d", animals), then the sea around it, then air, then fire, then each sphere, and of each: "there is no comparison ... not in size and not in fineness" (line 1040); then angels; Yetzirah; Beriah; Atzilut, which even Moshe did not grasp. Then the Shelah says:

> אתה בן אדם תלך בשכלך מלמטה למעלה עד רום המעלות רבון העולמים, אז תתמלא יראה (line 1048)

"You, son of man, walk in your mind from below upward to the height of heights, and you will be filled with awe." Then **down**:

> אחר כך תלך בשכלך מלמעלה למטה והתבונן כי אדם יחיד הנקרא אין סוף מתפשט בכולן (line 1048)

"Then walk in your mind from above downward and consider that the single Master called Ein Sof spreads through all of them." His short form (line 1142): everything is as a mustard seed before the spheres, the spheres as nothing before the angel, "all are as if they never were before the throne, and the throne before His glory; He is the cause of all causes and there is none but Him."

*What it is for:* inner fear, then (by turning to His humility) inner love. *Guard:* do not ask what is above Atzilut (line 903). *Tie:* this is technique `walk_down` and `breadth` in Chabad form, and very close to the Tanya ch. 33 and the Gate of Unity. The new element is the double movement, up to awe and down to presence. *Proposed technique:* "Up the worlds and back down".

## 3.2 Gratitude through the body

The Semag's list (line 1053): think of each part of the body and its use, eyes, eyelids ("to close the eyes and sleep, and not to look at evil"), ears, nose, mouth, teeth, tongue, throat, heart, stomach, liver, kidneys, spleen, bones, veins, skin, skull, hair, navel, hands, feet. Then the Shelah's own parable: if a doctor had cured your blindness you would love him all your life; "all this He gave you as a free gift; how will you not love Him with a whole heart" (line 1054). *For:* inner love. *Tie:* the guide's being-held work and S53 (glad). *Proposed technique:* "The body's inventory of gifts".

## 3.3 The redeemed captive

"Let him picture himself as if he were a captive, stricken, beaten, poor and crushed" (line 915), and a great king ransomed him, healed him, raised him and gave him his only daughter. That is the Exodus and the Torah. Said with the words "Blessed is our G-d who created us for His glory". *For:* love, and service out of thanks. *Tie:* `picturing` (the Piaseczner's technique); here as a morning practice.

## 3.4 Eye to eye at the Kedushah

"Raise your eyes to heaven in your house of prayer when you say Holy" (line 982, from the Heichalot). *For:* being seen and delighted in. A small, concrete act the guide can teach.

## 3.5 The Presence before you (shiviti)

"A person should remember always and not let his thought move from remembering G-d" (line 1281). The Rambam: you do not sit or speak alone as you do before a great king. Three ways to hold it: - **The dilemma** (line 1283): when tempted, ask: do I believe He sees me? If not, I deny Him, and I would rather die than that. If yes, "how can I be so brazen as to defy the King to His face, while His eyes see?" - **Facing you** (line 1287, from R. Ephraim, the Shelah's teacher): "picture in your mind the Presence truly facing you", so that your heart, on your left, is at His right, and the heart rules over the liver, seat of desire; "therefore my heart is glad". - **Above you** (lines 1288-1294): the fully righteous feels the Presence above his head, "hand under hand, foot under foot", limb holding limb; the penitent sets the Presence *facing* him, so his left becomes His right (sins become merits). Either way, "only the knowing and the bond with G-d should not stop for one moment" (line 1294).

*Tie:* S33 (before Whom), technique `before_whom`, `carry`. The two postures (above the head for the whole, facing for the one who returns) are new to the guide.

## 3.6 The tipping world

"Let him think the whole world is exactly balanced and a small thing will tip it" (line 1088); "this is wonderful advice: at every moment of a person's life, remember that the world is half and half" (line 1090). *For:* weight in small acts. *Guard (ours):* used without the Shelah's love it can become anxiety; he places it inside fear-from-love, not before it.

## 3.7 Prayer with a heart that does not wander

"For the heart's intent not to turn here and there to any thought, only to the intent of the prayer, and to be glad and cleaving to Him, is great service"; "a person must train himself much so that his heart cleaves always" (line 1107). *Tie:* `binding` (returning the wandering mind). The Shelah puts the difficulty plainly: the heart's service is the hard one.

## 3.8 Turning the traits

Repeated from Toldot Adam and taken further (lines 1189-1190): envy becomes the zeal of Pinchas or of scholars, desire becomes longing for "the hidden treasure" and "my soul yearns for Your laws", hatred becomes hatred of evil, even honor has its holy use. "The impurity itself turns into purity" (line 1189). Return is conquering; "good deeds" is turning the bad deeds themselves good, so "sins become merits". And the parallel for prohibitions (lines 1057-1059, his father's teaching): the Torah gave a permitted twin for every forbidden taste, so that we know the desire and give it up for His honor.

## 3.9 Being alone with the Maker

From Tomer Devorah under Chochmah (line 1252): wisdom has two faces, "his being alone with his Maker, to add to his wisdom", and teaching others. The word is *hisbodedus* (being alone with Him). Kabbalah study does the same: "the soul grows strong and is alone with its Maker" (line 894), and "whoever turns his heart from the world's business and gazes at the work of the chariot is accepted as if he prayed all day" (line 892). *Tie:* `own_words`, `hold`.

## 3.10 A Torah of one's own

A striking line of the Shelah's own (lines 1220-1222): the Torah could not set a measure for eating, sleeping or speaking, because "each person differs in thousands of ways, so each person would need a Torah of his own". So it gave a general command, "be holy", and "when you feel it is excess, make yourself holy." This is binding, not extra piety, and it is personal. For the guide this is a real principle: the measure is found by each person, honestly, in each season.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Suffering.** The fifth utterance is "eternal service": thank Him "for every measure He measures you" (line 1157). Four reasons from four sages (lines 1168-1180): suffering wakes a person to return and clears the mind of the world's fog (Ran); what looks bad is good we cannot see (the merchant held back by a thorn when the ship sank; "do what is good in *Your* eyes", "answer me with Your *true* salvation"); it saves from worse; the suffering of love repairs a flaw above. "A person errs and thinks this is good" (line 1170). "Even in our sickness we accept in love" (line 1133). Nachum Ish Gamzu: "this too is for good" (line 1247). *Kept honest:* the Shelah prays against suffering sent as vengeance (line 1181).
- **Being held.** "If one of them is sick, he watches over him more" (line 875): providence is greatest for the weakest. The stars in their billions "I created only for you, and you said: You forgot me" (line 1041).
- **Love and awe.** The ladder in section 2. Love grows from gratitude and from seeing greatness joined to humility: "wherever you find G-d's greatness, there you find His humility" (line 1117). Fear grows from shame before greatness, and then again from love.
- **Joy.** The Ari "merited holy spirit because he was very glad, with great joy, in doing the mitzvot" (line 1055). Service without joy has nothing to cling with (line 864).
- **Thought.** A good thought counts as a deed (line 1193); a passing bad thought is not counted, but dwelling on it is worse than the act (line 1275). Keep the mind clean even of a glance (line 1279).
- **Why the evil inclination.** "Even the evil inclination in him is for his good" (line 1144), because conquering it lifts him above the angels.

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **Renewal and "ein od".** Agreement, nearly verbatim, with the Gate of Unity chapters 1-3 and 6 (line 878). The Shelah even rejects the same wrong view (that G-d's hand "moved away" after creation) that the Tanya's Gate of Unity rejects in its opening chapters (the craftsman and the vessel). Difference: the Shelah's "no existence besides His" is carried by the flow's constancy; the Tanya adds that the creature is bittul, nothing, even now.
- **Fear and love.** The Shelah's inner fear (the Zohar's "great and ruling ... all before Him count as nothing", line 1023) is the Tanya's ch. 41-43 lower fear from contemplating greatness. His "fear within the within", which comes after love and fears for the union above, is close to the Tanya's higher fear (yirah ila'ah), but the object differs: the Tanya's higher fear is bitul before the Essence; the Shelah's is fear for the Name's wholeness, tzorech gavoah.
- **Servant above son.** The Shelah ranks "servant of the King" above "son" (line 1131), because "King" belongs to G-d's own being "even before the world was created" (line 1127), while "Father" belongs to the chain of cause and effect. Chabad often ranks the son's bond higher, and speaks of the servant's bittul too; here the two meet: the top servant is the one with no self left, for whom even reward is service.
- **Kabbalah study as duty.** The Shelah urges it strongly (lines 885-906), with guards: after filling oneself with Talmud and law, with holiness and humility. Chabad makes the teaching of the inner Torah the path; the Shelah stands at its door.
- **Particularism (kept, not carried).** The spiritual body at Sinai is "only for Israel" (lines 953, 957), and the rule that a good thought counts as a deed is reversed for other nations (line 1270). The guide does not carry these.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **"No existence besides His"** (line 878) as the Shelah's plain sentence for F01 and F02, in a voice before Chassidus.
2. **Up the worlds and back down** as a full contemplation with a clear aim at each end (awe going up, presence coming down).
3. **The ladder of fear and love**, especially *wanting* (love that is not sated by the mitzvah it just did) and *fear for the love above*. These refine S42-S46.
4. **Two postures of the Presence**: above the head for the steady, facing you for the one returning.
5. **Eye to eye at the Kedushah**, and **telling G-d "if I could, I would"** (line 1279).
6. **A Torah of one's own** for the measure of permitted things (line 1220).
