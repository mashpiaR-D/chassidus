**Summary.** Group-12 has seven works: one Chabad and six from other schools. The one idea is present in all seven, but each carries it differently, and the group shows clearly where it is the organizing center and where it is only the ground. The Tzemach Tzedek's Or HaTorah (6.75M words, OCR, with three garbled Rashi-script blocks) works the idea verse by verse through Torah, Nach, Siddur and Chazal. Its stated precision is that the two knowledges are 'two modes of His providence' (Havayah and Elokim), each with a root above, and that 'as nothing' does not mean literally nothing, since creation is a new thing (34040-34042). The dwelling below is the revelation of 'His very essence' against the mere 'ziv' that even Gan Eden receives, and it comes only through the deed of the mitzvos (7750). The Maggid's Maggid Devarav leYaakov is the idea in founding form, and its compiler states it as 'the key of the book': 'first of all know that the whole earth is full of His glory' (7). It supplies most of the spine's grammar (ayin joins opposites, yesh to ayin, the love that is the tzimtzum). R. Tzadok's Peri Tzadik sets it in sacred time: Shabbat is 'the secret of the higher and lower unity', and nothing is separate from Him even after creation (59). He carries it into the moral life with 'knowledge and choice are both true': before the act choice, after it 'all is from God' (44). That is kept as a position distinct from Chabad's. The Sefat Emet's register is the inner point of vitality hidden in nature and exile and revealed on Shabbat: 'every being is His vitality itself, only hidden' (5745). That it leans toward return-to-root ('echad') more than 'yachid' is our reading, offered with caution. Zera Kodesh (Ropshitz) reads the unity through the fillings of the Names and gematria, and keeps a stated guardrail: the glory that fills the earth is His garments, 'not from the light of His essence' (213). This is recorded as a disagreement with the Chabad emphasis. Bnei Machshava Tova (Piaseczno) makes the unity a forced, daily perception for a fellowship: 'the whole world is Godliness... only I made myself a thing in itself' (182). It yields brokenness before joy, against Tanya 33, and that tension is kept. Yakar MiPaz (Kozhnitz) is largely thin: a small collection centered on teshuvah, where the idea appears in two or three homilies ('even when one sins, he sins with My power', 38). Counts caveats: non-Chabad authors write השי"ת, חיות, נקודה, לית אתר פנוי and the like, so the fixed Chabad term set undercounts them badly; supplementary counts are given per work.

## Signature (reading, resting on stated passages)

Or HaTorah is the one idea worked verse by verse across the whole of Scripture, the prayer book and Chazal, in the Tzemach Tzedek's notebook form. Its angle is the relation of Ein Sof to the worlds held with scholastic precision. He does not let 'kulla kamei ke-la chashiv' be read loosely. It means 'as nothing', not literally nothing, because creation yesh me'ayin is the coming-to-be of a genuinely new thing (34040, stated). The lower knowledge, in which the creature is a separate 'something', is not merely our illusion either. It has its own root above, and the two knowledges are the two modes of His providence, Havayah and Elokim (34042, stated). That is the spine's G02 in its most developed form, and it gives the lower unity a standing the Alter Rebbe's SH 6 wording ('only toward the lower ones') leaves implicit. Its second axis is the dwelling below. The worlds, even Gan Eden, receive only 'ziv and hod', 'only from Your Name'. The dwelling is the revelation of 'His very being and essence', and it is made only through the physical deed of the mitzvos (7750, 7760, stated). Its method is the cross-reference: each verse sends the reader to Likkutei Torah, Torah Or, the Zohar, the Ari, the Rambam and the Shelah, so the whole library is read as one argument. That this gives 'Chassidus is chayus' (the Tzemach Tzedek's definition in Hayom Yom) its content is our reading; the book does not define itself. What it adds: the precision of the two knowledges, the dwelling as essence against ziv, and a seeing of the unity 'even from Asiyah' (80263, stated) set above hearing it.

## Structure

Arranged by Torah portion and book (Bereishis, Shemos, Vayikra, Bamidbar, Devarim), then Nach (Megillos, Esther, Shir HaShirim), the Siddur and Maamarei Razal, each block a scanned volume of the Kehot edition with editorial headnotes ('נדפס בהוצאה הראשונה...', 7749) and the author's own summaries ('קיצור', 7751, 80260). A typical entry takes a verse or midrash, asks a question ('וצ"ל הלא בלא"ה מכה"כ וא"כ מהו ענין דירה', 7750), answers by distinguishing levels of light (ziv/shem against mahus ve-atzmus; sovev/memale; higher/lower unity), and ends in avodah (mitzvos, Shema, prayer) with cross-references. Every portion is thus a run of G02 and G04: the particular verse is placed in the whole chain and in what is beyond it. The Siddur and Shir HaShirim blocks are where the book turns most to the person's seeing and bitul (79240, 80256). The idea is constant across the length; the distribution table shows no drift.

## Thin?

No. The unity is the stated subject on nearly every clean page. Caveats: the OCR (three garbled Rashi-script blocks, two front-matter blocks) and the notebook form, which often states the tie by cross-reference rather than in full.

## Faces

- **F02 The higher and the lower unity** (primary): Stated at 59: Shabbat is 'the secret of the higher and lower unity of Shema and Baruch Shem'. Higher unity: all attributes one in the supernal thought before creation. Lower unity: His kingship, from which 'the life and existence of all creatures... ein od milvado'. יחו"ע 27, יחודא עלאה 7.
- **F01 The world said into being now** (primary): 59 cites the Rambam ('all existence is only from the truth of His existence'), 'not only at the start of creation but their existence now... He renews in His goodness every day'. 564: even an inanimate piece of silver has a spirituality that is its existence, 'otherwise it has no existence, for ein od milvado'.
- **F09 The unity of opposites** (primary): Knowledge and choice 'both true' (44). Before the act choice rules; after it, 'the truth is that all is from God', and in the light of that knowledge the sins become like merits. 'הכל מהשי"ת/מהש"י' 105 lines. This is his signature (see disagreement below).
- **F12 Knowing the unity** (secondary): הכרה 54, ידיעה 28: 'the recognition of truth is the clear knowledge that ein od milvado' (92); the Shabbat of the future is 'the clear recognition... that ein od milvado' (32); 'to know that ein od milvado and return it to your heart' (464).
- **F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation** (secondary): 'There is no king without a people' as the reason for the lower unity (59). Choice exists for the trial, and evil was willed only 'for the trial of man... not to have real existence at all' (60).
- **F06 The soul as part of the One** (secondary): 60: 'as I am single above, so he is single below: the part is like the whole', and through man all creation is unified 'so that below too ein od milvado'; Israel 'one nation on earth'.
- **F08 Bitul** (minor): ביטול 41 (mostly the legal sense). The self as ayin appears as Aharon's 'ונחנו מה' and 'knowing that all is from God' (367).
- **F07 The home below** (thin): דירה בתחתונים 2, נתאוה 8. The Shechinah's descent into the Mishkan (60) is the nearest; tying the book to F07 is our reading.

## Signature (stated in the Shabbat treatise, reading for the whole)

Peri Tzadik puts the one idea into sacred time, and above all into Shabbat. The opening treatise states it: Shabbat is 'the secret of the higher and lower unity', and all creation exists to reveal His kingship. Even after creation 'there is nothing separate or outside Him', because He 'renews His work every day' (59, stated). Man, 'the part that is like the whole', unifies all creation 'so that below too ein od milvado' (60, stated). His angle is knowledge (hakarah): the unity is the truth, and the clear knowledge that ein od milvado is the root of the attribute of truth (92). What he adds, and what marks Lublin, is carrying the unity into the moral life. Choice is real before the act, but 'after the sin is done, the truth is that all is from God'. Knowledge and choice are 'both true', each in its own time, and the afternoon prayer is the moment that lights up the past with the knowledge that all is from Him (44, stated). This is the unity of opposites (F09) applied to sin and teshuvah. It is close to the Izhbitz doctrine of will, and it goes further than Chabad's practice, which keeps sin as simply against His will in conduct (spine G10; Tanya 32 holds love and hate as both true). That the portion-by-portion homilies are all one expression of this idea is our reading. The idea is stated often but not on every page, and much of the book is Talmudic and Midrashic exegesis in its own right.

## Structure

Two treatises on Shabbat come first and set out the theory: Shabbat as the unity, the higher and lower unity, rest as recognizing that all is from Him (163, 1534). Then the portions, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, Pesach and the other festivals follow in calendar order, each read through the same lens. Each time-unit is a station where some part of the recognition 'all is from Him' is revealed or tested. So the book's order (Shabbat first, then the week of portions and the year of festivals) itself mirrors its thesis that Shabbat is the root of the week (our reading, following his statement at 59).

## Thin?

No, though it is not the book's only center: the tzaddik, Oral Torah, choice and teshuvah take large shares. The unity is stated as the frame in the Shabbat treatise and recurs as a formula ('הכל מהשי"ת') throughout.

## Signature (stated in many teachings; 'one idea' is our reading)

Sefat Emet expresses the one idea as the inner point (נקודה פנימית), the point of vitality from God in every thing, hidden by nature, the week and exile, and revealed on Shabbat. It is stated in so many words: 'Hashem is One... that He is one, ein od, that every being is His vitality itself, only hidden' (5745). The whole purpose of creation was Shabbat, when every creature, acting as if by itself, knows that He gives it the power, 'like an axe in the hand of the hewer' (9). Its method is renewal (hitchadshut). Each year the same portion is read again, and the point is found anew under the husk. That the book's form enacts its thesis of daily renewal is our reading. Its practice is bitul to the point: 'the counsel is to nullify oneself to the inner point in everything' (570), and to remove the 'foreskin', the outer shell that covers the covenant point (404). Compared with Chabad, the register is recovery and return rather than the Essence dwelling in the lowest. The point is found beneath the outer layer and all things return to their root on Shabbat. The Chabad accent is the reverse: the lowest, as lowest, is the dwelling of the Essence (F07). In the spine's terms this is closer to 'echad', the many returned to one root, than to 'yachid'. That classification is our reading, and it is offered with caution, since 5745 reads 'אחד' as 'ein od'.

## Structure

Five books by portion, each portion subdivided by year of delivery (e.g. 'Bereshit 2:1 ⟶ תרל"ב'), plus festival sections (Chanukah, Purim, Pesach, Shavuot and others) inside the relevant book. Almost every teaching opens from a Midrash or Zohar ('במדרש...', 'בזוה"ק...') and ends by locating the inner point: in the day, in Shabbat, in the mitzvah, in Israel. The yearly repetition over the same text is the book's structure and its argument: the point does not change, and its revelation is renewed (reading).

## Thin?

No. The idea is the book's constant center in its own vocabulary (point, vitality, hiding, Shabbat), although the Chabad technical terms are nearly absent.

## Faces

- **F01 The world said into being now** (primary): 'The power of the maker is in the made' 8 lines; the Besht's 'Your word stands in the heavens' (64); intro 7: the vitality of the Creator dwells in every thing and every movement 'as a soul in a body'; without His Godliness the worlds 'could not exist one moment' (94). חיות 44, עולם הדיבור 21.
- **F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation** (primary): צמצום 28, אין מלך בלא עם 11. 'The love with which He loved Israel is itself the tzimtzum' (94, from the Besht); 'contraction after contraction... as a father narrows his mind for his son's delight' (216).
- **F09 The unity of opposites** (primary): Ayin joins fire and water, love and fear: 'in God there is love and fear as one, for He joins them' (14). The spine anchors this line.
- **F08 Bitul** (primary): 'Heaven and earth were made yesh from ayin, and tzaddikim make ayin from yesh', even in eating (18); through fear one 'reckons himself as ayin' (66); tzaddikim 'nullified from their existence' (92). The spine anchors 18 and 66.
- **F04 Filling and surrounding** (secondary): ממלא כל עלמין 8, לית אתר פנוי 5, מלא כל הארץ כבודו 4; intro 7 opens with 'the whole earth is full of His glory, no place empty of Him, and He is within the worlds'.
- **F12 Knowing the unity** (secondary): Intro 7: 'first of all one must know'; 105:1 (226): everything one sees and hears and every event comes to arouse him, 'and he should watch over everything' (G12).
- **F07 The home below** (thin): דירה בתחתונים 0. Intro 7 says 'He contracted His Shechinah that dwells in the lower realms', and 69:1 says God made this world and man in it 'so that He might dwell in him'. Reading these as F07 is ours.

## Signature (stated)

Maggid Devarav leYaakov is the one idea in its founding form, and its compiler says so. The second introduction, which he calls 'the key to the book' (5), begins: 'First of all one must know that the whole earth is full of His glory, no place is empty of Him'. The vitality of the Creator dwells in every thing, even a taste, a smell or a love, and in every movement, 'as a soul in a body' (7, stated). Every teaching after that assumes it. Its angle is the ayin: the source of all things is a nothing that joins opposites (14). Creation is yesh from ayin, and the tzaddik's work is the reverse, ayin from yesh, even in eating (18). Its method is from the soul (G06): as a father narrows his mind for his child, God contracts Himself for our delight (216), and the love itself is the contraction (94). It adds to the later corpus most of the grammar the spine uses: the ayin that joins (G10), yesh-to-ayin (G08), and every event as a summons (G12, 226). It is also the source the Alter Rebbe and the Chabad Rebbeim drew on. Its tzimtzum is non-spatial, a narrowing for the sake of the receiver. The spine's disagreement over a literal tzimtzum concerns Tzava'at HaRivash from the same circle, not this book.

## Structure

Two introductions by R. Shlomo of Lutzk, the second explicitly 'the key of the book' (5) and opening with the unity (7). Then numbered teachings, mostly short, each taking a verse or Zohar and turning it to avodah in thought, speech and prayer. Several are stated general rules ('זה הכלל', 'כלל הדברים', 211, 226). The order is not systematic, so the unity works as the key the introduction hands the reader, not as an argued sequence.

## Thin?

No. The idea is stated at the book's head and in many of its teachings; the spine already anchors five of its lines (14, 18, 64, 66, 216).
