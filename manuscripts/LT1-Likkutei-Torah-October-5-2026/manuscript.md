*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** This is a collection of the Alter Rebbe's maamarim (discourses) on the weekly portions, edited with many cross-references. Each discourse opens with a verse or a saying of the Sages, lays out its Kabbalistic structure (sefiros, the worlds, arousal from below and from above), and then usually turns in a paragraph or two to 'the service of man'. That turn gives short, sharp teachings on prayer, teshuvah, bitterness versus sadness, foreign thoughts, inspiration that fades, anger and desire. The medicine sits in those turns, scattered through a mostly cosmological text.

**How much of it is medicine.** some: most of the text is Kabbalistic and cosmological, but nearly every discourse closes with a brief, practical passage on prayer, teshuvah or refining the animal soul, and these are precise and usable.

**Its model of the person.** A person has a divine soul and an animal (vital) soul. The animal soul's desire and anger are fire drawn from a holy root, so they can be turned around, not only suppressed. The mind contemplates and the heart is meant to respond. Will is the inner core of the emotions and governs them. Divine fire in a person is limited and gets spent on anger, envy and lust. Lasting change comes from one's own labor (arousal from below). Arousal given from above wakes a person but fades. Prayer is the hour of sifting good from evil, and Torah study is the vessel that holds what prayer ignites.

**Its philosophy.** Everything is nullified before God (kulah kamei k'lo chashiv). The world only appears to be a separate 'something' because God's light is hidden. The deepest level of God is above time and blemish, so even someone whose light has gone out can reach it through teshuvah. God's presence stays pledged inside every Jew even in exile and sin. Evil that turns to good exceeds plain good, like light coming out of darkness.

**What it adds.** It tells joyless worry and bitterness about distance apart from sadness, and confines bitterness to a set hour (Tikkun Chatzos) so Torah and joy carry the day. It explains why foreign thoughts attack exactly at prayer. It diagnoses why inspiration fades after weeks. It shows how fervor without Torah sours into anger and ego. It has anger turned on one's own evil so it cools toward others. It has love as will ('avah') ruling the emotions, and the lost-hope penitent reaching the 'rock' above time.

## I contemplate God's greatness and my heart feels nothing; my prayer is dead

He does the contemplation of prayer faithfully, but love never comes. The heart stays stone, however true the ideas are.

**What is really going on.** He is tied to the world's vanities and experiences himself as a separate 'something'. Coarseness of spirit (gasus haruach) and physical craving cover over a love that is already present in the heart.

**Program: Arousing compassion on one's own soul.** (1) In prayer, contemplate God's greatness until the love of Shema should come. (2) If the heart stays stone, name the cause: attachment to worldly vanities and feeling yourself a separate 'something'. (3) Look to the soul's root ('know where you came from') and see how far it has fallen. (4) Arouse great compassion on your soul through the mercy-verses of prayer ('in Your great mercy have mercy on us'). (5) Let the heaviness and the felt lack draw down the mercy; ask for understanding that all is truly nullified. *When:* During morning prayer, in the blessings before Shema and at Shema itself; it is the prayer's own daily path. *For:* Someone who does the contemplation faithfully but feels nothing. *Not for:* Someone already sunk in sadness or self-loathing; the text's aim is mercy and understanding, not self-accusation. `LT1·Q03` · `LT1·Q06` · `LT1·Q17` · `LT1·Q31`

**Program: Turning desire itself toward God through prayer.** (1) Say Baruch She'amar and Pesukei deZimra, contemplating God's greatness in depth. (2) Continue through the blessings of Shema to 'One' and 'love with all your heart', meaning with both inclinations. (3) Know that love is an act of will ('ve'ahavta' from 'avah'), and will governs the emotions. (4) Let the fiery love consume the craving until the craving itself turns to desire God, the source of all delights. *When:* Daily morning prayer. *For:* Someone fighting physical cravings. *Not for:* Not a promise that desire vanishes quickly; the text speaks of lifelong refining. `LT1·Q23` · `LT1·Q24` · `LT1·Q30`

## The moment I start praying my head fills with junk; my prayer is worthless

Thoughts that never bothered him all day flood in exactly when he tries to cleave in prayer, and he concludes the prayer counts for nothing.

**What is really going on.** These are his own daytime thoughts rising with him, like a long garment dragging mud up a ladder. Prayer is the hour of sifting, so evil strains to cling to the good. The false conclusion is that the prayer is 'nothing'.

**Program: Meeting foreign thoughts in prayer.** (1) Expect foreign thoughts at prayer: it is the hour of sifting evil from good. (2) Recognize them as your own daytime thoughts rising with you, like mud on a garment climbing a ladder. (3) Do not let your heart fall or conclude the prayer is worthless. (4) Push them away and divert your mind from them. (5) Deepen your thought in God's greatness, filling and surrounding all worlds, before which all is as nothing; the thoughts lose their nourishment and fall away. *When:* Every prayer, at the moment the thoughts arrive. *For:* Anyone whose attempt to concentrate is flooded with distractions. *Not for:* Someone with clinical intrusive thoughts or OCD; 'push away' and 'these are your own thoughts' can feed obsession and guilt. `LT1·Q10` · `LT1·Q11` · `LT1·Q12` · `LT1·Q13` · `LT1·Q14` · `LT1·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"My prayer was full of junk thoughts, so it was worthless."* to **The thoughts rise because prayer is the hour of sifting; they are your own day-thoughts coming up with you. Push them away and keep praying; the prayer is not nothing.** Evil clings harder exactly when the good is being separated, so their appearance marks the work under way. This removes the despair (falling of the heart) that would end the prayer. `LT1·Q10` · `LT1·Q11` · `LT1·Q12` · `LT1·Q13` · `LT1·Q14`

## I was on fire for a few weeks and then it just stopped

A sudden inspiration came with no known cause, lasted a while, then vanished completely, and he is back to his old nature.

**What is really going on.** The arousal came from above only. He did no preparation or labor of his own, so when the light withdrew nothing held and he stayed 'on his standing and nature'.

**Program: Building arousal through one's own labor.** (1) Prepare the heart before prayer; do not rely on sudden inspiration. (2) Labor with soul and body: deepen the mind in contemplation, not mere recollection. (3) Let the heart break before God. (4) Then the light from above rests inwardly and gives strength for further effort. *When:* Daily, before and during prayer. *For:* Someone whose inspirations come and go. *Not for:* Not a demand to manufacture feeling by force; it asks for preparation and depth of thought. `LT1·Q19` · `LT1·Q18` · `LT1·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"My inspiration vanished, so it wasn't real, or I'm not cut out for this."* to **That was a gift from above meant to wake you. Light that lasts comes through your own preparation and labor.** Arousal from below makes the light from above shine inwardly and gives strength for more. What came only from above leaves when the light withdraws. `LT1·Q18` · `LT1·Q19`

## I've sunk so low into desire and anger that my hope is gone

He knows honestly that he is at the bottom rung, ruled by bad traits, and concludes it is over for him.

**What is really going on.** The conclusion is false. God's presence goes into exile with a person and gives him divine life even while he transgresses. Even a small mind can still fix faith in God's unity inside himself.

**Program: Speaking Torah law aloud when you cannot ascend.** (1) Admit honestly where you stand, even at the bottom rung. (2) Refuse the conclusion 'my hope is lost'; God's presence is with you even in exile. (3) Although you cannot climb in understanding, speak and review mishnah and halachah aloud. (4) By this speech, faith in God's unity is fixed and grasped within you, not hovering above the mind. *When:* Regular daily review; no length is given. *For:* Someone who feels too low or of too small a mind for contemplation. *Not for:* Not a replacement for addressing real harm he is causing; the text concerns faith and hope. `LT1·Q20` · `LT1·Q21` · `LT1·Q22`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm at the bottom, ruled by lust and anger; my hope is lost."* to **God's presence is with you even in exile and even as you transgress. Faith can still be fixed within you through the speech of Torah.** Divinity dwells in every Jew as a pledge that is not withdrawn, so his low state does not cut him off. Speech draws faith inward even where understanding cannot climb. `LT1·Q20` · `LT1·Q21` · `LT1·Q22`

## I did wrong and my fire went out; I have nothing left to hold onto

He is far from God's face and has no Torah or good deeds that could serve as a wick for the light.

**What is really going on.** The light of 'consuming fire' needs a wick. Bitterness at the point of the heart, which is above time, reaches the 'rock', the source above all being, from which fire can be struck anew.

**Program: Teshuvah as returning the face.** (1) Especially in the Ten Days of Teshuvah, contemplate how God, as Father of mercy, constricts Himself to sustain the worlds. (2) Look at your root and see how far your soul has fallen; arouse compassion on it. (3) Let the soul pour out in bitterness over its distance from God's face; cry out. (4) Lift your soul to Him: nullify your will so you have one will toward your Father in heaven. (5) Ask forgiveness, meaning that His will rest on you again to do His will wholeheartedly. (6) Turn face-to-face: do Torah and mitzvos from inner will, with joy. *When:* Especially the Ten Days of Teshuvah and Yom Kippur; the principle applies whenever. *For:* Someone who has sinned and feels his light gone out, or whose observance has gone cold. *Not for:* Someone already crushed by guilt or depression; the bitterness here is meant to end in joy and return, not despair. `LT1·Q04` · `LT1·Q05` · `LT1·Q06` · `LT1·Q07` · `LT1·Q08`

**Reframe.** From *"My fire went out and I have no Torah or good deeds left to hold the light."* to **Bitterness at the point of your heart reaches the 'rock' above time, from which new fire is struck.** The point of the heart is above time and place and connects to the source above all creation, where blemish does not reach. Deliberate sins can then become merits. `LT1·Q07` · `LT1·Q04` · `LT1·Q06`

## My anger, envy and hatred burn me up and leave nothing for what matters

After a day of anger, envy and craving, his heart no longer kindles in service. He is irritable toward others.

**What is really going on.** The divine fire in him is limited and clothed in the vital soul. Using it for bodily passions depletes it. Everyone contains good and evil, and the evil is anger itself.

**Program: Renewing the fire and turning anger on one's own evil.** (1) See that anger, envy, hatred and lust spend the limited divine fire in you. (2) Twice daily in Shema, contemplate God's oneness deeply, fixing the thought with force, not only remembering it. (3) In the heat of that contemplation, pour your anger onto the evil in yourself: why am I like this, far all day? (4) By this, the anger cools and you are not harmful to others at all. *When:* Shema morning and evening, every day, across a lifetime. *For:* Someone whose anger and passions drain his spiritual energy. *Not for:* Someone with self-hatred or depression: 'rage at your evil' can become self-attack. `LT1·Q25` · `LT1·Q26` · `LT1·Q27` · `LT1·Q28`

**Reframe.** From *"My anger is who I am; I just explode at people."* to **Everyone has anger. Pour it on the evil in yourself during prayer and it cools toward others.** The heat of Shema contemplation is itself gevurah. Aimed at one's own distance from God, it spends and cools the raw anger, so none is left to harm others. `LT1·Q27` · `LT1·Q28` · `LT1·Q25`

## After an intense prayer or spiritual high I turn irritable, proud or craving

Right after the fervor he snaps, feels superior, or is pulled toward desires.

**What is really going on.** Fervor (ratzo) without the vessel of Torah (shov) feeds the outside forces and gives rise to harshness, anger, ego and bad desires.

**Program: After prayer's fire, the vessel of Torah.** (1) Do not leave prayer's fervor standing alone. (2) Move after prayer into Torah study, which gives the fervor vessels. (3) Let Torah study gladden the heart that prayer left worried. *When:* Immediately after prayer, daily. *For:* Someone who becomes irritable, proud or craving after intense spiritual moments. *Not for:* The text does not say it cures temperament; it prevents fervor from souring. `LT1·Q29` · `LT1·Q02`

## Feeling how far I am from God weighs on me; is this pain good or harmful?

Contemplation makes him feel his distance and the hiddenness of God, and he cannot tell holy pain from depression.

**What is really going on.** The work separates sadness, which it rejects outright, from worry and bitterness about distance, which it asks for. Bitterness belongs at set times and is followed by Torah, which gladdens.

**Program: Bitterness confined to a set hour.** (1) In Shema, feel bitterness over the darkness hiding God and over your distance; cry out to God. (2) Bitterness only during prayer is not enough: fix a set time for it (Tikkun Chatzos is the traditional hour). (3) Distinguish it from sadness, which is forbidden: it is worry and bitterness, not depression. (4) After it, let Torah through the day carry the light; the bitterness at the set hour draws Godliness onto the whole day. *When:* A fixed daily time such as midnight, plus Shema; weekdays (Shabbos service is delight, not breaking). *For:* A stable person whose service is complacent and who feels no lack. *Not for:* Anyone depressed, anxious, or with trauma or self-harm risk. `LT1·Q15` · `LT1·Q16` · `LT1·Q01` · `LT1·Q17`

## Cautions

- Bitterness, heaviness of head and 'breaking the heart' should not be given to someone depressed; the text itself rejects sadness and limits bitterness to a set hour. - 'Pour your anger on the evil in you' can turn into self-hatred for a person already self-critical. - 'Push foreign thoughts away' and 'they are your own thoughts' can feed OCD or scrupulosity; such people need clinical care. - 'Looking at the root: how far your soul has fallen' can reinforce shame in trauma survivors. - 'Pleasures in coolness, as if compelled' (Sh'lach) can reinforce anhedonia or disordered eating if applied literally. - Several passages speak of Jewish souls specifically (e.g. the animal soul's eternity); adapting them universally needs care.
