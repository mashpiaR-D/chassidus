## What this cluster shows

The mitzvot look like a legal code: 613 commands, each with its own measure, time and object. Read against the spine, the sources treat each mitzvah as a single act in which the unity becomes actual (F10), and the cluster shows it from three sides. First, the mitzvah as such is a bond. The Maharash says the creation of the worlds is 'only a mere radiance', whereas 'through the mitzvot there is a tzavta and joining with the Essence of Or Ein Sof' (Toras Shmuel 5627). The Alter Rebbe, the Mitteler Rebbe, the Rayatz and the Rebbe say the same in their own words: the command gives the commanded his existence and joins him to the Essence (S089).

Second, the mitzvah's form is the tzimtzum. The 613 were given 'from the mouth of Gevurah' (IH 10; Maharash), so they have measure: tefillin two fingers by two, tzitzit twelve finger-breadths, sacrifices bounded, while charity alone may be given without limit and is 'more chosen than the altar' (S091, S092, S095). The split into do and do-not is itself that contraction; the negative commandments guard against the separation that is idolatry, and in the future they will be 'included together' with the positive (S090). Several mitzvot are read as the removal of a concealment: the foreskin as Elokim become total hiding (S094), forbidden food as life 'bound' to what stands apart (S097), the statute as the one place where reason no longer veils the will (S098).

Third, the mitzvah's place is the lowest. The Anochi is drawn 'in actual deed in this world' (Likkutei Torah); the fifth given in charity makes the whole business day 'a place for His dwelling' (Tanya 34); the Ark stood measured and took no space, 'the union of Havayah and Elokim' (the Rebbe); the covenant sealed in the soul must be revealed by deed in the flesh (Sefat Emet). This is F07, the home below, in the smallest unit.

The tie is stated for almost every subject, often more strongly than the subject list expected (tzitzit and milah were marked 'reading' there; Derekh Mitzvotekha states both). It is thinnest in two places. One is forbidden foods, where the sources state it for the category (bound and unbound) but not for individual laws. The other is priests and Levites, where it is stated for the functions they stand for (devekut from the yechidah, love in bitul, seeing providence) and not for the institution. Outside Chabad, the same mitzvot are usually organized around devekut (Chernobyl, Berditchev), the soul-body bond (Ger), faith (the Yismach Moshe) or the tzaddik's counsel (Breslov), and the differences are kept below.

## S089 · The mitzvah as bond with the supernal will · מצוה לשון צוותא

**Faces:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F05 The Essence beyond both · F07 The home below · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** צוותא · מצות · רמ"ח מצות · לשון צוותא

> The mitzvah is the unity of God as a bond: the command itself joins the one commanded to the Essence of the One who commands, which creation, 'only a mere radiance', does not do.

## Derivation

1. The spine's point is the Essence, beyond 'nothing' and 'something' (F05). The Maharash sets creation below it: 'the creation of the worlds is only from a mere radiance' (Toras Shmuel 5627, line 537), the same formula the spine keeps as the F05 tension, there from another maamar of the same year (F05j, Toras Shmuel 5627, line 713). (*stated*)
2. A mitzvah is His will, and His will is one with Him (F10). So what a mitzvah reaches is not the radiance but the Essence: 'through the mitzvot there is a tzavta and joining with the Essence of Or Ein Sof' (Maharash, same line); in 'asher Anochi metzavcha' it is 'not ziv and radiance but Anochi Myself', the revelation of His Essence (Likkutei Torah, Vaetchanan 7:7); 'Anochi' is 'His very Essence', joined through mitzvot in actual deed (Mitteler Rebbe, Toras Chaim, Noach). (*stated*)
3. The word carries the claim. Mitzvah, and 'vetzivanu' in every blessing, is read as tzavta, joining, by the Alter Rebbe (Likkutei Torah), the Mitteler Rebbe, the Tzemach Tzedek (Derekh Mitzvotekha, seen in search), the Maharash, the Rayatz and the Rebbe. Tanya 46 gives the same without the pun: 'who sanctified us' is a betrothal, a union with Or Ein Sof 'more so without end'. (*stated*)
4. The joining is made by the command, not by the act's effect. The Rayatz's parable: a king who commands a commoner who had no standing before him gives the commoner his very existence and unites them (Sefer HaMaamarim, Tammuz 5689; the same in Hayom Yom 8 Cheshvan). Hence 'it makes no difference whether the command is great or small' (Hayom Yom 8 Cheshvan:2). (*stated*)
5. So the mitzvah is its own purpose and its own reward (G09): 'the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah', the union itself (Hayom Yom 8 Cheshvan; Me'or Einayim, Vayera 8). The Rebbe: kept because it is His command, not for its reason and not even for its effect in the upper worlds, it is 'union with the Owner of the will, up to union with the Essence' (Rosh Hashanah 5743). (*stated*)
6. Its opposite is separation: 'aveirah is a word of separation from the Creator' (Maggid); the sinner passes out of God's domain (Rayatz). Separation is the root the spine calls idolatry (F08, Tanya 22). (*stated*)
7. The union is made below, in deed. Likkutei Torah (Vaetchanan 7:8) says the Anochi is drawn specifically 'in the doing of the act in this world, in actual deed', more than in the World to Come; this is the home below (F07) in the smallest unit. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Likkutei Torah, Vaetchanan 7:6-8 (the blessing over a mitzvah draws from above the chain; 'metzavcha' as tzavta; Anochi revealed in deed in this world); Tanya 46:5-6 (betrothal; union of both souls with Or Ein Sof); Likkutei Torah, Bechukotai 2:35 and Shemini Atzeret 1:19 (tzavta when the light is drawn into a vessel; seen in search).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim, Noach (line 525): 'Anochi', whom none knows but Himself, is His very Essence; 'metzavcha' is tzavta through mitzvot in actual deed, 'today' specifically.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha reads 'vetzivanu' as tzavta at lines 135, 167, 309, 317 (the Essence of Ein Sof bound to Israel through Yesod; joining us to the mitzvot, whose vessels are rooted above the lights). Seen in search; not quoted here.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627 (line 537): creation is a mere radiance, mitzvot join with the Essence; line 1094: the same tzavta through the ayin that mediates; lines 745 and 761 (seen in search): two senses of 'mitzvah', command and joining.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis reads 'tzav' and 'tetzaveh' as tzavta, but of Moshe with Israel (lines 2242, 3521; seen in search). No Rashab passage on the mitzvah as such was quoted.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim, Tammuz 5689 (line 1616): mitzvah joins with the Essence; parable of the king's command; aveirah as passing from His domain. Also lines 192, 355, 1077 (seen). Hayom Yom 8 Cheshvan gives the same parable.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Rosh Hashanah 5743 (line 114): the mitzvah as union with the Owner of the will and with the Essence, and Israel 'making a new will'; lines 427-429 and 1764 (seen): the command gives the commanded his existence; keeping 'from the side of the mitzvah itself' is where all Israel are equal.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (Maggid Devarav LeYaakov 133): mitzvah is joining, sin separation; one who keeps 'know Him in all your ways' has mitzvot without end. Chernobyl (Me'or Einayim, Vayera 8): tzavta is devekut of the divine part to its root; without it the mitzvah is a body without soul. Berditchev (Kedushat Levi, Vayera 34, read): the mitzvah joins the doer to the Creator, which rules out any personal motive. Ger (Sefat Emet, Ki Teitzei 15:2): through each mitzvah the soul's life is drawn into the matching limb, so tzavta joins body and soul. The real difference: in Chabad the command joins the person to the Essence from above; in Chernobyl, Berditchev and Ger the joining is the person's cleaving, or the limb joined to the soul, from below.

## Thin or forced?

Not thin. The etymology is a pun, but every Chabad Rebbe from the Alter Rebbe to the Rebbe draws the stated conclusion that the mitzvah joins with the Essence. The Maharash's line holds a tension with IH 20 (creation is a mere radiance, yet only the Essence creates yesh) that the spine already keeps. A second pull lies inside the Maharash himself: in Toras Shmuel 5631 (line 2794, quoted under S090 and S091) he says 'the whole matter of Torah and mitzvot is only a tzimtzum', like a giver bending to a poor man, for 'from the side of His Essence, if you sinned, what do you do to Him'. The Rebbe who says the mitzvot join with the Essence also says the Essence is untouched by them. The two are kept; reconciling them (the joining is by the Essence's own choice to contract into command) would be our reading. Outside Chabad the same pun carries devekut rather than the Essence.

## S090 · Positive and negative commandments · מצות עשה ומצות לא תעשה

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F09 The unity of opposites · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** מצות עשה · מצות לא תעשה · סור מרע ועשה טוב · מ"ע ול"ת

> Positive and negative commandments are the unity of God as its first two words: 'Anochi' drawn down into the 248 limbs, and 'you shall have no other gods' refusing every separate thing. Both are spoken from His Gevurah and are one again in their root.

## Derivation

1. 'Anochi' and 'you shall have no other gods' contain all 248 positive and all 365 negative commandments. To explain this, the Alter Rebbe turns at once to 'the unity of the Holy One, called yachid umeyuchad', unchanged by creation (Tanya 20:1-3). (*stated*)
2. Every prohibition is therefore idolatry in its core. What opposes His will is 'utterly separated from His unity', like the husk called 'idolatry' (Tanya 24:1), and a sin committed is, 'at the moment of the act', a separation from His unity (Tanya 25:3). The negative commandment guards the unity from the side of separation (F08). (*stated*)
3. The positive commandments draw Or Ein Sof into the 248 limbs of the animal soul. The negative ones cut its 365 sinews off from the impure husks. The joint aim is that 'the whole earth be filled with His glory' and 'you were shown to know that Havayah is Elokim, there is none else' (Tanya 37:11-14; F10, F07). (*stated*)
4. The ranking can reverse. Likkutei Torah (Pekudei 1:5-7) roots the negatives in Y-H, above the V-H of the positives. Because they come from so high, they have no vessel below and are kept only by refraining: the 'not' draws from 'a land where no man dwells', above the 'Adam' of the 248. The same author gives two emphases. (*stated*)
5. The split itself is contraction. From His Essence 'darkness and light are equal before Him'; that there is an 'Anochi' that sources the 248 and a 'lo yihyeh' that sources the 365 is 'from the mouth of Gevurah, a contraction' (Maharash, Toras Shmuel 5631). Do and don't are the tzimtzum's form of the one will (F03). (*stated*)
6. In the future the 365 will be raised in their root, the holy gevurot, 'sweetened in the chasadim of the 248, and included together' (Kuntres Acharon 5:9). The opposites are joined in the root and kept distinct in present conduct (F09, G10). (*stated*)
7. Outside Chabad: the Izhbitzer sets Pesach against 'Anochi' and the red heifer against 'lo yihyeh'. Moshe's soul clarifies 'His unity in the world', R. Akiva's purges the heart of evil, and God gave 'Anochi' first in mercy, because no one could first purify his heart (Mei HaShiloach, Bo 5). Berditchev gives a functional account (positive commandments bring down kindness, negative ones keep the outside forces from it) that does not name the unity; tying it to F08 is our reading. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 4 (love is the root of the positive commandments, fear of the negative; read in search); Tanya 20:1-3; 24:1-2; 25:3-5; 37:11-14; IH 10:2 (the 613 'from the mouth of Gevurah', read in context); Iggeret HaTeshuvah 1:2-4 (a positive commandment draws light, a transgression blemishes above; seen in search); Likkutei Torah, Pekudei 1:5-9; Kuntres Acharon 4:10 and 5:9-10.

- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5631 (line 2794): 'Anochi' and 'lo yihyeh' as the sources of the 248 and the 365, from the mouth of Gevurah, a contraction, whereas from His Essence 'darkness and light are equal'.
- **Rashab.** Not found with the full terms in Hemshech Ayin Beis; abbreviated forms were not searched.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim, Tammuz 5689 (line 1616): the parable of the king's command covers both 'to do a thing' and 'warning not to do' it; both give the commanded his existence.

## Thin or forced?

Not thin. Tanya states the tie directly (chapters 20, 24, 37). Berditchev's account is functional, and its tie to the unity is ours. The disagreement over which rank is higher (Tanya 37 against Likkutei Torah, Pekudei) is internal to the Alter Rebbe and is kept.

## S091 · Charity · צדקה

**Faces:** F07 The home below · F01 The world said into being now · F04 Filling and surrounding · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** צדקה · גמילות חסדים · נותן צדקה · צדקה ומשפט

> Charity is the unity of God as His own act done by a person. The One who gives life to worlds that have nothing of their own is imitated by someone who gives away the life he earned, so that his livelihood becomes a dwelling for Him.

## Derivation

1. The worlds exist only by His life given each moment (F01), and so they have 'nothing of their own': before Him all worlds are 'dalet', paupers, 'because all is as nothing before Him' (IH 10:10). His giving to them is without measure. (*stated*)
2. Charity is therefore 'His own mitzvah, what the Holy One Himself does always to give life to the worlds' (IH 17:4). The Maharash turns it round: the giving of all Torah and mitzvot is itself charity, God contracting Himself as a giver bends to a poor man (Toras Shmuel 5631). (*stated*)
3. When a person gives, he does the divine act in the world of action. Charity without limit draws the light that surrounds all worlds (sovev), which in the future will be revealed down to this world (IH 17:4-6). Hayom Yom 6 Tammuz: a coin before each mitzvah 'draws the surrounding into the inner' (F04). (*stated*)
4. It is called simply 'the mitzvah' because the whole animal soul, invested in the labour that earned the money, rises to God and is included in the Or Ein Sof clothed in the mitzvot; for the same reason it 'brings the redemption near' (Tanya 37:19-21). (*stated*)
5. The fifth that is given lifts the other four fifths with it, 'to be a place for His dwelling'; the business day itself becomes a dwelling below (Tanya 34:6-7; F07). (*stated*)
6. The person's side is bitul (our label; the text's word is faith). The Yismach Moshe: God gives charity to all creatures and the giver is only His messenger; to imagine one gives one's own is 'rebellion and sacrilege and lack of faith' (Re'eh 16), and 'the root of charity is faith' (Terumah 4, read). (*stated*)
7. The Rebbe: 'the way of Havayah, to do charity': Torah draws down, charity refines and lifts the lower. It must be done 'with judgment', judging oneself about what one really needs, and as itkafia (Vayera 5737; F11). (*stated*)

## S092 · Tefillin · תפילין

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F06 The soul as part of the One · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** תפילין · תפלין · הנחת תפילין · תפילין של ראש

> Tefillin are the unity of God as the nullifying of one's own mind and heart into His: they are bound on the head and arm of 'one nation in the land', and in God's own tefillin they are His delight in that bond.

## Derivation

1. The Or Ein Sof that is His will is clothed in these tefillin, and in putting them on one 'draws His light upon the part of God within one's body, to be included and nullified in His light' (Tanya 41:5; F06, F10). (*stated*)
2. Specifically: one's own Chochmah and Binah are nullified into the Chochmah and Binah of Ein Sof in the sections Kadesh and Vehaya ki yeviacha, and one's Da'at, with its love and fear, into the supernal Da'at in Shema and Vehaya im shamoa, 'as the Shulchan Aruch says: to subjugate the heart and the mind' (Tanya 41:6; F08). (*stated*)
3. The Tzemach Tzedek asks how tefillin, the supernal will in Keter, can be described as the mochin of Z'A. His link between that drawing and the physical act is 'the bitul of the yesh in putting on tefillin physically' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillin 8). (*stated*)
4. The Rebbe, from the Maggid: God's tefillin say 'Who is like Your people Israel'. Tefillin are the delight of both sides in their cleaving, above reason. That tefillin can subjugate mind and heart 'proves that they are above mind and heart' (Yud Shevat 5728; G05). (*stated*)
5. 'One nation in the land': even on earth Israel is bound to Havayah echad, because their root is the Essence's own delight, which earthly things cannot change. One putting-on draws this into revelation, which is why the category 'a head that never wore tefillin' applies only to someone who never put them on at all (same maamar; F05, F07). (*stated*)
6. The measure is the tzimtzum: because the mitzvot were given clothed in Gevurah, tefillin are 'two fingers by two' and square (IH 10:5; F03). (*stated*)
7. R. Tzadok: tefillin mean devekut, one's whole life hanging on it (Tzidkat HaTzadik 197). They are the surrounding light bound fast, and in practice they are to be kept at least during prayer (Tzidkat HaTzadik 220). (*stated*)

## S093 · Tzitzit · ציצית

**Faces:** F04 Filling and surrounding · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** ציצית · ציצת · טלית · תכלת

> Tzitzit are the unity of God as a surrounding light that is worn: His kingship drawn around the whole body, with white and blue threads that are the higher and lower bitul, and a sight that reminds the heart of His unity.

## Derivation

1. The surrounding light holds all equally and is not revealed within (F04). The tallit is 'the great surrounding light, head to foot' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tzitzit 1, after the Ari). (*stated*)
2. In wrapping, one draws upon oneself His kingship, 'the kingship of all worlds', to unite it upon us (Tanya 41:7). Placing this under the lower unity of Malchus (F02) is our reading; Tanya names only the drawing of His kingship. (*stated*)
3. White and tekhelet are the two unities. White is the essential bitul of 'ein od milvado' in the world of unity; tekhelet is the bitul of separate creatures below who nullify themselves. The spies called the land 'one that eats its inhabitants', but 'this bitul is the true life of creatures, not their end' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tzitzit 4; F02, F08). Mapping the two bituls onto the higher and lower unity is ours. The Sefat Emet reads the colours otherwise: white and tekhelet are the positive and negative commandments (Sh'lach 16:4), and tekhelet is holiness, above white's purity (Sh'lach 16:5), where for the Tzemach Tzedek tekhelet is the lower, gevurah-side bitul. The two readings are kept side by side. (*stated*)
4. Tzitzit equals all 613 because it holds both sides of the mitzvah, the will (tallit) and the reason (threads) (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tzitzit 3, skimmed). (*stated*)
5. Forgetting is the evil inclination's work: it makes 'the good thoughts of the Creator's unity' be forgotten from the heart. Tzitzit, a surrounding light that reminds, is the remedy for foreign thoughts (R. Tzadok, Tzidkat HaTzadik 220). That the reminder is F12's 'return it to your heart' is our label. (*stated*)
6. The Sefat Emet: wrapping in the tallit is 'to be united in the root of unity', a unity 'not found in the world of separation' but given to Israel in the soul (Sh'lach 16:3). Tzitzit on the outermost garment gathers the four corners to their root, for 'there is a hidden point in every place' (Sh'lach 5:5; G04, G05). (*stated*)
7. The measure (twelve finger-breadths) is the Gevurah through which the mitzvot were given (IH 10:5; F03). (*stated*)
8. Breslov reads tzitzit as a guard against the serpent's counsel and adultery, which opens one to the tzaddik's counsel (Likutei Moharan 7:4). No tie to the unity is made there, and none is forced here. (**reading**)

- **Other schools.** R. Tzadok (Tzidkat HaTzadik 220): tzitzit remind of the unity and push off foreign thoughts. The Sefat Emet (Sh'lach 5:5, 16:3, 16:4): tallit as union in the root of unity; white and blue as positive and negative commandments. Breslov (Likutei Moharan 7:4, 8:8): tzitzit guard the eyes from the serpent's counsel; the four corners are the four winds of breath. ## S094 · Circumcision and covenant · ברית מילה

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F05 The Essence beyond both · F06 The soul as part of the One · F07 The home below **Search terms:** ברית מילה · מילה · ערלה · ברית

> Circumcision is the unity of God as the removal of a concealment. In the flesh, the foreskin makes Elokim a total hiding; cutting it reveals a light from Ein Sof higher than there would have been, and seals in the body a covenant that belongs to the very essence of Israel.

## Derivation

1. The Tzemach Tzedek opens with the error of Enosh's generation, who served the sun and the stars, the instruments of Elokim, as though they were independent powers (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Milah 5). Then: 'the Name Elokim is the vessel that clothes the light; only in holiness are all one and Havayah is the Elokim, while in the husk it becomes total concealment' (Milah 6; G14). (*stated*)
2. The foreskin is that husk. We were commanded 'to remove from ourselves this concealment that hides entirely, for it does not belong to us at all' (Milah 8; F03). (*stated*)
3. Why create it, then? 'Lupines need repair': by removing it, 'a new light from Ein Sof is revealed, far higher than the light there would have been without it' (Milah 13). This is concealment serving revelation, with the revelation greater for the concealment. (*stated*)
4. That light is from Adam Kadmon, the Keter of all worlds ('Ani', the letters of 'Ayin'); so milah overrides Shabbat (Milah 13, 16). The Rebbe: milah draws a revelation 'above Havayah' (Torah Ohr: 'mi ya'aleh lanu hashamaymah', initial letters milah, final letters Havayah), and yet it was intended from the start of creation (Shabbat Shuvah 5723; F05). For that reason the Rebbe says the revelations drawn by the mitzvot, 'milah included, are not truly a new light' (unlike teshuvah's). This qualifies the Tzemach Tzedek's 'new light, far higher' (Milah 13); the two use 'new' in different senses, and the difference is kept, not smoothed. (*stated*)
5. Beyond being one of the 613, milah is the covenant 'that belongs to the very essence of Israel's holiness'. Someone who cannot be circumcised is still called 'mahul', because the name follows the obligation to enter the covenant (Likkutei Sichos 30, Lech Lecha 3; F06). (*stated*)
6. It is sealed in the flesh and by deed. Avraham first recognized his Creator (thought), then proclaimed Him (speech), and only then was circumcised (deed) (Hayom Yom 7 Cheshvan). The Sefat Emet: even one born circumcised needs blood drawn, since 'His covenant is sealed in the soul of Israel, yet must be revealed by the deed of man' (Ki Teitzei 15:2; F07, G11). (*stated*)
7. The person's side: the Tzemach Tzedek answered his weeping grandson, 'why did God appear to Avraham and not to us?': when a Jew and tzaddik at ninety-nine decides that he must circumcise himself, he deserves that God appear to him (Hayom Yom 9 Cheshvan). That this is the bitul of an already complete man (F08) is our reading. (**reading**)

- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim, Noach (line 721, seen in search): why Avraham's milah waited twenty-four years. Not read.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Milah 1-25 (lines 7-31): orlah as nogah; star-worship; removal of concealment; a new light from Adam Kadmon; peri'ah; netzach reaching the soul's essence above intellect (Milah 21-23).
- **Maharash.** Hayom Yom 9 Cheshvan: the story is about the Maharash's son (the Rashab) and the Tzemach Tzedek. Not in the Maharash's own text.
- **Rashab.** Hayom Yom 9 Cheshvan (as the child in the story). Not searched in his works.

## S095 · Sacrifices · קרבנות

**Faces:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F07 The home below · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** קרבנות · קרבן · מזבח · אש של מעלה

> Sacrifice is the unity of God as nearness made from below. The animal soul's fire rises into the fire that comes down from above, and what is refined is included in Godliness, so that His Godliness may be drawn down.

## Derivation

1. Korban is kiruv, drawing near. 'The whole matter of the korban is to draw down the revelation of His Godliness below', a tzavta of Or Ein Sof with creatures made through the ayin that mediates between Emanator and emanated (Maharash, Toras Shmuel 5627). (*stated*)
2. 'Adam ki yakriv mikem': 'from you' is the divine soul alone; 'your offering from the animal' is refining the animal soul, subduing and then transforming it by contemplating God's greatness until it, too, loves (Likkutei Torah, Vayikra 2:16-17; F11, F08). (*stated*)
3. The fire below in the offering rises and is included in the fire above that descends on the altar (Likkutei Torah, Pinchas 1:15). The Tzemach Tzedek: the outer altar is Malchus lowered into the lower worlds to refine nogah; the animal burns, the dross is cast out, 'and what is refined is included in Godliness in the fire above' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sanctuary 3). That this inclusion is bitul in the One (F08) is our gloss. (*stated*)
4. Sacrifices are bounded: 'the sacrifices are in measure and limit', which is why charity given without limit is 'more chosen than the altar' (IH 10:11). The measure comes from the Gevurah through which the mitzvot were given (IH 10:3-5; F03). (*stated*)
5. The ascent is not the goal. The Rebbe: sacrifices are an ascent from below, but 'the aim of the intention is the drawing down of Godliness below, for the Holy One desired a dwelling in the lowest realm'. So the will in sacrifices is the outer will, and kindness, drawing down, is the inner: 'I desire kindness and not sacrifice' (Mishpatim 5731; F07). (*stated*)
6. Now the table is the altar. Permitted meat and wine eaten for God's sake 'rise to God like a burnt-offering and a sacrifice' (Tanya 7:4). Chernobyl: eating and fasting are both called korban, 'two opposites in one subject', because both are devekut: eating draws near the sparks and the supernal life clothed in the food, from below upward, while fasting draws the divine part near its root from above downward (Me'or Einayim, Emor 11-13). Filing this under F09 is our reading. (*stated*)
7. Prayer takes the sacrifice's place (Likkutei Torah, Pinchas 1:15). Breslov: prayer is called korban for drawing the worlds near to their completion (Likutei Moharan 14:8, seen in search). (*stated*)

## S096 · The Sanctuary and the Temple · משכן ובית המקדש

**Faces:** F07 The home below · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F05 The Essence beyond both · F06 The soul as part of the One **Search terms:** משכן · בית המקדש · קדש הקדשים · מקדש

> The Sanctuary is the unity of God as a place. It is a dwelling below built after the pattern of the whole chain from the Essence, where the Ark stood measured yet took no space, Havayah and Elokim one in one spot; now it is every person and every four cubits of halacha.

## Derivation

1. The Mishkan and its Holy of Holies are 'for the resting of His Shechinah, which is the revelation of His unity' (Tanya 34:3; F07). (*stated*)
2. It is modelled on the whole chain drawn from the Essence of Ein Sof, circles, line and vessels, so that Godliness be revealed in Israel (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sanctuary 2; G04). It is built by subduing and transforming: 'the Mishkan and ''I will dwell among them'' is the dwelling below' (Likkutei Torah, Pekudei 1:4). (*stated*)
3. The place above place. The Rashab: the place of the Temple was not a physical place; 'in the Holy of Holies the place was above place', as the Ark took no space, which made it a vessel for Havayah above space and time (Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 3656). (*stated*)
4. The Rebbe: the Ark's bounds are Elokim, and its taking no space is Havayah, so 'the place of the Ark is not in measure' is 'the union of Havayah and Elokim'. Because the contraction of Elokim conceals only toward creatures, Havayah shines in the place itself (Matot-Masei 5722, lines 2737-2740; G14, F02, F03). (*stated*)
5. The Rashab's question is the spine's: how can the world, a place of concealment, hold this? His answer is not that the tzimtzum is abolished but that the tzimtzum itself stop concealing, while remaining (Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 3657). This is the Temple as the model of the world's end. (*stated*)
6. Since the destruction, His sanctuary in the world is 'the four cubits of halacha', and anyone who studies makes himself 'a sanctuary and a place for His dwelling' (Tanya 34:4). (*stated*)
7. 'Betocham, not betocho': in each person. The Degel: the Mishkan is written in the eternal Torah to show how every person can build a sanctuary within; Torah Ohr (Mishpatim 5:5): Israel are the vessels for His Shechinah, 'for the purpose of creation was a dwelling below' (F06, F07). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 34:3-4; Likkutei Torah, Pekudei 1:4 and following; Torah Ohr, Mishpatim 5:5-6 (read): the Shechinah now rests only in souls, not bodies, until the future.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not searched for this subject.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sanctuary 1-4 (lines 158-161): the Mikdash as the pattern of the chain; the altars; Betzalel's order (dwelling before vessels) following the order of emanation.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel lines 2070 and 10133-10154 (seen in search): in the Holy of Holies place itself was nullified; Aharon draws what is above space and time into space and time.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, lines 3656-3657; also 1392 and 5039 (seen): the revelation of Or Ein Sof itself in the Holy of Holies.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim lines 1071 and 1560 (seen in search): every measure of the Temple fixed and bounded. Not quoted.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Matot-Masei 5722 (lines 2733-2741): the union of Havayah and Elokim in the Ark's place; the third Temple as the full revelation of 'lit din atar hedyot'.
- **Other schools.** The Degel (Ki Tisa 3): every person a sanctuary, by refining his traits. Chernobyl (Me'or Einayim, line 428; seen): each Jew a mishkan. Ger (Sefat Emet, lines 1571, 2628; seen): the Mikdash is a sign of the light of holiness in Israel's souls. Kedushat Levi (Bamidbar 3): the Mishkan as the lights of the 613 contracted. The Chassidic masters agree on 'betocham'. The Chabad addition is the structural claim (Havayah and Elokim one in the Ark; the chain from the Essence).

## S097 · Forbidden foods · מאכלות אסורות

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F11 Darkness turned to light · F01 The world said into being now · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** מאכלות אסורות · כשרות · טרפה · בשר בחלב

> Forbidden food is the unity of God as a boundary. Everything holds a spark of His life, but what is drawn from the wholly impure husks is 'bound' and cannot yet be raised, so eating it binds the eater to what stands apart from Him.

## Derivation

1. There is 'a gleam of life in everything', but in measure and contraction (Sefat Emet, Shmini 14:3; F01, F03). Even forbidden animals and plants have their existence and life from the three wholly impure husks (Tanya 6:12). (*stated*)
2. Permitted food is 'unbound'. Eaten for God's sake it rises 'like a burnt-offering'; eaten for lust it descends, but can rise again with the person when he returns: 'that is the meaning of heter and mutar, not tied and bound in the hands of the outside forces' (Tanya 7:4-6). (*stated*)
3. Forbidden food is 'bound in the hands of the outside forces for ever', until death is swallowed up, or until teshuvah from great love turns sins into merits (Tanya 7:9-10). This is the limit of F11 in the present: some darkness is not yet transformable, and only teshuvah from great love reaches it now. Tanya 7 does not name the Essence here; tying this to F11's 'only the Essence can turn evil to good' is our reading. (*stated*)
4. The Rayatz: pure and permitted belong to holiness, where mitzvah is joining with the Essence; forbidden and impure are bound, and whoever does them 'passes from the domain of the Holy One to the domain of the husk' (Sefer HaMaamarim, 19 Kislev 5692; F08). (*stated*)
5. The Sefat Emet: forbidden food imprisons both the soul and 'the holy spark within it', which cannot be revealed. Israel, freed from Egypt, is not to draw strength from them, 'no strange god in you'; holy eating is freedom, 'the table before God', for 'the whole earth is full of His glory, only nature hides the truth' (Shmini 14:3; Re'eh 25:3). (*stated*)
6. Sochatchov: food becomes the body, so food from the impure husks spoils the body so that a holy soul cannot rest on it; the body's repair is 'submission and bitul and drawing to God's will' (Shem MiShmuel, Shmini 2:17-18). The tie to F08 is ours. (**reading**)
7. At the level of single laws the tie thins out. Derekh Mitzvotekha's section on the sciatic nerve maps it to Netzach and Tisha b'Av, and neither it nor the halachic works consulted for this cluster state a tie to the unity. (**reading**)

Sochatchov (Shem MiShmuel, Shmini 2:17-18): the eaten becomes the eater. The Sefat Emet frames it as freedom and 'no strange god', close to F08; Chabad frames it as the reach of birur.

## Thin or forced?

Partly thin. The tie is stated for the category: permitted food is unbound and can rise, forbidden food is bound to what is separate from Him (Tanya 7; Rayatz; Sefat Emet). It is not stated for individual laws (meat and milk, treifah, the sciatic nerve), where the sources give kabbalistic or halachic reasons. Here the unity appears negatively, as the boundary of what can now be raised.

## S098 · Supra-rational decrees · חוקים / פרה אדומה

**Faces:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F05 The Essence beyond both (our reading here) · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** חוקה · חוקים · פרה אדומה · גזירה

> The statute is the unity of God as a will above reason. In every mitzvah the Owner of the will stands beyond any reason given for it, and the red heifer is the one place where that will stands uncovered and is kept by bitul alone.

## Derivation

1. The mitzvot are His will (F10), and that will is 'above being grasped by any creature at all, even in Atzilut' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Carrying the Ark 3). (*stated*)
2. So 'in truth all mitzvot are called statutes', the King's decree, because the reasons revealed to us are not their true reasons, and to our eyes they are 'His will without an understood reason' (same). Berditchev: 'all the Torah and mitzvot are statutes for us'; the soul would serve without pause, and the body resists only because it does not know the reasons (Kedushat Levi, Chukat 1). (*stated*)
3. The Rebbe: the red heifer is 'the statute of the Torah' because 'the foundation of every mitzvah is statute: the mitzvot are the supernal will above intellect'. In every other mitzvah the reason conceals the will; in the red heifer the will is revealed (Chukat 5729). Calling this G03 turned inward (reason as the veil, the statute removing it) is our label; the Rebbe's own word is that the reason 'conceals' the will. (*stated*)
4. The response is kabbalat ol, and kabbalat ol is bitul, Moshe's quality ('and we are what?'). That is why the heifer is 'taken to you', named for Moshe, who gives every Jew the power to keep all the mitzvot as statutes (same maamar; F08). (*stated*)
5. Likkutei Torah (Chukat 2:2): 'the statute of the Torah which Havayah commanded' is a tzavta joining Havayah of Atik, above wisdom, to Chochmah, drawing the Torah from its root above wisdom into wisdom; the text calls this 'drawing and revealing sovev in memale' (F04). It speaks of Atik, not of the Essence itself, so an F05 link here is our reading. (*stated*)
6. A kept tension: David called the statutes 'songs', praising the will-side as a tune sweet however often repeated, and was punished by forgetting that the Ark is carried on the shoulder (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Carrying the Ark 2-3). The will is outer relative to delight, and delight differs in each mitzvah; the Rebbe's praise of the uncovered will seems to pull the other way. The two may be using 'will' in different senses (the Tzemach Tzedek's outer will against delight, the Rebbe's will above reason); the texts do not settle it, and we do not. (*stated*)
7. The Izhbitzer pairs the two statutes with the two first commandments: Pesach with 'Anochi', the heifer with 'no other gods' (Mei HaShiloach, Bo 5). That the statute thereby guards the unity against the 'other' is our gloss; the Izhbitzer states the unity for Moshe's 'Anochi' and speaks of the heifer's side as purging evil from the heart. (*stated*)

- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis, Chukat 5672 (lines 167, 185-188; seen): the double Havayah as memale and sovev. Not quoted.
- **Rayatz.** Not found for this subject in the clean texts searched.

Ruzhin on the heifer that purifies the impure and defiles the pure (Chassidus Concepts, pillar 14): not re-verified here. Berditchev and the Rebbe agree that all mitzvot are statutes; they differ on why. For Berditchev it is our ignorance of reasons; for the Rebbe the will is above even the true reasons.

## S099 · Sanctifying the Name · קידוש השם

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** קידוש השם · קדוש השם · חילול השם · על קידוש השם

> Sanctifying the Name is the unity of God as the soul's refusal to be separated from the One even for a moment. The Or Ein Sof clothed in every Jew's Chochmah, above time and reason, gives up the self rather than deny 'the One God'.

## Derivation

1. Chochmah is 'what is not yet grasped', and so Or Ein Sof, which no thought grasps, is clothed in it; therefore all Israel, even women and the unlearned, believe, 'for faith is above knowledge' (Tanya 18:10-11; F06, F12). (*stated*)
2. Hence 'even the lightest of the light and the sinners of Israel give their lives for the sanctification of God, and suffer harsh tortures rather than deny the One God', without knowledge or reason, 'as though it were impossible to deny' (Tanya 18:12-13). (*stated*)
3. Not even for a moment, with teshuvah after, because 'the light of God clothed in their soul is not in the category of time at all' (Tanya 25:5). By the same reasoning every sin, even a rabbinic one, is 'a separation from His unity, like idolatry itself' (Tanya 25:3; F08). (*stated*)
4. The Tzemach Tzedek: self-sacrifice needs no root, 'it is itself the main thing, for which man was created'. Chochmah is called 'kodesh', separate, because the Or Ein Sof clothed in it is separate from all worlds (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Sanctification of the Name). Kiddush Hashem draws the surrounding light into the filling; chillul Hashem blocks the revelation of Godliness and makes 'an empty void' even of the filling light (Profaning the Name; F04). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe: 'mesirut nefesh is bitul', and since man is a 'something' he needs the power of Moshe to keep it always in mind; keeping the Torah depends on remembering one's self-sacrifice (Chukat 5729, citing Tanya 25 on Shema). (*stated*)
6. Elsewhere: the Noam Elimelekh makes it a practice, to picture the fire in every idle moment and throw oneself in, with the thought counting as the deed. Breslov grounds it in 'Israel rose in thought' and God's delight even in sinners (Likutei Moharan 80:2). The Piaseczner, in the Warsaw Ghetto, names sufferings 'that we bear only with Him, sufferings of kiddush Hashem', in which Israel carries His burden (Esh Kodesh, Matot 5702). The Esh Kodesh does not name the unity; filing this under the spine (His presence with Israel inside the hiding, F03) is our reading, as is any tie of the Noam Elimelekh's and Breslov's grounds to the unity. (**reading**)

Breslov (Likutei Moharan 80:2): self-sacrifice as love followed by overcoming, chasadim then gevurot, 'Yosef' joined to 'Yisrael'. Piaseczno (Esh Kodesh, Matot 5702): suffering for the Name as suffering with Him. Berditchev (Kedushat Levi, Vayera, seen): Avraham reckoned Yitzchak 'as a lamb' for the sanctification of His Name. ## S100 · Priests and Levites · כהנים ולוים

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F01 The world said into being now · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F07 The home below **Search terms:** כהן גדול · כהנים · לוים · עבודת הכהנים

> Priests and Levites are the unity of God as a service set apart within Israel: the High Priest's cleaving from the yechidah, the Levite's sight of the flow from the Cause of causes, and the priest's love in bitul, through which blessing comes down without hindrance.

## Derivation

1. The High Priest's task is 'constant devekut with the Holy One, above measure and limit, a devekut that comes from the essence of his soul, the yechidah, which is united with Him' (Likkutei Sichos 33, Bamidbar 1; F06). He does not leave the Temple. (*stated*)
2. The priest is the chesed of Abba, 'love in the mode of bitul', drawn through Torah (Likkutei Torah, Chukat 8:15; F08). Aharon is 'nir'eh', the one 'through whom all the revelations and drawings of light from above to below' came, swiftly and without hindrance, like almonds; hence the priestly blessing (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Priestly Blessing). (*stated*)
3. The Levites stand for seeing 'how the flow comes at every moment from the Cause of causes' onto the intellect, where Israel stand for the intellectual lights of the 613; so the Levites are shaved, being 'without garment' (Kedushat Levi, Bamidbar 3; F01). (*stated*)
4. The Izhbitzer: 'a kohen is whoever serves God and sees that nothing happening in the world is by chance, but all is providence from God', and knows that God wills only good. So he is warned not to 'become impure for the dead', which means not to complain against His judgment (Mei HaShiloach, Emor 1; F01, G12). (*stated*)
5. The priesthood is not holiness in its own right. The Rebbe cites the Rambam: 'not only the tribe of Levi, but every person whose spirit moves him' may stand before God. R. Tzadok, in his own answer to a question his teacher (רה"ק) put to him: of the five holinesses said of the priests, three are God's and two derive from Israel's, 'the holiness of the priests is emanated from the holiness of Israel' (Peri Tzadik, Emor 7). That this roots the priesthood in the soul of Israel (F06) is our reading. (**reading**)
6. That the priesthood expresses the unity only through what it stands for (devekut, providence, kindness flowing down), and not as an institution of lineage, blemish and portions, is our reading of the sources above. (**reading**)

- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not searched for this subject.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Priestly Blessing (line 202); Blemished Priest (lines 70-74), which points back to other sections (love of one's fellow; milah on the eighth day) without its own statement.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel line 10136 (seen): 'Aharon's virtue is from above to below, from above space and time into space and time'. Not read in context.
- **Rashab.** Not searched for this subject.
- **Rayatz.** Not searched for this subject.
- **The Rebbe.** Likkutei Sichos 33, Bamidbar 1 (lines 11343-11349): Israel, Levi and the High Priest as three modes of service; the Rambam on 'every person'.
- **Other schools.** Izhbitz (Mei HaShiloach, Emor 1-3): the kohen as one who sees providence; the priest as the head of Israel's body, 'as thought is to the soul'. Lublin (Peri Tzadik, Emor 7): holiness conferred from Israel. Berditchev (Kedushat Levi, Bamidbar 3): the Levites and the flow every moment; Korach 9 (seen): the kohen is chesed and the Levi gevurah. The real difference is whether the priest's holiness is his own or Israel's. The plain sense and the Rebbe's High Priest (from the yechidah) speak of his own; the Izhbitzer and R. Tzadok say two of its five parts derive from Israel.

> — Berditchev: Israel are the lights of the 613; the Levites stand for seeing how the flow comes at every moment from the Cause of causes.

> שמקדושת ישראל נאצל קדושת הכהנים
> — R. Tzadok (his own answer to a question from his teacher): of the priests' five holinesses three are God's and two derive from Israel's; the priests' holiness is emanated from Israel's.

## Thin or forced?

Partly thin. The institution itself (lineage, blemishes, portions) carries no stated tie; Derekh Mitzvotekha's section on the blemished priest points elsewhere. The tie is stated for the functions the priest and Levite stand for: devekut from the yechidah, love in bitul, seeing providence. The subject list's 'the priest as chesed' is stated; R. Tzadok's point (Peri Tzadik) is about the source of holiness, not about the unity.

## Disagreements kept

| question | positions | subjects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does the mitzvah join? | Chabad: the command joins the person to the Essence, from above; creation is only a radiance (Maharash, Toras Shmuel 5627; Likkutei Torah, Vaetchanan; Toras Chaim; the Rayatz; the Rebbe, Rosh Hashanah 5743). Chernobyl and Berditchev: tzavta is devekut, the divine part in man cleaving to its root, and without it the mitzvah is a body without soul. Ger: each mitzvah draws the soul's life into its limb, joining body and soul. The Maggid: joining, with sin as separation; through 'know Him in all your ways' the mitzvot become countless. | S089 |
| Is creation or the mitzvah the bearer of the Essence? | The Maharash: creation is 'only a mere radiance'; the mitzvot join with the Essence (Toras Shmuel 5627, line 537). IH 20 and the Rebbe (spine F05): only the Essence can create yesh from nothing. The two stand; the spine already keeps this tension. | S089 |
| Do the mitzvot reach the Essence, or leave it untouched? | The Maharash in 5627: creation is a mere radiance, but through the mitzvot 'there is a tzavta and joining with the Essence of Or Ein Sof' (Toras Shmuel 5627, line 537). The Maharash in 5631: 'the whole matter of Torah and mitzvot is only a tzimtzum', for 'from the side of His Essence, if you sinned, what do you do to Him'; that there are positive and negative commandments at all is 'from the mouth of Gevurah' (Toras Shmuel 5631, line 2794). The Rebbe (Rosh Hashanah 5743): kept as His command, the mitzvah is union 'up to the Essence'. The two Maharash statements stand; any reconciliation is our reading. | S089, S090, S091 |
| Which is higher, the positive or the negative commandments? | Tanya 37: the negatives remove impurity, the positives draw Or Ein Sof. Likkutei Torah, Pekudei 1: the negatives are rooted in Y-H, above the positives' V-H, and are kept by refraining because they have no vessel below. Kuntres Acharon 5: in the future the negatives are raised in their root and included with the positives. The Izhbitzer: logically 'turn from evil' first, but God gave 'Anochi' first in mercy. | S090, S098 |
| Is the aim of sacrifice the ascent or the descent? | Likkutei Torah, Vayikra and Pinchas: refining the animal soul and including the fire below in the fire above. The Rebbe (Mishpatim 5731): the ascent is for the sake of the drawing-down, the dwelling below; so sacrifice is the 'outer will' and kindness the 'inner'. IH 10: charity without limit is 'more chosen than the altar', which is bounded. | S095, S091 |
| Is the will or the delight the deeper side of the statute? | The Tzemach Tzedek (Carrying the Ark 2-3): David praised the will-side of the statutes as a repeated song and was punished; will is the outer relative to delight. The Rebbe (Chukat 5729): the statute is where the will above reason stands uncovered, and all mitzvot are statutes on the side of the will. Berditchev: all mitzvot are statutes because we do not know their reasons. | S098 |
| Why do even sinners give their lives for the Name? | Tanya 18 and 25: Or Ein Sof is clothed in the soul's Chochmah, above knowledge and above time. Breslov (Likutei Moharan 80): because 'Israel rose in thought' and God delights in anyone still called Israel. The Noam Elimelekh makes it a daily imagined practice. The Piaseczner: suffering for the Name is suffering with Him. | S099 |
| Is the priest's holiness his own? | The plain sense ('holy to their God') and the Rebbe's High Priest, whose devekut comes from his yechidah. R. Tzadok (Peri Tzadik, Emor 7): two of the five holinesses of the priests derive from Israel's holiness. The Rebbe citing the Rambam: 'not only the tribe of Levi but every person'. | S100 |
| What are tzitzit for? | Chabad (Tanya 41; Derekh Mitzvotekha): drawing His kingship around one; white and tekhelet as the higher and lower bitul. R. Tzadok and the Sefat Emet: remembering the unity; union in the root of unity. The Sefat Emet also reads white and tekhelet as positive and negative commandments, with tekhelet (holiness) above white (purity), against the Tzemach Tzedek's tekhelet as the lower bitul. Breslov (Likutei Moharan 7): a guard of the eyes against the serpent's counsel, opening one to the tzaddik's counsel, with no tie to the unity. | S093 |
