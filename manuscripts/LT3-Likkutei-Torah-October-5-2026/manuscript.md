*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A collection of maamarim the Alter Rebbe delivered on the festivals, edited by the Tzemach Tzedek, who added a dense web of cross-references. This part covers the High Holidays, Sukkot and Shir HaShirim. Each maamar opens from a verse or a teaching of the Sages, builds a Kabbalistic structure (the surrounding and the indwelling light, the thirteen attributes of mercy, keter, and so on), and then turns to "avodah," the inner service that brings that structure down into the person. The High Holiday maamarim teach teshuvah as waking compassion for the fallen soul and bending one's own will. The Sukkot maamarim teach joy as the uncovering of what was hidden. Shir HaShirim treats hidden love and longing, and the descent that makes the soul beautiful.

**How much of it is medicine.** some. Most of the range is Kabbalistic cosmology and exegesis (partzufim, Atik, surrounding light, sefirot), but in almost every maamar there are real, concentrated avodah passages on teshuvah, worry, a heart that feels nothing, scattered attention, hidden love and joy.

**Its model of the person.** A divine soul, a literal "part of God" that is never separate from Him, is clothed in a body and an animal soul. It is so rooted in them that it feels only itself and its own wants. The mind can grasp a truth while the heart stays unmoved. Change runs through will, which governs thought and feeling. It happens by deep contemplation that humbles and softens, by compassion for the fallen spark, and by bitterness that turns into love. The concealment itself generates a "returning light" that is greater than the light was before. Joy comes from widening the mind. Growth moves as running and returning (ratzo v'shov), and it has to come back down into daily deeds.

**Its philosophy.** God is unchanging and beyond time: "I the Lord have not changed." From His side nothing ever separated; only the person's own sins and coarseness act as screens. Remove the screen and the infinite light shows itself of its own accord. Every hiding is there for the sake of a greater revelation and a greater joy. The soul's descent into a body exists for the sake of an ascent that the angels cannot reach.

**What it adds.** Teshuvah here is compassion for the fallen divine spark, not self-concern: praying for oneself is called the lowest level. The desiring power is holy at its root and can be turned into love. A heart broken 'by effort' still counts. Worry about livelihood is strange fire to be burned in morning prayer, while earning is praised. God's hiding is a father's game of hide-and-seek. 'Black yet beautiful': the beauty comes from the darkness itself. Ratzo without shov is pride. Weeping and joy are to be held together.

## I know God is everything, but I feel nothing

*Its own term:* לא יתפעל בנפשו / לא כליתם

He understands the ideas and even agrees with them, but nothing reaches his core. He is drawn after bodily needs, and the spiritual feels thin and far away.

**What is really going on.** The divine soul is clothed in, and rooted in, the animal soul. It is in a state of yeshus and coarseness (gasus), so it feels only itself and its own will, and the infinite light reaches it only from the outside, as surrounding light.

**Program: Humbling contemplation for the unmoved heart.** (1) Notice honestly that you understand the truth yet are not moved by it. (2) Refuse the plea 'this is how I was made'; nature can be changed and desire broken. (3) Contemplate in depth how far your yeshus and coarseness reach, until the contemplation humbles you. (4) Accept being 'broken by effort'. It is not despised even though it is not natural. (5) Be nullified to the light that surrounds you, even while it does not enter the heart. (6) Let the resulting kindness loosen the soul's attachment to the body's constraints. *When:* Especially the Ten Days of Repentance. Done through extended contemplation, as preparation for prayer. *For:* A stable person whose inner life has gone flat, who rationalizes and is complacent, and who has the strength to look at himself. *Not for:* Someone depressed, self-loathing or traumatized, for whom self-crushing ('bitush') would deepen harm. `LT3·Q03` · `LT3·Q04` · `LT3·Q05` · `LT3·Q06` · `LT3·Q07` · `LT3·Q08`

**Reframe.** From *"If my brokenness only came from forcing myself, it's fake and worthless."* to **A heart broken by effort is not despised. David prayed that it too would be accepted, and it makes you nullified to the light around you.** Kindness flows to what is low. Once he has become 'crushed' in his own eyes, he is nullified to the surrounding light, and the kindness separates the divine soul from its bondage. `LT3·Q07` · `LT3·Q08`

## My desires make me bad

He experiences his strong desire as proof that he is corrupt at the root.

**What is really going on.** The desiring power is neutral in itself, since it is the same for what is permitted and what is forbidden, and its root is in supernal holiness. Only the deed is evil. The same power can turn from the one end to the other, into love of God.

**Program: Teshuvah by compassion for the fallen spark.** (1) Know your root: the soul was once one with its source. (2) Know where you stand now; find where the heart has turned toward cravings. (3) Contemplate the 'part of God' in you that fell from 'a high roof' into a body, and arouse great mercy for it, not only for your own needs. (4) Say the confession in order to wake that mercy; weeping is for the soul that fell. (5) Do not stop at a little love; draw divinity down through Torah and mitzvot with intent. *When:* Rosh Hashanah through Yom Kippur above all, and in daily prayer and confession. *For:* Someone who feels far away or guilty and needs a way back that is not self-hatred. *Not for:* Someone who would turn 'compassion on the soul' into despair, or who is pressured to weep on demand. `LT3·Q10` · `LT3·Q11` · `LT3·Q12` · `LT3·Q16` · `LT3·Q09` · `LT3·Q02`

**Reframe.** From *"My strong desires prove I'm evil at the root."* to **The power to desire is holy at its root. Only the act is bad, and the same power can become love of God.** 'Bearing iniquity' lifts the desiring power back to its root, so the same drive is redirected rather than destroyed. `LT3·Q02`

## I can't stop worrying about money

Anxiety about livelihood binds him downward, even at prayer and before he eats.

**What is really going on.** The work itself is praised. The worry is 'strange fire', the fiery heat of the animal soul, and it has to be burned up by the higher fire of love in prayer. Total immersion in worldly affairs pulls the mind down and can lead to doubts in faith.

**Program: Burning worry in morning prayer.** (1) Separate the work of earning, which is praised, from the worry about it, which is strange fire. (2) Pray before eating, so that you are not bound below to the bread and to your worries. (3) Reckon honestly with the ordinary words and deeds that were not for God; let the bitterness become kindling. (4) In prayer, wake the natural love of the divine soul so that it flares up and consumes the worry. (5) From there move on to the 'water' of great love that quenches thirst. *When:* Every morning, at prayer and before eating. *For:* Someone whose ordinary money anxiety takes over his inner life. *Not for:* Someone in real financial crisis who needs practical help, or someone with clinical anxiety; the text does not dismiss the need to earn. `LT3·Q22` · `LT3·Q23` · `LT3·Q25` · `LT3·Q15`

**Reframe.** From *"Worrying about money is just being responsible."* to **Earning is praised; the worry is strange fire from the animal soul, to be burned in prayer.** Separating the task from the anxiety lets him keep working while giving the fear over to prayer. `LT3·Q22` · `LT3·Q23`

## My mind is scattered and I can't settle to pray

*Its own term:* פיזור הנפש / מחשבות זרות

His intellect and emotions are spread thin across pleasures, plans and schemes, and distracting thoughts crowd in.

**What is really going on.** The mind's three faculties and the heart's seven emotions have scattered into the pleasures of this world, and the daily teshuvah of gathering them back has not been done.

**Program: Daily gathering of the scattered self (Shema).** (1) At prayer time, turn from all your affairs as if it were Shabbat. (2) Set aside the distracting thoughts that trouble you. (3) Contemplate that spirit is life and matter is death, and choose life, the Life of all life. (4) Gather the scattered intellect and emotions back to their source. (5) Bend your will, since thought, mind and emotion all follow the will. *When:* Daily at the Shema. On Shabbat, the whole day is this kind of return. *For:* Anyone at all, including someone who never sinned; the soul's clothing in a body is enough. *Not for:* It should not be pursued as a forced blankness that punishes every stray thought. `LT3·Q18` · `LT3·Q20` · `LT3·Q21` · `LT3·Q30`

**Reframe.** From *"Teshuvah is for sinners; I don't need it."* to **Every soul in a body is separated from its source and needs daily return.** Teshuvah becomes the daily gathering of a scattered self rather than a verdict of guilt, which makes it open to everyone. `LT3·Q30` · `LT3·Q18`

## I blow up or cry over small things

Every small thing that goes against his will sets him off in anger or tears.

**What is really going on.** His awareness (da'at) is narrow, so it spills straight into the emotions. The wider the da'at, the harder it is to become angry. (The text gives this only as an analogy, not as a program.)

## My spiritual highs don't come down into my life

He wants only to rise and dissolve into God, and has no interest in ordinary deeds.

**What is really going on.** Running upward on its own is a kind of pride. The divine intent is a dwelling place below, so every run has to return into Torah study, charity and prayer.

**Program: Running and returning.** (1) When love and longing to cleave to God rise, let them rise. (2) Then return: bring it down into Torah study of practical law, charity, acts of kindness and prayer with intent. *When:* Whenever an inspiration comes. *For:* Someone prone to spiritual highs that escape life. *Not for:* No exclusions are stated. `LT3·Q36`

**Reframe.** From *"Real spirituality is rising above the world."* to **Running upward on its own is pride. The height is in returning to make a dwelling below.** It points the soul's longing toward concrete deeds, which is where the divine intent is fulfilled. `LT3·Q36`

Teshuvah and bitterness leave no room for gladness.

**What is really going on.** Joy is the uncovering of what was hidden. It grows from widening the mind, and it is meant to sit together with the weeping in the same heart.

## Cautions

- Self-crushing ('yevatesh atzmo', lowering the soul like dust) can deepen harm in someone depressed or self-hating; it is meant for complacent people, not broken ones. - The Sukkot program includes becoming 'loathsome in his own eyes and despising his life'. Never offer this to anyone with suicidal thoughts or severe shame. - 'Bear a life of hardship' is a call to break a rationalized habit. It is not a license for asceticism or for tolerating abuse. - Never demand tears on Rosh Hashanah. Tears are for the soul's descent, and someone who cannot cry is not defective. - Burning worry in prayer must not dismiss a real financial crisis or clinical anxiety; the text itself praises earning. - 'Sadness from murky blood' is premodern physiology. Do not present it as a medical account of depression. - The hidden-love teaching speaks of Jewish souls. Carrying it to everyone should be done openly as an extension, not presented as the text's plain claim. - The angry-child passage is only an analogy for divine anger; it gives no program for anger.
