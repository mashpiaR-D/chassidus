The Introduction (Hakdamah) opens the printed Zohar. It is not one teaching but a chain of openings, stories and night talks: the rose among thorns; "Who created these" (Mi and Mah); the letters that came before God asking to be the letter of creation; the donkey driver who turns out to be the son of Rav Hamnuna Sava; the night of the bride (the Shavuot night); and, at the end, Rabbi Shimon's list of the first fourteen commandments read out of the first chapter of Genesis. The whole Zohar is Aramaic. Quotes below are as the Sefaria text has them (nikud dropped when copying; the checker ignores nikud), with a plain English rendering.

## 2. The unity of God

The Hakdamah says "there is only One" in four ways, and each will come back through the whole book.

**Mi and Mah: the One is what cannot be asked about.** The highest level is called "Who" because it stands as a question: «ועל דקימא לשאלה ואיהו בארח סתים ולא אתגליא, אקרי מ"י» (Introduction 3:1; line 7): "because it stands open to question, and is hidden and not revealed, it is called Mi (Who)." The level below is "What": a person searches level after level to the end and arrives at "what have you seen?": «מה ידעת, מה אסתכלתא, מה פשפשתא, הא כלא סתים כדקדמיתא» (Introduction 3:2; line 8): "What have you known? What have you seen? What have you searched? It is all as hidden as before." And the two are one: «וכמה דאשתתף מ"י באלה, הכי הוא שמא דאשתתף תדיר. וברזא דא אתקיים עלמא» (Introduction 4:5; line 15): "As Mi joined with Eleh, so the Name is always joined; and by this secret the world stands." The world stands because the hidden and the named are never apart.

**The point and the palace.** «בשעתא דסתימא דכל סתימין בעא לאתגליא, עבד ברישא נקוד"ה … חדא, ודא סליק למהוי מחשבה» (Introduction 4:3; line 13): "When the Most Hidden of all hidden wished to be revealed, He made first one point, and this rose to be Thought." This is the Zohar's first picture of how the One becomes many without ceasing to be one: a point, then a palace, then a name. (The opening of Bereshit, lines 261-262, repeats it with the "spark of darkness" and the point that shines out of the split in Ein Sof. It is read in the next report.)

**All is one, and knowing it removes the other side.** The fourth commandment is to know that "Havayah is Elokim": «למנדע דאנון חד ולית בהו פרודא» (Introduction 24:1; line 208): "to know that they are one and there is no separation in them." The black light and the white light are one (line 209). Then comes the strongest line of the range for this index: «וכד ינדע בר נש דכלא חד ולא ישוי פירודא, אפילו ההיא סטרא אחרא יסתלק מעל עלמא» (Introduction 24:4; line 211): "When a person knows that all is one and makes no separation, even the other side departs from the world." And the reason: «אור בחבור דאתוון. מות בפירודא» (Introduction 24:5; line 212): "Light is in the joining of the letters; death is in separation." Evil in the Zohar is not a second power. It is separation. The serpent's sin was that it "joined below and separated above" (line 210).

**There is no other god, and He is the only one who does.** «בגין דלית אלהא אחרא בר קודשא בריך הוא בלחודוי» (Introduction 15:1; line 151): "for there is no other god but the Holy One, blessed be He, alone." The same holds in providence: the powers above rule the nations, and yet «לית בהו דיעביד אפלו מלה זעירא אלא כמה דפקיד לון» (Introduction 16:6; line 166): "there is none among them who does even the smallest thing except as He commanded them." And the letter Kaf, which "cannot be one moment except in Him" (line 86), is the Zohar's first hint of continuous creation.

**The Alef, the letter of unity.** When all the letters have gone before God, Alef does not come. God tells it: «לית בי יחודא אלא בך» (Introduction 6:17; line 38): "there is no unity in Me except through you." The world is created with Bet (blessing, a house), but unity is spoken with the silent first letter.

**Two points that are one.** The "beginning" is two points, one hidden and one revealed: «חד ולא תרין. מאן דנטיל האי נטיל האי. וכלא חד» (Introduction 13:3; line 122): "One and not two. Whoever takes this one takes that one. And all is one." In the Sabbath talk the point at the center is called "I", and on it rests the hidden one who is not revealed: «וההיא נקודה אקרי אני, ועלה שריא ההוא דסתים עלאה דלא אתגליא והיינו יי וכלא חד» (Introduction 12:12; line 85): "That point is called 'I', and upon it rests the hidden, supernal one who is not revealed, and that is Havayah, and all is one."

## 3. States of consciousness

Our reading: the Hakdamah gives five places a person can be.

1. **The question that stays a question (Mi).** You look up at the heavens, you ask "Who?", you search from level to level, and at the end you have "What?": nothing held, all as hidden as before (lines 7-8). From inside this is not failure but arrival: the not-knowing is the top of the knowing. Ties: S35 The edge of knowing, S36 Faith above knowing; technique 14 (stopping at the edge of understanding). New: see "Mah: searched to the end" below.
2. **Awe that is not fear of punishment.** Three fears (lines 190-193): fear for one's children and money, fear of hell, and true fear: «למדחל בר נש למאריה בגין דאיהו רב ושליט עקרא ושרשא דכל עלמין וכלא קמיה כלא חשיבין» (Introduction 21:3; line 191): "that a person fear his Master because He is great and rules, the root and source of all worlds, and all before Him is counted as nothing." Fear of punishment is not merely lower; the Zohar says the one who fears only punishment has on him the "evil fear", the strap, and not the fear of God (line 193). Ties: S45 Lower awe, S46 Higher awe; the Tanya's "lower fear" has the same content as the Zohar's true fear.
3. **Love in both directions.** Love that depends on wealth, long life and children turns to hatred when judgement comes (line 200). Complete love holds in both: «אפלו הוא נטיל נשמתך מינך. דא איהו רחימו שלים דהוי בתרין סטרין» (Introduction 22:4; line 201): "even if He takes your soul from you: that is complete love, which is in both sides." Ties: S42, S43; closer to S60 The essential bond. And fear must be joined to love: in good times fear lest one sin; in hard times fear so as not to harden the heart (lines 202-203).
4. **Held while knowing all is one.** The fourth commandment is a knowing that changes the world: the other side departs (line 211). Ties: S62 Only Him, S27 Knowing that holds.
5. **The one who was refined by suffering.** Rabbi Shimon comes out of the cave with his body covered in sores. Rabbi Pinchas weeps: "Woe that I see you so." He answers: «זכאה חולקי דחמית לי בכך דאלמלא לא חמיתא לי בכך לא הוינא בכך» (Introduction 20:4; line 188): "Happy is my portion that you see me so; for if you had not seen me so, I would not be so," that is, I would not be who I am. Ties: S05 Bitter with purpose, S85 Seeing Him in what happens.

## 4. Meditation and practice

The Hakdamah holds a full day's order, and these are instructions, not only ideas.

- **Looking at the heavens and blessing.** Elijah's first answer to "Who created these": these are the heavens and their hosts, the work of God, «דאית ליה לבר נש לאסתכלא בהו ולברכא ליה» (Introduction 4:2; line 12): "which a person ought to look at and bless Him." This is technique 11 (seeing the garment) in its oldest form, with blessing as its close. The Zohar then pushes past it: the secret is Mi, the question.
- **Ask until the asking gives way (Mi and Mah).** The search "from level to level to the end of all levels" that ends in "What have you seen?" (line 8) is a named practice of looking and not finding. Ours: this is the Zohar's form of technique 14, and it adds something Chassidus keeps implicit: the "What?" is not the point where the mind gives up, but a level with its own name (Mah) and its own world (this world, line 48).
- **Before sleep: accept the kingship and hand over the soul.** «בעי לקבלא עליה מלכותא דלעילא בלבא שלים ולאקדמא לממסר קמיה פקדונא דנפשיה» (Introduction 19:3; line 182): "he must accept upon himself the kingdom above with a whole heart, and first hand over to Him the deposit of his soul." The bedtime Shema in its root. Ties: S83 Accepting the yoke, S84 Trust; technique 24 (the evening look-back) is the Chabad partner, but the Zohar's night practice is handing over, not accounting.
- **Morning: bless, enter with awe, take counsel from the fathers.** On rising, bless; enter His house and bow in great fear; before entering the synagogue, "take counsel" with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, the love, awe and truth that made prayer (lines 183-184). Ties: technique 2 (before Whom).
- **Wash on waking.** The early pious kept water by the bed; at midnight when the rooster calls they rose, washed and learned (line 171). The origin of the midnight rising (tikkun chatzot) and of washing on waking.
- **The Shema as a daily unification.** «ולייחדא ליה בכל יומא יחודא כדקא יאות באנון שית סטרין עלאין» (Introduction 23:1; line 204): "and to unify Him every day with a proper unification in those six supernal sides," with the six words of "Shema Yisrael", directing the will upward and drawing out "One" (Echad) through the six. Then "Baruch Shem" is the lower unity, six more words (line 206). This is the source of the Chabad teaching of "higher and lower unity" (yichuda ila'ah and yichuda tata'ah) in the Tanya's Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah.
- **Say only Torah you know.** A new word of Torah makes a heaven (lines 61-66); a word said by one who does not know it makes a "firmament of emptiness" that the other side uses (lines 68-70). Rabbi Shimon begs: do not say words of Torah «דלא … ידעתון ולא שמעתון מאילנא רברבא כדקא יאות» (Introduction 11:11; line 71): "that you did not know and did not hear from the great tree as is proper." A guard for any teacher, and for this guide.
- **Joy that includes the poor.** Festival joy that gives nothing to the poor brings the accuser; on the festivals God comes to see «לאנון מאנין תבירין דיליה» (Introduction 18:2; line 175): "His broken vessels," and weeps when they have nothing to be glad with. Joy is a practice with a guard: it must go outward.
- **The night of the bride.** The companions stay awake the night before Shavuot adorning the bride with Torah, Prophets, Writings, midrash and secrets (lines 125-126). Source of the Tikkun Leil Shavuot.
- **Tefillin.** One who is dressed in tefillin «איהו הוי בצלם אלהים» (Introduction 30:7; line 243): "he is in the image of God": as God is united with the Holy Name, so is he.
- **Silence.** Rabbi Elazar, after he kept silent and was not answered: «שתיקא דילי בנא מקדשא לעילא ובנא מקדשא לתתא» (Introduction 5:3; line 18): "my silence built a sanctuary above and a sanctuary below." A word is worth a coin, silence two.

## 5. The big human questions

- **Why we exist.** Rabbi Elazar and the companions weep on hearing the secret of Mi and Eleh: «אלמלא לא אתינא לעלמא אלא למשמע דא דיי» (Introduction 4:5; line 15): "If we had come into the world only to hear this, it would be enough." The point of a life can be one true understanding of God.
- **Partner in creation.** Every new word of Torah makes new heavens; God says "you are My people" (ami), read as "you are with Me" (imi): «למהוי שותפא עמי» (Introduction 11:7; line 67): "to be a partner with Me." As God made heaven and earth by speech, so do you.
- **Held.** Of the sixty myriads, none is missing, above or below: «בגין למהוי כלא דיוקנא חדא. כמה דאיש לא נעדר לעילא, אוף הכי לא נעדר לתתא» (Introduction 5:6; line 21): "so that all be one image. As no one is missing above, so no one is missing below." Every soul is counted into one form.
- **Suffering.** Rabbi Shimon's sores (line 188); love that holds "even if He takes your soul" (line 201); the light of creation hidden so that judgement could join it and love could be complete (line 201). Suffering is the other side that love must include to be whole.
- **Turning darkness to light.** The heavenly voice asks: «מאן מנכון די חשוכא מהפכן לנהורא וטעמין מרירא למתקא» (Introduction 10:6; line 54): "Who among you turn darkness to light and taste bitter as sweet?" This is the phrase the Tanya takes up for the tzaddik's work (Tanya ch. 10 and 27, "ashkafya" and "ithapcha"), and the Zohar attaches to it a daily waiting for the light. Ties: C7 From darkness to light.
- **The poor and the rich.** The ninth commandment reads "in our image, in our likeness" as rich and poor: as male and female above give to each other, so rich and poor below must be joined and give to each other (line 234). One who pities the poor with the heart keeps the image of Adam (line 235).

## 6. What this range gives the guide (ours)

1. **Evil as separation.** Line 211 is a teaching the guide can use directly: knowing "all is one" is not only a comfort; it is what makes the other side leave. The Tanya's "there is nothing besides Him" gets its practical edge here.
2. **A fear that is not about punishment.** Lines 190-193 hand the guide a clean distinction a person can use at once: fear for what I might lose is not yet fear of God.
3. **Love that has passed through the other side.** Line 201 names the test of love: does it hold when things go wrong.
4. **The night and morning order** (lines 171, 182-184): hand over the soul at night; bless, bow, and take the fathers with you in the morning.
5. **Mah as a level.** The not-knowing at the end of search has a name and a world.

## Proposed states and techniques from this range

- **State, new: "Mah: searched to the end."** Inside: you asked and looked as far as you could, and now you stand in "what?", with nothing in your hand and no disappointment, because this is where the search was going. Enter: by the Mi and Mah search, level by level (lines 7-8). Near S35, but S35 is the edge; this is the arrival.
- **Technique, new: "Handing over the deposit."** At night, accept His kingship with a whole heart and hand the soul to Him as a deposit (line 182). Close: sleep. Guard: this is trust, not fear of death.
- **Technique, new: "Taking the fathers with you."** Before prayer, gather love (Abraham), awe (Isaac) and truth (Jacob) and only then go in (lines 183-184).
