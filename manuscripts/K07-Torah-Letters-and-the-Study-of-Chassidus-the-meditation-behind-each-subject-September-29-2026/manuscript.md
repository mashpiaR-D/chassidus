## How to read these

Every concept in Chassidus points to the unity of God, which cannot be grasped by definition; it can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (*hisbonenus*). So each subject is given here as a meditation in the Chabad way, not as an idea. **Hold** is one line of teaching from the entry's own sources. **The move** thinks it through slowly: it opens the line detail by detail, gives the source's own picture and says where the picture breaks, and brings it down to you, here, now. It uses only what the entry's derivation and passages say. **Stay** binds the mind to the line, as Tanya 3 describes da'as: the pauses are for thinking, and a stray thought is not argued with, only left for the line. **From inside** describes the glimpse and never asks for it; if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts. **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, adds one mitzvah or act of Torah in the person's measure, and gives a line to return to.

**Tie** says whether the move rests on derivation steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our own reading; the steps are listed. Where the entry marks a subject's tie as thin or partly thin, the meditation is kept modest and says so in one line. Where the sources differ on what the move touches, the difference is kept, not smoothed. Anchors are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly, OCR spelling included.

## S076 · Torah as His wisdom and will · אורייתא וקודשא בריך הוא כולא חד

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F05, F04. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The Torah in front of you is not a message about Him from far away. It is His own wisdom and will, one with Him, brought down until a mind and a hand can hold it.

**Hold.** 'The Torah is the wisdom and will of the Holy One, and the Holy One Himself: all one.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 4)

**The move.** Open it: no thought can grasp Him, and none can grasp His will and wisdom either. So He contracted His will and wisdom into the 613 mitzvot, their laws and the letters, and let them come down like water, from world to world, until they were clothed in physical things; and so a mind that grasps a law grasps Him. Tanya's picture is embracing a king, who is no less close for the robes between; it is only a picture, since He has no body, but it keeps this: the robes do not make the closeness less. Bring it down to one line of Torah on your shelf, here, now: it is not a message about Him from far away but His own wisdom, one with Him, in your hands.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the Torah and the Holy One, all one', and in the pauses think of the one line you will learn. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** An embrace. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over the Torah with your lips before you learn today; learn one line of Torah, even one, in your measure; and return during the day to 'all one'.

**Where the sources differ.** The same oneness is put with different centres: Chabad puts the union in understanding a law; the Sfas Emes in the point of life from the Torah hidden in everything, reached by bitul; the Maggid in His having no parts, so that each mitzvah holds all 613.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 4:5 (line 97): דאורייתא, היא חכמתו ורצונו של הקדושברוךהוא, והקדושברוךהוא בכבודו ובעצמו – כולא חד Tanya 4: the Zohar's 'Torah and the Holy One are one' read through the Rambam: Torah is His wisdom and will, and He is the knower and the known. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 4:7 (line 99): וצמצם הקדושברוךהוא רצונו וחכמתו בתרי״ג מצות התורה ובהלכותיהן He contracted His will and wisdom into the 613 commandments and their laws: the unity comes down into a graspable form. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 4:10 (line 102): ומשם נסעה וירדה בסתר המדרגות, ממדרגה למדרגה, בהשתלשלות העולמות, עד שנתלבשה בדברים גשמיים Torah 'like water' descends through the chain of worlds until it is clothed in physical things, so that even action can grasp it (G05).

## S077 · The letters · אותיות

*Face F01 The world said into being now; also touches F05, F10. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The stone on the path is held in being by the letters of its own name, and that name is its life. Everything around you is His word, spelled.

**Hold.** 'Its name in the holy tongue is a vessel for the life contracted in the letters of that name.' (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1)

**The move.** Open it: the letters of the Ten Utterances stand in every creature and give it life. A stone, never named in them, gets its life through letters handed down and exchanged until they spell 'even', stone, and that name is the stone's life; and the name can make something from nothing because the Torah and the Holy One are one. Likkutei Torah's picture is two kinds of letters, ink laid on parchment and separate from it, and letters engraved in stone, which are the stone itself; it is only a picture, for an engraved stone was there before its letters, and a creature would be nothing without its own. Bring it down to one plain thing near you, here, now, the bread on the table or a stone on the path: it is not labelled with a word; it is the word, spelled.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'a vessel for the life in its letters', and in the pauses think of one thing and its Hebrew name. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being held in being by a word that has not stopped. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over bread with your lips today, hearing the word 'lechem' in it; learn one verse of Torah slowly, word by word; and return during the day to 'its name is its life'.

**Where the sources differ.** Where the corpus uses letters for gematria and wordplay it usually draws no tie to the unity; and the Besht's letters are for cleaving in prayer and study, where Chabad's are the life of each creature.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1:6 (line 760): נמשך חיות לאבן על ידי צירופים וחילופי אותיות … ונמשך מהן צירוף שם ״אבן״, והוא חיותו של האבן SH 1: a stone's Hebrew name is a permutation of letters descending from the Ten Utterances, and that name is its life (G01). - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1:9 (line 763): וזה שמו אשר יקראו לו בלשון הקדש, הוא כלי לחיות המצומצם באותיות שם זה The name is the vessel for the contracted life in its letters, which have the power to create yesh from nothing because Torah and God are one. - Likkutei Torah, Bechukotai 2:5 (line 1051): אבל עכ״פ אותיות אלו הם בגוף האבן טוב ממש ואינו דבר נפרד ממנו Likkutei Torah: engraved letters are of the very stone, unlike ink letters that clothe the parchment as a separate garment.

## S078 · The ten utterances · עשרה מאמרות / דבר ה'

*Face F01 The world said into being now; also touches F05, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5, 7). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The ground under you would not be there for an instant if the words of 'let there be' left it. Creation did not only happen; it is being said now.

**Hold.** 'Forever, O God, Your word stands in the heavens': the letters of the Ten Utterances stand in every creature now, even the physical earth. (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1, after the Besht)

**The move.** Open it: if the letters by which the earth was created left it for an instant, it would return to absolute nothing. His 'speech' is a name for His light going out from hiding into revealing, and unlike ours it stays one with Him even after it has gone out, the change being only toward the creatures; the Maharash adds a limit, that making something from nothing by speech is 'a mere radiance' and 'Anochi', His Essence, is beyond it. And the Rebbe joins them: the Ten Utterances answer to the Ten Commandments, and the wise draw the Commandments into the Utterances, so the spoken world becomes a dwelling. Bring it down to the ground under your chair and the street outside, here, now: not made once and left standing, but being said at this moment.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'Your word stands', and in the pauses think of the ground, being said. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being held, not holding: not a second outside the word. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'Forever, O God, Your word stands in the heavens' with your lips once today; learn one of the 'let there be' verses of Bereshit slowly; and return during the day to 'Your word stands'.

**Where the sources differ.** Whether creation is the Essence or a radiance stands open: the Maharash calls creation by speech a mere radiance, below 'Anochi'; Iggeret HaKodesh 20 and the Rebbe say only the Essence can create something from nothing.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1:4 (line 758): אילו היו מסתלקות ממנה כרגע חס ושלום האותיות מעשרה מאמרות שבהן נבראת הארץ בששת ימי בראשית, היתה חוזרת לאין ואפס ממש SH 1: even the physical earth would return to nothing if the letters of the Ten Utterances left it for an instant (F01). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 21:6 (line 290): מיוחדות דבורו ומחשבתו של הקדושברוךהוא בתכלית היחוד במהותו ועצמותו יתברך, גם אחר שיצא דבורו יתברך אל הפועל בבריאות העולמות Unlike human speech, His speech stays one with His Essence even after creation; the change is only toward the creatures (F02). - Toras Shmuel, תרכ"ז / מאמרים / קיצור מד"ה מצות יאכל הנ"ל / רמג:3 (line 732): דפי' יש מאין מהארה בעלמא, בחי' דיבור בדבר ה' כו' The Maharash: creation by the word is only 'a mere radiance'; the Essence (Anochi) is beyond it. The kept tension of F05.

## S079 · Written and Oral Torah · תורה שבכתב ושבעל פה

*Face F02 The higher and the lower unity; also touches F10, F11. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** The hidden will in the verse and the detailed law that spells it out are one. Even a law of permitted and forbidden is where He, His will and His wisdom are all one.

**Hold.** 'The supernal will clothed in the 613 mitzvot of the Written Torah is hidden and covered, and is revealed only in the Oral Torah.' (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 29)

**The move.** Open it: the verse says 'bind them for a sign on your hand' and does not say how, what or where. The Oral Torah brings it out, and even its laws of forbidden and permitted are where 'He, His will and His wisdom, clothed in His speech, are all one'. Iggeret HaKodesh's picture is a mother who brings the father's seed, in which every limb is folded up, into a whole child; it is only a picture, but it keeps the point that nothing is added, only born. Bring it down to one practical law you keep, here, now, the square black box and the knot, or the way you light the candles: it is not something people added to the verse; it is the hidden will, born into your hands.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'hidden in the Written, revealed in the Oral', and in the pauses think of the one law. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Recognition: the will you could not see in the verse, looking back at you from the law. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'and you shall bind them for a sign on your hand' with your lips today; learn one practical law, even a short one, in your measure; and return during the day to 'revealed in the Oral Torah'.

**Kept modest.** The Alter Rebbe states the tie in his own terms (the Oral Torah as Malchus of Atzilus, 'all one'); reading the pair as the higher and lower unity is ours, so this meditation stays with his words.

**Where the sources differ.** The Zohar calls the laws of forbidden and permitted the 'tree of good and evil'; the Alter Rebbe answers that all revealed Torah is a tree of life and only its garments are mixed; the Rashab says that is why the Oral Torah must be learned in love and awe, or one can learn it and forget the Giver.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 29:22 (line 1466): רצון העליון ברוךהוא המלובש בתרי״ג מצות שבתורה שבכתב, הוא מופלא ומכוסה, טמיר ונעלם, ואינו מתגלה אלא בתורה שבעל פה Iggeret HaKodesh 29: the will in the Written Torah is hidden and is revealed only by the Oral Torah (tefillin, the 39 labors). - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 29:25 (line 1469): ככה ממש כל רמ״ח מצות עשה ושס״ה מצות לא תעשה באים מההעלם אל הגילוי בתורה שבעל פה The father's seed and the mother's birth: the Written Torah (Chochmah, 'father') holds everything concealed; the Oral Torah births it into revealed limbs. - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 26:19 (line 1399): שאוראיןסוף ברוךהוא מתייחד באצילות בתכלית היחוד, שהוא ורצונו וחכמתו המלובשים בדבורו שנקרא ״מלכות״ – הכל אחד Iggeret HaKodesh 26: the Oral Torah, even laws of forbidden and permitted, is Malchus of Atzilus, where He, His will and wisdom are all one.

## S080 · Torah study · תלמוד תורה

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F07, F08. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** When you understand a law, your mind holds His wisdom and His wisdom holds your mind. There is no union like it in the physical world.

**Hold.** 'It is a wondrous union, like which there is no union, and nothing comparable to it is found in physical things at all.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 5)

**The move.** Open it: a law, even about a case that may never happen, is His wisdom and will, and when you know it your mind grasps and encloses it and is also clothed in it. Learning draws the light of Ein Sof more than deeds do; and yet one stops learning for a deed no one else can do, because the purpose is a dwelling below. Tanya's picture is bread, which becomes one with the one who eats it, flesh of his flesh; it is only a picture, for bread is swallowed up in you, while here you are also held by what you hold. Bring it down to one law you actually understand, here, now, even the blessing on bread: holding it in your mind, you hold His wisdom, and it holds you.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'a wondrous union, like no other', and in the pauses think of the one law. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being held while you hold. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Learn one law today and say it aloud in your own words, since knowing it is the union; if a deed that only you can do comes up, do it first; and return during the day to 'a union like no other'.

**Where the sources differ.** Which is higher, knowing or doing, is kept: Tanya says knowing a law is the union like no other and draws more light than deeds, yet study yields to a deed only you can do; the Rayatz says the purpose of study is the deed.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 5:5 (line 111): הרי זה משיג ותופס ומקיף בשכלו רצונו וחכמתו של הקדושברוךהוא Tanya 5: knowing a law, one grasps His will and wisdom, which no thought can grasp except as clothed in the laws. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 5:6 (line 112): והוא יחוד נפלא שאין יחוד כמוהו ולא כערכו נמצא כלל בגשמיות The mind encompasses the Torah and is encompassed by it: a wondrous union with no parallel in physical things. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 37:26 (line 520): ומבטלין תלמוד תורה לקיום מצוה מעשיית כשאי אפשר לעשותה על ידי אחרים … להיות לו יתברך דירה בתחתונים דוקא Yet study yields to a deed no one else can do, because the purpose is the dwelling below (F07): the kept tension of study and action.

## S081 · Lishmah · תורה לשמה

*Face F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; also touches F10, F07. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 6, 7). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** Learning 'for its sake' means learning with nothing of your own in the way. Then nothing stands between you and the Torah that is one with Him.

**Hold.** 'Lishmah means: to bind his soul to God through grasping the Torah, each according to his mind.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 5)

**The move.** Open it: when learning is for one's own honour, Tanya says, the Torah goes into exile inside that self-interest for a while, until the person returns. The Rebbe describes the highest mode: because the Torah is one with the Essence, the aim of learning is the Torah itself, without even the thought of becoming attached, in complete bitul. And Tanya is not harsh about the start: learning not for its sake 'certainly' comes to lishmah, in this life or another. Bring it down to the small wish that rides along with your learning, here, now, to be seen as learned or to get something out of it: it does not cancel the learning, and the line asks only 'each according to his mind'.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'to bind his soul to God', and in the pauses think: each according to his mind, and mine is enough. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Lightness: nothing to hold up, nothing to defend. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over the Torah with your lips, slowly; learn one thing today that no one will know you learned; and return during the day to 'to bind my soul to Him'.

**Kept modest.** The tie is stated for the definition (a bond with Him, Tanya 5; the Rebbe, 5711), not for the long argument over how not-lishmah becomes lishmah, which is psychology and law, so the meditation keeps to the definition.

**Where the sources differ.** Whether and why not-lishmah leads to lishmah is argued: R. Tzadok says because the act itself puts holiness in the heart; Tanya says 'certainly'; the Degel warns the handmaid can come to despise the mistress; the Rashab says learning without love and awe makes one a 'something' and ends in not-lishmah outright.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 5:12 (line 118): ו״לשמה״, היינו כדי לקשר נפשו לה׳ על ידי השגת התורה, איש כפי שכלו Tanya 5: lishmah defined by the unity itself: to bind the soul to God through grasping the Torah. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 39:19 (line 557): אזי, אותה פניה שמצד הקליפה דנוגה – מתלבשת בתורתו, והתורה היא בבחינת גלות בתוך הקליפה לפי שעה Tanya 39: study for self-honor puts the Torah into exile inside the kelipah, until teshuvah; the self-interest is the separation (F08). - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ג / בס"ד. פורים ה'תשי"א:7 (line 1765): שנרגש אצלו שהתכלית דלימוד התורה שלו היא התורה עצמה … ולימוד התורה שלו הוא בתכלית הביטול The Rebbe (Purim 5711): because Torah is one with the Essence ('Anochi', I wrote Myself in), study's purpose is the Torah itself, in complete bitul.

## S082 · Halachah · הלכה

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F11, F09. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** 'Kosher', 'permitted', 'exempt': each small ruling is His inner will itself, drawn down. Getting a law clear lifts a spark of the Shechinah out of hiding.

**Hold.** 'All the laws are particular drawings-down of the inner supernal will itself.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 23)

**The move.** Open it: every ruling, 'permitted', 'kosher', 'exempt', 'liable', is His inner will itself, so thinking it and saying it joins you with Him. Iggeret HaKodesh says 'the word of God is halachah', and each law is a spark of the Shechinah that fell into the husks, raised when you clarify it. The Rashab warns of the other side: because the laws are clothed in cows, donkeys and a garment two people hold, one can learn them and forget the Giver. Bring it down to one small practical question from your own kitchen or week, here, now, this spoon or that blessing: it is exactly where the spark waits, and His will is in the answer.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the inner supernal will itself', and in the pauses think of the one small question. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The quiet of something made clear; a spark lifted. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Look up or ask one practical law you were unsure of today, say the ruling aloud as words of Torah, and act on it; and return during the day to 'His will, in the ruling'.

**Where the sources differ.** The Zohar calls forbidden and permitted the 'tree of good and evil'; the Alter Rebbe says all revealed Torah is a tree of life, and only its garments are mixed.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 23:8 (line 309): שכל ההלכות הן פרטי המשכות פנימיות רצון העליון עצמו Tanya 23: every law, permitted, kosher, exempt or liable, is a particular drawing-down of the inner supernal will itself. - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 26:28 (line 1408): וכל דברי תורה, ובפרט דבר הלכה, היא ניצוץ מהשכינה, שהיא היא דבר ה׳ Iggeret HaKodesh 26: a halachah is a spark of the Shechinah, 'the word of God is halachah', fallen into kelipas nogah and raised by study. - Kuntres Eitz HaChaim (scans, OCR), id 15741, scan p. 41 ¶1 (OCR) (line 261): וכשלומד ההלכות ה"ה עופק בענינים גשמיים יכול להיות-בבחי' יש ודבר ויוכל לשכוח על ה' נותן התורה The Rashab: because laws are clothed in physical cases, one can study them and forget the Giver (OCR: עוסק).

## S083 · New insight in Torah · חידושי תורה

*Face F11 Darkness turned to light; also touches F01, F07. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** A new understanding in Torah frees a piece of His wisdom that was in exile. The upper worlds, Iggeret HaKodesh says, come to hear it from us below.

**Hold.** 'And every Jew can reveal hidden things of wisdom.' (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 26)

**The move.** Open it: the wisdom behind the laws fell into the husks, and the upper beings cannot refine it, only those below, in bodies, so the upper ones come to hear new insights in Torah from the lower. Each Jew uncovers it by the root of his own soul, in law or in story, revealed or hidden; the Zhitomirer says a new combination of letters rises and becomes new heavens before Him. The Rebbe puts it another way: all that a faithful student will one day find was given at Sinai, and it appears when the world needs it. Bring it down to one small thing you once understood in Torah by yourself, here, now: it was wisdom freed from exile, and there is more that belongs to the root of your soul.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'every Jew can reveal hidden wisdom', and in the pauses think of the one verse you will sit with. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The freshness of something hidden coming to light. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say one verse of Torah aloud today; sit with it until you see one thing in it you had not seen, and write it down; and return during the day to 'hidden wisdom, freed'.

**Kept modest.** The tie is stated only in Iggeret HaKodesh 26 and in Zhitomir and Radomsk, so the meditation keeps to them and claims nothing about new insight in general.

**Where the sources differ.** Zhitomir and Radomsk make the insight a new creation, new heavens where old judgments do not reach; the Rebbe makes it the revealing of what was given at Sinai, in its time; R. Tzadok says its leaning, this way or that, is in God's hand.

## S084 · The giving of the Torah · מתן תורה

*Face F05 The Essence beyond both; also touches F07, F09. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Before Sinai, heaven was heaven and earth was earth. At Sinai the wall came down and He Himself, not a glow of Him, spoke into the lowest world, so that a plain physical thing can now be holy.

**Hold.** 'At the giving of the Torah He annulled the decree; all that is something from nothing, but Anochi is His Essence and being, which is not a radiance.' (the Maharash, Toras Shmuel 5627)

**The move.** Open it: before Sinai, 'the heavens are God's and the earth He gave to men'; at Sinai the decree was annulled, and only from 'Anochi', His Essence, comes 'below as above'. The Rebbe draws the result: since then the physical thing itself becomes the thing of a mitzvah, and upper and lower, which are opposites, are joined by the Ein Sof above both. And Tanya says that at Sinai they saw with physical sight that Havayah is Elokim, there is none besides Him. Bring it down to one physical thing you will use for a mitzvah today, here, now, the coin, the bread, the candlesticks: after Sinai it is itself where heaven and earth meet.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'Anochi, His Essence, not a radiance', and in the pauses think of the thing in your hand. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being chosen, not for anything you reached: He is in the plainest thing. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'I am Havayah your God' with your lips before one mitzvah today; do that mitzvah with a physical thing, slowly; and return during the day to 'Anochi, not a radiance'.

**Where the sources differ.** In Chabad Sinai is seeing and knowing ('you were shown, to know'); R. Nachman says Israel received the Torah only by casting away their wisdoms, in faith. And whether creation itself is the Essence or a radiance stands between the Maharash and Iggeret HaKodesh 20 with the Rebbe.

## S085 · Revealed and hidden Torah · נגלה ונסתר / פנימיות התורה

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F05, F11. Tie: stated (derivation steps 3, 4, 6, 7). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The Torah has a body and a soul, and they are one Torah. In the inner Torah, the Ein Sof that is in all of it shows through least covered.

**Hold.** 'As the soul gives life to the body, so the inner Torah gives life to the outer Torah.' (the Rashab, Kuntres Eitz HaChaim)

**The move.** Open it: without the inner Torah what one learns is 'a body without a soul'. The Rebbe says every part of Torah, plain sense, hint, homily and secret, clothes the Ein Sof in a form that defines it, while Chassidus is bound by no form, so its form does not truly hide Him, and it is the yechidah of the Torah that raises the other four; and the Besht wanted both studies every day. The picture is body and soul, and it limps: here the body is also Torah, and all of it, the Alter Rebbe says, is a tree of life. Bring it down to two pages side by side on your table, here, now, one of halachah and one of Chassidus: one Torah, and the one gives the other its life.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the inner gives life to the outer', and in the pauses think of the two pages. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A page that was only words, alive. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over the Torah with your lips today; learn a few lines of each, revealed and inner, as the Besht wanted every day; and return during the day to 'the inner gives it life'.

**Where the sources differ.** The Zohar calls forbidden and permitted the 'tree of good and evil', which would make the revealed Torah lower; the Alter Rebbe rejects that reading. The Rebbe and the Rashab rank the inner Torah as soul to body; the Besht gives the two studies two tasks, breaking husks and cleaving, without calling one the soul of the other.

## S086 · Parable and meaning · משל ונמשל

*Face F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'; also touches F02, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** You can know Him from your own flesh, from how a soul lives in a body. And the place where every such picture fails is the unity itself.

**Hold.** 'But in truth the parable does not resemble the thing at all, for soul and body are truly separate from each other in their root.' (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 6)

**The move.** Open it: 'from my flesh I see God', the Rebbe says, is how Chassidus brought the Torah's secret close to the mind, through pictures taken from the soul's own powers. Tanya is careful that even His 'speech' is called speech only as a parable. And where the soul-and-body picture fails is the lesson itself, for the world is not separate from His word but is like the sun's light inside the sun. Bring it down to any picture you use for Him, here, now, the soul in the body or the word from the mouth: use it, then let it go at the point where it breaks, and stay there, where the unity is.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the parable does not resemble the thing at all', and in the pauses think: closer than soul to body. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** What remains when the picture is let go. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'from my flesh I see God' with your lips once today; when you explain something about God to a child or a friend, give the picture and then say where it breaks; and return during the day to 'not like it at all'.

**Kept modest.** The method is stated (Inyanah 1:8) and so is its limit (Shaar HaYichud 6:7); calling the parable itself a face of the unity is ours, so the meditation keeps to the method and where it fails.

**Where the sources differ.** The Maharash says 'from my flesh I see' reaches the filling light while the surrounding light is held by faith (applying this to parable is our reading); Strashelye warns that the Ari's parables, taken literally, led later kabbalists into corporealism.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K07.json`): - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 6:7 (line 793): אך באמת, אין המשל דומה לנמשל כלל, כי הנשמה והגוף הם באמת נפרדים זה מזה בשרשם SH 6: the soul-body analogy fails at the point of unity: body and soul are separate in root, the world is not separate from His word (G06). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 21:3 (line 287): ולא נקרא דבורו יתברך בשם ״דבור״, רק על דרך משל Tanya 21: even 'His speech' is a parable; the name is borrowed from how human speech reveals hidden thought. - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 1:8 (line 8): וקירבתם אל השכל בדוגמאות ומשלים מכחות ותכונות הנפש – "מבשרי אחזה אלוקה" The Rebbe: Chassidus's innovation was to bring the Torah's sod close to the mind through analogies from the soul.

## S087 · Chassidus and Kabbalah · חסידות / קבלה

*Face F05 The Essence beyond both; also touches F12, F11. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 5, 6). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Kabbalah maps the worlds; Chassidus shows Him present in them, and brings Him into understanding, into the heart and into the day, even into what is not good.

**Hold.** 'Chassidus is Shema Yisrael.' (the Alter Rebbe, Hayom Yom 19 Cheshvan)

**The move.** Open it: the Alter Rebbe means reaching what is above the mind, and understanding that too with the mind. The Rebbe says the matters of Kabbalah come to be understood only through Chassidus, which draws down the Ein Sof itself and is not one more layer on top; Strashelye says the Maggid made parables to bring the Ari's wisdom close to the mind, as a way to serve God and cleave to His unity. And the Tzemach Tzedek says 'Chassidus is vitality', bringing life and light into everything, even the not-good. Bring it down to your own ordinary afternoon, here, now, the one no chart of worlds would mention: that is where Chassidus is meant to bring the Shema.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'Chassidus is Shema Yisrael', and in the pauses think of the afternoon ahead. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The map becoming a place you live in. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the first line of the Shema with your lips, drawing out 'One' and thinking that He rules heaven and earth and all four directions; learn one short paragraph of Chassidus today; and return during the day to 'Shema Yisrael'.

**Where the sources differ.** How far the mind may go is argued: the Rayatz calls Chabad Chassidus a divine inquiry that opens wisdom and understanding; Strashelye forbids speaking of the inwardness of Adam Kadmon, only of what is revealed from it in the worlds; R. Nachman rejects inquiry for faith.

## S088 · Outside wisdom and philosophy · חכמות חיצוניות / חקירה

*Face F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; also touches F12, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 5). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** The same knowledge can make you smaller or bigger. The more the Rambam understood of God, the smaller he grew in his own eyes, and that, not the knowing, is the difference between holy and profane.

**Hold.** 'The more the Rambam grasped of God's truth, the more he recoiled and grew smaller in his own eyes; and the more Aristotle grasped, the more he became a yesh.' (the Tzemach Tzedek, Derekh Mitzvotekha, Milah 7)

**The move.** Open it: they studied the same philosophy, and bitul, the Tzemach Tzedek says, is the whole difference between holy and profane. The Rayatz adds the order: first toil to understand as far as you can, and what you cannot understand, believe with simple faith; and Tanya allows outside wisdom only 'as a spade to dig with', for a living or for serving God. Bring it down to the thing you know best, your trade or your field, here, now: the knowing is neither the danger nor the holiness; what it does to your size in your own eyes is.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the more he grasped, the smaller in his own eyes', and in the pauses think of what you know best. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Lightness: knowing without having to be the one who knows. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'there is nothing besides Him' with your lips once today; learn one line of Torah before you start your work, so the work is the spade and not the master; and return during the day to 'smaller in his own eyes'.

**Kept modest.** The tie is stated only through bitul (the Tzemach Tzedek) and the hiddenness of the vacated space (R. Nachman); Tanya 8 speaks of impurity, not of the unity, so the meditation stays with the Rambam and Aristotle.

**Where the sources differ.** Tanya 8 treats outside wisdom as impurity for the mind, allowed only as a spade; R. Nachman says the questions of the vacated space have no answer by inquiry, only faith passes over them; the Rayatz says the Rambam wrote 'to know', not 'to believe'. The disagreement with Breslov is real.
