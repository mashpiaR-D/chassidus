*The Chassidus Unity Index as a meditation manual. Part III of five (I how to sit, II the ladder, IV inside, V when it's hard). It turns the ladder of Part II into things to do: a forty-day course, a daily ten-minute practice, a three-minute practice for busy days, a table for specific troubles, and a simple log. Built 2026-09-29. Machine-readable twin: `part3.json`.*

The sittings are in Part II. This part is about when, how long, and how to tell whether any of it is working. Two sentences from the Tanya stand behind all of it. Stay with a thought "as far as your mind can grasp, and as your free time allows." And: "when he trains himself this way constantly, the habit becomes nature."

<small>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 41, line 601: ויאריך בה כפי יכולת השגת מוחו ומחשבתו, וכפי הפנאי שלו — *Let him stay with it as far as his mind can grasp, and as his free time allows.*</small><br><small>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 44, line 664: וכשירגיל עצמו כן תמיד הרי ההרגל נעשה טבע — *When he trains himself this way constantly, the habit becomes nature.*</small>

Danger comes before every program. If you are in danger, or thinking of not being alive, stop and reach a person now: the emergency number where you are, or a crisis line.

## (a) The forty-day course

Forty days through the ladder, with repetition built in. Each rung is sat two or three times, because the sources say the second and third time are where the work begins: the first time you catch only the glimmer, and with practice you arrive at the thing itself at once. Start on a Sunday, so that Shabbos falls on days 7, 14, 21, 28 and 35. If you start on another day, keep the order and move the Shabbos sittings so they land on Shabbos.

**How long each day.** A working day: the ten-minute practice (b). A free morning or Shabbos: twenty to forty minutes, using the whole sitting. A day when there is no time at all: the three-minute version (c). A three-minute day still counts. Don't skip; shorten.

**Shabbos days.** On Shabbos the sitting is lighter and longer, and it sits inside the day's own practices: the Shema, Kiddush, the bread, the table. There is no writing on Shabbos; write the log and the weekly check after Havdalah. If you are not Jewish, keep the same day as a quieter sitting on your own day of rest, without the Jewish practices.

**Low days.** On a day you feel low, do sitting 1 (or its three-minute version) instead of whatever the course says, and pick up the course tomorrow. Sittings 6 and 12 are never for a low day.

**Why this order.** Weeks one and two climb the rungs you can think (sittings 1 to 5), with the first rung revisited on day 12 once the hiding is known. Week three binds them (sittings 6 and 7). Week four goes above the mind (8 to 11). Week five comes back down (12 to 14). The last five days sit the three hardest rungs a third time, give one day to the rung your own log shows was most alive, and on day 40 walk the whole ladder in one sitting, from the spark to the hand.

**Day 40: the aftermath check.** Read your log from day 1 to now and answer the four weekly questions (e) for the whole forty days, in one line each. Then decide: another round (the ladder is a circle; the second time through, each rung starts where its third time left off), or the daily practice on the rungs that are yours.

## (b) The daily ten-minute practice

For a working day. One rung, one line, about ten minutes. This is the ten-minute column of Part I's table ("The whole sitting at a glance"), with Part II's sitting dropped into it; Part I explains each stage. Which rung? The one you are on in the course, or, outside the course, the one the trouble table (d) gives you, or the one that has been most alive in your log. Stay on the same rung for a week before changing, unless it has gone completely cold for several days; then change the rung, not the effort.

| Clock | Time | Part | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | 1 min | Before | A coin in the charity box (anyone can do this). Read the sitting's line with its source note in Part II. Step out of the day: phone face down, one place, one time. |
| 1:00 | 30 sec | Arrive | Name where you are in one sentence, starting from what is already moving in you: 'I'm worried about the rent.' 'I'm fine, a bit flat.' |
| 1:30 | 1 min | Quiet | If your head is racing, calmly watch the thoughts, asking 'what am I thinking?', until they slow. The Piaseczner teaches this: look at them, not into them, and don't push. It is only clearing the table. It is not the meditation. |
| 2:30 | 20 sec | The line | Say the sitting's line softly, once. |
| 2:50 | 4 min | Think it through | Part II's 'Think it through' for this sitting, in the version for the first, third or seventh time. Breadth, length, depth, then your own life. Each time you come back to the line, add one thing you didn't say before. When the mind drifts, come back without arguing. |
| 6:50 | 1 min | The picture | The sitting's picture, and in a few words where it breaks. |
| 7:50 | 1 min | Your own words | Talk to Him about it, in your words. Part II gives a sample; don't read it, say yours. |
| 8:50 | 1 min | The lips | The words for who you are: a Jew, another faith, or unsure. |
| 9:50 | 30 sec | After | Name today's act. Pick a cue of up to six words from the line to use once during the day. Write the log line afterward, not during (and not on Shabbos). |

- **The quiet is not the meditation.** It is only clearing the table. The Piaseczner's instruction is to look at the thoughts, asking "what am I thinking?", and they thin out; pushing them away makes them stronger. Then go straight to the line.
- **During the sitting, don't argue with stray thoughts.** The Tanya: act as if you don't hear them, and go back. Don't analyse why they came.
- **Each return to the line adds one thing.** A detail, an example, something it rules out. If you're only repeating it, go into a detail.
- **Don't check how you feel.** The Mitteler Rebbe's question is not "how strong was it?" but "what moved me?": the thing itself, or the wish to be moved.
- **End in the day.** The last minute is about today's act, never about the heights.

<small>Derekh HaMelekh, Mind-quieting advice 4 (the Piaseczner), line 1556: שיתחיל האיש להביט על מחשבותיו שעה קלה — *Let the person begin by looking at his thoughts for a short while.*</small><br><small>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 28, line 401: רק יעשה עצמו כלא יודע ולא שומע ההרהורים שנפלו לו — *Only let him act as one who neither knows nor hears the thoughts that fell into his mind.*</small><br><small>Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 7:3 (the Mitteler Rebbe), line 135: ולא התפעלות חיי בשר — *And not the arousal of the life of the flesh [the test is what moved you].*</small>

## (c) The three-minute practice

For days with no room at all. It keeps the thread, and the Tanya says a long sitting once a day lets even a light thought at any hour bring it back. Three minutes, the same four steps every time:

| Clock | Step | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Where you are, and the line | One sentence about where you are. Then the line of the sitting you're on, or the rung the trouble table gives you, said softly. |
| 0:20 | One detail | One thing in front of you, held with the line: this cup, this hand, this doorway. Think it through in that one thing only. |
| 1:30 | The lips | The words for who you are (for a Jew, the Shema's first verse with 'One' drawn out), then one sentence of your own to Him. |
| 2:30 | The act and the cue | Name the one act and do it now if you can. Carry the cue into the day. |

Each sitting in Part II ends with its own three-minute version. Use that one if you are on the course. Part I's "3-minute thread" is the same shape.

**Four anchors that cost nothing.** These are already in a Jewish day, and each has a version for anyone. On a day when even three minutes won't happen, one of these is the practice.

- On waking, before anything: Modeh Ani (or a thank-you in your own words). The Rashab says giving your whole self to God in a general way, as in Modeh Ani, is very near to every Jew. - At a doorway: the line of the day, once. For a Jew, with a touch of the mezuzah. - Before eating: the blessing, slowly (or a thank-you to Him). - At night: the bedtime Shema, with the two thoughts of sitting 3 (or Psalm 16:8 and a thank-you).

<small>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, line 639: כשיחזור ויתבונן בזה אפילו בהתבוננות קלה, בכל עת ובכל שעה — *When he comes back and contemplates it even with a light contemplation, at any time and any hour.*</small><br><small>Kuntres HaAvodah (the Rashab), scan p. 5 (OCR), line 29: זה קרוב מאד לכאו"א מישראל — *This is very near to every single Jew [giving oneself to God in general, as in Modeh Ani].*</small>

## (d) For a specific trouble

Every trouble looks, from inside, like something standing on its own: the world, the self, time, another person, God far away. Each rung answers one of those. The table gives the rung to start with, the one to go to next, the full healing method for that trouble in the hisbonenus library (fifty of them, each built from the sources and verified), and a few of the subject meditations. Use the first rung for a week with the ten-minute practice, then the second.

Before the table, two rules. Danger stops everything; reach a person now. And a trouble is not a sign of a weak soul or weak faith. Depression, anxiety and grief are not practice failures.

**How the healing methods and the ladder fit.** A healing method is a full course for one trouble, in stages, with spoken samples and guards. The ladder is the unity itself, rung by rung. They meet at the rung: each healing method rests on one or two faces, and the table sends you to the sitting for that face. When a method and a sitting say different things about what to do, the method's guards for that trouble win, because they were built for it.

**All fifty healing methods** are in `../../Chabad Library/hisbonenus/healing/`. Those not in the table, and the rung that fits them best: a-child, waiting (L04); aging, too-small (L13); amends-and-forgiveness (L12); decisions (L03); entitled, not-my-fault, im-fine (L06); growing-yet-worse, knowing-not-changing, shallow (L07); helping-fails (L14); illness (L04); laziness, this-is-how-im-built, obstacles-too-big (L12); punishing-the-body (L01); remorse-no-joy (L08); stray-thoughts (Part I and the daily practice); suffering-and-anger-at-god (L05, with its own guards); survivors (L04, L11).

## (e) Keeping a simple log

A notebook, or a note on your phone. Two minutes a day, and ten after Shabbos. The log is not for grading sittings. It is for seeing, over weeks, whether your life is changing.

**After each sitting (four short lines).**

- **When and what.** Date, the rung, how many minutes. 'Day 12 · L02 (third time) · 11 min.'
- **The line, in my own words.** One sentence. Can you say something about it you didn't hear from the book? If yes, you entered it. If you can only repeat the line, you were told it. Both are fine; the log just shows which.
- **What moved me, if anything.** Not how strong. What moved you: the thing itself, or the wish to be moved? Leave it blank if nothing moved. Blank is a real answer.
- **The act.** What you said you'd do today, and later a tick when it's done.

**In the evening (one line).** One line before sleep: did the line come back by itself today? Where? ('At the red light.' 'Didn't.')

> Day 12 · L02 (third time) · 11 min
> The line in my words: The mug isn't holding itself up, and the thing I'm scared about isn't either.
> What moved me: the idea that it wouldn't fall, it just wouldn't be. Then nothing much.
> Act: say it at the door. ✓
> Evening: came back at the red light, and once when the boss called.

**Once a week, after Shabbos: the aftermath test.** Read the week's lines. Then answer four questions, one line each. These are the real measure. The sources are clear that a strong moment is not the measure, the afterward is.

- **A little quieter?** Fewer loops in the head, slower to react, less rushing. Look at the week, not at one day.
- **A little less full of myself?** Was there an insult or a slight that stung less than it would have? Did you need less to be seen or credited? The Mitteler Rebbe gives this as the real sign of bitul.
- **More my own master?** Was there one reaction you would usually have had that didn't happen this week? One pull with a little less grip? The Rashab's measure is a trait that has changed, not a sitting that felt better.
- **The same person?** The Piaseczner asks after every hour of study or prayer: am I the same person I was after the idle hours? Answer honestly, in one line.

<small>Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 40:6, line 829: כי נבזה בעיניו נמאס באמת מפני שאין לו מצד עצמו כלום — *For he is truly lowly in his own eyes, because he has nothing of his own.*</small><br><small>Esh Kodesh, 5702, Bo (the Piaseczner), line 525: אינו אותו האיש של אחר שעות הבטלה — *He is not the same person he was after the idle hours.*</small><br><small>Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 29:19, line 556: יותר לא ירגיש בה — *The more [inward it is], the less he feels it.*</small>

**How to tell, over weeks, that it's working.**

- **Look at weeks, not days.** One bright sitting proves nothing; neither does one flat week. Read four weekly checks side by side.
- **Quieter, less full of yourself, more your own master.** If two of the three are moving, even a little, it is working. Of the three, the second, an insult that stings less, is the one the Mitteler Rebbe names as the sign.
- **The line in your own words gets longer.** Early on you can only repeat the line. Later you can say things about it you never read. That is the sign you have entered it and not just been told it.
- **The evening line fills in.** "Came back at the red light" starts to appear more often, without effort.
- **The joy and the quiet grow together.** The Mitteler Rebbe says real contemplation brings joy at what you grasp and bitul before what you can't, in equal measure. If only the joy grows, watch for self; if only the lowness grows, go back to sitting 1.

<small>The Gate of Unity 5:39 (the Mitteler Rebbe), line 199: ולפי ערך השמחה כך ערך הביטול ממש — *And as the measure of the joy, so the measure of the bitul, exactly.*</small>

**What not to log.** Don't rate feelings from one to ten. Don't log the intensity of a sitting. Don't write down highs as progress: a feeling made into the main thing is, in the Mitteler Rebbe's words, serving yourself. And don't write a self-accounting on a low day.

**When the log says something is wrong.**

- *Always the same sentence in "the line in my own words."* You're repeating, not entering. Next time, go into one detail you haven't touched.
- *Lots of words, no acts ticked.* Shorten the thinking and give the act the last two minutes.
- *Flat on the second and third time.* Normal. The Rashab says the real work often begins colder. Stay.
- *Flat for two weeks on one rung.* Change the rung, not the effort.
- *Worse, frightened, or things feel unreal after a sitting.* Stop that rung. Go back to sitting 1 or 13, write one line about it, and use only the three-minute versions for a few days.
- *A sense of a special mission, racing thoughts, no sleep.* Stop the ladder and talk to someone you trust today.
- *Danger.* Stop everything and reach a person now.

For the usual snags (nothing happened, the mind won't stop, head but not heart, it worked yesterday and not today, no time), see Part V. For telling a real inner state from a counterfeit, see Part IV.

## A note on the sources

Every Hebrew line in this part was checked with `find_he.py verify "<file>" "<phrase>" --line N` and came back FOUND. Kuntres HaAvodah is uncorrected OCR and is quoted as the scan has it. The file paths are listed in Part II's note on sources, plus the Piaseczner's `Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Derekh HaMelekh (Warsaw, 1931).txt` and `Esh Kodesh (Warsaw Ghetto, 1941).txt`.

What is ours: the forty-day calendar, every minute count, the ten-minute and three-minute shapes, the trouble-to-rung table, and the log's questions. The sources give "a long, unbroken time" and "as your time allows," never minutes. They give the direction of each choice here (repetition, habit, a light return at any hour, the afterward as the test, joy and bitul together, the particular over the general), and the choices are built to follow it. The craft rules behind the practice are in `../../Chabad Library/hisbonenus/paths/CRAFT.md` (C2.2 length, C8.5 a thread for the day, C9.1-C9.5 repetition), and the Piaseczner's quieting in `../PIASECZNO.md` §3.
