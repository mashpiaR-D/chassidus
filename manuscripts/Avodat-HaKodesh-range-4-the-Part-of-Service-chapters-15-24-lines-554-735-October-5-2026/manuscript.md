## The shape of the range

The last range proved that service is a need on high. This range asks how it works, what comes of it, and why a soul was sent down to do it.

1. **How can the lower move the upper?** (554-587). A rejected answer (two lutes tuned alike) and ibn Gabbai's own: man is built in the form of the upper Man, and the Torah is the thread that joins the two forms. The shadow that laughs when you laugh.
2. **Reward is the nature of the deed, not a promise** (588-625). Why the Torah speaks of rain and peace and not of the world to come. The blessings are "telling", not "promising".
3. **Death is not natural** (626-686). Man was made to live; sin brought death; the Torah was given to give back life. The body and soul keep each other.
4. **Why the soul came down** (687-704). It had to come to be whole from above and below. "One hour of return and good deeds in this world is better than all the life of the world to come."
5. **The soul comes wise and forgets on purpose** (705-723). Against the Rambam: the soul is not a mere capacity. The angel's blow is a gift, so that we will toil.
6. **Speech and deed, both with thought** (724-735). Thought, speech and deed must be joined, and the thought fixed in Ein Sof.

## The unity of G-d

**The lower moves the upper because it is shaped like it.** R. Yitzchak Arama had said: the world and man are like two lutes tuned alike; pluck a string on one and the same string sounds on the other (line 562). Ibn Gabbai rejects this as the reason: the world is a created, separate thing, "and neither this nor that came to His help or His need" (line 567); Arama "wanted to bring under the net of reason what is above it" (line 568). His own answer rests on five foundations, and the fourth is the key:

> והכלל כי לא יתעורר הכבוד כי אם בהתעוררות דבר שיש לו עמו יחס ודמיון באמצעות דבר יש לו יחס ודמיון עם שניהם ואז מחבר ומתיים ביניהם ומשלימם (Avodat HaKodesh 2:16:9; line 577)
> The rule is that the Glory is only awakened by the awakening of a thing that has a relation and likeness to it, by means of a thing that has a relation and likeness to both, and then it joins and twins them and completes them.

Man is built "in the form of the upper Man", not in the form of the cosmos (line 572). The Torah, woven of the ten sefirot, is the middle thing that resembles both (line 575). So:

> יתחייב מזה ויאמן כי בהתנועע זה הדמות בפעולותיו גם הדמות העליון יתנועע לקראתו וישמע ויענה אליו (Avodat HaKodesh 2:16:10; line 578)
> From this it follows, and is believed, that when this form moves in its deeds, the upper Form also moves toward it, and hears and answers it.

"Whoever keeps one mitzvah below keeps it above ... this is the secret of 'He rides the heavens with your help', with your help, truly" (בעזרך ממש, line 575). Then the lutes are allowed back, since now the two instruments really are tuned alike (line 579): "I rule man; who rules Me? The righteous" (line 579).

**The shadow.** The Midrash he brings is a picture any person can hold:

> מה צלך אם אתה משחק לו הוא משחק לך, ואם אתה בוכה לו הוא בוכה כנגדך ... כשם שאתה הווה עמו הוא הווה עמך (Avodat HaKodesh 2:16:12; line 580)
> What is your shadow? If you laugh to it, it laughs to you; if you weep to it, it weeps opposite you ... just as you are with Him, He is with you.

"The Lord is your shadow": "Ehyeh asher Ehyeh: as you are with Me, so I am with you" (line 580). The Torah and mitzvot are "like the thread that joins and sticks two pieces of cloth" (line 583). "You who cling to the Lord your G-d": "by the clinging of thought to the powers of the Great Name ... for you have a relation and likeness to Him" (line 584).

*Ours.* The shadow is the book's clearest image of how G-d relates to the one who serves, and it is the source of the Chassidic "the Lord is your shadow" teaching (Baal Shem Tov, Kedushat Levi, and the Rebbe often). Chassidus reads it the same way: as your face below, so His face above (F02, S63). The guide can offer it as a state: **mirrored**. What I bring to Him, He brings to me.

**The Torah is the Holy One.** "The Holy One is called Torah ... and there is no Torah but the Holy One" (ואין תורה אלא קב"ה, line 611). "Those who are busy with it and hold it are busy with and hold the Name of the Lord and cling to it" (line 671). F10 in its strongest pre-Chassidic form.

**Everything was made with His Name, so His Name joined to us keeps it alive.** At Sinai: "all was created with My Name, and when My Name is joined by your hands with what was created by it, it will keep it forever" (הכל נברא בשמי ובהיות שמי משתתף על ידיכם עם הנברא בו הנה הוא יקיימנו לעד, line 677). *Ours.* This is F01 (the world said into being) and F07 (His Name joined to the world by our deeds), in one sentence.

**"A king without a queen is not a king."** "When is the Holy One called great? When He is in the Assembly of Israel ... a King without a Matrona is not a King, and not great, and not praised" (מלכא בלא מטרוניתא לאו הוא מלכא, line 696). The cause of that unity "is the completeness of the soul in Torah and mitzvot" (line 696).

## The purpose of the soul's descent

This is one of the most important passages in the whole book for the guide, and it bears on the owner's question about the purpose of creation.

**The question.** Souls come from the "Ark of the Covenant" (Malchut) and return to it. "If so, why were they created? Since they come from there and return there, it would be good for them to stay there" (line 697). And the descent is dangerous: if the matter turns aside, the soul will be punished "bitterly" (line 698).

**The answer.** The soul must be "included from above and from below" to complete the Glory (line 697). The Zohar: souls go out from the Ark into the upper Tzaddik and into the tzaddik below, "to unify the unity below and to draw the glory of the Holy One from above to below", and return, "so that all souls be included from above and below, and the glory of the Holy One be included from above and below, whole from every side" (line 692). Ibn Gabbai:

> הנה גלה ע"ה טעם ביאת הנשמה לחול בגוף ושובה אל המקום אשר באה משם, שהכל הוא צורך גבוה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:22:7; line 693)
> Here he revealed the reason for the soul's coming to dwell in the body and its return to the place it came from: all of it is a need on high.

> יפה שעה אחת בתשובה ומעשים טובים בעולם הזה מכל חיי העולם הבא (Avodat HaKodesh 2:22:14; line 700)
> One hour of return and good deeds in this world is better than all the life of the world to come.

> והנה הנשמה בעודה שם אין כוונת הייחוד והתפשטות המציאות נשלמת, ואין הכבוד משתלם ... ובבואה להשלים כל זה הנה שעה אחת שתעסוק להשלים השלמות ההוא יפה מכל חיי העולם הבא שהיתה שם קודם בואה ... וזה טעם בואה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:22:14; line 700)
> While the soul is there, the intent of unity and the spreading of being is not complete, and the Glory is not complete ... and when it comes to complete all this, one hour it spends completing that completion is better than all the life of the world to come it had there before it came ... and that is the reason it came.

*Ours.* This is the Tanya's answer, and the Rebbe's, before them. Tanya ch. 37 and Iggeret HaKodesh 4 teach that the soul goes down to this world not for its own sake (it was perfect above) but to refine the body and the world, and that "one hour of return and good deeds" is the proof. The Rebbe Rashab and the Rebbe make "descent for the sake of ascent" (ירידה צורך עליה) their frame. Ibn Gabbai gives the whole structure: the soul is sent, the risk is real, the gain is not for the soul but for the Glory, and one hour here is worth more than eternity there. For the guide this may be the single most useful teaching of the book: **your hour here matters more than heaven**. It answers "why am I here?" at the root (S82, F07).

**Better not to have been born, but now that you are...** The houses of Shammai and Hillel voted: "better for man not to have been created; now that he was created, let him examine his deeds" (line 699). Ibn Gabbai: better not, because the matter leans to sin; but "He created the Torah as a spice", so let him examine and repair, and "let him feel (ימשמש) his deeds", as one feels one's tefillin every hour "so that he takes to heart that he is a throne of glory and a dwelling for the Presence" (line 699).

**Why we forget what we knew.** Ibn Gabbai argues against the Rambam that the soul is not a mere capacity to receive wisdom (line 705). It comes wise: "a lamp lit on its head", taught the whole Torah, shown the righteous crowned and the wicked punished, then struck by an angel and made to forget (lines 707-711). Why forget?

> ואלו יצא לאויר העולם והוא חכם ויודע וזוכר כל מה שראה ולמד שם, הנה לא היה מקום אל ההשתדלות בתקון הכבוד ולא תשלם הכוונה בייחוד (Avodat HaKodesh 2:23:8; line 712)
> Had he come out into the world wise and knowing and remembering all he saw and learned there, there would be no room for effort in repairing the Glory, and the intent of unity would not be completed.

"So G-d meant the forgetting for good, so that he will always toil" (לטובה חשבה אלהים השכחה ההיא כדי שיהיה יגע תמיד, line 713). And the Midrash: "for his good a man learns Torah and forgets; had he not forgotten, he would learn two or three years and go back to his work" (line 714). *Ours.* A deep and gentle teaching for the guide: the feeling that one once knew and has lost it, the longing to recover, is by design. The effort is the point. It fits S13 (the unnamed longing) and technique 23 (coming back to the same line).

**A different view of the soul from the Rambam's.** For the Rambam the soul's crown is knowledge of G-d and His works through study of the sciences. Ibn Gabbai: then a righteous man who spent his life in Talmud and mitzvot would lack the crown, "and this would be a great destruction ... turning the dish on its face" (line 718). He quotes the Rambam against himself: "whoever keeps one mitzvah of the 613 as fit, and joins no worldly intention to it, but does it for its own sake from love, has earned the life of the world to come" (line 720). *Ours.* This is exactly where Chassidus stands (Tanya ch. 5, 35-37): the unity is reached through Torah and mitzvot, not through philosophy.

## States of consciousness

**Mirrored by Him.** From inside: the feeling that what one brings, G-d brings back. Joy draws joy; tears draw tears; a closed face draws a closed face (line 580). It is not a threat but an invitation. A proposed state (JSON). It sits between S33 (before Whom) and S63 (the world held).

**Sent for an hour worth eternity.** From inside: a quiet seriousness about ordinary hours, because this hour of return and good deeds is worth more than the soul's whole life above (line 700). Not anxiety; weight and dignity. A proposed state: **the hour worth heaven** (JSON). It is the Chassidic "descent for the sake of ascent" felt in the present.

**A throne and a dwelling.** The one who touches his tefillin through the day remembers "that he is a throne of glory and a dwelling for the Presence" (line 699). *Ours.* A state that can be entered by a touch: the body as a place where He rests. It fits S81 (the deed in the hand) and S82 (the home below).

**Body and soul keeping each other.** The body helps the soul by its deeds; the soul helps the body by its intention; "and so life is completed for both, coming out from between them" (line 635). The 248 positive mitzvot heal the 248 limbs (line 636). *Ours.* This is a teaching against the split of body and spirit, and the guide should use it: the body is not an obstacle; it is the partner that does.

## Meditation and practice

**Serve for His sake, not for yours.** "That the service be for His need and not that we intend any goal for ourselves, for then the service would be for us and not for Him" (כי אז תהיה העבודה לנו לא לו, line 558). "And when we serve fittingly, even if we do not intend for ourselves, in the end honor will come and blessings will rest on our heads, for so the King set in the nature of the service" (line 558).

**Reward is nature, not payment.** The Torah's blessings (rain, peace, children) are not promises like a king's pay:

> כי כל הטובות ההם אינם בדרך יעוד כמלך המיעד לעבדיו לשלם להם שכר פעולתם ... לפי שהטוב הבא על דרך יעוד יורה שאינו נמשך אל העבודה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:18:5; line 613)
> All those goods do not come by way of promise, like a king who promises his servants to pay them for their work ... for good that comes by way of promise shows that it does not flow from the service.

They are "telling and narration": the good flows from the service by its nature, as grain from a tilled field (lines 614, 623). "Whoever tills his soil will have plenty of bread" (Prov. 12:11): the soil is the upper earth, the Shechinah, which "needs to be kept, hoed and watered" like a garden (line 623). *Ours.* This changes how a person can hold reward. Not "I did this so I will get that", but "this is a garden, and tending it yields". It removes the bargain from service. The guide can use the garden picture for daily practice (technique 22, carrying a line through the day).

**Oil above, incense below.** "'Oil and incense gladden the heart': oil from above, incense from below, and then is the joy of all" (line 616). The oil that flows from above "was not ready until the service below rose" (line 616). *Ours.* A simple picture for prayer: my small rising smoke meets the oil coming down; only together do they gladden the heart above.

**Speech, deed and thought, never apart.**

> כי הדבור שאינו נשלם במעשה ומחשבה, או מעשה שאינו נשלם בדבור ומחשבה אינו שלם, ואין התקון עולה כראוי (Avodat HaKodesh 2:24:4; line 727)
> For speech that is not completed by deed and thought, or deed that is not completed by speech and thought, is not whole, and the repair does not rise as it should.

The soul came "included in the power of speech and the power of deed" like the place it came from (line 721), and it returns "with added height and glory" (line 726). *Ours.* This is the Tanya's three garments (thought, speech, deed; ch. 4) and the guide's C3 current (mind to heart to hand), with ibn Gabbai's demand that each carry the other two.

**Learn for its own sake: build palaces above and below.** "Whoever studies Torah for its own sake makes peace in the household above and below ... as if he built palaces above and below ... shields the whole world ... brings the redemption near" (line 731). Learning "for its own sake" means with the intention of unity (line 732). And David "did not keep silent day or night, and he joined ... he joined the Torah above to the Holy One" (line 729).

**Fix the thought in Ein Sof.** The last step of every service:

> צריך לשלמות הכל להעלות הכל עד אין סוף וליחד את שמו בו, ולתקוע בו המחשבה וזה תכלית העבודות כלן (Avodat HaKodesh 2:24:11; line 734)
> For the completion of all, one must raise all up to Ein Sof and unite His Name in Him, and fix the thought in Him; and this is the goal of all services.

The Zohar: "there is an order that stands to know and to look, and there is an order that stands in will, to look higher and higher to Ein Sof, where all wills and thoughts are fixed and do not stand on any word at all; as He is hidden, so all His words are in hiddenness" (line 735). *Ours.* This is the last move of a meditation: after the words and pictures, let the will rest, with no word, toward the One beyond all. It is technique 14 and technique 15 (the quiet after), and S36 (faith above knowing). A proposed technique: **fixing the thought in the Infinite** (JSON).

## The big human questions

**Why am I here?** To complete the Glory from below; an hour here is better than eternity above (line 700).

**Why is life hard?** Because the matter leans to sin (line 699), and because we forgot what we knew, so that we would toil (line 713). The toil is the point.

**Why do we die?** Not by nature. Man was made to live forever; sin removed the higher soul (neshamah) and brought death (lines 656, 659). The Torah was given to restore life: "freedom from the angel of death" (line 679).

**What is reward?** The fruit of a tended garden, not a wage (line 623). Honor comes by itself to one who serves for its own sake (line 558).

**Does G-d respond?** Like a shadow: as you are with Him, He is with you (line 580).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 588-608 (the debate of the Rambam, the Ramban, the Ran, Albo, ibn Shem Tov and ibn Bibbag on why the Torah does not name spiritual reward), 626-634 (the heavens as proof that composite things need not decay; Adam's heel dimming the sun) and 660-668 (Enoch who became Metatron, and Isaiah against those who say man dies by nature) were read in full. They serve the argument that death is not natural and reward is intrinsic.
