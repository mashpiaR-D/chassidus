<a id="c09"></a> ## C09 · I doubt that my faith and devotion are real

*7 findings from Torah Ohr (part 1 of 2), Kuntres HaHispaalus, Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3), Biurei Tanya, Sha'arei Avodah, Chanah Ariel*

> Is my devotion real, or just my personality? My love is mixed with self-interest, so it must be fake. I slip, so maybe I don't love God. I don't trust what I feel, and sometimes I doubt I believe at all.

**What is really going on.** Sha'arei Avodah (R. Aharon of Strashelye): devotion mixed with self-interest and imagination is still whole; the arousal comes from a hidden love that arrives mixed, and each person's measure, even imagined, is true at its root. With the Mitteler Rebbe's Kuntres HaHispaalus it rejects shutting feeling down for fear of ego: let the heart be moved, then take the 'I' out of the feeling. Chanah Ariel: lapses from laziness or overwhelming desire do not touch love; its real opposite is resentful service. Biurei Tanya: doubts are not the self, since forced to deny, the person would give his life. Likkutei Sichos: prayer born of one's own trouble or plain fear is real prayer. Torah Ohr is the counterweight: temperament does mix even into good traits, and knowledge must reach the heart's inner layer. The mixture is admitted as a reason to refine, not to discard.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"My devotion is mixed with self-interest and imagination, so it's fake."* → Though mixed, it is whole. The arousal comes from the hidden love in every heart, only it arrives mixed; each person's measure, even as imagination, is true at its root. `SA·Q08` · `SA·Q10` · `SA·Q12` · `SA·Q18`
- *"If I feel it, it's ego; it's safer not to feel."* → That is called the error of those who lack understanding. Toil in thought until the heart is moved; only then remove the sense of self from the feeling. `SA·Q05` · `SA·Q17` · `SA·Q06` · `KH·Q03` · `KH·Q13`
- *"My lapses prove I don't really love God."* → A lapse from the body's laziness or a desire that overpowered him does not touch his love; he never intended to act against the Other's will. `CA·Q20` · `CA·Q21`
- *"My doubts mean I don't really believe."* → If forced to deny, he would give his life. The doubts only rule him; the counsel is not to believe them. `BT·Q28`
- *"My prayer comes only from my own troubles or plain fear, so it isn't real prayer."* → Even fear of punishment clears the mind for prayer, and it makes no difference what caused the bitterness; the lowliness itself is what is heard. `LS2·Q07` · `LS2·Q08` · `LS2·Q09`
- *"My passion for holy things is surely pure."* → Even a trait that seems wholly good may still carry some mixture. Without understanding one imagines oneself whole; and the arousal must not be taken for one's own pride. `TO1·Q05` · `SA·Q01` · `SA·Q16`

1. **Let the heart be moved, then take the self out of it** · *from Sha'arei Avodah, Kuntres HaHispaalus* (1) In prayer, contemplate one divine idea at length, in breadth and detail, the way news about your own affairs grips you. (2) Toil in thought until the heart itself is moved; do not forbid the feeling. (3) If one line of contemplation does not move you, turn to another: that there is nothing besides Him. (4) Once moved, do the real work: don't take the arousal for your own pleasure or pride, don't make a 'something' of it, don't dwell on feeling it. (5) Turn the heart's arousal back into strength for the mind to go deeper. (6) Accept partial victory, each by his measure; in a war some days are won and some are not. (7) Carry it into deeds. *When:* Daily at Shema and prayer; a lifelong effort whose intensity varies with the hour. *For:* Anyone whose devotion has gone dry, head-only or habit-only, or who distrusts every feeling. *Not for:* Not a warrant to chase emotional highs; Kuntres HaHispaalus puts craving the feeling at the lowest level. Someone depressed, for whom a 'feel your distance and lack' step could deepen despair. `SA·Q06` · `SA·Q15` · `SA·Q16` · `SA·Q11` · `SA·Q09` · `KH·Q13` · `KH·Q25` · `KH·Q14`
2. **Don't believe the doubts** · *from Biurei Tanya* (1) Notice that the doubts rule you, though you have never actually denied. (2) Ask the test question: if forced to deny, would I give my life rather than do it? (3) If so, do not take the doubts as your own voice; the counsel is simply not to believe them. *When:* Whenever doubts dominate. *For:* Someone who would never deny, but is harassed by doubts. *Not for:* Someone with honest intellectual questions, who needs them engaged, not dismissed. Someone with obsessive doubt, for whom repeated self-testing becomes a compulsion. Evidence is one passage. `BT·Q28`

- From *"My devotion is mixed and imagined, so it's fake."* to **Mixed, yet whole. Your measure of self-giving, even as imagination, is true, and God meets you in it.** The arousal comes from a hidden love at the root; fear of impurity stops being a reason to withdraw. *(Sha'arei Avodah)* `SA·Q08` · `SA·Q10` · `SA·Q12` · `SA·Q07` - From *"Real depth, and safety from ego, mean not feeling."* to **Feeling in the heart is the aim. Be moved first, then take the 'me' out of the feeling.** Forbidding feeling cuts the mind off from the heart and leaves the animal soul at full strength; the spark has to be drawn out together with the feeling self and then refined. *(Sha'arei Avodah, Kuntres HaHispaalus)* `SA·Q05` · `SA·Q06` · `SA·Q17` · `KH·Q03` · `KH·Q13` · `KH·Q25` - From *"My lapses prove I don't really love God."* to **A lapse out of laziness or overwhelming desire doesn't touch the love; the loyal man sometimes spoils even his own affairs that way.** Love is measured by what one wants for the Other, not by never failing; its real opposite is grudging service. *(Chanah Ariel)* `CA·Q20` · `CA·Q21` - From *"My doubts mean I don't really believe."* to **The doubts are not you. If forced to deny, you would give your life.** Moving the doubt outside the self frees the person from identifying with it; readiness for self-sacrifice shows what the core holds. *(Biurei Tanya)* `BT·Q28` - From *"My prayer only comes from my own troubles or fear, so it isn't real."* to **Even simple fear settles the mind for prayer, and bitterness from a private lack is itself the lowliness God hears.** Lowering the threshold and honoring the pain makes prayer possible now, while the inner soul still reaches toward the highest. *(Likkutei Sichos)* `LS2·Q07` · `LS2·Q08` · `LS2·Q09` - From *"My passion for holy things is surely pure."* to **Even a trait that looks wholly good may carry some mixture of temperament. Seek the inner heart through knowledge.** Mixture belongs to the outer layer of the heart; knowledge draws out the inner layer, which it does not reach. *(Torah Ohr)* `TO1·Q05`

**Where the works agree.** The works agree that devotion need not be pure to be real: a hidden love lies beneath the mixture, each person's measure counts, and refinement is gradual. Sha'arei Avodah and Kuntres HaHispaalus agree that feeling in prayer is wanted, that suppressing it out of fear of ego is an error, and that the self must then be kept out of the feeling.

**Where they differ.** Torah Ohr leans to vigilance (even a trait that looks wholly good may be mixed); Sha'arei Avodah to reassurance (mixed yet whole, even imagination true). On feeling, Sha'arei Avodah says feel it, then remove the 'I'; Kuntres HaHispaalus warns against dwelling on the feeling at all. Likkutei Sichos lowers the threshold to plain fear; Biurei Tanya meets doubt by disowning it rather than engaging it.

**Order and timing.** First let the heart be moved in contemplation; then refine the self out of the feeling, accepting partial success; carry it into deeds. Doubt and lapses are met by a test of the core (what you would never do, what you want for Him), not by more self-scrutiny.

- Scrupulosity about sincerity (religious OCD): 'even a good trait may be mixed' can feed endless purity-checking; lead with 'mixed, yet whole'. - Obsessive doubt: the 'would I give my life?' test can become a frightening compulsion. - Honest intellectual questioning: calling doubts 'from the other side' can shame the person or close off real inquiry. - Depression and trauma: numbness in prayer may be illness, not a lack of faith; don't read absent feeling as absent love. - Depression: steps that ask a person to feel his distance and lack can deepen despair. - 'Let yourself be moved' is not a license to chase emotional highs, and not for someone prone to emotional flooding.

**Truths for the guide** *(candidates, each checked against its passages; see CHECKS.md)*

- **Mixed is still real** · Devotion tangled with self-interest, habit or imagination is still devotion. Nothing human arrives pure; it is refined over time, not disqualified at the door. *Check: yes.*
- **A lapse is not a lack of love** · Slipping through tiredness, or an urge that overpowers you, does not show you don't care. Love is measured by what you want for the other, not by never failing. *Check: yes.*
- **Let yourself feel, then step out of the center** · A feeling doesn't have to be pure to be allowed. Shutting it down to stay safe from ego leaves you dry and unchanged; let it come, then gently move yourself out of its center. *Check: yes.*

**What the core works do not cover.** Doubt is handled by disowning it; nothing engages the content of intellectual doubt or helps someone whose belief has gone. Dryness is not told apart from depression or trauma numbness. Only one passage on doubt. TO1:P05's reframe runs the other way (against overconfidence) and belongs partly with complacency (C29).

<a id="c21"></a> ## C21 · The love and warmth I had for God is gone

*6 findings from Torah Ohr (part 2 of 2), Likkutei Torah (part 2 of 3), Likkutei Torah (part 3 of 3), Toras Chaim (part 1 of 4), Toras Chaim (part 2 of 4), Sefer HaSichos (part 2 of 2)*

> I remember a closeness that isn't there anymore. After years of distraction and failure, I feel there's no love for God left in me. My light has gone out.

**What is really going on.** Every work here says the same: the love is hidden, not gone. Torah Ohr: it sits in the inner point of every Jewish heart, 'Esther', covered by worldly pursuits taken up with the whole heart. Likkutei Torah: the spark is lost and in exile in the very thoughts, words and deeds that were not for God, and must be sought there; the pain of distance is felt only by a limb still attached. It is present even in transgressors, like a candle in a dark place. Toras Chaim: the love is rooted like nature in the will, above reason, cannot be put out, and at times surfaces by itself; even a defiled soul's lamp does not go out. Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz): the essential light is fire in flint lying in mud, and a memory of one's upbringing can strike it out. Recovery is uncovering, through compassion for the soul, not self-hatred, and then giving the light vessels so it stays.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"I feel no love for God, so there is none left in me."* → Not as people think: everyone has it in the inner point of the heart, even the least worthy; it is hidden, not felt openly. `TO2·Q19` · `TO2·Q20` · `LT3·Q09` · `LT3·Q38`
- *"My sins have put out my light."* → Nothing can uproot the hidden love; even after the soul is defiled its lamp does not go out, and it can rise and be purified. `TC1·Q02` · `TC1·Q28`
- *"I've drifted too far from where I came from; nothing is left."* → No current, however strong, puts out the essential light. It lies hidden like fire in flint and comes out when something wakes the memory. `SHS2·Q09` · `SHS2·Q10` · `SHS2·Q11`
- *"My coldness proves I'm cut off."* → Pain is felt only by a limb still attached. The ache of distance is itself proof of the bond. `LT2·Q05` · `LT2·Q04`
- *"I have to make the love myself, from nothing."* → It comes from above by itself at times, without preparation; it only needs to be revealed. `TC2·Q30`
- *"Getting a little feeling back is the goal."* → Do not stop at a little love; draw it into deeds, study and charity so it stays. `LT3·Q12`

1. **Seek the spark where it was lost** · *from Likkutei Torah* (1) Know that the king is in the field: anyone who goes out to meet him is received warmly. (2) Know that the love is there, a spark of God within you, lost and in exile, not destroyed. (3) Know your root, where you came from, so you know where to return. (4) Search where it was lost: in the thoughts, words and deeds that were not for God. (5) Let the heart's bitterness turn into great compassion for the exiled spark; if you weep, weep for the soul that fell, not from self-hatred. (6) Do not stop at a little love. Give the light vessels in study, charity and deeds, so it stays. *When:* Elul through Yom Kippur above all; also daily prayer and confession, since return is for all one's days. *For:* Someone who feels far away or has lost earlier warmth and wants a way back that is not self-hatred. *Not for:* Someone prone to obsessive self-scrutiny: the search must end in compassion, not an audit. No one should be pressed to weep, and it is not for someone in whom compassion for the soul slides into despair. `LT2·Q01` · `LT2·Q03` · `LT2·Q04` · `LT3·Q10` · `LT3·Q11` · `LT3·Q16` · `LT3·Q12`
2. **Esther's way: when the mind is dark** · *from Torah Ohr* (1) Know that the love is certainly there, hidden in the inner point of the heart. (2) Name the thorns that hide it: worldly pursuits taken up with the whole heart and soul. (3) Treat arrogant or defiant thoughts as a garment on the spark, not as your self. (4) Plead for mercy on the spark clothed in those garments. (5) When understanding is gone, cry out from the distress itself, without contemplation, and hold to faith as if your eyes saw. *When:* In prayer; Purim is the time it breaks open. *For:* Someone who feels no love at all, who is in a 'night', or who is caught in arrogant or defiant thoughts. *Not for:* Not a way to excuse the thoughts. Not for someone whose crying out comes from an acute crisis that needs practical help. `TO2·Q19` · `TO2·Q20` · `TO2·Q21` · `TO2·Q22` · `TO2·Q23` · `TO2·Q24`
3. **Strike the flint: memory and gathering** · *from Sefer HaSichos 5706-5710* (1) Set regular gatherings, monthly and on festivals. (2) There, let each person wake the other. (3) Recall and retell memories of a home and upbringing: its stories and melodies. (4) Let a memory be the strike that brings out the fire hidden in the flint. *When:* Monthly and on every festival. *For:* People who have drifted from a traditional background. *Not for:* Someone whose memories of upbringing are painful or abusive, or who carries religious trauma. `SHS2·Q10` · `SHS2·Q11` · `SHS2·Q15`
4. **Soften the stone heart** · *from Toras Chaim* (1) If real inspiration collapses into desire right after prayer, read it as a heart that was never broken open in true return. (2) Return from the depth of the heart, with great submission. (3) The hardness breaks; the heart becomes soft and its good is roused to follow God. (4) The pull of desire is separated from the love, so inspiration rises unmixed. *When:* Linked to prayer and return; no schedule in the text. *For:* Someone whose inspiration keeps collapsing into desire, or whose heart is hard. *Not for:* Anyone depressed, shame-ridden or traumatized, for whom 'breaking the heart' may deepen despair. `TC1·Q22` · `TC1·Q23` · `TC1·Q24`

- From *"I show no love for God, so I don't have any."* to **The love is hidden, not missing, like a candle in a dark place. Everyone has it in the inner point of the heart; it needs to be drawn out.** The problem moves from 'I lack' to 'it is covered', which turns despair into the task of removing what covers it. *(Torah Ohr, Likkutei Torah)* `TO2·Q19` · `TO2·Q20` · `LT3·Q09` · `LT3·Q38` - From *"My failures and coldness prove I'm cut off."* to **The spark has not left you; it is in exile. Pain is felt only by a limb still attached, and you are attached.** Bitterness turns into compassion for one's own captive soul, which moves him to return instead of giving up. *(Likkutei Torah)* `LT2·Q04` · `LT2·Q05` - From *"My love for God is gone."* to **A hidden love is rooted like nature in the will. It cannot be put out, and at times it surfaces on its own.** Nothing has to be manufactured; the love is uncovered, which removes hopelessness and gives a reason to watch for it. *(Toras Chaim)* `TC2·Q30` - From *"My sins have put out whatever spark I had."* to **Many waters cannot quench it. The soul's lamp does not go out even after it is soiled.** The love is the soul's own nature, so the person works with an undamaged core, not a lost one. *(Toras Chaim)* `TC1·Q02` · `TC1·Q28` - From *"I've drifted too far; whatever was in me is gone."* to **The essential light is like fire in flint that has lain for years in mud: dirty outside, the fire intact, and it comes out when struck.** Water puts out a flame but not the fire in flint; the self is hidden, never lost, and a memory can strike it out. *(Sefer HaSichos 5706-5710)* `SHS2·Q09` · `SHS2·Q10` · `SHS2·Q11` - From *"I'd have to earn my way back in."* to **The king comes out to the field, and anyone who goes out to meet him is received with a smiling face.** The return begins where the person is, not at the palace, which removes the sense that one must first be worthy. *(Likkutei Torah)* `LT2·Q01`

**Where the works agree.** Every work agrees the love is hidden, not gone; that it is present in everyone they address, even the transgressor; that it cannot be extinguished; and that return is uncovering, not manufacturing. The Alter Rebbe's and the Mitteler Rebbe's works both turn bitterness into compassion for the soul rather than attack on the self.

**Where they differ.** How it is uncovered: searching where it was lost (Likkutei Torah); crying out beyond understanding and holding to faith (Torah Ohr); it surfaces by itself from above (Toras Chaim); a memory or a gathering strikes it (Rayatz); or a hard heart must first be broken (Toras Chaim, Bereishis vol. 1). Scope differs: the Alter and Mitteler Rebbe speak of every Jewish soul; the Rayatz's flint passage of those from chassidic families.

**Order and timing.** First, the knowledge that it is there. Then, as one can: search and compassion (Elul and daily prayer); in a dark night, the cry and plain faith; memory and gathering to strike the spark; then vessels (study, charity, deeds) so the light stays. Heart-breaking work only for one whose inspiration keeps collapsing, and with guidance.

- Depression and anhedonia: lost warmth may be illness; bitterness, weeping and 'breaking the heart' can deepen it. Care comes first. - Grief: losing warmth toward God after a death needs room, not a program. - Scrupulosity: 'search the thoughts, words and deeds' can become an endless audit; it must end in compassion. - Religious trauma or a harmful upbringing: memories of home can wound, and pressing return can harm. - Someone who left by conviction should not be told what is 'really' inside him against his own account. - No one should be pressed to weep on demand.

<a id="c25"></a> ## C25 · The ache of feeling far from God

*6 findings from Likkutei Torah (part 1 of 3), Likkutei Torah (part 3 of 3), Imrei Binah, Sha'ar HaYichud, Derech Mitzvosecha, Chanah Ariel*

> I feel so far from God, stuck in a body, and His hiddenness weighs on me like worry. The more I contemplate, the more He slips away. I'm weary of the dark, and I can't tell if this pain is good or harmful.

**What is really going on.** The works treat the ache as holy longing and set it apart from sadness. Imrei Binah says love is born from distance, as the absent are longed for most. Likkutei Torah (Tazria, Beha'alotcha) rejects sadness outright but asks for worry and bitterness over not grasping God. That bitterness belongs at a fixed hour, such as midnight, and is followed by Torah study, which gladdens the heart. Derech Mitzvosecha says the same worry is not bodily sadness, and Torah study brings joy even unseen, like holding a closed vessel of coins. Sha'ar HaYichud says the heart that worries and the heart that rejoices are one force in two equal lines; weeping comes from delight and longing, and without understanding it is an empty image. Likkutei Torah (Shir HaShirim) says 'black, yet beautiful': the beauty comes from the blackness itself. Chanah Ariel counts even the weary wish to flee the dark, and a flash that fades at once, as real service.

**Where the works agree.** The ache of distance is real and holy longing. It is not the same as sadness, and it must not be allowed to become sadness. It belongs together with joy, and it is meant to pass into gladness through Torah, mitzvot and understanding. Distance feeds love; it does not disqualify.

**Where they differ.** Likkutei Torah asks a complacent person to set bitterness deliberately at a fixed hour. Imrei Binah treats distance as natural fuel for love. Sha'ar HaYichud demands that joy and ache come in proportion and warns of self-deception. Chanah Ariel honors even weariness and the wish to flee. Derech Mitzvosecha emphasizes joy in a light that is present though unseen.

**Order and timing.** The bitterness has its fixed hour only (midnight, or Shema) and only on weekdays. Torah study follows at once and gladdens. Joy carries the rest of the day, widening on the festivals. After any contemplation, check that ache and joy came together; if only the ache came, return to understanding.

- Never cultivate bitterness in someone depressed, anxious, grieving, traumatized or at risk of self-harm. The texts' own distinction from sadness is the guardrail. - Sha'ar HaYichud's phrase about weeping 'until he loathes his very life' must never be offered to anyone. It could be heard as endorsing suicidal feeling. - Depression can wear the words of spiritual longing. If the heaviness does not lift after its hour, spreads through the day, or affects sleep, appetite or functioning, it needs care, not more contemplation. - Weeping must never be demanded, and its absence must not be read as a deficient soul. - The proportion test can become self-condemnation for harsh self-critics.
