The two parts differ sharply. The Preface (1-74) has 29 stated, 29 structural, 15 none and 1 reading. The Introduction (75-96) has 20 stated and 2 structural, because it is an anthology of kabbalistic sayings about Ein Sof, and almost every one of them states the unity directly.

## The shape of the range

The Preface is a defence of writing the book. It is not yet the doctrine. Still, it keeps stopping to say what the doctrine is for. So F12 (knowing the unity, and bringing that knowing to the heart) runs through the whole Preface. It leads the count, as in no Tanya range. The Preface also shows the author's method. It sets three conditions on explaining by parable (lines 33-50). That is the SPINE's grammar rule G06 ("explain by analogy from the soul, and mark where the analogy fails") made into a formal rule set. Around these two threads are many lines of biography, praise, apology and practical advice. Those are marked *none* (15 lines) or *structural*.

The Introduction is almost pure F05 (the Essence beyond both). It gives nineteen short excerpts from the Raya Mehemna, the Zohar, Etz Chaim, Adam Yashar, the Raavad, Pardes, Elimah and R. Azriel. All of them say that nothing may be said, pictured or counted of Ein Sof. Then it turns to the Alter Rebbe's Shaar HaYichud 3-9 (lines 94-96) as the key to reading them. F07 (the home below) does not appear anywhere in the range, and no line has been stretched to include it.

## Preface 1-8 (lines 1-8): Kabbalah is knowledge of the unity, and the Zohar seems to contradict itself

The author states the premise of the whole book in line 3. Kabbalah is needed because the root of all service is to unite the Holy One and His Shechinah, and that union comes only through knowing: > וכל עיקר היחוד הוא על ידי הידיעה (line 3)

But He is beyond knowing. Human reason cannot find His unity in worlds that look separate: > אשר בשכל אנושי לא יוחקר יחודו יתברך בעולמות הנגלים ליש ונפרדים (line 3)

So Kabbalah is defined here by its subject. It is the received tradition of how to know the unity. Line 4 gathers the Zohar sayings that make this knowing a duty. The sharpest is the curse on one who "does not know how to unite the holy Name": > מאן דלא ידע לאחדא שמא קדישא (line 4)

Lines 6-8 set against these the Zohar's warnings not to reveal secrets, and they state the contradiction. The first set of sayings makes knowing Him a duty on all Israel. The second limits the secrets to the few "sons of the ascent". Line 8's "you who cleave to Havayah" is used only as proof that the verses are said to all Israel. It is marked structural, not F06.

## Preface 9-16 (lines 9-16): the two knowings, and why only understanding brings inner awe

The answer is the Alter Rebbe's, as the author heard it (line 9). There are two kinds of knowing. The first comes before awe and is needed by everyone. It is knowing His unity in the worlds. The line packs in the core of F05 and F02. We know His existence in the worlds, not His essence. He is utterly simple and has no relation to them, yet He is in all of them: > ופשוט בתכלית הפשיטות ומושלל מערך העולמות בתכלית השלילה ואף על פי כן הוא נמצא בכל העולמות (line 9)

He is alone after creation just as before: > וכמו שהיה לבדו מקודם שנבראו העולמות כן הוא יתברך לבדו אחר בריאת העולמות (line 9)

Line 10 is the first real piece of doctrine. Its model is Jacob's ladder. His flow, which is beyond limit, is joined to the limited "something" only by His power of wonder (pele). That is F09, the unlimited joined to the limited, and the text states it: > שהוא בחינת השפעתו יתברך למעלה מן הגבול הנקרא שמים עם בחינת התחתון שהוא בחינת הגבול והיש הוא על ידי התקשרותו יתברך בבחינת פלא (line 10)

The rungs are the chabad and middot of each world. Contemplation climbs them from below, and the flow is drawn down them from above. When all the rungs are bound and become vessels, "His Essence is revealed, that there is none besides Him" (line 10).

Lines 11-14 are the psychology of this knowing. Love needs knowledge of what is loved, and cleaving means "two essences becoming one" (line 11). But He is unlimited and we are limited, so the question stands (line 11). Awe (yirah) is seeing (re'iyah). Inner awe, the bitul of one's "something", comes only from understanding the unity: > וכולא ממש כלא חשיבא לגביה יתברך ואין דבר נמצא זולתו (line 13)

Plain faith that the worlds are nullified leaves them looking real, so it yields only outer fear (line 13). The closeness of seeing is to see that "all is His Essence": > שאינו רואה בכל הנפעלים בעולמות דבר ומהות בלעדו חס ושלום רק שהכל הוא עצמותו ברוך הוא (line 13)

Without this understanding, "the whole earth is full of His glory" traps one in the philosophers' dilemma: > אם עצמותו נמשך בעולם הרי עצמותו נתגשם חס ושלום ואם לא הרי העולם דבר שחוץ ממנו חס ושלום (line 13)

Kabbalah's answer is F05 in one clause (line 14): > שעצמותו ברוך הוא דוקא נמשך בעולמות ואף על פי כן לא נשתנה (line 14)

Line 15 is the Preface's longest paragraph, split into eleven sub-entries. Love is real inclusion, and "Israel are held in His very Name" (15.1, F06). A gloss grants, citing the Alter Rebbe's Likkutei Amarim, that faith-based natural love is also "whole service". But love born of understanding the unity excels it "as the speaking excels the animal" (15.2). Soul-roots and hours differ. The "heel" generations come mixed, so everyone must try (15.4-15.6). Then comes the range's key definition (15.7). The Holy One is His Essence. The Shechinah is His revelation in the worlds. As revealed, they are opposites: > אשר הקדוש ברוך הוא נקרא עצמותו יתברך ושכינתיה הוא הגילוי בעולמות (line 15) > אשר מצד הגילוי המה ממש שני הפכים (line 15)

Israel's service unites them. That is F09 and F05 together, and F02 as the goal. The first knowing is a duty on "every single Jew, each by his measure" (15.8). The second knowing covers the Names, combinations, palaces and kavanot. It is not for arousing love but for giving delight above (15.9). It belongs to those whose whole service is to unite the Holy One and His Shechinah "and all the worlds to Him" for His sake (15.10, F08). Line 16 then places the Tanya's phrase: > אין לו עסק בנסתרות כאלו כי אם לידיעת יחודו השייך לנפשו (line 16)

## Preface 17-32 (lines 17-32): why reveal now, and the chain from the Ari to the Alter Rebbe

Line 17 says the revealing belongs to the last generations and brings Mashiach. It is marked *reading* for F11. The index connects "the lowest generation receives the most" with the unity; the text makes the link only through line 3's definition. The Alter Rebbe's parable in line 18 does the work. A king lets each intimate into one treasury only, but opens all his treasuries to those standing outside, so that they are awed and nullified. Line 20 applies it to today's souls: > כדי שעל ידי ראותם גדולתו ותפארתו יתפעלו בביטול היש ולהתאחד אליו יתברך (line 20)

Line 20's second parable (those far from the light need a great light) is a second *reading* sub-entry for F11. Line 22 gives the book its method. Today's souls are ruled by physicality and are moved only by long, detailed explanation: > לכן לא יתפעלו כי אם באריכות הביאור אופן היחוד ממקורו ושרשו בפרטים גדולים (line 22)

Lines 23-28 trace the chain of teachers. The Ari's writings were taken literally "with great corporealization" (line 23). The Besht explained them with parables of soul and body, "from my flesh I see God": > ולהיות נתפס יחודו יתברך בלב כל אחד ואחד (line 24)

Then come the Maggid (line 25) and the Alter Rebbe (line 27), and the Alter Rebbe's passing (line 28). These are praise and lament. Only lines 24, 25 and 27 name the unity, as the teaching's purpose. Lines 29-32 warn against leaning too far. One who wants to hold everything in human reason "vomits even the little". But one who knows the Torah is beyond grasp and believes has enough to bind and nullify himself: > אז די לנפשו להתקשר אל הוי"ה ולבטלה אליו יתברך על ידי תורתו הקדושה (line 30)

## Preface 33-50 (lines 33-50): the three conditions on a parable

This is the Preface's doctrinal centre, and it is G06 turned into law.

1. **Know the root first** (lines 34-40). Only one who holds the wisdom from its root can find true parables in nature or in soul and body. The reason is itself a statement of the unity (F02, F04): > כי באמת אצלו יתברך הכל הוא בחינה אחת (line 35)

Lines 36-40 are proofs and kabbalistic mappings (Solomon's 3,000 parables, the "foxes" as Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah). They are structural. 2. **Parable only for His extension into the worlds, never for His being** (lines 41-44). The rule: > אבל מצד אמיתותו יתברך שלא מצד התמשכותו יתברך בעולמות כי אם מצד מהותו יתברך בזה חס וחלילה להמשיל איזה משל (line 41)

The soul in the body is allowed for how He fills the worlds, as long as there is "no change or division": > והעולמות בטלים ומתייחדים אצלו יתברך בלי שינוי ופירוד (line 43)

Line 44 gives a definition this index had not met: Atzilut is the power of the unity itself. > כח היחוד הזה נקרא אצילות (line 44) 3. **Never match the parable on every side** (lines 45-50). He quotes Shaar HaYichud 6 (line 45). Soul and body are separate in their root, but heaven and earth are made from nothing by the standing word (line 46, F01). He also quotes Tanya 42, "only to soothe the ear" (line 47). The man-parable holds at one point only. A man is one in many powers, so He includes all worlds "in one power" (line 48). It fails where man's middot change. The middot said of Him exist only through tzimtzum, as we see them: > הוא רק מצד הצמצום וההסתר בעולמות אשר נגלים לגבי דידן אבל לגביה איהו ומדותיו חד ממש (line 48)

The parable itself must be nullified to what it teaches (line 49). That is F08's shape applied to thinking; the index notes the likeness but has not tagged it. Line 50 draws the conclusion: > וכל תכלית הידיעה בזה הוא אשר לא נדע (line 50)

## Preface 51-74 (lines 51-74): the book's purpose, and knowledge as felt life

Lines 51-62 give the occasion, the author's humility and credentials, and the absence of approbations. They are mostly *none*. Line 58 is doctrine. All understanding of the unity rests on faith above reason, "wonder", yet these are deep understandings "as darkness to light" beside human reason: > כי כל השכלות האלו הנאמרים ביחודו יתברך כולם הם על פי האמונה שהוא למעלה משכל האנושי (line 58)

Line 59 names the foundation. The whole work is built on the Alter Rebbe's Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah. Line 63 states its aim: > כי כל עיקר הבנת שרשי היחוד הוא בשביל יחוד העבודה (line 63)

Line 66 is the simplest statement of the unity in the range, promised even to the reader who follows little: > שאין זולתו יתברך והוא נמצא בכל העולמות ומתייחד בהם באופן דלית אתר פנוי מיניה (line 66)

Line 68, the "first great warning", defines knowledge. It counts as knowledge only when felt as life: > כי עיקר שם הידיעה היא כשנתפסת ונרגשת בנפשו בבחינת חיות ממש (line 68)

And the unity is above understanding. It is felt in the divine soul of every Jew, whose root is above wisdom (F06): > כי בחינת יחוד הוא למעלה מן הבנה כי אם בבחינת תפיסת והרגשת יחודו יתברך בנפשו האלק"ית של כל אחד ואחד מישראל (line 68)

The "second warning" (lines 69-72) gives F10 its place. Torah is bread. Outwardly, the Godliness felt in contemplation is higher. In truth Torah and mitzvot are higher, because contemplation reaches only each person's measure, while Torah is His will itself: > אבל בתורה ומצות בזה הוא גילוי רצונו יתברך ממש ואיהו ורצונו חד ואורייתא וקודשא בריך הוא כולא חד ממש (line 71)

But bread feeds only the living (line 72). So Shema and prayer, the contemplation of the unity, come before study.

## Introduction 1-19 (lines 75-93): the anthology on Ein Sof

Every excerpt is F05, and the author's own point is that "all prophesy in one style" (line 97, outside the range). Nothing may be pictured before creation (line 76): > ומאן דאשתמודע ליה קדם בריאה דאיהו לבר מדיוקנא אסור למעבד ליה צורה ודיוקנא (line 76)

The same excerpt holds F03: He takes the form so that He can be known, "if His light did not spread over all creatures, how would they know Him" (line 76). The Raya Mehemna gives F02's two views of one reality (line 77): from the Cause, "you saw no image"; from the creatures, "the image of Havayah". All His names are appellations for creatures' sake (line 78).

The Raavad and Elimah excerpts bear directly on the SPINE's first precision, that the unity is 'yachid' and not 'echad': > דע כי האין כתר עליון לא יתכן עליו מנין לא מנין אחדות ולא מנין ריבוי (line 87) > כי שם מספר לא יפול אלא על דבר שהוא ישות ולא על דבר שהוא אין אלא שהוא השואת הכל (line 88) > שבאין סוף לא יצדק בו לומר לשון אחד כי אם על צד השלילה (line 92)

The Raavad also gives F09 in the form of a substrate. The opposite qualities of the elements are borne by Chochmah, called "mah", which comes from ayin (line 86). He poses the philosophers' dilemma (line 85), the same one as line 13. Pardes gives the sefirot hidden in Ein Sof "united in a strong unity", like fire in flint (line 89). Elimah says all is from "a new will, freely given" (line 90), that the sefirot are not part of His essence (line 93), and R. Azriel says that in Ein Sof there is no will or desire: > דע כי אין סוף אין לומר בו לא רצון ולא חפץ לא כוונה ולא מחשבה (line 93)

## Introduction 20-22 (lines 94-96): the Alter Rebbe as the key

Line 94 tells the reader to read all this against Shaar HaYichud. Sunlight in its source is not "something". Havayah creates from nothing through chesed, and gevurah hides so that the finite is not nullified: > הן הן גבורותיו אשר כל יכול לצמצם החיות ולהסתיר שלא יבוטל הבעל גבול הנברא במציאות (line 94)

Line 95 gives Shaar HaYichud 6, the SPINE's F03 in the Alter Rebbe's words: > אך אין הצמצום אלא לגבי התחתונים (line 95)

Line 96 summarizes Shaar HaYichud 7-9. Creatures add nothing to Him. That He and His middot are one is to be believed ("the secret of faith"). The Rambam's "knower and known" applies from Atzilut down, not above it.

## What this book adds that the Tanya does not say, or says differently

- **Kabbalah is defined by the unity.** The Tanya does not frame the whole of Kabbalah this way. Here, line 3 makes knowledge of the unity the reason Kabbalah exists. The Zohar's praise of "knowing Him" and its warnings against revealing secrets are then settled by dividing knowing into two kinds (lines 9, 15, 16). The first is a duty on every Jew; the second is for the few.
- **The Tanya's "we have no business with hidden things" is re-aimed.** In Tanya 39 (line 575 of the Tanya file) it sets aside a model, and in SHVE 9 (line 861 there) it closes off the union of Essence and attributes. Here it closes off only the second knowing, the Names and kavanot (line 16). The first knowing stays a duty, "each by his measure" (15.8).
- **The Holy One and the Shechinah are named as Essence and revelation, "two opposites"** (15.7). This makes the goal of service (uniting them) F09 and F05 at once. The Tanya reports in this index do not record this definition.
- **Atzilut is "the power of the unity"** (line 44), the power by which He is one in the worlds He sustains.
- **"All is His Essence."** Line 13 says one who sees truly sees "that all is His Essence", and line 14 says "precisely His Essence is drawn into the worlds and yet unchanged". The Alter Rebbe says the unity is present in the worlds, but he does not put it in this language of seeing.
- **A rule set for parables.** The Tanya marks where an analogy fails, case by case (G06). This book makes three standing conditions and names the one point where the man-parable holds: one man in many powers (line 48).
- **Knowledge is defined as felt life** (line 68), and the unity as above understanding but grasped in every Jew's divine soul. This is close to Tanya 42's da'at as binding, but it adds the soul's root above wisdom as the organ of that grasp.
- **The last generations need more explanation, not less** (lines 20-22). The Alter Rebbe's parable of the treasuries (line 18) is quoted only here.
- **The pre-Lurianic negative theology** (Raavad, Elimah, R. Azriel) is brought in as the frame for the Alter Rebbe. It gives the yachid precision in older words: not even "one" may be said of Ein Sof except by negation (line 92).

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- Fifteen lines are *none*: the apology (1), praise and lament (26, 28), the Talmudic pilpul note (31), the history of the writings and warnings to readers (51-55), biography and approbations (59-62), and reading advice (65, 67). Sub-entry 50.5 is also none. - Two ties are *reading*: line 17 and sub-entry 20.2, which join the revealing in the last generations to F11. The text makes the revealing the knowledge of the unity (line 3). It does not itself call the lowest generation the place where the unity shows most. - The praise lines 25 and 27 and the practical lines 73-74 are structural with F12. They name the unity only as the purpose of the teaching. - The kabbalistic mappings (10.3 Yesod as the ladder, 39-40 the "foxes") and the proofs of line 36-38 carry no face. - Line 8's "you who cleave to Havayah" is not tagged F06. The text uses it only to show that the verses are said of all Israel. - Line 70's point that bread's hidden root is higher than the soul it feeds is the "highest in the lowest" shape (G05). It is kept structural; line 71 carries the tie. - F07 (the home below) has no entry in this range.

## Disagreements kept, not smoothed

- **How much can be known (F12).** The author says the first knowing is a duty for every Jew, and that today's souls need long, detailed explanation (lines 15, 22). Yet in the same range he says the understandings "all rest on faith above human reason" (line 58), and that "the whole purpose of knowing here is that we do not know" (line 50). In line 96 he cites SHVE 9's "to be believed" for the union of Essence and attributes. The Alter Rebbe in Iggeret HaKodesh 25 (Tanya line 1411-1413 in this index) holds the unity as "the revealed things, for us and our children", believed "without investigating with human reason", and says investigation is a newcomer's matter. The author's program of detailed explanation for the "heel" generations pulls the other way. He places himself inside his teacher's line, and the tension is left as it stands.
- **Intellectual against natural love.** The gloss in line 15 cites the Alter Rebbe to grant that faith-based natural love is "whole service". It then ranks it below love from understanding, "as the speaking excels the animal". The Tanya's hidden love of every Jew is not ranked that way in the Tanya ranges read so far. The emphasis here is the author's own.
- **Will and desire in Ein Sof.** The author quotes R. Azriel with approval: no will, desire, intention or thought may be said of Ein Sof (line 93). Elimah says even "the will of wills" is new, not eternal (line 92). This sits against the Tanya's "supernal will" at one with Him (F10, "He and His will are one", which the author himself uses in line 71). It also sits against F07's "the Holy One desired a dwelling below". The range does not reconcile them. Presumably the book will: "will" belongs from Atzilut down, as line 96's gloss does for "knower and known". That would be our reading, not the text's.
- **"Not part of His essence."** Elimah forbids calling the sefirot "part of His essence" (line 93). The Tanya calls the soul "a part of God above, truly" (F06). The two statements concern different things, sefirot and souls, so this is not a contradiction. It is recorded so that later ranges can see whether the book reads F06 through this warning.
- **The known dispute with the Mitteler Rebbe** over felt arousal (hitpa'alut) in prayer is not argued in this range. Line 68 ("it is not called knowledge at all" without the grasp and arousal of the middot) is where the author's side shows. The dispute itself is outside these lines and was not verified here.
