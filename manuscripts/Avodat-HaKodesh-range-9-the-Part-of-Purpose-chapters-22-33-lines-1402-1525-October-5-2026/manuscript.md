## The shape of the range

1. **The Torah comes from the upper Thought, and so does its explanation** (1402-1410). The tablets were cut from "the place of full freedom".
2. **The voice that never stopped** (1411-1426). If reasoning is forbidden, how can the sages argue and rule? Because the source still flows, and every true teaching was received at Sinai from "a great voice that did not stop". "These and these are the words of the living G-d."
3. **The Torah shows herself only to those who love her** (1427-1436). The Zohar's maiden in the palace.
4. **The image of G-d** (1437-1456). Man is built in layers like the worlds above, each a garment for the one inside. The words "hand" and "eye" of G-d are signs, not likenesses. Purifying a limb makes it a throne for its root above.
5. **Descent and ascent; Onkelos' careful words** (1457-1499). The Shechinah is not a created light (against the Rambam). "Blessed is the Glory of the Lord from His place": His place is Himself.
6. **"Show me Your glory"** (1500-1525). Against the Rambam's reading. Abraham already knew G-d's ways by walking in them.

## The unity of G-d

**The source never stops, or the world would stop.** This is the heart of the range.

> כי החכמה העליונה העדן העליון המקור האמתי, שממנו נאצלו ונחקקו תורה שבכתב ותורה שבעל פה, אינו פוסק אבל הא נובע ובא תמיד, ואלו היה פוסק אפילו רגע היו חוזרים כל הנבראים להעדרם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:23:9; line 1419)
> For the upper Wisdom, the upper Eden, the true source from which the Written and Oral Torah were drawn, does not stop, but flows and comes always; and if it stopped even a moment, all creatures would return to nothing.

That is why the Torah itself never stops growing. "A great voice, and it did not add" (Deut. 5:19) is read by Onkelos as "a great voice, and it did not cease": "for it calls forever in its eternity" (כי לעולם קורא בנצחיותו, line 1419). The sages in every generation are "like trumpets to the mouth of the one who blows" (line 1419): nothing new from their own minds, only what they received at Sinai brought from potential to act. And:

> ורצה אדון העולם שיצא לפועל על יד התחתונים המתקנים ועושים השם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:23:12; line 1422)
> And the Master of the world wished that it come into act by the hands of the lower ones, who repair and "make" the Name.

The Aramaic translation of the Song of Songs describes the Holy One who "renews new teachings every day" (מחדת שמעוון חדתין בכל יומא ויומא, line 1424).

*Ours.* This is F01 (the world is being said into being every moment) in the language of Torah: Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 1-2 teaches that if the word stopped for a moment, the world would return to nothing, and the Baal Shem Tov's teaching that "the Torah is given every day". Ibn Gabbai says both in one breath: the same unstopping flow keeps the world in being and keeps the Torah new. The guide can offer a state: **the voice that did not stop**. Sinai is not only past; it is calling now.

**"These and these": one Giver, many receivers.**

> כפי מקום עמידתו וקבולו, והכל בא ממקום אחד והולך אל מקום אחד (Avodat HaKodesh 3:23:14; line 1424)
> [Each one received his own] according to the place where he stood and received, and all comes from one place and goes to one place.

The source has front and back, the root of every pair of opposites (pure and impure, forbidden and permitted). The great voice included them all, and each sage received his side. So:

> והכל אחד מצד הנותן ברוך הוא, אבל מצדנו הם דברים שונים (Avodat HaKodesh 3:23:16; line 1426)
> And all is one from the side of the Giver, blessed be He; but from our side they are different things.

*Ours.* This is the Chabad reading of eilu ve'eilu as well: both views are rooted above, in different sefirot (kindness and strength), and the law follows one only because below we can act on one (a common Chassidic reading, which roots the two schools in kindness and strength). It is also a teaching for inner life: many true views, one Giver. The guide can use it to calm the fear that disagreement means one side is cut off from G-d.

> שהתורה צורת השם הגדול ממש (Avodat HaKodesh 3:24:7; line 1433)
> That the Torah is the very form of the Great Name.

The Name is hidden and revealed; so is the Torah (lines 1431-1432). One who says the Torah has only its plain sense "shows a stop in the source that flows always" (line 1430).

**His place is Himself.** "Blessed is the Glory of the Lord from His place": ibn Gabbai reads "from His place" as "from His own self":

> הכוונה האמיתית והסוד הנעלם באמרו ממקומו כאלו אמר מעצמו כי אין מקומו דבר זולתו (Avodat HaKodesh 3:32:8; line 1497)
> The true meaning and hidden secret of "from His place" is as if it said "from Himself", for His place is nothing other than Him.

And: "the end is fixed in the beginning and the beginning in the end ... to show that all is one unified unity" (line 1497). "Nothing goes out of the true unity" (line 1499).

*Ours.* Chassidus says the same of G-d and place: "He is the place of the world, and the world is not His place" (Bereishit Rabbah 68, used across Chabad teaching). Ibn Gabbai's reading goes further, in the direction of Chabad's "there is nothing but Him" (ein od milvado, Shaar HaYichud ch. 6): not only does the world sit in Him, His "place" is not other than He.

**The Shechinah is not a created light.** The Rambam said the Glory that filled the Tabernacle was a created light. Ibn Gabbai: then those who served in the Temple and blessed "Blessed is the Glory" would be serving a creature (line 1477). "The dwelling of the Shechinah among us is the condition of His being our G-d; how could He make it depend on a created light?" (line 1476). *Ours.* This is a principle Chabad holds firmly: the Shechinah is G-d Himself as He is present (Tanya ch. 41, 52). The guide should never describe the Presence as a thing other than Him.

**Descent is unity.** When the Torah says G-d "went down", it means "the Lord goes down to the Lord", the uniting of the lights with each other (שיורד יי' ליי', line 1457). "None of these things is said of anything outside His own self and truth, and there is no body in any of it" (line 1460).

## The Shechinah with us

"The main dwelling of the Shechinah was in the lower worlds, for when she is in the lower ones the worlds are balanced" (line 1492). "In every place they were exiled the Shechinah is with them, for the mother cannot leave her children among their enemies, but goes into exile with them to watch over them, guard them and shield them" (line 1492). The Sifre: "Like a king who told his servant: if you look for me, I am with my son" (line 1494). "'Its inside was paved with love': this is the Shechinah" (line 1499).

*Ours.* The guide can use the Sifre's line directly with a person who feels far: *if you look for Him, He is with His children*, which is to say, with you.

## The Torah and her lovers

The Zohar's parable (line 1434): a beautiful maiden hidden in a palace; her one lover walks past her gate every day; she opens a small window, shows her face for a moment, and hides again, and only he sees. So the Torah. Ibn Gabbai's reading:

> פעם תגלה אליהם פתאום ומיד תתכסה ותתעלם מהם רצוא ושוב כמראה הבזק (Avodat HaKodesh 3:24:7; line 1433)
> At times she shows herself to them suddenly and at once covers herself and hides from them, running and returning like a flash of lightning.

She speaks first from behind a thick curtain (plain sense and simple teaching), then a thin veil (aggadah, riddles), "until she sets their heart on fire", and then brings them inside and speaks face to face (line 1433). And:

> למדנו שצריך האדם להתעורר מעצמו, ולהיות מאוהבי התורה וחושקיה ולשקוד על דלתותיה יום יום (Avodat HaKodesh 3:24:9; line 1435)
> We learn that a person must wake himself, and be among the lovers and yearners of the Torah, and wait at her doors day by day.

*Ours.* "Running and returning" (ratzo vashov) is Ezekiel's word for the living creatures, and it is the Chabad name for the soul's two moves, yearning up and returning to the task (Tanya ch. 50; Likkutei Torah). Here it is the Torah that runs and returns, and the lover's heart is set on fire by it. A state for the guide: **at her door**. The daily, patient waiting at the Torah's gate, with glimpses given and taken.

## The image of G-d and the body

**Layers, each a garment for the one inside.** "Let us make man in our image": not the Rambam's "intellect", but a structure like the worlds above, "one inside another, one a garment for the other, the brain inside and the shell outside" (line 1442). The Zohar: "all is brain inside and many shells for the brain, and the whole world is like this, above and below" (line 1441). The real person is the inner spirit; "the flesh is the garment of the man" (line 1446). Nefesh, ruach and neshamah are layers too, each a body for the one within (line 1446). From the Zohar Chadash: G-d placed His own Name in man; the Yud is "the soul of the soul" (line 1447).

> ואלו יזכה האדם לטהר אבר מאיבריו, יהיה אותו אבר כדמיון כסא לאותו דבר העליון הפנימי הנקרא בשם זה (Avodat HaKodesh 3:26:3; line 1452)
> If a person merits to purify one of his limbs, that limb will be like a throne for that upper inner thing called by that name.

Man was built in the pattern of the Chariot because his task, repairing the Glory, can only be done by Torah and mitzvot, "and these are only completed by physical limbs" (line 1452). And the Idra: "All is one; He was, is and will be; He did not change, does not change, will not change" (line 1453). "So the upper likeness depends on the lower one, and needs its repair" (line 1453).

*Ours.* This is the Tanya's "the limbs become a chariot" (ch. 23, 34): a hand giving charity, a mouth speaking Torah, becomes a vessel for the divine will. The guide can use it as a practice: **a limb made a throne**. One limb, one act, done wholly for Him.

## Knowing His ways by walking in them

Against the Rambam's reading of "Show me Your ways" (that Moses did not yet know G-d's attributes of action), ibn Gabbai points to Abraham, who knew those ways without asking, because he lived them: "the merciful", bringing people under the wings of the Shechinah; "the gracious", his bread open to all; "slow to anger", "let there be no quarrel" with Lot (lines 1517-1519). "He knew the Lord after he knew His ways and walked in them" (line 1520). And Moses, before any prophecy, showed mercy to the beaten Hebrew and to Jethro's daughters (line 1520).

*Ours.* This is a quiet but strong teaching: one knows G-d's ways by doing them, not by studying them first. It matches "walking in His ways" (range 7).

**The best proof.** "The truth for me is that the prophets and sages did not confirm His existence by proofs of reason, for they could have refuted them by their wisdom, but by the proof of prophecy, which is better beyond measure; and for the rest of the people by miracles" (line 1511). *Ours.* The Kuzari's point again: knowing by contact over knowing by argument.

## The big human questions

**Is revelation over?** No. The voice from Sinai "calls forever" (line 1419).

**Why do wise people disagree?** Each stands at a different place before one source (line 1424).

**Is my body holy or in the way?** It is the garment of the real person; a purified limb becomes a throne (lines 1446, 1452).

**Where is G-d when I am far?** "If you look for Me, I am with My son" (line 1494).
