This is the last part of Netivot Olam. It opens with the longest path after Torah, the Path of Return (teshuva). Then come fourteen shorter paths: suffering, eagerness, rebuke, shame, wholeness, the good heart, the good eye, joy and scoffing, contentment, trust, anger, generosity, a good name and decent conduct.

The main idea of the range is this. Everything that exists returns to G-d, because nothing stands by itself. A person who does teshuva is only doing on purpose what the whole world does by nature. He goes back to his beginning. And the way back is by becoming *simple* (pashut): broken, without a claim, without an edge.

## 2.1 Nothing stands by itself, so everything returns

This is the center of the whole range. G-d receives those who return because of what beings are:

- "כי אין להם קיום בעצמם" (950, Netiv HaTeshuva 1:20): "for they have no standing in themselves." - "ואין לו קיום מצד עצמו, רק שהוא שב אל השם יתברך" (970, 2:20): "it [the world] has no standing from itself, only in that it returns to G-d." - "ואליו שבים כל הנמצאים" (970): "and to Him all beings return."

He says this is "the order of the world". Things do not exist and then, as an extra, turn to G-d. Their turning to Him *is* their existing. So a person who does teshuva is not doing something odd. He is lining up with how everything is. That is why sins can even become merits (966-971). The farther away a person was, the harder he pulls to come back, like fire that is far below and rushes up. The distance itself turns into force of return.

## 2.2 The Beginning and the one who returns

- "וכאשר הוא שב אל השם יתברך, אז האדם חוזר אל התחלתו" (959, 2:9): "when he returns to G-d, the person goes back to his beginning." - "כי הכל ראוי לשוב אל דבר שהוא התחלה לו" (1005, 4:19): "for everything is fit to return to what is its beginning."

G-d is the Beginning of all. A person was made at first without sin. Return takes him back to that first state, so the sin falls away. When people return, the world goes back to its beginning too, and that is why return brings healing (959).

## 2.3 Simplicity reaches the Throne

- "כי השבור הוא הפשיטות, כאשר אין לו שם 'כלי' כלל" (944, 1:14): "for the broken is simplicity, when it no longer has the name 'vessel' at all." - "והוא אומר בכלי שבור אני משתמש" (946, 1:16): "and He says: I use a broken vessel." - "כי התשובה הזאת היא הפשיטות הגמור" (964, 2:14): "for this returning is complete simplicity."

A whole vessel has a shape, a name, an edge. A broken one has lost that. The Maharal says G-d uses broken vessels, because a broken spirit is simple, and the simple is closest to the One who is simple. So return reaches up to the Throne of Glory. When a whole community returns, it reaches G-d Himself (964).

He says the same thing about sin in color terms (1054). Sin is red, a stain. A mitzvah is white, which is no color at all. A person who makes himself small, "בעבור שהוא משים עצמו כאילו לא היה כלל" (1054, 7:2): "because he sets himself as if he did not exist at all", becomes wholly simple, and in the simple there is no sin.

## 2.4 Confession makes the confessors one, as He is One

- "כמו שהוא כל הודאה שמוסר עצמו אל מי שחטא לו" (1013, 5:7): "as with every confession, that he hands himself over to the one he sinned against." - "כך בעלי הודאה מתאחדים גם כן" (1014, 5:8): "so those who confess become one too." The full line says: "as He is One, so those who confess become one too."

The Hebrew word hodaah means both "confessing" and "thanking". Both are handing yourself over. Once you have handed yourself over, the pull toward sin has nothing to hold, "since the evil pull has no joining to G-d" (1013). Confession is also why the dying say viduy. They hand their soul over, and He receives it (1016).

## 2.5 Nothing is owed

"כי אין לבריות על בוראם יתברך רק חסד ורחמים, ואין כאן חוב" (1033, 6:5): "for creatures have nothing from their Creator but kindness and mercy; nothing here is owed." This is why the 13 attributes begin "Hashem, Hashem", mercy before sin and mercy after. Even a person who never sinned lives on mercy. Being is a gift, not a wage.

## 2.6 He is One and all is in His hand

The trust path ends on unity. Nachum Ish Gam Zu said of every trouble "gam zu letovah", "this too is for good". Why can the worst become the best? "וכל זה מפני כי הוא ית' אחד ובידו הכל" (1217, Netiv HaBitachon 1:7): "all this is because He is One and all is in His hand." The words gam zu add up to 13, which is also the number of echad (One). If there were two powers, bad could stay bad. Since there is One, bad is only a hidden form of good.

## 2.7 Being like Him by having no edge

"Three G-d loves: one who does not get angry, one who does not insist on his traits (letting things go), and one who does not get drunk" (1222). The reason: "כי באלו המדות מתדמה אל בורא הכל והדומה יאהב את הדומה" (1222, Netiv HaKaas 1:3): "by these traits he becomes like the Creator of all, and like loves like."

The one who lets things go "אין לו גדר והוא פשוט" (1222): "he has no fence and he is simple." G-d has no edge. A person who does not stand on his rights also has no edge, and so he is like Him. Anger is the opposite: "כי הכעס הוא התפעלות האדם" (1220): "for anger is a person being acted upon." G-d is never acted upon.

## 2.8 The name is the self

"כי השם מורה על עצמו של דבר" (1235, Netiv Shem Tov 1:1): "for the name points to the very self of a thing." Wisdom, strength and wealth are "oil" poured into a person. His name is himself. This fits the Maharal's picture of G-d's Name: it is not one of His qualities but His own being as known in the world (984).

**Proposed MH-S13 "Back to the beginning".** Inside: I am going home, not climbing somewhere new. Under the sin there is the first me, made clean, and the way back is open because everything goes back to its Beginning. How to enter: name the wrong plainly, then turn the mind to G-d as the Beginning of you, and rest in the thought "I am going back to where I started". Source: Netivot Olam 959, 970, 1005. This is the state that goes with the technique `mh_back_to_beginning` already proposed from Gevurot Hashem.

**Proposed MH-S14 "Content in oneself".** Inside: I have what I need. I am not a pit waiting to be filled but a spring. Nothing is missing, so nothing is pulling. The Maharal calls this the very trait of the world to come: "כי אין עה"ב רק שאין מקבל מזולתו" (1194, Netiv HaOsher 1:3): "for the world to come is only this: not receiving from another." And "והמסתפק בעצמו אין מקבל מזולתו רק עומד בעצמו" (1194): "the one content in himself does not receive from another; he stands in himself." How to enter: take stock of what you have now. Enjoy it as a gift from the Giver, which brings love of Him (1194). Notice where you feel like a pit, and let it go. Source: 1193-1197.

A note (ours): "stands in himself" does not clash with 2.1, where nothing stands by itself. The content one does not lean on *other creatures*. He still stands on G-d. That is why his joy in what he has turns into love of the Giver.

## 4.1 The broken vessel (`mh_remnant`, `quieting`)

"האדם עצמו נחשב כמו מקדש" (943, 1:13): "the person himself counts as a Temple." A broken spirit is worth all offerings. What is a flaw in an animal offering (a break) is the very thing that makes a person fit. Practice: when you feel broken, do not try to look whole. Say plainly, "I am a broken vessel, and You use broken vessels" (946). This belongs with the technique `mh_remnant` (make yourself like what is left over) proposed in the earlier report.

## 4.2 Confession that hands you over (`own_words`; new: `mh_hand_over_in_confession`)

The Maharal says confession is the main part of return (1016), because in it "מוסר עצמו אליו", "he hands himself over to Him". How: say the wrong in your own words, speaking to G-d, "I did this, to You." Stop there. Do not argue or explain. Feel the self go into His hands. Then say the 13 attributes from "Hashem, Hashem" (the Maharal's own practice, 1052). Source: 1013-1016, 1052.

## 4.3 "It depends only on me" (`cheshbon`)

Elazar ben Durdaya asked the mountains, the heavens, the sun and moon, the stars to plead for him. Each one said it was passing too. Then: "אין הדבר תלוי אלא בי" (1092, 8:26): "the matter depends only on me." The Maharal explains that he was looking for a place to stand through others, and nothing that passes can hold you (1092-1101). Practice (ours): list what you are waiting on to change. Cross each one out with "this passes too". Then turn to G-d alone.

This passage ends with death. The guide must not use that part. Use only the turn from others to oneself before G-d.

## 4.4 No self-torment

The Maharal is clear that fasting is holiness, not pain: "(שאין) השם יתברך רוצה שיהיה האדם מצער נפשו" (1056, 7:4): "there is no such thing that G-d wants a person to torment himself." One who cannot fast should instead "see yourself as if a holy one is in your innards" and not follow every appetite (1060). This is the Maharal's own guard on harsh practice, and the guide should keep it.

## 4.5 Trust at the moment of action (new: `mh_trust_at_the_act`)

"בטח אל ה' בכל לבבך ואל בינתך אל תשען" (Proverbs 3:5). The Maharal reads it this way. Do everything with wisdom and effort. But at the moment you act, set your thought on G-d that He be with you: "ודבר זה הוא כמו תפלה אל הש"י" (1211, Netiv HaBitachon 1:1): "and this is like a prayer to G-d." How: before a hard task, stop for one breath and say inside, "You are with me in this." Then do the work fully. Source: 1211.

He adds that fear itself is a snare. The fearful one "והוא כמו כלי רעוע" (1212): "is like a cracked vessel", and a cracked vessel breaks easily. Trust is strength, not only comfort.

## 4.6 Gam zu (new: `mh_gam_zu`)

How: when something bad happens, say "gam zu letovah", "this too is for good", and hold the reason in mind: He is One and all is in His hand (1217). Do not pretend the bad is not bad. Say that the One can make it good. Source: 1215-1217.

## 4.7 Be still (`quieting`, `mh_silent_trust`)

Against his enemies, Mar Ukva was told "דום לה' והתחולל לו" (1219, 1:9): "be still before the L-rd and wait for Him." Go early and late to the house of study, and they will fall by themselves. "הוא מוסר עצמו אל השם ית'" (1219): "he hands himself over to G-d," and then his war is G-d's war. This matches `mh_silent_trust` from Gevurot Hashem.

## 4.8 Forgive everyone before sleep (new: `mh_forgive_before_sleep`)

Mar Zutra said each night when he went to bed: "שרי ליה לכל מאן דצערן" (1231, Netiv HaNedivut 1:3): "I forgive everyone who hurt me." The Maharal puts this under letting go, which makes a person like G-d who has no edge (1222). How: in bed, say these words, and name anyone who comes to mind. Source: 1231, 1222.

## 4.9 Joy tied to the Master (`heart_after`)

When a sage looked "too joyful", he said "תפילין מנחנא" (1183): "I am wearing tefillin." The Maharal: "כי אין השמחה רק מפני שהש"י אדון עלי" (1183): "for the joy is only because G-d is master over me." Joy that comes from "I am whole" is risky, since no one is whole here (1182). Joy that comes from "He is my Master" is safe, because in it I know my Cause.

## 4.10 The four animals (`act`)

"Bold as a leopard, light as an eagle, swift as a deer, mighty as a lion" (1125) are four steps against a heavy body: wake up, get up, move, do it in full. This is a plain ladder for anyone stuck in the morning or before a mitzvah.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why can I change?** "והחרטה שייכת לאדם במה שאין כל מעשיו שכליים" (933, 1:3): "regret belongs to a person because not all his deeds are of the mind." A person is not pure mind. That is why he falls, and it is also why he can turn. Torah, which is pure mind, itself calls him back.

**Can the worst be undone?** Yes. Sins done from far away can become merits, because the distance becomes the force of return (966-971). The broken are the vessels G-d uses (946).

**Why do I suffer?** First look at your deeds. If you find nothing, see if you neglected learning. If you find nothing, it is suffering of love: G-d wants you close and the body is in the way (1105). Small troubles count too: a shirt put on inside out, reaching for three coins and getting two (1113). They show "שאין האדם בעל השלמה עד עולם הבא" (1113): "a person is not complete until the world to come." The Maharal does not say suffering is good in itself. He says that when it comes, "צרה ויגון אמצא ובשם ה' אקרא" (1114): "trouble and sorrow I find, and I call on the Name of G-d."

**How should I live with others?** "שהם כמו אדם אחד כי הם עם א'" (1144, Netiv HaTochacha 2:5): "for they are like one person, since they are one people." A wound in one limb, all feel it. That is why each one is responsible for the others. Rebuke must be wise and gentle, or it is better to be silent (1146).

**What is it to be whole?** "כשאתה תם אתה עם ה' אלקיך" (1168, Netiv HaTmimut 2:3): "when you are whole, you are with the L-rd your G-d." The whole person does not scheme: "אבל הערמה והתחבולה הוא לאדם מצד עצמו, ובשביל כך אינו עם הש"י" (1165, 1:3): "but cunning and scheming come from the person himself, and so he is not with G-d." Such a person "אינו ברשות עצמו רק הוא עם הש"י" (1165): "is not in his own domain; he is with G-d."

**Can I be happy in this world?** Yes, but not full-mouthed laughter. "וכל שנוי הוא רע" (1184): "and every change is bad." This world is under time, and time is change. Full laughter waits for "then" (az), a point beyond time. Meanwhile there is the tefillin joy (4.9).

**What does money do to me?** "כל מקבל חסר וכל חסר דבק בו ההעדר" (1197, Netiv HaOsher 1:6): "every receiver lacks, and lack clings to every lacking thing." The spring is "living water" because it flows from itself. The eye takes in all the gold in the world and is never full, until the dust of the grave covers it (1207).

**Where do I rest?** "והתורה היא מקום קביעתו ומנוחתו" (1021, 5:15): "and the Torah is his fixed place and his rest." A person is always changing, like a bird that wanders from its nest. Torah is the nest.

**Is decency part of holiness?** "אם אין דרך ארץ אין תורה" (1248, Netiv Derech Eretz 1:8): "if there is no decent conduct there is no Torah." Decency came before Torah (1248).

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **Teshuva as return to the source.** The Tanya's Iggeret HaTeshuva (ch. 4 and on) reads teshuva as "tashuv hei", returning the letter hei to its place, and the soul to its root. The Maharal reads it as returning to one's *beginning*, and G-d as the Beginning of all. Same shape. The Maharal also uses the letter hei (987-990): it is open below so anyone can leave, and has a window above so one can come back in. The Chabad reading and this one sit well together.
- **Nothing stands by itself** (950, 970) is close to the Gate of Unity (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 2), where the created is always being made anew from nothing and would fall back to nothing without the word of G-d. Where the Tanya says "made anew every moment", the Maharal says "always returning". The Tanya puts the weight on the flow down from G-d. The Maharal puts it on the pull back up to G-d. For the guide, both can be one move: held from above, turning back to Him.
- **Sins becoming merits** (966-971) matches Tanya ch. 7 on teshuva from love. The Maharal adds a reason the Tanya does not spell out: the distance becomes the force of return, like fire rushing up.
- **The broken vessel** matches Tanya ch. 29-31 on a broken heart, though the Tanya warns the broken heart must be at set times and must end in joy. The Maharal's "simplicity" is a different frame: brokenness is valued because it removes the edge of the self, not because it hurts.
- **Tzimtzum** does not come up. The Maharal needs no contraction story. For him things depend on G-d because they have no standing in themselves.
- **No drink.** The Kaas path joins anger and drunkenness as the two things that break the bond with G-d (1225). This fits our rule: never drink.
