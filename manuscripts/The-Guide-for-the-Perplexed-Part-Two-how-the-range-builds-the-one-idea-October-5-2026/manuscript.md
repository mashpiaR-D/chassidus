## The shape of the range

Part Two has three subjects, and one thread runs through them: G-d acts by will, not by necessity, and He acts *through* things.

1. **G-d proved (II:1-2).** Twenty-five premises from Aristotle, one more granted for the sake of argument (that motion is eternal), and four proofs: the first mover, the necessary being, the one who brings potential into act, and the argument from the world's being one. Whatever the world's origin, "the existence of G-d, His unity and His not being a body" follow.
2. **The intelligences and creation (II:3-31).** Angels are the separate intellects that move the spheres; "all the forces are angels" (line 678). G-d's action is called *shefa*, "overflow", like a spring that flows on every side. Then the long defense of creation from nothing: Aristotle never proved eternity; the Torah's view is possible and has fewer difficulties; the world was made by intention and need not end. Shabbat is given so that this belief should live in deeds.
3. **Prophecy (II:32-48).** Prophecy is natural perfection that G-d may withhold. It is an overflow from G-d through the Active Intellect to the rational power and then to the imagination. It needs three perfections: mind, imagination and character. The chapters end with eleven degrees of prophecy and a key teaching: Scripture attributes every event to G-d, whether it came by nature, human choice, animal will or chance.

## 1. The unity of G-d in this range

**The proof holds either way.** The Rambam wants unity on a floor no one can shake. Existence, unity and incorporeality are proved "whether the world is created after nonexistence or not; it is all the same" (line 658). Two necessary beings would share necessity and differ in something else, so each would be composite (line 651, line 654). And since the world is one individual bound together, its maker is one (line 655).

**Only He, before anything.** The Torah's view: "G-d alone existed and nothing besides Him, not an angel, not a sphere, nor what is within the sphere" (line 706, 2:13:2), and time too was created. This belief is "the foundation of the Torah of Moses, second to the foundation of unity" (line 710).

> ושהאלוה ית׳ לבדו היה נמצא ולא דבר בלעדיו (line 706)

**Overflow, not touch.** Bodies act by contact; what is not a body acts by *shefa*, "by likeness to a spring of water that flows on every side and has no special side" (line 703, 2:12:4). "With You is the source of life... in Your light we see light": by the overflow of intellect from You, we understand (line 704). Here the Rambam supplies the image Chassidus will take for the whole of creation (the light from the luminary), but for him it names the *mode* of G-d's action, not the nothingness of the world.

**Nature is His will at work.** Aristotle's god could not "lengthen a fly's wing or shorten a worm's leg" (line 800). The Rambam's G-d made everything by intention: "there is no doubt that each thing among them is necessary according to the intention of the One who intended" (line 780). Miracles do not overturn nature; they were written into it at creation, "the world goes on in its way" (line 860).

**Everything is His act, through its causes.** II:48 is the Rambam's most practical unity teaching. Every event has near causes, natural, chosen or random, and "all this is attributed to G-d in the books of the prophets" (line 1010). Joseph: "it was not you who sent me here, but G-d" (line 1012). David of Shimi: "for G-d told him, curse David" (line 1012). "Whether the causes are essential, or accidental, or by choice and will, all alike" are spoken of as His command, His word, His sending (line 1014):

> לא אתם שלחתם אותי הנה כי האלהים (line 1012)

## 2. States of consciousness in this range

- **Longing that moves.** A picture in the mind does not move a person; one moves "only when a longing arises for the thing pictured" (line 664, 2:4:1). The sphere moves forever because it longs for the beloved, G-d, "longing to become like what it grasped". *Inside:* a mind that has seen something and a heart that now wants it. *Ties to:* C3 (From mind to heart to hand), S25 (The line takes hold), S13 (The unnamed longing). The Rambam's version is cosmological, but its psychology is the same as Chassidus's hisbonenus: understanding alone does not move; desire must be born.
- **The mind darkened and brightened.** Of prophetic hyperbole: the defeated man "sees every light as black and finds every sweet thing bitter" (line 842); in grief the eye darkens, and "with joy and the soul's spreading outward... a person sees as if the light were greater" (line 844). *Ties to:* S04 (Low and heavy), S53 (Glad). A plain observation from the Rambam on how a state colors what one sees.
- **Faith and its joy do not leave.** On Isaiah's "new heavens": "faith in G-d and joy in that faith are two things that cannot depart... from anyone who has had them" (line 848, 2:29:10). *Ties to:* S27 (Knowing that holds), S53. A line the guide can carry.
- **Sadness closes the door.** "Prophecy does not rest amid sadness or laziness" (line 929, the Talmud's line, which the Rambam explains by the imagination being a bodily power). Jacob in mourning received no prophecy. The exile's sadness, being "a slave bought by fools", is why prophecy stopped: "the vessel has been cancelled" (line 930). *Ties to:* S04, S03. This is a direct root of the Tanya's teaching on sadness (atzvus) as the blocker of service.
- **The fire in the bones.** The prophet with more overflow than he needs must speak, even to his harm. Jeremiah: "it was in my heart like a burning fire shut up in my bones, and I was weary of holding it in, and could not" (line 936). "The divine overflow moves them and does not let them rest" (line 936). *Ties to:* S52 (The song that sings itself), S43 (Love like fire). *Proposed new state* **R-S3 "The overflow that must be given"**.
- **Counterfeits of the imagination.** Some people with strong imagination and weak mind "have wondrous imaginings... until they think they are prophets", and "true things are mixed in them with imagined things" (line 933). Old ideas left in the imagination, when all else goes quiet, seem to come from outside, like the one person left in a house after a thousand have gone out, who seems to have just come in (line 942). "This is one of the misleading, killing places" (line 943). *Ties to:* S18 (Chasing the high), S20 (Unreal, spacey or frightened), and technique `discern`. The Rambam supplies a precise account of why meditative "revelations" can be old material.
- **Prophecy comes and goes.** "The prophet does not prophesy all his days in cleaving" (line 978). *Ties to:* C5 (Running and returning).
- **Degree one: moved to a great good deed.** The first degree of prophecy is "that divine help accompanies a person and moves and urges him to a great good deed", to save the oppressed; Moses could not stand by an injustice even as a frightened stranger (line 980). *Ties to:* S81 (The deed in the hand), S54 (Compassion). *Proposed new state* **R-S4 "Moved to rescue"**.
- **Degree two: words that come.** "A person finds as if something came upon him and a new power made him speak", in wisdom or praises, awake: the Psalms were written so (line 981). *Ties to:* S52, S55 (Own words to Him).

## 3. Meditation and practice in this range

- **Contemplate the heavens.** The strongest proof of intention is the heavens: "the prophets took the stars and spheres as proof of G-d... Lift your eyes on high and see who created these" (line 781). "This is the true proof, in which there is no doubt" (line 781). *Ties to:* technique `breadth`; the Chassidic "lift your eyes" contemplations of the heavens (Tanya's Shaar HaYichud begins from the firmament).
- **Preparation for prophecy as a practice for everyone.** The three perfections (line 928): the rational power by study; the imagination by nature; character by giving up bodily pleasures and the craving for rank. The prepared person's "longings are all to know the secrets of existence", "his thought always looks out to noble things", he has stopped wanting bodily pleasures and honor, and "sees nothing but G-d and His angels" (line 927). And what occupies the waking mind is what the imagination works on in sleep (line 926): a simple instruction for what to think about before sleep. *Ties to:* technique `carry` (a line through the day) and `cheshbon`.
- **Guard: the Torah is balanced.** Its laws are "righteous", meaning balanced: no hermit's hardship, no license (line 947). He names "the worship of the one who withdraws to the mountains, separating from meat and wine" as excess. *Guard* for any practice that pushes toward extreme withdrawal.
- **Ideas need deeds.** "Opinions, if they do not have deeds that establish them, publicize them and keep them among the many forever, will not remain" (line 899). This is his reason for Shabbat: it keeps creation believed. *Ties to:* technique `act`.
- **The test of a teacher.** How to know a true prophet from a pretender: the greatest proof is his "leaving bodily pleasures and despising them" (line 955). A plain guard the guide can use for any teacher of meditation.
- **Honesty about limits.** "I have explained and told my confusion in these matters" (line 821), on astronomy; "the heavens are the heavens of G-d, and the earth He gave to people", because the earth "is his world and his house into which he came down, and he is part of it" (line 820). To strain after what the mind has no instrument for is "lack of sense or a kind of madness" (line 820).

## 4. The big questions in this range

- **Providence through the ordinary.** II:48 is the guide's best Rambam line for "being held": the same event can be nature, someone's free choice, or chance, and still be called G-d's sending (lines 1010-1014). Joseph could say to the brothers who sold him "not you, but G-d".
- **Trust.** "We will not fear when the earth changes", because "we lean on His salvation, not on our sword and strength" (line 856). S84 (Trust).
- **Joy.** Faith and the joy of it stay (line 848); sadness closes the higher gifts (line 929).
- **Why teach.** Perfection overflows: "whoever reaches perfection must pour it on others" (line 866); a teacher writes not for himself but because "the nature of this intellect is always to overflow" (line 935). Withholding would be "robbing the truth of those who deserve it" (line 1019).

## 5. Where Chassidus reads this range, and where it departs

- **Shefa and light.** The Rambam's spring (line 703) and "in Your light we see light" (line 704) are the root of the Chassidic habit of calling all divine action *or* and *shefa*. But Chassidus turns the image into ontology (the ray has no existence apart from the luminary; Tanya, Shaar HaYichud 3); for the Rambam it only describes how a bodiless cause acts.
- **Angels as forces.** "All the forces are angels" (line 678) is a naturalizing reading Chassidus does not take. For the Tanya, angels are real beings of Yetzirah whose self-nullification is less than a Jew's in mitzvah.
- **Prophecy through the imagination.** The Rambam makes the imagination the vessel of prophecy (line 925) and its weakness the reason prophecy fails (line 930). Chassidus inherits the idea that sadness blocks (Tanya ch. 26-31) but locates the block in the heart's vessel, not in a bodily faculty.
- **Everything is His act (II:48).** Read by Chassidus much more radically: the Baal Shem Tov's "hashgachah pratit" extends to the turning of a leaf, where the Rambam (see Part Three) limits individual providence to humans in proportion to their intellect.

## What this range adds

- The psychology of longing (line 664) and of joy and grief (lines 842-848). - A plain account of counterfeit revelations (lines 933, 942), useful for `discern`. - The prophet's three perfections as a program of preparation (lines 925-928). - II:48 as a direct teaching on seeing G-d's sending in human choices and chance.

## Where the thread is thin

- II:3-11 and II:19-24 are Aristotelian cosmology and astronomy. Their inner content is small; their value is the Rambam's honesty about what is not known. - II:29-30 are biblical exegesis of creation and prophecy's figures of speech; little on inner life beyond the lines noted.
