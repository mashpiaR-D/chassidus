## Summary (one page)

This slice runs from Shavuos 5672 to the second night of Shavuos 5674. It is mostly long, technical Kabbalah: Tohu and Tikkun, Keser and the hidden Chochmah, the incense and the offerings. Through all of it, the Rebbe Rashab keeps finding one plain point under each light of the soul. He also keeps showing that the deepest point is the lowest one, and the easiest to reach.

- **Will is under love.** Love comes from understanding, so it has a limit. Under it is a will with no reason, not even pleasure. It is like a father's will for his son: 'either way, they are My children'. This will stays when love is gone. It is why a simple Jew can give his life. It also rules over pleasure: no pile of pleasures can put it out, and even someone sunk in pleasure who says 'I want it too' is telling the truth (C1, C2, X1, X2).
- **What fits you does not change you.** Love from contemplation settles in the heart like a drink of water and quenches the thirst. So nature stays the same, and for most of us even the 'good' we feel is partly physical. Love that is too big for the heart (reuta d'liba) is what turns a person from cruel to merciful. The mashal is a teacher who gives more than the student can hold (C3, X5).
- **You are room given.** Two proud people cannot unite; two humble ones can. True loftiness makes room even for the smallest thing, without that thing becoming 'something' of its own. The worlds exist only because the Infinite gives them place. 'Nothing else' is lived inside the solid world, where a soul that is 'someone' can set its whole self down (C4, C12, X3, X8).
- **The subtlest ego is felt humility.** Real bittul does not know that it is batel. 'I have no place', 'it feels good to be nothing', and 'I want my humility seen' are each a self coming back (C8, X4).
- **The narrow place reaches highest.** Pain and bitterness reach above even essential pleasure. Teshuvah reaches G-d's delight in the soul itself, and a new will follows. The darkness of exile is the father's anger, which is love in a hard shape. A test is an absence dressed up as a wall (C15, C17, C18, X6, X11).
- **The return is required.** Running up without coming back (Nadav and Avihu) is a sin. The Essence is found in Torah after prayer and in mitzvos done as a command. Giving to others adds to the giver, but only when it is done with bittul. Every deep teaching here lands in plain, whole (tamim) action. The self-surrender of the soul's depth is the same in every Jew and easy to reach (C5, C9, C19, C21, X9, X10).

Corrections to DESCENT [ours]: D8 should be read as *will* (ratzon atzmi), deeper than love and ruling over pleasure. D2 (no one left to watch) should add that the watcher hides most easily inside felt humility. D9 should add that the turn comes most strongly from the narrow place, from pain and sin, which reaches higher than delight. D10 should add 'room given': the world exists only because He gives it place.

## Chains

### AB1-C1: Why can a simple Jew give up his life for G-d when his love of G-d is small? *Where:* Shavuos 5672 (L27); Pinchas 5672 (L223-224); Nitzavim 5672 (L485); Shabbos Shuvah 5674, ch. 201 (L1645)

1. **M1**: Love that grows out of understanding always has a measure. It goes as far as the reason goes, so it can never demand that you give your life. [stated] *How:* The text sets the problem: love built on a reason is limited by that reason, so it cannot be the source of self-sacrifice. > באה בהגבלה ואינו מחייבת ענין המס"נ לפי שהאהבה היא מוגבלת (L27) > "it comes with a limit and does not demand self-sacrifice, because the love is limited"
2. **M2**: Under love there is a wanting. A father wants his son, not because the son is good for him, but because the son is his own self. [stated] *How:* It separates two things we lump together: love (because something is good for me) and will (because it is my very self). > ואהבה עצמיות היא בחי' רצון והוא מה שרוצה בדבר שהוא עצמו ומהותו (L223) > "essential love is really will: wanting a thing because it is one's own self and essence"
3. **M12**: So the wanting stays even when the love is gone. When the son disappoints and no love shows, the father still wants him. 'Either way, they are My children.' [stated] *How:* It holds both sides: love can disappear while will stays, and it applies this to G-d and Israel. > לא מאסתים ולא געלתים כו', וכמארז"ל בין כך ובין כך בני הם (L224) > "I have not despised them nor loathed them; as the Sages say, either way they are My children"
4. **M8**: Your soul has the same wanting toward G-d. When the soul's core shows, it is pulled to Him like a flame rising by itself, asking for nothing in return. [stated] *How:* It moves from the father's will to the soul's will: rooted in the Essence, it is drawn with no request and no account for itself. > כשלהבת העולה מאליה שנמשכת (זיא ציהט זעך) לאלקות בבחי' רצון עצמי (L485) > "like a flame that rises by itself, that is drawn (it pulls itself) to G-dliness as an essential will"
5. **M15**: That is why giving one's life happens with no feeling of sweetness at all. If it ran on pleasure, it could fail. [stated] *How:* The highest act of devotion is the plainest: it has no reason and no felt delight, and that is exactly why it holds. > וכמו במס"נ בפו"מ על קדה"ש שאין בזה שום הרגש עונג כלל (L1645) > "as in giving one's life in deed for the sanctity of G-d's Name, where there is no feeling of pleasure at all"
6. **M14**: What holds it is one thing only: it is impossible for him to be separate from G-d. [stated] *How:* Why at all? Not reason, not pleasure, but the soul's bond itself, which cannot be cut. > רק מצד הרצון העצמי דהיינו עצם ההתקשרות הנשמה באלקו' שא"א לו כלל להיות נפרד (L1645) > "only from the essential will, the very bond of the soul with G-dliness, so that it is impossible for him at all to be separate"

**Bottom:** A wanting with no reason and no pleasure behind it: the soul's own bond with G-d's Essence. Nothing underneath it is a 'why'. [stated] **Return:** It comes back down as plain deed: the same will is what keeps the mitzvos that have no reason (chukim), and what a person keeps even when he feels nothing. [stated L1645; the link to today's dry mitzvah is ours]

### AB1-C2: Pleasure is deeper than will. So why does will win? *Where:* Shabbos Shuvah 5674, ch. 202 (L1647-1651)

1. **M2**: Will runs out toward something. Pleasure stays hidden inside you. [stated] *How:* It defines both: ratzon is from 'running' (meirutzah), the soul going out; pleasure is the soul at home in itself. > דרצון הוא ל' מרוצה שהוא התגלות והמשכת הנפש (L1647) > "will (ratzon) is from the word 'running': it is the soul showing itself and going out"
2. **M5**: The deepest pleasure is not in what you own, and not even in your good qualities. It is simply being glad in yourself. [stated] *How:* Higher still: essential pleasure is in the essence itself, not in its qualities. The Yiddish gloss names it. > (צו פרידין פון זיך אליין), שזהו העונג בהעצם לא במה שהוא העצם (L1648) > "(to be content with oneself alone): the pleasure is in the essence, not in what the essence is"
3. **M1**: Yet pleasure cannot change what you want. A great mind loves ideas, but he wants only holy ones, and no clever idea will make him want the opposite. [stated] *How:* The question that breaks it: the higher power (pleasure) does not rule the lower (will). The text proves it from the wise man. > וזהו הטעם מה שהרצון שולט על התענוג ואין התענוג שולט על הרצון (L1649) > "this is why will rules over pleasure, and pleasure does not rule over will"
4. **M9**: So no pile of pleasures can put out a Jew's want for G-d. 'Many waters cannot quench the love.' [stated] *How:* It applies the rule to every Jew: the many strange wants are the 'many waters'. > מים רבים דריבוי רצונות ותאוות זרות כו' לא יוכלו לכבות האה' והרצון העצמי (L1649) > "'many waters', the many foreign wants and desires, cannot put out the love and the essential will"
5. **M9**: Even a man sunk in pleasures, at a happy moment, will tell you 'I want this too, I just have obstacles.' It is not a lie. The want is really in him. [stated] *How:* The text's own case: a person far from practice who still praises the G-d-fearing. His words come from the real will. > וכ"ז אינו שקר בנפשו דהאמת הוא שרוצה באלקו' כי הרצון העצמי יש בו (L1650) > "and this is no lie in his soul; the truth is that he wants G-dliness, because the essential will is in him"
6. **M3**: Will can even turn pain into pleasure: a fast hurts, but someone who badly wants to fix himself enjoys it. [stated] *How:* It shows will ruling pleasure from the other side: pain becomes delight when wanted. > דע"ה צער לנפש ה"ה לו לעונג מפני שרוצה מאד בתיקון נפשו (L1650) > "though it is pain to the soul, it is pleasure to him, because he wants very much to repair his soul"
7. **M4**: Why? When you want, your self itself goes out. When you enjoy, only a glow of the pleasure comes out. [stated] *How:* But in truth: what goes out in will is the essence; what goes out of hidden pleasure is only a ray. So the one that carries the self wins. > מפני שהרצון שהוא בבחי' מרוצה והמשכה נמשך העצמו' שלו (L1651) > "because in will, which is running and drawing out, its very essence is drawn"

**Bottom:** Will carries the self itself into the world, so the will toward G-d cannot be bought off by any pleasure. [stated] **Return:** A baal teshuvah comes to despise the old pleasures completely, because the want has taken them over. The person far away is told: your want is real; act from it. [stated L1650; the advice is ours]

### AB1-C3: Why does real love in prayer leave a person's nature the same? *Where:* Lech Lecha 5674, ch. 220-221 (L1793-1805)

1. **M2**: Love that comes from contemplation fits inside the heart. It quenches the thirst, the way a drink of water cools the fire inside you. [stated] *How:* The mashal: thirst is fire; drinking cools it. Love that 'arrives' at G-d satisfies, and so it settles. > וכמו השותה מים לצמאונו שיסוד האש שהי' תחלה בהתגברות בלבבו נתקרר ע"י השתי' (L1793) > "like one who drinks water for his thirst: the fire that was strong in his heart is cooled by the drink"
2. **M4**: But that is not yet true service. It has no end-point; it is a step, not the goal. [stated] *How:* The text says plainly that satisfied love in prayer is not yet true service. > ובאמת א"ז עבודה אמיתי' כי אין בזה תכלית עדיין (L1793) > "and in truth this is not real service, for there is no fulfillment in it yet"
3. **M7**: The real closeness comes afterward, in doing Torah and mitzvos all day. [stated] *How:* From inside feeling to outside deed: the love's purpose is the day of mitzvos, where the light settles as inner light. > הרי עיקר הדבקות והגילוי הוא בקיום המצות (L1794) > "the main cleaving and revelation is in keeping the mitzvos"
4. **M10**: Even so, for most of us the body's heart closes around this love like a jug around water. Even the 'good' we feel in G-dliness is mixed with a physical good. So our nature stays. [stated] *How:* Where the comparison breaks: the benoni's spiritual good is felt through a material sense, so it changes only how far the animal soul spreads, not what it is. > שא"ז רק טוב אלקי כ"א יש בזה תערובות חומריות (א חומריות'ער טוב) (L1797) > "it is not only G-dly good; there is a mixture of the physical in it (a materialistic good)"
5. **M5**: Love that does not fit, that never quenches the thirst, changes your very nature: from cruel to merciful, from coarse to humble. [stated] *How:* Higher still: reuta d'liba is bigger than the heart's vessel, so it does not settle, and that is why it reaches the essence of the animal soul. > ה"ז פועל על הטבעי' שתתבטל בעצם מהותה עד שיוכל להתהפך עי"ז מאכזרי לרחמני (L1800) > "it acts on the natural soul so that it is nullified in its very essence, until it can turn from cruel to merciful"
6. **M16**: Think of your will: it can put your foot into icy water against all your nature, because will is not held in one organ. [stated] *How:* Mashal from the body: inner powers stay in their organs; will goes everywhere and so can change nature. > כמו לשום רגלו במים קרים ביותר או במים חמים ביותר (L1801) > "such as putting his foot into very cold or very hot water"
7. **M10**: And a teacher who gives what is beyond the student's head: the student grasps only that it is precious, and that is what refines his mind itself. [stated] *How:* A second mashal: teaching that fits leaves the student coarse; teaching above him lowers him and makes him a vessel. > רק יודע ומקבל יוקר הענין (די רייכקייט פון דער זאך) (L1804) > "he only knows and receives the preciousness of the matter (the richness of the thing)"

**Bottom:** What fits you leaves you as you are; what is too big for you changes what you are. [stated, phrased by us] **Return:** So do not look for love you can hold. Pray toward what you cannot hold, then carry it into the day's mitzvos, where it settles. [ours, following L1794-1805]

### AB1-C4: Why could the sefiros of Tohu not live together? *Where:* Bamidbar 5674, ch. 230 (L1881-1888); Shavuos night 1 5674, ch. 231-235 (L1891-1919)

1. **M1**: Two great ministers, each very big in his own eyes, can never truly unite. Each fills the whole room. [stated] *How:* The text's mashal for Tohu: the bigger each 'yesh', the less room for the other. > כל שהמה גדולים בעצם ביותר יותר לא יוכלו להתחבר ולהתאחד (L1882) > "the greater they are in themselves, the less they can join and become one"
2. **M10**: Two humble poor people, even with opposite natures, will love each other and give way to each other. [stated] *How:* Where the comparison turns: lowliness makes room, so opposites can join. That is Tikkun. > וכמו ב' עניים שפלים בטבעם ונמוכי רוח אע"פ שהן ב' הפכים בטבעם יתחברו ויאהבו זא"ז (L1883) > "like two poor people, lowly by nature and humble of spirit: though opposite in nature they join and love each other"
3. **M1**: Yet true greatness is drawn to the lowly. A king has more mercy than his ministers, because before him everyone is small. [stated] *How:* A new question: loftiness makes room too. So loftiness itself cannot be the problem. > וזה הטעם שגבי המלך ימצא ענין הרחמים יותר מבשרים הגדולים (L1886) > "this is why mercy is found more with the king than with the great ministers"
4. **M4**: The difference is how you see the other. If you see him as truly nothing, you are drawn to him. If you see him as 'something', you cannot bear him. [stated] *How:* But in truth: the lofty one who sees the other as existing feels him as a rival. > שאם רואה אותו בבחי' העדר המציאות באמת אז ה"ה נמשך אליו (L1887) > "if he sees him as truly without existence, then he is drawn to him"
5. **M11**: The root of it is in the lofty one: he feels himself as 'something'. [stated] *How:* Root of what blocks: not the other's existence, but my felt existence. > מה שהמרומם הוא בבחי' מציאות מורגש בעצמו (L1894) > "that the lofty one is a felt existence in his own eyes"
6. **M5**: Real loftiness gives room to everything, the smallest thing as much as the largest. [stated] *How:* Higher still: essential loftiness, which is not an 'existence' at all, makes room without being threatened. > וגם לדבר הקטן ביותר נותן לו ערך ומקום כמו לדבר הגדול (L1915) > "and even to the smallest thing it gives worth and place like the great thing"
7. **M15**: That is how you exist. The worlds are not 'something' from their own side. They are here only because the Infinite gives them room. [stated] *How:* It lands on the daas elyon: creation is real only as room given by Him. > ומה שהן במציאות זהו רק מה שהאוא"ס נותן להם מקום ומציאות (L1918) > "and that they exist is only that the Infinite Light gives them place and existence"

**Bottom:** You are not a 'something' next to G-d; you are room He gives. [stated, phrased by us] **Return:** Like King David: full royal power and, at once, 'I am poor and needy'. In practice: give room to others the way you are given room. [stated L1912; practice is ours]

### AB1-C5: Why did the sefiros of Tohu refuse even to give, when giving makes one great? *Where:* Shavuos night 2 5674, ch. 237-239 (L1933-1950)

1. **M1**: 'What's mine is mine and what's yours is yours' is the trait of Sodom: each one complete, needing no one. That was Tohu. [stated] *How:* It names the Tohu stance with the Mishnah's phrase. > האומר שלי שלי ושלך שלך ה"ז מדת סדום (L1934) > "one who says 'mine is mine and yours is yours' has the trait of Sodom"
2. **M2**: In Tikkun each quality feels it is not whole alone: even kindness feels that kindness alone is not complete. [stated] *How:* The matter is: bittul makes each power sense its lack, so it receives from its opposite. > יש בה ההרגש דחסד לבד אינו שלימות (L1934) > "there is in it the feeling that kindness alone is not complete"
3. **M1**: But why should anyone give? To teach, a teacher must first hide his own mind completely, to find the small piece that fits the student. [stated] *How:* The question sharpens: giving costs the giver many contractions, against nature. > דבתחלה צריך להעלים אור שכלו לגמרי בכדי שימצא בו האור שכל השייך אל המקבל (L1936) > "first he must hide the light of his mind completely, in order to find in it the light that fits the receiver"
4. **M14**: Because by giving, the giver gains. You can understand an idea in detail and still feel it is 'not comfortable yet'. That means there is more depth you have not reached. [stated] *How:* Why at all: speaking an idea to someone opens depths in the giver. > דעם היות שמשיג את הענין היטב בפרטי הענינים שבו ומ"מ אינו נוח לו עדיין (L1940) > "though he grasps the matter well in its details, still it does not sit right with him yet"
5. **M15**: Speech, the lowest power, has its root above the mind. That is why teaching brings out ideas you never had. [stated] *How:* The deepest lands lowest: the 'receiver' (speech) is rooted higher than the giver (mind). > דשרש הדבור (שהוא בחי' מקבל כו') הוא למע' מהשכל (L1941) > "the root of speech (which is a receiver) is above the intellect"
6. **M9**: The student who asks hard questions makes his teacher wiser. [stated] *How:* It points to the familiar case of 'from my students most of all'. > ובפרט בתלמיד המחכים את רבו ע"י הקושיות וסתירות (L1942) > "and especially a student who makes his teacher wise through questions and contradictions"
7. **M4**: The giver grows only when he gives by setting himself down. [stated] *How:* But in truth: the gain comes only with bittul, setting oneself down to the true intent. Tohu lacked this. > ודוקא כאשר ההשפעה היא בבחי' ביטול אז דוקא מתוסף בהמשפיע (L1948) > "only when the giving is with self-nullification is something added to the giver"

**Bottom:** Giving is laying yourself down to what G-d intends; through the receiver, light without limit comes to the giver. [stated] **Return:** 'I am G-d your G-d who took you out of Egypt': out of limits, through light that settles in vessels. Teach what you know, and you will be given more. [stated L1950; advice ours]

### AB1-C6: Can a person really know G-d is the only reality? *Where:* Ki Savo 5673, ch. 189-190 (L1549-1551); Vayera 5674, ch. 226 (L1842-1846)

1. **M10**: Whatever picture of 'nothing' or 'pure light' you make in your mind is really a something. [stated] *How:* Where the comparison breaks: we learn 'light' from sunlight and mind-light; even the negation is still a 'yesh'. > דכל מה שאנו מציירים בחי' אין ה"ז בחי' מציאו' באמת (L1549) > "whatever we picture as 'nothing' is in truth a something"
2. **M3**: What we can grasp is that nothing makes itself. Every thing needs a G-dly power making it and giving it life. [stated] *How:* Lay a foundation from the created thing itself. > וההשגה הוא מהנבראי' עצמן שהרי אין דבר עושה א"ע (L1551) > "the grasp comes from the creatures themselves, for nothing makes itself"
3. **M16**: You can know this and still be far from it. Then it does nothing to you. [stated] *How:* Knowing far: a fact held at a distance. > הרי הידיעה הוא בריחוק אצלו ואינו פועל בו דבר (L1551) > "the knowledge is far from him and does nothing in him"
4. **M16**: By thinking it through deeply, the knowing comes near. You feel it, and it begins to remove your sense of self. [stated] *How:* Knowing near: deep contemplation turns a fact into a felt reality. > וע"י ההתבוננות בהעמקת הדעת נעשה הידיעה בקירוב אליו ונרגש בו (L1551) > "through contemplation with deep focus, the knowing becomes near to him and is felt in him"
5. **M15**: Down here this is most certain, because you watch things fall apart every day. What decays cannot exist by itself. [stated] *How:* The deepest lands lowest: the senses give a certainty the upper worlds lack. > שה"ה נפסדים ורואה הפסד הדברים בכל עת (L1845) > "for they decay, and he sees the decay of things all the time"
6. **M7**: The example from the physical world is not a mere picture. The example IS the thing it shows. [stated] *How:* From outside to inside: so 'from my flesh I see G-d' is real seeing-by-sense. > וידוע דהמשל הוא הוא הנמשל (L1845) > "it is known that the parable is itself the thing it teaches"
7. **M7**: When the animal soul is broken open by this certainty, like a nutshell, the fruit shows: the G-dly soul sees G-d's essence. [stated] *How:* 'To know' (lada'as) also means to break. Breaking the shell reveals the soul's own seeing. > וכמו האגוז כששוברים הקליפה מתגלה הפרי (L1846) > "like a nut: when the shell is broken the fruit is revealed"

**Bottom:** The soul's own seeing of G-d, more than it had in Gan Eden, opened by the body's plain certainty. [stated] **Return:** In the future the body itself will see. For now: look at one decaying thing today and ask what holds it up while it lasts. [stated L1850; the exercise is ours]

### AB1-C7: I understand it all. Why do I feel nothing? *Where:* Shoftim 5673, ch. 180 (L1479-1483); Shemini Atzeres 5674, ch. 215 (L1749-1751)

1. **M3**: Think: all the worlds are only a glow, and they change nothing in G-d. [stated] *How:* The foundation: 'I G-d have not changed'; everything is a ray that takes no room. > והיינו דמההתבוננות איך שהכל הוא הארה לבד שאינו תופס מקום כלל (L1480) > "from contemplating how everything is only a ray that takes up no place at all"
2. **M8**: This should make you vanish like a small child before a great adult, who simply loses himself. [stated] *How:* The expected response, with the Yiddish image of the child. > וכמו הקטן לגבי הגדול הרי מתבטל לגמרי (ער פארלירט זעך) (L1481) > "like a small child before an adult, who is completely nullified (he loses himself)"

## Depths beyond the descent

### AB1-X1: The wanting under the love (extends D8) Under every love of G-d there is a will that has no reason and needs no pleasure: the soul's bond, like a father's will for his son. It remains when love is gone, and it is what lets a plain Jew give his life. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Notice something you love because it is good for you. If it stopped being good, the love would fade. 2. Now think of a father and his child. He wants the child even when the child brings no joy: 'either way, they are My children'. 3. Your soul wants G-d in that second way. It is not because G-d is good for you. He is your root. 4. So when you feel no love, the wanting is still there. Look for it, not for the feeling. 5. Rest in this: it is impossible for me to be separate from Him.

*Inside:* A flame rising by itself; a pull with 'no request at all'; a father's longing for a son. [L485, L1639] *Lands:* In plain deeds done with no taste: chukim, and holding firm when tested. ### AB1-X4: The humility that watches itself (extends D2) The subtlest ego is felt humility. Real bittul does not know it is batel; if asked what changed, it would say 'I know nothing'. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Picture standing before a great king. 2. In that moment you are not thinking about how humble you are. 3. Then notice the steps down: 'I have no place', 'it feels good to be nothing', 'I want my humility seen'. 4. Each step is a self coming back. 5. Turn back to the King and let the watcher go.

*Inside:* 'I knew nothing'; 'he has no place for himself'. [L1744] *Lands:* Do kindness without saying 'see what I did for you'. [L1749] > דאמיתי' הביטול הוא שאינו מרגיש כלל שהוא בטל (L1744): "true bittul does not feel itself" > שרוצה שיהי' נראה ביטולו (L1745): "he wants his bittul seen"

### AB1-X5: Love too big to hold (extends new) Love that fits the heart quenches thirst and leaves nature unchanged. Love that cannot be held, reuta d'liba, changes what you are: cruel to merciful. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Notice how a good prayer can satisfy you, like a drink of water. 2. That satisfaction is real, but it settles and the old self stays. 3. Now turn toward what is beyond you, which you cannot contain. 4. Do not try to hold it. Let it be too big. 5. That overflow is what reaches your nature.

*Inside:* Water in a jug vs a foot put into icy water by will; the student who senses only 'the richness of the thing'. [L1796-1804] *Lands:* Carry it into the day's mitzvos, where the light truly settles. [L1794] > וכמו השותה מים לצמאונו שיסוד האש שהי' תחלה בהתגברות בלבבו נתקרר ע"י השתי' (L1793): "like drinking water that cools the fire" > ה"ז פועל על הטבעי' שתתבטל בעצם מהותה עד שיוכל להתהפך עי"ז מאכזרי לרחמני (L1800): "it changes nature from cruel to merciful"

### AB1-X6: The narrow place reaches highest (extends D9) Pain and bitterness reach above even essential pleasure. The 'not-good' of sin reveals the soul's whole self, which touches G-d's delight in the soul itself, so that a new will comes and sins turn to merits. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Feel honestly where you are far. 2. Do not cover it. Let it press, like a man fleeing who leaps a ditch. 3. Know that G-d delights in you, not only in your deeds: 'only in your fathers did He delight'. 4. Cry from the heart, not the voice. 5. Let a new will come, and act on it.

*Inside:* Jumping a great ditch while fleeing; 'from the narrow place I called'. [L1812] *Lands:* Regret plus a real resolve for the future; then Torah and mitzvos with fresh will. [L1585-1586] > דזהו כלל גדול דצער ומרירות הנפש מגיע למע' מעונג הנפש (L1812): "pain reaches higher than pleasure" > ויכול להיות עונג בלא רצון, וכמו רק באבותיך חשק ה' (L1584): "delight without will"

### AB1-X7: The settled sense under the mind (extends D5) Beneath understanding is a 'laying' of the mind (hanacha) where the essence of a matter is felt. In Chochmah, that settled point is G-d as the only true existence and all grasp as nothing. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Recall the moment an idea first 'clicked'. 2. Notice it later felt unclear when you worked out details. 3. The click was not wrong; it was deeper than the details. 4. Tie yourself back to it. 5. Apply this to 'He alone is'. Let it lie in you before you explain it.

*Inside:* 'It becomes right and very good'; 'it has a place in the mind'. [L1083-1085] *Lands:* In contemplation, return to the point; do not chase details. [L292] > והעצה הוא שיקשר עצמו עוד הפעם (ער זאל זעך צו טראגין) אל עומק ופנימי' הענין (L292): "bind yourself again to the depth" > וזהו שהחכ' הוא בחי' ד"ע והיינו שבחכ' מאיר האוא"ס יש האמיתי (L294): "in Chochmah the Infinite shines as true existence"

### AB1-X8: 'Nothing else' lives in the something (extends D4) Will leaves no trace; the world is made by G-d's will; so the world is an existence that is not an existence. That is why 'there is nothing else' belongs to the solid yesh, and why the soul below can set down its whole self. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Want something, then stop wanting. Nothing is left. 2. Thoughts stay in memory; wants leave nothing. 3. The world is G-d's want. If He stopped, nothing. 4. So even now it is not a 'thing' of its own. 5. And I, a 'someone', am the place to lay that someone down.

*Inside:* The soul inclining and withdrawing; a thought that stays vs a want that vanishes. [L353-354] *Lands:* Hanachas atzmuso in prayer: you set yourself down because you exist. [L1458] > ובזה יובן איך שענין אין עוד הוא במציאות היש דוקא (L356): "'nothing else' is in the yesh" > והביטול דאין עוד הוא בבחי' יש דוקא (L1458): "the bittul of 'nothing else' is in the yesh"

### AB1-X9: Equal at the depth (extends D7) In understanding, souls differ. At the soul's depth all are equal, and the self-surrender that comes from there is in every Jew and easy to reach. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Admit you may not understand as others do. 2. That is the level of mind, where people differ. 3. Go under it, to where you simply belong to G-d. 4. There, your soul is the same as every other. 5. Lay yourself away to Him, wholly.

*Inside:* A servant, not a lover; a child before an adult who loses himself. [L410-411] *Lands:* Guard the senses: shut eyes from evil, ears from hearing, mouth from speaking. [L413] > ומדרי' זו יש בכאו"א מישראל ובנקל לבוא לזה (L412): "this is in every Jew and easy" > אבל בהעלאת מ"ן דבחי' פנימי' ועומק הנפש כולם שוין (L1628): "at the depth all are equal"

### AB1-X10: Giving makes you more (extends new) To give, you must hide your own mind and find what fits the other. Yet the giver gains, because speech, the receiving power, is rooted above the mind. True giving is bittul to G-d's intent; through it, light without limit comes to the giver. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Take something you know and explain it to someone. 2. Notice what you had to set aside to make it fit. 3. Notice what new depth opened as you spoke. 4. That depth came from a root higher than your mind. 5. Give as one setting himself down to what G-d wants.

*Inside:* The teacher hiding his mind; the student's hard question; 'it is not comfortable yet'. [L1936-1942] *Lands:* Teach, explain, share Torah daily. [ours from L1940-1948] > דשרש הדבור (שהוא בחי' מקבל כו') הוא למע' מהשכל (L1941): "speech is rooted above intellect" > ודוקא כאשר ההשפעה היא בבחי' ביטול אז דוקא מתוסף בהמשפיע (L1948): "only in bittul does the giver gain"

### AB1-X11: Darkness made of love (extends D9) The father's anger at a dirty son is love itself in a hard form. So the exile's deep darkness comes from G-d's essential love, from the hidden Wisdom whose nature is to hide; standing firm in it and turning it to good will reveal that hidden Wisdom itself. [stated]

*Hisbonenus:* 1. Picture the father angry because the son is precious. 2. The anger would not exist without the love. 3. Our darkness is that kind of severity. 4. Accept it with love and mend. 5. Then the love shows, more than before.

*Inside:* The father and the son in the mud; 'my heart and my flesh sing'. [L1831-1836] *Lands:* Stay strong in Torah and mitzvos despite every obstacle. [L1835] > הרי שעצם מהותה של הגבו' היא אה' (L1832): "the essence of the severity is love" > שגם בריבוי העלמות וההסתרים יהי' חזק בקיום התורה והמצות (L1835): "be strong despite concealments"
