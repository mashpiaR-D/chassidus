# Pardes Rimmonim, lines 3271-3445: combination, the soul, and intention

*Range:* lines 3271-3445: Gate 30 (HaTzeiruf, combination), Gate 31 (HaNeshamah, the soul), Gate 32 (HaKavanah, intention). End of the book.

---
These are the last three gates of the Pardes. Gate 30 lays out the tables of letter-combination, substitution and gematria. It is technical, and its one practical passage, name-permutation in seclusion, is **not for the guide**. The Ramak himself hedges it (line 3277).

Gates 31 and 32 are the book's landing place, where all the maps come back to the person. They ask what a soul is, why it comes down into a body, how a sin stains it and a return frees it, and how to pray so that the prayer reaches the One. Several of the Tanya's most famous phrases appear here first: "the soul is a part of God above", the animal soul in the chamber of the heart, and garments in the Garden from deeds below and from the heart's intention above. The book ends with the Ramak's own answer to the question every contemplative asks: to whom am I praying? His answer: to Him, "who is all".

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**To whom do I pray? To Him, who is all.** The Ramak lists three problems with praying "through the sefirot". Scripture says "to Him, not to His attributes" (אליו ולא למדותיו, line 3437). Any attribute I picture, I make physical. And picturing one attribute separates it from the rest. His answer: "Ein Sof, the King of kings, has no name or word that limits Him" (הא"ס ממ"ה ב"ה אין שם ומלה שיגבילהו, line 3438). The words point to attributes, "but his intent is to the Essence that spreads in Greatness" (אמנם כוונתו על העצמות המתפשט בגדולה, line 3438), and so for all of them. The Tikkunim: "in Him one must intend, of whom one cannot say voice or speech, only thought" (וביה צריך הכונה דלא תלייא למימר ביה קול ודבור אלא מחשבתא, line 3438). And the summary: "the intent is that a person intend Him, that He is all, and He is the one who unites the attributes and pours into them" (אלא הכונה שיכוון האדם בו שהוא הכל והוא המיחד המדות והמשפיעם, line 3439). This is the Ramak's last word on the unity in his great book. The sefirot are His garments and tools. The prayer goes to Him, and He is all.

**The soul is a part of God above.** Gate 32 opens: "a person is a part of God above, if he is pure and upright in his deeds, and he is held by ropes of love in the roots of holiness" (שהאדם הוא חלק אלוה ממעל … והוא אחוז בעבותות האהבה בשרשי הקדושה, line 3431). The soul's chain runs all the way up: "and that neshamah is bound in Ein Sof" (וההוא נשמה אתקשרת בא"ס, line 3352). There is no gap between the smallest soul and the Infinite. The rope is love.

**The higher longs for the lower.** Why would pure spirit join a body? "According to how the lower things are prepared, so the higher ones long to cleave to them" (כי לפי הכנת הדברים התחתונים כן חשק העליונים לדבק בהם, line 3392). The Mishkan proves it: dead materials, shaped rightly, drew the Presence. "As the effect longs to rise to its cause, so the cause longs for its effect to be near it" (וכמו שחשק המסובב לעלות אל סבתו כן חשק הסבה שיהיה מסובבו קרוב אליו, line 3392). If the cause wanted only to rise and the effect only to fall, "the unity and the bond would come apart". God's longing for the world is part of what holds it together.

**The body holds everything.** Limbs, sinews, hair, teeth, eyes, heart and lungs each map a level above. "The human body, though it is coarse matter, contains all, upper and lower" (כי גוף האדם עם היותו גשם עכור כולל הכל עליונים ותחתונים, line 3403). Each hair of the head has its own root, "for before Him there is no narrowness of eye at all" (אשר אין לפניו צרות עין כלל, line 3396). The Source gives to each thread separately. The details are for learners. The point for everyone is that the body is not outside the unity. It is a map of it.

## 3. States of consciousness

**A channel for others (RK-S25, proposed).** The Ramak's parable: a great spring as thick as a press beam, fed by hundreds of thousands of needle-thin pipes. Close one pipe and the whole spring is less. The pipes are the souls. A sin "closes the pipe", and no other soul can repair it for him; "this is 'all Israel are guarantors for one another'" (וזהו כל ישראל ערבים זה לזה, line 3431). The other side is also true. One who prays with intent rises rung by rung "until he becomes the seat and base of the flow, and from him it is divided to all the world" (ויהיה הוא מקום מושב ומכון להשפעה וממנו יתחלק לכל העולם, line 3434), "the whole world is fed for the sake of Chanina My son" (line 3435). Inside: my small prayer is one thin pipe of a great river. If I close it, everyone gets a little less. If I open it, some of the world is fed through me. How to enter: before praying, picture the pipe, one thread among many, and pray as one who carries water for others. Near S82 (The home below) and S65 (Given right now). What is new is that the self becomes a channel, and that matters for others.

**Servant on weekdays, son on Shabbat (named).** "One who has a soul of Beriah is called a servant of God, and one who has a soul of Atzilut is called a son" (מי שיש בו נשמת הבריאה יקרא עבד ה' ומי שיהיה בו נשמת האצילות יקרא בן, line 3349). So "six days you shall *serve*": on weekdays even the upright stand as servants. On Shabbat the added soul comes from higher up. And "as a person draws near his Maker, so his spirit is poured on him" (כי לפי מה שהאדם מתקרב אל קונו כן יושפע עליו רוחו, line 3353). "A person sanctifies himself below, and they sanctify him from above" (בר נש מקדש גרמיה מלרע, מקדשין ליה מלעילא, line 3353). Ties to S75 (Weekday: sorting) and S76 (Shabbos). What is new is that the two are named as two relationships: servant and son.

**The dead pray for the living (named).** "When the world needs mercy, and the living are in pain, they pray for them" (וכד אצטריכו בני עלמא רחמין ואינון חייא בצערא יתבין אינון מצלי צלותא עלייהו, line 3348). The nefesh stays near to "look, know and protect the generation". Grief can hold this: the one I lost still prays for me. Near S84 (Trust).

**The evil urge becomes a defender (named).** When a person walks straight, the evil urge "becomes his servant", and then "he goes up and becomes an advocate" (כדין איהו סליק ואתעביד סניגורא, line 3359) and says: find merit for him. The enemy inside becomes the one pleading for you. Pairs with RK-S22.

**Nothing good is lost (named).** One who did good and later regretted it loses it for himself, but "those goods and merits are not lost" (אינון טבוון וזכיין לא אתאבידו, line 3385). God gives them to a righteous person who died young. And the blessings of one who fell and came back are not despised: "one must not think those blessings or mitzvot that sank there will be loathsome to Him afterward" (line 3427). This belongs with RK-S23 (Counted though I could not).

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Voice, speech, and the thought beyond (RK-T27, proposed).** This is the Ramak's instruction for every prayer. The voice is Tiferet, "and the speech, the cutting of the words, is Malchut" (קול בת"ת ודבור שהוא חיתוך המלות במלכות, line 3443), and in saying voice and speech together one unites them. The thought goes to the One who has no voice or speech, "only thought" (line 3438), "that He is all" (line 3439). What it is for: prayer that goes to Him and not to a picture. How: in each blessing, hear your voice rise, feel the words being cut by your lips, and let your thought rest past both, on Him who is all and joins all. When the word says "great" or "mighty", intend Him who acts through greatness, not the greatness. Guard: no picturing of sefirot or colors. The Ramak himself offers colored Names for advanced learners ( line 3440), but the guide keeps only the plain thought: "Him, who is all". Belongs with "before_whom" and "binding".

**Give the prayer wings (RK-T28, proposed).** The Ramak's parable: a commoner cries outside the palace. The king does not turn his face but has a servant answer him outside. That is prayer by rote, which "has no wings to fly". "But one who intends in his prayer makes himself wings to fly to the heavens" (עשה יעשה לו כנפים לעוף השמים, line 3436), and it reaches the King. So "prayer without intention is like a body without a soul" (תפלה בלא כוונה כגוף בלא נשמה, line 3377), "and the intention is the soul of the act" (והכוונה הוא נשמת המעשה, line 3375); "according to the measure of the intention will be the greatness of its soul" (לפי שיעור הכוונה תהיה גודל הנשמה, line 3377). Garments too: "the garments below, in the Garden on earth, are from deeds; the garments above are from the spirit, the will, and the intention of the heart" (לבושין דלתתא בגנתא דארעא בעובדין, לבושין דלעילא ברוחא ורעותא וכוונה דלבא, line 3369). What it is for: turning habit into prayer. How: before a blessing or a mitzvah, stop for one breath and say inside what you are about to do and for whom. Then do it. Guard: the plain prayer is still answered, from outside the palace (line 3435). Never skip the prayer because the intention is weak. Belongs with "arrive" and "act".

**The broken spirit at the altar (RK-T29, proposed).** Why do returners stand where the fully righteous cannot? The returner goes back into the shells he entered and breaks them from within (line 3420). The Zohar: "a person must stand at the altar with a broken spirit and be ashamed of his deeds, so that that hard spirit is broken" (ובר נש בעי כדין למיקם על מדבחא ברוח נשברה ויכסף מעובדוי, line 3420). The Ramak: "by being ashamed of his sins he humbles the power of Gevurah and judgment" (ובהיותו מתבייש מעונותיו יכניע כח הגבורה והדין, line 3421). What it is for: breaking a hard, defended spirit after a wrong. How: stand quietly, name the deed, and let yourself feel ashamed of the deed, not of being you. Let the hardness crack. Then turn to Him. Guard: shame of the deed, short, and followed by joy. Never self-hatred (S11 is the trap). Near S48 (Returning) and S77 (The lower return).

**Strengthen the part you choose.** Every soul holds all ten, "and if a person wishes, he can strengthen in his soul by his deeds whatever part he wishes" (כן גם אם ירצה האדם יגביר בנשמתו מצד פעולותיו איזה חלק שירצה, line 3344): learning Torah strengthens Tiferet, kindness strengthens Chesed. "The Patriarchs wanted to cleave only to a high place" (והנה האבות לא רצו לידבק כי אם במקום גבוה, line 3344). Your trait is not your fate; your deeds choose it. This is the soul side of RK-T16 (Become the chariot of the trait you practice).

**A new Torah thought is a new heaven.** "When a person makes a new insight in Torah" (כי כאשר יחדש האדם חידושי תורה, line 3404), the letters in his mouth form something spiritual that rises, like the new heavens and earth of the Zohar. And "the intent is clothed in the act of the mitzvah" (והכונה מתלבשת במעשה המצוה, line 3404). So mitzvot need intent, as a body needs a soul.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why was I sent down here, if the soul was better off above?** The Ramak asks it sharply: "it was better for them there than in this world" (כי טוב היותם שם ממציאותם בעולם הזה, line 3371), where they are near loss and far from reward. His answer: before coming down "it was naked" (כי קודם בואה בעולם הזה היתה ערומה, line 3369). A soul cannot stand before the King until it wears the garments woven from deeds done here: "those deeds draw from the upper radiance a garment" (אינון עובדין משכי מנהורא דזיווא עלאה לבושא, line 3379). That is why Israel was not taken straight into Eden after Sinai. They had no garments yet. Life here is not exile from the good. It is the loom where the soul's clothing is made. "Let one understand the worth of the one who engages in inwardness in this world, for he does in this world the mitzvot of the world to come" (וישכיל מעלת העוסק בפנימיות בעה"ז כי פעל בעה"ז מצות העה"ב, line 3383).

**Am I stuck with the soul I was born with?** No. There is "a nefesh, and a higher nefesh than the nefesh" (אית נפש ואית נפש עלאה מנפש, line 3355). A person may live only on the animating life and earn more step by step. And a soul comes at the time of a mitzvah: "there are times when the neshamah is poured on a person when he fulfills some mitzvah" (ויש פעמים שהנשמה יושפע על האדם בעת יקיים איזה מצוה, line 3358).

**What does my sin do to others?** It narrows the river for all (line 3431). The chasid's sin darkens more, because more flowed through him (line 3433). But his return breaks shells that a never-fallen person never touched (line 3420).

**Does God keep a picture of me?** Each soul's image is engraved above. The angels go up, see it, come down, look at the person, "and see that it has not changed from the image above, and fear him" (וחזאן דלא אשתני מדיוקנא דלעילא ודחלין מניה, line 3417). When a person walks straight, "the holy spirit rests on him" (בשעתא דבר נש אחזי לתתא עובדא באורח מישר, line 3418).

**Is He near when I am in trouble?** For those who pray with heart, "at the time they are in distress, He is with them" (בשעתא דאינון בעאקו הוא לגבייהו, line 3435).

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **"A part of God above."** The Ramak's line 3431 is the Tanya's chapter 2 phrase almost word for word. The Tanya says: "the second soul in Israel is a part of God above, truly" (ונפש השנית בישראל, היא חלק אלוה ממעל ממש, Tanya line 86). The Tanya adds "truly" and roots it in the breath of God ("He who blows, blows from within"). The Ramak conditions it ("if he is pure and upright") and stresses the rope of love that binds it. It is the same claim with a different stress: the Tanya on the soul's essence, the Ramak on its connection.
- **The animal soul in the heart.** The Ramak's vital soul, "called by some the animal soul... a smoky vapor dwelling in the chamber of the heart" (line 3409), is the Tanya's "the dwelling of the animal soul from kelipat nogah... is in the heart, in the left chamber, full of blood" (מקום משכן נפש הבהמית שמקליפת נוגה בכל איש ישראל, הוא בלב בחלל שמאלי, Tanya line 181). The difference: for the Ramak the vital soul is a natural product of refined matter, a mount for the nefesh, morally neutral. For the Tanya it comes from kelipat nogah and has its own will that fights the divine soul. The Tanya's whole drama of two souls grows from the Ramak's quieter anatomy.
- **Garments from deeds and from the heart.** Line 3369 is quoted by the Alter Rebbe: "in the upper Garden the garments are from the will and intention of the heart in Torah and prayer" (אבל בגן עדן העליון הלבושים הם מרעותא וכוונה דלבא באורייתא וצלותא, Tanya line 1498), and "the garments of the souls in the Garden are the mitzvot" (שלבושי הנשמות בגן עדן הן המצות, Tanya line 139). Full agreement, through the same Zohar.
- **The returner.** The Ramak's reason for "where returners stand" (they break the shells from within, line 3420) and the Tanya's reason (the one far off thirsts more, "his soul thirsts with greater strength than the thirst of the righteous", ולזאת צמאה נפשו ביתר עז מצמאון נפשות הצדיקים, Tanya line 162) are two different explanations of the same teaching. The Ramak's is cosmic, the Tanya's is about longing. The guide can use both.
- **"Him, who is all" and the Gate of Unity.** Line 3439 is the nearest the Ramak comes, in the Pardes, to the saying Chabad attributes to him, "He is all that exists". Here he says it about intention: intend Him, "who is all". The Tanya would add that all is Him because nothing exists apart from Him. The Ramak stays with "He is all, and unites and pours into all". In both, prayer goes to the Essence clothed in the attributes. The Tanya's "intend to draw His light on the source of his soul and the souls of all Israel to unite them" (ויתכוין להמשיך אורו יתברך על מקור נפשו ונפשות כל ישראל לייחדן, Tanya line 615) is the Ramak's channel (RK-S25) made into a daily "for the sake of the union".
- **Tzimtzum.** Nothing in these gates addresses tzimtzum. The Ramak's picture of descent is a chain of garments and clothed lights, not a withdrawal. Where Chabad would speak of concealment, the Ramak speaks of longing, the cause for its effect (line 3392).
