*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A short open letter (1900) from the Rebbe Rashab to his Chassidim, who kept asking "how does one pray?" It says prayer, the service of the heart, is the core of inner work. Learning Chassidus is only preparation. The heart and character actually change only when that learning is contemplated at length and in detail during prayer. The work diagnoses several "illnesses" it sees among Chassidim, gives five preparations for prayer, and adds separate tracks for beginners, busy businessmen and everyone. It closes by asking that learning be "in order to do".

**How much of it is medicine.** substantial: almost the whole work is diagnosis and treatment of inner states (fake vs real feeling, character that won't change, self-satisfaction, distraction), though it is framed entirely as devotional practice for observant Chassidim.

**Its model of the person.** A divine soul lives, as if in exile, inside an animal soul. The animal soul's natural traits are strong, in three grades: the goat (immovable), the ox (aggressive but movable) and the sheep (soft). The faculties run from mind to heart. Da'at is both binding oneself to an idea and feeling it. Through da'at, what the mind grasps inwardly draws the heart after it by itself. Feeling that is only "surrounding" (makif) or forced is imaginary and fades. For most souls change is bottom-up: it is earned through labor in prayer, trait by trait. A broken heart makes a person a vessel. Habit can hide a nature that has not changed.

**Its philosophy.** All worlds are as nothing before the Infinite, who is present everywhere. The soul came down from a root above the angels to refine the animal soul, and a person's days are measured by the refining he must do. Prayer is a time of favor above and below, suited to taking in light. Knowledge that is not felt is lost, while light that is felt in mind and heart lasts.

**What it adds.** A diagnostic manual for telling real feeling from fake. It separates feeling that is forced, comes from a clever new interpretation, or comes only from the bottom line, from feeling that arises from inside. It defines da'at as feeling, and so ties refinement to feeling rather than to knowledge. It shows why general resolutions fail and particular ones last. It names false humility as a trick of the inclination. It gives separate tracks for beginners, for busy businessmen and for everyone.

## I study and pray a lot, but my character never changes; my bad traits even get stronger

*Its own term:* מתאוננים... אין שום שינוי לטוב בהמדות טבעיים

Years of learning and regular prayer, yet anger, desire and coarseness stay as they were at birth, or grow worse, and the person complains about it.

**What is really going on.** His contemplation in prayer is general and only 'surrounding' (makif): it never becomes felt inside, so it has no power over the natural traits. For most souls, learning alone does not refine them.

**Program: Detailed, long contemplation in prayer.** (1) Beforehand, learn a Chassidic concept thoroughly; this is only preparation. (2) In prayer, contemplate it in its many details, not only in general. (3) Bind yourself to the divine thing itself, not just its explanation, until it settles in the mind and shines. (4) Do not force the heart; the feeling follows from the mind by itself, through da'at. (5) Follow the order of the prayers: greatness of the Infinite in Pesukei deZimra, the angels' nullification, unity in Shema. (6) Before the Amidah, briefly think that you stand before the King; awe and inner nullification follow. *When:* Daily morning prayer; an hour or more for the contemplation itself. *For:* Someone who has already learned the material well and has time for long prayer. *Not for:* Young beginners without enough learning (they will fool themselves), and busy businessmen on weekdays (see G07). `KT·Q01` · `KT·Q04` · `KT·Q05`

**Program: Training the mind outside prayer.** (1) Set aside time each day to be alone and go deeply into one concept of Chassidus. (2) Keep it up over time until depth becomes habit. (3) Then long contemplation in prayer comes easily. *When:* An hour or two a day, outside prayer, kept up over a long period. *For:* Anyone who feels deep contemplation is beyond him. *Not for:* No exclusion is stated. `KT·Q09` · `KT·Q08`

**Reframe.** From *"I learn a lot of Chassidus, so I should be changing."* to **Learning is only preparation. Change happens in prayer, where understanding becomes felt; without that, learning is forgotten or turns into ego.** Refinement happens through da'at, which is feeling, and it is feeling that tells good from bad. The more one feels the divine, the more one feels how low evil is, and choice follows. Prayer is the time suited to taking this in. `KT·Q01` · `KT·Q03` · `KT·Q11` · `KT·Q12`

## What I feel in prayer is fake, or it disappears right away

A flash of excitement in the mind, or a heart pushed into feeling, gone the moment prayer ends. Sometimes excitement over a clever new interpretation of the words, which lasts a few hours at most.

**What is really going on.** The mind is attached to the explanation or to its bottom line (the בכן, 'therefore'), not to the thing itself, or the heart is forced. Real feeling comes only when da'at binds the mind to the idea until it is felt.

**Program: Detailed, long contemplation in prayer.** (1) Beforehand, learn a Chassidic concept thoroughly; this is only preparation. (2) In prayer, contemplate it in its many details, not only in general. (3) Bind yourself to the divine thing itself, not just its explanation, until it settles in the mind and shines. ## Real inner work is too high for someone like me

The work looks far away and wondrous. 'Who am I to take on something so great? Why wear myself out on what is beyond me?'

**What is really going on.** Only someone who has never tried thinks it is far. It is partly the inclination's trick: a drop of bitterness disguised as humility. The mind is in a person's control, and effort of the mind is easier than effort of the heart.

**Program: Training the mind outside prayer.** (1) Set aside time each day to be alone and go deeply into one concept of Chassidus. (2) Keep it up over time until depth becomes habit. (3) Then long contemplation in prayer comes easily. *When:* An hour or two a day, outside prayer, kept up over a long period. *For:* Anyone who feels deep contemplation is beyond him. *Not for:* No exclusion is stated. `KT·Q09` · `KT·Q08`

**Reframe.** From *"Work like this is beyond someone like me."* to **That humility is false, a drop of bitterness from the inclination. The mind is in your control, and effort of the mind is easier than effort of the heart.** By naming the discouragement as the inclination's tactic and moving the task to the mind, which a person controls, the work becomes something within reach. `KT·Q06` · `KT·Q07` · `KT·Q08`

## I can't get my worries and business out of my head when I pray

He comes to prayer straight from outside, full of worries. Many complain that they cannot remove the distractions from their heart.

**What is really going on.** He has not prepared, and prayer is not dear enough to him. He already clears his worries every night in order to sleep.

**Program: Five preparations before prayer.** (1) Clear every distraction: pause after coming in; argue with yourself that you clear your worries to sleep; stand in one place. (2) After putting on tallit and tefillin, think over a Chassidic idea for about half an hour. (3) Bring about lowliness through an honest accounting (see G04). (4) If the heart did not break, arouse great compassion on your own soul in Ahavat Olam. (5) Before the Amidah, contemplate standing before the Infinite. *When:* Every prayer; the half hour of study before praying; bitterness only at chosen times. *For:* Anyone preparing to pray with depth. *Not for:* Step 3's bitterness is not for daily use by most people; the text limits it (see G04). `KT·Q14` · `KT·Q15` · `KT·Q21` · `KT·Q13`

**Reframe.** From *"I simply can't get my worries out of my head."* to **You do it every night to fall asleep. If you can for your body, how much more for your soul.** A self-accounting based on his own experience shows him that he has the ability, and that the real issue is how dear prayer is to him. `KT·Q14`

## I resolve to be good, but in the moment of anger or desire I have no control

In prayer he truly wants good and makes a strong resolution. Later, anger or a desire comes, or he acts from habit without thinking, and he forgets the resolution.

**What is really going on.** The resolution was about 'the bad' in general. A general resolve does not reach particulars and has nothing to hold on to. He does not know his own specific evil.

**Program: Working one particular trait across many prayers.** (1) Know your own particular evil traits in detail; they differ in every person. (2) When the heart is stirred in prayer, direct it to overcome that one trait. (3) Stir up the holy attribute that opposes it. (4) Make a firm resolution in prayer about that particular trait. (5) Repeat over many prayers until the trait is subdued and the desire weakens. *When:* Within daily prayer, over many prayers, for one trait at a time. *For:* Those who already pray with mind and heart but see no change in their traits. *Not for:* No exclusion is stated. `KT·Q10` · `KT·Q27` · `KT·Q28` · `KT·Q29`

**Program: Daily vigilance under accepted authority.** (1) Accept the yoke of heaven fully. (2) Examine every trait and worldly matter against the Torah. (3) Do nothing without awareness, not even for a moment. (4) Let a wrong done be felt; resolve not to repeat it; uproot the trait it came from. *When:* Constant, through the day. *For:* Everyone; the text says this is equal for every Jewish soul. *Not for:* No exclusion is stated. `KT·Q30`

**Reframe.** From *"I hate the bad in me and resolved to be good; why don't I change?"* to **A general resolve does not reach particulars. Know your own specific trait and work on it.** A resolution made about a specific trait, and felt, lasts and weakens the desire itself; a general one has nothing to hold on to. `KT·Q27` · `KT·Q28` · `KT·Q29`

## I feel nothing inside; spiritual study is a burden; I'm fine as I am

*Its own term:* חיצוני... איש שלם לגמרי בעצמו

He enjoys hearing Chassidus but won't work at it, and it lights nothing in him. He does good only out of habit and company, so some event could bring the bad out in full force.

**What is really going on.** He was not raised in it and came to it from liking, not from true return. He has no broken heart and is 'whole in himself', so what he hears stays outside him.

**Program: Return to break a coarse nature.** (1) Set aside long, frequent periods to take account of all your thought, speech and deed. (2) Make an honest accounting between yourself and your Maker until the spirit breaks. (3) The text also prescribes fasts and mortifications, and accepting rebuke from others. (4) The broken heart becomes a vessel for inner divine light. *When:* A long hour, often; the accounting also on fast days. *For:* Someone whose learning stays external and who feels whole in himself. *Not for:* Anyone depressed, ashamed or traumatized. The fasts and humiliation are not for modern use (see cautions). `KT·Q24` · `KT·Q25` · `KT·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm fine as I am; I enjoy Chassidus."* to **Feeling whole in yourself keeps everything outside you; a broken heart is the true vessel.** Only what enters a broken heart becomes one with the person. Without that it passes through, and the unchanged nature can come out in full force. `KT·Q24` · `KT·Q25`

## Business swallows my whole mind; I have no time to pray properly

Nearly all his soul and vitality is tied up in the business pressing on him from every side, a drunkenness that confuses mind and heart.

**What is really going on.** Being enslaved to business is called drunkenness. If he does not hold firm at the moment of prayer, he falls straight into the pressures.

**Program: The busy person's morning.** (1) Each morning, realize how business consumes mind and heart; let the heart break. (2) Don't chat about business before prayer. (3) Learn a little before praying, as a fixed rule. (4) Pray slowly, attending to the words themselves. (5) Know that every extra moment buys life for the whole day; its imprint guards you, and later aim a mitzvah such as charity at God. *When:* Every weekday morning; even one moment longer counts. *For:* People in business who cannot pray at length on weekdays (from the Mitteler Rebbe). *Not for:* Not a replacement for G01 for those who can do it. `KT·Q31` · `KT·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"A few rushed minutes of prayer don't matter; my day belongs to business."* to **Each extra moment buys life for the whole day; its imprint guards you in the middle of business.** Even a little connection leaves an imprint that keeps him from being far from God and draws him back when he is free. `KT·Q31` · `KT·Q32`

## I feel low and sad over worldly things

Sadness over ordinary worldly matters that comes by itself.

**What is really going on.** The work reads this sadness (following Tanya ch.31) as a reminder from above to do an honest accounting. After that, the person is to gladden himself and pray with joy.

**Program: Bitterness, then joy.** (1) Choose a suitable time: tikkun chatzot (midnight), or when already sad over worldly things. (2) Be alone, never in front of others. (3) Make an honest accounting of thought, speech and deed; let the heart break. (4) Afterwards, gladden the soul with the themes of Tanya ch.31 and 33, and learn Chassidus. (5) Pray with joy. *When:* Only at times for most people; nightly only for tzaddikim. At night, in private. *For:* One who needs to become a vessel through lowliness. *Not for:* Not every night for ordinary people. Fasts are only for one with real sins. Not a method for someone depressed (see cautions). `KT·Q16` · `KT·Q17` · `KT·Q18` · `KT·Q19` · `KT·Q20`

**Reframe.** From *"This sadness over worldly things is just misery."* to **Treat it as a call from above to make an honest accounting, then turn to joy and pray gladly.** The sadness is used for a short, private accounting, and the work then requires turning to joy before prayer. `KT·Q19` · `KT·Q20`

## Cautions

- The bitterness and broken heart practice would harm someone depressed or full of shame. The text itself limits it to certain times, in private, followed by joy; keep all three limits. - Fasts, mortifications and accepting humiliation or rebuke from others (ch.12) must not be offered as practice; they risk self-harm and abuse. - Calling an emotion 'false imagination' can invalidate a person's real experience; the text's test is whether the feeling lasts and changes behavior, so use that, not dismissal. - 'You clear your worries to sleep, so you can clear them for prayer' can shame people with anxiety or insomnia. - The demand that the young submit to elders and received teaching could be exploited by controlling teachers. - Hour-long contemplation presupposes deep learning; the text itself warns beginners will deceive themselves. - Midnight tikkun chatzot and nightly accounting can disrupt sleep and health.
