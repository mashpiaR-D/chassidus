"The holiness of eating" closes the letter *kuf* (holiness). It is long and mostly law: forbidden foods and the Shelah's own stricter rulings (lines 1732-1818), the laws of the Land of Israel (1819-1871), health and danger (1872-1888), and a whole code of the blessings on food taken from his father's *Emek Beracha* (2113-2325). Inside the law sits a strong teaching: eating is where the body and the soul are joined, and a table can be an altar.

## 1.1 The word inside the bread

The key teaching comes from the Ari (line 1728). The philosophers asked why food keeps the soul in the body, since the soul does not eat. The Ari's answer:

> אין לך דבר שאין בו צד קדושה (line 1728)

"There is nothing that has no side of holiness." Every plant has an angel over it that "strikes it and says: grow". That word from above is the food's soul; the visible food is its body. When a person eats, his soul enjoys the food's soul and his body the food's body. So the verse "man does not live by bread alone, but by all that comes from G-d's mouth" means:

> אלא על כל מוצא פי ה', רצה לומר שיש למוצא הלחם פי ה', דהיינו ההשפעה שמכה בו ואומר לו שיצא לחוצה ויגדל, ע"ז יחיה האדם (line 1728)

"The bread has G-d's word coming out in it, the flow that strikes it and tells it to come out and grow; by that a person lives." The Shelah calls it "a wonderful explanation". This is the same teaching the Tanya's Gate of Unity (ch. 1-2) and Chassidus in general build on (the word of G-d in each thing is its life), here in the Ari's own voice, applied to a meal.

## 1.2 Two worlds tied in one knot

In his youthful gloss (line 2068) the Shelah reads Jacob and Esau, born in one womb, as this world and the world to come: opposites that are "held and tied on the side of holiness, and yet separate",

> מכל מקום הם קשורים בקשר אחד בהקב"ה, כי הוא מחיה את כולם ומהווה את כולם (line 2068)

"yet they are tied in one knot in G-d, for He gives life to all and brings all into being." The same holds in the body: the windpipe (breath, speech, the soul) and the gullet (food, the body) sit side by side in one throat.

## 1.3 Blessing fills the earth

From Toldot Yaakov (line 2122): "G-d's blessing fills the earth; without it, it would not stand a moment." So we bless over everything we enjoy, to wake that blessing from above. Before the blessing, "the earth and its fullness are G-d's"; to enjoy it without blessing is to take from the holy (line 2114). After the blessing, "He gave the earth to people". The world is His, and becomes ours by thanking Him.

## 1.4 One place, one aim

The vav of Jacob, spelled out, equals 13, the number of *echad*, one: "all his deeds go to one place, to the Creator's intent" (line 2100). The unity of G-d becomes a unity of aim in the one who eats for Him.

## 3.1 Eating before the King

The central instruction of *Emek Beracha* (lines 1958, 2056): at the table, think that you are eating before G-d, with no wall between. The Shelah's father draws two things from it: holiness and modesty ("awe of Heaven will be on him") and freedom from greed ("one who eats to fill his desire drives away awe and shame"). A person has two souls, the living one that eats food and the thinking one that "eats" the intentions and mitzvot of the meal (line 1958). *Tie:* `before_whom`, `carry`. *Proposed technique:* "Eating before the King".

## 3.2 The order of a meal

The Shelah's own order (line 1913): relieve oneself, wash and say the blessing for the body, confess, pray for one's food, then wash for bread. The prayer for food asks "that G-d pour him the spiritual food that is the root of the food, so that his eating be in holiness" (line 1911). At washing, have the intent of the priest (line 1904). Hold the bread with all ten fingers, the ten sefirot; bread shaped like a *vav* between two hands of five (two *heh*s) with the blessing as the *yud* completes the Name (line 1907). Leave a little of the first bread for the end (line 1908). Before grace: "I am ready to fulfil the command ... for the sake of the unity" (line 1925); wear an outer garment (line 1926); say it aloud, "for the voice wakes the intent" (line 1927). *Tie:* `act`, `lips`, `binding`.

## 3.3 Why He wants our blessing

Reshit Chochmah, quoted at line 1928: G-d needs nothing, "but He desired to be blessed by those below, so that they be ready for Him to pour His good on them". So bless "with longing and love", word by word, remembering that He fed you though you did not deserve it. *For:* love from gratitude. *Tie:* S41, S53.

## 3.4 Stop while still wanting

"While he still wants to eat, let him leave off his desire for the honor of the Creator" (line 1968, from Rabbeinu Yonah). The middle way: a third food, a third drink, a third left empty (line 2039); the manna, "each according to what he eats" (line 1975). *Guard (ours):* the Shelah also uses harsh images to kill appetite (the latrine, the worms, lines 1972-1975); the guide keeps the moderation and the "for His honor", not the disgust.

## 3.5 Eat in joy

"One should not eat the bread of sadness" (line 1994). Eat in joy with what G-d gave, "whether little or much" (line 1999); set even a poor table as if it held many dishes. The joyful eater "knows his Creator" (line 1997). *Tie:* S53, S84. *Proposed technique:* "The glad table".

## 3.6 Feed the poor and say Torah

The table atones "when the Temple is gone" (line 2024): give the poor the best on the table (lines 2047-2049, Abraham gave his guests the three tongues and kept none); if there is no poor guest, say words of Torah; if you are alone, learn alone; even a psalm is better than nothing, and Psalm 23 is good at every meal (line 2046). Words of Torah are "the spiritual bread the soul enjoys" (line 2042). Restoring a poor man's soul "G-d counts as if he had created that soul" (line 1920). *Tie:* S86.

## 3.7 Specific blessings, dear things first

Why say "who creates the fruit of the tree" and not a general blessing? Because the specific blessing "thanks Him for the personal providence with which He watches His creatures to give each one what he needs" (line 2244). And "every blessing depends on how dear the thing is" (line 2129). A French sage used to bless first on the food dearest to him, "to praise my Maker as I love" (line 2240). *For:* S85 (seeing Him in what happens), at the smallest scale. *Tie:* `details`.

## 3.8 Drinking

The Rambam, quoted at line 1987: "happy is one who was never drunk in his life." The Shelah does defend sages who drank at mitzvah meals to open their hearts to Torah (lines 1988-1993), but ends with a warning not to teach it: "one may say *they* drink, not *let them* drink". *Ours:* the guide never recommends drink. It keeps the warning and leaves the defense.

## 3.9 Health as service

The body's health comes first in time, since no one can think of G-d while sick or hungry (line 2003, from Shvilei Emunah and the Rambam). So eating for health is a mitzvah, and "the health of the body ... is a service of G-d". Even so, if sick, pray and "above all, let a person put his trust in the Creator" (line 2022). *Tie:* the guide's body care, framed as service.

## 3.10 Bring yourself into the duty

On coming to the Land of Israel, the Shelah chose to keep the sabbatical year though others found excuses (line 1820): "a person should bring himself into the duty of a mitzvah in order to keep it" (line 1821). Do not look for exemptions. *For:* S83 (accepting the yoke) as eagerness, not burden.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Why the body eats.** Food joins soul and body; the soul lives on the word inside the bread (line 1728). "By this he makes the body holy and joins it to the soul" (line 1891).
- **The point of life.** "The purpose of man's creation in image and likeness was ... to be like Him in all His ways" (line 1891); so eating too must be like His ways, for others and for Him.
- **Providence and trust.** Blessing each food by its kind is thanking Him for care that reaches each creature (line 2244). "He knows G-d will not leave him" even when the purse is empty (line 2074).
- **Joy and sadness.** Eat in joy; sadness at the table is lack of faith (lines 1994-1999). But one sorrow belongs there: to grieve for the ruined Temple at a happy table "G-d counts as if he built His house" (line 1921).
- **Repentance.** The Shelah's youthful gloss ends on Jacob's vow reread: "all my deeds will be for His name and not mine" (line 2111).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **The word in the thing.** The Ari's teaching at line 1728 is the root of the Tanya's Gate of Unity ch. 1-2 and of the Baal Shem Tov's teaching on eating (raising the sparks in food). The Shelah gives it as the reason the soul eats; Chassidus adds that the eater raises the spark by the blessing and the aim. The Shelah comes close: the eater is the priest, the food the offering (line 1904).
- **Eating as service.** The Tanya ch. 7-8 places permitted eating "for the sake of Heaven" on the side that can rise to holiness, and eating for lust on the side that stays in *kelipat nogah*. The Shelah's father and the Tur say the same (lines 1962-1963): even hunger satisfied for pleasure is "not praiseworthy".
- **Health.** Both accept the Rambam's rule that the body must be well to know G-d. Chabad puts more weight on joy and less on diet; the Shelah gives both.
- **Drink.** The Chassidic *farbrengen* uses a little drink to open the heart; the Shelah allows the sages this and then warns. The guide takes neither and never recommends drink.
- **Particularism.** Some rules (non-Jewish wine, bread, cooking; the "unlettered" at the table, line 2050) belong to halachic and social settings the guide does not carry.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **The word in the bread** (line 1728): the plainest image of "given right now" at every meal.
2. **Eating before the King** (lines 1958, 2056), with no wall between.
3. **Stop while still wanting, for His honor** (line 1968): a small, daily act of love, not a diet.
4. **The glad table** (lines 1996-1999): eat in joy and trust, even when there is little.
5. **Specific blessings as thanks for personal care** (line 2244), and **praise what you love most** (line 2240).
6. **Feed someone at your table**, or say one true thing from the Torah (lines 2023-2046).

- About two thirds of the range is halachah: forbidden foods and the Shelah's stringencies, the laws of the Land, and the full laws of blessings.
