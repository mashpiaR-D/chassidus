## The shape of the range

The first chapters proved that the ten sefirot are G-d Himself. This range turns from the One to the one who serves Him. It asks why man was made in the image, why Israel, what the Torah is, what the mitzvot do, and then it names the two great doors of service: fear and love.

1. **Names and the one Name** (140-163). "By My deeds I am called." Ehyeh three times: no change before, now or after. The four-letter Name holds all ten. How to say the Name.
2. **The lower chambers** (164-179). The throne, the halls, the angels: all below the ten, all held by the Name.
3. **Man in the image, to testify and to repair** (180-212). Man is made in the pattern of the upper Man, so that he too will testify to the One and have the power to repair the glory. The world is to be set right through him.
4. **Israel as the key** (213-225). The key of the King's palace is in Israel's hand. The Name is joined to them: "the Name is not whole except with them".
5. **The Torah is the Name** (226-290). The Torah is woven of the Name, "and it is He". The Oral Torah is the lamp for the dark room. The King's treasures handed to the grown son.
6. **The wick of the flame** (291-301). Israel are like the wick that lets the blue light hold to the white.
7. **Fear** (302-334). Outer fear and inner fear. Abraham's fear after the binding of Isaac.
8. **Love** (335-342). Love is unity. The lover takes up the Beloved's work.

## The unity of G-d

**He does not change: "I was, I am, I will be".** "By My deeds I am called": Elokim when He judges, Tzevaot when He wars on the wicked, Shaddai when He waits for a sinner, the Name when He has mercy (line 140). But why is Ehyeh said three times? "I am He who was, I am He now, I am He in the future" (line 146). Ibn Gabbai reads it as a guard: one might think the emanation changed Him, so the Name "that shows oneness and being in one state always, without change" is said for before, during and after. "The alef in it shows oneness" (line 146).

**The ten are one power.** "They are ten that are one power, for the power of each is in the other, and their power is the power of Ein Sof who emanates them" (line 148). The name "atzilut" (emanation) is from "have you not kept aside (אצלת) a blessing for me": the blessing Isaac kept was not a thing apart from his soul (line 148). And "Chochmah is Binah and Binah is Chochmah ... the only difference between them is hidden and revealed" (אין הבדל ביניהם כי אם מצד ההעלם וההגלות, line 154).

> שלא ברא הקב"ה את העולם בשום דבר זולת עצמו ואמתו, והוא הוא החכמה שלו, והוא הוא התבונה שלו, והוא ההדעת שלו (Avodat HaKodesh 1:14:9; line 155)
> The Holy One did not create the world with anything but Himself and His truth. He is His wisdom, He is His understanding, He is His knowledge.

*Ours.* This is the Rambam's "He is the knower, the knowing and the known" (Yesodei HaTorah 2:10), which the Tanya ch. 2 and ch. 48 quote. Ibn Gabbai says it of the sefirot. The Tanya says it of His knowledge that holds the world. The same claim of unity carries both.

**Why emanate at all? So that He be known as One.** The Zohar on "King Solomon made himself a palanquin": before the world "He and His Name were hidden in Him as one", then He made the upper trees "for Himself, for His repair, to show that He is One and His Name One" (line 150). Ibn Gabbai dares a sentence that anticipates Chassidus:

> וכביכול זה היה תקונו וצורך עצמו, והוא כי היה [צריך] להכיר באחדותו הוא ושמו אחד (Avodat HaKodesh 1:14:6; line 152)
> And as it were, this was His own repair and His own need: that His oneness be known, He and His Name one.

**The Torah is the Name.** "The whole Torah, from the bet of Bereshit to the lamed of 'before the eyes of all Israel', is built on the Names of the Holy One and His traits ... so it is divine, and is called the Torah of the Lord, for it is the very Name of the Lord" (line 226). And:

> והתורה נארגת בשם יי' והיא הוא ממש (Avodat HaKodesh 1:21:15; line 240)
> The Torah is woven of the Name of the Lord, and it is He Himself.

So the Torah cannot change, "for it is not in time; it is above time, and it made time" (line 243). The five books are the four letters of the Name (lines 234-238). The Torah is the form of Man, 248 positive and 365 negative mitzvot as limbs and sinews, "so it fitted man, and by it man is man" (ובה האדם אדם, line 241).

*Ours.* This is the root of the Chassidic "the Torah and the Holy One are all one" (אורייתא וקוב"ה כולא חד, F10), which the Tanya ch. 4 and ch. 23 make the key to why learning unites the soul to G-d. Ibn Gabbai is one of its main pre-Lurianic teachers.

## The need on high, continued

**Man was made in the image to testify to the One and to repair the glory.** As emanation was to show His being and oneness, "so the simple will was to create one creature, complete and whole, made in the image and likeness of the pattern of the upper holy Tabernacle", the upper Man (line 180). Why?

> כדי שיעיד גם הוא על אחדות השם המיוחד ... נמצא האדם העשוי בצלם ודמות אות מעיד ומפרסם האלהות (Avodat HaKodesh 1:17:4; line 183)
> So that he too will testify to the oneness of the unique Name ... so man made in the image and likeness is a sign that testifies to and makes known G-dliness.

And "being made of all the traits, in the pattern of the Tabernacle, he will have power in his hand to unify and repair the glory, for that was the intent in him" (line 184). The Zohar on "all that is called by My Name, for My glory I created it": "this glory below ... is only repaired above from within the repair of the people of the world, when they are righteous and pious and know how to repair it" (line 186). "Happy is the man who merits by his deeds to be like this" (line 187).

**All things are to return to the good, through man.** This is the book's "why is there evil":

> רצה יתברך עם זה שכל הדברים ישובו אל שרשם באופן ההוא מהטוב אשר היו בו, ושיתוקן הכל בסוף (Avodat HaKodesh 1:19:7; line 209)
> He willed, with this, that all things return to their root, in that way of good which they were in, and that all be repaired in the end.

> ורצה יתברך שיגיע זה התקון והשלמות על יד האדם (Avodat HaKodesh 1:19:8; line 210)
> And He willed that this repair and completion come about by the hand of man.

Because man is made of good and evil (the two inclinations), he can turn his whole self to good, "until the serpent in him is turned downward", and then even the body merits eternal life (line 209). When body and soul "rejoice to do the will of their Maker, and the body does not burden the soul at all", they rise like Enoch and Elijah (line 210).

**Israel hold the key; the Name is joined to them.** The Yerushalmi's parable: a king had the key to a small palace; lest it be lost he tied a chain to it. So G-d "joined His great Name to Israel, and they live" (line 223). Ibn Gabbai reads: Israel are the key, "handed over to them to open the King's palace or to close it as they will", opening the spring of light for all worlds by mitzvot, or closing it by sin (line 223). Then the sharpest line on the need on high in the range:

> כביכול שאין השם שלם אלא בהם, ולזה אי איפשר בהם החלוף ולא הכליון, כי הם צורך גבוה והגבוה צרכם (Avodat HaKodesh 1:20:13; line 225)
> As it were, the Name is not whole except with them; so they cannot be exchanged or destroyed, for they are a need on high, and the High One is their need.

**The King handed His tools to His son.** "This month shall be for you": while the son was small the King kept His treasures; when he grew, He handed them over (line 264). Six sages give six pictures (lines 270-275), and ibn Gabbai reads each. The clock: time is subject to the Torah, and was handed to Israel "to turn its sign from bad to good" (line 277). The watchman's hut: providence over the world now spreads from Israel's keeping of Torah (line 278). "I sold you My Torah; as it were, I was sold with it" (line 278). The seal: even the upper ones yield to the one who holds it (line 280). The carpenter's tools: the Torah is the tool by which the world was made, "and Israel too have in their hand to act on the matter of the world as they will", as the Talmud says "if the righteous wished, they could create a world" (line 283). The medicine chest: the Torah heals body and soul, and if a person sinned, "the Torah will also counsel him to return to health" (line 288).

**The wick: why the service is asked of Israel.** In the flame there is a white light and a blue one; the blue joins the white above, and holds to the wick below. "Were it not for the wick, there would be no place for this repair" (line 294):

> כן ישראל הם כדמיון הפתילה אל הכבוד שהוא נאחז ומתיחד בה והם מתקנים אותו להאיר (Avodat HaKodesh 1:24:4; line 294)
> So Israel are like the wick to the glory, which holds and is joined to it, and they prepare it to shine.

The service of unifying all to Ein Sof "is the repair of the glory and a need on high, and so He asks it of Israel" (line 293). "For your good" means "their need and the need on high are in their hand" (צרכם וצורך גבוה בידם, line 298). And the blue flame "consumes whatever clings to it below", but Israel cling and live: "you who cling to the Lord your G-d are all alive today" (line 299).

*Ours.* The wick is the best single picture in the range for the guide. The lower one does not add light to the Light; it gives the light a place to hold. It is the Tanya's candle (ch. 35: the Shechinah rests on the deed as the flame on the oil) in an earlier form, and the Tanya uses nearly the same Zohar. F07, F10.

## States of consciousness

**Inner fear: the fear of not being worthy to come near.** Ibn Gabbai's chapter on fear (lines 315-334) is one of the finest in the book. Outer fear is fear of punishment, of judges, of life's troubles. It is good, a door, "for from not for its own sake one comes to for its own sake" (line 319). But "this one fears only for himself" (line 317). Inner fear is different:

> והנה השלם המתעסק ומשתדל תמיד אחר זאת ההשגה ממה שהוא משער מהמעלה הנפלאה בהשגה ההיא, ירא תמיד שאינו כדאי ולא ראוי אליה לגודל מעלתה (Avodat HaKodesh 1:26:10; line 324)
> The whole one, who is always busy and striving after this grasp, from what he senses of its wondrous height, is always afraid that he is not worthy or fit for it, because of how great it is.

From inside: a longing to be near, with awe that one is too small, so one keeps adorning oneself. "His own flesh and all the more his wealth, all is counted as nothing to him" (line 324). It never lapses, "for it does not depend on anything outside" (line 331). He fears as a servant who might be sent out of the King's hall for a blemish, so he always beautifies his soul (line 332). This is S46 (higher awe) described more fully than in most Chassidic texts, and it answers a real question: how can fear and longing be one feeling? A proposed state: **fear of not being worthy** (JSON).

**The middle fear: fear for His sake.** Between them is one who keeps from sin "because he knows that one who sins defiles the Temple above ... separates the Chief of the world", "and his fear is a need on high" (ויראתו צורך גבוה, line 330). This is higher than fear of punishment, lower than love. *Ours.* A state the guide can offer: not "what will happen to me" but "what will this do to Him". It is close to what Chassidus calls fearing to be cut off from His will (Tanya ch. 41), and to S33 (before Whom).

**Abraham's fear after the binding.** Abraham was called "one who fears G-d" only after the binding of Isaac, the tenth test. Why? Because he could have answered G-d: "You said, in Isaac shall your seed be called", and he did not; "I conquered my compassion to do Your will" (line 327). He entered "beyond the line of the law" (לפנים משורת הדין). So inner fear, in its full form, is above love (line 327). *Ours.* This is S61 (will and delight above reason) in a story: the deed done with no reason the mind could accept, out of attachment itself.

**A full chariot.** One who keeps every limb busy with mitzvot, so that no limb is empty, "for the whole rests on the whole, not on the flawed and lacking", becomes a throne: "such a man is a full chariot for the glory, and the glory leaves the upper ones and comes to rest on him below" (line 326). The fathers were the chariot "while they lived" (line 326). *Ours.* This is the Tanya's "the fathers are the chariot" (ch. 23, 34) and its call to make oneself a chariot through mitzvot, stated here as a state of the whole body.

**Love is unity.** "Love of the unique Name is the highest level in holy service, for the whole service is the service of unity ... and love means true unity, and true unity is called love; so love (אהבה, 13) equals one (אחד, 13)" (line 335). The letters of the Name "are joined to each other in love, with no separation, in longing and constant clinging" (line 337). The soul came from the place of love and longs to be like it, "as every kind longs to cling to its kind" (line 337):

> כי מי שהיה למוד ומורגל לשכון במקום האור המאיר והועתק אל החשך יתאוה וישתוקק להעתק מן החשך אשר הוא בו לשוב אל האור (Avodat HaKodesh 1:27:3; line 337)
> For one who was used to dwelling in the shining light and was moved into darkness will crave and long to be moved from the darkness he is in, back to the light.

"There is no service like love of the Holy One" (line 338, from the Zohar). This is S41 (the hidden love wakes) and S13 with a reason: the soul's longing is homesickness for the place where all is joined.

**No split in himself.** "A man must unite himself with his Creator and see no division in himself, for the will was that all be repaired and unified by man" (ולא יראה שום חלוק בעצמו, line 341). Love with both inclinations means bringing even the evil impulse into the love. *Ours.* A deep state for the guide: **undivided**. It is not the absence of the lower pull but its inclusion in the one love. It fits S34 (two voices) as its resolution, and F11.

## Meditation and practice

**Say "Adonai" with the mouth, direct the heart to the Name as written.** "The true servant, when he mentions the Name with his mouth, mentions it by its epithet (Adonai), and in his heart directs to it as it is written (YHVH); by this intention he unifies the Unique One, and this is the goal asked of the servant" (line 163). Adonai is the "gate" (זה השער לה'), the hall in which the Name dwells (line 162). *Ours.* A simple and old practice, still at the base of Chassidic prayer: every time the Name is said, two layers: the Master I stand before, and the Being who is all. Technique proposed: **the gate and the Name** (JSON).

**Write the Name as one.** Ben Kamtzar tied four pens to his four fingers and wrote the Name at once, "to testify to the unity of the great Name" (line 159). One who cannot should write it so that it shows the unity as far as possible, yud joined to its thorn (line 160).

**Fear first, then Torah and wisdom.** "Whoever's fear of sin comes before his wisdom, his wisdom endures" (line 303). One who fears sin "rejoices in what he learns, for it confirms him in the way he is used to" (line 305). "One who has Torah and no fear of heaven is like a treasurer given the inner keys but not the outer ones: how will he get in?" (line 308). The salt (חומטון) mixed with grain to keep it is fear mixed with learning (line 309).

**Adorn yourself with mitzvot for His sake.** "The mitzvot are the fine ornaments and garments the King glories in, and His will is always with the servant who adorns himself with them" (line 332). He wants "His mitzvot, not the reward of His mitzvot" (line 332). "As He is merciful, so you be merciful" (line 333): one was created only to be called by His Name.

**The lover takes up the Beloved's craft.** "It is the way of love that the true lover takes up his beloved's craft and works at it always, without stop, to be like his beloved" (line 339). Abraham took up the work of Chesed: "all the days Abraham was in the world, I did not need to do my work, for Abraham stood in my place" (line 339). He fed wayfarers and brought them to serve G-d. "The goal of the lover's love is to acquire and bring lovers to his Beloved, for he wants all to love Him" (line 340). *Ours.* This is a practice: love of G-d expressed as kindness that brings others to Him. Chassidus holds it in "love your fellow" as the gate to love of G-d (Tanya ch. 32).

**Bless on the bad as on the good.** "A person must bless on the bad as he blesses on the good, as it says: you shall love the Lord your G-d with all your heart" (line 342). Ibn Gabbai: this is to show and make known "the true oneness in all things, to unify them and return them to the true unity" (line 342). *Ours.* Blessing on the bad is not pretending it is good. It is a practice of unity: this too comes from the One. S68 (the hiding seen as His), S85.

## The big human questions

**Why was man made?** To testify to the One, and to repair the glory (lines 183-186). "Man is the end of all that was made in the six days, and the goal of every created thing" (line 188).

**Why do Israel matter?** Because the Name is joined to them; they are a need on high, and so they cannot vanish (lines 223-225). They are "children of the King born in His house" who inherit His treasures (line 289). *Ours.* The guide is universal (D28). What it can take from here without the doctrine of peoples is this: whoever serves is a key the King has put in human hands, and a wick the light holds to. Ibn Gabbai's claims about the souls of the nations (lines 192-193, 219) are his and are left in the text; the guide does not use them.

**Is the world good?** Evil exists so that it can be turned to good by man, "and all be repaired in the end" (line 209).

**Can one stay whole in body and soul?** Yes, if the body follows the soul and "does not burden it"; the Torah is "a drug of life" for both (line 287).

**Fear and love.** Fear is the gate (line 302); love is the unity itself (line 335); and the highest fear, Abraham's after the binding, is beyond both (line 327).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 164-179 (the throne of glory, Akatriel and Metatron, the seven halls, Sandalfon, the ranks of angels) and 189-208 (the limbs of man matched to the sefirot, the image above the head, souls from the inner and outer sides) were read in full. They give the map of the worlds below the ten. Lines 249-261 (the Oral Torah as the lamp in the dark room, the six orders of the Mishnah, with glosses by his son R. Chaim and by R. Yehudah Gedaliah) were read; the lamp is noted above.
