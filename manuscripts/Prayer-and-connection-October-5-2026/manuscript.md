<a id="c01"></a> ## C01 · I understand it, but my heart feels nothing

*18 findings from Tanya, Torah Ohr (part 1 of 2), Torah Ohr (part 2 of 2), Likkutei Torah (part 3 of 3), Kuntres HaHispaalus, Sha'arei Teshuvah, Imrei Binah, Sha'arei Orah, Sha'ar HaYichud, Toras Chaim (part 1 of 4), Toras Chaim (part 3 of 4), Derech Mitzvosecha, Sefer HaSichos (part 2 of 2), Sha'arei Avodah, Pelach HaRimon*

> I've learned it, I can explain it, I even believe it. But when I pray, nothing stirs. It stays in my head and never reaches my heart, and when I try to feel something it feels fake.

**What is really going on.** Every work here treats knowing without feeling as a known condition, not a verdict on the person. Tanya: the love may be real but hidden, never born into the heart; its job is to drive the deed, and it is true even when it feels imagined. Torah Ohr: this world's pleasures and affairs block the passage from mind to heart; most souls start from below, with study, deeds and the voice. The Mitteler Rebbe's works (Kuntres HaHispaalus, Sha'arei Teshuvah, Imrei Binah, Sha'ar HaYichud, Toras Chaim, Sha'arei Orah): the thought was not held deep and long enough, or never carried into the heart. Feeling follows depth by itself. The block is local, sometimes temperament, and the light is hidden, not gone. Derech Mitzvosecha: faith is whole but sits above the mind; contemplation moves the emotions, and love comes unchased. Sha'arei Avodah and the Rebbe Rayatz: understanding that never reaches the heart is not yet understanding.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"If I don't feel it, it isn't real, and my faith must be fake."* → The love can be real while hidden, and true in itself even when it seems imagined. Faith stays whole even in the thief praying at the tunnel. Faith does not move feelings. Attention does. **Where the works agree.** All agree that knowing and being moved are different things, that the gap is common, and that it proves no fraud. Feeling can't be made to order. It follows sustained attention, deeds, or both. The mind's work is real even when the heart lags, and the missing feeling is covered or blocked, not absent. The remedy is steady daily effort in prayer and study over a long time.

**Where they differ.** On order: Torah Ohr puts study and deeds first for most souls, while the Mitteler Rebbe, Imrei Binah and Derech Mitzvosecha put deepened contemplation first, and Tanya lets understanding carry the deed. On brokenness: Kuntres, Sha'arei Teshuvah and Torah Ohr (Mishpatim) want a broken or emptied heart first, but the Tanya reading keeps this person away from self-crushing. On worth: Tanya counts the deed without feeling, while Sha'arei Avodah calls understanding that moves nothing 'mere thought'.

**Order and timing.** First, name it: not fake, not lost. Keep the deeds and study daily, without waiting. For a stable person only, a short grief then joy before prayer. In prayer, hold one idea deeply, aloud where possible; if it stays dry, change the angle or want to want. Remember it through the day, and review with a friend at fixed times. Expect years.

- Loss of all feeling for everything, not only in prayer, may be clinical depression or anhedonia. Refer to care (see C14); do not treat it as a spiritual blockage. - Never give the bitterness, crushing or 'feel your distance' steps to someone depressed, grieving or self-hating. The texts themselves warn that lengthened bitterness is a mistake, and Torah Ohr says the naturally crushed need rinsing, not more crushing. - Deep, single-pointed contemplation can feed rumination in anxious or obsessive minds. Keep it short, and stop if it loops. - Do not quote lines like 'counted as dead' or 'the lowest level' to someone who already fears they are empty. - Don't promise feeling on a schedule, or hint that its absence means unworthiness before God. - For scrupulous people, 'act without feeling' must not become grim self-coercion.

<a id="c42"></a> ## C42 · Alone with it, with no one to pull me out

*2 findings from Sefer HaSichos (part 1 of 2), Hayom Yom*

> I carry my worries alone. There's no one who feels what I'm going through, no older friend to see my faults and pull me out of the mud.

**What is really going on.** The evidence is thin, from two works. Hayom Yom reads 'worry in a man's heart' two ways: put it out of mind, or speak it to others. The Tzemach Tzedek adds that the others must be one with you, people who feel what you are going through. Early Chassidim said the Alter Rebbe's achievement was that neither Rebbe nor Chassid is alone. On livelihood worry, do what is needed and leave the rest to God, awakening trust and gladness. The Rebbe Rayatz remembers older Chassidim who loved a young man, saw his faults and understood his virtues, and helped him crawl out of the mud. He prescribes a daily hour of study with a partner, a friend, being a student, and the farbrengen, where the hide softens. The reader adds his warning that warmth without daily toil in learning and prayer is not enough. Kuntres HaHispaalus (in C01) likewise prizes review with a friend and private guidance.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

- *"I have to carry this alone."* → A worry can be spoken to someone who is one with you and feels what you are going through. The path was built so that no one is alone. `HY·Q14` · `HY·Q32`
- *"No one can see me clearly enough to help me out."* → An elder who loves you can see your faults and understand your virtues together, and help you out of the mud. `SHS1·Q27`
- *"My livelihood rests on me alone."* → Do what you need to do, and God will do what He has to do. Awaken trust and be glad, as if it were already provided. `HY·Q16` · `HY·Q15`

1. **Speak it to someone who feels it** · *from Hayom Yom* (1) When a worry sits in your heart, choose: set it out of mind, or speak it. (2) If you speak it, speak it to someone who is one with you and feels what you are going through, not just anyone. (3) For livelihood worry: do what is actually needed, honestly, and leave the rest to God. (4) Deliberately awaken trust, and let yourself be glad. *When:* Whenever worry sits heavy. The livelihood part belongs to the working day. *For:* Someone carrying worry alone, especially about income. *Not for:* Not for avoiding practical steps a real problem needs. Confide only in someone safe. `HY·Q14` · `HY·Q15` · `HY·Q16` · `HY·Q32`
2. **Friend, student, elder, and gathering** · *from Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz), 5680-5691, Kuntres HaHispaalus (Tract on Ecstasy)* (1) Study one hour a day with a partner at a fixed time. (2) Have a friend, and make yourself a student. (3) Let an elder who loves you see your faults, understand your virtues, and help pull you out of the mud. (4) Review what you learn aloud with a friend, and seek private guidance aimed at your own soul. (5) At the gathering, let the hide soften and make a firm resolution. (6) Don't rely on warmth alone: keep up the daily toil of study and prayer. *When:* An hour a day with a partner. - From *"I'm alone with this; no one feels what I'm going through."* to **The path was founded so that no one is alone. A worry is meant to be spoken to someone who feels it with you.** Isolation is treated as a condition with a remedy, sharing with one who is united with you, rather than as one's fate. *(Hayom Yom)* `HY·Q14` · `HY·Q32` - From *"My livelihood is on me alone."* to **Do what you have to do, and God will do what He has to do.** Dividing the burden into your part and God's part lightens the worry without excusing effort. *(Hayom Yom)* `HY·Q16` · `HY·Q15`

**Where the works agree.** Both works hold that a person is not meant to struggle alone. Healing comes through someone who truly feels with you, sees you whole, faults and virtues together, and helps you act.

**Where they differ.** Hayom Yom offers two paths for worry, setting it aside or sharing it, and adds trust about livelihood. The Rebbe Rayatz stresses structured companionship (partner, elder, gathering) and warns that warmth without daily effort is not enough.

**Order and timing.** First: find one person who feels with you and speak the worry. Then: a fixed daily hour with a partner, and an elder or guide who sees you whole. Periodically: gatherings. Throughout: do your part in practical matters and leave the rest in trust.

- Loneliness with depression or suicidal thoughts needs professional help, not only community. - Confide only in safe people. Abusive or controlling mentors can do harm. - An elder's view of your faults must come with love and understanding of your virtues. Harshness can harm someone already ashamed. - Gatherings with alcohol are not for anyone with drinking problems. - 'Put it out of mind' must not mean ignoring a real problem that needs action. - Trust about livelihood must not replace practical steps.

**Order and timing.** First keep the act, so practice does not hang on mood. At the opening of prayer, want to want. Then give real time to contemplation, step by step. For the steady, a brief heaviness before prayer that turns straight to joy, with Torah study after to hold what returned. The deeper turning of the face belongs to seasons of return.

- Coldness and loss of taste may be depression or anhedonia. The guide should not treat clinical numbness as spiritual laziness. - The bitterness steps (heaviness before prayer, becoming bitter over one's distance) are not for anyone depressed, ashamed or self-punishing. - Kuntres HaAvodah's reading of coldness as unwillingness, and phrases like 'counted as dead' or 'like an animal', can shame a vulnerable person. Do not pass them on. - The plain yoke can become self-coercion in scrupulous or obsessive people, or a way to override grief and trauma. - Coldness that comes from religious trauma, abuse or a crisis of faith is not what these texts address.

**Truths for the guide** *(candidates, each checked against its passages; see CHECKS.md)*

- **What you do without feeling still counts** · A good act done without feeling is still a real act. Feeling is not what makes it count, and what you keep doing on dry days may be the most faithful thing you do. *Check: yes.*
- **Wanting to want is already the start** · When you cannot feel what you wish you felt, the wish itself is the first live coal. Start there rather than waiting for the feeling, and know that coldness means something is hidden, not gone. *Check: yes.*
- **The chill of beginning is not a verdict** · The first stretch of real inner effort often feels colder than the easy glow before it. That chill is the weight of starting, not a sign that you are unsuited, and it lightens with steady practice. *Check: yes.*

**What the core works do not cover.** None of the works treats coldness caused by doubt, loss of faith, burnout, depression or religious trauma. They assume a male prayer setting. They also say nothing for someone who wants to stop observing. LS3:P11 (an external commitment the heart doesn't feel) concerns return more than rote practice, and fits here only partly.
