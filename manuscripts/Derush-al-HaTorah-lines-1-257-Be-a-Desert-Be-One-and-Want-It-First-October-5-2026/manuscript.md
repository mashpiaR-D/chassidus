Derush al HaTorah (A Sermon on the Torah) was given for Shavuot. It reads the verses before Sinai ("In the third month... they came to the desert of Sinai") one by one, and asks: what makes a person able to receive the Torah? The last third is a sharp call to fix how Torah was being learned in his day: review, learn Mishnah first, learn every day, and stop empty sharpness.

Five teachings matter most for the guide:

- **Know your lack.** "A person must know his own lack himself" (48). One who thinks he is whole cannot be completed. "The one who lacks is ready to receive everything" (66).
- **Be a desert.** Torah lasts in one who "makes himself like a desert that all walk on" (75), and who holds himself "as fully simple" (85). Stepping back from the body's cravings is a ladder "to be with G-d and near Him" (74).
- **Be one.** "You are one, and I am One, and the Torah is one" (121). "A thing of oneness fits only one who is also one" (126). In teshuvah "they all have one heart for the One G-d" (120).
- **Truth is His seal because He is One.** Anything with a second is changed from the first; "the thing that is one is full truth" (206). And all views in a dispute have truth "from some side", as all creatures come from the One (209).
- **Want it first.** "Moshe went up", and only then "G-d came down" (182). The whole readiness is love and longing.

## 2.1 He is One, so truth is His seal

"אמת חותמו להודיע כי הוא אחד" (206, 27:2): "His seal is truth, to make known that He is One." Why does truth show oneness? Whatever has a second, that second must differ from it in some way, so the first is not "true" to it. "אבל הדבר שהוא אחד הוא אמת גמור" (206): "but the thing that is one is full truth." So the Torah opens with alef, the one. "האמת אין לו שנוי כלל, אבל הבלתי אמת אין לו קיום" (205, 27:1): "truth has no change at all, but what is not true has no lasting." Take the alef from "emet" and "met" (dead) is left.

## 2.2 You are one, I am One, the Torah is one

The midrash of the letters: the world was made with bet (two), because a world of many is a blessing. The alef was silent until G-d said: "אתה אחד, ואני אחד, והתורה אחת" (121, 15:2): "you are one, and I am One, and the Torah is one." The Maharal draws the rule: "לפעמים הדבר שאין בו רבוי יותר טוב, כאשר הדבר הוא אחד בעצמו" (125, 15:6): "sometimes the thing that has no many is better, when the thing is one in itself." And "כל דבר אחדות לא יאות כי אם לאשר גם הוא אחד" (126, 15:7): "a thing of oneness fits only one who is also one." So even the mitzvot are one: "כי כל המצות הם דבר אחד" (131, 17:1): "for all the mitzvot are one thing," and so one mitzvah pulls another.

## 2.3 All views from the One Shepherd

In a dispute, how can both sides be Torah? "כולם נתנו מרועה אחד" (208, 27:4): "all of them were given by one Shepherd," from "the Master of all deeds". Just as His works differ and even oppose each other: "עם כל זה כלם מן השם יתברך שהוא אחד, וכלם יש בהם האמת מצד" (209, 27:5): "even so, all are from G-d, who is One, and all have truth in them from some side." He is "פועל אמת שפעולתו אמת" (209): "a true Maker whose work is true." Water is true to what it was made for, and so is fire. One view is nearer the whole truth and becomes the law; the others are still not pushed away. So "עשה אזנך כאפרכסת" (208): "make your ear like a funnel." Only a lie a person knows is a lie is not from Him (211-212).

## 2.4 Upper and lower become one

Before Sinai, "the heavens are G-d's and the earth He gave to man" (155), like a king's rule that Romans may not go down to Syria. When he married off his daughter, he lifted the rule. At Sinai "נעשו עליונים ותחתונים אחד" (154, 20:3): "upper and lower became one." "השלום הוא החבור הפשוט, עד שאינם מחולקים עוד" (157, 20:6): "peace is the simple joining, until they are no longer divided." And the world needs this: "אין קיום לחצי דבר" (158, 20:7): "half a thing cannot last." Through learning for its own sake, "יתחברו התחתונים אף עם השם יתברך בעצמו וכבודו" (159, 20:8): "the lower join even with G-d Himself and His glory."

## 2.5 He came near from far

When they came to Sinai in teshuvah, He "בא אליהם ממקום רחוק, מן השמים, להיות להם אלקים קרובים" (120, 15:1): "came to them from a far place, from heaven, to be a near G-d to them." Sin splits people; teshuvah joins them: "אבל כשחוזרים בתשובה יש לכולם לב אחד לאל אחד" (120): "but when they return in teshuvah they all have one heart for the One G-d."

## 2.6 He gives the Torah every day

"השם יתברך משפיע לנו התורה תמיד בכל יום" (174, 23:3): "G-d pours the Torah to us always, every day." That is why the blessing ends "who gives the Torah" in the present tense, and "You grant man knowledge" is in the present. When we are far, the same voice must be louder.

**Proposed MH-S28 "The desert".** Inside: emptied of wants and of self-importance, open ground that anyone may walk on, and so able to receive what has no limit. Three sides of it: - no cravings: "בהסתלקו מהגוף ותאוותיו, הרי יש לו סולם שעולה בו מן הגוף השמימה להיות עם השם יתברך, וקרוב אליו" (74, 8:4): "when he steps back from the body and its cravings, he has a ladder by which he climbs from the body to heaven, to be with G-d and near Him"; - no claim: "אם משים אדם עצמו כמדבר שהכל דשין בו, תלמודו מתקיים בידו" (75, 8:5): "if a person makes himself like a desert that all walk on, his learning lasts in his hand"; - no self as a thing: "שיחשוב את עצמו כאילו איננו דבר ומציאות לעצמו, ושאינו כלום, כמדבר הזה" (105, 12:4): "that he think of himself as if he is no thing and no being of his own, and that he is nothing, like this desert." "כי אין סגולת הקבלה כי אם למי שהוא חסר" (105): "for the gift of receiving belongs only to one who lacks."

A name is a boundary. The Torah "איננה מתקיימת במי שיחשוב את עצמו בעיניו לדבר מה" (84, 9:9): "does not last in one who thinks himself something in his own eyes." Moshe said "What am I?", "והוחזק בעיני עצמו לפשוט גמור" (85, 9:10): "and he held himself in his own eyes as fully simple." How to enter: see 4.1. Map: close to S56 The self quieted, S59 True lowliness and S57 No self to watch; the Maharal's form of bittul. Guard: this is humility, not self-hatred and not neglect of the body's needs.

## 4.1 Become a desert before learning or prayer (`bittul`; new: `mh_become_a_desert`)

Before learning or prayer, take a minute. Three steps from 74, 75, 105: (1) set down one craving for now (food, phone, comfort); (2) set down one claim ("I deserve", "I am the one who"); (3) say "What am I?" (85) and let yourself be open ground. Then begin. The Maharal promises that this is the ladder "to be with G-d and near Him" (74).

## 4.2 Answer for your own lack (`own_words`; with `mh_reckoning`)

G-d told Moshe to answer the angels himself, because "צריך האדם להכיר חסרונו בעצמו" (48, 3:6): "a person must know his own lack himself." "ואם ידמה בדעתו שהוא שלם ואינו בעל חסרון, אין שייך בו השלמה כלל" (48): "and if he imagines he is whole and lacks nothing, there is no completing him at all." One of the 48 ways Torah is acquired is "המכיר את מקומו" (48): "one who knows his place." Practice: say in your own words, once a day, one thing you lack and ask Him for it.

## 4.3 Want it first (`heart_after`; new: `mh_want_it_first`)

"Moshe went up to G-d" before "G-d came down on the mountain" (182). "צריך האדם מצד עצמו תשוקה והכנה אליה קודם שמקבלה" (182, 24:8): "a person needs, from his own side, longing and readiness for it before he receives it." "וכל ההכנה היא כאשר אוהב התורה ותשוקתו אליה" (182): "and the whole readiness is when he loves the Torah and longs for it." Practice: before opening a text, say aloud what you hope to find in it and why you want it.

## 4.4 Love with heart, soul and means (`act`)

"ואהבת את ה' אלקיך בכל לבבך ובכל נפשך ובכל מאודך" (186, 25:4): "and you shall love G-d your G-d with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might." The Maharal reads: heart = mind and thought; soul = deeds (the soul is what acts); might = money. Practice: once a week check all three: did I think of Him, do for Him, give for Him?

## 4.5 Drag it to the study house (`act`; `mh_drag_to_the_study_house`)

"בני, אם פגע בך מנוול זה, משכהו לבית המדרש" (97, 11:4): "my son, if this ugly one meets you, drag him to the study house." "בראתי יצר הרע, ובראתי לו תורה תבלין" (98, 11:5): "I created the evil inclination, and I created the Torah as its spice." The Satan "אינו מתנגד אל האדם רק מצד הגוף" (61, 6:2): "opposes a person only from the side of the body." Guard: this is for pulls and cravings; it is not a treatment for illness or compulsion that needs a doctor.

## 4.6 Review until it is yours (`act`; new: `mh_review`)

Torah is "hard to get like gold and easy to lose like glass" (164). Reviewing is like reaping grain from the soil: it frees learning from the body so it becomes mind (227). The covenant is in the oral Torah because it lives in the person: "והוא הברית וחבור שיש לאדם בעצמו עם השם יתברך" (243, 29:10): "and this is the covenant and joining a person has in himself with G-d." Practice: learn a little every day, "לשקוד על דלתותי יום יום" (236, 29:3): "to watch at My doors day by day," and review it until you can say it without the book.

## 4.7 The funnel (`accept`; new: `mh_ear_like_a_funnel`)

When you meet a view you think is wrong, first ask what side of truth it holds (209). "עשה אזנך כאפרכסת" (208): "make your ear like a funnel." Then choose, without pushing the other away.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why am I never satisfied?** Because the soul is from above and lives below: "גם הנפש לא תמלא" (64, 7:1): "and the soul too is not filled." That lack is its gift: "כי החסר מוכן לקבל הכל" (66, 7:3): "for the one who lacks is ready to receive everything."

**Why must life be work?** "כל אדם לעמל נברא" (34, 1:6): "every person was made for toil." Man is like soil: "נתן השם יתברך הנשמה הטהורה והזכה, חלק אלקי ממעל בגוף האדם, ונשרשת שם ומוטבעת בגוף, כאשר מוטבע החטה באדמה" (33, 1:5): "G-d put the pure and clear soul, a divine part from above, in a person's body, and it takes root there and is sunk in the body, as wheat is sunk in the soil." It must be grown into fruit. "כל עוד היות האדם על האדמה איננו בן חורין, אבל לעמל הוא יולד" (37, 2:1): "as long as a person is on the earth he is not free, but is born for toil." So be "כשור לעול וכחמור למשאוי" (40, 2:4): like "an ox to the yoke and a donkey to the load" for Torah: the body carries without rest, the soul plows deep.

**Why suffering?** The three good gifts (Torah, the land, the world to come) are not of the body, and "היסורין הם הכנה עצמית לקבלת התורה" (7, Introduction 1:7): "suffering is a real preparation for receiving the Torah," because it thins the body's hold. Guard: this is a reading of one's own path, not a reason to seek pain or to explain someone else's.

**Can I serve from love if I must?** Yes. Best of all is "כאשר העבד מקבל גזירה הכרחית ההיא מרצונו הטוב גם כן, ועושה אותה מאהבה" (4, Introduction 1:4): "when the servant accepts that binding decree by his good will too, and does it from love."

**What if everyone around has given up?** "אם ראית שהבריות נתייאשו מן התורה, תחזיק בה ותקבל שכר כלם" (241, 29:8): "if you see that people have given up on the Torah, hold on to it and you will receive the reward of them all."

**Is it too late?** "כל העדר הקודם בנושא מביא ההויה" (245, 29:12): "every lack that comes first in a thing brings being." And "כל דבר שהוא בכח בהכרח יצא לפעל" (256, 31:6): "whatever is in potential must come out into act."

**What makes wisdom last?** Fear of sin. "כל שחכמתו קודמת ליראת חטאו, אין חכמתו מתקיימת" (145, 19:2): "whoever's wisdom comes before his fear of sin, his wisdom does not last." "כי זה כל האדם" (145): "for this is the whole of man." Awe is the end that the whole plan aims at: "תכלית המעשה הוא ראשית המחשבה" (144, 19:1): "the end of the deed is the start of the thought." Love keeps you going when it is heavy; fear keeps what you gained (163). Love is a joining from both sides: "רק כשיתחברו זה בזה וזה בזה" (163, 21:2): "only when this joins that and that joins this."

**Is the mind inside time?** "כל דבר שכלי איננו בגדר הזמן" (107, 13:1): "nothing of the mind is inside the bounds of time." The Torah was given in the "third", the "now" that joins past and future.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **"His seal is truth, to show He is One"** (206). The Tanya does not use this argument, but the Chabad teaching that only the Essence is fully real (and all else has being only from it) fits it: what has a second is not fully true to itself. The Maharal stays with truth and oneness; he does not say that the world is not real.
- **Tzimtzum.** Not present. The nearest idea is 120 and 174: He "came from far" and gives every day, and the distance is on our side (a louder voice is needed only because we are far). This matches the Gate of Unity's "receiver's side".
- **The Rambam's negative attributes.** Not argued here. The Maharal's One is the one with no second, which is close to the Rambam's "One not by number".
- **The Ramak's formula.** Not stated. 209 comes near its spirit: all things, even opposites, are from the One and hold truth from a side, yet none of them is the whole truth.
- **Be a desert** (74-75, 84-85, 105) is the Maharal's bittul. It matches Tanya ch. 6 and the Chabad call to be "like nothing" before G-d. The Maharal's reason is his own: a name bounds, and the unbounded Torah fits only the unbounded.
- **Know your lack** (48) matches the Chabad use of lowliness (shiflut) as the vessel. Chabad adds joy; the Maharal adds that the one who lacks can receive "everything" (66).
- **One heart for the One** (120, 126) is close to Tanya ch. 32 (love of Israel, "one father"), and to the Rebbe's teaching that unity below draws His oneness down.
