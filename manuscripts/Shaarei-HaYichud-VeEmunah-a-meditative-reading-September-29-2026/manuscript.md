## 1. The book as one meditative journey

This book is one long climb and one long return. R. Aharon of Strashelye, the Alter Rebbe's closest student, takes the whole map of the Kabbalah (sefirot, faces, lights and vessels, smallness and greatness) and uses every piece of it to say one sentence: as He was alone before the worlds were made, so He is alone now. Read slowly, the map stops being a diagram of heaven and becomes a way of seeing the room you are sitting in.

**It starts with a warning and a hope (Preface and Introduction).** The author says why he dares to write: the hidden things are needed now, and they were received from his teacher, not invented. He sets two warnings over the whole book. Knowledge counts only when it is felt as life. And Torah is bread, so eat it slowly, in order. Then he gathers the Zohar, the Ari and the early Kabbalists to one point: before creation there is no image at all, and the contraction is 'only in relation to those below'.

**Then the mind is taught to hold two views at once (First Gate).** From our side there are levels, names, a contraction and a line. From His side there is nothing but Him. The gate trains the reader to go out to the details and come back: 'if your heart runs to grasp how they divide, return to the place you were in, that there is nothing besides Him.' It gives the book's hardest words, 'yachid' and 'echad', and then takes even 'yachid' away from the Essence. It ends by telling you to seek His face always, the way one seeks what one does not find.

**Then the traits of God are opened, and closed again (Second Gate).** Kindness, strength, beauty and the rest are shown as one power seen through our vessels. 'As His power is without limit, so His power is in limit, with no difference at all.' Here the book says its sharpest thing about contemplation itself: unity through understanding 'still leaves someone who understands'. So the gate ends with an order of service: first unite the worlds through knowing, then be nullified above knowing.

**Then the faces (Third Gate).** The Ancient One, the Long Face and the Short Face become a portrait of how His care reaches us: an eye that never closes, a forehead of pure goodwill, a patience that waits. The gate turns inward at its end. In this time the work is bitul inside the yesh itself, a gem that shines while staying a gem, and 'there is no other' shows precisely through the voice that says 'I, and none else'.

**Then lights and vessels (Fourth Gate).** The vessels, which hide, turn out to be rooted higher than the light, which shows. A great teacher can hide his wisdom in a parable within a parable; the world is His parable. Four confessions are weighed, and only Moshe's 'there is none else, even in the empty space' is whole. Then the gate names the book's most daring point: the arousal that reaches His Essence does not come from understanding. It comes from overcoming the opposite, in exile, when His face is hidden like a father testing a son.

**Then smallness and greatness (Fifth Gate).** The world has small days, when it runs by nature, and great days, when His intent shows through nature. Which one we live in 'depends on the service of those below'. Man, formed from Havayah and Elokim, joins them. The book ends by turning to the reader. All this knowledge is so that the soul will rise to Him and then keep His Torah, 'for this is all of man', and 'without the unity from man's service, no worlds at all can be described of Him'.

**From where to where.** From a map of heaven to the ground under your feet. From 'He is alone' as a teaching to 'He is alone' as the thing you see. From understanding to what is above understanding, and back down into the yesh, the hard, plain, separate-looking world, where the book says the deepest bitul is now meant to happen.

## Preface 1-8 (why Kabbalah exists, and a Zohar that seems to argue with itself)

- **Where:** Preface 1-8 (why Kabbalah exists, and a Zohar that seems to argue with itself), lines 1-8.
- **The movement:** It puts one thing at the center of all religious life: uniting God with His presence in the world, and it says that union happens only through knowing. Then it lets a real contradiction stand for a while, so the mind feels the problem before it gets the answer.
- **The line to hold** (line 3): > וכל עיקר היחוד הוא על ידי הידיעה > > *And the whole of the union comes through knowing.*
- **Contemplating it:** The author begins by asking why Kabbalah is needed at all. His answer: every mitzvah and every page of Torah is for one purpose, to unite "the Holy One and His Shechinah", God as He is and God as He shows in the world. And that union, he says, is made by knowing. Not by feeling first, not by doing first. "Know today and return it to your heart." But then the problem. God is beyond knowing. And the worlds we see "appear as something, and separate". Human reason, looking at a separate world, cannot find its unity. So the knowing has to be received, handed down "mouth to mouth" from Sinai. Then he sets two sets of Zohar sayings side by side. One says: whoever does not know how to unite the holy Name, better he had not been born. The other says: woe to whoever reveals secrets. Both are true. He does not rush to fix it. Bring it home. You too hold two things. You are told to know Him, and you know you cannot. Sit in that gap for a minute without closing it. The book is written from inside it.
- **From inside:** A seriousness settles in: knowing God is not a hobby for scholars but the root of everything a person does. There is also a strange relief in hearing that reason alone cannot find the unity in a world that looks separate. The failure you may have felt is not yours; it is how the world is built.
- **At the edge:** The text says knowing Him "is not in the measure of grasp at all", so the knowing it asks for has to come from above reason, received. The answer to the contradiction waits for the next passage.
- **Into the day:** Before one mitzvah today, say to yourself: this is for uniting Him with His presence here.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Preface 9-10 (the two kinds of knowing, and Jacob's ladder)

- **Where:** Preface 9-10 (the two kinds of knowing, and Jacob's ladder), lines 9-10.
- **The movement:** It splits knowing in two and hands the first kind to everyone. Then it gives the mind a ladder: rungs that go up and down between the limited and the unlimited, joined by a power the mind cannot picture.
- **The line to hold** (line 9): > וכמו שהיה לבדו מקודם שנבראו העולמות כן הוא יתברך לבדו אחר בריאת העולמות > > *As He was alone before the worlds were created, so He is alone after the worlds were created.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Alter Rebbe answered the contradiction, the author says. There are two knowings. One comes before awe and gives birth to love and awe. That one is for every Jew. The other, the secret Names, is for the few. What is the first knowing? To know that He is really in all the worlds, from the top rung to the bottom, and yet "utterly simple", with no relation to them at all. We know that He is there. What He is, we do not know. Now the line. Before anything was made, He was alone. Now there are skies, seas, cities, you. And He is still alone. The worlds do not add to Him, do not change Him, do not make Him many. Then the picture: Jacob's ladder, standing on the earth with its head in heaven. The rungs are the minds and feelings of each world. Contemplation climbs them from below; His flow comes down them from above. What joins the top to the bottom is His "power of wonder" (pele). The picture breaks there: a real ladder is wood joined to wood, but nothing can join the unlimited to the limited except a wonder. Look around your room. Everything in it came after. And He is as alone now, in this room, as He was before there was a room.
- **From inside:** The room does not vanish. It gets quieter, as if it were standing inside something older than itself. The text says that when all the rungs are joined, "His Essence is revealed, that there is none besides Him", and God "stands over" the ladder like a king.
- **At the edge:** The joining of limit to no-limit is "wonder", which the mind can name but not picture. And His "what" stays unknown: "His existence in the worlds, but His essence is not known."
- **Into the day:** When you walk into a room today, say: He is alone here as before it was built.
- **Ladder:** L03

## Preface 11-14 (love, awe, and seeing the King)

- **Where:** Preface 11-14 (love, awe, and seeing the King), lines 11-14.
- **The movement:** It asks an honest question: how can you love or be in awe of what you cannot see? Then it shows that awe is a kind of seeing, and that only understanding the unity brings the King close enough to be seen.
- **The line to hold** (line 13): > שאינו רואה בכל הנפעלים בעולמות דבר ומהות בלעדו חס ושלום רק שהכל הוא עצמותו ברוך הוא > > *He sees nothing in all that is made in the worlds as a thing or being apart from Him, God forbid, but that all is His Essence.*
- **Contemplating it:** Love needs knowing what you love. Cleaving means "two beings stuck together until they become one." But He is unlimited and we are limited. So how can there be love, or cleaving, at all? The text lets the question stand. Then awe. Yirah (awe) has the letters of re'iyah (seeing). A man standing before the king, seeing his greatness, is ashamed and shrinks to nothing. A man who only heard about the king is afraid of his punishments, but he does not shrink. The king is too far. So too here. Believing in a general way that the worlds are nullified to God leaves the worlds looking solid. That brings only outer fear. Only understanding the unity, seeing that "there is nothing besides Him", brings the King near enough to be seen. And what is seen? Not a king above the world. That all of it, all that is made, is His Essence. The line refuses the philosophers' trap: either His Essence is in the world and has become physical, or it is not and the world is outside Him. Kabbalah says: His Essence is drawn into the worlds and is not changed at all. Now look at your own hands. Do you see them as a thing on their own? Or can you let them be what is made, with nothing in them apart from Him?
- **From inside:** A shyness, the text's own word: bushah, being ashamed before greatness, the way a person goes quiet in the presence of someone great. The self does not get hurt; it gets small, and the smallness feels right.
- **At the edge:** The mind reaches the dilemma it cannot solve: how His Essence is in the world and unchanged. The text calls this true only by the received unity, "precisely His Essence drawn into the worlds, and yet not changed".
- **Into the day:** Look at one person today and remind yourself: nothing in them is apart from Him.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Preface 15-16 (love as two breaths joined; the duty "each by his measure")

- **Where:** Preface 15-16 (love as two breaths joined; the duty "each by his measure"), lines 15-16.
- **The movement:** It gives love a body: two breaths joined in a kiss until they are one. Then it widens the circle so that no one is left out: every Jew must try to know the unity, each by his own measure, on good days and bad.
- **The line to hold** (line 15): > וכד האי רוחא כלול באחרא וההוא אחרא כלול בהאי אתעבידו תרין רוחין כחדא > > *And when this breath is held in the other, and that other held in this one, the two breaths become as one.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Zohar says love is "breath cleaving to breath", like a kiss. Four breaths, each held inside the other, until two are one. That is what love of God is meant to be: not liking Him from a distance, but being held in Him. So the Shema comes before "and you shall love". First you think through "One", and love comes out of it. Then a kind aside. Love that comes from simple faith is also "whole service". But love born of understanding is higher, "as a speaking being is higher than an animal". And people differ. There are days you can think deeply and days you cannot. In these late generations everyone comes mixed. Then the goal of all of it. The Holy One is His Essence. The Shechinah is His showing in the worlds. Seen from outside, they are "truly two opposites". Israel's service joins them. Bring it to your own day. When you can think, think. When you cannot, serve by faith. Neither is wasted. The text says: "You are not required to finish, nor free to stop."
- **From inside:** The picture of breath inside breath is intimate and quiet. It suggests that love of God is not a strong feeling you have to produce, but a closeness where the edges between two lives soften.
- **At the edge:** The union of the Essence with its showing is two opposites made one. The text says this union is the work, and that the deeper knowing, the Names and palaces, belongs only to those whose whole service is "to give pleasure to their Maker".
- **Into the day:** On a day you cannot think deeply, say one Shema slowly and let that be your service.
- **Ladder:** L03

## Preface 17-22 (the king who opens all his treasuries to those outside)

- **Where:** Preface 17-22 (the king who opens all his treasuries to those outside), lines 17-22.
- **The movement:** It turns the reader's lowness into the reason for the teaching. Because we are far, we are shown more. It moves the mind from shame at distance to wonder at being let in.
- **The line to hold** (line 22): > לכן לא יתפעלו כי אם באריכות הביאור אופן היחוד ממקורו ושרשו בפרטים גדולים > > *So they will not be moved except by a long explanation of how the unity works, from its source and root, in great detail.*
- **Contemplating it:** A king has many treasuries. His close friends may each enter one room only, the one he trusts them with. But for the people standing outside, he opens every treasury, so that they will see his glory and be awed. They cannot steal; the doors are locked. A second picture. Someone near a light needs only a small light; a big one would hurt him. Someone far away sees nothing by a small light. He needs a great one. A third. Isaiah was like a city man who sees the king often and is not amazed by details. Ezekiel was like a villager who sees the king once and is moved by every button and ribbon. To the far-away, you have to describe everything. Then the line. In earlier generations, souls were close, and a small hint of the unity moved them. Today souls are far, physical, busy. They are moved only by long, detailed explanation. That is why the book is long. Take it personally. If you feel far, you are the villager. The detail is for you. Every step you think through is a room the King opened because you are outside.
- **From inside:** A shift from embarrassment to gratitude. The far place becomes a gift, the reason the doors were opened. The text says the purpose is that "seeing His greatness and beauty, they will be moved to nullify their 'something' and be united with Him".
- **At the edge:** The passage does not reach the edge; it prepares the long road. It says the closest ones once entered "the King's palaces" in real grasp, and that today the doors are locked.
- **Into the day:** When something about God seems too detailed to matter, say: this room was opened for me.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Preface 23-32 (the chain of teachers, and honey eaten to excess)

- **Where:** Preface 23-32 (the chain of teachers, and honey eaten to excess), lines 23-32.
- **The movement:** It shows a light passed hand to hand, from the Ari to the Besht to the Maggid to the Alter Rebbe. Then it warns the mind against its own appetite: wanting to hold everything in human reason loses even the little it had.
- **The line to hold** (line 30): > אז די לנפשו להתקשר אל הוי"ה ולבטלה אליו יתברך על ידי תורתו הקדושה > > *Then it is enough for his soul to bind itself to God and to nullify itself to Him through His holy Torah.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Ari opened the wisdom but sealed it in riddles and pictures of a body. Later Kabbalists took it almost literally. Then the Besht began to explain it with parables of soul and body, "from my flesh I see God", so that His unity could be "grasped in the heart of each one". The Maggid added light; the Alter Rebbe spread it like the sun. Then the sun set. His words stayed on paper, and paper is not a living voice. Then the picture. "Found honey? Eat what you need, lest you be full and vomit it." A little honey feeds you. Try to eat your fill and you lose even the little. So with Torah. If a person learns deeply and knows it is far beyond him, and believes there are depths he cannot grasp, that is enough to bind him to God. If he wants to hold it all in his own reason, he loses it. Now your own study. Where do you want to be full? Where can you stop at "enough", and let what you cannot hold still bind you?
- **From inside:** A lighter grip. There is freedom in not needing to own the whole of it. The little that is taken with awe stays, and it binds.
- **At the edge:** The Torah has "deep things that are not in the measure of grasp at all". The text says binding and bitul come precisely from knowing that, not from grasping more.
- **Into the day:** Stop one study session today a little early, and say: enough for my soul to be bound.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Preface 33-40 (the first condition on a parable: know the root)

- **Where:** Preface 33-40 (the first condition on a parable: know the root), lines 33-40.
- **The movement:** It slows the mind down before it builds any picture of God. It asks the mind to go to the root first, and it shows why: above and below are really one thing, so only one who knows the root can find a true picture below.
- **The line to hold** (line 35): > כי באמת אצלו יתברך הכל הוא בחינה אחת > > *For in truth, with Him, all is one level.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Besht and his students made "handles" for the wisdom: parables, so the mind can hold on. But parables are dangerous. The author sets three conditions. This is the first. Whoever wants to explain God's wisdom with a parable must first stand at the root of that wisdom, as received from a teacher. Only then can he find a true parable, in nature or in how the soul lives in the body. Why? Because "the lower world is like the upper world". Everything above has its match below. And the reason is the line: with Him, all is one. So the scientists, who argue with each other and cannot make even one gnat, cannot build a parable of heaven from nature. They do not know nature's root. Solomon could speak three thousand parables because he stood at the root. Bring it home. When you picture God, whose picture is it? One you received, or one you made up? Hold your picture more loosely. Let it wait for the root.
- **From inside:** A kind of carefulness, like handling something fragile. The mind becomes humble about its own images, and more curious about where the true ones come from.
- **At the edge:** The root of nature is His "power of wonder": "wondrous are Your works". The mind cannot reach it by searching, only by receiving.
- **Into the day:** Notice one picture you use for God today, and ask: where did I get this?
- **Ladder:** none

## Preface 41-50 (the second and third conditions; the handles of the basket; "that we do not know")

- **Where:** Preface 41-50 (the second and third conditions; the handles of the basket; "that we do not know"), lines 41-50.
- **The movement:** It lets the mind use pictures and then takes them away again. The picture is allowed only for how He fills the worlds, never for what He is, and even there it holds at only one point. It ends the mind at its own edge: the purpose of this knowing is not to know.
- **The line to hold** (line 50): > וכל תכלית הידיעה בזה הוא אשר לא נדע > > *And the whole purpose of knowing here is that we do not know.*
- **Contemplating it:** Second condition: a parable may describe how He extends into the worlds, never what He is. The soul fills the body with "no limb empty of it", as He fills the worlds with "no place empty of Him". That is allowed. His Essence has no parable. Third condition: never match the parable on every side. The soul and the body come from different places. Heaven and earth come from nothing, by His word still standing in them. A man is one person in many powers; that much holds. But a man's moods change, and He does not change. Then a picture of the picture. The Torah was like a basket without handles until Solomon made parables as handles. The handles let you lift it. But the basket is what matters, and the handles serve it. Then the line. Whoever knows more understands better that He is beyond knowing. Every name we give Him is how the worlds look to us. Bring it to your own mind. Pick up your best picture of God. Use it to lift the thought. Then set it down and stay with what it was lifting.
- **From inside:** A quiet that comes after thinking hard, not instead of it. The text describes the one who explains by parable "imagining he has grasped how it is above", and the one who has truly learned knowing he has not. What opens is the second: a clear, unanxious not-knowing.
- **At the edge:** This passage lives at the edge. Of the Essence there is no parable, no grasp, "no question at all". What remains is faith and the not-knowing that the whole study aims at.
- **Into the day:** When you catch yourself sure about how God works, add quietly: and more than that, I do not know.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Preface 51-67 (why write now; received, not invented; read slowly and in order)

- **Where:** Preface 51-67 (why write now; received, not invented; read slowly and in order), lines 51-67.
- **The movement:** It turns the reader into a slow reader. It asks for labor over every word, no skipping, and it promises that even the reader who follows little will come away with one plain truth.
- **The line to hold** (line 66): > שאין זולתו יתברך והוא נמצא בכל העולמות ומתייחד בהם באופן דלית אתר פנוי מיניה > > *That there is nothing besides Him, and He is present in all the worlds and united with them, so that there is no place empty of Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** The teacher is gone and his words are on paper. People are busy with making a living, and their minds are not free. A busy reader might take the parables literally. So the author writes, to guard the reader. He says none of it is his own. For almost thirty years he served his teacher and labored over each word he heard, until he reached its root. If he did not understand the first time, he understood the second. Then his request. "Read it with depth of mind, and weigh every word." Whoever reads quickly, "at first glance, on the run", he testifies, will get nothing. Whoever labors will find that each time he goes deeper, he sees he needs more labor. And do not skip from gate to gate. And for the one who understands little, the line: he will still come away knowing that there is nothing besides Him, and no place empty of Him. And from that, love and awe. Bring it to your table. What in your day do you read "on the run"? Choose one thing to read the other way, slowly, word by word.
- **From inside:** The book's pace becomes your pace. Something unhurried opens, a willingness to stay with one sentence longer than is comfortable. The text promises that "the gates of light will open" to the one who studies this way.
- **At the edge:** These understandings "all rest on faith that is above human reason", yet they are deep reasonings, "as darkness beside light" compared to ours. The mind is asked to work hard toward what its work cannot reach.
- **Into the day:** Read one sentence of Torah today three times, slowly, before moving on.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Preface 68 (the first warning: knowledge is only knowledge when it is felt as life)

- **Where:** Preface 68 (the first warning: knowledge is only knowledge when it is felt as life), lines 68.
- **The movement:** It moves knowing from the head into the life of the soul. It says plainly that understanding which does not live in the heart is not called knowledge at all.
- **The line to hold** (line 68): > כי עיקר שם הידיעה היא כשנתפסת ונרגשת בנפשו בבחינת חיות ממש > > *For it is only truly called knowing when it is grasped and felt in the soul as real life.*
- **Contemplating it:** The first warning: the whole aim of this book is to be moved to serve with love, awe, self-giving and bitul. Knowing is the road there. What counts as knowing? Not holding the idea. Knowing is when it is "grasped and felt in the soul as real life". The verse says "know today and return it to your heart", precisely the heart. And the unity itself is "above understanding". It is grasped and felt in the divine soul of every Jew, because that soul's root is above wisdom and understanding. Da'at (knowledge), the Alter Rebbe taught, is the life of the feelings. "By knowledge the rooms are filled", the text reads: the rooms of the heart. So bring it close. What do you know about God that you have never felt? Pick one. Stay with it until it is something you live, not something you have.
- **From inside:** A warmth that is also a wakefulness. The truth stops being a sentence and starts being a presence. The text's own word is chayus, life: the knowing becomes like breath you do not have to think about.
- **At the edge:** The unity is beyond understanding, but not beyond the soul. The text says its root is "above wisdom and understanding", and there the unity can be grasped and felt.
- **Into the day:** Take one thing you know about God, and say it aloud once today as if you meant it.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Preface 69-74 (the second warning: Torah is bread; prayer comes first; the closing prayer)

- **Where:** Preface 69-74 (the second warning: Torah is bread; prayer comes first; the closing prayer), lines 69-74.
- **The movement:** It humbles contemplation before the plain law, and then it humbles the law before a living soul. It puts contemplation, study and deed in their order, each needing the others.
- **The line to hold** (line 71): > אבל בתורה ומצות בזה הוא גילוי רצונו יתברך ממש ואיהו ורצונו חד > > *But in Torah and mitzvot, this is the revelation of His very will, and He and His will are one.*
- **Contemplating it:** The second warning: do not make this book your main study. Your fixed study should be Talmud and law, to know what to do. Why? Torah is called bread. A soul is higher than bread: it thinks and feels, and bread is only grain. Yet bread keeps the soul in the body. So bread must come from a higher root than the soul it feeds. So too here. The life a person feels in contemplation is higher on the surface. But it reaches only as far as each person's measure. In Torah and mitzvot is His will itself, and He and His will are one. The plain deed reaches further than the finest feeling. But bread feeds only the living. A dead man cannot eat. So Shema and prayer come first, to wake the soul, and then all day, study and mitzvot feed it, like a man who wakes and then works all day for his bread. Bring it to your own morning. First wake the soul in prayer. Then eat the bread of Torah and do the deed. Neither replaces the other.
- **From inside:** A sense of order and calm. Nothing is in competition. The prayer is the waking, the study is the meal, the deed is the will itself. The author closes asking only that his words "enter the heart" and wake it toward "their Father in heaven".
- **At the edge:** The unity "as it is with Him" is not grasped at all, not even in the best contemplation. What reaches it is the deed, where His will is openly there and He and His will are one.

## Introduction 10-19 (the early Kabbalists: not even "one")

- **Where:** Introduction 10-19 (the early Kabbalists: not even "one"), lines 84-93.

- **The line to hold** (line 88): > כי שם מספר לא יפול אלא על דבר שהוא ישות ולא על דבר שהוא אין אלא שהוא השואת הכל > > *For a number applies only to something that is a "something", not to what is "nothing"; rather, it is the evenness of all.*

## First Gate, ch. 1 (the simple and the composite)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 1 (the simple and the composite), lines 97-105.
- **The movement:** It takes away the picture of God as a bundle of powers, even hidden ones. The mind is left with one bare fact: there is no power besides Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 105): > אשר אין כח זולתו והוא לבדו מבלי התערבות כח אחר חס ושלום ואפס בלעדו > > *That there is no power besides Him, and He is alone, with no other power mixed in, God forbid, and nothing apart from Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** The gate opens with its rule: no name, sefirah or image that implies division may be said of Ein Sof, or even thought, "even in potential and hiddenness". What remains is to believe that all is from Him, and to unite all beings to Him. Then it builds slowly. "Composite" means two things joined, like a horse and its rider. Everything we know is composite: stone, plant, animal, person, all mixed from fire, air, water and earth. Every difference in the world comes from the mix. Even "simple" water is only simple compared to the rest. Boil it enough ways and it turns to stone, the text says. And the soul is one power; its many forms, thinking, feeling, walking, show only through the body. Now apply it to Him. His simplicity is not like water's. It is not one power winning over hidden others. Nothing else is in Him at all, not even hidden. To place created powers in Him even deep in hiding "makes Him a master of many powers, and there is no greater sin". Look at your own life, made of many pulls and parts: moods, wants, fears. He is not like that. Not a stronger version of you. Alone, with nothing mixed in.
- **From inside:** A clean, uncluttered feeling, like a room after it has been emptied of old furniture. The mind stops trying to picture God as a character with traits. What is left is quieter than any picture.
- **At the edge:** The text says we may not even "think about it, or raise it in the heart" with any inquiry. What is left is faith: "only to believe that all is from Him". The mind is at its edge from the first line of the gate.
- **Into the day:** When you notice two pulls in yourself today, say: in Him there is no mix.
- **Ladder:** L09

## First Gate, ch. 2 (the ten sefirot as vessels that hide)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 2 (the ten sefirot as vessels that hide), lines 106-116.
- **The movement:** It answers the question the mind has been holding: if He is simple, where does all the difference come from? It moves the difference from Him to the vessels, and leaves Him equal in all things.
- **The line to hold** (line 110): > ומצד עצמותו שוה התמשכותו יתברך בשמים ובארץ ובכל חילוקי דרגין בהשואה גמורה > > *And from the side of His Essence, His drawing-forth is equal in heaven and on earth and in every difference of level, in complete equality.*
- **Contemplating it:** The question is sharp. He is simple. Nothing is outside Him. So where do fire and water, hot and cold, high and low, come from? And how can there be limit from the unlimited? The answer is the ten sefirot. They are called "vessels", to hide His light in each thing in its own way. From their side, the world looks limited. The picture is the soul in the body. The same soul is in the brain and in the foot. In the brain it shows as thought, in the heart as feeling, in the foot as walking. The proof it is one: the moment you decide to walk, the foot walks, with no delay. A limp does not make the soul limp. A second picture: clothes. The same man looks different in fine clothes and in rags. He does not change; only how he shows. So He shows through kindness or strength as a person shows through a garment. Now bring it to your body. Your mind decides, and your hand moves at once. Feel how the one life runs the whole of you equally. Then widen it: heaven and earth, high and low, equally held by Him.
- **From inside:** A levelness. The high moments and the low ones, the holy place and the kitchen, are held with the same fullness of Him. The difference is real in how things look, and it does not reach Him.
- **At the edge:** The sefirot are "without what": they have no being of their own. Why He is simple and yet shows as many is "a thing not in the power of the mind to grasp".
- **Into the day:** When you move your hand today, notice how quickly it obeys, and say: one life, equally in all.
- **Ladder:** L04

## First Gate, ch. 3 (Moshe saw the unity in every creature; even "unity" is from our side)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 3 (Moshe saw the unity in every creature; even "unity" is from our side), lines 117-128.
- **The movement:** It shows a way of seeing: looking at each creature and seeing its unity, not its separateness. Then it takes even the word "unity" away from Him, and leaves the mind at the doorway of what has no trace.
- **The line to hold** (line 120): > שהיה מביט בכל הנבראים יחודו יתברך > > *He looked into all creatures and saw His unity.*
- **Contemplating it:** The sefirot are "vessels", but do not think they are really separate from Him. There is nothing besides Him, so the vessels too are not other. Before Him they are nullified "like the ray of the sun in the sun". Then Moshe. The verse says he "beheld the image of Havayah". What did he see? He looked at every creature and saw it from inside, as it is before God, united. He did not look at it "from the side of the creatures", where it hides and looks separate. But the text will not let the mind rest even there. The word "unity" only fits where we see division: many levels, and yet one. Of His Essence even "unity" does not fall, "before One, what can you count?" The Zohar says Ein Sof "has no trace at all". A wise man's idea leaves a trace in his mind before he speaks it, because he is made of parts and needs to reach outside himself. Ein Sof needs nothing, reaches for nothing, and holds no trace. Try Moshe's seeing. Look at the cup in front of you. First see it as a separate cup. Then try to see it as held, united, from inside. Then let even that picture go.
- **From inside:** A double motion: the world softens into oneness, and then the oneness itself goes quiet. The text's image for the sefirot is sunlight in the sun: bright, and yet not there as anything of its own.
- **At the edge:** Ein Sof is "hidden of all hidden ones", past every level called "hidden". "No question applies to Him at all." The text says "enough for one who understands".
- **Into the day:** Look at one ordinary object today as Moshe would: held and one, not separate.

## First Gate, ch. 4 (new from nothing; a will that does not change)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 4 (new from nothing; a will that does not change), lines 129-140.
- **The movement:** It brings the mind to a wall it cannot climb: a will that does not change, a thing and its opposite in one. Then it shows that "from nothing" means the mind cannot see where it came from.
- **The line to hold** (line 139): > אבל אצלו יתברך שהוא למעלה משכל כל רעיון יכול להיות כל הדברים מעצם ונושא אחד > > *But with Him, who is above every thought and every mind, all things can be from one essence and one subject.*
- **Contemplating it:** The sefirot are as new as creation itself. They were not hiding in Him, waiting. The Ramak's picture: fire in a flint, not there as fire, yet struck out of it. But the author heard from his Rebbe that even this fits only Keter, the highest sefirah. Of Him Himself, not even hidden fire. The sages say "when it rose in His simple will". "Will" is to say the world is not by chance. "Simple" is to say it is not a will like ours, which changes and wants what it lacks. Reason cannot hold it: a will, and yet no change. "A thing and its opposite in one subject." The line says: with Him, it can. Then a picture of "from nothing". Kindness comes out of wisdom; you can see where it came from. But if a stone came out of a thought, you could not say the stone was hiding in the thought. There is no path. That is what "from nothing" means: we cannot grasp where it came from. Take one thing near you and ask where it came from. Follow the answers back, step by step. Somewhere the path ends in a wonder you cannot follow. That end is not a gap. It is the place of His power.
- **From inside:** A sense of wonder that is not confusion. The mind admits it cannot hold two opposites, and there is peace in the admission. The text names that place pele, wonder: the root of creation "is not in the measure of anything".
- **At the edge:** The mind reaches "a thing and its opposite in one subject" and stops. The text says: "Ein Sof, no question applies to Him, no thought or gazing of the mind at all; only to believe that all is from Him."
- **Into the day:** When you meet something you cannot explain today, say: this is where His wonder is.

## First Gate, ch. 5 (what may be asked: "Who created these?")

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 5 (what may be asked: "Who created these?"), lines 141-146, 148-153.
- **The movement:** It gives the mind permission to ask, and then shows what the asking is for. The question "Who created these?" is not a request for information. It is a way of dissolving the things back into their Source.
- **The line to hold** (line 142): > והשאלה הוא בכדי לבטלם לאין סוף יתברך > > *And the question is in order to nullify them to Ein Sof.*
- **Contemplating it:** "Do not search out what is too wondrous for you; contemplate what you are permitted." What are we permitted? What comes from Him. We see that all things are made by Him. The contemplation is that all of them come from "the truth of His being", and to unite them with their source. The sages say: you may ask from the day God created man on earth, and "from one end of heaven to the other". You may not ask "what is above". The author reads the end of heaven as Binah (understanding), where the question "Who created these?" lives. The question is real. How do limited things come from what has no limit? But it is asked "to ask about their source, not to know by reason how they were made". The question is itself a way of bowing. The Zohar says: "Trust in God until 'ad'." - **From inside:** The question stops being restless. It becomes a way of holding things lightly, like picking up an object and feeling its weight go somewhere else. The text's word is bitul: things set down before their Source.
- **At the edge:** Above Binah is "what is above", about which no question may be asked. The text says the main contemplation there is "that there is no change in Him at all, from before the world was created to after", held by faith above understanding.
- **Into the day:** Pick up one object today and ask, slowly: who created this? Then set it down.

## First Gate, ch. 6 (R. Azriel's perfection; the wise man and the smallest child)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 6 (R. Azriel's perfection; the wise man and the smallest child), lines 154-161.
- **The movement:** It shows that God's limitlessness must include limit, or it would be lacking. The mind is stretched to hold both at once: in limit, without limit.

- **Contemplating it:** Someone asks R. Azriel: why ten sefirot at all? Why not just Ein Sof? He answers: Ein Sof is perfection without lack. If He had power without limit but no power in limit, He would be lacking. So He must be "in limit, without limit". The picture is a perfect sage. - **At the edge:** Limit and no-limit are "truly two opposites". The text says they are equal only in His Essence "that is not in the measure of showing", and there the mind cannot follow.

## First Gate, ch. 7 (the tzimtzum, the line, the breath, the breaking and the repair)

## First Gate, ch. 10 (in limit without limit; the Ark that took no space)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 10 (in limit without limit; the Ark that took no space), lines 180-184.
- **The movement:** It holds the mind in a place where two opposites are both true, and gives it a picture from the Temple: the Ark that stood in a room and took up no room.
- **The line to hold** (line 184): > מקום ארון אינו מן המדה > > *The place of the Ark is not part of the measure.*
- **Contemplating it:** R. Azriel's answer again, read slowly. If He has no power in limit, He is lacking. But if the limit is really "in" His power, even hidden, then His power is limited. So: "in limit, without limit". The limit comes from Him; it is not in Him. The sefirot are called tools. They make lack and fullness, "the things that divide one thing from another". Yet they are not separate: "He and His causes are one". "This is not in the mind of man to grasp: how two opposites are in one subject." The sages give a picture from the Temple: "the place of the Ark is not part of the measure". The eye saw the Ark standing in its place, and yet it took no place. The philosophers call that impossible. The text says: He is able, because He is above the mind. So too the worlds: they stand, and He is not measured by them. Look at a thing on your table. It takes up space. Now try to hold, at the same time, that before Him it takes no space at all. Do not make one of them false. Hold both.
- **From inside:** A kind of spaciousness. The room is full and yet there is more room. Many find the Ark picture lets them stop choosing between "the world is real" and "only He is real".
- **At the edge:** The text calls the power that holds both "Keter", "the mediator between the Emanator and the emanated", and says: "this is above the mind of every thought".
- **Into the day:** When you are stuck between two things that both seem true, say: the Ark took no space.
- **Ladder:** L08

## First Gate, ch. 11 (egg and chick, the rotting seed; Keter as the joiner of opposites)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 11 (egg and chick, the rotting seed; Keter as the joiner of opposites), lines 185-194.
- **The movement:** It shows the mind a moment in nature it cannot explain, when an egg is no longer an egg and not yet a chick. It names that moment "ayin" and "wonder", and uses it to picture how limit comes from the limitless.
- **The line to hold** (line 186): > ובלשון המקובלים נקרא בחינה זו בשם כתר דהיינו כח המחבר שני הפכים ביחד ומאחדם באחדות נפלאה > > *In the language of the Kabbalists, this is called Keter: the power that joins two opposites together and unites them in a wondrous oneness.*
- **Contemplating it:** A seed rots in the earth before it sprouts. An egg stops being an egg before a chick forms. Until the first thing is gone, the second cannot come. But at the moment the egg is gone, is it nothing? If it were nothing, why does a chick come, and not something else? It is there and not there. The text calls that "ayin": like something you are looking for that is surely here, and you cannot find it. That moment is wonder. Philosophers call it "hyle". Kabbalists call it Keter: the power that joins two opposites. So limit comes from the limitless through a power that holds both. And to let the limit appear, the limitless must hide. A teacher must hide his great wisdom to reach a small student. Then the picture breaks. The egg is gone once the chick is there. His will does not come to be or pass away. "In truth all is He alone." Not even a soul-power may be placed in Him; "the God of Israel is the great unity with nothing besides Him". Think of a change in your own life, a moment between who you were and who you became. There was a point where you were neither. That point is not empty. It is the place of His wonder.
- **From inside:** A respect for in-between moments. Waiting, not-yet, the seed in the dark: these stop feeling like nothing and start feeling like the place where He joins what cannot be joined.
- **At the edge:** "This is beyond the heart of man to know fully, because He is not in the measure of knowledge and mind." The text says Keter joins the opposites; what Keter is, "is He alone".
- **Into the day:** In a moment of waiting today, say: this in-between is His wonder.
- **Ladder:** L08

## First Gate, ch. 12 (wisdom from nothing; the hair and the lamp)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 12 (wisdom from nothing; the hair and the lamp), lines 195-200.
- **The movement:** It brings the mind close to a light, and as it gets close the rays disappear: there is only the lamp. It teaches that looking closer at the many shows the One.
- **The line to hold** (line 198): > כד יקרבון למנדע לון לא שכיח אלא בוצינא בלחודוי > > *When they come near to know them, there is found only the lamp alone.*
- **Contemplating it:** Wisdom did not lie hidden in Him, waiting. It is new, "from nothing". The philosophers need a first matter holding all forms. Our Torah says: "Wisdom comes from nothing." Its source is not found. The Idra's picture: the Ancient One is like a lamp. Rays spread from it on every side. But when you come near to know the rays, you find only the lamp. A second picture: hair. Hair grows from the head, yet it is not the brain. Cut it and nothing of the soul is lost. So the traits He "grows" do not change Him, and yet He is one with them. The chapter ends: "only to believe, with faith above the mind, that all is drawn from Him", and yet does not touch His Essence. Try it with your day. There are many rays: tasks, people, worries. Come near to one of them, slowly. What is it made of? What gives it being? Come nearer, and let it lead you to the lamp.
- **From inside:** A focusing. The scattered feeling of the many rays gathers toward one center. It is not that the rays are false; up close they are the lamp.
- **At the edge:** The text says: "woe to whoever stretches out his hand to the holy beard", that is, to ascribe even "desiring kindness" to Him as a trait. The mind is stopped at "faith above the mind".
- **Into the day:** When your day feels scattered, pick one task and do it slowly, as a ray that leads to the lamp.
- **Ladder:** L03

## First Gate, ch. 13 (Keter is black before the Cause of causes; "wise, but not with a known wisdom")

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 13 (Keter is black before the Cause of causes; "wise, but not with a known wisdom"), lines 201-212.
- **The movement:** It brings the mind to the edge of the laws of nature. Every law has a reason, but why this law and not another is not known. Wisdom itself is "found", like something you were not looking for.
- **The line to hold** (line 212): > לכן נקרא מאין תמצא בבחינת מציאה דייקא > > *Therefore it is called "found from nothing", precisely as a find.*
- **Contemplating it:** Keter is "pure, clear light", and yet "it is black before the Cause of causes". It is the first showing, and every showing is a kind of darkness next to Him: "He made darkness His hiding place." "You are wise, but not with a known wisdom." A man's wisdom tells him why he needs a thing. God needs nothing, so His wisdom does not force anything. The text's own example: scientists know that this is hot and that is cold, this pulls and that pushes, and they can work with it. But why this nature was set in this thing, they do not know at all. It could have been otherwise. So wisdom "is found from nothing", like a person who finds a find he was not thinking about. There is no path back to where it came from. Look at one plain law in your day: water flows down, bread fills you, fire warms. Know it well. Then ask why it is so and not otherwise. Let the question stay open. That openness is where the text says wisdom is "found".
- **From inside:** Ordinary things start to look found rather than owed, like gifts. There is a quiet surprise in water running downhill.
- **At the edge:** The wisdom behind the showing "is hidden from the eye of all living". "The nine palaces are not lights, not spirits, not souls, and no one stands in them."
- **Into the day:** Once today, when something works as it always does, say: found, not owed.
- **Ladder:** L10

## First Gate, ch. 14 (Havayah does not change; the flame in the coal; run and return)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 14 (Havayah does not change; the flame in the coal; run and return), lines 213-216.
- **The movement:** It teaches the mind a motion. Run toward grasping how the many come from the One; when you cannot, return to the place you stood: there is nothing besides Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 216): > אם רץ לבך להשיג איך הוא התחלקותם שוב למקום שהיית בו דהיינו שאין זולתו > > *If your heart runs to grasp how they divide, return to the place you were in, that is, that there is nothing besides Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Name Havayah means He brings all into being. Its four letters are the levels. And yet: "I, Havayah, have not changed." All the levels, and He is as simple as before creation. The Sefer Yetzirah's picture: the sefirot are "like a flame bound to a coal". In the coal the flame is not seen as a flame. Blow on it and it lights many candles. The picture breaks: a flame, once caught in wood, leaves its coal. The sefirot never leave Him. Another picture: "their look is like a flash of lightning". It shows for a moment, and when you try to look at it, it is not there. So the text gives a practice. Your heart runs to grasp how the one power divides. It cannot. Then return: go back to the place you stood before, that there is nothing besides Him. Run and return, ratzo vashov. Try it now. Let the mind run: how can all these different things come from One? Follow it until it tires. Then come back, without effort, to the one plain sentence.
- **From inside:** The mind stops fighting its own limits. The running is not a failure; it is half of a rhythm. The returning is rest, not defeat.
- **At the edge:** How the divided are one is "one of the wonders of the Creator, with no grasp or understanding in any mind". The returning is to faith: "to believe that all is one, with no division."
- **Into the day:** When a thought about God tires you today, return to one plain sentence: there is nothing besides Him.
- **Ladder:** L07

## First Gate, ch. 15 (Keter as a crown on the head)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 15 (Keter as a crown on the head), lines 217-221.
- **The movement:** It gives the mind a picture for what is between God and the world: a crown, counted with the king and not the king. Then it says every combination in the sefirot is "from our side".
- **The line to hold** (line 219): > כמו למשל הכתר כשהוא על ראשו של אדם הוא בכלל האדם ונחשב ממהות האדם אבל מהות עצמו אינו ממהות האדם > > *As a crown on a man's head is counted with the man and reckoned as part of him, yet in itself it is not of the man's being.*
- **Contemplating it:** Every showing needs someone to show to, and a reason, and a way. None of that applies to Him, alone and simple. So the first showing is "new", and it is called Keter, the crown. The picture: a crown on a king's head. When he wears it, it is part of the king. But the crown in itself is not the king. So Keter faces down, toward the worlds, and is counted with them; in itself it is His own equal power. Below Keter, in the sefirot, are "vessels", tools in the craftsman's hand. And yet: "He, His life and His causes are one." The vessels too. Then the rule: all the combinations the Kabbalists describe, "this sefirah joined to that", are "from our side". "He has not changed and will not change: as He was before creation, simple, so He is after." Think of your own "crown": your name, your role. It sits on you and is counted with you. It is not you. Now think of Him wearing the whole world like that.
- **From inside:** A lightness about roles and titles, your own and others'. They are worn; they are not the wearer. The world too is worn by Him, and He is not the world.
- **At the edge:** "What there is no power in the mouth to say, nor in the mind to think, how it is." The text stops at the edge of speech.
- **Into the day:** When someone uses your title today, remember: a crown is worn; it is not the king.
- **Ladder:** L04

## First Gate, ch. 16 ("a single Master with no second": yachid and echad)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 16 ("a single Master with no second": yachid and echad), lines 222-224.
- **The movement:** It gives the mind two words and a clear difference. "Echad" is one made of parts joined; "yachid" is alone, with nothing besides. It lets the mind feel the difference between unity and aloneness.
- **The line to hold** (line 223): > כנודע החילוק שבין יחיד לאחד כי תואר יחיד הוא דבר שהוא יחיד לבד ואין זולתו > > *As is known, the difference between "yachid" and "echad": "yachid" describes something that is alone, with nothing besides it.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Sefer Yetzirah says: the sefirot have no being, their end is fixed in their beginning, like a flame bound to a coal, "for the Master is single (yachid) and has no second. And before One, what do you count?" The difference is plain. Yachid is like Isaac, "your only son": there was no other. Echad is two things joined, as Ezekiel was told to join two sticks "and they became one in your hand". Echad also is a number, and a number has a second. The sefirot are called one, echad: many that are one. The will, Keter, is yachid: alone in its drawing-forth. The sefirot have no being of their own because He has no second. Hold the two words side by side. Echad: the many held together. Yachid: nothing to hold together. Which one does your mind reach for when you say "God is One"? Now look at the people you love. Together you are echad, many joined. Before Him, even that joining is held inside a oneness with no second.
- **From inside:** A deepening, like a floor dropping lower. "One" first feels like harmony, then like aloneness, not lonely, but whole, with nothing outside.
- **At the edge:** The next chapter says even "yachid" is said only from the side of drawing-forth; of Him as He is, nothing, "only by negation". The mind is taken to the last word and told it too falls short.
- **Into the day:** When you say Shema today, feel for a moment the word under "One": alone, with no second.
- **Ladder:** L09

## First Gate, ch. 17 (not even "yachid"; "without end", not "without beginning")

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 17 (not even "yachid"; "without end", not "without beginning"), lines 225-227.
- **The movement:** It takes away the last word the mind was holding. Even "alone" is said only from the side of His drawing-forth. Of Him as He is, only denial. The author says this knowing is the root of all understanding of the unity.
- **The line to hold** (line 225): > אין לתאר בו יתברך שום שם אפילו בחינת יחיד כי אם על צד השלילה > > *No name may describe Him, not even "yachid", except by way of denial.*
- **Contemplating it:** Last chapter gave the word "yachid", alone. Now the text says: that word belongs to Keter, His will, alone in its drawing-forth. "The Only One of the world": there is a world, and He is alone in it. But of Him as He is, not drawing forth at all, "not even yachid". Every name says something. He is not a something to be named. So He is called "Ein Sof", without end, because what comes from Him can go on without end. But not "without beginning", because "beginning" would treat Him as a being that could have one. Even "first" and "ancient" are said only by denial: first, as no other is first. A picture: a great sage's wisdom is "hidden" from a child; both are wisdom. But you cannot say a thought is "hidden" from a stone. So He is not even "hidden" from us. "Hidden of all hidden" is itself a denial. Sit with the word you use for God. Say it once. Then say: not even that. Do not replace it with another word. Let the space after the word be the prayer.
- **From inside:** A silence that is not empty. Many find that after all the words fall away, something remains that did not need them. The author says he spent so long here "because this knowing is the root and main thing in understanding His unity".
- **At the edge:** This is the edge itself. Beyond "yachid" there are no words, only negation. The text does not describe what lies beyond; it only keeps every word from pretending to reach it.
- **Into the day:** Once today, after saying "God", pause a moment in silence before going on.
- **Ladder:** L09

## First Gate, ch. 18 ("hidden" and "stored away"; providence down to the smallest worm)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 18 ("hidden" and "stored away"; providence down to the smallest worm), lines 228-232.
- **The movement:** It distinguishes two kinds of unknown: what is shown but not understood, and what is not shown at all. Then it brings Him down, in plain providence, to the smallest creature in the sea.
- **The line to hold** (line 231): > והוא יתברך נמצא בכל פרט מריש כל דרגין עד שילשול קטן שבים בהשגחה פרטיות על כל דבר ודבר > > *And He is present in every detail, from the top of all levels to the smallest worm in the sea, with particular providence over each and every thing.*
- **Contemplating it:** Kabbalists speak of ten sefirot "stored away" in Him. Do not think there is any division in Him, even hidden. The text separates two words. "Satum", closed, is for what shows but cannot be understood: a deep idea, or a will that has no reason. "Ganuz", stored away, is for what does not show at all, like a king's treasure: everyone knows he has one, but no one knows where. So the ways all creatures are held in His power are "stored away". We may not look for them in Him. Asking there would already picture Him as divided. And yet, in the same breath: He is present in every detail, down to the smallest worm in the sea, with particular providence over each thing. He brings each into being, divides them, holds them. Think of the smallest creature you have seen today, an ant, a gnat. He is watching over that, in particular. Now bring it to yourself, to the small things in your day. None is outside His particular care.
- **From inside:** Tenderness toward the small. The greatness that cannot be searched and the care that reaches the worm are the same greatness. Many find it both humbling and comforting: far beyond reach, and closer than anything.
- **At the edge:** How all things are in Him is "stored away": "just as His essence is not known, so it is not known how things are in His power". Therefore "it is forbidden to ask about it at all".
- **Into the day:** When you see a small creature today, say: He is watching over this one in particular.
- **Ladder:** L04

## First Gate, ch. 19 (the three Ancient Ones: what may be asked, and what may be known)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 19 (the three Ancient Ones: what may be asked, and what may be known), lines 233-237.
- **The movement:** It gives the mind a map of three kinds of contemplation: one where no question is asked at all, one where you ask without knowing, and one where you ask and know. And it says that all of them are to bind the self to Ein Sof.
- **The line to hold** (line 233): > כי אדרבה כל עיקר ההתבוננות ועבודתינו הכל הוא לקשר לאין סוף יתברך > > *On the contrary, the whole of contemplation and our service is to bind to Ein Sof.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Zohar speaks of three Ancient Ones. One "does not stand to be asked at all". One "stands to be asked, but not to be known". One "stands to be asked and to be answered". The first is Him as He is. No question. But the text is careful: that does not mean never think of Him. "On the contrary, the whole of contemplation and service is to bind to Ein Sof." The Zohar says the cleaving of priests, Levites and Israel reaches "up to Ein Sof". You bind to Him, without asking what He is. The second is His power to bring forth levels. You may ask: how does the One give rise to the many? You will not know. The asking is to nullify. The third is Atzilut, the order of the sefirot. There you ask and you know: "Know today that Havayah is God, in heaven above and on the earth below; there is no other." Here the knowing is a command. Try all three in one sitting. Begin with what you can know: this world is from Him, and there is no other. Then ask how the many come from the One, and let it stay unanswered. Then simply bind yourself to Him, with no question left.
- **From inside:** The mind finds its place. It can work where work is asked, wonder where wonder fits, and be quiet where only binding is left. The text calls the last "to hide in that hiding that is not grasped and not known".
- **At the edge:** "When you look at them, all three are one." From our side there are three; from His, "there is none besides Him". Above the Malchut of Adam Kadmon, "it is forbidden to speak at all".
- **Into the day:** Tonight, before sleep, bind yourself to Him in one sentence, without asking anything.
- **Ladder:** L07

## First Gate, ch. 20 (no colors in Atzilut; "He sees and is not seen")

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 20 (no colors in Atzilut; "He sees and is not seen"), lines 238-245.
- **The movement:** It clears the mind of pictures of the upper worlds as colors and shapes. What is left is a Seer who is not seen, and in the holiest chamber, one will reaching to Ein Sof.
- **The line to hold** (line 238): > כי אין שם לא מראה ולא גוון רואה ואינו נראה המתגאה על כל גאה > > *For there is no appearance and no color there: He sees and is not seen, exalted over all that is exalted.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Ari warns against those "tall in stature" who say there are sights and colors in the upper worlds. "There is no appearance and no color there." White for kindness, red for strength: these are names of powers, not paint. "Who is so foolish as to think colors are in Atzilut", the author asks, "where you cannot even say 'spiritual'?" The colors show only in Beriah and below, through Malchut, which gives each power a form "for us". "He sees and is not seen." He sees every division and detail. He is not seen as divided. In the Zohar's highest palace, the Holy of Holies, there is "a will that is never grasped", and then "all is one will, up to Ein Sof". A picture: all a man's thoughts and words come from his soul, yet in the soul itself they are not divided. So all the levels hang on Him. Close your eyes for a moment and see the dark behind them, without color. Now remember: He is not that dark either. He sees you now, and is not seen.
- **From inside:** A feeling of being seen without seeing back. Many describe it as exposure that is not frightening, like being known completely by someone who is kind.
- **At the edge:** In the Holy of Holies, contemplation is "complete nullification, with no division at all", "not cleaved to and not known". The text says it is enough for one who understands.
- **Into the day:** When you pray today, say one line knowing you are seen and not seeing.
- **Ladder:** L10

## First Gate, ch. 21 (seek His face always: seeking in order not to find)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 21 (seek His face always: seeking in order not to find), lines 246-250.
- **The movement:** It turns seeking into a way of life. At the highest level you seek Him precisely so as not to find Him, and the not-finding keeps the seeking alive. Lower down, you seek and do find.
- **The line to hold** (line 247): > לכן נאמר בקשו פניו תמיד דייקא כאדם שמבקש איזה דבר תמיד מחמת שאינו מוצאו > > *Therefore it says, "Seek His face always", precisely: like a person who always seeks something because he does not find it.*
- **Contemplating it:** The sages say: "Do not search what is too wondrous for you; do not probe what is covered from you; contemplate what you are permitted; you have no business with hidden things." The author reads each phrase as one of the three levels. Of Him as He is: do not even search where things come from. "Flee to your place." Of His power to bring forth: you may seek, and you should. But seek so as not to find. "The end of knowing is that we do not know." "Seek God and His strength; seek His face always." Always, because if you found it, you would stop seeking. Of Atzilut: "From there you will seek God, and you will find." Here you do find: all is from Him and there is no other. Think of someone you love and cannot fully know. You keep looking for them, in their face, their words. You never finish. That is not failure. That is how love stays alive. Seek His face like that.
- **From inside:** Seeking stops being frustration and becomes a kind of faithfulness. The text's picture is simple: a person looking for something all the time because he has not found it. The looking itself is the bond.
- **At the edge:** Of the Essence, "you have no business with hidden things": not even to think in the way of not-asking. The hidden is a "contradiction" to our levels, "what the heart cannot think".
- **Into the day:** Say once today, slowly: I seek Your face always.
- **Ladder:** L08

## First Gate, ch. 21, the gloss inside line 251 (the tzimtzum is not literal: a star, and a finger's shadow)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 21, the gloss inside line 251 (the tzimtzum is not literal: a star, and a finger's shadow), lines 251.
- **The movement:** It takes the most famous picture of Kabbalah, God "contracting Himself", and says taking it literally is a grave sin. Then it gives two pictures that make the contraction ours: a star that twinkles only from far away, and a shadow cast by a finger, not by the paper.
- **The line to hold** (line 251): > כי הצל לא נראה כי אם מצד האצבע אבל לא מצד הנייר כלל > > *For the shadow is seen only because of the finger, not because of the paper at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** R. Chaim Vital wrote nothing above the lowest level of Adam Kadmon, and the Alter Rebbe praised him for it. Some later Kabbalists tried to describe what is above, and read the contraction literally, "as if He contracted Himself". That, the author says, "is a grave sin", forgiven only because it came from not understanding. All the contractions are "for us". A great sage narrowing his wisdom to a child's lesson seems to go through many steps. For the sage it happens in one moment, and the small lesson does not hide his wisdom from him. The Ari's students gave two pictures. A star seems to twinkle and shift colors when we look at it from here. Go to where it is, and it is plain light, without any motion. And: hold your finger over white paper. A shadow appears on the paper. Take away the finger, and the paper is white. The shadow came from the finger, not from the paper at all. Try it, if there is paper near you. The finger is our side, our standing-place far from the source. The paper is His light, never darkened. What we call hiding is our finger.
- **From inside:** A release from the idea that God pulled away. The hiding stays real to us, but it is not a wound in Him. There is a lightness in seeing that the shadow was never on the paper.
- **At the edge:** "The whole matter of the contractions is from our side; toward Him there is no contraction at all." What He is, without contraction, cannot be described even as "equality".
- **Into the day:** Put your finger over a white page once today, and say: the shadow is from my side.
- **Ladder:** L05

## First Gate, ch. 22 (every name is from His works; before Him, darkness is as light)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 22 (every name is from His works; before Him, darkness is as light), lines 252-255.
- **The movement:** It gathers everything so far into one rule: every name and every contraction is from our side. Then it gives a verse that turns the darkness itself transparent.
- **The line to hold** (line 254): > אשר אין צמצום לפניו יתברך וקמיה כחשיכה כאורה > > *For there is no contraction before Him, and before Him darkness is as light.*
- **Contemplating it:** The rule: nothing may be said or thought of Him that implies change. He is "known from His works". When a work looks like wisdom, we call it wisdom; when it looks like kindness, we call it kindness. Of Him in Himself, no name. "I, Havayah, have not changed." Not even Havayah, the Name that brings things into being, changes in bringing them. Every contraction implies change, "so the contraction is seen only from our side". Some, the author says, place the contraction before creation and read it literally, "as if, to create His world, He withdrew Himself". That, he says, is to understand Kabbalah "with great corporealization". "Before Him there is no contraction, and before Him darkness is as light." The Zohar and the Ari do speak of what came "before" creation. But they speak from creation's side, to find the root of each power and join it to Him. "The upper things are known from the lower." Think of the darkest hour you remember. It was dark to you. Now hold the verse over it: before Him, that darkness was as light. Not that it was not dark. That it hid nothing from Him.
- **From inside:** A quiet steadiness about hard memories. The dark does not have to be explained away. It is held inside a seeing that was never darkened.
- **At the edge:** How the powers are in Him "is not known at all". The mind is left with the verse, not an explanation.
- **Into the day:** When the lights go out tonight, say: before Him, darkness is as light.
- **Ladder:** L05

## First Gate, ch. 23 (the soul in the body: closing the eyes does not lessen sight; the surrounding soul)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 23 (the soul in the body: closing the eyes does not lessen sight; the surrounding soul), lines 256-259.
- **The movement:** It turns to the reader's own body for a picture. The soul is one power; its many forms show through the body's vessels. The limits are in the vessels, not in the soul. And the soul's deepest presence is not inside any limb but surrounding the whole body.
- **The line to hold** (line 259): > כי אם יסתום אדם עיניו לא יהיה מזה חסרון לכח הראות שבכח נפשו > > *For if a person closes his eyes, it causes no lack in the power of sight that is in his soul.*
- **Contemplating it:** We call a person wise, kind or strong by what they do. But the soul itself is one power. It shows as thought in the brain, sight in the eye, action in the hand, each according to the vessel. The limits are in the vessels. A strong man may not be wise because his mind is a small vessel. The soul itself has no more or less. Close your eyes: the power of sight in the soul is not lessened at all. Yet we credit everything to the soul, because flesh by itself does nothing. How all the powers are in one simple soul "is not known even in the soul of man". That is called the soul's will, and wonder: "He does wonders, joining the physical and the spiritual." And the deepest part: the soul does not live in one place. It "surrounds" the body from head to foot, equally, not as a showing. That is called makif, the surrounding. Close your eyes now for a moment. Nothing of your sight is gone. Open them. Now feel your whole self, not in one place, held from all around. That is a picture of the worlds held by Him.
- **From inside:** A sense of being more than your limits. The weak spots are vessels, not you. And the life in you is not squeezed into one organ; it holds the whole of you from around.
- **At the edge:** The makif, the surrounding, is "precisely not a showing". It "is not revealed in any place". The text uses it as a picture; next it will say where even this picture fails.
- **Into the day:** When you feel limited today, close your eyes for a moment and say: the power is not lessened; only the vessel.
- **Ladder:** L04

## First Gate, ch. 24 (the contractions are only in the lower worlds; all seen in one glance)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 24 (the contractions are only in the lower worlds; all seen in one glance), lines 260-262.
- **The movement:** It applies the soul picture to God and the worlds. The contractions are needed and real for creation, and yet He is equal in heaven and on earth, and sees all creatures in one glance.
- **The line to hold** (line 262): > וכולם נסקרים בסקירה אחת > > *And they are all surveyed in a single glance.*
- **Contemplating it:** Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah, the lower worlds, are like the body. His light fills them, keeps them alive, each creature in its own way. Since nothing is outside Him, every difference must be "in His power". How, is a wonder. The contractions make room for creatures. Without them, His endless light would leave no place for anything. But in His power itself there is no contraction. "He is equal in heaven and on earth." "No place is empty of Him." The Alter Rebbe taught three contractions, one for each lower world. The author holds that creation truly needs them. But what they are "with Him" is not known. "He forms their hearts together", and the sages say "they are all surveyed in a single glance". A great sage seems to go through many steps to reach a child. For the sage it happens in a moment. Think of everyone you know, near and far. You can hold only one at a time in your mind. He sees them all in one glance, each in particular. Include yourself in that glance.
- **From inside:** Being seen without being one of a crowd. The single glance is not a blur; it is each one, all at once. Many find this calms the fear of being forgotten.
- **At the edge:** How the contractions are "with Him" is "not known at all". The text says we call them contractions "because there is no other name".
- **Into the day:** Before sleep, say: I am in His one glance tonight, with everyone.
- **Ladder:** L04

## First Gate, ch. 25 ("He is the place of the world"; He grasps all and nothing grasps Him)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 25 ("He is the place of the world"; He grasps all and nothing grasps Him), lines 263-267.
- **The movement:** It marks where the soul picture fails and turns the failure into a deeper picture. A soul enters a body that was made before it. He does not enter the world; the world is in Him, and cannot hold Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 264): > איהו תפיס בכולהו עלמין ולית מאן דתפיס ביה > > *He grasps all the worlds, and there is none who grasps Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** The soul picture fails. The soul feels what happens to the body. And the body is made separately, so the soul can enter it. "The Holy One cannot be said to spread inside the worlds like a soul in a body." There is nothing outside Him to enter. So the sages say: "He is the place of the world, and the world is not His place." He is where the vessels are. The vessels do not hold Him. "He grasps all the worlds, and none grasps Him." He holds each thing in being, in detail. But the worlds do not grip Him, do not change Him. The Rambam's "He is the knower, the known and the knowing". In a man, the knower and the known are separate. With Him, the known things, even the vessels, "are also His Essence". The author adds: even "knowing" is said only from Atzilut down. Look at the room you are in. You think of God as in the room. Turn it around: the room is in Him. He is its place. Rest in that for a minute.
- **From inside:** The feeling of looking for God inside things turns into the feeling of being inside Him. It is like realizing you have been swimming in what you were looking for.
- **At the edge:** "How two opposites are in one subject... no mind can grasp it, for He is not in the measure of knowing." The root of "place" itself is "from the Head that is not known".
- **Into the day:** When you enter a room today, say: this room is in Him.
- **Ladder:** L04

## First Gate, ch. 26 ("light of offspring"; bitul with and without grasp; "to Him, not to His traits")

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 26 ("light of offspring"; bitul with and without grasp; "to Him, not to His traits"), lines 268-273.
- **The movement:** It shows two kinds of bitul: one without understanding, and one through it. Then it settles an old question. We cling through His traits, but we call to Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 273): > אבל באמת קריאתינו הוא אליו יתברך דהיינו אין סוף המתייחד בהם > > *But in truth our calling is to Him, that is, to Ein Sof, who is united in them.*
- **Contemplating it:** What we see below is "light of offspring": not the light itself but a likeness, the way a teacher gives a student not his own mind but a copy fitted to the student's size. Each world is a likeness of the one above. Chochmah (wisdom), in the Kabbalah, is "the creatures' nullification in His Essence, without grasp or understanding". Binah (understanding) is their nullification "through grasp and understanding". Two bituls: one simple, one thought through. Then the question. The sages say: "Can one cleave to Him? He is a consuming fire! Cleave to His traits." And they also say: "To Him, not to His traits." Which? Both. We cling through the traits, His kindness and strength as they reach us. But the call goes to Him, "Ein Sof, who is united in them". When you say "You" in a blessing, notice where the word lands. It passes through what you know of Him, His kindness, His care, and goes past them to Him.
- **From inside:** A clarity in prayer. The attributes are the road, not the address. Many feel their prayer becomes more direct: grateful for the kindness, but speaking to the One who is kind.
- **At the edge:** Above Atzilut "we have no business", "His essence is not known". The calling goes to Him, and the text does not say what He is.
- **Into the day:** In one blessing today, say "You" and let it pass through His kindness to Him.
- **Ladder:** L07

## First Gate, ch. 27 (the seeming contradiction about asking)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 27 (the seeming contradiction about asking), lines 274-277.
- **The movement:** It takes a real contradiction in the sources, where some say no asking even in wisdom, and others speak of higher still, and resolves it in a way that frees the mind. You ask through His traits, in order to bind; you do not ask into their root.
- **The line to hold** (line 275): > כי בבחינת שאלה וידיעה בכדי לאתקשרא ולאתדבקא ביחודו יתברך הוא דוקא על ידי מדותיו > > *For asking and knowing, in order to bind and cleave to His unity, is precisely through His traits.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Zohar says even Chochmah and Binah may not be gazed at. Yet the Zohar itself speaks of them, and of Keter, and higher. The Ari says above a certain level "it is forbidden to speak", and yet speaks of that level. It looks like a great contradiction. The answer. Asking and knowing, done to bind yourself to Him, go through His traits, as they act in the world. "He is known from His works." What you may not do is ask how the traits are inside Him, where they came from, as if you could understand it. The sources speak of higher levels only to show that the traits are from Him and yet He has none. "Not by understanding at all." The picture again: you cannot call the mind the "source" of a stone. It is too far. So He is not even the "source" of the vessels in a way we could understand. We describe steps, "contractions", so the mind has a way to speak. Bring it close. When you think about God, aim the thinking at binding, not at owning an answer. Ask, and let each question tie you tighter, not make you the judge.
- **From inside:** Relief from the fear that thinking about God is forbidden, and from the pride that it can finish. The asking becomes a rope, not a ladder you climb to look down.
- **At the edge:** The inner Adam Kadmon, His "first thought" in itself, "is forbidden to speak about". The text calls it "in order to deny traits of Him, and yet the traits are from His Essence".
- **Into the day:** When a question about God comes today, add: and may this bind me to You.
- **Ladder:** L07

## First Gate, ch. 28 (the grades of unity are our measuring; binding all levels, and the unity rises by itself)

- **Where:** First Gate, ch. 28 (the grades of unity are our measuring; binding all levels, and the unity rises by itself), lines 278-281.
- **The movement:** It ends the gate on what a person can actually do. We cannot know Him as He is. We can bind every level to Him. And when we do, the unity rises by itself to Ein Sof.
- **The line to hold** (line 281): > ממילא יעלה היחוד הזה בבחינת אין סוף בעצמותו יתברך > > *By itself this unity will rise to Ein Sof in His very Essence.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Kabbalists describe grades of unity: in the traits, in the mind, in the Father and Mother, in the Crown, each one closer. Do not think these are changes in Him. They are our measuring, from where we stand. The soul picture one last time: the traits join to the mind, the mind to the will, the will to the soul itself. Each step "more bound". But in truth the soul is one power in all. A gloss names the error the whole gate was written against: thinking "before Him" means "before the contraction", as if after it something changed. "There is no greater destruction." If anything changed, there would be something besides Him. Then the line. The Ari says we can raise "the waters of longing" only as far as Zeir and Nukva; they raise them higher, level to level, to Ein Sof. We have no power except to bind all the levels to Him: all is from Him, He is in every detail, and He is one in them. And then, by itself, the unity rises to Ein Sof. Bring it to your hands. You cannot reach Him as He is. You can bind this hour, this table, this task to Him. Do that, and leave the rest to rise.
- **From inside:** A kind of rest in doing your part. The pressure to reach the top falls away. What is asked is the binding; the rising is not your job.
- **At the edge:** "To know how it is with Him, apart from His drawing into the worlds, we have no power to understand." Beyond the binding, the text leaves it to the levels above, "by itself".
- **Into the day:** Bind one ordinary hour today to Him in words, and then let it go.
- **Ladder:** L13

## Second Gate, ch. 1 (why exactly ten; every creature has reason, nature and body)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 1 (why exactly ten; every creature has reason, nature and body), lines 282-290.
- **The movement:** It starts the second gate from the ground: an apple, an animal, a person. It asks the mind to see three layers in every created thing before it climbs to the worlds. And it reminds the mind first that all of this is "from our side".
- **The line to hold** (line 283): > כי אין זולתו ואפס בלעדו ולית בר מיניה > > *For there is none besides Him, nothing apart from Him, and nothing outside Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** The question of the gate: why are the sefirot ten, "ten and not nine, ten and not eleven"? First the rule again. Of Him no trait may be said. There is none besides Him and nothing outside Him. All the sefirot and names are "from the side of creation". "Atzilut" means both "set apart", because no trait fits Him, and "beside Him", because every power is one with Him. Now the anatomy of a creature. Take a fruit. There is what you can touch and weigh: its body (the "felt"). There is its taste, sweet or bitter: its nature. And there is the reason it is so, what it is for: its meaning (the "understood"). An animal the same: its body, its drives, its reason for loving what helps it and fleeing what hurts it. And a person: the body, the feelings, the thinking that steers them. Pick up something to eat. Feel its weight. Taste it. Ask what it is for. Three layers in one bite. Then remember the line: none of it is outside Him.
- **From inside:** A slower way of meeting things. The fruit in your hand becomes layered, like a house with rooms. And under all the rooms, the one plain fact that nothing is outside Him.
- **At the edge:** The passage does not reach the edge; it builds the ground floor. It says only that of Him "no trait applies".
- **Into the day:** At your next meal, taste one bite slowly, and name its three layers.
- **Ladder:** L03

## Second Gate, ch. 2 (the form that shows everything and is nothing of its own)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 2 (the form that shows everything and is nothing of its own), lines 291-295.
- **The movement:** It points to something the mind usually overlooks: the outward form of a thing. The form has no being of its own, and yet it shows everything inside and tells one thing from another. The mind learns to see showing as a power.
- **The line to hold** (line 293): > ובחינה זו היא המבדלת בין עצם לעצם > > *And this aspect is what distinguishes one essence from another.*
- **Contemplating it:** Besides body, nature and meaning, every creature has a form: the face of a person, the shape and color of a fruit, the look of an animal. The form is not a fourth thing. You cannot eat the shape of an apple apart from the apple. And yet the form shows all the rest. See an apple, and you know its taste and use. "A man's wisdom lights up his face." And the form is what tells things apart. Without it, all things would look the same, and no one could tell one from another. So the form, which has nothing of its own, is the root of all difference. The text sets it beside the four elements: earth has no taste of its own, yet it holds and carries everything. And the fourfold pattern runs up to the four letters of God's Name. Look at a face you love. The face is not the person. Yet in it you see who they are. Hold both: the form is nothing of its own, and it shows everything.
- **From inside:** A new respect for appearances. Not the respect of taking them for the thing, but of seeing them as a gift that lets the inside show.
- **At the edge:** The passage does not reach the edge. It prepares the picture of Malchut, His kingship, which later gates will call the power of "likeness and image".
- **Into the day:** Look at one face today and let it show you something of the person.
- **Ladder:** L13

## Second Gate, ch. 3 (three lines in everything: reaching, holding back, and the balance)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 3 (three lines in everything: reaching, holding back, and the balance), lines 296-301.
- **The movement:** It shows a pattern in every living act: a pull outward, a pull back, and a middle that holds them. The mind begins to see balance as a structure of creation, like a pair of scales.
- **The line to hold** (line 301): > ובחינות שלשה קוין הנזכרים לעיל נקרא בשם משקל כמו משקל המאזניים שעומד זה כנגד זה בשוה > > *And the three lines are called "weight", like the balance of scales, one side standing against the other, even.*
- **Contemplating it:** Each layer has three lines. In the mind: wisdom (the flash that holds the whole), understanding (the working-out), and da'at (knowledge: the felt recognition, "this is it"). In the feelings: kindness, reaching toward what belongs to you; strength, holding back; and beauty (tiferet), the middle, wanting the right thing. In the body: a pull to move, a pull to stop, and the middle that walks where it needs to go. "With the right line alone, the hands would never stop working." So there are nine, three by three, and the form that holds them is the tenth. The three lines are called "weight", like scales standing even. Watch yourself do one thing today, reach for a cup, say a word. Feel the reaching, the holding back, and the middle that does just what is needed. Creation is built that way.
- **From inside:** A calm, balanced feeling, like standing on both feet. The holding back stops feeling like a failure of kindness, and the reaching stops feeling like greed. They are two sides of one scale.
- **At the edge:** The passage does not reach the edge. It gives the structure that the next chapters lift into the worlds.
- **Into the day:** Before one decision today, feel both pulls, and choose the middle.
- **Ladder:** none

## Second Gate, ch. 4 (the world of doing: plant, animal, person; a star over every blade of grass)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 4 (the world of doing: plant, animal, person; a star over every blade of grass), lines 302-309.
- **The movement:** It lifts the anatomy into the visible world and the stars. It ends with an old picture that makes every growing thing feel addressed from above.
- **The line to hold** (line 309): > לכן אין לך עשב שאין לו מזל למעלה המכה אותו ואומר לו גדל > > *So there is no blade of grass that has no star above it that strikes it and says to it, "Grow!"*
- **Contemplating it:** In the world we see, the plant shows mostly body, the animal shows nature, and the person shows understanding. Each still has all three. Even the taste of a fruit has all three: the felt sweetness, the sweetness itself, and the knowing that it is good. The heavens too: nine spheres, three for body, three for nature, three for mind, and a tenth, "a curtain that does nothing", like the form. Then the sages' saying: there is no blade of grass without a star above that strikes it and says, "Grow!" The text notes the word "strikes": a star touches a plant, which is all body. Go outside, or look at a plant indoors. Picture the word "Grow!" spoken over it, and over you. Nothing here grows unaddressed.
- **From inside:** A gentle wonder at ordinary growth. Plants, children, one's own slow change feel spoken to, not left to chance.
- **At the edge:** The passage stays in the world of doing. It does not reach past it.
- **Into the day:** Water a plant today, or look at one, and say: someone said "Grow!" to you.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Second Gate, ch. 5 (the three worlds; every creature longs to be nullified; sweetness at its root)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 5 (the three worlds; every creature longs to be nullified; sweetness at its root), lines 310-318.
- **The movement:** It turns the whole of nature into longing. Every creature's love and fear is a wish to be nullified to its Source. Even the sweetness you taste is, at its root, the sweetness of bitul.
- **The line to hold** (line 311): > מתיקות גשמי הוא עיקרה מתיקות הרוחני המרגיש מתיקות ועריבות הביטול להבורא יתברך > > *Physical sweetness, at its root, is the spiritual sweetness that feels the sweetness and pleasure of being nullified to the Creator.*
- **Contemplating it:** Asiyah, the world of doing, is the world of the felt. Everything there is grasped as solid "something", even the life in the stars. Yetzirah, the world of formation, is the world of nature: the angels, the powers behind each creature. There His kindness, strength and beauty bring each thing into being. And their love and fear is this: "they long to be nullified to their Source". So the text says: physical sweetness is, at its root, the sweetness of bitul. Physical love is, at its root, spiritual love. The taste is the heart of the fruit, and the body serves the taste. Beriah, the world of creation, is the world of meaning: "every creature has a special intention from the Creator, that His unity be revealed in them". Nothing is made without a purpose. Taste something sweet today. Stay with it one moment longer than usual. Ask: what is this sweetness, at its root? Let the answer be: the sweetness of coming home.
- **From inside:** Pleasure stops being a rival to holiness and becomes its outer edge. Something softens in the heart toward one's own wants: they are longings in disguise.
- **At the edge:** The passage names the longing but not what it longs for. Beriah shows "the first revelation from the Creator"; before it, "it is not in the measure of revealing".
- **Into the day:** Before eating something sweet, say: the sweetness of coming home to You.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Second Gate, ch. 6 (Beriah as the throne; horse and rider; even "bitul" is our word)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 6 (Beriah as the throne; horse and rider; even "bitul" is our word), lines 319-324.
- **The movement:** It shows a throne that is seen only by its legs, and a rider carried by a horse that "does not count". Then it takes away even the word bitul: before Him, there is no one to be nullified.
- **The line to hold** (line 324): > כי בבחינת עצמותו יתברך אינו נופל לשון ביטול מאחר שאינו מושג כלל ואין זולתו > > *For of His Essence the word "bitul" does not apply, since He is not grasped at all and there is none besides Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** Beriah is called "the throne", kisei, from the word for "covered". In a person, the mind covers all his words and deeds, and they are covered in it. If the thought left for a moment, the word would stop. The mind is the soul's chariot, like a horse carrying its rider. The horse brings the rider where he wants and "does not count" beside him. A throne shows the king's glory, and yet no one looks at the throne; they look at the king. When a man sits on a chair, you see only its legs; the seat is covered by him. So in Beriah what shows is only "the four legs of the throne", and the throne itself is the creatures' bitul, covered by His glory. Then the careful line. The throne is called bitul "relative to His Essence". But of His Essence itself, the word bitul does not apply. There is no one there to be nullified. Think of what carries you: your body, your chair, the ground. You rarely notice them. They carry you and do not count. Be that for Him for a moment: carrying, not counting.
- **From inside:** A quiet dignity in being a carrier. There is something freeing in not being the one who counts, like the horse that knows the road.
- **At the edge:** The text says even bitul is a word for creatures. At His Essence "there is none besides Him", so there is no one to be nullified, and no word.
- **Into the day:** When you sit down today, notice the chair holding you, and say: let me carry Him like that.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Second Gate, ch. 7 (head, body and feet: one person by a wonder)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 7 (head, body and feet: one person by a wonder), lines 325-328.
- **The movement:** It uses the reader's own body as the proof of a wonder. Head, heart and feet cannot be compared, and yet they are one person. The mind learns to feel that joining as God's own act.
- **The line to hold** (line 327): > וקישורם ויחודם הוא בחינות פלא שהוא יתברך מפליא לעשות > > *And their binding and union is a wonder, for He "does wonders".*
- **Contemplating it:** Every level has three layers and each layer three lines: ten in all, and no more. The layers are not alike. Mind is higher than feeling "beyond comparison"; feeling is higher than the body. The head is not like the feet. And yet a person is one. Head, body and feet are "bound and united and become one, called one man". How? "Their binding is a wonder: He does wonders." The joining works by clothing. The lowest part of the higher layer dresses itself in the next one down. What the mind feels, "love this, avoid that", becomes the mind of the heart; what the heart feels becomes the resolve of the hands. So the worlds are joined, each to the next. Feel your own body from head to foot, not to watch it but to wonder at it. Thought in your head moves your feet. They are nothing alike. They are one you. Say: this joining is His wonder.
- **From inside:** A fresh surprise at being one person. The gap between thinking and doing, which can feel like a failure, is also the place of a daily wonder. The text's own word: "He does wonders", in joining the spiritual to the physical.
- **At the edge:** The joining is "pele", wonder, which the mind can name but not explain. The text says it is "by His power, equal in all".
- **Into the day:** When your hand does what your mind decided, say once: a wonder.
- **Ladder:** L08

## Second Gate, ch. 8 (the levels are real, and still one power; "like the appearance of a man")

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 8 (the levels are real, and still one power; "like the appearance of a man"), lines 329-333.
- **The movement:** It holds the mind to both sides at once. The levels are not an illusion; each is a real power made on purpose. And yet all of them are one power, His. The mind is not allowed to escape into either half.
- **The line to hold** (line 329): > אך הגם שכל החילוקים הוא מעצמותו יתברך אף על פי כן המה כח אחד מיוחד בלי התחלקות כלל > > *But although all the differences are from His Essence, they are still one power, united, with no division at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** All the layers and levels are real as we see them. But look at them as they are in His power, and they are one power, simple and equal. And then the author turns and argues with those who say the levels only "seem" divided to us. "That is not so. We see with our senses that each level has its own real power", made on purpose, each creature with its own intention. So both. Every difference is from His Essence, and yet they are one power without division. That is what "Atzilut" means: many that are, and are one. We may speak of His "wisdom" and "kindness" by way of parable, "You are wise, but not with a known wisdom". Of Him Himself, before Atzilut, not even "wonder" can be said. Think of two people you know who are very different. Their difference is real; do not wish it away. Now hold, at the same time, that both are held in one power. Keep both true.
- **From inside:** Many find relief in not having to call the world an illusion. The differences between people and things can be honored. And under them, the one power holds steady.
- **At the edge:** "Before Atzilut we have no business." Of His Essence not even pele, wonder, may be said: "Is anything too wondrous for God?"
- **Into the day:** When you notice how different someone is today, say: real, and one.
- **Ladder:** L03

## Second Gate, ch. 9 ("like the appearance of a man", not "the appearance of a man"; the letters of thought)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 9 ("like the appearance of a man", not "the appearance of a man"; the letters of thought), lines 334-337.
- **The movement:** It gives the mind the picture of a man and then shows the one word that breaks it: "like". A soul feels its body's pain; He is not changed by the worlds. Then it shows how an idea takes form in the letters of thought.
- **The line to hold** (line 334): > ואפילו כל בחינות הנבראים בפנימיותם וחיצוניותם הכל הוא לבדו יתברך > > *And even all the aspects of creatures, their inside and outside, are all He alone.*
- **Contemplating it:** Ezekiel saw "like the appearance of a man" above the throne. A man is many levels and one person. So He holds all powers in one. But the verse says "like", not "the appearance of a man". A man's soul feels its body. Cold, heat, pain reach it and change it, because soul and body are two created things. With Him there is nothing outside Him. "Even all the aspects of creatures, inside and outside, are He alone." So nothing changes Him. The creatures add nothing to Him. Then a picture from your own mind. When an idea comes, it shapes itself in words, the "letters of thought". The letters are not the idea; they are only letters. Yet they fit it exactly, and each new idea brings new letters. That is Malchut: the power that gives every power its form, "the lamp that gathers all lights". Think one sentence now, silently. Notice the words forming. They are not the thought, and yet they show it. Then think: the whole world is like those letters, showing Him and not Him.
- **From inside:** A clearer sense of the gap and the bond. The world shows Him the way words show a thought. Nothing in the world is outside Him, and nothing in it reaches back to change Him.
- **At the edge:** The text says we "describe all these aspects in Him" because the powers are from Him, though "He is one power". How He is untouched by them is not grasped.
- **Into the day:** Before you speak an important sentence today, feel the thought first, then let the words come.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Second Gate, ch. 10 (Malchut as speech: "the word of God")

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 10 (Malchut as speech: "the word of God"), lines 338-342.
- **The movement:** It names the power that shows all the others: His kingship, which gives each power its form, as speech shows a person's whole mind. (Lines 340-342 repeat the words of 334-337 almost exactly; they are read with the previous unit.)
- **The line to hold** (line 339): > כמו למשל באדם שעל ידי הדבור נגלה חכמתו ודעתו ובינתו ומדותיו בכלל לחוץ > > *As in a person, through speech his wisdom, knowledge, understanding and feelings are revealed outward.*
- **Contemplating it:** Malchut, His kingship, is "the likeness of every sefirah". It stands between one sefirah and the next and tells them apart. Without it, "all would be one power". It completes each one and brings it into act. It works in two ways: in general, bringing all the powers out into the worlds, and in particular, giving each one its own form. So it is called "the word of God". A person's speech brings his whole inner life outward: what he knows, what he feels, what he wants. And there is a power of speech inside him even before he speaks. The worlds are His speech. Not His Essence, but His inner life brought out, each thing a word. Listen to someone speak today. In their words you hear their mind and heart. Then listen to the world that way, as words that show the One speaking.
- **From inside:** The world feels addressed, spoken, rather than merely there. Many find it makes ordinary sounds and sights feel like a message not yet understood.
- **At the edge:** The passage stays with Malchut. It does not reach the Speaker; the next chapter says His name and He are one, and what that means is not grasped.
- **Into the day:** Once today, stop and listen to the world as if it were speech.

## Second Gate, ch. 11 (a name is only for others; how much shows depends on service)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 11 (a name is only for others; how much shows depends on service), lines 343-350.
- **The movement:** It makes the mind notice that a name is not for oneself. His name, Malchut, exists for the creatures. It also moves the mind from seeing to serving: how much of the upper worlds a person sees depends on their work.
- **The line to hold** (line 347): > כי בחינת שם הוא הכל לזולת כי לעצמו אין צריך שם כלל כי אם לזולת > > *For a name is all for another; for oneself one needs no name at all, only for another.*
- **Contemplating it:** Look at a fruit from outside and you see only its body. Look deeper and you see its taste and purpose. Eat it and the taste shows fully. A person too: look closer and you see a mind and a heart. So with the worlds. See Asiyah from outside, and it is only matter. Nullify it to Him through love, and you begin to see Yetzirah. Understand the creatures' nullification, and you see Beriah. "This depends on the service of Israel." Malchut is His name. And a name is only for others. You do not need a name for yourself. So His kingship exists to show Him to creatures. But toward Him, "He and His name are one". The contraction too is only for us. "Toward Him, there is no contraction or hiding that conceals before Him." To put contraction in Him would be to change Him. Say your own name quietly. It is not for you; you do not need it to be yourself. It is for others to reach you. Now think of God's names that way: His gift, so we can reach Him.
- **From inside:** A tenderness about names. His names feel less like labels and more like doors He opened. And a quiet motivation: seeing more depends on serving more.
- **At the edge:** The root of Malchut is "the Malchut of Ein Sof, whose essence is not known". "Even the angels ask: where is the place of His glory?"
- **Into the day:** Before you say God's name in a blessing today, remember: this name is His gift to me.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Second Gate, ch. 12 (the curtain; the sage's parable; the same power in every world)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 12 (the curtain; the sage's parable; the same power in every world), lines 351-357.
- **The movement:** It shows how the same wisdom can appear as a parable to a child and as itself to a sage. All the differences between the worlds are in the hiding and the likeness, not in what is hidden.
- **The line to hold** (line 355): > השתנותם אינו מצד העצם כי הכל כח אחד להם בלי שינוי הוא כוחו יתברך ושינוייהם הם מצד ההסתרה והדמיון > > *Their change is not in their essence, for all of them have one power without change, His power; their changes are from the hiding and the likeness.*
- **Contemplating it:** Between Atzilut and the worlds below there is a curtain (parsa), to hide the light, "so that creatures can appear". Without it there would be no room for anything limited. The picture: a great sage teaching a student far below him. He must hide his real wisdom completely and dress it in a parable fitted to the student. The parable still resembles his wisdom, the way a tree's root resembles the root of an idea: both are "roots", though one is wood and one is thought. A wise student will reach the sage's real wisdom through the parable, "because their likenesses have one root". So the angel Michael's kindness in Beriah is, in its essence, the kindness of Atzilut itself. Only the showing differs. The change is "from the hiding and the likeness". Think of a lesson you learned as a child through a story. Now you understand what the story meant. The story did not change the truth; it hid it at your size. Look at your world as that story.
- **From inside:** Patience with the world's "stories". The simple forms, the plain things, are not less true; they are truth fitted to us. A wise receiver finds the root in them.
- **At the edge:** How Malchut can hide and give likeness "we may not search at all, and we have no business with it". Its root is "the Malchut of Ein Sof".
- **Into the day:** When something plain happens today, ask: what is this a parable of?
- **Ladder:** L05

## Second Gate, ch. 13 (the rose that changes color; one set of ten in every world)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 13 (the rose that changes color; one set of ten in every world), lines 358-364.
- **The movement:** It shows one power passing through many worlds, like a rose that shows different colors and is one rose. The mind learns to see the change as coming from the showing, never from what is shown.
- **The line to hold** (line 359): > ולכן נקראת שושנה דאשתניאת מגוונא לגוונא אבל בעצם כוחו יתברך הוא בלי שינוי כלל > > *So she is called "a rose that changes from color to color", but in the essence of His power there is no change at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** The curtain hides the unity of Atzilut. Malchut, His kingship, breaks through the curtain and shows a likeness below. But once the kindness shows below, "it is the very kindness of Atzilut", His power alone. So the Zohar calls her "a rose that changes from color to color". The colors change with the world. The rose does not. That is why the Kabbalists speak of only ten sefirot, not forty. By the count of worlds, there should be ten in each of four worlds. But the ten of Atzilut are the ten of Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah. "The same ten sefirot." The sage again: to teach, he dresses his wisdom in a thick garment, a tree's root for the root of an idea, water flowing down for the drawing-down of thought. The garment is far from the wisdom. Through it, the wisdom passes. Think of a single kindness you received. It came as bread, or a word, or a coin: different colors. Look under the color. It is the same kindness, the same rose, passing through every world to you.
- **From inside:** A steadiness under change. The colors of the day shift, good and hard, and the one power under them does not. Many find this makes change less frightening.
- **At the edge:** How Malchut gives likeness "we have no business with at all". Its root is "the Malchut of Ein Sof, not in the measure of knowing".
- **Into the day:** When the day changes color today, say: the same rose.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Second Gate, ch. 14 (the curtain as a railing; even the power of "something" is His Essence)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 14 (the curtain as a railing; even the power of "something" is His Essence), lines 365-369.
- **The movement:** It turns the hiding into protection: a railing on a roof, so no one falls. And then it says the boldest thing so far: even the solid "something" of the world is His Essence.
- **The line to hold** (line 367): > אפילו כח היש הוא עצמותו יתברך > > *Even the power of the "yesh" (the separate-seeming something) is His Essence.*
- **Contemplating it:** The curtain is called a "railing", ma'akeh, like the fence the Torah requires on a roof, "so that one who falls does not fall". Without it, the light would break the lower vessels, as it broke the first ones. In Atzilut you cannot even call kindness "kindness". Kindness is for someone else, and there is no one else there. Only below does it become kindness to another. The curtain is made by Binah, the understanding of His Essence, which can dress what is above in what is far below, the way the wiser a man is, the farther the parable he can find. Then the line. The difference between Atzilut and the lower worlds is only in the vessels, only "for us". "Toward Him there is no difference, for there is nothing besides Him, even the power of the yesh is His Essence." Touch something solid: a wall, a table. It feels like the most separate thing there is. Now let the line sit on it: even this, its very solidness, is His Essence. Not in spite of being solid. Its solidness too.
- **From inside:** A startling closeness in the most ordinary things. The hardness of the world stops feeling like a wall against God. It is Him, hidden, keeping us from falling.
- **At the edge:** How kindness is in His power "is not revealed to us, and we have no grasp of it". The mind holds the statement without seeing through it.
- **Into the day:** Put your hand on something solid today and say: even this.
- **Ladder:** L10

## Second Gate, ch. 15 (the gloss: inward and outward; "as His power is without limit, so it is in limit")

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 15 (the gloss: inward and outward; "as His power is without limit, so it is in limit"), lines 370-376.
- **The movement:** It takes the mind on two journeys. Inward, level by level, until not even "wonder" applies. Outward, down to the plain physical earth, where His Essence's power shows most. Both end in the same place.
- **The line to hold** (line 373): > כי כאשר כוחו בלי גבול ככה כוחו בגבול באין הבדל כלל > > *For as His power is without limit, so His power is in limit, with no difference at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** "He grasps all the worlds." Filling and surrounding, inside and outside, "all is His power alone, with no change". "He is the place of the world." The inward journey: look past the outer clothing (Malchut), and you find the higher unity; deeper, His power of wonder; deeper still, only His power, where you cannot even say "wonder". That is "the Head that is not known". The outward journey goes the other way. "The more outward something is as yesh, the more His power shows." Down to the physical earth, the most solid "something", with nothing below it. There, precisely, "the power of His Essence is revealed, which is not in the measure of levels at all". And the line: as His power is without limit, so it is in limit, with no difference. The goal: that His glory show even "toward us", through prayer that nullifies the yesh and Torah that brings His will into limit. In the future, "what no eye has seen" will be "in doing", in the physical world. Stand on the floor. Feel the ground under you, the lowest, plainest thing. Say the line slowly. The deepest inward and the farthest outward meet under your feet.
- **From inside:** A kind of homecoming to the physical. Many describe the ground under them feeling holy, not because it glows, but because it is where He is most Himself, unseen.
- **At the edge:** "How it is, is not known." The text says only "one must know, in general": all that exists is from His Essence, and every curtain is only to hide "for us".
- **Into the day:** Stand still on the ground for one moment today and say: here too, with no difference.
- **Ladder:** L10

## Second Gate, ch. 16 ("the curtain divides for you"; breaking in the showing, repair through the yesh)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 16 ("the curtain divides for you"; breaking in the showing, repair through the yesh), lines 377-383.
- **The movement:** It reads one Torah word, "for you", as the key to the whole idea of hiding. Then it shows how love takes the shape of what it pictures, and places the breaking and the repair in that shaping.
- **The line to hold** (line 378): > לכן נאמר והבדילה הפרכת לכם לכם דייקא לגבי דידן הוא ההבדל אבל לא לגביה יתברך > > *So it says, "The curtain shall divide for you": for you precisely; the division is toward us, not toward Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** In the Tabernacle, a curtain divided the holy from the Holy of Holies. The verse says it divides "for you". The division is for us. Not for Him. The author says the main contraction is at the curtain between Atzilut and the lower worlds, not above. And again: those who read the contraction "literally, from the Malchut of Ein Sof" are mistaken, "may God forgive, for all the people erred". Then a picture of the breaking. Love, in you, is one power. But it takes the shape of what you picture: a beautiful thing, a food you want, a garment. The love changes by the picture. So His traits, one with Him, took the shape of limited, separate things. That is called "breaking". And the repair: "when through the yesh His unity is revealed", when the separate thing is nullified to Him by Torah and service. The breaking is in the showing; the repair is also in the showing. Notice what your love pictures today. The love is good; the picture shapes it. Turn one picture toward Him, and see the repair in small.
- **From inside:** A gentler view of one's own misplaced wants. The love under them is not bad; it took a broken shape. Turning it back feels less like fighting and more like repair.
- **At the edge:** "The whole contraction is only from the side of the creatures." How it is with Him, "no contraction conceals before Him", is not grasped.
- **Into the day:** When a strong want rises today, say: the love is His; let me give it its right shape.
- **Ladder:** L12

## Second Gate, ch. 17 (Zeir Anpin, "the small face"; the heart is the person)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 17 (Zeir Anpin, "the small face"; the heart is the person), lines 384-387.
- **The movement:** It shows that the center of a fruit is its taste, and the center of a person is the heart. So the center of the worlds is His traits, and the center of service is to unite the heart's traits with Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 386): > כן באדם עיקרו של אדם הוא הלב שהוא המדות > > *So in a person, the main part of the person is the heart, which is the feelings.*
- **Contemplating it:** The nine powers of Atzilut are called Zeir Anpin, "the small face". Why small? Because all the levels together are "smallness and contraction" compared to Ein Sof. Of the three layers, the middle one, nature, is the main one. A fruit's main thing is its taste. Its meaning and its body serve the taste. So in a person: the main part is the heart, the feelings. The mind guides them; the hands carry them out. The worlds too: the main thing is Yetzirah, where His traits run everything. So the Mishnah, "forbidden and permitted, fit and unfit", belongs to Yetzirah; it is "the main intention of His drawing into the worlds". "The whole of service is to unite His traits and nullify all traits to Him." Put your hand on your chest. Not to watch it, but to find your center. The heart is the main part of you. What does it love right now? Offer that love to Him.
- **From inside:** A sense of finding one's center. Not the busy mind, not the busy hands, but the heart that wants. Offering its wanting feels like offering the whole self.
- **At the edge:** The passage stays with the traits. It says only that all nine are "in one power, Ein Sof", and before Atzilut nothing may be described.
- **Into the day:** Before one task today, ask your heart what it wants, and turn that want toward Him.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Second Gate, ch. 18 (the king who conquers far islands: the aim is the world of doing)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 18 (the king who conquers far islands: the aim is the world of doing), lines 388-393.
- **The movement:** It answers a question the mind raises: if the heart is the center, why is the physical world the goal? Because a king's glory grows the farther it reaches.
- **The line to hold** (line 390): > כי באמת עיקר כוונתו יתברך להיות כבודו ואלק"ותו יתברך בבחינת עשיה דוקא > > *For in truth His main intention is that His glory and Godliness be precisely in the world of doing.*
- **Contemplating it:** Earlier the text said the heart is the main thing. Elsewhere it says "the end of deed is first in thought": the deed is the aim. Which? Both. The deeds carry out the heart. In Asiyah, the world of doing, is "the full yesh, the other, the felt". When that is nullified to Him and His will is drawn into it, His glory shows fully: "in heaven above and on earth below, there is no other." The picture: a king longs to show his glory. He cannot, except by ruling many lands and distant islands. If he defeats his enemies, his glory grows. "The farther, the greater his glory." His traits are the longing. The world of doing completes it. So Malchut, the source of the yesh, is "the completion of all sefirot", and "without her He is not called One". Think of the farthest place from holiness in your week: a commute, a chore, a hard conversation. That is a distant island. His glory there is greater than in the nearby rooms.
- **From inside:** The plain and distant parts of life gain weight and dignity. They are not interruptions; they are the islands where the King's glory is largest.
- **At the edge:** Above the order of the worlds, in His Essence, "the yesh, His Malchut, is the main thing", "a woman of valor, crown of her husband". The text promises to explain later.
- **Into the day:** Pick the most "distant" task of your day and do it on purpose, for His glory.
- **Ladder:** L13

## Second Gate, ch. 19 (three stages: working by reminding yourself, working from love, and working without needing to think)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 19 (three stages: working by reminding yourself, working from love, and working without needing to think), lines 394-399.
- **The movement:** It describes, from a person's daily work, three stages any contemplative knows: doing it coldly because you know you must, doing it from love that needs reminding, and doing it from love so full that thought is no longer needed.
- **The line to hold** (line 395): > אבל בעת התגלות מדותיו באהבה אינו צריך להשים בינתו אל המעשה כי על ידי הרגשת אהבתו ותשוקתו ממילא עושה הדבר > > *But when his feelings are open in love, he does not need to set his mind to the deed, for by feeling his love and longing he does it by itself.*
- **Contemplating it:** Watch a person at work. Some days he works with love, and it shows in every act. Some days he works coldly, from stubbornness, with no feeling. But look closer: even then, love is there, hidden. He does it because his mind reminds him that he needs it. When the love is open, he does not need to think about it. He does it by himself. He only stops now and then to think about why he needs it, to keep the love awake. When he understands fully, he does not need to think at all. The Kabbalah calls these stages "pregnancy", "nursing" and "maturity". In the middle stage, the unity is known only by understanding: "the yesh still counts as a thing of its own, only by understanding is all one". In maturity, understanding is not needed. "Understanding belongs only where things are separate." And all three depend on service below: the deed and the yoke of heaven; love with self-giving through contemplation; bitul so full that the world does not hide Him, "as in the time of the Temple". Where are you today? Working by reminding yourself? That is real. Working from love that needs waking? That is real. Do not rush to the third. Keep contemplating; it is how love stays awake.
- **From inside:** Compassion for your own coldness. The cold day is not empty; love is hidden in it. And a glimpse of a love so steady it needs no reminders, which the text calls the mind's "maturity".
- **At the edge:** In maturity, understanding gives way to "His Essence revealed", "a new flow without pause from Ein Sof", "delight". The text says it depends on service, and on special days, Shabbat and festivals.
- **Into the day:** On a cold day, before a mitzvah, remind yourself in one sentence why you need it.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Second Gate, ch. 20 (Malchut as one point: He made the world and is its King)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 20 (Malchut as one point: He made the world and is its King), lines 400-404.
- **The movement:** It reduces the whole of kingship to one plain point, "He made the world and is King over it", and then shows how that point grows to ten when His children imitate Him. It ends with the wicked kingdom vanishing "like smoke".
- **The line to hold** (line 400): > אשר ברא העולם והוא מלך על העולם והעולם בטל אליו יתברך > > *That He created the world, and He is King over the world, and the world is nullified to Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** Malchut in itself is "one point". A king's first showing is only this: he is king over his people. Look closer and you see his greatness, strength and wisdom. So God's first showing in the world: He created it, He is King over it, and the world is nullified to Him. Before, "hidden of all hidden"; now, a King. The point grows to ten when Israel "make the form like its Maker". A son who copies his father's deeds brings out his father's strength; one who loves as his father loves brings out his father's love; one who thinks as he thinks brings out his mind. When Israel cling to the yesh, the nine withdraw, and only the bare point is left: the worlds live, but His unity does not show. In exile even the "wicked kingdom" gets its power from the holy one, for nothing is outside Him. "And all wickedness will vanish like smoke." Say the one point aloud: He made the world, He is King over it. Then ask what you do today that makes you look like Him. That is how the point grows.
- **From inside:** Clarity, like returning to a first sentence after a long book. And a quiet responsibility: the King shows more when His children resemble Him.
- **At the edge:** In the future, Malchut "will rise to its root in Ein Sof", "the source of likeness", "not in the measure of revealing, and not known".
- **Into the day:** Do one act today that is like His: a kindness given freely.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Second Gate, ch. 21 ("ten and not nine": the world is not necessary, but given freely)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 21 ("ten and not nine": the world is not necessary, but given freely), lines 405-412.
- **The movement:** It rules out two errors and leaves the mind with a gift. The world is not a machine that had to be. It is "by will and freely given", held together by a power that bears all opposites.
- **The line to hold** (line 406): > ואין העולמות מחוייבים המציאות כי אם הכל ברצון ובנדבה > > *And the worlds do not exist by necessity; rather, all is by will and freely given.*
- **Contemplating it:** Why "ten and not nine"? So you will not think the first thing to come from Him was wisdom, as if wisdom were in Him. And so you will not think the world is run by the sefirot alone, as if nature ran by itself. If the world ran by wisdom and traits alone, it would be "necessary": it would have to be as it is. The line says no. "All is by will and freely given." His Essence is drawn into the worlds "without change, from before they were created to after". The first thing is Keter, wonder. It "bears all opposites in one subject": many different powers, and yet no contradiction to Him, equal with Him. Patach Eliyahu says: "You are the One who brought out ten rectifications... and You bind them and unite them." Everything is joined "as before they came forth". Look at something you take for granted: the sun rising, your own heartbeat. It did not have to be. It is given, freely, this moment. Receive it as a gift.
- **From inside:** Gratitude of a clean kind, not for something earned, but for something that did not have to be. Many find it lightens the sense of a cold, mechanical world.
- **At the edge:** Keter is "wonderful and covered; no mind grasps it". Of the Emanator Himself, "no aspect, no revealing, no name".
- **Into the day:** Thank Him today for one thing that did not have to be.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Second Gate, ch. 22 ("ten and not eleven": Malchut has nothing of her own; the other side exists to be turned)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 22 ("ten and not eleven": Malchut has nothing of her own; the other side exists to be turned), lines 413-417.
- **The movement:** It shows that God's showing is not a separate thing added to Him: He and His name are one. Then it gives the other side, the forces against holiness, a surprising reason to exist: to be turned around.
- **The line to hold** (line 417): > כי הבחינות בעצמם הם רק כחו אשר נתהוו על ידי כחו הפלא מטעם להפכם ולאכפיים > > *For the aspects themselves are only His power, brought into being by His power of wonder, for the purpose of turning them over and subduing them.*
- **Contemplating it:** "Ten and not eleven." Malchut, the power of likeness, is not a separate eleventh thing. A man's image of something is not himself. His image is Him. "He and His name are one." "I will not give My glory to another." Malchut "has nothing of her own". She receives from all and, at her root, gives to all. On the other side, the forces against holiness, there are eleven. There Malchut goes out as a thing on its own, "precisely separate". Its "wisdom" is wisdom for grasping the yesh alone: the wisdom of Egypt, of those who deny the unity. Why do they exist? "Only His power, brought into being by His power of wonder, in order to turn them over and subdue them." So the incense had eleven spices, to sort and turn them, with frankincense, holiness, at their head. Name one force in your life that pulls against holiness: a habit, a craving, a voice. It exists by His power, for one reason: to be turned. You are the one who turns it.
- **From inside:** The struggle takes on meaning. The pull against holiness is not an equal enemy; it is material given for turning. Many find this makes them less afraid of their worst moments.
- **At the edge:** The passage names the other side's purpose but not how it will vanish; that comes elsewhere, "like smoke".
- **Into the day:** When the pull comes today, say: you exist to be turned. Then turn one small thing.
- **Ladder:** L12

## Second Gate, ch. 23 (the half-shekel: the rich not more, the poor not less; what "wonder" means)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 23 (the half-shekel: the rich not more, the poor not less; what "wonder" means), lines 418-424.
- **The movement:** It defines wonder from inside the experience: the amazement of seeing something impossible and seeing that it is so. Then it says even the hiding power, the "poor" one, is His, and lacks nothing.
- **The line to hold** (line 420): > כי פלא נקרא בבחינת פליאה כאדם שמתפלא על אופן הנמנע שיהיה הדבר ואף על פי כן רואה שהדבר כן הוא > > *For "wonder" means amazement, like a person amazed at something that cannot be, and yet he sees that it is so.*
- **Contemplating it:** The half-shekel: "the rich shall not give more, the poor shall not give less". The "rich" one is Zeir Anpin: all the details are in his power, and he lacks nothing. "Not more than ten": do not count Ein Sof among the sefirot. The first thing to hang on is Keter, wonder. And wonder means this: a man sees something that cannot be, and yet he sees that it is so. Like the Ark that took no space. Two wonders: that nine divided powers are one, and that anything at all comes from Ein Sof. So Keter is "between the Emanator and the emanated", and it too is emanated. "Of Ein Sof, not even wonder can be said." The "poor" one is Yesod, the end of the flow into Malchut, into the creatures that lack. "Not less than ten": do not think the receiving side is other than Him. "Toward us it is called contraction and lack; toward Him there is no lack." Remember one moment when you saw something that should not have been possible, and it was. Stay in the amazement. That feeling, the text says, is the name of His first showing.
- **From inside:** Amazement that does not need to be solved. And a quiet dignity for the poor and lacking parts of life: they receive, and they lack nothing before Him.
- **At the edge:** "Of Him there is no wonder, since there is nothing besides Him." Wonder is our side of a oneness that is not amazed at itself.
- **Into the day:** Once today, let yourself be amazed at something ordinary that should not be possible.
- **Ladder:** L08

## Second Gate, ch. 24 (da'at: the felt knowing, the spine of the self)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 24 (da'at: the felt knowing, the spine of the self), lines 425-431.
- **The movement:** It defines da'at (knowledge) from inside a person: not another idea, but the felt knowing that makes ideas steady and ties them to the heart. It gives the mind a picture of a steady person, whose yes stays yes.
- **The line to hold** (line 427): > כמו אדם שקורין אותו דעתן דהיינו שלא ישתנה ממהות למהות ולא יאמר על הן לאו ועל לאו הן > > *Like a person called "a man of da'at": he does not change from one thing to another, and does not say no to yes or yes to no.*
- **Contemplating it:** Da'at is not a sefirah of its own. Wisdom grasps what a thing is; understanding works it out. Da'at is knowing it: giving your mind to it until it is felt and recognized, "tied to your soul". Without da'at, a person may love, but he does not feel his love, and it has no strength. A child has feelings but not da'at, "so his feelings change, from yes to no and from no to yes". Da'at is like the spinal cord. It binds all the limbs and moves them, and it is not counted among them. Its root is above the mind: the soul's will, the wonder that joins soul and body. Above, His da'at is the felt showing of His unity in all creatures. When the worlds are united "through Shema, prayer, Torah and mitzvot", it shows. When not, only "nature" shows. Of Him Himself, da'at does not apply: da'at needs two, and there is none besides Him. Think of something you know about God that goes back and forth in you, true on good days, doubtful on bad. Da'at is staying with it until it stops flipping. Sit with it today one minute longer than you want to.
- **From inside:** Steadiness. Not stubbornness, but a spine. The knowing stops wobbling with the weather of the day. The text's picture is plain: the person whose yes stays yes.
- **At the edge:** The root of da'at is Keter, the "wonder" that joins what has no likeness; "the wonder itself is not in the measure of grasp", only its showing, which we feel as life.
- **Into the day:** Pick one thing you know about God, and say it the same way morning and night.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Second Gate, ch. 25 (exile as hidden intention; "and God heard their groaning")

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 25 (exile as hidden intention; "and God heard their groaning"), lines 432-435.
- **The movement:** It shows exile as a time when God's intention is hidden inside His acts, like a man who does not reveal his aim until the work is done. And it shows the turn: it began with a cry from below.
- **The line to hold** (line 434): > כמו שכתוב וישמע אלקי"ם את נאקתם דהיינו שהיה אתערותא של ישראל בביטול היש והעולמות לייחדם אליו יתברך > > *As it says, "And God heard their groaning": that is, Israel had an awakening in nullifying the "something" and the worlds, to unite them to Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** In Egypt the world ran as usual. Everyone knew God made it. But His unity did not show in it. It was like a child in the womb: alive, hidden. A picture: a man goes about his work and does not reveal his real aim. The aim is in his mind, hidden in every act. Only when the work is complete does everyone see what he wanted. So it is with the worlds. They exist "to reveal His unity". In exile only the acts show, not the aim. That is every exile. What turned it? "God heard their groaning." The groaning was an awakening from below: Israel setting aside their "something" and binding the world to Him. Then came "God remembered His covenant... God saw... and God knew." Da'at, knowing, was revealed. Think of a time in your life when nothing seemed to show God's aim. Now think of the cry that rose from you then. The text says that cry was the start of the turn.
- **From inside:** Hope that is not cheerful but patient. The hidden time is not empty; the aim is inside it. And a new respect for the groan: it counts.
- **At the edge:** In the hidden time, the worlds were held "by His power of wonder" alone, "for all before Him is as nothing". What the full revealing is like is left to the gates to come.
- **Into the day:** When you feel stuck today, let yourself say one honest sentence to God about it.
- **Ladder:** L12

## Second Gate, ch. 26 ("He is not a body" means the worlds are not separate from Him)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 26 ("He is not a body" means the worlds are not separate from Him), lines 436-440.
- **The movement:** It takes the first principle of faith, that God has no body, and turns it around. If the worlds were a separate body, He would be divided or changed. So even the worlds are not a body apart from Him: toward Him, "there is no world at all".
- **The line to hold** (line 440): > אבל לגביה אין עולם כלל > > *But toward Him there is no world at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** A soul knows its body: it feels it, is changed by it. We call His presence in the worlds "da'at" in the same way. But His knowing is not like that. "He and His knowing are one. In knowing Himself He knows all creatures." Some, "lacking knowledge", say: God is not a body, but the worlds are. The author says this cannot escape two sides. If the worlds are separate from Him, He is divided. If He spreads into them, He is changed. Both destroy faith. So "we must believe that the worlds are not a separate being from Him. He and the worlds are all one." And yet He is unchanged, as before creation. How? "It is not in the power of the mind to recognize." Then the line: from our side they are called worlds; "toward Him there is no world at all". Even the hiding is His power. He knows "even the light talk between a man and his wife", every detail, and is not changed. Say the word "world" and feel how big and solid it is. Now say the line. Do not try to make the world disappear. Let it stand, and let it be held inside a view where it is not a second thing.
- **From inside:** A deep quiet, like standing in a huge space and realizing there is no wall at its edge. The world remains; the sense of a rival to God goes.
- **At the edge:** "You have no business with hidden things." How He knows every detail and is not changed "is not known or grasped by us at all".
- **Into the day:** When you feel God is far from the world today, say: toward Him there is no world apart.
- **Ladder:** L03

## Second Gate, ch. 27 ("He wraps Himself in light like a garment"; not even "yachid")

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 27 ("He wraps Himself in light like a garment"; not even "yachid"), lines 441-449.
- **The movement:** It takes away even light as a word for God. Light is showing, and showing needs someone to show to. So light itself is created, a garment. At the far end, not even "alone" can be said.
- **The line to hold** (line 443): > ולכן נאמר עוטה אור כשלמה שהוא בחינת לבוש > > *Therefore it says, "He wraps Himself in light like a garment": it is a garment.*
- **Contemplating it:** All the contractions are "of the light", we have said. But even "light" cannot be said of Him. Every light is a showing, and every showing is to another. There is no other. So the sages ask: "From where was light created?" Light itself is created, from nothing, "a new song, for He has done wonders". "He wraps Himself in light like a garment." A man's garment is foreign to him. So light is "like a foreign thing" to His being. And yet: "He and His name are one." The garment is Him. How? "Hidden things." Atzilut is His unity in the light, as it is for us. "Before Atzilut" is His simplicity as it is for Him. There, not even "unity" applies, for unity means two. "Not even yachid can describe Him." "Flee to your place." Think of the brightest light you have seen. Now think: even that is His garment. Not less holy for being a garment. And He is not the brightness.
- **From inside:** A reverence that goes past brightness. Many find that the wish for spiritual "light" loosens; what is sought is not the light but the One who wears it.
- **At the edge:** "How these two opposites are in one subject is not open to inquiry, knowledge or grasp at all." "It is forbidden to ask."
- **Into the day:** When sunlight touches you today, say: His garment.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Second Gate, ch. 28 (nothing is too small to be made on purpose; upper and lower to be equal)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 28 (nothing is too small to be made on purpose; upper and lower to be equal), lines 450-462.
- **The movement:** It guards the other side. After so much "from our side", the mind might decide the worlds are only appearance. The text says no: nothing is so small that it was not made on purpose, with His Essence drawn into it.
- **The line to hold** (line 450): > אין לך בחינה קטנה שבקטנות שלא נעשה בכוונה מאתו יתברך ובהמשכת עצמותו ברוך הוא בהם דייקא > > *There is no aspect so small that it was not made by His intention and by the drawing of His Essence into it, precisely.*
- **Contemplating it:** "Let the reader not err." All the titles are from our side, yes. But that does not mean the light and the sefirot are only our perception. On the contrary: nothing is too small to be made on purpose, with His Essence in it. Since there is none besides Him, "even the light and the showing are His Essence". "He and His name are one." The purpose: "that upper and lower be equal", that below should show as above, His equality revealed. "The glory of God will be revealed." "Israel rose first in thought": the power of revealing itself is called Israel and the souls of the righteous. And His providence is in every detail: "He tells a man his speech", "He counts my steps", "He searches the heart". Look at the smallest thing on your table: a crumb, a thread. It was made on purpose, with His Essence drawn into it. Stay with that for a moment, no longer, and let it be true.
- **From inside:** The world feels less accidental and more intended, down to the crumbs. Many feel a gentle weight in small things, as if each were placed.
- **At the edge:** How His Essence and His showing are "in complete equality" is hidden: "as the power of His being is hidden from us, so is the power of His equality in both".
- **Into the day:** Pick up one small thing today and set it down gently, as something made on purpose.
- **Ladder:** L04

## Second Gate, ch. 29 (Atik: "the Head that is not known", called delight)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 29 (Atik: "the Head that is not known", called delight), lines 463-465.
- **The movement:** It brings the mind near to the highest showing, which it calls delight. Delight is when two become included in one. Then it stops: of His Essence not even "head" can be said. "Flee to your place."
- **The line to hold** (line 464): > ונקרא בחינת תענוג כי בחינת עונג הוא מצד שני המהותים המתכללים בהשואה אחת > > *And it is called delight, for delight comes from two beings included in one evenness.*
- **Contemplating it:** Atik, "the Ancient One", is the source of all Atzilut, "the Head that is not known". It is called "end of the worlds of the Emanator" only "to soothe the ear". It is called delight. What is delight? A man delights when his wish is fulfilled and he lacks nothing. The delight is in his soul, but it shows because of the thing that fulfilled his wish. So His equality, hidden in Him, shows as delight when it meets the levels. That is Atik: "the Head that is no head". A head of a being; but His equality has no being beside it to be head of. How all the levels, shown by hiding and contraction, are in His equality, which contradicts every contraction: "flee to your place, for it cannot be asked at all". Remember a moment of real delight, when something you longed for arrived and nothing was missing. That wholeness is the text's picture. Hold it. Then let the picture go, and let the delight point past itself.
- **From inside:** Something like quiet joy, not excited, but full. The text places delight at the top of what can be named, just below what cannot.
- **At the edge:** This is the edge: "the Head that is not known", "not in the measure of knowing at all". Above it, "the Head of all heads", no name.
- **Into the day:** When something delights you today, say: this is a hint of His delight.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Second Gate, ch. 30 (three unities: the father who narrows his mind for his son; the open eye that does not sleep)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 30 (three unities: the father who narrows his mind for his son; the open eye that does not sleep), lines 466-474.
- **The movement:** It uses the love of a father and a small son to show three kinds of unity. And it gives the mind a picture of watching without sleep: an eye that is always open.
- **The line to hold** (line 469): > ולכן נקרא התמשכותו ברוך הוא והשגחתו בכל פרטי הדרגין עינא פקיחא דלא נאים ולא אצטריך נטורא > > *So His drawing-forth and His providence over every detail are called "an open eye that does not sleep and needs no guard".*
- **Contemplating it:** A father narrows his mind and feelings to his small son, so the boy can be close to him. The boy grasps the closeness at his own size. That is the first unity, "Zeir Anpin", "small face": "for Israel is a boy, and I love him". The second: the same closeness as it is on the father's side. The boy cannot grasp it. That is Arich Anpin, where not even "one" applies, only "yachid", alone. The third comes before both: the father's delight in the son, simply because they are one at the root. From that delight comes all the reaching toward him. That is Atik. And Arich's providence is "an open eye that does not sleep". Sleep belongs to body and soul; one who is one does not sleep. It needs no guard. Think of a parent bending down to a small child, speaking in small words. Then think of the delight that made the parent bend. You are the child. The eye watching you now does not close.
- **From inside:** Being held in a gaze that does not tire. Many find the father picture brings warmth, and the open eye brings safety, especially at night.
- **At the edge:** "How the levels are in His Essence" is the first wonder, "from the side of the Emanator", "not known at all".
- **Into the day:** Before sleep tonight, say: an open eye that does not sleep is watching.
- **Ladder:** L04

## Second Gate, ch. 31 ("Adam Kadmon" is a borrowed name; the smallest of the small)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 31 ("Adam Kadmon" is a borrowed name; the smallest of the small), lines 475-485.
- **The movement:** It teaches the mind to hold names lightly: even "Primordial Man" is a borrowed name. At the root, the levels are so one in Him that not even "joined" or "delight" can be said.
- **The line to hold** (line 476): > עד שאין נופל לייחס עליהם אפילו לשון התאחדות והתכללות אפילו בבחינת תענוג > > *So that one cannot even apply to them the words "union" and "inclusion", not even as delight.*
- **Contemplating it:** All three unities belong to Atzilut. But the levels as they are in His Essence are so equal "that not even union or inclusion can be said of them, not even as delight". Delight needs two. There they are one. Their equality is called "Adam Kadmon", Primordial Man. But the name is borrowed. It is not a man. A man is body and soul, many powers joined. The borrowing is like calling a spring's source by the river's name. So wisdom and the traits are named in Adam Kadmon by borrowing, from what came of them. But da'at is never borrowed for him, "not even as a loan", because da'at is joining two, and there is no two there. And the whole showing, the line of light, is "the smallest of the small" of His power, a detail next to the endless. Take one word you use for God. Hold it the way you hold a borrowed book: gratefully, carefully, knowing it belongs to Someone else.
- **From inside:** Humility about language, without losing it. Words become borrowed tools, precious because lent. The mind becomes quieter and more respectful.
- **At the edge:** "Toward Him, the power not from the levels and the power of His equality from the levels, all is one power." "Enough for one who understands in depth."
- **Into the day:** When you say a Name of God today, remember: borrowed, and given.

## Second Gate, ch. 32 (two measures; understanding still leaves an understander; stripping the garments)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 32 (two measures; understanding still leaves an understander; stripping the garments), lines 486-494.
- **The movement:** It lays out the whole road of contemplation, from the plain world up to Ein Sof, and then shows its last step. Understanding the unity still leaves "someone" who understands. The final bitul is past understanding, like a body that does not "understand" its bitul to the soul.
- **The line to hold** (line 491): > כי היחוד על ידי השכלה הרי עדיין יש מי שמשכיל ביטולו > > *For unity through understanding still leaves someone who understands his own nullification.*
- **Contemplating it:** Two measures. From the worlds' side: nothing exists that He did not intend and draw Himself into, and He is one in it. From His side: not even "He is one in them". Our work is the first: "to unite them in Shema and prayer". The second we cannot reach, so "cleave to His traits". And yet "all our binding is to Ein Sof Himself": to give the soul over in unity, to be nullified "in the way of not-known". "Once His unity is revealed to us, we must know further that His being is not known." The road: strip the garments, from inside out. See Asiyah, and behind it His kindness making each thing, "no blade of grass without a star". See that nothing is without intention, and that His intention is beyond us: Beriah. Then understand that the creatures are not beings apart: Atzilut, "He and His life are one". The line: understanding still leaves someone who understands. The body's bitul to the soul is not like that. When you speak with a person, you speak with the life, and the body does not count at all. So the final bitul. And the morning prayer walks this road: before Baruch She'amar, Asiyah; then Yetzirah, Beriah; the Amidah, Atzilut. Walk the road once now, slowly. The table. The kindness that made it. The purpose you cannot see. The One in whom it is not apart. Then notice the "you" who has understood all that. Let even that one bow.
- **From inside:** A letting-go that is not effort. The one who contemplates becomes less of a spectator. Many describe it as the difference between looking at a light and being in it.
- **At the edge:** The main bitul is "above understanding and grasp". Beyond it the text uses the Zohar: "all binding and unity is to hide in the hidden place, not grasped and not known".
- **Into the day:** At the Amidah today, try for one moment not to watch yourself praying.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Second Gate, ch. 33 (inner and surrounding reversed; what we grasp is "inner", what we cannot is "surrounding")

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 33 (inner and surrounding reversed; what we grasp is "inner", what we cannot is "surrounding"), lines 495-506.
- **The movement:** It turns the picture around twice. Looking up from below, Ein Sof is the innermost. Looking down from Him, this physical world is the innermost and He surrounds all. And then it defines "surrounding" in one simple, freeing sentence.
- **The line to hold** (line 506): > מה שנשכיל התפשטותו בעולמות לזה יקרא פנימי ומה שאי אפשר להשיג נקרא מקיף > > *What we understand of His spreading through the worlds is called "inner", and what cannot be grasped is called "surrounding".*
- **Contemplating it:** From below: every world is a shell to the one above, and the one above is its core, "until all rises to Ein Sof". The deeper you look, the more you see that all is Ein Sof. He is the innermost. From above it is reversed. The physical world is innermost, and He surrounds everything, circle over circle: heavens, angels, worlds, and around all of them, the circle of Ein Sof. The picture: a great sage explains something to students. What he explains is "inside" their minds. What he holds back, the rest of his wisdom, "surrounds" it on every side. To the sage, all of it is one wisdom. So: what we understand is called inner; what we cannot grasp is called surrounding. "I am first and I am last": first from inside, last from around. "He spreads the earth, by Me alone." Notice what you understand about God right now. Call it inner. Then notice all you do not understand, pressing in from every side. Do not call it darkness. Call it the surrounding, and let it hold you.
- **From inside:** The not-knowing stops feeling like a gap and starts feeling like an embrace. Many feel held from all sides by what they cannot grasp.
- **At the edge:** "From His side there is no dividing between inner and surrounding, for He is one." The makif is precisely "what cannot be grasped".
- **Into the day:** When you do not understand something today, say: this is the surrounding, and it holds me.
- **Ladder:** L04

## Second Gate, ch. 34 (even the contractions are Him; grasp differs, bitul is equal)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 34 (even the contractions are Him; grasp differs, bitul is equal), lines 507-512.
- **The movement:** It levels the field. People differ in how much they grasp. They do not differ in how nullified they can be. Prince, servant and commoner stand before the king alike.
- **The line to hold** (line 510): > אבל בביטולם כולם שוים שבטלים לעצמותו ברוך הוא > > *But in their nullification they are all equal, for they are nullified to His Essence.*
- **Contemplating it:** "I am first and I am last, and there is no god with Me in the middle." Even the middle, the contractions, is Ein Sof alone. He is "between every sefirah, within every sefirah, and above every sefirah". How much shows depends on the awakening below. So the Torah names Him differently: sometimes Havayah, sometimes Ehyeh, sometimes Elokim, "according to the receivers". Some grasp Godliness as it is in Asiyah, some in Yetzirah, some in Beriah. "He has no known name." Then the line. In grasp people differ. In bitul all are equal. The prince, the servant and the commoner grasp the king's greatness differently. But before the king they are nullified alike. Moshe's grasp was in Beriah, "where the yesh is fully nullified into ayin". Atzilut itself "no man ever grasped". Our awakening reaches to uniting Zeir and Nukva. Think of someone you consider far above you in understanding. Before God, your bitul and theirs are the same. And think of someone you consider below you. The same.
- **From inside:** Relief and dignity at once. The fear of not understanding enough loosens. What matters most is open to anyone: to stand before the King with nothing held back.
- **At the edge:** Atzilut, His showing "as it is to Him", "no man ever grasped". "No man shall see Me and live."
- **Into the day:** Before prayer today, say: I may understand little, but I can bow as fully as anyone.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Second Gate, ch. 35 (a body without a soul; the teacher delighted by his student)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 35 (a body without a soul; the teacher delighted by his student), lines 513-521.
- **The movement:** It shows how our small work reaches where we cannot. We unite what we can; His delight in it raises it the rest of the way, like a teacher who, seeing his student grasp, is moved to give from his deepest wisdom.
- **The line to hold** (line 515): > ממילא נתעלה יחוד הזה עד בחינת ההשתוות אשר בכח האין סוף ברוך הוא > > *By itself this unity rises to the equality that is in the power of Ein Sof.*
- **Contemplating it:** As they appear, the worlds are "a body without a soul": separate, His unity not showing. When we unite them, when "we see in all things the showing of His light, unchanged", then His light is drawn, and we feel it, the way a body feels its soul. Our awakening cannot reach "before Atzilut". There is no "below" there to awaken. But His intention is that upper and lower be equal. So when we nullify the worlds in our measure, "by itself this unity rises". The picture: a teacher narrows his wisdom for a student. The student works hard and grasps it fully. The teacher is delighted, and from his delight he opens his deepest wisdom and gives more. The student could not reach the teacher's depth. The teacher's delight did. "That is our service: to unite the yesh and the limit to Him", as the body is nullified to the soul. The rest rises through His delight. Think of one small piece of your service, a blessing, a kindness. You cannot see where it goes. Picture a teacher smiling at a student who finally understood. Let that be enough.
- **From inside:** Trust. The pressure to reach the top eases. One's small work feels received, even enjoyed, by the One who can carry it further.
- **At the edge:** His equality "is not grasped at all, first because it is beyond grasp, and second because there is no other to reveal it to". The rising is His, not ours.
- **Into the day:** Do one mitzvah today carefully, and then let it go, trusting it is received.

## Second Gate, ch. 36 ("like the appearance of a man": the four worlds as one person)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 36 ("like the appearance of a man": the four worlds as one person), lines 522-526.
- **The movement:** It lays the four worlds over one human being: body, feelings, mind and life. What makes a person a person is the life in him. So in all the worlds, look, and there is only His power.
- **The line to hold** (line 524): > נמצא כד תסתכל אשר בכל העולמות לא נמצא רק כחו יתברך לבדו > > *So when you look, in all the worlds there is found only His power alone.*
- **Contemplating it:** A person has a body, feelings that guide it, a mind that guides the feelings, and a source of mind. Above the mind is the joining of soul and body. And yet he is one person. What makes him "a man"? Not the body. "A body without life is only flesh; you would not call it a man, only a man's flesh." He is called a man for the life in him. So the worlds: Asiyah, the visible world, is the body; Yetzirah, His traits, the angels that run it, are the feelings; Beriah, the mind; and Atzilut, His power that gives all of them life. The worlds are named for that life. And yet the body is how the soul shows. "Without Asiyah there is no place at all for His power to show." So Atzilut itself is called "the man of Asiyah". Look at someone you love. You do not love their flesh; you love the life in them. Now look at the world that way. What makes it the world is the life in it, His power alone.
- **From inside:** A shift in how one sees a crowd, a street, a forest. Many describe the world beginning to look alive, as a face does.
- **At the edge:** Atzilut itself "is not grasped at all": "no man shall see Me and live". Before Atzilut there is no parable at all.
- **Into the day:** Look at one person today and remind yourself: I am seeing the life in them.
- **Ladder:** L03

## Second Gate, ch. 37 (borrowed names; the space between heaven and earth; "He stands behind our wall")

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 37 (borrowed names; the space between heaven and earth; "He stands behind our wall"), lines 527-536.
- **The movement:** It ends the practical teaching of the gate with a verse from the Song of Songs. The Beloved stands behind "our" wall. The wall is ours; for Him there is no wall. Our work is to take it down, at least on our side.
- **The line to hold** (line 536): > וזהו שכתוב הנה זה עומד אחר כתלינו כי הכותל המה הצמצומים ולגבי דידן הוא כותל > > *This is what is written, "Behold, he stands behind our wall": the wall is the contractions, and toward us it is a wall.*
- **Contemplating it:** A soul's powers, as they are in the soul alone, can only be described by borrowing names from the body. So too the levels above Atzilut: by borrowing, never by inquiry. Above the Keter of Adam Kadmon, not even borrowing. The soul too has hidden powers, and they show when the body is refined: "some merit nefesh, some ruach, some neshamah", each at different depths. The Ari's "empty space" between Ein Sof and the worlds is like the space between heaven and earth. Everything on earth comes from heaven, "no blade of grass without a star", and yet between them there looks to be a great distance. Then the verse. "Behold, he stands behind our wall." Our wall: the contractions. Toward us it is a wall. Toward Him there is none. "All our service is to remove the wall even for us, through unity and self-giving in Shema, prayer, Torah and mitzvot." Picture someone you love standing on the other side of a thin wall, close enough to hear. The wall is real to you. To them it is nothing. Say one word through it.
- **From inside:** Nearness, and longing. The Beloved is close, the wall is thin, and it is ours. Many feel the Song of Songs' tenderness come into the whole of this long abstract gate.
- **At the edge:** "His being, not in His spreading into worlds, has no parable at all." In the future "the glory of God will be revealed" and "God alone will be exalted on that day".
- **Into the day:** Say the Shema today as if speaking through a thin wall to someone who is right there.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Second Gate, ch. 38 (three measures; the potter's wheel)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 38 (three measures; the potter's wheel), lines 537-544.
- **The movement:** It gathers everything into three ways of seeing God, and gives one homely picture of how His changes come "from our side": a potter's wheel, which shapes the vessel by the way it is turned.
- **The line to hold** (line 539): > כן הוא בבחינת עולמות כאשר התחתונים מתנהגים בחסד אזי מאיר עליהם גם כן מבחינת החסד > > *So it is with the worlds: when those below conduct themselves with kindness, it shines on them too from kindness.*
- **Contemplating it:** Three measures. First, the worlds as "a man": the world a body, His traits and mind within it, His power giving life to all. Here we may speak by parable. Second, the root of the worlds in His power, "Adam Kadmon", a man only by borrowing. Third, Ein Sof Himself, where not even borrowing applies. A man changes: he loves, then the opposite; he learns something and his mind grows. That is because things are outside him. Nothing is outside God, so He does not change or grow from the worlds. Then the Zohar's picture: a potter's wheel. It keeps turning. Turn it to the right, and the vessel takes one shape; to the left, another. The changes in the vessels come from the turning. So the worlds. When those below act with kindness, kindness shines on them from Him; and the opposite. "All the changes are from our side." Toward Him, all the traits are one power. Think of how your day felt: kind or harsh. Now ask how you turned the wheel. Not to blame yourself, but to see that your kindness is part of how His light comes to you.
- **From inside:** A sense of partnership without pride. What comes down is shaped by what goes up. Many find this makes small kindnesses feel weighty and hopeful.
- **At the edge:** At the third measure, "we may not speak even by borrowing". There the powers are "hidden and stored away", and we must speak of an "empty space".
- **Into the day:** Turn the wheel once on purpose today: do one act of kindness first thing.
- **Ladder:** L13

## Second Gate, ch. 39 (the image of a man holds upper and lower; "and He breathed into his nostrils"; Havayah is Elokim)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 39 (the image of a man holds upper and lower; "and He breathed into his nostrils"; Havayah is Elokim), lines 545-552.
- **The movement:** It shows the human form as the meeting-place of the unlimited and the limited. And it ends the long teaching on the divine "faces" with a plain sentence: the Name that is beyond and the Name that hides are one.
- **The line to hold** (line 549): > ובאמת הוי"ה הוא אלקי"ם שהכל הוא כח אחד > > *And in truth Havayah is Elokim, for all is one power.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Idra says: "The image of man is the image of upper and lower, included in it." Upper is His unlimited power; lower is His power in limit. His perfection holds both. A person holds both too: soul and body, far apart, one image. The "repairs" of the Ancient One and of Zeir Anpin are made in this image. Zeir Anpin is the unity of the parts, like a body's limbs working as one. The Ancient One is the root, like the soul holding all the limbs in its power. "And He formed" is spelled with two yuds: a form within a form. Elokim is His power contracted into levels; Havayah is His power of equality. And in truth, Havayah is Elokim: all is one power. "And He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life": into this whole "man" of upper and lower, the breath of Ein Sof Himself, which never divides into levels. All of it so that His perfection can show, "from level to level to the end of all levels". Put your hand on your face, the human image. It holds the limitless and the limited in one form. Say the line: the Name beyond and the Name hidden in nature are one.
- **From inside:** A quiet dignity in being human. The body is not a disguise; it is part of the image. Many find the line "Havayah is Elokim" steadies them: nature is not a rival to God.
- **At the edge:** "The breath of all life above and below hangs on that breath", Ein Sof, "which does not descend into levels at all". The text says it shortened this and relies on the reader's depth.
- **Into the day:** Look in a mirror once today and say: an image of upper and lower.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Second Gate, ch. 40 (the conclusion: first unite through da'at, then be nullified above it)

- **Where:** Second Gate, ch. 40 (the conclusion: first unite through da'at, then be nullified above it), lines 553-562.
- **The movement:** It closes the gate by turning the whole teaching into service in two stages. First, bind the worlds to Him through knowing. Then go past knowing: be nullified above it, and step out of the limits of your own vessels.
- **The line to hold** (line 558): > להבטל למעלה מן הדעת ולהבין שהכל הוא לבדו יתברך > > *To be nullified above da'at, and to understand that all is He alone.*
- **Contemplating it:** All the worlds are bound to Ein Sof in one binding; "from the top of all levels to the end of all levels, all is His Essence alone". The repairs exist only to show "that there is none besides Him". When the repairs show His Essence, they themselves melt into their source, "and only His Essence shows, as He was alone before creation". This is done "through the service of those below". The order of service is from below up. First, unite the worlds in "Adam", through da'at: "Know today and return it to your heart." Then rise from there "to be nullified to the Essence of Ein Sof, above da'at". The sages say both: "cleave to His traits" and "to Him, not to His traits". At the start of service, you cannot cleave to Ein Sof; cleave to His traits, through knowing. After you have united the worlds, "the main bitul is to Him": above knowing. "A person must go out of the bounds of his vessels, and raise his thought and binding above the vessels." Do both today, in order. First, think one truth through until you know it: all is from Him. Then, without adding another thought, let the knower step back, and just be before Him.
- **From inside:** A two-step quiet: the fullness of having understood, and then the lighter quiet of not needing to hold it. Many describe the second as a kind of rest after work.
- **At the edge:** This is the book's edge: "above da'at", "the hidden place, not cleaved to and not known". The author adds that these rules are "received mouth to mouth, without human reasoning".
- **Into the day:** After you pray today, sit one moment without any thought, only before Him.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Third Gate, ch. 1 (the gate's three questions; the "long face" that reaches the lowest place)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 1 (the gate's three questions; the "long face" that reaches the lowest place), lines 563-571.
- **The movement:** It opens with real questions a person asks: does God watch every detail, or does He hide His face? Then it gives the first answer: everything lives this moment only from Him, and His "long face" reaches the lowest place without shortening.
- **The line to hold** (line 564): > ואלו היה הסתלקות כוחו יתברך חס ושלום מהעולמות אפילו רגע אחד היו מתבטלים לגמרי והיו כלא היו > > *If His power withdrew from the worlds even for one moment, God forbid, they would be completely nullified, as if they had never been.*
- **Contemplating it:** The gate names its questions. Many verses say He watches every detail: "He looks to the ends of the earth." Others say He hides: "I will hide My face from them." Which? And how can He know what we will choose, if we are free? First answer: all worlds live only from His Essence. If His power withdrew for one moment, they would be as if they never were. The thin line of that power is called the kav. Then two ways He shows. One is "Arich Anpin", the long face. The Zohar's picture: a man in a lit room cannot see into the dark; but "He knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with Him". His providence reaches the lowest place with no shortening, like something long that reaches far down. This is why "You do great wonders": the being of every creature, the ties between them, their natures, all are beyond any wisdom. Think of the darkest corner of your life, the part you think He does not reach. The long face reaches there, with no shortening. It is lit for Him, even when it is dark for you.
- **From inside:** A sense of being reached. The distant and hidden parts of oneself do not feel abandoned. Many feel a steady, unhurried safety.
- **At the edge:** The second way, the "small face", is where His providence is measured and hidden. How the two are one is "a wonder": "how can these two opposites be in one subject".
- **Into the day:** Before sleep, name one hidden corner of your life and say: reached.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Third Gate, ch. 2 (the "small face" and why it needs vessels)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 2 (the "small face" and why it needs vessels), lines 572-574.
- **The movement:** It explains why God's showing needs "vessels" at all. A thing that stays what it is needs no vessel; only changing one thing into another does. So the vessels are what let the endless appear as limited things, each one itself.
- **The line to hold** (line 573): > כמו למשל מים לשתות אין צריך לשינויים ואין נצרך שום כלי לזה > > *For example, water to drink needs no change, and no vessel is needed for it.*
- **Contemplating it:** The "small face", Zeir Anpin, is His power narrowed so that each creature can appear as itself: each kind, each world, each thing in its own place and measure. "No level is like its neighbor." So there are vessels. The picture: to drink water you need no process. But to make water into another drink, or to make fire from water, you need a vessel. A wise man speaking to another wise man needs nothing; to reach a child he needs tools. So wisdom, understanding and kindness are called vessels: they change His simple, endless power into many limited things. "In a simple thing, wisdom does not apply." In the long face, there are no vessels at all; His power is equal everywhere. Drink a glass of water slowly. Nothing had to change for you to receive it. Now think of everything else you receive: bread, work, a friend. Each came through vessels, through changes. Behind them is the same simple water.
- **From inside:** A gentle sorting of the simple from the made. Many feel drawn toward the simple: the plain water under all the drinks.
- **At the edge:** The passage stays with the vessels. Their root, the power that needs no vessel, is named but not opened.
- **Into the day:** Drink one glass of water today with nothing added, and say: simple, from Him.
- **Ladder:** L04

## Third Gate, ch. 3 (a king's bright face and angry face: one life in both)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 3 (a king's bright face and angry face: one life in both), lines 575-580.
- **The movement:** It gives the mind a human picture for God's changing "faces": a king who sometimes shows a warm face and sometimes an angry one. The face changes; the life in it does not.
- **The line to hold** (line 578): > אבל בבחינת המשכות עצמו המתמשך הכל הוא המשכת עצם חיותו בפנים הזו ובפנים הזו ואין הבדל ביניהם כי אם בהתגלות הפנים > > *But in what of himself is drawn forth, it is all his very life, in this face and in that face; there is no difference between them except in how the face shows.*
- **Contemplating it:** With Him all levels are equal. The changes come from the vessels, which hide His light for creatures. The human picture breaks at one point: a man is changed by what he narrows himself into, because it is outside him. For God, even the vessels are not outside. "He, His life and His causes are one." The Idra says: "Zeir Anpin is Atika, with no separation; but the paths divide, and from our side they differ." From our side, precisely. So the differences are all in the "face". A king shows his servants his greatness, or speaks at their level; shows a glad face, or an angry one. In all of them, it is the king's own life. Remember a time God's face seemed angry to you, when things went hard. Remember a time it seemed bright. Hold both memories together. The same life was in both faces.
- **From inside:** A steadiness through the moods of life. The hard days and the kind days are two faces of one presence. Many find this eases the fear that God has changed toward them.
- **At the edge:** The text will not let the picture stand fully: God "does not feel His narrowing", as a king does. "From our side precisely" they change.
- **Into the day:** On a hard day, say: this is His face today; the life in it is the same.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Third Gate, ch. 4 (the open eye, the brows, the nose: "life and the life of life")

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 4 (the open eye, the brows, the nose: "life and the life of life"), lines 581-583.
- **The movement:** It reads the features of the divine "face" as ways His care reaches us. The open eye never sleeps. The nose breathes out two kinds of life: the life of creatures, and "the life of life", their being bound back into Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 583): > וחיין דחיין הם התאחדותם וקישורם וביטולם לעצמותו יתברך > > *And "the life of life" is their union, their binding and their nullification to His Essence.*
- **Contemplating it:** The differences between the two faces are only in the face: forehead, eyes, nose, beard, mouth. Arich's eye is "an open eye that never sleeps": providence over every detail without pause. "Your eyes are open to all the ways of men." Zeir's eyes have brows that cover them: providence measured to each creature, angel, person, animal, plant. And sometimes it "sleeps": "Awake, why do You sleep?" That is when only the creatures show, not His unity. The nose: from one nostril "life", from the other "the life of life". Life is a creature's own vitality. The life of life is its being bound and nullified to Him. The gloss on the forehead: a king's hidden will shows on his face when his servants do his will and he delights in it. So His will "lights up" when those below do it. Notice your own aliveness right now: warm, awake. That is life. Now ask: what in you leans back toward Him, wants to be bound to Him? That is the life of life. Feel both.
- **From inside:** A double aliveness: the ordinary vitality of the body, and under it a quieter life that leans toward its Source. Many recognize the second as the thing they were missing.
- **At the edge:** The will behind His delight, "Atik Yomin", "is not known at all". "How can pleasure be ascribed to Him?" It is named only from its effects.
- **Into the day:** When you feel fully alive today, add: and bound to You.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Third Gate, ch. 5 (why create at all: "Be silent, so it rose in thought")

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 5 (why create at all: "Be silent, so it rose in thought"), lines 584-589.
- **The movement:** It takes the mind to the question under all questions: why would the One create a world of hiding? Reason says He should not. The answer is "will", which has no reason, like the silence Moshe was told to keep.
- **The line to hold** (line 586): > כי אין טעם לרצון כמו שתוק כך עלה במחשבה > > *For will has no reason, like "Be silent; so it rose in thought."*
- **Contemplating it:** Nothing can be said of Him that implies change, and yet we see intention, wisdom and care in every creature. How? The Kabbalists explain the contraction, the breaking, the repair, all to show He is unchanged. Why create? "To reveal His unity in creatures, precisely from their side." But since nothing is outside Him, making a world of hiding is "the opposite of reason". So it is called "will". The Talmud's picture: Moshe saw Rabbi Akiva's Torah, and then his terrible death, and asked, "This is Torah, and this its reward?" He was told: "Be silent. So it rose in thought." Will stands between two opposites: the Emanator, where there is nothing besides Him, and the emanated, where there are many. It is called pele, wonder, and tohu, "that astonishes people". Bring your own "why" to the table: the question you most want answered about your life. Say it. Then, gently, be silent. Not because it does not matter, but because this is where His will is.
- **From inside:** A quiet that has weight. Not resignation, but the silence one keeps before someone greater. Many find it holds grief better than any answer.
- **At the edge:** This is the edge: will "above reason", "for which there is no reason". The text stops at "Be silent".
- **Into the day:** When a "why" comes today that you cannot answer, keep one moment of silence before Him.
- **Ladder:** L08

## Third Gate, ch. 6 (the skull and the hidden brain: every will has a hidden wisdom)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 6 (the skull and the hidden brain: every will has a hidden wisdom), lines 590-592.
- **The movement:** It shows the mind that even a will with no stated reason has a hidden wisdom inside it. The mind learns to trust that there is sense in what it cannot see.
- **The line to hold** (line 590): > כי לכל רצון יש חכמה סתימאה ומכוון שברצון אך הוא סתים > > *For every will has a hidden wisdom and intention inside it, but it is closed.*
- **Contemplating it:** In the long face there are two parts: the skull and the hidden brain inside it. The skull is His will from His own side, "it rose in thought", with no reason given. Nothing shows from it, like bone. The hidden brain is the order of creation inside that will: every creature's place and measure, every contraction, "all by wisdom". It is called "hidden wisdom", though it is not really wisdom as we know it. The picture: a person is drawn to something before he can say why. Later, when his mind works it out, the reason comes. But the reason was there from the start, closed. From this hidden wisdom come all the "hairs", the channels of the small face, the wisdom we can see in nature. Think of a turn in your life you did not choose and could not understand. The text says a will like that has a hidden wisdom inside it. You do not need to see it to trust it is there.
- **From inside:** Trust that is not naive. The mind can say "I do not understand" and "there is sense in it" at the same time.
- **At the edge:** The skull is "bone that reveals nothing". How the intention is in Him "has no grasp at all".
- **Into the day:** Once today, when something makes no sense, say: there is a hidden wisdom in this.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Third Gate, ch. 7 (three heads, and when you look, one)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 7 (three heads, and when you look, one), lines 593-595.
- **The movement:** It lays out three "heads" of the divine, one inside the other, and then collapses them: when you look closely, they are one. It also names the two unities in the Zohar's words.
- **The line to hold** (line 593): > אבל כד תסתכל בהון כולא הוא בחינה אחת > > *But when you look into them, all is one.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Idra Zuta: "Three heads are engraved, one inside the other, one above the other." The first is the Ancient One, "the head that is not a head": from the Emanator's side, where no creature can be ascribed. The second is the skull: His will to show Himself. The third is the hidden wisdom: the order of creation inside that will. "But when you look into them, all is one": the power of His Essence, not grasped at all. The three are only "from the side of the drawing-forth". Then the two unities: His drawing toward creatures, Zeir Anpin, is "the higher unity"; His unity shown in creatures, the Shechinah, is "the lower unity". Both in Him without change. Try it with yourself: your will, your reasons, your plans. Three layers. Look closely: one you. Then with Him: three heads, and look closely, one.
- **From inside:** A settling, as if three voices in the mind became one. Many find the phrase "when you look" is itself the practice.
- **At the edge:** The first head is "the head not known". "What is in that head is not known."
- **Into the day:** When your day feels like many pulls, look closely once and say: one.

## Third Gate, ch. 8 (the Shema: bringing the higher unity down until nothing is separate)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 8 (the Shema: bringing the higher unity down until nothing is separate), lines 596-598.
- **The movement:** It turns the whole map into what a person does in the Shema. You contemplate the higher unity, and carry it down into the hidden world, until nothing is separate. From that comes the readiness to give your life.
- **The line to hold** (line 596): > הכל אין דבר זולתו להיות בחינת נפרד ומחמת זה יהיה הביטול בבחינת מסירות נפש > > *All of it, there is nothing besides Him to be separate; and from this the nullification will be as giving up one's life.*
- **Contemplating it:** "All our service is to unite the Holy One and His Shechinah from our side": to show His unity even below. How? In the Shema. First contemplate the higher unity, the worlds as they are united in His power. Then bring it into the lower: even in His kingship hidden in the lower worlds, "there is nothing besides Him to be separate". And from that, the bitul becomes mesirus nefesh, the readiness to give up one's life: the self held so lightly it could be given. Then, through prayer, Torah and mitzvot, "light is added to the vessels". The addition is in the vessels, in how much shows. Nothing is added to Him. Say the first line of the Shema slowly. On "One", let the thought go down from the heavens to your own room, your own body, until nothing is left separate. Then notice how lightly you hold yourself.

- **At the edge:** The passage names the goal but not how it feels in the heights. "In His Essence the vessels do not grip Him at all."
- **Into the day:** Say the Shema once today with eyes covered, bringing "One" all the way down into your room.

## Third Gate, ch. 9 (the two faces shine into each other when we unite them)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 9 (the two faces shine into each other when we unite them), lines 599-607.
- **The movement:** It shows the "small face" and the "long face" lighting each other up, and makes it depend on service. When people unite the worlds, the stern face becomes long and gentle; when they do not, it stays narrow.
- **The line to hold** (line 600): > ואז מאירין שני בחינות המשכות זה בזה ונעשו לאחדים ונעשו פניו אריכין כמותו אפילו בבחינת נבראים > > *Then the two drawings-forth shine into each other and become one, and His face becomes long like the other, even in creatures.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Idra says: when Zeir Anpin looks toward the Ancient One, "all below is set right, and his face spreads out and grows long". When those below do not unite the worlds, His providence stays narrow: red eyes, a forehead of judgment, an angry nose. When they do, even through contraction, His equality shines, "the eyes are washed, the nose is sweetened". So "God was angry", "evil in God's eyes", "you weakened the Rock that bore you": these are about His reach toward us, not His Essence. The hairs: the long face's hair is white, soft and even, "all in one balance". The small face's is black and tangled, because the levels mix and need each other, as a minister needs a servant and a servant a minister. Think of a harsh stretch in your life. Then think of one act of uniting you did: a blessing said with heart, a kindness. The text says that act lengthens His face toward you.
- **From inside:** Agency without anxiety. The stern face is not fixed; it softens as the worlds are joined. Many feel hope that their small service changes how His face turns.
- **At the edge:** "Toward Him all is equal." The changes are "in the vessels", where His purpose for creation lies.
- **Into the day:** Do one act of uniting today, a blessing with full attention, and say: may Your face be long.
- **Ladder:** L13

## Third Gate, ch. 10 ("a man's wisdom lights up his face"; the hand of God and the great hand)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 10 ("a man's wisdom lights up his face"; the hand of God and the great hand), lines 608-610.
- **The movement:** It gives two plain pictures. A person's face shows his wisdom. And God's "hand" is the power we see in the world: in the ordinary order it is "the hand of God", in a miracle it is "the great hand".
- **The line to hold** (line 610): > כי יד נקרא כח המתגלה בבחינת עשיה > > *For "hand" means the power revealed in doing.*
- **Contemplating it:** "A man's wisdom lights up his face." In the long face, wisdom is closed and undivided, so what shows is undivided. In the small face, the mind divides into three, so what shows is measured. The Idra: "as if two brains joined outside and became one brain". "As if": from the outside they look like two; in truth "the lower brain stands only by the upper". Then the arms. A person does everything with his hands: makes, carries, lifts. His strength shows in them. So His power in the world is His "hand". When the world runs by its order, by wisdom and kindness, it is "the hand of God", the small face. When His wonders show above the order, the sea splitting, vinegar burning like oil, it is "the great hand". Look at your hands. They are where your strength becomes deeds. Then look at the ordinary world, bread rising, rain falling: His hand. And remember one moment above the ordinary: His great hand.
- **From inside:** The ordinary world looks handled, held. And the rare wonder feels like the same hand, only uncovered.
- **At the edge:** The passage stays with what is shown. It says only that in the long face, wisdom is "closed, not divided at all", beyond knowing.
- **Into the day:** Before you use your hands for work today, say: His hand, through mine.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Third Gate, ch. 11 (even a miracle is clothed; the feet; Sinai and the dew of Torah)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 11 (even a miracle is clothed; the feet; Sinai and the dew of Torah), lines 611-618.
- **The movement:** It shows that even miracles come dressed in nature: the sea still water, the vinegar still vinegar. Then it takes the mind to Sinai, where the Essence showed, souls flew out, and the dew of Torah brought them back.
- **The line to hold** (line 618): > כי בחינת טל תורה הוא התקשרותו מצד עצמותו אשר מצד עצמותו שוין היש והאין > > *For the dew of Torah is His binding from His Essence, where "something" and "nothing" are equal.*
- **Contemplating it:** The long face's arms come clothed in the small face. Even at the sea, His wonder was dressed: water that flows down stood like a wall. Vinegar that does not burn, burned. The water and the vinegar were still vessels. Only in the future "He will bare His holy arm". The feet of all the faces end together at the "floor of Atzilut" and do not show below. Feet are for standing, and if they stood in our world, nothing could exist. "The earth is My footstool." At Sinai, "God came down upon Mount Sinai", and "all the people saw the voices". At every word, their souls flew out: the yesh was nullified. Then "the dew of Torah" revived them. That dew is His binding from His Essence, where yesh and ayin are equal. Only from there could something both be nullified and live. Think of a moment of Torah that overwhelmed you, or a truth too big. Then think of the plain, gentle study that brought you back. The text calls that dew: it restores you from the place where being and not-being are one.
- **From inside:** The sense that Torah can both undo you and restore you. Many feel a gentleness in study, like dew, after the storm of a large truth.
- **At the edge:** At Babel and Sodom, the same revealing nullified the levels, "for the need of the hour". The feet will show only "on that day".
- **Into the day:** After a hard moment today, learn one line of Torah gently, as dew.
- **Ladder:** L14

## Third Gate, ch. 12 (the long face has no ears; "He who planted the ear, shall He not hear?")

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 12 (the long face has no ears; "He who planted the ear, shall He not hear?"), lines 619-623.
- **The movement:** It removes even hearing from the long face, since hearing takes in something from outside, and there is no outside. Then it returns the prayer: every cry reaches Him. How, is above the mind.
- **The line to hold** (line 621): > הגם שכל תפלה באה אליו דייקא כמו שכתוב הנוטע אוזן הלא ישמע > > *Though every prayer comes to Him precisely, as it says, "He who planted the ear, shall He not hear?"*
- **Contemplating it:** The long face's head, eyes and nose do not come clothed in our vessels. We see His providence and His life only through their effects. If the life itself were revealed, "no creature would exist". Its mouth is "closed": it does not speak to those below. Its root is the source of all speech; at Sinai it opened; in the future "they will see eye to eye". And the long face has no ears. Ears take in a voice from someone else. There is no one else before Him. And yet: "every prayer comes to Him, precisely". "He who planted the ear, shall He not hear?" How, "is above the mind". In the small face there are ears to hear the cry, and a mouth: "the word of God". Say a prayer today, even one sentence. Know two things at once: there is no one outside Him to hear from, and your words reach Him, precisely. Do not solve it. Pray inside it.
- **From inside:** Prayer feels less like shouting across a distance and more like speaking inside a presence that already holds you.
- **At the edge:** "How hearing is ascribed to Him is above the mind." The ears are "hidden".
- **Into the day:** Say one prayer today, short and honest, trusting it arrives.
- **Ladder:** L04

## Third Gate, ch. 13 ("Is there right and left before Him?": all opposites one)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 13 ("Is there right and left before Him?": all opposites one), lines 624-628.
- **The movement:** It gathers every opposite the Torah uses of God, right and left, standing and sitting, front and back, and says: all of these are ours. Before Him, all opposites are one, held by faith above the mind.
- **The line to hold** (line 627): > וכל ההפכים אצלו יתברך ביחודא חד בלי שום פרודא כלל > > *And all opposites, with Him, are in one unity, with no separation at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** All the differences between the long face and the small face are in how they reach us. Toward Him, "Zeir Anpin is Atika". The human picture fails: a person feels the change in himself as he narrows his mind. With Him, all these powers are "one power without change". "He evens the small and the great, and bears the upper and lower in one subject." So the sages ask: "Is there right and left before the Holy One?" and say "above there is no right or left, no standing or sitting, no front or back". Yet the verses say "Your right hand, God". Both are true. We say right and left because everything comes from Him. But He is not described by them. That is why these are called "the secret of faith": no mind can grasp them, "only believe with faith above the mind". Think of two things you hold that pull against each other: justice and mercy, closeness and awe. Do not choose. Say: before Him, these are one.
- **From inside:** The tension between opposites stops feeling like a problem to solve. Many feel something like relief: they do not have to settle what He holds together.
- **At the edge:** "The secret of faith, above the minds of creatures." The text gives no picture here, only faith.
- **Into the day:** When two good things seem to clash today, say: in Him they are one.
- **Ladder:** L08

## Third Gate, ch. 14 ("above the mind" is not "too deep for the mind"; "who does wonders")

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 14 ("above the mind" is not "too deep for the mind"; "who does wonders"), lines 629-637.
- **The movement:** It defines "above the mind" carefully. It is not a very deep idea we cannot reach. It is what is not in the category of mind at all, like the Ark that took no space. Then it finds that wonder in the blessing a person says after using the bathroom.
- **The line to hold** (line 637): > אבל שורש התקשרות החיות עם הגוף שיהיה כלי לזה לזה חתם ומפליא לעשות > > *But the root of how life binds with the body, so that one is a vessel for the other, he sealed with "who does wonders".*
- **Contemplating it:** "Above the mind" does not mean "too deep", as the Rambam puts it. A deep idea is still an idea. Above the mind is "the ancestor of the mind", not wisdom at all. You cannot say a thought is too heavy to lift with your hands. The sages' examples: the Ark was a cubit and a half wide, yet measured, it took no space. "Keep" and "Remember" were said in one word. Ibn Ezra denied it, "because the light of Kabbalah did not shine on him". Wisdom to Him is like a tool to a man. The tool does not force his hand. He could have made the world any way. And yet we see wisdom in everything: in the body's veins and nerves, in how the brain holds thought and the heart holds feeling. That is why the blessing Asher Yatzar begins "who formed man with wisdom". But how flesh can be a vessel for spirit, no wisdom explains. So the blessing ends: "who does wonders". Next time you say Asher Yatzar, slow down at the last words. "Who does wonders": the wonder that your spirit lives in this body at all.
- **From inside:** A daily wonder in an ordinary place. Many find the body becomes a quiet miracle rather than a machine.
- **At the edge:** Above the mind is "not in the category of wisdom at all". The joining of spirit and flesh "is above the mind".
- **Into the day:** Say Asher Yatzar slowly once today, leaning on "who does wonders".
- **Ladder:** L08

## Third Gate, ch. 15 (three wonders in soul and body, and when you look, one wonder)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 15 (three wonders in soul and body, and when you look, one wonder), lines 638-646.
- **The movement:** It counts three wonders in the joining of soul and body, then shows they are two, then one. The mind is walked from many mysteries to one: "He is able."
- **The line to hold** (line 644): > וכאשר תסתכל יותר הכל הוא פלא אחד שהוא כל יכול > > *And when you look further, all is one wonder: He is able.*
- **Contemplating it:** First wonder: spirit bound to flesh, though they have nothing in common. Second: once bound, where do the body's many parts come from, each built to receive its own power? The eye for sight, the hand for touch, all ordered by a wisdom "hidden in the soul". Third, above both: how the soul and body are "equal in one power", so that the binding could happen at all. "Not known, and not standing to be known." Look again: the three are two, where they came from, and how they are joined. Look further: "all is one wonder: He is able." The text says to remember this as a rule for all of creation: stone, plant, animal, heavens. Look at your eye in a mirror. Three wonders: its form, the sight in it, and that sight and flesh are joined at all. Then let them become one: He is able.
- **From inside:** The mind rests in a single, simple fact after counting many. Many find "He is able" a sentence they can carry all day.
- **At the edge:** The third wonder is "the head not known". The final word is not an explanation but a name for the power: "able".
- **Into the day:** Once today, when you are amazed by anything, say: He is able.
- **Ladder:** L10

## Third Gate, ch. 16 ("flee to your place": bind by faith above knowing)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 16 ("flee to your place": bind by faith above knowing), lines 647-654.
- **The movement:** It takes all the wonders back to the one who made them and stops the mind at the door of "the head that is not a head". The instruction is not to look further, but to bind what is shown to Him by faith.
- **The line to hold** (line 654): > דהיינו לקשר ההתגלות אליו יתברך בבחינת אמונה למעלה מן הדעת > > *That is, to bind what is revealed to Him with faith above knowing.*
- **Contemplating it:** All the powers of all creatures are His power alone. They look separate only because of His kingship, which hides. As they are "beside Him", they are Atzilut. And the three wonders appear again: where each creature's wisdom comes from; how the endless reaches the limited; and how any of it comes from the simple One. We see order and intention, so it must be in Him. But He is not in the category of wisdom. So He is "the head that is not a head", and "what is in that head is not known". Then the instruction: "Flee to your place": bind what is revealed to Him "with faith above knowing", and do not imagine any division in Him. Picture yourself at a door you may not open. Do not force it and do not walk away. Stand there and bind everything you know to the One behind it. That is the practice.
- **From inside:** A trust that is active, not passive. Many find "flee to your place" not a rebuke but a kindness: the mind is sent home, not shamed.
- **At the edge:** This is the edge: "not known what is in that head". Beyond it, faith above knowing.
- **Into the day:** When a thought about God runs too far today, return it with one word: faith.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Third Gate, ch. 17 (the hairs through the skull: "Be silent, so it rose in His will")

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 17 (the hairs through the skull: "Be silent, so it rose in His will"), lines 655-658.
- **The movement:** It pictures the hidden wisdom reaching our world like hair growing through the skull: through bone that reveals nothing. The mind learns that the showing comes only through a hiding, and that the hiding is His will.
- **The line to hold** (line 657): > אך אין לומר על זה כי אם כך עלה ברצונו יתברך > > *But of this one can only say: so it rose in His will.*
- **Contemplating it:** "Wisdom cries out outside." The small face's wisdom, the order of all creatures, flows from the long face's hidden wisdom through "hairs". A hair comes from the head and yet is nothing like the brain. What we see is wisdom in each creature. But the wisdom did not force the creature to be. The source is hidden. The hairs grow through the skull. The skull is bone: nothing of the brain shows through it. So His will is the great hiding. How the Other and the limited come from Him "one can only say: so it rose in His will". "Be silent: so it rose in thought." If His hidden wisdom showed, there could be no divided world. Only through the hiding does anything appear. Touch your head. Under the bone is your thinking; on the outside, hair grows. The hair is not the thought and comes from it. Let this be a picture of the world: grown through a hiding, from a wisdom you cannot see.
- **From inside:** A peace with not-seeing. Many find it touching that the hiding is described as will, not as absence: He chose to be hidden so we could be.
- **At the edge:** "No reason for will." The hidden brain behind the skull is not opened.
- **Into the day:** When you do not understand God's ways today, say: so it rose in His will.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Third Gate, ch. 18 (the growing power hidden in the dust)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 18 (the growing power hidden in the dust), lines 659-663.
- **The movement:** It gives the most earthy picture in the gate: the power that makes plants grow is hidden in dust, and only through that hiding does it show. So His showing needs His great hiding.
- **The line to hold** (line 661): > בכדי שיהיה התגלות כח הצומח מוסתר כח הצומח בארץ ובעפר שהוא דומם גשמי > > *In order for the growing power to show, the growing power is hidden in the earth and the dust, which is lifeless matter.*
- **Contemplating it:** A teacher who wants to reach a student far below him hides his real intention completely. He shows only that he wants to teach, and then narrows his thought many times, until he speaks at the student's size. So God's intention in creating is "very deep", so deep it is not even "thought". To reach us, it had to be hidden completely by His will. The picture: for a thing to become another, the first must be as if gone, "ayin". The growing power hides in the dust, lifeless matter, and only through that hiding do plants rise. So too from plant to animal to person. The picture breaks: in the garden, growth goes from below up. Here it goes from above down. And the text says there are countless "skulls", hidings, from level to level, and the whole repair depends on them. Put your hand in soil, or picture it. Dark, lifeless. The whole garden is hidden in it. Your darkest, most lifeless stretch may be that soil.
- **From inside:** Patience with the dark and the dull. Many find hope in the image: the lifeless ground is where the growing power waits.
- **At the edge:** The hiding is called ayin. What it hides, "His intention in Ein Sof", is not revealed.
- **Into the day:** Plant something, water a plant, or simply look at soil today, and say: hidden, to grow.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Third Gate, ch. 19 (the teacher and the distracted child; scattered letters gathered into words)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 19 (the teacher and the distracted child; scattered letters gathered into words), lines 664-675.
- **The movement:** It shows the patience of repair. A teacher first gathers a distracted child's mind before he can teach him, and speaks gently, or the child will run. So the broken world is first gathered, like scattered letters into words, before its meaning can show.
- **The line to hold** (line 670): > והאותיות כשהם מתחברים ומתצרפים לתיבה אז נגלה בהם המכוון אבל כשהם מתפרדים אזי אינו נגלה בהם שום מכוון > > *When letters join and combine into a word, the meaning shows in them; but when they are scattered, no meaning shows in them.*
- **Contemplating it:** There are two ways the hidden wisdom reaches us: from behind, to build and keep the world as it is; and from the front, to show His inner intent, that the world be one with Him. That is His main purpose. The picture: a wise man wants to teach a child who is busy with childish things. First he must gather the child's mind, speak at his level, make him a vessel. Only then can he share his real intent. At the breaking, the vessels were like letters scattered: they showed His power as "something", but no meaning. The first repair gathers them into words, and a meaning appears. And "the words of the wise are heard gently". The long face's hair is soft. If the teacher is strict with a far-away child, "he will run away altogether". Think of a scattered part of your life: pieces with no meaning. Do not force meaning into it. Gather it gently, one letter to another, and wait for the word.
- **From inside:** Gentleness toward one's own disorder. Many find permission to repair slowly: meaning comes when the letters join, not before.
- **At the edge:** The root of the joining "is from the power of His Essence, where the power of yesh is with Him in complete equality". How, is not shown.
- **Into the day:** Gather one scattered thing today, a drawer, a list, a relationship, gently.
- **Ladder:** L12

## Third Gate, ch. 20 (the longing of the giver: a second forming, to receive the inner)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 20 (the longing of the giver: a second forming, to receive the inner), lines 676-679.
- **The movement:** It shows why God's inner light needs a new preparing in us, and what moves Him to give it: the giver's longing, which comes from being one with the receiver at the root.
- **The line to hold** (line 679): > וכמשל גודל חשקת ותשוקת המשפיע להשפיע להמקבל מצד התכללותו עמו בכח השפעה אחת > > *Like the great desire and longing of the giver to give to the receiver, from his being included with him in one power of giving.*
- **Contemplating it:** To receive the world's outside, the vessels needed forming, nursing and growing. To receive His inside, His unity, they need it again: "a second forming". The outside makes them vessels; the inside undoes them into Him. These are opposites. The root of this forming is in "the head not known", where limit and no-limit are equal. From there comes the understanding of how to make a limited vessel hold the limitless. The picture: a giver longs to give to a receiver, because at the root they are one. But in how they appear, they are far apart. So the longing becomes understanding: how to prepare the receiver. When the vessel is ready, he gives his real intent. So with Him: His delight and His longing to show His unity come first. From them, the patient work of making us vessels. Think of someone who longed to give you something and waited until you were ready. Feel the patience in that longing. That is the text's picture of how He waits for you.
- **From inside:** Being wanted. Many feel that the delays in their spiritual life are not rejection but preparation, driven by a longing to give.
- **At the edge:** "Hidden wisdom, not opened at all, because of its height." The longing's root is "the head not known".
- **Into the day:** Prepare one small thing today to receive: a clean table for Shabbat, a quiet corner for prayer.
- **Ladder:** L11

## Third Gate, ch. 21 (the thirteen attributes of mercy: love that breaks the rules; "truth")

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 21 (the thirteen attributes of mercy: love that breaks the rules; "truth"), lines 680-689.
- **The movement:** It shows mercy as the love of a father for a son who was once one with him and is now far away. That love grows so strong it breaks its own bounds. And it names the attribute "truth": even the vessels, in truth, are His Essence.
- **The line to hold** (line 684): > כי מצד אמיתיותו יתברך הכל הוא עצמותו ברוך הוא אפילו מצד הכלים > > *For from the side of His truth, all is His Essence, even from the side of the vessels.*
- **Contemplating it:** The beard's hairs are "strong of the strong", to bring down the thirteen attributes of mercy from the Most Ancient. The picture: when a teacher or a father must give to someone far below him, he needs love that overflows. The sages say: "When our love was strong, we lay on a sword's edge", and "love spoils the order of things". Love so strong both go out of their bounds, and meet. A father and son who were once one, now far apart, the father high and the son low: from that comes "great mercy", "compassionate and gracious", "long of face". So the vessels, one with Him at the root and far from Him in showing, draw His mercy. And "truth": "from the side of His truth, all is His Essence, even the vessels". Only the hiding makes them seem far. Think of someone you love who has gone far from you. Feel the pull that would break every rule to reach them. The text says that is the root of His mercy toward you.
- **From inside:** Being loved beyond the rules. Many feel a softening toward themselves: if He is this merciful toward the far, they can be too.
- **At the edge:** If His Essence showed fully, "there would be no room for the vessels". Mercy works through "the light of the face", not full revealing.
- **Into the day:** Say the thirteen attributes, or just "compassionate and gracious", once today, thinking of someone far away.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Third Gate, ch. 22 (two brains become one; one voice of the heart becomes many words)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 22 (two brains become one; one voice of the heart becomes many words), lines 690-693.
- **The movement:** It shows how the one becomes many without ceasing to be one: like the heart's one simple voice becoming countless words in the mouth. All the words are the one voice.
- **The line to hold** (line 692): > וכמו שאנו רואים בקול הנמשך ממנו שהוא קול אחד פשוט > > *As we see in the voice that comes from it: one simple voice.*
- **Contemplating it:** "As if two brains joined outside and became one brain." "As if" is our measuring; "became one" is the truth. The hairs of head and beard are "white as snow": no contraction in them. The picture from a person: in the soul, all powers are one life. They divide into many only when they act on something. And speech: all the combinations of letters come from the feelings, through the "breath of the heart". In the heart they are not separate words. We see this in the voice itself: one simple voice. And yet it becomes countless words in the mouth. So His power in the worlds: one, "Zeir Anpin is Atika". All the powers of all creatures are in it, one. They show as many through His kingship, "the word of God". Say a sentence aloud now. Listen under the words to the one voice carrying them. Then think of the world as His words, carried on one voice.
- **From inside:** Hearing the one under the many. Many find conversations, music, even noise begin to carry a hint of the single voice.
- **At the edge:** How the one voice is in the heart before it divides, "all is included in the heart", is not opened further.
- **Into the day:** When you speak today, notice once the one voice under your many words.

## Third Gate, ch. 23 (Malchut has nothing of its own; the letters of speech)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 23 (Malchut has nothing of its own; the letters of speech), lines 694-699.
- **The movement:** It turns to the power of showing, Malchut, and says: it has nothing of its own. Everything it shows, it receives. Like the letters of speech, which come from a soul they are nothing like.
- **The line to hold** (line 694): > והנה בחינת המלכות לית לה מגרמה כלום > > *And Malchut has nothing of its own.*
- **Contemplating it:** Malchut, His kingship, the power of showing, has nothing of its own. All it has comes from His traits. The channels from the traits to it are "hairs", so that the levels can show in the lower worlds. The picture: a soul has in it will, wisdom and feeling, and also a power of speech, whose work is to show them. That power gathers all the others, "like a lamp that gathers all lights". Then the actual letters come out through the mouth, through the body. They are nothing like the soul: one is spirit, the other breath and lips. And yet they come from it. They are "hairs" to it. So the whole world, spoken as "the word of God", has nothing of its own. It shows what it receives. Think of yourself as speech: you show what you receive. Your gifts, your words, your life have nothing of their own. That is not a loss; it is what makes them able to show Him.
- **From inside:** A humility that feels like freedom. Many find relief in not having to own their gifts: they can simply let them show where they came from.
- **At the edge:** "Before the showing it is not in the measure of revealing at all." How the power of showing is in Him is hidden.
- **Into the day:** When someone praises you today, say inside: nothing of my own.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Third Gate, ch. 24 (red hair: divided and still joined; holding back in speech what is in thought)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 24 (red hair: divided and still joined; holding back in speech what is in thought), lines 700-705.
- **The movement:** It shows why God does not show all that is "in His understanding". A person's thought can rage or love without limit, but in speech he holds back, or no one could bear it. So His judgment comes softened, divided and still joined.
- **The line to hold** (line 704): > אבל בדבור נותן מעצור לרוחו מה לדבר ומה שלא לדבר וכובש כעסו וגם אהבתו > > *But in speech he restrains his spirit, what to say and what not to say, and he holds back his anger and his love too.*
- **Contemplating it:** The small face's hair is black: it shows every detail, even the opposite, so that the unity can show more. Malchut's hair is red, neither white nor black. White would show no division at all. Black, total division, would leave creatures unable to join: "each would swallow the other alive". Red: divided, and still joined, "softened". The gloss gives a picture from inside a person. When a feeling rises, anger or love, the thought runs with it without limit, imagining everything. But in speech a person holds back: what to say, what not. He restrains his anger and even his love. If he spoke all his thought, no one could bear it. So His traits: in His understanding are all the details of every judgment. If they came out as they are there, "the world could not stand". So they come through speech, softened: "the judgments of the feminine are hard at first and soft at the end". Think of the last time you held back a word that would have hurt. That restraint, the text says, is a small copy of how He speaks the world into being: softened, so it can stand.
- **From inside:** A new respect for restraint. Holding back stops feeling like suppression and starts feeling like mercy. Many find it changes how they speak to those they love.
- **At the edge:** The root of the division is "His will, as wonder". Why His will is to show His glory precisely through division is not opened.
- **Into the day:** Hold back one sharp word today, and say inside: softened, so it can stand.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Third Gate, ch. 25 (turning the traits: from the wisdom of nature to the wisdom of God)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 25 (turning the traits: from the wisdom of nature to the wisdom of God), lines 706-709.
- **The movement:** It shows where the struggle really lies. The same wisdom that sees nature can say "I, and none else", or it can see God in nature. The same love that reaches for pleasure can reach for Him. The work is to turn them.
- **The line to hold** (line 708): > ולאהפכא חשוכא לנהורא דהיינו מחכמת טבעיות הנבראים ישיג חכמת אלקו"ת > > *And to turn darkness to light: from the wisdom of the nature of creatures, to reach the wisdom of Godliness.*
- **Contemplating it:** The small face's head hair is from behind: it keeps the world running, each thing in its place. Its beard is from the front: it shows His inner unity in the vessels, to nullify them into Him. Through wisdom, one can understand His unity in creatures. Through kindness, the love that keeps each creature alive, one can come to love Him. Through strength, awe. But the same powers flow to the other side. The wisdom of nature can become "the wisdom of Egypt", "which denies God and His unity, saying I, and none else". The love that sustains can become craving for physical things. So the service: to subdue the other side, and "to turn darkness to light": from the wisdom of nature, reach the wisdom of God; from the love in creatures, reach love of Him. At the sea, "God is a man of war": the Egyptians, the other side, were turned into bitul. Think of one thing you are good at understanding: numbers, people, machines. Turn it once today: see in it a trace of His wisdom. Think of one thing you love: let it lead your love toward Him.
- **From inside:** The sense that nothing in you needs to be thrown away, only turned. Many feel their talents and loves become paths rather than rivals.
- **At the edge:** The passage names the turning but does not reach past the vessels. That comes next: a bitul beyond them.
- **Into the day:** Use one skill today, and afterwards say: this wisdom is Yours.
- **Ladder:** L12

## Third Gate, ch. 26 (two kinds of bitul: in the vessels, and above them)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 26 (two kinds of bitul: in the vessels, and above them), lines 710-714.
- **The movement:** It separates two kinds of self-giving. One happens inside the self: wisdom, love and strength turned toward Him. The other goes out of the self entirely, like love so great one no longer feels the body. The first leads to the second.
- **The line to hold** (line 714): > דהיינו על ידי השגתם בחכמה מתבטלין למעלה מן החכמה > > *That is, through their grasp in wisdom, they are nullified above wisdom.*
- **Contemplating it:** In the long face's beard the vessels are fully nullified: they are only a passage for His Essence. The picture: a person with a love so great he goes out of his vessels and does not feel his body at all. So are those who reach "the stripping off of physicality". In the small face's beard, the bitul is inside the vessels: a person grasps His unity through wisdom, loves Him through kindness, overcomes the other side through strength. That is why the small face's beard lacks the attribute "truth": the bitul is not yet as it is in truth, where there is none besides Him. "Happy is one who holds the King's beard": who turns his traits. But "woe to one who stretches his hand to the holy beard" of the long face: there, bitul is only by faith above knowing. And the bridge: when Israel serve with real self-sacrifice, "through their grasp in wisdom they are nullified above wisdom". Then the small face's beard turns white like the long face's. Think something through about God until you understand it as well as you can. Then, at the end of your understanding, step past it: let the knowing carry you out of yourself for one moment.
- **From inside:** Understanding that ends not in pride but in going beyond it. Many describe a moment where knowing turns into giving.
- **At the edge:** The long face's beard may not be touched: bitul there is "above wisdom and grasp", "by faith above knowing".
- **Into the day:** After you understand something about God today, say: and beyond this, I am Yours.

## Third Gate, ch. 27 (bitul inside the yesh itself: the gem that shines, the bird that speaks)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 27 (bitul inside the yesh itself: the gem that shines, the bird that speaks), lines 715-718.
- **The movement:** It names the book's central aim with a lovely picture. It is no wonder that things melt when His light shows. The wonder, and His delight, is when the solid "something" stays solid and is still nullified: a stone that shines, a bird that talks.
- **The line to hold** (line 715): > אבל בבחינת נוקבא הוא דייקא הכוונה שיהיה נגלה הביטול בבחינת היש דייקא > > *But in Nukva, the intent is precisely that the nullification be revealed in the yesh, precisely there.*
- **Contemplating it:** Malchut has no beard. Nothing flows from her face. Her work is to show everything in its separate detail. And the whole intent is "in heaven above and on the earth below, there is no other": below, precisely. In the small face, the unity shows by the yesh melting. In Malchut, the intent is that the bitul show in the yesh itself, while it stays yesh. That is the purpose of Torah and mitzvot: His will shown precisely in the physical. The picture: a gem that shines is precious and beautiful. A candle shines more, and no one marvels. A person who speaks, no one marvels. A bird that speaks, everyone delights. A stone is not light, and yet it shines. So when creatures melt because His light shows, there is nothing new. But when the yesh, which is the opposite of bitul, stays yesh and still is nullified, "that is new, that is delight". Her beauty comes from Israel adorning her with their service. Look at your most solid, ordinary self: body, job, habits. Not something to melt away. Something that can shine while staying what it is, like a gem.
- **From inside:** A deep dignity for ordinary, physical life. Many feel relief: they do not have to escape the world to be holy; the world is where His delight is.
- **At the edge:** The root of this bitul is "very high": from "the Head that is not known". The text says the essence of Ein Sof shows "precisely in the bitul of the yesh".
- **Into the day:** Do one physical thing today, eating, working, and let it be done for Him while you stay fully in it.

## Third Gate, ch. 28 (yachid and echad again; one glance to the end of the generations)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 28 (yachid and echad again; one glance to the end of the generations), lines 719-724.
- **The movement:** It sets the book's precision side by side once more. The long face is "yachid": alone, with no second at all. The small face is "echad": many powers joined into one. And it gives a picture of seeing: one eye that sees to the end of all generations.
- **The line to hold** (line 722): > ואין להעריך בכח הזה אפילו בחינת אחד כי אחד הוא התאחדות מצד כוחות מחולקים > > *And one cannot even ascribe to this power "one", for "one" is a joining of divided powers.*

The long face is His power alone, "no mix of other powers at all". So it is called yachid: alone, "without a second partner". You cannot even call it echad, "for echad is joining divided powers". The small face is echad: many that are one. "I, I am He." "I am first and I am last." He "looks and sees to the end of all generations with one eye", in one showing, with no division of levels. How, "is not known". Hold the two words again, yachid and echad. Then imagine being seen, you and every person who will ever live, in one glance, with nothing divided. Rest in that glance.
- **From inside:** A stillness that is not lonely. Aloneness, here, means nothing is outside; and the one glance means no one is left out.
- **At the edge:** "How all levels are in this power and it is still yachid is a wonder", received from "the power of His equality", Atik.
- **Into the day:** Say once today, slowly: You are alone, and I am seen.

## Third Gate, ch. 30 ("I will be": all worlds in one hidden thought; a spring from the ground)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 30 ("I will be": all worlds in one hidden thought; a spring from the ground), lines 730-738.

In that will is every world and every detail, past and future. So it is called Ehyeh, "I will be": all is in it, not yet shown. ## Third Gate, ch. 31 (the student who sees only the details drifts from his teacher)

- **Where:** Third Gate, ch. 31 (the student who sees only the details drifts from his teacher), lines 739-744.
- **The movement:** It shows how the same teaching can bring close or push away. A student who grasps the teacher's aim through the details comes near. A student who sees only the details moves away. So with the world: seen only from outside, it hides Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 740): > אבל כשהמקבל אינו מבין עומק המכוון של רבו ואינו רואה כי אם התגלותו של חכמת רבו בענין חילוק הדרגין והבדלם הרי הוא אדרבה מתרחק מרבו בתכלית הריחוק > > *But when the receiver does not understand the depth of his teacher's aim, and sees only the showing of his teacher's wisdom in the dividing of levels, he moves, on the contrary, as far as possible from his teacher.*
- **Contemplating it:** The first showing has two narrowings. One makes the powers appear divided and still joined. The other shows each detail on its own. The picture: a teacher who wants to share his whole wisdom must first explain every detail separately, then how they connect. A wise student, through the details, grasps the aim and becomes one with his teacher. A student who sees only the details "moves as far as possible from his teacher". So the world: shown in details so that His unity could show through them. Whoever follows only the outside, the stars, the forces, the desires of this world, is going against the very power in them. That was the sin of the Baal worshippers: they chased His power in nature from the outside. But when Israel understand the unity through the details, "all good comes from His great good, for the nature of the good is to do good". Look at your day's details: tasks, messages, errands. Ask once: what is the Teacher's aim in all this? Let the details point past themselves.
- **From inside:** A sense of direction in the busy details. Many find that asking "what is the aim?" gathers a scattered day into one movement.

- **The movement:** The gate opens by naming its subject, lights and vessels, rising and falling, more light and less. Then it lays down the ground under all of it: every rise and fall happens in the realm of the vessels, but the life of the worlds, and even the power that contracts, is His Essence.
- **The line to hold** (line 782): > כי אפילו כח הצמצום הוא עצמותו ברוך הוא > > *For even the power of the contraction is His Essence, blessed be He.*
- **Contemplating it:** The gate lists what it will explain: lights and vessels, how light is added to vessels, how it leaves them, "greatness and smallness". All the ups and downs of the worlds belong to this realm of vessels. The vessels exist to hide the light and to show it, so that His will and His unity can be seen even in worlds that look separate. But under all the ups and downs, the life of every world is "from His power, in His Essence, and there is nothing besides Him". Even the hiding is not something else. "He is the place of the world, and the world is not His place." Picture a room that seems to wall you off from someone. Now learn that the walls are made of that person's own presence. The picture breaks, since no walls are made of people; here even the wall is Him. Think of a day when God felt hidden. The book does not say the hiding was fake. It says the hiding itself is His power.
- **From inside:** A settled feeling under the changes. Days of more light and days of less are real, but they happen on the surface. Many find this steadies them without asking them to feel more than they do.
- **At the edge:** The gate warns that all its talk of adding and taking away belongs to the vessels. In the Essence there is no adding and no taking away.
- **Into the day:** When something today feels like a wall between you and God, say quietly: even this wall is His.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Fourth Gate, ch. 2 (two kinds of light: the essential life and the shine)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 2 (two kinds of light: the essential life and the shine), lines 783-786.
- **The movement:** It separates two kinds of light. One is the life of a thing, which does not show at all. The other is the shine, the part that shows, different in every vessel. The soul in the body is the picture.
- **The line to hold** (line 784): > ובבחינת חיות שוים כל האברים מהמוח עד הציפורן הכל הוא חיות אחד בשוה > > *In terms of life, all the limbs are equal, from the brain to the fingernail; all is one life, equally.*
- **Contemplating it:** There is "the light of the essence", which is the life of each thing, and there is "radiance and shine", which is what shows. The soul gives life to the whole body equally. The brain is not more alive than the fingernail. That life does not show itself; it works "in a hidden power". What shows is different in each limb: thought in the brain, feeling in the heart, seeing in the eye. That is the shine, divided by the vessel it lights. So with the worlds. His Essence does not show; only a "likeness of the light of His Essence" shows, and the worlds differ by how much of it they show. Look at your hand, then think of your mind at its best. One seems higher. In life, they are equal. This is how every creature stands before His life-giving power.
- **From inside:** A leveling calm. The dull parts of your life and the bright parts are equally alive from Him. Many find this lifts shame about the "fingernail" hours of the day.
- **At the edge:** The Essence "is not in the category of revealing at all". All the distinctions are in the shine only.
- **Into the day:** Do one plain, "fingernail" task today and remember: the same one life is in this.
- **Ladder:** L04

## Fourth Gate, ch. 3 (the source and its light; the soul stays around the body; the line)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 3 (the source and its light; the soul stays around the body; the line), lines 787-791.
- **The movement:** Each light has a source, and the source never goes down into the flow. The soul is mostly around the body, not in it. A wise man drawing out one small idea never leaves his whole wisdom. So the contraction was in the drawing of light, never in Him.
- **The line to hold** (line 791): > והנה מבחינת הקו הזה הוא אור וחיות כל העולמות > > *And from this line is the light and life of all the worlds.*
- **Contemplating it:** The source, "the luminary", is not drawn down at all. What is drawn down is a thin line of light from it. The soul cannot pour its whole self into the body; "the body could not bear it". Most of it stays "surrounding" the body. Yet it never leaves even the smallest part. A wise man drawing out one small point still does it by all his wisdom. His full wisdom stays around the small point, hidden. So the contraction was only in what shows. "The power of His Essence did not leave even the thin drawing, which is the line." If the line were taken away "even for a moment", the world would go back to nothing. Think of the moment you are in now. It is held by a thin line from Him, and behind the line stands all of Him.
- **From inside:** A sense of being carried moment by moment, not once long ago. Many feel both small and held.
- **At the edge:** The book says plainly that the contraction was in the light, "not, God forbid, in His Essence". How the Essence is present without being drawn it does not explain.
- **Into the day:** Once today, stop and say: this moment is on the line.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Fourth Gate, ch. 4 (the teacher and the student who does or does not look for the point)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 4 (the teacher and the student who does or does not look for the point), lines 792-800.
- **The movement:** The long parable of the teacher. A wise teacher contracts his mind to a student's size and uses a parable. If the student looks for the point, the teacher's joy enters even the simple level. If the student takes the parable at face value, the teacher's heart goes back to his own wisdom.
- **The line to hold** (line 799): > אבל כשהמקבל אינו מתעצם להבין כוונת רבו שבהמשל ומבין את המשל כפשוטו > > *But when the student does not strain to grasp his teacher's intent in the parable, and understands the parable only at face value.*
- **Contemplating it:** His Essence does not show in wisdom and traits; "the heavens and the heavens of heavens cannot contain You". What shows is a shine. A teacher who wants to reach a far-off student first narrows his great wisdom to one thin point, then weighs how to give it. He measures the student's mind by his own wisdom, and joins it to his own. His life flows into this teaching fully, as into his own deep thinking. Only what shows is smaller. If the student works to catch the point inside the parable, the teacher takes delight even in the student's small mind. If not, the teacher treats the student's mind as "his back", something apart. The world is His parable. Read today as a parable and ask what He means by it. Not the surface only, but the point.
- **From inside:** A sense of being taught, personally. The ordinary day becomes a lesson someone wants you to get. Many find that looking for the point brings a quiet closeness.
- **At the edge:** The text says the parable "is not like the thing it pictures in every way". With a human teacher the student's mind is really outside him; with God nothing is.
- **Into the day:** Pick one thing that happens today and ask: what is the point inside this parable?

## Fourth Gate, ch. 5 (the line and "Adam Kadmon": to show equality in real, separate things)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 5 (the line and "Adam Kadmon": to show equality in real, separate things), lines 801-804.
- **The movement:** It names the purpose. In His power all things are one and equal. He wants that equality to show in things that are really separate, each on its own, and still one.
- **The line to hold** (line 804): > דהיינו שיתגלו למהותים נבדלים ואף על פי כן יהיו מיוחדים > > *That is, that they be revealed as separate beings, and even so be united.*
- **Contemplating it:** When His simple will rose to show His perfection "even in vessels and in yesh", there was the contraction and the thin line. In that line is everything from top to bottom, but all in "equality", not yet separate. This inclusion is called "the Primordial Man". The whole aim is that His equality show "in actual fact": that every level and every detail show on its own, and even so be joined. Look at the people around your table. Each one really different. The aim is not to blend them into sameness, but that they be themselves and still be one.
- **From inside:** Many feel respect for difference, and hope that difference is not the enemy of unity.
- **At the edge:** In His Essence, "one cannot measure the details of limit" at all. The separateness is real only in its showing.

## Fourth Gate, ch. 6 (breaking and repair; Keter as wonder joining opposites)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 6 (breaking and repair; Keter as wonder joining opposites), lines 805-808.
- **The movement:** Showing the details separately is "breaking". Joining them back is "repair". The joining is done by Keter, the power of wonder, which holds two opposites as one, like soul and body.
- **The line to hold** (line 807): > כח הפלא לחבר שני הפכים בנושא אחד > > *The power of wonder, to join two opposites in one subject.*
- **Contemplating it:** Two things are needed: that the levels show in their differences, and that they be joined. The first is "breaking", the pulling back of the even light so that details show apart. The repair joins all the details to Him, the way the teacher joins the student's mind to his own. The joining is by Keter, called "wonder". The soul and the body are opposites "from end to end", yet truly one; the soul's life shows only through the body's work. That is a wonder. The ten sefirot are called "a man". The vessels are "His causes", the light is their life, but the power that holds them together is Ein Sof Himself. You are soul and body, opposites. You have never once been two. Stay with that wonder a while.
- **From inside:** A quiet amazement at being a person. Many feel less torn between "spiritual" and "physical" parts of the day.
- **At the edge:** What holds the man together is "the Essence of Ein Sof, whose nature is not known".
- **Into the day:** Eat one meal today slowly, as soul and body at once.

## Fourth Gate, ch. 7 (the curtains between worlds; to Him the vessels of Atzilut and of Asiyah are the same)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 7 (the curtains between worlds; to Him the vessels of Atzilut and of Asiyah are the same), lines 809-816.
- **The movement:** Each world is hidden from the one below by a curtain, so each can show on its own. Malchut gives likeness down the line. But to Him there are only ten, not forty, and even the hiding comes from His highest power.
- **The line to hold** (line 814): > והכל הוא על פי כלים דאצילות > > *And all is according to the vessels of Atzilut.*
- **Contemplating it:** The second repair is that His light and unity show even in creatures that feel separate. This is done by Malchut, by hiding the light that is united in Atzilut behind a "curtain". The teacher again: he narrows his mind to the student's vessels. So the kindness of Beriah is only "a likeness" of kindness in Atzilut. From world to world: Yetzirah shapes Asiyah, until "there is nothing seen in Asiyah" — stone, plant, animal, speaker — that is not a likeness of something above. To us there are four worlds; to Him there are ten sefirot only, not forty. "The very sefirot of Atzilut are the very vessels of Asiyah." And the hiding power of Malchut "has a very high root". Look at a tree or a stone. It is a likeness of a likeness. And to Him, it is the same as the highest light.
- **From inside:** Many feel the plain world grow deep without leaving it. Nothing needs to be climbed away from.
- **At the edge:** The root of the hiding is "His power that did not come down to drawing at all, not in knowing or grasping".

Second: His showing in the vessels of Atzilut, level by level, and still one with them. Its root is a "simple will". Since no will can be said of Him, even this is "wonder". Third: Malchut of Atzilut, showing Him "in actual fact, in every detail of creation". "For this was the main purpose." The last is first in thought. The smallest creature, living right, is what the whole plan was for. Think: you are in the third measure. You are where the plan comes true.

## Fourth Gate, ch. 10 (Adam Kadmon as primordial thought; the hidden agreement)

That is the "circle of Ein Sof" surrounding at a distance, and the distance is only from our side. The line first shows in "Adam Kadmon", His primordial thought, where the worlds are held equal, not yet divided. A man may have a deep thought toward something far below him, for a hidden reason. ## Fourth Gate, ch. 16 (Malchut's root is very high; service unites the Holy One and His Shechinah)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 16 (Malchut's root is very high; service unites the Holy One and His Shechinah), lines 859-863.

## Fourth Gate, ch. 17 ("give strength to God"; "in secret places My soul weeps"; the lion before the fox)

- **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 17 ("give strength to God"; "in secret places My soul weeps"; the lion before the fox), lines 864-869.
- **The movement:** Our deeds cannot touch His Essence; "if you are righteous, what do you give Him?" Yet His hidden will for the worlds is touched. There, "in the inner rooms", He weeps when His will is not done, and rejoices when it is. Outwardly, "strength and joy are in His place".
- **The line to hold** (line 867): > שהבכייה היא דוקא בבתי גוואי שהוא בבחינת הסתר > > *That the weeping is precisely in the inner rooms, in the hidden place.*
- **Contemplating it:** In His flow there is no more and no less. But in His intent, in the vessels, there is. When people do good, they "give strength to God". When not, "you weakened the Rock". Some verses are written with "lo" (not) and read with "lo" (to Him). Written: the worlds do not touch Him. Read: "to Him are deeds weighed". Both are true, joined in His equal power. The sages ask: it says "in secret places My soul weeps", yet it says "strength and joy are in His place". They answer: weeping in the inner rooms, joy in the outer. The inner rooms are His hidden will, "beyond grasping". A great king loses something worth a penny. Outwardly it means nothing to him. But he had a hidden reason for that thing. Think that your small act reaches that hidden room.
- **From inside:** A mix of awe and tenderness. You are very small, and still what you do matters to Him. Many find this moves them to care without fear.
- **At the edge:** How the weeping touches Him is "hidden from every thought". The book keeps both: nothing touches Him, and He weeps.

In that gap there is room for "the opposite", in order "to subdue it and turn it". When you feel the gap, the place where His life does not show, know that the gap is also held by the circle. - **Where:** Fourth Gate, ch. 19, the great gloss (circles and straightness; prayer breaks the firmaments; the four confessions), lines 874-875.
- **The movement:** A long gloss "opens a small door". A craftsman first takes in the thing as a whole (circle), then pours his skill into its details (straightness). Prayer breaks through firmaments and airs. Then four confessions of God are weighed: Yitro, Naaman, Rachav, and Moshe, who alone said "there is none else", even in the hiding.

The inner union, which brings added light and new souls, needs "feminine waters" from man's service, rising all the way to Ein Sof. This is His delight, and delight comes most from the far and the opposite, like a king whose rule reaches distant islands.
- **The line to hold** (line 1056): > וכל עיקר התענוג הוא דוקא מצד ההיפוך ומצד הריחוק > > *And the main delight is precisely from the opposite and from the far.*
- **Contemplating it:** The outer union came from "the spirit her husband left in her" at creation. He "brings them into being every moment". The inner union needs man's service and self-giving. The waters rise to Father and Mother, then to Arich, then to Atik, "up to Ein Sof in His Essence". A person's delight comes from his will being done. And it comes most "from the opposite and the far", when something distant joins him. A king whose rule reaches far islands, with no enemy left: there his kingship shows. "In this greatness the Torah and mitzvot were given." You may feel far away. The book says: that is where His delight is greatest when you turn.
- **From inside:** Many feel hope instead of shame about their distance.
- **At the edge:** Unity is always there in Him: "there is no separation between the Holy One and His Shechinah". Its showing depends on us.
- **Into the day:** Turn to Him once today from the place you feel farthest.
- **Ladder:** L12

## Fifth Gate, ch. 21 (binah and tevunah: two contemplations; go from below upward)

- **Where:** Fifth Gate, ch. 21 (binah and tevunah: two contemplations; go from below upward), lines 1060-1069.
- **The movement:** There are two ways to contemplate. Binah looks at His unity itself, and bitul comes by itself. Tevunah contemplates for the soul's sake, to beget love and awe. Above both is the bitul of Moshe, David and Rabbi Shimon, from the Essence alone. But one must go from below upward.
- **The line to hold** (line 1069): > אך אף על פי כן צריך לילך ממטה למעלה > > *Even so, one must go from below upward.*
- **Contemplating it:** A teacher gives a child one kind of teaching, a wiser student another. All of it is by his measure. So the levels of revealing are endless. "There are 25,000 levels for the righteous", and endless levels for the middle people, "according to their understanding of His unity". The first contemplation looks at His unity, "that there is none besides Him", without asking what it does for the soul. The second contemplates to bind the soul through love and awe. Above both: Moshe said "erase me", David said "who do I have in heaven", Rabbi Shimon said "with one knot I am bound". Their bitul was "from the Essence alone". Start where you are. First contemplate so as to love and fear. Then His unity for itself. Then, if it comes, the Essence. Do not skip.
- **From inside:** Many feel relief at the order. You are allowed to start with what moves you.
- **At the edge:** Even contemplating His unity is only "known from His deeds". The Essence is beyond.
- **Into the day:** Today, contemplate one thought that makes you love Him. Save the higher steps for later.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Fifth Gate, ch. 22 (all is from the side of revealing; He is Zeir Anpin, He is Atika)

- **Where:** Fifth Gate, ch. 22 (all is from the side of revealing; He is Zeir Anpin, He is Atika), lines 1070-1074.
- **The movement:** Everything said about smallness and greatness depends on man and belongs to the revealing. The main arousal is to raise the Shechinah from her fall. But in His own power no traits can be described at all. "The Holy One" means His Essence, even though we call it Zeir Anpin.
- **The line to hold** (line 1073): > אבל מצד כחו לבדו אין לתאר אליו מדות כלל > > *But from the side of His power alone, no traits can be described of Him at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** "As the lower ones wake, so His unity is revealed." The outer union needs no arousal; "the arousal is to renew old souls". The inner brings new drawings "from the Emanator". All service is through the Shechinah: "to raise her from her fall, to reveal her from her hiding, and join her to her Husband". "They are called traits according to the deeds of people." From His power alone, "no traits at all". "He turns by His devices for their works." When you call Him merciful or strict today, add in your heart: this is how He shows to us.
- **From inside:** Many feel humble honesty in their words about God.
- **At the edge:** "The power of the vessels in His power is His Essence, without difference." Beyond revealing, "no aspect or vessel at all".
- **Into the day:** When you name a trait of God today, add: from our side.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Fifth Gate, ch. 23 (a great wonder; the ladder of wonders; for Him nothing is wonder)

- **Where:** Fifth Gate, ch. 23 (a great wonder; the ladder of wonders; for Him nothing is wonder), lines 1075-1080.
- **The movement:** That all is His power alone and still there are worlds: "this is a great wonder". The will is the power that bears two opposites in one subject. The book climbs a ladder of wonders, from the physical made by the spiritual, up to the simple will. The more you inquire, the more you grasp His height. But for Him nothing is a wonder.
- **The line to hold** (line 1075): > דהיינו שבאמת הכל הוא כחו לבדו ואף על פי כן יהיה נגלה בבחינת עולמות זהו פלא גדול > > *That in truth all is His power alone, and even so it be revealed as worlds: this is a great wonder.*
- **Contemplating it:** "I the Lord, that is My Name, without separation." The revealing is one with Him. The soul-body picture does not fit Him. The will "bears two opposites in one subject". It can hide, and yet not hide from Him. The ladder: how the physical comes from the spiritual is a wonder. How traits come from wisdom, a wonder. How limit is in Ein Sof, a wonder. How the primordial thought comes from Ein Sof, a wonder from the simple will. "The more you inquire, the more you understand His exaltation", and also how His Essence is drawn into all. Climb the ladder slowly once today, one rung at a time: the stone, the feeling, the thought, the will. At each, say: wonder.
- **From inside:** Many feel awe that grows with understanding instead of ending in it.
- **At the edge:** "Is anything too wondrous for the Lord?" A wonder needs two things far apart. For Him there is nothing else. The wonders are all "from our side".
- **Into the day:** Choose one ordinary thing today and trace it up one step: say what made it possible.
- **Ladder:** L08

## Fifth Gate, ch. 24 (sin as a body without a soul; "if you are righteous, what do you give Him?"; the vessels from the reshimu)

- **Where:** Fifth Gate, ch. 24 (sin as a body without a soul; "if you are righteous, what do you give Him?"; the vessels from the reshimu), lines 1081-1084.
- **The movement:** The worlds do not touch Him; they exist only from the hiding. Still, His will was to be revealed from our side, so our service matters. When people do not do His will, the vessels remain "a body without a soul". The verses that say our deeds do not touch Him speak of His drawing; the Shechinah in the worlds depends on us. And the vessels come from the reshimu, from His Essence alone.
- **The line to hold** (line 1081): > וכל בחינת העולמות הם רק מצד ההסתרה > > *And all the worlds exist only from the side of the hiding.*
- **Contemplating it:** Rising and falling, adding and lacking, are "only to us". Nothing hides before Him. But because His will was to be seen from our side, "when those below unite", more is revealed. If people do not do His will, His will withdraws from the revealing, and the vessels stay "like a body without a soul". They hide even more from us. "If you sin, what do you do to Him? If you are righteous, what do you give Him?" That is about His drawing. His Shechinah, dwelling in the worlds, "depends precisely on the service of those below". All His drawing into the worlds is "not even a drop to the ocean". The vessels come from the reshimu, from His Essence alone. Hold both: you are tiny, and you matter.
- **From inside:** Many feel two things at once: humility and responsibility, without fear.
- **At the edge:** The book does not resolve how both are true. It holds them side by side.
- **Into the day:** Do one thing today as if the Shechinah's dwelling depended on it.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Fifth Gate, ch. 25 and close (added light without change; the Shechinah below is a need above; the purpose of all this knowledge)

- **Where:** Fifth Gate, ch. 25 and close (added light without change; the Shechinah below is a need above; the purpose of all this knowledge), lines 1085-1092.
- **The movement:** When His will is done, light is added from His very Essence, yet He does not change. How both are true is a wonder, for He can bear two opposites as one. "The Shechinah in the lower worlds is a need on high." Then the book speaks to the reader: all this knowledge is so your soul can rise to Him, feel awe, keep the Torah, and unite the worlds. Without man's service, no worlds can be spoken of at all. It ends in prayer.
- **The line to hold** (line 1091): > ובלא בחינת היחוד מצד עבודת האדם אין לתאר אליו יתברך עולמות כלל > > *And without the unity from man's service, no worlds at all can be described of Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** "Do not say there is change in Him." All the adding is from our side. Yet He reveals more of His Essence. "Just as His nature is not known, so His acts are not known", "for He can bear two opposites as one". "You should have helped Me." "Ishmael My son, bless Me." The Shechinah is "the gate to go in and come out". "The Shechinah below is a need on high." No damage can touch Him, but because His will is for revealing, "the damage reaches without end". How, "is not in the category of knowing". To the reader: these gates open the gates of Etz Chaim. "The main point of understanding is to raise your soul to Him", with longing to leave the hiding of the body; from it comes awe and shame before His greatness, and the keeping of His Torah and mitzvot. "For this is all of man." Close your study with the book's own prayer: "to plant in our hearts His oneness, His love and His awe, to serve Him in truth and with a whole heart."
- **From inside:** A gathered, grateful quiet, like finishing a long walk. Many feel the whole book fold into one wish: to serve Him truly.
- **At the edge:** "How it is in His power is not known at all." The author ends asking for help "to finish more" on the service of His unity.
- **Into the day:** Say the closing line of the book once today: may the Lord plant in our hearts His oneness, His love and His awe.
- **Ladder:** L14

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations, ranked

1. **Preface 9-10 (the two kinds of knowing, and Jacob's ladder)** (line 9): וכמו שהיה לבדו מקודם שנבראו העולמות כן הוא יתברך לבדו אחר בריאת העולמות *As He was alone before the worlds were created, so He is alone after the worlds were created.* The book's thesis in one line, and the widest contemplation in it. Every later gate is this sentence seen from another side.
2. **Second Gate, ch. 15 (the gloss: inward and outward; "as His power is without limit, so it is in limit")** (line 373): כי כאשר כוחו בלי גבול ככה כוחו בגבול באין הבדל כלל *For as His power is without limit, so His power is in limit, with no difference at all.* Limit is not less of Him. The key that lets the whole map of sefirot and vessels be held without making God small.
3. **First Gate, ch. 14 (Havayah does not change; the flame in the coal; run and return)** (line 216): אם רץ לבך להשיג איך הוא התחלקותם שוב למקום שהיית בו דהיינו שאין זולתו *If your heart runs to grasp how they divide, return to the place you were in, that is, that there is nothing besides Him.* The book's own instruction for sitting: go out to the details, and when the heart runs, come back to 'nothing besides Him'. The pulse of the whole practice.
4. **Second Gate, ch. 32 (two measures; understanding still leaves an understander; stripping the garments)** (line 491): כי היחוד על ידי השכלה הרי עדיין יש מי שמשכיל ביטולו *For unity through understanding still leaves someone who understands his own nullification.* A contemplation about contemplation: understanding leaves an understander. It shows why the book cannot stop at knowing.
5. **Second Gate, ch. 40 (the conclusion: first unite through da'at, then be nullified above it)** (line 558): להבטל למעלה מן הדעת ולהבין שהכל הוא לבדו יתברך *To be nullified above da'at, and to understand that all is He alone.* The order of service in two steps, knowing and then above knowing. The book's plainest map of the inner road.
6. **Fourth Gate, ch. 19, the great gloss (circles and straightness; prayer breaks the firmaments; the four confessions)** (line 875): דהיינו שאין עוד שאפילו בבחינת הצמצומים הכל הוא עצמותו ברוך הוא בבחינת השוואה גמורה *That is, there is none else: even in the contractions, all is His Essence, in complete equality.* Four ways of saying God is one, and only Moshe's reaches the empty space. A mirror for which one a person actually lives by.
7. **Fourth Gate, ch. 23 (the Shechinah in exile; arousal comes from the opposite)** (line 897): אבל עיקר ההתעוררות הוא מצד ההיפוך מצד התגברותו נגד ההיפוך *But the main arousal is from the side of the opposite, from one's overcoming against the opposite.* The deepest arousal comes from overcoming the opposite, not from insight. It gives dignity to plain struggle on dark days.
8. **Third Gate, ch. 27 (bitul inside the yesh itself: the gem that shines, the bird that speaks)** (line 715): אבל בבחינת נוקבא הוא דייקא הכוונה שיהיה נגלה הביטול בבחינת היש דייקא *But in Nukva, the intent is precisely that the nullification be revealed in the yesh, precisely there.* Bitul inside the yesh itself: the gem that shines and stays a gem. The work of this time, done in the plain world.
9. **First Gate, ch. 21 (seek His face always: seeking in order not to find)** (line 247): לכן נאמר בקשו פניו תמיד דייקא כאדם שמבקש איזה דבר תמיד מחמת שאינו מוצאו *Therefore it says, "Seek His face always", precisely: like a person who always seeks something because he does not find it.* Seek His face always, as one seeks what one does not find. Seeking becomes the meeting, not the way to it.
10. **Preface 68 (the first warning: knowledge is only knowledge when it is felt as life)** (line 68): כי עיקר שם הידיעה היא כשנתפסת ונרגשת בנפשו בבחינת חיות ממש *For it is only truly called knowing when it is grasped and felt in the soul as real life.* Knowledge is only knowledge when it is felt as life. The warning over every other contemplation in the book.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

Compared with the Chabad method in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md` and with the other books. Kept, not smoothed.

- **The peak is bitul above da'at, not the arousal of the traits.** The Chabad method in `00_HISBONENUS.md` runs through binah to arousal and then drawing down, and the heart's being moved is the main fruit. This book agrees that knowledge must be 'felt as life' (Preface 68) and names fervour and kelot hanefesh as the fruit of understanding (Fifth Gate 25, line 1089). But at its summits it says unity through understanding 'still leaves someone who understands' (Second Gate 32, line 491). The order is: first unite through da'at, then 'be nullified above da'at' (line 558). Understanding is the ladder, not the roof.
- **The felt-arousal dispute is not settled here, and it should not be.** `00_HISBONENUS.md` records that R. Aharon's Sha'arei Avodah defends felt hispaalus, with da'at as the divider, against the received summary that makes him the champion of bitul alone. This earlier book shows why both summaries arose. It asks for knowing felt as life (line 68), and it also puts the highest bitul above knowing (line 558). These pages do not argue with the Mitteler Rebbe. So the meditative guide above keeps both: 'From inside' describes what is often felt, and 'At the edge' marks where feeling is not the measure.
- **The arousal that reaches the Essence comes from the opposite, not from understanding.** The ontology's highest state is 'arousal from the essence', reached through contemplation. This book says a villager cannot arouse a scholar with his wisdom, so no understanding can reach Ein Sof (Fourth Gate 23, line 897). What reaches Him is bitul against one's own contrary traits, in hiding (lines 898, 911; Fifth Gate 17, line 1038). Contemplation reaches a shine; the struggle reaches the Essence. That turns the obstacles in the ontology (coldness, the pull of the other side) into the place of the highest service.
- **Our service changes something 'above', and He also does not change.** The Tanya and the SPINE say the change is only in the receivers. This book says so too ('He is the Ancient One, He is Zeir Anpin, with no change', line 843; line 1085). But it also says service adds light 'from His very Essence', He weeps 'in the inner rooms' when His will is not done (line 867), damage 'reaches without end' (line 1087), and 'without man's service no worlds at all can be described of Him' (line 1091). It holds both as a wonder, 'He can bear two opposites as one', and does not resolve them. The units above do not resolve them either.
- **Seeking in order not to find, and yet: the more you inquire, the more you see.** The Tanya's Shaar HaYichud ends its hardest points in faith above understanding. This book sets firm limits: three 'Ancient Ones' with graded rules on what may be asked (First Gate 19), 'we have no business with this at all' above the primordial thought (line 857), and 'seek His face always' as one who does not find (line 247). But its final move is the reverse of a stop: 'the more you inquire, the more you understand His exaltation' (line 1079), and the whole Kabbalah exists 'so that this knowledge brings true bitul' (line 1091). Both are kept.
- **'Yachid' belongs to the will, and not even 'yachid' to the Essence.** The SPINE takes the yachid/echad distinction from the later Rebbeim. Here it is already exact and a little different: echad is the union of two in Atzilut (line 942), yachid is said of Keter, the will (line 986), and of the Essence one may say 'no name, not even yachid, except by denial' (line 225). A contemplation built on 'He is yachid' would, by this book, still be one step short.
- **The soul-body picture is used freely, and withdrawn only at the top.** The Alter Rebbe in Shaar HaYichud refuses the soul-body analogy at its root. R. Aharon builds on it for Atzilut: one life from brain to fingernail (line 784), soul and body as opposites joined by Keter (line 807). He withdraws it only for the Essence (line 1076). The guide above uses the body often, because the book does, and marks the break each time.
- **In this time, the simplest bitul is the deepest.** The hisbonenus method prizes duration, breadth and worked analogy. This book says the last exile is of Malchut, and its work is 'the yoke of heaven', bitul 'without understanding or traits' (Fourth Gate 26, line 910), inside the yesh (Third Gate 27, line 715). A person with no insight who accepts His will in a heavy moment is doing what the book calls the work of the age. The ladder guards still apply: L06 and L12 are not for low days, and the units say so.
- **The map itself is the object, taken from below upward.** The ontology lists 'the order of descent' as one object among several. Here the whole Lurianic structure, down to joints and thirds, is the object, and the book says so at the end (line 1091). But it gives an order: begin with tevunah, contemplating for love and awe, then the unity itself, then the inner (Fifth Gate 21, line 1069). Moshe, David and Rabbi Shimon, whose bitul came 'from the Essence alone', are the goal, not the starting point.
