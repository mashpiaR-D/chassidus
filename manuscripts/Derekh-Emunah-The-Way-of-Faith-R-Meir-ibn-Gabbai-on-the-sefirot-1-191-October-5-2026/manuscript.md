## What the book is

A short book of answers. A student, Yosef HaLevi, wrote to ibn Gabbai with ten questions about the sefirot (the ten divine attributes through which G-d shows Himself) (¶3-18):

1. How do we know the world has a Leader?
2. Why must there be sefirot at all, and not only the Ein Sof (the Infinite)?
3. Why ten, and how are they one power?
4. Were they emanated or created?
5. How can He be one while they are ten?
6. Why do they have limit and measure?
7. When did they come to be?
8. What are they?
9. What are their names?
10. How can each of the hundred daily blessings match them, if Malkhut (Kingship, the last sefirah) is "dry" and has nothing of her own?

Ibn Gabbai answers each, leaning on R. Azriel of Gerona, the Zohar, the Tikkunei Zohar, the Sefer Yetzirah and the Bahir, and often pointing back to "my great work" (Avodat HaKodesh) and to Tola'at Yaakov. He finished it on 23 Adar I 5299 (early 1539) (¶190). He named it for "faith": the book is "a way and a gate to the upper, complete faith" (¶187).

For the guide, this book is the clearest short statement of ibn Gabbai's view of unity. Avodat HaKodesh is long and scattered; here the whole teaching fits in one answer after another.

## The unity of G-d

**He is inside, above and around.** Answering the first question, ibn Gabbai uses the Talmud's five likenesses between the soul and G-d (Berakhot 10a): as the soul fills the body, sees and is not seen, and so on. Then he pushes past the likeness. Unlike the soul, which the body can limit:

> הוא נמצא בתוך העולם ההוא והוא למעלה ממנו וחוצה לו והוא מקיפו ומגבילו מכל צד ואין לו מקיף ומגביל (Derekh Emunah ¶29; line 29)
> He is found within that world, and He is above it and outside it; He surrounds it and bounds it from every side, and nothing surrounds or bounds Him.

> ואם הוא מבלי גבול הנה אין חוץ ממנו (¶31; line 31)
> And if He is without limit, then there is nothing outside Him.

*Ours.* This is very close to the Chabad pair of memale kol almin (filling all worlds) and sovev kol almin (surrounding all worlds) (Tanya ch. 48 and 51). Ibn Gabbai does not use those terms, but he holds the same two sides: within, and above and around.

**The sefirot stay inside Him.** This is the heart of the book. The sefirot are not things beside G-d:

> כי אם נאצלו המדות לא נפרדו אבל נשארו במאציל בייחוד גמור (¶53; line 53)
> For though the attributes were emanated, they were not separated; they remained in the Emanator in complete unity.

He is wise from Himself and understands from Himself, and so:

> כי הוא החכמה והוא התבונה והוא הדעת ואינן זולת עצמו (¶53; line 53)
> For He is the wisdom, He is the understanding, He is the knowledge, and they are nothing other than Himself.

"And the sefirot are the very essence of the Godhead" (¶53). Emanation brought nothing new: it only revealed what was hidden (¶53, ¶129). From the Tikkunim: "one who separates one of these ten sefirot from its fellow, it is as if he separated in You", like cutting a limb from a living body (¶59-60).

*Ours.* The phrase "He is the knowledge" is the same formula the Tanya takes from the Rambam: "He is the Knower, He is the Knowledge, He is the Known" (Tanya ch. 2 and Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 8-9, as we recall it). Ibn Gabbai uses it for the sefirot. This is his firmest stand: the sefirot are G-d Himself, not tools He made (¶79-81). If they were created, praying to them would be idolatry (¶81).

**All change is on our side.** Why do the sefirot seem to change from mercy to judgment? Like a flame whose colors come from the wick and the wood:

> כן הבריות גורמים השנוי והכל כפי מה שמעוררים למטה במעשיהם כך מעוררים למעלה ואין בפועל שום שנוי חלילה (¶102; line 102)
> So the creatures cause the change, all according to what they arouse below by their deeds they arouse above; and in the act itself there is no change, G-d forbid.

"I the Lord have not changed" (Malachi 3:6). The Idra says the same: "from our side they differ one from another" (¶110). *Ours.* The guide can use this with a person who feels that G-d has turned against them. The light is the same. What changed is the wick.

**Light only within darkness.** From the Zohar: "there is no light except within darkness, and no darkness except within light" (¶103). Kindness has a little judgment in it, and judgment a little kindness (¶105). That is why a righteous person can turn judgment into mercy, and why reward and punishment are possible at all (¶108). If each attribute were pure, no deed would reach it.

**The King's face always shines.** A parable from the Zohar: a perfect king's face always shines, even when he judges. Fools see the shining face and grow careless. The wise say:

> אנו רואים שבאור ההוא יש חשך וברחמים דין אלא שהוא נעלם ונסתר (¶142; line 142)
> We see that in that light there is darkness, and in the mercy there is judgment, only it is hidden and concealed.

## Why there are sefirot: tzorech gavoah in the simplest form

The second question is the book's teaching on the purpose of creation. The Ein Sof has no will, intent or thought that can be named (¶34). So why emanate? Because:

> ועוד כי בזולת נבראים לא היה נכר כבוד האין סוף וייחודו ואלהותו (¶35; line 35)
> And besides, without creatures the glory of the Ein Sof, His unity and His Godhead, would not be recognized.

With one simple attribute, only simple creatures like angels would come out, and in them His glory is not fully seen:

> כי אין כבודו נראה ונגלה כי אם על נבראים מחומר כלולים מכל הצדדים והדרכים (¶35; line 35)
> For His glory is seen and revealed only on creatures of matter, made up of all sides and ways.

*Ours.* This is remarkable. Ibn Gabbai says in 1539 that G-d's glory is revealed only in beings of matter who are made of good and bad together, and not in angels. This is one of the roots of the Chabad teaching that the dwelling is in the lowest world, and that man, not the angel, is the purpose (Tanya ch. 36 and 39, as we recall it).

He says "for His need" outright. On "the Lord made everything for His sake" (Proverbs 16:4):

> וכביכול לו לצרכו כי לולא אצילותו לא היה ניכר ייחודו ואלהותו (¶58; line 58)
> And as it were, for Him, for His need; for were it not for His emanation, His unity and Godhead would not be recognized.

And he says the deed below adds strength above. When people do kindness, "they arouse that attribute and give it room, and as it were add strength to it" (¶56), "for as there is arousal below, so there is arousal above" (¶56):

> כי כפי התעוררות למטה מתעורר למעלה (¶56; line 56)
> For according to the arousal below, so is the arousal above.

**The limit is for us.** Why do the sefirot have measure? Not for Him. He made limit to show that He has power in limit too, and so we know "there is nothing outside Him" (¶115):

> המציא הגבול להגביל כל הנבראים בהגבלתו למען יוכלו להכיל אורו ותעצומו (¶115; line 115)
> He brought limit into being, to bound all creatures within its bounds, so that they could hold His light and His might.

> ובזה נדע כי אין חוץ ממנו ויש לו כח בגבול מבלי גבול (¶115; line 115)
> And by this we know that there is nothing outside Him, and that He has power in limit, without limit.

*Ours.* This is the Chabad claim that the Infinite has power in the finite too, and that one who says He has only power without limit makes Him lacking (the Rebbe Rashab's "koach bli gvul and koach hagvul"; compare the Avodat HaKodesh passage, line 2159). Ibn Gabbai quotes it from R. Azriel (¶37): "He is in limit, without limit."

**Why then and not before?** The seventh question: why did emanation come when it did? Ibn Gabbai's answer is the nature of will itself:

> כי זהו ענין הרצון שירצה בעת שרצה ולא קודם (¶131; line 131)
> For this is what will is: that He wills when He wills, and not before.

*Ours.* This is the same move as Chabad's "a desire has no reason" (and Avodat HaKodesh line 1114, "אין טעם ברצון"). The will is the last answer.

## The soul of the Torah and the body of the person

The Torah is shaped like a body, and each mitzvah hangs on a limb (¶123-124). So:

> המקיים כל המצות הרי צלמו ודמותו שלם (¶125; line 125)
> One who keeps all the mitzvot, his image and likeness are whole.

His body "becomes a throne and dwelling for the likeness that faces it" (¶125). A mitzvah done with the right intent "wakes and rises and binds with its likeness above" (¶125). Words of Torah and prayer said with intent rise and unite the great Name, and this is possible only because the words and letters "have a soul and a breath of life" (¶125). Then:

> ודע כי נשמת התורה היא השכינה (¶125; line 125)
> And know that the soul of the Torah is the Shechinah.

*Ours.* The Tanya holds that the letters of Torah and prayer carry divine life, and that the limbs doing a mitzvah become a chariot (Tanya ch. 23, 37). Ibn Gabbai's version is almost word for word.

## What the sefirot are: like the will of the soul

The eighth question: what is the essence of the sefirot? Ibn Gabbai compares them to the will of the soul:

> יש לדמות מהות הספירות לרצון הנפש שהוא שוה לכל החפצים (¶138; line 138)
> The essence of the sefirot is to be likened to the will of the soul, which is equal toward all desires.

The soul's will is one, though it can want one thing and its opposite. So the sefirot are one power, able to hold light and dark (¶138). And since man was made in that image, he has the same power: "and so free choice is in his hand" (¶138), to wake above whatever he wakes below. The Zohar: "one who sanctifies himself below is sanctified from above" (¶139).

**The sapphire that has no color.** "Sefirah" is also read as sapphire (livnat hasapir): as forms are seen in a sapphire without adding to it, "so the sefirot are no addition and no multiplicity in the Cause of causes" (¶150). And Malkhut is like clear sapphire. She has no color of her own, "and so she is ready to receive all colors and all forms" (¶175). If she had her own color, she would be a separate power. Her emptiness is what makes her the place where all of them are one (¶175-180).

*Ours.* This is a beautiful teaching for the guide. The receiver who has no color of her own is not poor. She is the bond of all. The Chabad teaching of Malkhut, "she has nothing of her own", and of bittul as the vessel for the Essence, agrees (Tanya ch. 35, the Presence rests only on what is nullified). The guide can offer this to a person who feels empty: emptiness toward G-d is the vessel for all His colors.

**He fills all names and has none.** From the Tikkunim: "You have no known name, for You fill all names, and You are the completeness of them all" (¶163). He can put on a name and take it off as needed (¶163).

## Blessings and faith

**A hundred blessings.** The last question, about the hundred daily blessings, becomes a teaching on blessing itself:

> כי הברכה כובשת ומסלקת הדין ומעוררת הרחמים (¶167; line 167)
> For blessing subdues and removes judgment, and wakes mercy.

The righteous turn judgment into mercy, "and so all is mercy" (¶167). One who says ninety nine and leaves out one "cuts" Malkhut from the rest (¶167).

**Faith is the Shechinah.** Malkhut is called "faith" because the faith of upper and lower hangs on her, and through her "the Cause of causes is revealed and known" (¶184-185).

**Knowing before serving.** The book closes with David's words to Solomon, "know the G-d of your father and serve Him" (1 Chronicles 28:9):

> משמע שאי איפשר לעבדו אם לא תקדם לו הידיעה בו (¶189; line 189)
> This means one cannot serve Him unless knowing Him comes first.

This knowing cannot come by reasoning alone. It comes by receiving (¶189). Then "he will be set aflame to serve his Maker" (¶189).

*Ours.* This is the Chabad method in a sentence: da'at (knowing) comes first and leads to love and awe (Tanya ch. 3 and 42; "da et Elokei avicha" is the Tanya's own proof text in ch. 42). The guide already follows this order: contemplation first, then feeling and deed.

## The big human questions

**Does the world have a Leader?** Yes, as the soul leads the body, only more: He fills it and surrounds it (¶24-29).

**Why did G-d make a world?** So His unity and Godhead would be known, and they are known only in creatures of matter, made of all sides (¶35, ¶58).

**Why did He make it then?** Because that is what will is: He willed when He willed (¶131).

**Has G-d changed toward me?** No. "The creatures cause the change" (¶102).

**Can I choose?** Yes; man was made in the image of the one will that holds all things, "and so free choice is in his hand" (¶138).

**How do I come to serve Him?** First know Him, by what is received, and the knowing will set you aflame (¶189).
