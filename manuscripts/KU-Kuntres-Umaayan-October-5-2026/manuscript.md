*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Twenty-eight short maamarim on the verse "A spring shall come out of the house of G-d and water the valley of Shittim." It reads shittim as shtus, a spirit of folly. The work is a clinical anatomy of how the yetzer fools a person: indulgence, "it won't cut me off", excuses, crediting oneself, pride, and the overbusy merchant. Each folly is exposed by reasoning. For someone whose G-dly soul does not shine and who cannot win by reasoning, the cure is holy stubbornness from the soul's essence, above reason. Each section ends with the author's own summary.

**How much of it is medicine.** substantial: about half the work (maamarim 1-2, 12-17, 25-28) directly diagnoses and treats desire, self-deception, excuses, pride and work-absorption; the other half is Kabbalistic cosmology that grounds the argument.

**Its model of the person.** Two souls. The animal soul comes from Tohu and klipas noga. It is present from birth and deepened by habit and surroundings. It carries anger, pride, lust, levity, laziness and sadness. The G-dly soul arrives at 13 but its root is earlier and higher. The soul has revealed faculties (mind, emotions) and an essence above reason. Since Eden, natural sensing of physical good dominates and produces self-deception. Self-love hides one's own faults, so a friend sees them better. Intellect limits the will; a will beyond reason is unbounded. Change: act from the essence over time, which weakens natural sensing and reveals the G-dly faculties, and then holy understanding can lead.

**Its philosophy.** A person is created to rise beyond his own level by uniting with G-d's wisdom and will through Torah. G-d desires a dwelling below, where darkness is turned to light. Whatever a person binds himself to, he becomes like. Nature is only a garment for divine blessing, which is allotted from above. The pull of the yetzer is not a real good. It is a spirit of folly that bends a person from the truth, and the soul's essence has the strength to overturn it.

**What it adds.** A graded map of self-deception (shtus). The sinner still knows he sinned. The self-justifier blames others but knows he needs repair. The self-crediter thinks he needs none. The proud man damages himself further, and each is subtler and worse than the last. The cure is itself a folly: holy stubbornness, above reason, for those whose G-dly soul does not shine, as a bridge to understanding later. It also holds that the simple man's pained, pleasureless prayer outranks the scholar's, and it diagnoses workaholism as treating a vessel as the source.

## When I'm near the thing I'm hooked on, I lose my mind and can't reason my way out

He knows it's wrong. The moment the familiar thing appears, feeling floods him, his reasoning disappears, and he fools himself. Arguing with himself doesn't work.

**What is really going on.** Repeated indulgence has made the animal soul strong and the G-dly soul weak. Since Eden, natural sensing of physical good overpowers divine sensing. Reason cannot win this contest; only the essence of the soul, above reason, can.

**Program: Holy stubbornness: do the opposite, with no argument.** (1) Name the specific thing you are sunk in, whether a desire or overbusy work. (2) Do not enter into reasons, calculations or back-and-forth with the urge. (3) In that very thing, do the exact opposite in practice, with great force, without weighing what you will lose. (4) Draw the strength from the soul's essence, above reason, the same stubbornness by which a Jew gives his life rather than betray G-d. (5) Apply the same firmness to fixed times for Torah, to prayer with at least the simple meaning, and to doing mitzvos beautifully. (6) Keep acting this way over time: natural sensing weakens and the G-dly soul's faculties are revealed. (7) Then conduct yourself by holy understanding, knowing Him in all your ways, including business. *When:* At every encounter with the thing he is sunk in, and in keeping fixed daily times, sustained 'for a period of time' until the natural sensing weakens. *For:* Someone whose G-dly soul does not shine openly and who is caught in one area where reasoning with himself has already failed. *Not for:* Those in whom the G-dly soul shines. They work through understanding and contemplation. It is also not a substitute for clinical care of compulsions or addiction. `KU·Q01` · `KU·Q02` · `KU·Q29` · `KU·Q30` · `KU·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"I have to be convinced and feel ready before I can stop; until then I'll keep arguing with myself."* to **Don't argue at all. Act from the soul's essence, above reason, with holy stubbornness. Understanding follows later.** Intellect limits the will, while a will not bound by reason spreads without limit. Acting from the essence over time weakens natural sensing and reveals the G-dly faculties, and then holy understanding can lead. `KU·Q29` · `KU·Q30` · `KU·Q01` · `KU·Q32`

## I keep chasing pleasures, even permitted ones, and it's coarsening me

Food and comfort beyond what he needs feel like his good. Bit by bit the permitted becomes the barely permitted, the appetite grows, and his taste for anything higher fades.

**What is really going on.** The yetzer presents lower pleasure as his good. Binding to something lower makes him like it and lowers him. Each indulgence strengthens the animal soul and weakens the G-dly soul.

**Program: The honest question at the moment of pull.** (1) When the yetzer says 'this is good for you', stop and put it to your heart. (2) Ask: is this my good, according to the level of my soul? Is this why I was created? (3) Remember that whatever you bind yourself to, you become like. (4) Treat its appeal to the eye as the very reason to keep far from it. (5) Move your delight to real wisdom, Torah and Chassidus, which is the good that fits you. *When:* In the moment of temptation, and ongoing as the direction of one's pleasures. *For:* Someone drifting into bodily pleasures, including permitted excess, who still responds to reasoning. *Not for:* Someone already sunk and overpowered, for whom reasoning fails. That is the case for G01. `KU·Q06` · `KU·Q07`

**Program: Waging war on the small things.** (1) Know that the animal soul and the yetzer are your greatest enemies and work by cunning, not open attack. (2) Watch how it leads from the fully permitted to the barely permitted and trains an appetite. (3) As a general stance, where the yetzer says yes say no, and where it says no say yes. (4) Pay attention to small matters, like where the eyes go, since the enemy wins through strategy. (5) Watch above all for excuses, its cleverest weapon. *When:* A standing posture throughout the day. *For:* Anyone refining the body and animal soul, including the righteous, whose subtle faults also matter. *Not for:* People prone to scrupulosity, obsessive self-monitoring or restrictive eating, for whom 'always do the opposite' can become harmful. `KU·Q18` · `KU·Q19`

**Reframe.** From *"It feels good, so it's good for me."* to **Is this my good, at the level of my soul? What I bind myself to, I become.** Attaching to and taking pleasure from something lower makes a person like it and lowers him. Seeing the pull as a descent rather than a gain removes its claim to be 'good'. `KU·Q06` · `KU·Q07`

## It's not really my fault: my hot temper, the people around me, they pushed me

Even while regretting and crying, he finds causes: his hot nature, a friend who led him on, his surroundings. He credits himself with his good and others with his bad.

**What is really going on.** Self-love covers his faults and invents excuses. He chose the friends and the environment. The excuse reduces his bitterness, so his remorse and resolve are not real and his essence does not change. The work calls this folly worse than the sin.

**Program: Choose the friend who sees you.** (1) Accept that self-love hides your own faults from you. (2) Acquire a good friend who sees what you cannot, rebukes you and speaks to your heart. (3) Talk with G-d-fearing people. Such conversation does a great deal. (4) Keep far from a bad friend and a bad neighbour, since they plant bad traits. *When:* Ongoing, in who one keeps company with. *For:* Anyone, and especially someone who blames others. *Not for:* Controlling or abusive 'rebuke' relationships. The friend in view is a caring equal. `KU·Q12`

**Program: Teshuvah without excuses.** (1) Notice the pull to attribute your bad to temperament, friends or environment. (2) Own that you chose those friends and surroundings. (3) Let the sorrow over the wrong be real, not diluted by finding merit for yourself. (4) From that real bitterness comes true remorse and a true resolve for the future, and the will is uprooted. (5) Such teshuvah changes a person's essence and brings him to where the fully righteous cannot stand. *When:* When reckoning with a wrong, and especially in times of favour such as Elul and the Days of Awe. *For:* A penitent who regrets but keeps explaining himself. *Not for:* Someone depressed or traumatised. Deepening bitterness, or refusing any account of real coercion, can harm. `KU·Q08` · `KU·Q13` · `KU·Q23`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm hot-tempered by nature. I can't help it."* to **A strong nature was given with equally strong soul-powers. You fail only when you don't awaken them.** G-d gives no one more than he can bear, and the load matches the camel. The excuse turns into a summons to awaken hidden powers that no obstacle can stand against. `KU·Q09` · `KU·Q10` · `KU·Q11`

**Reframe.** From *"They led me on. My environment did this."* to **I chose those friends and surroundings. My self-love is writing my defence.** The excuse dulls bitterness, so remorse and resolve stay shallow and the essence does not change. Owning it restores full teshuvah. `KU·Q08` · `KU·Q12` · `KU·Q13`

## I've sunk so low; people like me are worse than animals

*Its own term:* כל הגבוה ביותר יורד למטה ביותר

He sees in himself, or in others, traits worse than a beast of prey and concludes that the soul itself is bad.

**What is really going on.** Bad traits come from the body's tendencies. The soul in itself is supremely high. The highest soul is given the coarsest body because it can refine it, and it can rise level after level.

**Reframe.** From *"I've fallen so far that I must be worse than others."* to **The higher the soul, the further it falls. The highest soul is given the coarsest matter so it can refine it.** A stone from the top of the wall lands furthest away. The depth of the fall becomes evidence of height, and the soul can climb level after level. `KU·Q16` · `KU·Q17`

## I've accomplished spiritually and I quietly take credit for it

A serious learner and one who prays at length with contemplation feels good about his depth and delights in the praise. Sometimes this turns into open pride.

**What is really going on.** His understanding and strength are given from above, and this is what he was created for. Crediting himself shows he never reached self-nullification. He has been serving himself, so the service is not service at all.

**Program: Accepting the yoke as the foundation against self-credit.** (1) Remember that your understanding and the strength of your service are given from above. (2) Remember you were created for this, so there is nothing to credit yourself for. (3) If you do credit yourself, read it as a sign you have not reached bittul, and be pained that you missed your purpose. (4) Make accepting the yoke of Heaven the foundation of all your service. (5) Be careful even in small things, since they are great at your level. (6) Toil anyway: a broad mind still attains nothing without effort. *When:* Ongoing, around learning and long contemplative prayer. *For:* Serious learners and those who pray at length with contemplation. *Not for:* Someone already crushed by self-contempt. It is aimed at inflated self-regard. `KU·Q21` · `KU·Q22` · `KU·Q24` · `KU·Q25`

**Reframe.** From *"I reached depth in learning and prayer. I deserve some credit."* to **It was given to you and it is what you were made for. Taking credit shows you have been serving yourself.** Crediting oneself turns the service into self-love, like any other appetite. Seeing this redirects the person toward bittul. `KU·Q21` · `KU·Q22` · `KU·Q24`

## I'm ashamed: I'm simple, I don't even understand what I'm praying

He is humiliated by his ignorance and heartbroken at his lowness. People mock his mistakes, yet he keeps saying Tehillim aloud.

**What is really going on.** The work holds that his service, done without the pleasure of understanding and by accepting the yoke of Heaven, is greater than the learned man's.

**Reframe.** From *"I'm simple and ignorant. My prayer is worth nothing."* to **Prayer said in simple faith and acceptance of the yoke, without the pleasure of understanding, is higher service than the scholar's.** Acceptance of the yoke is the foundation of all service and carries no self-interest. His shame turns out to be the very place of his worth. `KU·Q20`

## I feel bad about what I did, but I'm not really doing teshuvah

He knows he did wrong and it bothers him, but this doesn't feel like real repentance.

**What is really going on.** That it bothers him is already a movement of teshuvah, and a time of favour can wake it fully. What blocks real teshuvah is self-justification, which dulls the bitterness that true remorse needs.

## Work swallows me; I can't stop to pray or learn or I'll lose money

His prayer is rushed and full of stray thoughts, he skips shiurim, and his mind stays full of business. Even Shabbos goes to idle things.

**What is really going on.** He treats the business, which is only a vessel for blessing, as the source of his livelihood. The work compares this to bowing to the sun. The excuse rests on a false imagining.

**Program: Ordering the working day: blessing first, business as vessel.** (1) Settle in your mind that the blessing comes from G-d and the business is only a vessel for it. (2) Pray on time, with the congregation, slowly, with at least the simple meaning, and ask for your needs. (3) Keep fixed times for Torah without any hindrance. (4) Then do business: a little effort is enough if the blessing is there, and a lot does not help if it isn't. (5) Give yourself over to G-d as the one who feeds you. *When:* Daily: prayer and fixed Torah times come first, then work. *For:* Businesspeople and workers whose work crowds out prayer and learning. *Not for:* Someone in real financial danger. This is not a licence to neglect honest work or obligations. `KU·Q26` · `KU·Q27` · `KU·Q28`

**Reframe.** From *"If I stop to pray or learn, I'll lose money."* to **The business is only a vessel for blessing. If the blessing isn't there, more hours won't help; if it is, a little work is enough.** Removing the false 'main thing' (the work) frees mind and time. Treating a garment as the source is a kind of bowing that the person's own faith rejects. `KU·Q26` · `KU·Q28`

## I'm bright enough, I don't need to push; or I'm too tired and weak to learn

*Its own term:* לא יגעתי ומצאתי אל תאמין

The mind says: you grasp things quickly, why strain? Or: your health and eyes can't take it.

**What is really going on.** The spirit of folly covers the truth. Nothing is attained without toil, and a broad mind that toiled would reach far higher.

**Program: Accepting the yoke as the foundation against self-credit.** (1) Remember that your understanding and the strength of your service are given from above. (2) Remember you were created for this, so there is nothing to credit yourself for. (5) Be careful even in small things, since they are great at your level. (6) Toil anyway: a broad mind still attains nothing without effort. *When:* Ongoing, around learning and long contemplative prayer. *For:* Serious learners and those who pray at length with contemplation. *Not for:* Someone already crushed by self-contempt. It is aimed at inflated self-regard. `KU·Q21` · `KU·Q22` · `KU·Q24` · `KU·Q25`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm bright; I don't need to exert myself (or: I'm too weak)."* to **No one finds without toiling, and a broad mind that toiled would reach far higher.** Unmasking the 'reason' as the spirit of folly turns talent from an excuse into an obligation to effort. `KU·Q25`

## Cautions

- Harsh labels (the sinner is worse than a gnat or a beast; overwork is like idol worship) can deepen shame in someone depressed. Lead with F07 and F08. - 'Do the opposite of whatever the urge says, with no calculation' can feed OCD, scrupulosity or restrictive eating. It also does not replace clinical treatment for addiction. - The text makes real remorse depend on deep bitterness. That can harm a depressed person; offer F11 (being touched is already teshuvah) first. - Rejecting every excuse from temperament or environment can shame someone who was abused or coerced. The text means peers a person chose. - The 'either way, more work won't help' argument can hurt someone in real poverty. It is not a licence to neglect earning or obligations. - Maamar 11 frames hardship as loving punishment to bring return. Do not apply this to a person who is suffering. - The work contrasts Jewish and non-Jewish souls and faith. A guide for anyone must not carry that over as a judgment of the person.
