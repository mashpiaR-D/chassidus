Pardes Rimmonim ("The Orchard of Pomegranates") is the Ramak's great system of Kabbalah, finished in 1548 when he was about 27. It has 32 gates. This range holds the front of the book and its first two gates. Gate 1 argues that the sefirot (the ten "measures" through which God acts) are exactly ten. Gate 2 asks why there is an emanation at all. Most of the pages are close reading of Sefer Yetzirah and the Zohar. But three things in them matter a great deal for the guide: the Ramak's reason for the sefirot (they let the simple One stay simple and still care for each person), his reason for the whole chain of worlds (so that the Infinite can be known, the way sunlight is softened by screens), and his rules for how to enter this study at all (humility first, and "running and returning" when the mind touches the sefirot themselves).

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**The One does not change. Providence does.** The Ramak's first argument for the sefirot is not mystical at all. It is a problem of faith. We believe God is simple and one. We also believe He watches over the world, even over each person. How can both be true? The simple One is "the cause of all causes... removed from change and from attributes":

> האחד הפשוט סבת כל הסבות ועלת כל העלות מסולק מן השנוי ומן המדות (line 353, Pardes 1:8:1)

So judgment and mercy, anger and favor, all the words of the Bible that picture God as having a body or a mood, belong to His measures and not to Him. We must hold the sefirot "so that the roots of our faith do not contradict one another":

> כדי שלא יהיו עיקרי אמונתנו סותרים אלו לאלו (line 355, Pardes 1:8:3)

This is the Ramak's first map of how the world stands in the One. The world does not touch the Infinite directly, and the Infinite does not become many. Between them is an order of ten, and the One acts through that order without being moved by it. Even the highest of the ten is called "nothing" (Ayin), and the One far more so:

> אפילו הנאצל נקרא אין כ"ש המאציל (line 355)

**The prayer that goes to "the Master of the lights" alone.** He draws a hard line here. Some thinkers said they would pray only to the Infinite itself, "the Master of the lights", and leave the sefirot out:

> ואינו רוצה לכוין בתפלתו אלא לבעל האורות (line 359, Pardes 1:9:3)

He calls this an error. The Infinite in itself "is not a thing of changes, that would change from judgment to mercy, or be moved in will, or change from one will to another through prayer":

> ואינו בעל שנויים שישתנה מדין אל רחמים ושיתפעל ברצון וישתנה מרצון אל רצון על ידי התפלה (line 359)

Prayer changes things only through His measures, "which are His providences". And then at once he stops himself: the reader should not push "inside the curtain" to understand how change and providence work:

> ואל ידחוק המעיין ליכנס מתוך דברינו אלה לפנים מהמחיצה (line 359)

**Nothing but the One?** In reading Sefer Yetzirah's "ten sefirot without what", the Ramak explains why the book insists they are really a number. It is so that we should not say, out of the difficulty of it, "that nothing exists but the simple One":

> שאין במציאות כי אם האחד הפשוט (line 299, Pardes 1:1:5)

He rejects that as a way to talk about the sefirot. They are truly ten, and truly without limit. This is worth setting beside Chassidus, which is happy to say exactly that "there is nothing but Him" about the world (see section 6). The Ramak here guards the reality of the divine order, not the reality of the world.

**Why there is a chain of worlds.** Gate 2 chapter 6 gives two reasons. The first: the Infinite wanted to bring into limit things that have no limit, so that a finite world could exist, with an end, with reward and punishment:

> להביא בגבול הדברים שאינם בגבול (line 412, Pardes 2:6:1)

He then sets this reason aside. After the resurrection there will be no more reward and punishment, and the sefirot will not stop being. So the real aim was something else:

> אמנם היה עקר הכוונה להודיע אלקותו אל זולתו (line 412)

"The main intention was to make His Godliness known to someone other than Himself." And this comes from His goodness: His way is to give, "to do good to the creatures":

> מדתו להשפיע אל זולתו ולהיות שפעו הטוב להטיב אל הנבראים (line 412)
> וזהו ממדות טובו היות רצונו לגלות רוממותו אל השפלים (line 412)

**The parable of the window.** Sunlight comes into a house through a small window. It is so bright that no one in the house can bear it. So the owner hangs a screen, then a second, then more, ten in all. Now the light is still bright but it can be used: "a bright light, a sweet light, a fragrant light":

> אור בהיר אור מתוק אור מבושם (line 412)

Ask why there are exactly ten screens, and you have not understood the point. Fewer would blind, more would darken. The sefirot are the screens by which the Infinite becomes a light people can live in. And the point is to reveal, not to hide:

> ואין זו הכוונה אלא לגלות האצילות והאלקות ולא להעלימו (line 425, Pardes 2:7:13)

When we see how high the first sefirah is, and then know that its cause is higher still, "how great His Name becomes in the eyes of all His creatures" (כמה יתגדל שמו בעיני כל ברואיו, line 427).

**The gap that cannot be closed.** Even the crown of the sefirot, the purest light, is "black before the cause of causes":

> אוכמא איהו קדם עלת העלות (line 377, quoting Tikkunei Zohar)

After we remove every limit from the sefirot, "we are obliged to raise Him still higher". And then: "little speech on this is beautiful and fitting" (ומעוט הדבור בענין הזה יפה ונאות, line 377). This is the Ramak's own form of knowing by "not this".

**All reality one lamp.** Each level contains all four worlds in small, and the lowest point of one world is the cause of the highest point of the next, so that all of reality is "like a lamp of joined pieces, united and embracing one another":

> כמנורה של פרקים מתיחדים ומתחבקים איש באחיו (line 420, Pardes 2:7:8)

**The soul as a part of the whole.** In Gate 1 chapter 5 he explains why a soul drawn from the Shechinah itself holds all ten sefirot: "one who breathes, breathes from his own self" (כל הנופח מעצמותו הוא נופח, line 333), and "whatever is in the part is in the whole" (כי כל מה שיש בחלק יש בהכל, line 333). That is F06 of the spine, the soul as a part of the One, in a Kabbalist of 1548.

**The end.** While the world is soiled by "the filth of the serpent" the sefirot are not united as they should be and His Godliness is not revealed. In the end "the emanated ones will be united in a complete unity" (והנאצלים יתאחדו ייחוד גמור, line 412), and "on that day God will be One and His Name One".

## 3. States of consciousness

The range is mostly argument, but four places in it describe where a person stands.

**Waking at twenty (line 1).** He writes of his youth as days "spent chasing every desire and ended without hope", and then: "my Maker woke me, like a man who wakes his sleeping friend". He speaks to his own soul: "How long will you hide, wayward daughter? How long will you be drunk?" This is the turn from S07 (Restless wanting) toward S13 (The unnamed longing): the soul hears a call and gets up to look for rest. He then names the book as something "to restore my soul, to delight in it in this world and the next" (למשיב נפש להתעדן בה בעולם זה ולעולם הבא, line 1).

**Learning as delight.** The Pardes is "an orchard I planted to take pleasure in". Study itself is a place of pleasure (S44, Love as delight; S79, Learning: mind clothed in His wisdom).

**Awe before a gap that does not close.** Lines 376-378 and 416 describe a mind that has removed every limit from the highest thing it can think, and then must say that the One is higher still. The Ramak does not describe the feeling, but the instruction he adds shows it: "a person must contemplate this and not rush to answer" (והדבר הזה צריך האדם להתבונן ואל יתבהל להשיב מהר, line 378). This is S35 (The edge of knowing) and S46 (Higher awe). He even asks forgiveness for having said too much: "long speech about Him is surely forbidden, and may God forgive what we have said at length until now" (line 417).

**The one who never learned.** He is gentle with the person who never learned about the sefirot. Such a person is no heretic. But "he never merited to see lights in his days, and never tasted the sweetness of the Torah" (לא זכה לראות מאורות מימיו ולא טעם במתק התורה, line 358). This is S12 (The flat world, nobody there), stated with pity rather than blame.

**Proposed state (ours), RK-S01. Light through screens.** The Ramak's parable gives a state the map lacks: to feel that what reaches you is the Infinite itself, softened so you can take it. Not less of Him, but Him made bearable. Inside: the light of this hour is exactly as bright as I can hold; more would blind me, less would leave me dark. How to enter: picture the window and the ten screens (line 412), then look at one thing in your day and ask what light it is that reaches you through it.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Before study: clean thoughts and humility (line 291).** "It is not fitting to come at every time into the holy place until he has cleaned his thoughts of the vanities of the time":

> אין ראוי לבא בכל עת אל הקדש עד יטהר מחשבותיו מהבלי הזמן (line 291)

He must "clean his head of pride", which is a shell that stops a person from seeing the pleasantness of God. He must "make himself low as dust before every person, hear his shame and be glad in suffering":

> צריך להשפיל עצמו כעפר לפני כל אדם ויהיה שומ' חרפתו ושמח ביסורין (line 291)

and "behave with lowliness for a time, until he sees it in himself, and then enter":

> לכן צריך להתנהג בשפלות זמן עד אשר יראה בעצמו ואז יכנס אל מקדש ה' (line 291)

This is a real practice with an entry test. It ties to C5 (the accounting) and E1 (thinking oneself nothing), but it is set as the gate to contemplation, not as a practice on its own.

**The prayer before entering (line 294).** A short prayer to be said before study. It begins with praise of the One above all four worlds, "who alone is Master and there is no second, uniting with them a complete union" (שאדון יחיד ואין שני מתייחד עמהם ייחוד גמור, line 294), then confesses with every limb and part of the soul, and asks: "open my eyes." This is A4 (the declared intention) and before_whom.

**How to learn the hidden wisdom (line 345).** This is the richest instruction in the range. Reading "understand with wisdom, be wise with understanding" in Sefer Yetzirah, he makes it a method of study:

- Take what you received from your teacher, and work it until you can draw one thing out of another, "so that he has a mind that gives birth and not a barren mind" (שיהיה לו שכל מוליד ולא שכל עקר). - Then test what you have produced against the words of the masters. "Do not lean on your own understanding." - When the mind touches the sefirot in themselves, "the testing must be only by way of running and returning, and by way of humility, and to care for the honor of your Maker":

> תהיה הבחינה בדרך רצוא ושוב בלבד ובדרך ענוה ולחוש על כבוד קונך (line 345)

- The effects of the sefirot, on the other hand, may be searched with full force. - And all of it has one aim: "the union of the emanated, who are united with the Emanator, and the Emanator with the emanated" (יחוד הנאצלים המתייחדים במאציל והמאציל בנאצלים, line 345).

"Running and returning" (ratzo vashov, from Ezekiel's creatures) is here a rule for thought: touch, and draw back; do not stay and grasp. It is the same phrase the map uses for C5 and S47-S48, but the Ramak puts it inside the mind, as a guard on contemplation of the divine. That belongs with the edge technique (`edge`).

**Proposed technique (ours), RK-T01. Touch and draw back.** When contemplation reaches something about God Himself, hold the thought for a moment, then let go of it and turn to what it does in the world. Do not try to picture it or pin it down. Return to it later. What it is for: to keep the mind from making an image of God while still letting it come near. Guard: the Ramak's own, "do not push inside the curtain" (line 359), and "little speech here is good" (line 377).

**The Shema said with meaning (line 358).** When a child is small we teach him the Shema, and as he grows he "will begin to wander in his thought over these words" and ask their meaning. The Ramak reads the verse: God's Name means He was, is and will be and brings all into being; "our God" means "He watches us in particular" (משגיח בפרטיות בנו); "One" means to intend that He is king above and below and in the four directions, and so is no body. A small, real guide to kavanah in the Shema.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why do we exist?** Because His way is to give. The main aim of the whole chain of worlds is "to make His Godliness known to another" (line 412), and this is "from His measures of goodness, that His will is to reveal His greatness to the lowly". The world is not a fall. It is a window.

**Why is the world finite, and why do things end?** So that there can be deeds that matter, reward and punishment, and an end to the damage the wicked do (line 412). But this is the lesser reason.

**Providence.** He insists on particular providence against the philosophers who, by taking every attribute away from God, "lost sight of His providence" (מפני הרחקת התארים נעלם מעיניהם השגחת אלקות, line 412). The sefirot exist so that a God beyond all change can still watch each person. This is the ground for being held (S84 Trust, S85 Seeing Him in what happens).

**Suffering.** The only word on it here is the practice of line 291, "be glad in suffering", given as a preparation for study, and his own witness: "we are young in years, and we have had our fill of scorn... the straps of poverty hang on the walls of our house" (line 290).

**The soul.** A soul from the Shechinah holds all ten sefirot; a person earns it twice, by the holiness of his parents and by his own deeds (line 333).

## 6. Against Chabad (first notes, ours)

- **Prayer to Ein Sof itself.** The Ramak says prayer cannot change the Infinite in itself, and that praying to "the Master of the lights" alone is a mistake (line 359). Chassidus keeps the same fear of change in God but moves the weight: the one who prays addresses the Essence, and the change is in the drawing down, not in Him. The two agree that He does not change. They differ on whether the heart may aim past the measures to Him.
- **"Nothing but the One".** Here the Ramak refuses to say that only the simple One exists when speaking of the sefirot (line 299). The Tanya and the Gate of Unity say that, from God's side, the world itself is as nothing. These are not the same claim, and the Ramak's own famous line on the world in God comes from Elima Rabbati, not from this range (see RAMAK.md).
- **Why worlds.** The Ramak's reason, to make Himself known and to do good, is the classic answer that Samach Vav sets aside as not deep enough (SAMACH-VAV.md: the will with no reason). The guide can hold both: the Ramak's is the answer a person can feel (He wants to give); the Rashab's is what remains when every reason runs out.
