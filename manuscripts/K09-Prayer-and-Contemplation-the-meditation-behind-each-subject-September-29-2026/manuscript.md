## Why a meditation behind each subject

Every concept in Chassidus points to one thing, the unity of God, and that unity cannot be understood: by definition it is beyond the mind. It can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (hisbonenus). So behind each subject below is a meditation, and the meditation, not the idea, is what matters. Each one specializes the meditation of the subject's first face.

## How to use it

This is hisbonenus, the Jewish way of contemplating as Chabad does it, not mindfulness. Take one subject per sitting.

- **Hold** is one line of teaching. Say it, and fix the mind on it.
- **The move** thinks it through slowly. It opens the line detail by detail, brings it down into your own day, gives the sources' own picture and says in a few words where the picture breaks, and makes it particular: you, here, now. The pauses between its steps are time to think, not silence to watch anything.
- **Stay** binds the mind: say the line again slowly, and when the mind drifts, come back to it without arguing with the stray thought (Tanya 3 and 28).
- **From inside** describes the glimpse. It is never asked for, and if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.
- **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, then gives one mitzvah or act of Torah in your own measure, and a line to return to during the day.

## Labels

- **Tie** says whether the move rests on steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our construction (*reading*).
- **Thin** follows the entry's own marking (its 'thin?' column; 'partly' or 'split' counts as thin). A thin subject's meditation is kept modest and says why in one line.
- **Where the sources differ** keeps a disagreement the entry records, where the meditation touches it, rather than smoothing it.
- **Note** marks any other place where the meditation leans on our reading.
- **Anchors** are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly (reference, line and Hebrew), each with the entry's own gloss. *OCR* marks uncorrected machine transcription, quoted as the entry quotes it.

## S101 · Prayer · תפילה

**Face:** F05 The Essence beyond both · **also touches:** F06 The soul as part of the One; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F04 Filling and surrounding **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** When you pray you are speaking to Him Himself, not to one of His lights, and it is the essence of your soul that speaks. The One who alone can do anything is the One being asked.

**Hold.** 'Prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul with the Essence of Ein Sof' (the Alter Rebbe, Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei).

**The move.** Open it. To whom: to Him and not to His attributes, the Essence that every simple Jew knows by faith. For what: that the light itself come down and change a thing from what it is, since He alone can do anything; Torah and mitzvot draw His light by clothing it, prayer asks for the light itself. With what: words that are His word, so that your thought, dressed in them, becomes one with them. The Besht adds that since you are a part of Him, you ask for the lack in the whole, the Shechinah, and not only for your own. Bring it down: the one request you will make today, for a sick friend, the rent, a child, is asked of Him Himself. The picture is the Besht's, a part and its whole; it is only a picture, since He has no parts. You, here, now: in plain faith, the essence of your soul is joined to Him.

**Stay.** 'Prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul to His Essence', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Less like one self petitioning another than like being joined: the words are His, and your thought is dressed in them. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'I have set the Lord before me always.' Then pray one prayer today in its time, with your one request in it. Line for the day: 'To Him Himself.'

**Where the sources differ.** Prayer's rank against Torah study is recorded, not resolved: Tanya 23 puts Torah study above prayer, 'the unification of the upper worlds'; Kuntres Acharon 8 makes prayer the main service in the footsteps of Messiah. This meditation does not rank them.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Hayom Yom, תשרי / יא תשרי:1 (line 501): תפלה באמונה פשוטה איז מחבר עצמות הנשמה מיט עצמות א"ס The Alter Rebbe (Motzaei Yom Kippur 5556, via Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei): 'to Him and not to His attributes' means the Essence of Ein Sof, and prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul with the Essence of Ein Sof, so that the Essence itself heals the sick and blesses the years. - Tanya, Part V; Kuntres Acharon 4:5 (line 1519): שהיא המשכת החיות מאיןסוף ברוךהוא, שהוא לבדו כל יכול The change prayer makes comes 'by the hand of Heaven' from Ein Sof, 'who alone is omnipotent': prayer is where the Essence's power over nature is asked for (F05). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 41:23 (line 598): מחשבתה מיוחדת ומלובשת באותיות התורה והתפלה, שהן דבר ה׳ ומחשבתו יתברך, והיו לאחדים ממש Tanya 41: prayer is self-offering, like the soul leaving the body; the thought is clothed in the letters of prayer, which are God's word, and they become literally one.

## S102 · Intention · כוונה

**Face:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Intention is where the one doing a mitzvah is joined to the Will inside it, and He and His will are one. With it a blessing is not only a sound but a joining.

**Hold.** 'One intends, in doing them, to cleave to Him by fulfilling His will, for He and His will are one' (Tanya 38).

**The move.** Open it. A blessing without intention, the Tanya says, is a body without a soul. Its soul is meaning to cleave to Him by doing His will, and He and His will are one. The Besht adds that He is clothed in the letters, so saying them makes a oneness with Him. And under all the detail the Mitteler Rebbe finds one line: 'all before Him is as nothing'. Bring it down to the bread on your table and the blessing you usually say in a rush. The picture is the Tanya's, a body and a soul; it is only a picture, since even words said without intention have His life in them, as a stone does, only not the light a soul has. You, here, now, at this meal: meaning Him in these words.

**Stay.** 'He and His will are one', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Something like an embrace through the King's garment: the letters are His garment, and the closeness is no less for it. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, at your next meal, the blessing over the food, slowly, meaning Him in each word. Then say the blessing after the meal with the same intention. Line for the day: 'He and His will are one.'

**Where the sources differ.** Must intention follow the kabbalists' kavanot? The Rivash: better to pray plainly to God with intention. The Tzemach Tzedek defends the kabbalists: the flow is from Him alone even through a sefirah, and one still calls 'to Him'. The Maggid: praying 'according to Kabbalah', every word is a Name. Hayom Yom 11 Adar I: one who cannot hold them may intend generally to be heard 'with all the kavanot'. This meditation uses the general intention and does not rule on the rest.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 38:8 (line 531): שהאדם מתכוין בעשייתן כדי לדבקה בו יתברך על ידי קיום רצונו, שהוא ורצונו אחד Kavanah is intending to cleave to Him by doing His will, and He and His will are one. Intention is the unity of the doer with the Will, which is the Essence itself. - Tzava'at HaRivash 108:1 (line 221): יחשוב כשמתפלל שבאותיות מתלבש השי"ת Tzava'at HaRivash 108: when praying, think that God is clothed in the letters; speech clothes thought, and so, the Besht says, 'a oneness is made with Him'. - Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 14:3 (line 291): העולה מתמצית המכוון הקיצור מכל ענין התבוננותו בממלא וסובב וכהאי גוונא שהוא העיקר ושרש הכוונה הנזכר לעיל דכולא קמיה כלא חשיב The Mitteler Rebbe (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 14): the root and core of intention, distilled from contemplating filling and surrounding light, is 'all before Him is as nothing'.

## S103 · Contemplation · התבוננות

**Face:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · **also touches:** F04 Filling and surrounding; F05 The Essence beyond both **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** The unity is not only believed but known in detail, until even the plainest thing in front of you, a lectern, is seen coming from Him Himself.

**Hold.** 'I prayed with the lectern, for the coming into being of the physical is from the Essence' (the Alter Rebbe's answer, as the Rayatz tells it).

**The move.** Open it detail by detail, not as a general idea; the Rashab insists on detail, since the general only touches the mind from outside. Every detail, joined to the whole, is drawn back, the Mitteler Rebbe says, to the Essence before any contraction. And the object may be the highest or the lowest: the Mitteler Rebbe prayed with the bitul of Adam Kadmon, his father 'with the lectern'. Bring it down to the table or lectern in front of you this morning: it does not come from some middle level but from the Essence itself. The picture is the Tanya's, training the eye to see heaven and earth as the King's outer garments; it is only a picture, since He has no body inside them. You, here, now: this plain thing is from Him Himself.

**Stay.** 'The physical comes from the Essence', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** What was an idea becomes as plain as the thing in front of you; the heart may answer, as a face answers its reflection in water. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'The Lord is God in heaven above and on earth below; there is nothing else.' Then learn one short passage of Chassidus before you pray tomorrow, to think over in the prayer. Line for the day: 'From the Essence, even this.'

**Where the sources differ.** What is the object of contemplation, the highest or the lowest? The Mitteler Rebbe prayed with the bitul of Adam Kadmon; the Alter Rebbe 'with the lectern'. This meditation takes the lectern and keeps the other. On felt arousal the sources differ too: R. Aharon of Strashelye has it strengthen the understanding of the unity; the Mitteler Rebbe puts 'the intention in the heart' above the heart's arousal.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Kuntres Toras HaChassidus (scans, OCR), id 15658, scan p. 7 ¶12 (line 46) *(OCR)*: אני התפללתי עם הסטעגדער, דהתהוות הגשמיות הוא מהעצמות The Alter Rebbe's answer: he contemplated 'with the lectern', since the physical comes into being from the Essence. The highest object is found in the lowest (G05). OCR: סטענדער. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 42:19 (line 622): אשר כל מה שרואה בעיניו, השמים והארץ ומלואה, הכל הם לבושים החיצונים של המלך הקדושברוךהוא Tanya 42: the essence of contemplation is habit, training the mind always to see heaven and earth as the King's outer garments, which is why this is called emunah, 'training'. - The Gate of Unity 7:29 (line 315): אזי יומשך הכל אל המקור הראשון שהוא בחינת עצמיות אור אין סוף שלפני הצמצום The Mitteler Rebbe (Shaar HaYichud 7:29): contemplating every detail from Malchus of Ein Sof down to Malchus of Asiyah draws everything to the first source, the Essence of the light before the tzimtzum (G04).

## S104 · The Shema · קריאת שמע

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** The Shema is not only a statement that He is One: the moment you mean it, your soul is included in that One. Nothing stands in the way but the will.

**Hold.** 'The moment one wills it, accepts and draws His Godliness upon oneself, and says "the Lord our God, the Lord is One", the soul is included in His unity of itself' (Tanya 47).

**The move.** Open it. The first verse is the higher unity: every detail you know of the world is drawn into the One, and 'before One, what is there to count?' The next line, 'Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom', is the lower unity: the world is real from below, and as nothing before Him from above. And the Tanya adds the soul's side: in the Shema you draw His unity upon yourself, and the only barrier is the will. Bring it down to tonight's bedtime Shema, said tired, in your own room. The picture is the Tanya's, a daily going out of Egypt; it is only a picture, since nothing travels anywhere: the will turns. You, here, now: willing it is enough to be included.

**Stay.** 'The Lord is One', said again slowly, meaning to be included. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A small daily going out of a narrow place into the One; something like the lightness of a ray inside the sun. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, eyes covered: 'Hear, Israel, the Lord is our God, the Lord is One', drawing out 'One' and thinking that He alone rules heaven and earth and the four directions. Then say the bedtime Shema tonight in full. Line for the day: 'Included in the One.'

**Where the sources differ.** Which is higher, the Shema or the Amidah? The Tzemach Tzedek: the Shema's union is higher, but the Amidah, said standing, is in Atzilus itself. The Rashab: the Shema is contemplation, the Amidah bitul. R. Aharon of Strashelye: the Shema's self-offering through contemplation still keeps a hold of feeling. This meditation does not rank them.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 7:1 (line 795): דפסוק ״שמע ישראל״ – הוא ״יחודא עילאה״, ו״ברוך שם כבוד מלכותו לעולם ועד״ – הוא ״יחודא תתאה״ Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 7:1: the Zohar's pair. The Shema verse is the higher unity and Baruch Shem is the lower unity; the chapter then explains the concealment as being for kingship. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 47:4 (line 674): אבל מיד שרוצה ומקבל וממשיך עליו אלהותו יתברך ואומר ״ה׳ אלהינו ה׳ אחד״ – הרי ממילא נכללת נפשו ביחודו יתברך The moment one wills it and says 'Havayah our God, Havayah is One', the soul is automatically included in His unity; the only barrier is the will. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 41:1 (line 257): הא' למסור נפשו באחד The Tzemach Tzedek (Shoresh 41): the Shema holds two self-offerings, the first 'to give one's soul in Echad'. 'Shema' means understanding, contemplating how He is one as before creation, without change.

## S105 · The Amidah · שמונה עשרה

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F02 The higher and the lower unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** You stand before the One who is found in every single place, and even the words you say are His: He opens your lips, and your mouth only carries the praise.

**Hold.** 'Lord, open my lips': the Name of kingship is what opens the lips of the one who prays (Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim).

**The move.** Open it detail by detail. Before whom you stand, the Rashab says: not a light but the Essence, found in every single place, so the main point of this prayer is to stand before Him with nothing of your own. Whose words these are, Likkutei Torah answers: His, for the praise is His own and the mouth only draws it down. And the bows repeat in deed the kingship the Shema accepted in words. Bring it down to your own place, in the synagogue or a corner of the kitchen, feet together, siddur open. The picture is the Maggid's, a shofar that sounds only while someone blows into it; it is only a picture, since He has no mouth and no body, but it shows whose voice this is. You, here, now: He is where you stand, opening your lips.

**Stay.** 'Lord, open my lips', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Something like lightness: nothing of your own to say, the words given as they are said. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, before the Amidah: 'Lord, open my lips, and my mouth will declare Your praise.' Then pray one Amidah today in its time, bowing at the blessings as accepting His kingship in deed. Line for the day: 'He opens my lips.'

**Where the sources differ.** Which is higher, the Shema or the Amidah? The Tzemach Tzedek: the Shema's union is higher, but the Amidah, said standing, is in Atzilus itself. The Rashab: the Shema is contemplation, the Amidah bitul. R. Aharon of Strashelye: because the Amidah draws Godliness into the self, the self-offering of nefilat apayim must follow at once. This meditation does not rank them.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Kuntres HaTefillah, scan p. 25 ¶1 (line 91) *(OCR)*: ועצמות המאור הוא בהתגלות שנמצא בכל מקום ומקום ממש All the lights are as darkness before the Essence, and the Essence of the Luminary, found in every single place, is the One before whom one stands and prays. Hence the main point of the Amidah is inner bitul (OCR). - Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2:7 (line 4356): בחי׳ שם אד׳ שהוא בחי׳ מל׳ הוא הפותח שפתי האדם המתפלל Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2: 'Adonai, open my lips'. The Name Adnut, Malchus, opens the lips of the one praying; the praise is 'Your' own, and the mouth only draws it down. - Maggid Devarav leYaakov 68:1 (line 151): יחשוב שעולם הדיבור מדבר בו וזולתו א"א לדבר כמ"ש אדני שפתי תפתח The Maggid: think that the world of speech speaks in you, and without it you could not speak, like a shofar that sounds only while someone blows into it.

## S106 · The order of prayer as a ladder · סדר התפילה / סולם מוצב ארצה

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F06 The soul as part of the One; F11 Darkness turned to light; F04 Filling and surrounding **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Prayer is a ladder set on the earth with its head in heaven: you start exactly where you are, and each rung is a closer look at the One, until the ray of your soul that is here is joined to its root.

**Hold.** 'Prayer is the ladder of the soul's bond with Godliness' (Hayom Yom 5 Kislev).

**The move.** Open it rung by rung, as the Rashab lays it out. First, plain acknowledgment. Then the psalms, where the world is made from nothing. Then the Shema with its blessings, 'the Lord is One' and 'Blessed be the name'. Then the standing prayer, before Him with nothing of your own. The rungs are climbed in order, since in prayer, unlike study, skipping achieves nothing; and the Maharash gives the reason for the shape: only a ray of your soul is in the body, its root stays above, and prayer joins them. Bring it down to tomorrow morning and wherever you start from, however low; the Rebbe says that even from the lowest point, 'earthward', the ladder reaches heaven. The picture is Yaakov's ladder; it is only a picture, since it is a ladder of bond, not of place. You, here, now: this is your first rung.

**Stay.** 'Prayer is the ladder of the soul's bond with Godliness', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Being drawn up one rung at a time, with no leap asked of you; the ray of you that is here being joined to its root. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, on waking: 'Modeh Ani', the first thanks of the day, as the bottom rung. Then pray the morning order through in its time, without skipping. Line for the day: 'One rung, then the next.'

**Where the sources differ.** Is prayer or Torah study the higher service? Tanya 23 puts Torah above prayer; Kuntres Acharon 8 makes prayer the main service in the footsteps of Messiah, because it refines the sparks; the Rashab: without prayer, learned Chassidus turns to selfhood; the Rebbe: Torah and prayer contain each other. Kept, not resolved.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Hayom Yom, כסלו / ה כסלו:1 (line 585): תפלה היא סולם ההתקשרות של הנשמה באלקות Hayom Yom 5 Kislev: prayer is the ladder of the soul's bond with Godliness. It is set on the earth, beginning with mere acknowledgment, and its head reaches heaven, in essential bitul, by way of comprehension in Pesukei deZimra and the Shema with its blessings. - Toras Shmuel, תרכ"ז / מאמרים / ויאמר כו' לך לך כו' / י:2 (line 34): כי התפלה היא סולם מוצב ארצה וראשו מגיע השמימה בחי' שרש נשמתו The Maharash (5627): only a ray of the soul is in the body, while its root stays above in ayin. Prayer is the ladder that joins the two, with four rungs matching the soul's four descents ('pure, You created, formed, breathed'). - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ג / בס"ד. ש"פ קדושים, מבה"ח אייר ה'תשי"ד.:7 (line 1997): דגם כשהאדם הוא בדרגא הכי תחתונה (ארצה), ע"י הסולם דתפלה הוא מגיע ועולה השמימה The Rebbe (5714): as the infinity of Ein Sof shines even in this world's creatures, making them long to leave their existence, so prayer lifts a person from the very lowest point 'earthward' to heaven. The lowest must contemplate his own lowliness too.

## S107 · Solitary prayer · התבודדות

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F07 The home below; F02 The higher and the lower unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** Alone with Him, speaking in your own words, the self that stands in the way grows less; R. Nachman says that in the end the world is included with your soul in its root, in Him.

**Hold.** 'One cannot come to bitul except through hitbodedut: being alone and pouring out one's words between oneself and one's Maker' (Likutei Moharan 52).

**The move.** Open it as R. Nachman orders it. Creation's purpose is to be included in His oneness. That needs bitul, the self set aside. And bitul comes by speaking to Him in your own words, one trait at a time, until even the last self-regard is set aside. The Besht makes the seclusion inward: one who cleaves can be alone with the Creator even in a house full of people. Bring it down to ten minutes tonight in a closed room, telling Him about one thing in plain words. The picture is R. Nachman's, the world folded back with your soul into its root; it is only a picture, and the Rebbe keeps the other side of it: the purpose is the world made His dwelling, so the time alone is short. You, here, tonight: speaking to Him, who is here.

**Stay.** 'Between me and my Maker', said again slowly; then go on telling Him about the one thing in your own words. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The plainness of talking to Someone who is there. Where R. Nachman says it leads is his description, reached by stages, and not promised here. If nothing is felt, the talking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'I have set the Lord before me always.' Then take ten minutes tonight, alone, to speak to Him about one thing, and keep it to ten: the rest of the evening is the world you are making His home. Line for the day: 'Between me and my Maker.'

**Thin.** Split: the tie is stated and strong in Breslov (Likutei Moharan 52), while in Chabad the practice by this name is marginal and the Rebbe sets withdrawal beneath making the world a dwelling, so the meditation stays short and goes back out.

**Where the sources differ.** Seclusion: the road to the unity, or a retreat from its dwelling? R. Nachman: bitul and inclusion in the Necessary Existent come only through hitbodedut, 'higher than everything'. The Besht: seclude with a companion; one who cleaves can be alone among people. The Rebbe: the purpose is to engage the world and make it a dwelling; seclusion is for a few, or for a small part of one's time, and Chassidus's way is raising the world, not withdrawing. That the Rebbe is answering Breslov's practice is our reading; he speaks of withdrawal from worldly affairs.

## S108 · Blessings · ברכות

**Face:** F01 The world said into being now · **also touches:** F07 The home below; F05 The Essence beyond both **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** The food in your hand is being said into being by His word right now, and a blessing wakes that word in it. 'Blessed are You' draws Him down until He is 'You', present and addressed.

**Hold.** 'When a person takes a fruit and blesses it with intention, saying "Blessed are You, Lord", as he mentions the Name the life by which that fruit was created is woken' (the Besht, Keter Shem Tov).

**The move.** Open it. 'Forever, Lord, Your word stands in the heavens': the word that made the fruit stands in it now, not only once, and when the blessing wakes it, it feeds the soul. 'Blessed' means drawing down, Torah Ohr says, so that the hidden One becomes 'You', present and spoken to face to face, as the Tanya says of 'Blessed are You'. And the aim, the same passage ends, is a dwelling for Him in the lowest place. Bring it down to the apple or the bread on your plate today, before the first bite. The picture is Torah Ohr's, a child hidden in the womb and then born; it is only a picture, since nothing of Him grows or is born, but it shows the hidden becoming revealed. You, here, at this plate: speaking to Him in the thing you hold.

**Stay.** 'Blessed are You', said again slowly, as to Someone present. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Face to face, the Tanya says of 'Blessed are You': the One addressed is not far off but in what you are holding. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, at your next meal, the blessing over the food, slowly and in full. Then say the blessing after the meal too, without hurrying. Line for the day: 'Your word stands in it.'

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Keter Shem Tov 1:159:1 (line 222): וכשנוטל אדם פרי או דבר מאכל ומברך עליו בכוונה ואומר ברוך אתה ה' כשמזכיר את השם מתעורר אותו החיות שעל ידו נברא הפרי ההוא The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:159), with the Ari: the fruit exists by the word that created it, 'Forever, O God, Your word stands in the heavens'. Blessing with intention wakes that life, and it feeds the soul (F01). - Torah Ohr, Chayei Sara 1:25 (line 371): ברוך אתה הוי' פי' ברוך הוא המשכה ורבוי שיהי' הוי' שהוא בחי' צמצום והתפשטות כו' יהי' בחי' אתה לנוכח התגלות Torah Ohr, Chayei Sara: 'baruch' means drawing down. Havayah, contracted and extended, should become 'You', present and revealed; for now the body hides it, so bitul is shown outwardly by bowing. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 41:24 (line 599): ובפרט באמירה לה׳ לנכח, כמו ״ברוך אתה״ Tanya 41: the soul is bound through thought joined to His thought and speech to His speech, 'especially in speaking to God face to face, as in "Blessed are You"'.

## S109 · Melody and dance · ניגון

**Face:** F04 Filling and surrounding · **also touches:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** A tune can carry what words cannot: the Mitteler Rebbe hears in it the steps of a contemplation, and R. Nachman the faith in the Ein Sof that surrounds all worlds, from which every melody comes.

**Hold.** 'It is the melody that belongs to faith in the light of Ein Sof itself, which is above all' (Likutei Moharan 64).

**The move.** Open it from both sides. The Mitteler Rebbe says ordered song follows the unfolding of a contemplation, each turn of the tune a step of the explanation, and above it is 'a voice not heard' that never stops. R. Nachman says every wisdom has its melody, and the highest is the melody of faith in the Ein Sof that surrounds all worlds, from which all melodies come. Likkutei Torah adds that sometimes, as soon as His unity comes to mind, the heart is drawn of itself. Bring it down to one slow niggun you know, sung before you pray, over something you learned about Him. The picture is the Mitteler Rebbe's, the Levites' ordered song; it is only a picture, since the unheard voice above it has no notes at all. You, here, now, singing: the tune is about Him.

**Stay.** 'Faith in the light of Ein Sof itself, above all', said again slowly between turns of the tune. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Whether the heart is drawn of itself is not in your hands, and nothing here asks for it; the Mitteler Rebbe hears above every song a voice that never stops. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Before you pray tomorrow, sing one wordless niggun through slowly, then say with the lips the first words of the prayer with its intention. Line for the day: 'The voice wakes the intention.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie is stated for melody (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 30, Likutei Moharan 64), the Chabad customs around niggun describe practice without stating it, and for dance no tie was found, so dance is left out here rather than forced.

**Where the sources differ.** Where does the highest melody come from? The Mitteler Rebbe: niggun follows contemplation, and above it is Moshe's unheard voice from the core of the idea. R. Nachman: the highest melody is faith in Ein Sof above all wisdom, which only the tzaddik who is 'Moshe' has, and it lifts souls out of the void. That both place it with Moshe is our observation.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 30:11 (line 574): שבא הניגון מסודר לפי המשך ההסבר של ההתבוננות באופנים שונים מסודרים בתנועות הניגון The Mitteler Rebbe: the ordered niggun of the Levites follows the unfolding of contemplation, its movements arranged like the steps of the explanation. - Likutei Moharan 64:5:5 (line 3438): כי הוא הזמר השיך להאמונה בהאור אין סוף עצמו, שהוא למעלה מן הכל R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 64): every wisdom and faith has its melody; the highest is the melody of faith in the light of Ein Sof itself, which surrounds all worlds, and all melodies come from it. - Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2:3 (line 4352): מיד שתעלה במחשבתו אחדות ה׳ וגדולתו תמשך הלב מאליה בהתפעלות Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2: sometimes, as soon as God's unity comes to mind, the heart is drawn of itself. That is Malchus singing without pause above ('so that glory may sing to You and not be silent').

## S110 · Midnight lament · תיקון חצות

**Face:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · **also touches:** F11 Darkness turned to light; F09 The unity of opposites **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**Before you start.** Now and then, briefly, alone, and always followed by joy, as the Rashab has it. Do not take it up when you are already low (a limit of ours).

**The glimpse.** Only someone who knows He fills everything can grieve that He is hidden; the grief is for His sake, and the joy that follows has the brightness of light that comes out of darkness.

**Hold.** 'To learn in true joy in God that comes after sorrow: this joy has the advantage of light that comes out of darkness' (Tanya 26, on the order after tikkun chatzot).

**The move.** Open it in its order. First His greatness, filling and surrounding all worlds, since only one who knows that can grieve that He is hidden. Then the exile itself: the flow of His Name, sent down by our deeds into places that hide Him, and mercy for it and for your own soul. Then, after the lament, a joyful psalm, as the Ari ordered it; the Mitteler Rebbe gives the reason, that for now the joy of the One breaks through only by way of its opposite. Bring it down to one late hour this week, alone, with the siddur open at the lament. The picture is the Tanya's, light that comes out of darkness; it is only a picture, since the dark here is His hiding, not a place without Him. You, here, tonight: grieving for Him, then glad in Him.

**Stay.** 'Light that comes out of darkness', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Something like the Tanya's picture of weeping lodged on one side of the heart and joy on the other: a grief that is about Him, and a gladness with more light in it for having come out of the dark. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, once this week at a late hour: 'The crown of our head has fallen', and at once a joyful psalm after it. Then learn one line of Torah in that joy, as the Ari's order has it. Line for the day: 'Hidden, and He fills all.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie is stated through the exile of the Shechinah and through light from darkness; the timing and the order of Tikkun Rachel and Tikkun Leah are not tied to the unity in anything read, so the meditation keeps only the grief and the joy after it.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7:4 (line 930): ובפרט כאשר יתבונן המשכיל בגדולת איןסוף ברוךהוא, ממלא כל עלמין וסובב כל עלמין The bitterness grows from contemplating the greatness of Ein Sof, filling and surrounding all worlds. The 'humiliated King' is grieved over only by one who knows that He fills all (F04, F03). - Tanya, Part III; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7:2 (line 928): לעורר רחמים על השפעת שם הוי׳ ברוךהוא, שנשתלשלה וירדה תוך היכלות הסטרא אחרא הטמאים להחיותם Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7: the lower teshuvah is to arouse mercy on one's soul and on the flow of Havayah that one's deeds sent down to give life to the chambers of impurity. That is the exile of the Shechinah. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 26:3 (line 351): כדי ללמוד בשמחה אמיתית בה׳ הבאה אחר העצב; שיש לשמחה זו יתרון, כיתרון האור הבא מן החשך דוקא Tanya 26: the Ari's order of saying a joyful psalm after tikkun chatzot, before study, is so the joy that follows sorrow has the advantage of light that comes from darkness (F11).

## S111 · Psalms · תהלים

**Face:** F01 The world said into being now · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F06 The soul as part of the One **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** The psalms were His speech in David's mouth; saying them, your mouth too carries His word, the word that stands in the heavens and holds them now.

**Hold.** 'Forever, Lord, Your word stands in the heavens' (Psalm 119, as the Besht read it).

**The move.** Open it. The Besht heard in this verse that His word stands in the heavens now and holds them in being. David himself said 'the spirit of the Lord spoke in me', his mouth only the vessel, because there is no place empty of Him. And R. Nachman adds that each psalm was said for all Israel and for each one, so you are to find yourself in it. Bring it down to one psalm you will say today, on the bus or after prayer, at an ordinary pace. The picture is Likkutei Torah's, the mouth as a vessel; it is only a picture, since a vessel is separate from what it holds, and here there is nothing besides Him. You, here, now: His word in your mouth.

**Stay.** 'Your word stands in the heavens', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Being a vessel rather than an author: the words are His, and you find yourself in them. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips one psalm today, slowly, and stop at the one verse where you find yourself. Then say that psalm once more for someone who needs it. Line for the day: 'His word in my mouth.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie is stated through David's speech as His (Likkutei Torah) and in the Tzemach Tzedek's readings of particular psalms; the custom of saying Tehillim is framed by its power and merit, and linking that to the unity is our reading.

**Where the sources differ.** How much Tehillim, and when? The Tzemach Tzedek: if you knew their power you would say them always. Tzava'at HaRivash 38: not many before prayer, lest the day's obligation lose its devekut. R. Nachman: find yourself in each psalm.

**Note.** That the book of Psalms as such grounds the face of continuous creation is our reading; the Besht's reading of the one verse is stated.
