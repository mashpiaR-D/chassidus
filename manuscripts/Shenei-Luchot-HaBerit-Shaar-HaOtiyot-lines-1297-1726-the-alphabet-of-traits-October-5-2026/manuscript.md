"The Gate of Letters" is the Shelah's book of traits, one trait for each letter of the alphabet (line 1297). It is his most practical part: how to get up, how to do business, how to treat a wife, a child, an enemy, a stranger, one's own body. The unity of G-d stands under it all, and in a few places he says so outright.

## 1.1 Before One, what do you count

The Shelah's note on the second of the thirteen principles is his sharpest statement on the oneness of the sefirot. G-d is not one as a kind, a species, a compound or a number. The names of judgment and mercy are "truly one". Division exists only for those who receive:

"Before One, what do you count?" In Atzilut one can speak of "joining, not separation". At the root one cannot even say that:

> אבל בשרשם לא שייך לומר אפילו חיבור לרוב ההעלם בעצם הפשוט (line 1300)

"At their root one cannot even say joining, so hidden are they in the simple Essence." He comes back to it under the good inclination (line 1543): the ten in Atzilut

> שהם אחדות אחד במוחלט, והמספר אינו אלא בדרך השתלשלות בעילה ועלול, אבל הכל אחדות אחד (line 1543)

"are one absolute oneness; number is only by way of the chain of cause and effect, but all is one oneness." From this he draws a practical rule: every mitzvah holds all the mitzvot, every limb holds all the limbs. One mitzvah done fully makes "a whole angel" (line 1543).

## 1.2 He does not need them; they need Him

On the first principle (line 1299), from the Rambam:

> ואילו נעלה על לבנו בטול כל הנמצאים זולתו, לא בטלה מציאותו בעבורם, כי אין צריך לזולתו, והנמצאים צריכים אליו (line 1299)

"If we imagined all beings but Him gone, His being would not be gone; He needs nothing else, and they need Him." This is the Rambam's side of the unity: the world leans on Him, He does not lean on it. It sits beside the Shelah's own stronger line from Asara Maamarot ("no existence besides His", line 878) without contradiction. The Rambam says what G-d is not dependent on; the Shelah adds that nothing else stands on its own.

## 1.3 Truth is oneness

Under the good inclination, a short and striking argument (line 1550). Ask what a thing is. The true answer is one: this is a cup. False answers are endless.

> נמצא האמת והאחדות הוא ענין אחד (line 1550)

"So truth and oneness are one thing." That is why G-d's seal is truth. The Shelah uses it to say that both inclinations were made "for one reason": even the evil one serves the good (see 3.4). For the guide this is a plain door into the unity: honesty itself leans toward the One.

## 1.4 Only sins divide

"All were fit for this cleaving, branches in their root; only sins divide" (line 1322). "Through the Torah we are joined to Him in personal providence, as a face seen in a mirror" (line 1325). This repeats the thread of the whole book: no wall stands between us and G-d except what we do.

## 1.5 The unity of G-d and the unity of people

The Shelah ties them together three times:

> הנה מחוברים אלו ב' אהבות ומתייחדים ע"י אחדותו ית' (line 1378)

"These two loves [of G-d and of one's fellow] are joined and made one through His oneness."

> כי אנחנו כנסת ישראל מיוחדים בסוד האחד האמתי, וכשיש מחלוקת אז ח"ו יש חלוקה ופירוד למעלה (line 1391)

"We are made one in the secret of the true One; when there is strife there is division and separation above." And: "love G-d matches love your fellow; who keeps one keeps the other" (line 1396). The image is the body: "the whole community is like one body divided into limbs" (line 1383). If your hand hits your foot, the foot does not hit back.

## 1.6 The body as the Name

The last letter, holiness, maps the Name onto the body (lines 1669-1680): two eyes like two *yud*s, the nose a *vav*; the head a *yud*, the ten fingers the two *heh*s, the trunk a *vav*; below the navel the same shape again, "hinting that all is from Him, so it is in the shadow above" (line 1677). The heart is the Holy of Holies, the pupils the cherubim, the whole body the Menorah: "the soul of man is the lamp of G-d" (line 1680). This is the image of Toldot Adam, now made into a way to see yourself.

## 3.1 The morning, before anything

"When a person gets up from bed, let him do no business in the world and not say even one word", only wash, and then "think of the Creator with a clinging thought, that He is single and one" (line 1354). Then look at heaven and earth, their size and wisdom, until "you will be filled with awe" and "love will also enter his heart to cleave to Him". Only then say the first blessing. *Tie:* `arrive`, `before_whom`, `breadth`. This is the Asara Maamarot contemplation (up the worlds) set as the first act of the day.

## 3.2 Trust in the work of the day

Say "if G-d wills" over every plan (line 1355). Work is the vessel, not the source: "will G-d open windows in heaven to drop him a sack of coins? G-d provides by His good will through nature". On profit, say "with G-d's help". On loss, "this was from G-d", search your deeds and mend. Do not cut your giving after a loss. Before a job of work, a short prayer:

> חלוק לי מחסדך ליתן ברכה במעשה ידי בפעולה זו (line 1356)

"Share Your kindness with me and put blessing in the work of my hands in this task." *Tie:* S84, S85. *Proposed technique:* "If G-d wills" (a word said over every plan).

## 3.3 Saying the intention aloud, even for weekday work

The Shelah gathers sources: Reshit Chochmah says before a mitzvah "I am going to unite the Presence with the Holy One"; the Zohar says to name G-d's name over every work; the women who spun for the Tabernacle said "this is for the Sanctuary". He concludes that one should say *leshem yichud* over weekday work too (lines 1359-1363):

> נמצא שאף החול נעשה קודש, והכל הוא מצוה (line 1363)

"So even the weekday becomes holy, and all of it is a mitzvah." *Guard:* do not say G-d's name in vain or in an unclean place. *Tie:* `act`, `lips`. *Proposed technique:* "Name the aim before the act".

## 3.4 The inner aim of the tempter

The strongest practice of this part (lines 1546-1548). The evil inclination was made for a double good: so that a person will want, refuse, and so earn more. Its surface says "sin"; its inside says "overcome me". One who follows the inside

> ונמצא הכל יצר טוב, ואדרבה תוכיות הרע הוא טוב ביותר (line 1547)

"so all is the good inclination; on the contrary, the inside of the bad is the best good." When a desire for sin comes, let him say: how can I obey this?

> הלא עצמיות תוכיות כוונתו היא להיפך (line 1548)

"Its own inner aim is the opposite." *For:* to meet temptation without panic and without self-hatred, as a test sent by the One. *Tie:* `discern`; S30, S31. This is close to the Tanya's ch. 9 and 27 (the King's test, the harlot parable in the Zohar), which the Shelah here cites from the Zohar Terumah via the Pardes. *Proposed technique:* "Read the tempter's inner aim".

## 3.5 Breath by breath

From the Rabbeinu Bachya and Reshit Chochmah: 1080 breaths an hour, each carrying a letter of the Name (lines 1458-1462). "Since G-d gives the breath and the life, all one's breaths should be for His service." *For:* awe, humility, not wasting life on sin. *Tie:* close to `hold` and `carry`, but the anchor is the breath. *Proposed technique:* "Each breath a letter". *Guard (ours):* this is not breath control or a relaxation method; the point is Whose life it is.

## 3.6 A verse against a bad thought

From a manuscript of the Ramak: "an elder taught us to cancel a thought: say this verse many times, *a fire shall burn on the altar always, it shall not go out*" (line 1579). The Shelah thinks the elder was Eliyahu, and adds "I hate divided thoughts, and I love Your Torah", said "with great arousal, strong as a lion". *Tie:* `lips`. *Proposed technique:* "A verse against the thought".

## 3.7 Forgiving, at once and at night

If someone hurts you, forgive at once (the hand and the foot, line 1383); pray for him, as Abraham did for Avimelech. "Let him be glad that this came his way" (line 1394), a chance to pass over his traits. Each night before sleep, like Mar Zutra: "I forgive anyone who hurt me" (lines 1394, 1638). The reason: do not wake judgment above. "Every humble person wakes the measure of mercy" (line 1638). *Tie:* S86. *Proposed technique:* "Release before sleep".

## 3.8 Slowness (matun)

His father's saying: "slow, slow is worth four hundred zuz" (line 1584). Take time before every act and every word, "even when he hears himself insulted, let him not answer until he has set it on his heart and taken counsel with his mind ... and remember G-d and His holy Torah" (line 1587). "In stillness and calm you will be saved" (line 1585). Zeal is for plain mitzvot; everything that needs weighing needs slowness (line 1589). *Proposed technique:* "The pause before the word".

## 3.9 A friend and a daily hour

"Let them fix one hour to search their deeds and talk of waking to return and mending their ways" (line 1496). Argue gently; first understand your friend, then admit the truth (line 1491). *Tie:* `cheshbon`, done in pairs. *Proposed technique:* "The searching hour with a friend".

## 3.10 Humility, Tomer Devorah's three herbs

Flee honor; see your own lowness honestly; welcome insults as the best of sufferings, since they take nothing from your body, food or children (line 1624). David on Shimi: "G-d told him to curse" (lines 1357, 1625). *For:* S59. *Guard (ours):* the Shelah's thought exercise on the body's filth (line 1623) is his, and belongs to his time; the guide keeps the humility, not the disgust.

## 3.11 Hisbodedus

"Walk modestly with your G-d" is read as seclusion (lines 1660-1667): stay in your own four cubits, go out for a mitzvah and return. Noach needed it; Abraham was strong enough to go among people and turn them. The one who is alone and thinks each day of return "is in return all his days" (line 1663) and may come to holy spirit. *Tie:* technique 20 (own words, hisbodedus). The Shelah's form is a way of life, not a daily session.

## 3.12 Mikveh as cleaving to the Name

Forty *se'ah* are the four letters of the Name, each ten: "so the one who immerses truly cleaves to the Name, to purify body and soul" (line 1521). The chain runs from immersion to a pure heart, pure thought, pure eyes, the whole body (line 1522). Clothes are the body's garment and the body the soul's; cleaning one wakes the next (line 1525).

## 3.13 Sanctifying each limb

"Be on the side of holiness like Me: eye for eye, hand for hand, foot for foot" (line 1686). He then lists, limb by limb, what each must do and not do (lines 1687-1726), and how the whole body serves Torah: mind and heart, ears, eyes, voice, hands to write, feet to run to the study hall (line 1690). Two prohibitions warn "to remember G-d at every moment" (line 1692). In prayer, "eyes down and heart up" (line 1699). *Tie:* `act`, S81.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Why the evil inclination?** For good: so that refusing it earns double; its inside is good (3.4). The soul came before the body; the fetus sees "from end to end" by the light of knowing G-d's unity (line 1532), then forgets so that choice is free.
- **Suffering.** "Even if G-d sends some trouble, let him think that G-d meant it for good" (line 1463). Bless on the bad as on the good. Insults are the kindest of sufferings (line 1624). Aaron's silence (line 1632).
- **Trust and providence.** Nature is His vessel (line 1355); trust belongs to need, not greed (line 1601); trust is cleaving (line 1602).
- **The point of life.** "To be with Him in His company" (line 1616); the whole Torah is for dveikus.
- **Joy.** The one who understands "is glad to do his Maker's will even with no reward" (line 1530). Pray from joy of the mitzvah, not from sadness (line 1696). Sadness and laziness are "the outside forces" that make the limbs dead (line 1486).
- **Love.** Of people and of G-d as one love (1.5). Love every creature: "even the smallest of creatures should be very precious in his eyes" (line 1621).
- **The soul.** A part of G-d above, to be honored (line 1417); an envoy (line 1399).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **Oneness of the sefirot.** "Before One, what do you count" and "all is one oneness" (lines 1300, 1543) agree with the Tanya's Gate of Unity ch. 4-5 and Iggeres HaKodesh 20 that the sefirot are one with Ein Sof in Atzilut. The Shelah goes further than "joined" at the root: not even joining can be said. Chabad would put it: the sefirot are united with the light, and the light with its Source.
- **The Rambam's first principle.** The Shelah takes the Rambam's "He does not need them" (line 1299) as the base. Chabad accepts it and adds that the world is not only dependent but, from His side, nothing (Gate of Unity ch. 3, 6).
- **The evil inclination's hidden good.** Close to the Tanya ch. 9 and 27 and the Zohar's harlot parable (Zohar II 163a). The Shelah's word "inner aim" gives the guide a clear handle.
- **Love of G-d and of people as one.** The Tanya ch. 32 says the same, and uses the same reason: all souls are one at the root. The Shelah's body image (hand does not strike foot) is the Jerusalem Talmud's, also used in Chabad.
- **Hisbodedus as a way of life.** Chabad prefers set times of contemplation within ordinary life; the Shelah praises seclusion as a way of living. The guide keeps the daily hour, not the withdrawal.
- **Particularism.** Some lines in derech eretz and modesty belong to their time (the lines on women at 1447, 1656 and 1724, and the ranking of companions at 1494). The Shelah also teaches to greet everyone first, "even a non-Jew in the market" (line 1643), and to return money to non-Jews to sanctify the Name (line 1479). The guide keeps the second kind.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **"Truth and oneness are one thing"** (line 1550) as an entry into the unity for a person who starts from honesty.
2. **Read the tempter's inner aim** (3.4): meeting desire as a test sent from the One, whose inside is good.
3. **Each breath a letter** (3.5): the plainest bodily form of "given right now".
4. **Name the aim before the act**, even weekday work (3.3), and **"if G-d wills"** over every plan (3.2).
5. **The pause before the word** (3.8) and **release before sleep** (3.7): two small daily acts with the unity behind them.
6. **Mouth and heart equal** (line 1576) as a state the guide can name.
7. **Trust as cleaving** (line 1602), which joins S84 to dveikus.
