*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** The Alter Rebbe's maamarim on the weekly portions from Korach to Nitzavim, edited by the Tzemach Tzedek with cross-references and glosses. Each discourse opens with a verse or a saying of the Sages, lays out Kabbalistic structure (sefirot, surrounding and indwelling light, shells), and often turns to avodah: prayer, Torah study, teshuvah, love and awe. The late-summer maamarim (Re'eh through Nitzavim) are about Elul and the Days of Awe: the king in the field, searching for the lost spark, teshuvah as healing, and a heart that cries from its depths.

**How much of it is medicine.** some — most of the range is Kabbalistic cosmology and exegesis, but the Elul and Nitzavim maamarim, and scattered lines elsewhere, give concrete inner work on distance from God, numbness, sadness, worry, anger and scattered attention.

**Its model of the person.** A person holds a divine soul whose inner point is an actual spark of Godliness, above reason, that pulls naturally upward. He also holds an animal soul whose desiring force is fire from a high root that fell. Mind (contemplation) should rule the heart. Emotions without mind split apart: anger with no mercy in it. Sin and empty pursuits scatter the soul's powers and exile the spark. They do not remove it. Change comes by searching, by bitterness that turns into compassion for one's own soul, by uprooting the will, by spoken confession, and by building vessels of Torah and charity. Only in this world can a person change what he is.

**Its philosophy.** All vitality, even the life of evil and of suffering, comes from God. Its root is good but too high to be grasped. So concealment itself contains Godliness, and standing firm reveals it. Teshuvah reaches Anochi, the Essence, which is above the commandments. That is why it can heal what the commandments cannot. At set times (Elul) the Infinite light shines toward everyone who comes out to meet it.

**What it adds.** It gives the Elul king-in-the-field image: God meets everyone in plain clothes with a smiling face. It treats the lost spark as something sought in the very place it was lost, with bitterness turned into compassion for one's own exiled soul. It diagnoses sadness as coming from demanding greatness, and says humility belongs in the mind, not a numbed heart. It says spoken confession gathers a scattered self, and that only in this world can a person change what he is.

## I feel far from God and don't know how to even begin coming back

God seems enthroned and unreachable. Return feels like something for great sinners or great tzaddikim, not for me as I am.

**What is really going on.** He thinks one must be worthy or dressed up to approach. The work says that in Elul the king comes out to the field and receives everyone warmly. Teshuvah is turning from one's former path at any time, and it is not separated from anyone.

**Program: Elul search and return: seek the lost spark where it was lost.** (1) Know that the king is in the field: anyone who comes out is received warmly. (2) Recognize the hidden love, a spark of God within you, now lost and in exile. (3) Search where it was lost: examine past thoughts, words and deeds that were not for God. (4) Let the heart grow bitter at this, then turn it into great compassion for the exiled spark, and return. (5) Build vessels so the light stays: Torah letters (written and oral) and charity. (6) Nullify your own will before His, so that no other will remains. *When:* The month of Elul, from Rosh Chodesh to Yom Kippur. The text adds that one should be in teshuvah all one's days. *For:* Someone who feels far from God or has lost earlier warmth and wants a way back. *Not for:* Someone prone to obsessive self-scrutiny or scrupulosity. The search is meant to lead to compassion, not endless auditing. `LT2·Q01` · `LT2·Q02` · `LT2·Q03` · `LT2·Q04` · `LT2·Q06`

**Program: Teshuvah from the depths of the heart.** (1) Cry out to God from within the heart and gather heart, spirit and soul to Him. (2) Go inward, to the inside of your inside, the true point of the heart. (3) Let a weeping come that arises by itself. It does not come from melancholy. (4) Know that teshuvah is healing and reaches the Essence. *When:* Especially Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. *For:* Someone ready for a deep inner turning. *Not for:* Someone depressed. Do not push for tears, and do not treat an absence of weeping as proof of a deficient soul. `LT2·Q08` · `LT2·Q30` · `LT2·Q10`

**Reframe.** From *"God is far off on a throne. I'd have to become worthy before I could approach."* to **In Elul the king is in the field. Anyone who wants may go out, and he receives all of them with a pleasant, smiling face.** Awakening from below draws the revealed shining of the Thirteen Attributes. Once he believes he is welcome, he actually goes out, and that going out opens the heart. `LT2·Q01`

**Reframe.** From *"Teshuvah is for serious sinners. It isn't about me."* to **Teshuvah is not only for sins. It means turning from one's former path, and a person should be in it all his days.** It moves return from punishment to growth, so anyone can undertake it without first condemning himself. `LT2·Q06`

**Reframe.** From *"Teshuvah reaches the Essence of God. How could someone like me get there?"* to **It is not separated from you and not far away.** The height of the goal stops being a reason for despair, because the divine spark inside him is already joined to it. `LT2·Q09`

**Reframe.** From *"I am what I am. I'll never change."* to **This world is exactly the place where a person can turn himself around. In the next world he stays as he is.** It makes the present moment and one's own choice the lever of change. `LT2·Q07`

## The love and warmth I once had for God is gone; my inner spark feels lost

*Its own term:* אבידה; ניצוץ אלהות בבחי׳ גלות

He remembers a closeness that is now absent. His days feel like a desert of words and deeds that lead nowhere.

**What is really going on.** The hidden love is still there. It is lost and in exile in the very thoughts, words and deeds that were not for God. It has to be sought where it was lost, and then given vessels (Torah, charity) or it slips away again.

**Program: Elul search and return: seek the lost spark where it was lost.** (1) Know that the king is in the field: anyone who comes out is received warmly. (2) Recognize the hidden love, a spark of God within you, now lost and in exile. (3) Search where it was lost: examine past thoughts, words and deeds that were not for God. The search is meant to lead to compassion, not endless auditing. `LT2·Q01` · `LT2·Q02` · `LT2·Q03` · `LT2·Q04` · `LT2·Q06`

## I look at my failures and feel nothing; contemplation doesn't move me

He contemplates, examines his deeds, and his heart stays cold. He concludes something in him is dead or cut off.

**What is really going on.** Feeling pain means one is still attached, like a toe to the brain. The fitting response to numbness is compassion on one's own soul and asking for mercy. Numbness is not a verdict.

**Program: Compassion on one's own soul when unmoved.** (1) Contemplate God's unity and kingship. (2) Notice honestly that you are not moved. (3) Do not read this as being cut off. Pain and concern are signs of attachment, like a toe still joined to the body. (4) Arouse great compassion on your soul, the spark bound up in vanities. (5) Ask for mercy in prayer that Godliness be revealed to you. *When:* During prayer, especially the pleas of the Amidah. *For:* Someone who contemplates and examines himself but whose heart stays cold. *Not for:* Someone with clinical numbness or depression, if it is used to prove he is spiritually dead. `LT2·Q24` · `LT2·Q05` · `LT2·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"My failures and my coldness prove I'm cut off."* to **The spark has not left you. It is in exile. Pain is felt only by a limb still attached, and you are attached.** Bitterness then turns into compassion for his own captive soul, which moves him to return instead of giving up. `LT2·Q04` · `LT2·Q05`

## I'm sad because I'm not the great, fiery person I should be

He compares his learning and prayer to the giants and feels flat. When he falls from the love he felt in prayer, joy disappears.

**What is really going on.** The work names the source: sadness comes from demanding greatness, the level of R. Shimon bar Yochai. Joy in Torah and mitzvot, which quench the soul's thirst, is open to everyone, even someone who has fallen from love.

**Program: Joy in Torah even after falling from love.** (1) Right after prayer, learn with a thirsty soul while the love is awake. (2) When fallen (at work, or even while learning), contemplate that Torah is Infinite light, one with God. (3) Remember that permitted involvement in the world does not bind you: you can return. (4) Know your place and do not demand the level of the greatest tzaddikim. *When:* Daily: after prayer, and during the rest of the day when one has fallen from the feeling. *For:* A learner or working person whose joy depends on feeling spiritually high. *Not for:* The self-abasement step (Q13) should not be given to someone with low self-worth or depression. `LT2·Q12` · `LT2·Q11` · `LT2·Q13`

**Program: Preparing for prayer with sober humility of the mind.** (1) Before prayer, reach sobriety (koved rosh): regret that the divine is not shining openly in you. (2) Hold the humility in the mind, as clear knowing. It need not be felt in the heart. (3) Do not aim for a numbed heart: morning is the time kindness wakes. (4) Then remove obstacles: immersion in a mikveh, and giving charity. *When:* Each morning, before prayer. *For:* Someone who thinks that a heavy, dead heart is required before prayer. *Not for:* Someone who uses the bitterness to spiral into self-hatred. `LT2·Q14`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm sad because I'm nowhere near the great ones."* to **Sadness comes from demanding greatness. The joy of Torah, which quenches the soul's thirst, is open to everyone, even when fallen from love.** Dropping the demand to be great removes the source of the sadness and makes simple joy available. `LT2·Q11` · `LT2·Q12`

**Reframe.** From *"Humbling myself will crush my joy."* to **Humility belongs in the mind, as clear knowing, not as a dead heart. Lowliness makes joy in Torah double.** Someone who expects nothing of himself receives the light freely. A sober mind lets the heart stay warm. `LT2·Q14` · `LT2·Q13`

## Why is this suffering happening to me? It's just bad

Pain looks like plain evil and punishment, and it fills the whole view.

**What is really going on.** Judging by appearance. The root of every harshness is good and comes from God. It is too high to be revealed down here.

**Reframe.** From *"This suffering is simply bad."* to **Do not judge by what the eyes see. Its root is good and too high to be grasped. It is 'also for the good.'** Contemplating that its life comes from God brings the harshness back to its root, where it can be sweetened. `LT2·Q15` · `LT2·Q16`

## I can't stop worrying and scheming about money

*Its own term:* דאגה; יגיעת המוח ביותר מדאי

The worries wake up with him. He over-strategizes his business as if everything rides on his cleverness, and his mind grows heavy.

**What is really going on.** Excessive mental strain in business removes trust in God (my strength and my hand). Deep immersion in material matters, even permitted ones, makes the mind dull, and contemplation of God will not stick to it.

**Program: Move the mind off worry and over-scheming.** (1) Notice worry as the mind engaging the material. (2) Remove the mind from the worry and fix it on Torah and the service of God. (3) Work with your hands in the normal way, but do not strain the mind too much over livelihood. (4) Use Torah and mitzvot to clear the dullness that immersion in material things produces. *When:* Whenever worry rises, and it rises on waking. *For:* Someone consumed by money anxiety and constant strategizing. *Not for:* Someone in real financial danger who needs practical planning. It is not a reason to avoid prudent action. `LT2·Q17` · `LT2·Q18` · `LT2·Q19` · `LT2·Q25`

**Reframe.** From *"If I don't keep strategizing about money, it will all fall apart."* to **Work with your hands, but excessive strain of the mind is counterproductive (lit. 'commits trespass'). It displaces trust and dulls the mind.** Taking the mind off worry frees it to cling to God. That restores trust and lifts the dullness. `LT2·Q18` · `LT2·Q17` · `LT2·Q19`

## Stray, unwanted thoughts keep invading, especially in prayer or when I'm idle

While he tries to contemplate in prayer, foreign thoughts stand against him. When his mind is unoccupied they pour in.

**What is really going on.** They come from the other side's chambers. They enter because the mind is vacant, like a courtyard with no roof. Each time he pushes one away, the concealing force is subdued.

**Program: Pushing away foreign thoughts and filling the mind.** (1) When a foreign thought rises in prayer, push it away and divert your attention from it. (2) Know that each dismissal subdues the concealing force. (3) Do not leave the mind vacant (a courtyard with no roof). Keep it occupied. *When:* In prayer and in idle moments. *For:* Someone troubled by stray thoughts during prayer or study. *Not for:* People with OCD-type intrusive thoughts, where fighting them can feed them. They need clinical care. `LT2·Q20` · `LT2·Q21`

## When I get angry I have no mercy at all for the person

In the moment of anger there is only severity toward the other person, with no kindness mixed in.

**What is really going on.** The emotions are not led by the mind, so each one stands alone, split from the others. When wisdom dresses the emotions, mercy mixes into anger.

**Program: Let the mind govern the heart in anger.** (1) See that anger unled by mind has no mercy in it. (2) Bring wisdom into the emotions so they act as understanding requires. (3) Subdue the anger so kindness and mercy mix into it. (4) Be soft like a reed, not hard like a cedar. *When:* In the moment of anger, and as an ongoing trait. *For:* Someone whose anger shuts out all mercy. *Not for:* Victims of abuse. This is not a call to suppress justified protection of oneself. `LT2·Q22` · `LT2·Q23`

## My self is scattered across empty talk and pursuits

His powers and attention are spread across idle words and worldly vanities. He has no gathered self.

**What is really going on.** The soul's light spread out into thought, speech and action about trivial things and got caught in many garments. It has to be gathered back.

**Program: Gathering the scattered soul: uproot the will and confess aloud.** (1) Recognize how the soul's powers scattered into empty thought, speech and action. (2) Uproot the will from those pursuits. (3) Confess in words (ashamnu, bagadnu), because the fallen letters are raised by speech. (4) Uprooting the will alone is not enough. Speak it too. *When:* In the season of teshuvah, and in the confession of the prayers. *For:* Someone who feels dispersed and fragmented. *Not for:* Compulsive confessors who repeat confession endlessly. `LT2·Q27` · `LT2·Q28`

## The same urge beats me every single day

*Its own term:* יצרו של אדם מתגבר עליו בכל יום

Yesterday's victory doesn't carry over. The pull toward pleasure returns daily.

**What is really going on.** Each day the inclination renews itself, and each day a new spark of the animal soul is to be raised. So each day needs fresh fire from above, drawn down through prayer.

**Program: Daily prayer as battle: raising the animal soul's desire.** (1) Accept that the inclination renews itself daily and needs fresh fire daily. (2) In the blessings before Shema, contemplate how the angels, root of your desiring force, dissolve in love. (3) Let that love fall on your own desire and draw it away from physical pleasures. (4) Take physical pleasures only coolly, turning desire into a flame for God. *When:* Every day, in the morning prayer. *For:* Someone who fights the same desire daily. *Not for:* Someone who would read the daily battle as proof he is hopelessly bad. `LT2·Q31`

## Trials and obstacles keep coming at me

He feels opposed on every side, as if God has hidden His face.

**What is really going on.** A trial is concealment, and inside the concealment is the power of God. Standing firm breaks the shell and brings knowledge of God into the open.

**Reframe.** From *"These trials mean God has abandoned me."* to **Trials are concealment, and within the concealment itself is Godliness.** The obstacle becomes a shell that hides fruit. Standing firm breaks it and brings knowledge of God into the open. `LT2·Q26`

## I feel above other people and don't really need them

He counts himself the head relative to his fellow.

**What is really going on.** Each person has qualities the other lacks. No one is complete without his fellow, so he should be humble before everyone.

**Reframe.** From *"I'm the head here. I don't need him."* to **You have no completeness without your fellow. He completes what is lacking in you.** Seeing this lack in himself makes him humble before others. Through that humility people become one, and the divine unity rests on them. `LT2·Q29`

## Cautions

- Q13's self-abasement ('despised in his own eyes, like leftovers') can deepen depression or shame. Offer the mind-only sober humility of Q14 instead, and never to someone with low self-worth. - Searching one's past deeds (G01) can feed scrupulosity or OCD. It must end in compassion for oneself, not endless auditing. - 'Whoever does not weep has no whole soul' must not become a test that shames people who cannot cry. - 'Its root is good' about suffering must not be pressed on someone in acute grief or trauma, or used to silence their pain. - Diverting from money worry is not advice to ignore real financial danger or skip practical planning. - Fighting intrusive thoughts can worsen clinical OCD. Such cases need professional care. - The dichotomy of divine and animal souls, and the frame that Jews are the 'spark of God', must be translated with care for a universal audience.
