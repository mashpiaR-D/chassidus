This part of the book goes down under the mind and then comes back up into the day. Chapters 19-22 find a love in the soul that is older than any reason, a flame that leans home, and then give the book's own plain statement of the unity: before Him everything is as nothing, His word never leaves Him, and the only real separation is feeling yourself a thing of your own. Chapters 23-32 turn that into daily inner work: the hand that gives as a limb of the King, the soul that stays faithful even in a sin, the love you can wake at any moment, joy instead of heaviness, what to do with stray thoughts, how to lower the swollen self and then rise into the joy of a prince let out of prison, and love for others, since there is one Father to them all. Chapter 33 is the summit: He alone fills this very place, and He chose to live here. Chapters 34-35 bring it into the hands: a room made for Him in your fixed hours of learning and in your giving, weeping and joy in the same heart, and the body as a wick whose oil is good deeds. Each chapter was read in the Hebrew before it was retold. The source line under each is checked word for word against the Hebrew text at the line given. The Tanya often speaks of the Jewish soul; where a chapter does so, a short note says so, and the retelling applies it to every person, as the owner has ruled.

## Chapter 19 · The flame that leans home

"The soul of a person is the lamp of G-d." The Tanya stops on this verse and looks at a candle.

A candle flame always flickers upward. It does this by its nature. The fire wants to leave the wick and go back to its root above, to the great fire it came from. If it got there, it would go out. It would give no light down here, and up there its light would be swallowed up and gone. Still, this is what it wants.

The soul is like that. By its nature it longs to leave the body and cling to its source, to G-d, the Life of all life. Even if it would become nothing there, with nothing left of what it was, this is still what it wants.

"Nature" here means something that does not come from reasons. This longing is not something you worked out. It sits above what the mind can follow, in the soul's *chochmah*, its first point of wisdom, where the light of the Infinite rests. Everything holy is like this. It is not a thing in itself. It is given over to the light that is clothed in it.

Its opposite is the "other side", the self that says "give, give, feed me", that wants to be something on its own. The chapter names where that self is found, in its own terms: in the souls of the nations "who work for themselves", and in the sinners of Israel before a real test comes. It calls that way of life "dead", for "wisdom gives life."

So the love for G-d in the soul is called a *hidden love*. In one who sins it is covered, as if under a sackcloth, and a "spirit of folly" gets in. But the love is not gone. Its root, up in the mind, is only asleep. When a real test of faith comes, one that touches the soul itself, it wakes up "like one who was sleeping." Then a person who gave in to his desires all his life can stand firm, with no reasons at all, and choose G-d, even at the cost of his life. All the shells melt "like wax before fire", as if they had never been.

This light is so strong that the other side cannot touch even its outer clothing. Such a person will not bow to an idol even if his heart does not believe in it. He will not say one word against G-d's oneness, even with his heart whole. This is called *awe held inside love*: because the soul longs to cling to its root, it is afraid by its nature to touch, even at the edge, anything that denies the One.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

The soul's deepest wish is to be nothing in Him. The only thing holiness is not is a self standing apart. So the pull you feel upward is the unity itself, felt from inside.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Whatever pulled at you today, let it pull. You don't need to stop it yet. - **The line, softly:** "My soul leans home, like a flame." - **Think it through.** A candle. The flame keeps reaching up, all by itself. No one taught it. Now think of the one thing you would never do, never deny, no matter what. That "no" in you, with no argument behind it, is the flame. - **Where the picture breaks.** A flame doesn't know what it wants. The soul's pull is a love, and it lives in the soul's wisdom. - **From inside.** A homesickness without words. Not for a place. For a root. - **The edge.** Why the soul would want to be nothing in Him is above reason. The chapter says so. Don't explain it. Let it stay. - **Into the day.** Light a candle tonight, or look at one, for one minute with the line.

## Chapter 20 · One word, and everything

The first two of the Ten Commandments, "I am the L-rd your G-d" and "You shall have no other gods", hold the whole Torah. "I am" holds every command to do, and "no other" holds every command not to do. That is why the people heard those two straight from G-d.

To see why, the Tanya first says what His oneness is. He is called "One and alone", and "all believe that He alone is", just as He was before the world was made. We say in prayer: "You were before the world was created, You are since it was created." The same, with no change at all. "I, G-d, have not changed." Making the worlds, high and low, changed nothing in His oneness.

How can that be? Because "before Him everything counts as nothing, truly as zero." All the worlds come from nothing, and they keep existing, and do not fall back into nothing, only because His word, "the breath of His mouth", is clothed in them.

Here is the picture. Say one word. That word, by itself, is almost nothing next to your power of speech, which could go on saying words forever. It is even less next to your thought, where the word came from. And next to your soul itself, its wisdom, understanding and knowing, it is nothing at all. Thoughts are made of letters too, only finer. But the soul's own powers have no letters yet.

Think of how a want begins. A longing falls into your heart. Before it rises to your mind to be thought about, it has no words. It is just a simple wanting. Before that, it was only in your mind as knowing: this thing is good and sweet, a subject to learn, a food to taste. Only when the wanting rises back up to the mind, to plan how to get it, do letters and words form, in whatever language you speak.

So one word of yours is nothing next to your soul. The whole world is one word of His.

The world doesn't add anything to Him or take anything from Him. It is real, and next to Him it is one word. He is as alone now as He was before anything was.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Let the day's noise be there. Everything in it is words. - **The line, softly:** "Before Him, everything is as nothing." - **Think it through.** Say one word aloud, slowly: "cup". Now think of all the words you could still say. Then of the thought behind them. Then of you, the one thinking. The word shrinks to almost nothing. Now let the whole room be that word. - **Where the picture breaks.** Your word goes out of you and leaves you. The next chapter says: His never does. - **From inside.** The world thins out. Not into unreality. Into something small and held. - **The edge.** "Before Him" is His side, not ours. We can't stand there. We can only know that it is so. - **Into the day.** Once today, hear yourself say a word and remember: all this is one word of His.

## Chapter 21 · His word never leaves Him

G-d is not like a person. When you speak, the breath of your words comes out and is felt as a thing of its own, apart from you. His speech is never apart from Him, "for nothing is outside Him, and there is no place empty of Him." His thoughts are not our thoughts.

It is only called "speech" as a picture. Your speech brings out what was hidden in your mind. His "speech" is His light and life coming out of hiding to make the worlds and keep them alive: the ten sayings of creation, and all the Torah and the prophets.

His speech and thought stay one with Him. Think of your own words while they were still only in your wisdom, or only a longing in your heart, before they became letters. They were completely one with you there. That is how His speech is one with Him, even after the worlds came out, exactly as before. Nothing changed on His side. The change is only for the creatures. They get their life from His word as it comes down, step after step, through many narrowings, until they can take it in and not dissolve.

All these narrowings are a "hiding of the face". They hide His light so it won't come out so strongly that the lower worlds can't bear it. So to the creatures, His word looks like something separate from Him, the way a person's word seems separate from his soul.

But as for Him, nothing hides anything before Him. "Darkness is like light." "Even darkness is not dark to You." The narrowings and coverings are not something apart from Him. They are like the snail, whose shell grows out of its own body. "For G-d is the L-rd": the Name that shows and the Name that hides are one. So before Him, everything is truly as nothing.

The hiding is real for us and is not there for Him. The cover itself is made of Him. The dark day and the bright day are the same to Him.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Bring to mind a day that felt empty of G-d. Don't fix it. - **The line, softly:** "Even darkness is not dark to You." - **Think it through.** A word while it is still in your heart, before it has letters: one with you. His word is always like that. Now the snail: its shell is part of it. The cover that hid Him that day was made of Him. - **Where the picture breaks.** A snail can leave its shell behind. His covering never stops being His. - **From inside.** The fear about the dark times loosens. Held, even there. - **The edge.** How the hiding is real for us and not there for Him, the mind can't hold both at once. Faith holds it. - **Into the day.** At one dark moment today, say quietly: "Even darkness is not dark to You."

## Chapter 22 · Thrown over the shoulder

The Torah still calls it "speech", in human language, because this really is how life comes down to the creatures: through many strong narrowings, kind after kind, making many kinds of creatures.

The hiding became so strong that even unclean things could come into being: the "shells", the "other side". They too get their life from G-d's word, but through the hiding and the long way down.

That is why they are called "other gods". Their life does not come from His face. It comes from His back. The picture: a man who has to give something to his enemy, against his will. He turns his face away and tosses it over his shoulder.

His "face" is His inner will, what He truly wants: to give life to everything close to Him in holiness. The other side is what He hates. He does not give it life from His inner will. He tosses it over His shoulder, only so that the wicked can be punished and the righteous can be rewarded for holding it down. Even this "back" of His will is not really inside it. It only hovers over it from above. So the other side is a place of death and impurity. The little light it does draw in is in exile inside it.

And because the holy light inside it is in exile, it does not bow to Him at all. It lifts itself "like an eagle" and says, "I am, and there is nothing besides me." "My river is mine, I made myself."

So the sages said that arrogance is like idolatry. The root of idolatry is not denying G-d completely. Idolaters even called Him "G-d of gods". The root is feeling yourself a thing in itself, separate from His holiness. Holiness rests only on what is given over to Him. That is why the Zohar calls them "mountains of separation." And that is a denial of His true oneness: that everything is as nothing before Him, given over to His will, which keeps bringing it into being from nothing, always.

There is no second power. Even what stands against Him lives from Him. The only "other god" is a self that forgets this and says "I made myself."

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Think of one moment today when you needed the credit. Just see it. - **The line, softly:** "I am not a thing of my own." - **Think it through.** The eagle that says "I made myself." Your need for credit said the same thing, in a small voice. That was the whole of idolatry, in miniature. Now let the truth in: you are being brought into being right now, from nothing. - **Where the picture breaks.** A man who throws something to his enemy gives it once and turns away. He keeps even the other side alive every moment. - **From inside.** Lighter, like putting down a load you didn't know you were carrying. - **The edge.** Your self has no ground of its own. The mind can say this. Only being given over knows it. - **Into the day.** Let someone else take the credit for one thing today.

## Chapter 23 · The hand that gives

The Zohar says, "The Torah and the Holy One, blessed be He, are all one." And it says the 248 commandments are "the 248 limbs of the King." Now we can see why.

The mitzvot are His inner will, what He truly wants. That will is clothed in all the worlds and keeps them alive. So the doing of a mitzvah is the inner clothing of His inner will. From that deed, His light comes down into the worlds.

That's why they are called the King's limbs. Think of your own body. The moment you want to stretch out your hand or your foot, it moves. You give it no order. It doesn't wait. It is completely given over to you. In the same way, the life inside a mitzvah is completely given over to His will, like a body to a soul.

And so the part of you that does the mitzvah becomes the same. The hand that gives charity to the poor, the feet that walk to a good deed, the mouth that speaks words of Torah, the mind that thinks about G-d's greatness: at that moment they become a "chariot", a vehicle carrying His will and nothing else. The sages said, "The fathers are the chariot." Every limb of theirs, all their lives, carried only His will.

Torah study goes further. When you think and speak words of Torah, you are not only a chariot. You are one with His will, because His will *is* the law you are thinking and saying. This is permitted, that is not; this one owes, that one is free. Each ruling is His will, spelled out. So the Torah is not just a limb of the King. It is one with Him.

When you learn, His will is fully open in your mind and mouth, not hidden at all. So your soul, at that hour, is one with the Infinite, more than the upper worlds are. For the Torah is His wisdom and will, the light that "surrounds all worlds" and cannot fit inside them. That very light is clothed in you when you learn. You don't see it. That's why you can bear it.

A thoughtful person can draw great awe from this while learning. Not every mind can hold this awe. But even someone who can't hold any of it is not held back from doing what he must. The deed still counts.

His will is not only above you. In the mitzvah it is in your hand, and in the words of Torah it is one with your mind.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Look at your hands. Whatever they did today, let it be. - **The line, softly:** "This hand can be a limb of the King." - **Think it through.** Your hand moves the moment you want it to, with no order. A mitzvah is like that for His will. The coin you gave, the door you held, the page you learned: His will moved there, the way your will moves your hand. - **Where the picture breaks.** Your hand obeys without choosing. You choose to be His hand. And in learning Torah it is more than a hand. It is one. - **From inside.** Awe at a plain page. The hands feel different. - **The edge.** The light no world can hold is in you when you learn, "though you don't see it." You can't see it. That's the mercy. - **Into the day.** Before you learn today, stop and say: "This is one with Him." If you don't learn Torah, give something with your own hand and say: "His will, in my hand."

## Chapter 24 · Faithful even then

This is the sharpest mirror in the book. Hold it on a steady day.

If the mitzvot are His will, the prohibitions are the opposite of His will. Doing them is complete separation from His oneness, like the other side itself. At that moment, the thought, speech and deed that do it are joined to that other side.

And worse. The other side has no body, and it knows its Master. It never rebels against Him. It does nothing He has not sent it to do. As Bilaam said, "I cannot go against the word of G-d." It even calls Him "G-d of gods". So a person who goes against His will is, at that moment, further from His oneness than the other side is. Even a gnat, the sages said, came before the sinner. Every wild animal keeps to the task it was given. And before the righteous, whose face never loses the image of G-d, even lions bow, as with Daniel.

The sages said: "No one sins unless a spirit of folly enters him." Even someone light-headed could master her desire, if not for that folly covering the hidden love in her soul, the love that would never agree to be cut off from Him. She would give her life rather than bow to an idol, even for a moment, even without believing in it. Holding back a desire is far easier than dying. The difference she feels between the two sins is itself the folly.

In truth, even a small sin, at the moment it is done, puts a person far from His oneness. (The chapter explains that saving a life can outweigh other commands not because they are lighter, but because "keep one Shabbat less so he can keep many.")

After the sin, unless it is one that cuts the soul off at the root, the soul is not dead. Its hold on its root is only damaged a little. Then the animal soul and the body come back up from the other side, and draw close again to the holy soul inside them, the soul that believes in the One G-d.

And even at the time of the sin, that soul stayed faithful to Him. It was only in exile, inside the animal soul, which dragged the body down. There is no greater exile than this. The soul comes from His own wisdom, "and He and His wisdom are one." The chapter gives a hard picture: it is like grabbing a king by the head, dragging him down, and pushing his face into a pit of filth. There is no greater insult, even for a moment.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

The shells never leave His will, and the soul never left Him either, even in the worst moment. Nothing is truly outside His oneness. Only a choice can turn its back on it, and even then the deepest self stays faithful.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Choose a steady day for this, not a crushing one. Let yourself be honest. - **The line, softly:** "Even then, the soul stayed faithful." - **Think it through.** One thing you did that you knew was wrong. The gnat, the lion, every creature kept its task. And in you, at that very moment, something did not agree. Find it. It was in exile, not gone. - **Where the picture breaks.** In the picture, the king is someone else. Here the one who drags and the king are both in you. And the king in you never agreed. - **From inside.** A sober honesty. Under it, a strange comfort: I was not all there in my sin. - **The edge.** The soul's faithfulness runs under what you can feel. You don't have to feel it for it to be true. - **Into the day.** Do one small thing today exactly as He wants it, adding nothing.

## Chapter 25 · Closer than you think

"For the thing is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, to do it." This is what the verse means: at every moment, it is in your hand and in your power to push away the spirit of folly and forgetting, and to remember and wake up your love for the One G-d. That love is hidden in your heart, surely, without any doubt.

That love holds an awe inside it: never to be cut off from Him in any way, even if it costs your life. Not because of reasons. By a G-dly nature.

So breaking a desire, which is much easier than dying, is closer still. Turning away from wrong, even a small wrong, is near to you. At the moment you do it, any sin cuts you off from His oneness the way idolatry does. (Even after idolatry a person could return later. The chapter adds: one who says "I'll sin and then return" isn't helped to get there, but if he does return, nothing stands in the way of return.) And yet a person is ready to give his life rather than bow to an idol even for a moment, planning to return later. Why? Because the light of G-d in the soul is not in time at all. It is above time.

The same goes for doing good. The inclination makes the body heavy and lazy. Stand up against it like a lion: work hard at learning, pray with all your strength, give charity. It tries to make you cool and careful about your money and your health. To stand against it is very near, once you put it to your heart: all of this is far easier than dying, and you would accept dying with love rather than be separated from Him for one moment. How much more should you accept this, in order to cling to Him forever.

Because when you do His will, His inner will is revealed there, openly, not hidden at all. And where nothing hides His will, nothing is separate. Both your souls and all their clothing are one with His will at that moment.

Above, this union is forever. He and His will are above time, and so is His Torah. Below, in time, it lasts only while you are doing it. When you turn to empty things afterward, you are separate again. But when you come back and ask forgiveness for the time you could have learned and didn't, He forgives. That's why "Forgive us" is said three times every day.

This is why Moshe told the people to say the Shema twice a day, to accept G-d as King with readiness to give their lives. He had promised them victory. But keeping the whole Torah depends on this: remembering always your readiness to give your life for His oneness, fixed in your heart day and night. With that you can stand against the inclination at every moment.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

The love that would give everything for His oneness is already in you. The problem is not lacking it. The problem is forgetting it.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Notice the one small thing you are putting off. Let it be there. - **The line, softly:** "It is very near to me, in my mouth and in my heart." - **Think it through.** The one thing you would never give up, even at the cost of your life. That love is in you right now. Next to it, the small desire you're fighting is easy. And the moment you do His will, nothing is separate. - **Where the picture breaks.** A lion fights with its strength. Your strength here is remembering. - **From inside.** Like finding a key in your own pocket. - **The edge.** The light in the soul is above time. That is why one moment of doing His will is joined to forever. - **Into the day.** Say the Shema tonight as a readiness, not a recitation. Or say in your own words: "Whatever happens, I stay with You."

## Chapter 26 · The heavy wrestler

Here is a big rule. Two men are wrestling. If one is slow and heavy, he will lose easily, even if he is the stronger one. It's the same with the inclination. You can't beat it slow and heavy. Heaviness comes from sadness, from a heart gone hard as stone. You beat it with quickness, and quickness comes from joy, from an open heart, clean of every bit of worry and sadness.

The verse says, "In all sadness there is gain." Look closely: the sadness itself has no worth. The gain is what comes after it. That is the true joy in G-d that comes after true sorrow over your sins, at set times, with a bitter soul and a broken heart. That sorrow breaks the iron wall between you and your Father in heaven, and then "let me hear joy and gladness." Joy that comes after sorrow has an edge, like light that comes out of darkness.

For worries about this world, even about children, health and livelihood, the advice is known. The sages said to bless G-d for the bad just as for the good, and to accept it with joy, as you would a good that you can see. For "this too is for the good", only it is not seen by eyes of flesh. It comes from the hidden world, which is higher than the revealed one. (The chapter ties the two worlds to letters of G-d's Name.) So "those who rejoice in their sufferings are like the sun going out in its might." Their joy comes from loving closeness to G-d more than life itself, and that closeness is greater, without end, in the hidden. In the future the hidden will come out, like the sun from its sheath, and shine on all who sheltered in its shade.

Sadness about holy things also has to go. When you pray or learn, you must serve with joy. And if you are busy at work and a sadness about holy things falls on you, know that it's a trick. The inclination wants to bring you down into desires afterward. Where would a true sadness from love of G-d come from in the middle of business?

So whenever sadness falls on you, at prayer or not, tell yourself: this is not the time for true sorrow, not even over serious sins. That needs set times, with a settled mind, to think about the greatness of the One you sinned against, until your heart truly breaks. Right after those times, take the sadness out of your heart completely, and believe fully that G-d has taken away your sin, that He "forgives much." That is the true joy that comes after sorrow.

The good and the hard both come from Him. Some of it is hidden, and the hidden is higher. Joy is how you stay light enough to move.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Name one thing that went wrong today. Don't argue with it. - **The line, softly:** "This too is for the good." - **Think it through.** Two wrestlers. The heavy one loses. What made you heavy today? Now the hidden world: higher than what you see. This thing came from there. Bless it as coming from a hidden place: the Jewish blessing for hard news, or in your own words, "You are good in this too." - **Where the picture breaks.** A wrestler's strength is in his body. Yours is in a heart that stays open. - **From inside.** Light comes back into the heart. The world gets less heavy. - **The edge.** The good you can't see is not less good. It is higher. - **Into the day.** Say "this too is for good" about one disappointment today, and mean it as far as you can.

## Chapter 27 · Perhaps for this you were made

What if the sadness comes from bad thoughts and desires that keep falling into your mind? If they come while you are at work, not at prayer, the Tanya says: be glad. Each time you turn your mind away, you keep a commandment, "Do not stray after your heart and your eyes." That verse isn't about the righteous. It is about people in the middle, like you, whose minds wander. The sages said, "One who sat and did not sin is rewarded as if he did a mitzvah." So rejoice in it, as in any mitzvah.

Being sad about it is really pride. It comes from not knowing your place, from being upset that you are not a *tzaddik*, someone whose bad is gone. If you knew your place, you'd see you are far from that. Would that you were a *beinoni*, one in the middle, never bad for even one hour! And this is exactly the middle one's work: to push down the thought that rises from the heart to the mind, and push it away with both hands.

Every time you push one away, the other side is bent down here below. And that stirs something above: the other side up there, which lifts itself "like an eagle", is brought down too. "If you rise like an eagle, from there I will bring you down, says G-d." The Zohar says nothing gives Him such pleasure. His glory rises over everything, more than from any other praise.

So don't let your heart fall, and don't be very troubled, even if you are in this war all your days. Perhaps this is what you were made for. This is your work: to keep bending the other side, always. Job said, "You created the wicked." Not truly wicked, but people whose thoughts go where the wicked go, and who fight them all their lives and never finish the job. Finishing it is the work of the righteous.

There are two kinds of pleasure before Him. One is when the bad is gone completely and bitter turns sweet and dark turns to light: the work of the righteous. The other is when the other side is bent while it is still at full strength, still lifting itself like an eagle: the work of the ones in the middle. "Make me tasty dishes, as I love." Dishes, plural. Like two kinds of food: sweet ones, and sharp or sour ones, spiced and cooked until they become a delicacy.

Even in things that are allowed, whenever you hold back a desire even a little, the same happens. You want to eat, and you put off your meal for an hour, and learn. You hold your tongue from saying something you really want to say. You hold back a thought. Even a tiny bit. His glory rises above, a lot. And from that, holiness comes down to help you a lot. "Sanctify yourself a little below, and you are sanctified a lot from above." "Make yourselves holy", though you aren't yet, "and you will be holy": in the end, with that help, the bad will leave your heart little by little.

Even the struggle belongs to Him. The war you can't finish is one of the two dishes He loves.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Bring back one thought you pushed away today. No shame. Just see it. - **The line, softly:** "Perhaps for this I was made." - **Think it through.** You turned from it once, maybe three times. Each time, His glory rose. The sharp dish, spiced until it is a delicacy. The work isn't to be done with the war. The work is the war. - **Where the picture breaks.** A cook spices the dish. Here you are both the sharp food and the one who holds it back. - **From inside.** A quiet satisfaction where the shame used to be. - **The edge.** Why He wants this kind of pleasure too, the mind can't fully say. "Perhaps" is the chapter's own word. - **Into the day.** Hold back one unnecessary word today.

## Chapter 28 · Don't answer. Ask.

What about stray thoughts that come right in the middle of prayer or learning? Don't pay attention to them. Turn your mind away at once.

Don't be foolish and try to "lift them up" to their holy root. That was said only about the righteous, whose stray thoughts come from other people. If the thought is your own, from the bad in your own heart, how can you lift it up when you yourself are tied down with it?

But don't let your heart sink into sadness about it either. Prayer needs great joy. Do the opposite: get stronger. Pray with more joy and more effort. Tell yourself: this thought comes from the other side, which is at war with the good soul in me.

You know how wrestlers are. When one pushes harder, the other pushes harder too. So when the good soul makes an effort to pray, the other side pushes back, to confuse it with its thoughts.

People make a mistake here. They think the stray thoughts prove their prayer is worth nothing: if I prayed right, they wouldn't come. That would be true if a person had only one soul, and the same one was praying and thinking the stray thoughts. But really there are two souls fighting in your mind. Each one wants to rule you, to fill your whole mind by itself. Thoughts of Torah and of G-d come from the one. Worldly thoughts come from the other.

Picture a man praying with all his heart, and a wicked man stands in front of him talking and talking, trying to throw him off. What should he do? Not answer at all, not a good word and not a bad one. Act deaf. "Do not answer a fool in his folly, or you'll become like him." Wrestle with a filthy man and you get filthy too. So don't argue with the thought. Act as if you don't hear it, put it out of your mind, and pray harder.

And if it's too hard, if the thoughts keep crowding in, then lower yourself before G-d. Plead with Him in your thoughts to have mercy on you, the way a father has mercy on the children who came from his own mind. So may G-d have mercy on your soul, which came from Him, and save it from "the raging waters." Let Him do it for His own sake, "for G-d's portion is His people."

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

Your soul came from Him the way a child comes from a father's mind. So even in the middle of the fight, you can turn and be a child again.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Whatever is in your head right now, let it talk. You don't have to answer. - **The line, softly:** "Father, have mercy on what came from You." - **Think it through.** Two souls in one mind, both wanting the room. The stray thought isn't a verdict on your prayer. It's the other one pushing back because you pushed. Don't answer it. Go on. - **Where the picture breaks.** The man who talks during prayer will go home eventually. This one lives with you. So the silence is kept up, not won once. - **From inside.** The fight softens into a plea. Being small again, in good hands. - **The edge.** When your own effort runs out, the plea goes where effort can't. - **Into the day.** When you're distracted in prayer, or in any quiet moment with G-d, say silently "Father, have mercy", and go on.

## Chapter 29 · The log that won't catch fire

Sometimes, often even, the heart of a person in the middle goes dull, like a stone. He can't open it to prayer no matter what. He can't fight even for small things, because his heart is so heavy.

The Zohar gives the advice. "A log that the light won't catch in, you split it. A body that the light of the soul won't catch in, you break it." That means: the light of the soul and mind doesn't shine strongly enough to rule the coarseness of the body. You understand G-d's greatness, but it doesn't take hold. The reason is the coarseness of the shell, which lifts itself up over the soul's light and darkens it.

So it has to be broken and brought down to the dust. That means setting times to humble yourself, to be "low in your own eyes", with a broken heart. In a person in the middle, the animal soul is still at full strength in his heart. It is, in a way, "the person himself." The holy soul is something "You gave within me", as the morning prayer says, not yet the whole of him. Only in the righteous is it the other way around. Hillel, going to eat, would say he was doing a kindness for a poor stranger, his body. For him the body really was a stranger.

So a person in the middle can feel very far from G-d. His desires could still want forbidden things, even if he never does them. "I am a worm and not a man." He should remember the wrongs of his youth, which above, beyond time, are as if done today. Even if he returned, return is in the heart, and the heart has many depths. If today the light won't catch, perhaps he is being called to a deeper return. And one who is clean of great sins should still make an honest reckoning of all his thoughts, words and deeds since he was born: were they for G-d, or not? (The chapter adds that empty, troubled dreams show the soul isn't rising at night.)

The more he stays with this, the more he brings down the swollen self that darkens his light. Then he should speak to it, sharply, in his thoughts. The chapter gives him the words. He calls it by its hard names, and then says: "How long will you hide from me the light of the Infinite, which fills all worlds, was, is and will be the same, also in this very place where I stand, just as He was alone before the world was made? You are denying what is plain to the eyes: that everything before Him is truly nothing, as if seen with my own eyes."

This helps the holy soul see the unity "as by sight, not only by hearing and understanding." That is the root of all the work. For the other side has no real substance. It is like darkness, which is nothing and simply goes away before light. It was only given permission to lift itself up so that you would wake up and lower it. When you do, it loses its power, "from there I will bring you down," and goes away by itself.

Look at the spies. First they said the people of Canaan were "stronger than us" (the sages read: than Him). Then, with no new sign or wonder, they said, "Here we are, let us go up." Where did their faith come from? Moshe only told them G-d was angry. When they heard those hard words, their hearts broke, the swollen self fell, and what was left was simply this: they believe. So too anyone troubled by doubts about faith. The doubts are only the voice of the other side, lifting itself up. The people themselves are "believers, children of believers." Even the other side has no doubts. It is only allowed to confuse you, like a harlot sent by the king himself to test his son, so the son's reward will be greater.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

He fills this very place, now, exactly as before anything was. The only thing hiding it is a self that has puffed itself up, and that self has no substance of its own.

**Sit with it** *(7 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Not for a day when you are already crushed. The book itself sends the next chapters toward joy. Come on an ordinary day when the heart is just dull. - **The line, softly:** "He fills this very place where I stand." - **Think it through.** Stand still in one ordinary spot. The Infinite fills it, the same as before the world. Now turn to the heaviness in you, not to your true self, and say firmly, in your thought: "How long will you hide Him from me? He is here. You are denying what is plain." - **Where the picture breaks.** You split a log with an axe. Here nothing is harmed. What is brought down is only the swollen self, and it goes like darkness before light. - **From inside.** A sharpness, like cold water on the face. Then clarity: the spot where you stand is a place He fills. - **The edge.** "As by sight": not proof, but seeing. The mind can't make that happen. It can only take away what blocks it. - **Into the day.** Stand still in one ordinary place today and say: "He fills this very place."

## Chapter 30 · Until you stand in his place

Here is another thing to take to heart. The sages said, "Be humble before every person." Really humble, before every person, even the lowest of the low. How? Through another saying: "Do not judge your fellow until you reach his place."

His place is what makes him sin. His living may keep him out in the market all day, sitting on the corners. His eyes see every temptation, and "the eye sees and the heart wants." His desire burns "like an oven." You may go out only a little and stay home most of the day. Or even if you are out all day, you may not be so hot by nature. Not everyone has the same inclination.

It's true that even the one who burns has no excuse. He should have held himself back from fear of G-d, who sees everything he does. The mind rules the heart by nature. But it is a huge war to break a desire that burns like fire. It is a real test.

So each person has to weigh himself, where he stands. Do I serve G-d with a war that big? When I pray, do I pour out my soul with all my strength, and fight whatever in me blocks it, before and during prayer? Do I say blessings with real attention? Do I learn much more than I feel like, by a real fight with myself? If you learn only a little more than your nature wants, that's a small war. It's nothing like his.

It's the same with giving, and with not doing wrong. Can you stop in the middle of a juicy conversation? Or in the middle of a story that puts someone down, even a small thing, even if it's true, even to clear your own name? (The chapter tells how Rabbi Shimon said, "I didn't write this, Judah the tailor did," and his father said, "Stay away from gossip.") Or hold back in things that are allowed? These are the sins people "trample under their heels." They've done them so often they feel allowed.

But in truth, someone who knows, who holds on to G-d's Torah and wants to be close to Him, carries a much greater guilt for not fighting hard than the man on the corner who is far from all this. For those who don't know, even deliberate sins count as mistakes.

The chapter doesn't reach for the heights. It clears the ground. The one you look down on and you stand before the same G-d who sees all.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Bring to mind someone whose failing annoyed you today. Let the annoyance be there. - **The line, softly:** "Not until I stand in his place." - **Think it through.** His street, his work, his fire. Now your own quiet corner. What would it cost you to hold back what he has to hold back? And the war you do have, prayer, learning, giving, a story left untold: do you fight it that hard? - **Where the picture breaks.** You can never really stand in his place. That is exactly why you can't judge. - **From inside.** The contempt drains out. A humbler, warmer way of seeing. - **The edge.** Only He sees each person's fire. Leave the weighing to Him. - **Into the day.** Stop in the middle of one piece of gossip today.

## Chapter 31 · The prince let out of prison

What if all this lowering, an hour or two of it, leaves you very sad? Don't worry about it.

It's true that sadness does not come from the holy side. Holiness is "strength and joy." The Presence rests only where there is joy. (The Ari said even worry about sins belongs only to the time of confession, not to prayer or learning.) Still, you bend the other side with its own kind, the way an axe handle is cut from the same forest it will fell. "In all sadness there is gain", and the gain is the joy that comes after it.

In truth, a broken heart and bitterness over being far from G-d aren't "sadness" at all. Sadness is when the heart is dead as a stone, with no life in it. Bitterness has life in it. It feels, it is moved. (The chapter says the heart holds both, a holy severity and a holy kindness.)

The best time for this is when you are sad anyway, about ordinary things or for no reason. Turn that into an honest reckoning, and you are freed from the ordinary sadness. And then true joy comes.

Tell your heart: "Yes, it's true, I'm very far from G-d. But all that is only me, the body and the life that runs it. Still, within me is truly a part of G-d, which is there even in the lowest of the low. It is in exile. So the farther I am, the greater its exile, and the more I should pity it. That will be my whole aim: to bring it out and home to its Father's house, as it was before it came into my body, and to cry out to G-d for it from its exile, in prayer." This is what return and good deeds really are: bringing the part of G-d back to its source.

Let this be your work all your life, with great joy, the joy of the soul going home in the hours of Torah and prayer. There is no joy like leaving exile. Picture a king's son held captive, grinding in a prison, filthy in the dirt, and then set free, home to his father the king.

The body stays as it is. The Tanya calls it, bluntly, a snake's skin. Still, value your soul more than your body, and don't let the body's gloom mix into the soul's joy.

The going out of Egypt was like this. Why does it say "the people fled", if Pharaoh had to let them go? Because the bad in them was still strong. All they wanted was to get the soul out and cling to G-d, "my refuge." In the future, when G-d removes impurity from the earth, "you will not go out in flight, for G-d goes before you."

For deeper return and more joy, tell your heart this too: "I did not make myself. Why did G-d do this? Why take a part of His own light, which fills and surrounds all worlds, before which everything is as nothing, and clothe it in a snake's skin? It can only be that this going down is for the sake of going up: to lift up to G-d the whole animal soul, its thought, speech and deed, by clothing them in the thought, speech and deed of Torah. So this I will do all my life: bind my thought and speech to His, which are the laws laid out before us, and my deeds to His mitzvot." That's why the Torah is called "restoring the soul": back to its root. "The commands of G-d are right, making the heart glad."

The farthest place from Him has a part of Him inside it. The descent was His idea, and it is for the sake of going up.

**Sit with it** *(7 minutes)* - **Arrive.** If you are sad today anyway, good. This is the hour the chapter chooses. - **The line, softly:** "Within me is truly a part of G-d." - **Think it through.** Say the honest part first: I am far. Then: but that is only me, the outer me. Inside is a part of G-d, in exile. The heavier I am, the more it needs me to bring it home. Then the prince in the prison, and the door opening. - **Where the picture breaks.** The prince walks out and leaves the prison behind. Here the body comes along, and even it is meant to be lifted. - **From inside.** Two things at once: honest sorrow and real joy. The chest gets lighter. - **The edge.** Why He put His light in a snake's skin, the mind can't reach. The chapter answers only: down for the sake of up. - **Into the day.** Learn a few lines of Torah today, or say a psalm, "to bring the part of G-d home."

## Chapter 32 · One Father

If you live this way, with the body small in your eyes and your joy the joy of the soul, you have a straight and easy road to "Love your neighbor as yourself", everyone, great and small.

Why? The body means little to you now. And the soul, who knows how great it is at its root, in the living G-d? All souls match, "and there is one Father to them all." That's why they are called "brothers" for real, from the root of their souls in the One G-d. Only the bodies are separate. So people who make the body the main thing can't have true love and brotherhood between them, only a love that depends on something.

That is why Hillel said this commandment "is the whole Torah; the rest is explanation." The root of the whole Torah is to raise the soul above the body, up to its source, and to draw the Infinite light into the source of all souls, so that they will be "one in One." He does not rest where there is a break. As we pray: "Bless us, our Father, all of us as one, in the light of Your face."

The Talmud says that if you see your friend sin, you should hate him. But that's someone who is your friend in Torah and mitzvot, whom you have already rebuked, and he did not change. Someone who is not close to you, Hillel said, "be among the students of Aharon, loving peace, loving people and bringing them close to Torah." Even those far from G-d's ways, whom we call just "people", draw them close with "thick ropes of love." Perhaps you'll bring them close. And if not, you haven't lost the reward for loving them.

And even the one you rebuked who did not change: you must love him too. Both are true. Hate for the bad in him, and love for the hidden good in him, the spark of G-d in him that keeps his soul alive. Wake up pity in your heart for it, because it is in exile inside the bad. Pity cancels hate and wakes love.

(The chapter adds that King David's words "I hated them with a complete hatred" were said only about heretics who have no part in G-d.)

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

Under the bodies, the souls are one, and their oneness is the place He rests. Loving another person is not separate from knowing the One.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Bring to mind someone who is hard for you. Let the hardness be there. - **The line, softly:** "One Father to us all." - **Think it through.** Two bodies, separate. Under them, two souls from one root. The spark in that person, keeping them alive, is in exile too, maybe more than yours. Let pity come. Watch it cancel the hate. - **Where the picture breaks.** Brothers in a family can drift apart for good. At the root, these can't. - **From inside.** A warmth toward others that doesn't need them to earn it. - **The edge.** "One in One": souls one with each other, and He one with them. Past feeling. Held in faith. - **Into the day.** Say a warm word today to someone you usually avoid.

## Chapter 33 · The King came to live in your house

Here is another true joy of the soul. Especially when you see, at set times, that your soul needs cleaning and brightening with a glad heart.

Then think deeply and picture in your mind His true oneness. He fills all worlds, above and below. "The whole earth is full of His glory", even this earth, right here. Before Him everything is truly as nothing. He alone is, above and below, just as He was alone before the six days of creation. Even in this very place where the world now stands, the heavens and the earth and all in them, He alone filled it then, and so now, alone, with no change at all.

Because all created things are given over to Him in their very being, the way the letters of your thoughts are given over to your soul before they are thought, as chapters 20 and 21 said. Or think of the sun. Its light shines out into the world. But inside the sun itself, the light is there, even brighter, and yet it counts for nothing there. It's as if it doesn't exist. That's how the whole world is next to its source, the Infinite light.

If you go deep into this, your heart will be glad and your soul will sing, with all your heart and soul and might, in this faith. Because it is real closeness to G-d. And this is the whole purpose of a person, and of all the worlds: that He have a home here below.

Think how happy a simple, lowly man would be if a king came and stayed with him, living in his house. How much more, without end, when the King of kings comes near and lives with you.

That's why every morning we give thanks: "How fortunate we are! How good is our portion, how beautiful our inheritance!" Like a person who inherits a fortune he never worked for. Our inheritance is the true oneness of G-d: "that even on the earth below there is nothing else besides Him, and this is His dwelling in the lower realms."

The sages said that the prophet Habakkuk brought all 613 commandments down to one: "The righteous shall live by his faith." When your heart is glad in this faith, as if it were your only commandment and the whole purpose of creation, the life of that joy lifts your soul above everything that blocks you from keeping all the rest. "Shall *live*": like coming back to life.

And the joy is double. There's your own joy, in being near Him with Him living here. And there's His joy, the pleasure He has in this faith, because the darkness of this world is bent and turns to light. Especially outside the Land of Israel, where the air is full of what hides Him. There is no joy before Him like the light that comes out of darkness. "Let Israel rejoice in its Maker": rejoice in His joy, His joy in His home here below. This physical world is called "the public domain", a world of many and apart. Through this faith it becomes "the private domain" of His oneness.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

This is the heart of the book. Even here, on the earth below, there is nothing but Him, and He chose to live here. Knowing it is the joy.

**Sit with it** *(8 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Wherever you are, a kitchen, a bus, an office, let it be exactly that. - **The line, softly:** "Even here, there is nothing else besides Him." - **Think it through.** Before the six days, He alone filled this place. ## Chapter 34 · A room for Him

"The fathers were the chariot." All their days, they never stopped, not for one hour, binding their minds and souls to the Master of the world, given over to His oneness. After them came the prophets, each at his level, and Moshe above them all: "the Presence spoke from his throat." At Sinai the whole people tasted this, but they couldn't bear it. At every word their souls flew out of them, given over entirely.

So right away He told them to build Him a Tabernacle, with a Holy of Holies, for His Presence to rest in, the revealing of His oneness. And since the Temple was destroyed, He has no dwelling in His world except "the four cubits of law": His will and wisdom, clothed in the laws laid out before us.

So after you have thought as deeply as you can about how everything is given over to Him, say this to your heart: "My mind is small, and the root of my soul too small, to be a chariot and a dwelling for His oneness in full truth. No thought of mine can grasp Him at all, not a trace of what the fathers and prophets grasped. So I will make Him a dwelling in another way: in my study of Torah, as much as my free time allows, at fixed times by day and by night. Even one chapter in the morning and one at night."

Then let your heart be glad. Give thanks for your portion, with a good heart, that you get to host the Almighty twice a day, as much as your time and strength allow.

If G-d gives you more, add more. And the rest of your day, when you are at work and business, can become His dwelling too, through the charity you give from what you earn. Charity is one of His own ways: "As He is merciful, so be merciful." Even if you give only a fifth, that fifth lifts the other four parts with it, making all of it His dwelling. The sages said charity is equal to all the offerings. In the Temple, one animal lifted up all animal life, and one measure of flour lifted all that grows. (And the chapter says that in the hours of Torah and prayer, what you ate and used from the other four parts rises too.)

None of these joys stops you from being low in your own eyes and broken-hearted, at the very same moment. The lowness is from the body and the animal soul. The joy is from the holy soul and the spark of G-d that gives it life. As the Zohar says: "Weeping is lodged in my heart on this side, and joy is lodged in my heart on that side."

You don't have to be a chariot to give Him a home. A fixed hour and an open hand are a room He lives in. And tears and joy can live in one heart because both belong to Him.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Let yourself be exactly as small as you are. That is the starting point here. - **The line, softly:** "Weeping on this side, joy on that side." - **Think it through.** I can't grasp Him. Not a trace. So I will build Him a room: this half hour of learning, this coin I give. Then feel both at once: how far I am, and how glad I am that He comes in. - **Where the picture breaks.** A room is built once and stands. This one is built again every day you keep your hour. - **From inside.** Tears and joy together, neither one cancelling the other. - **The edge.** Opposites in one heart. The chapter states it and doesn't resolve it. Neither should you. - **Into the day.** Fix one time today, even a short one, as "His room": a chapter of Torah, a psalm, or a few words to G-d of your own. And give something.

## Chapter 35 · The wick, the oil, the light

Here is a hard question. The person in the middle never gets rid of the bad in him. It stays at full strength in his heart all his life. It only doesn't get out into his deeds. So why did his soul come down into this world at all? To toil for nothing, fighting all his life and never winning?

Here is his comfort, a double comfort, to gladden his heart in G-d, who lives with him in his Torah and his service.

The Zohar tells of a child who explained a verse: "The wise man's eyes are in his head." Where else would they be? He answered: a person should not walk four cubits bareheaded, because the Presence rests on his head. A wise man's eyes are on what rests on his head. He knows that the light burning there needs oil, "because a person's body is a wick, and the light is lit above." And King Solomon cried out, "Let oil not be lacking on your head." That oil is good deeds.

What does it mean? The Presence is like the light of a candle. A candle won't burn on a wick without oil. The Presence won't rest on the body, the wick, without good deeds. The soul, though it is "a part of G-d above", isn't enough to be the oil.

Why not? Because even a perfect *tzaddik*, serving G-d with awe and love and delight, is still not completely given over to Him. He is still a thing in himself: someone who fears G-d and loves Him. But the mitzvot are His will. His will is the source of life for all the worlds. It reaches them through many narrowings and hidings, until they can exist as separate things. The mitzvot are His *inner* will, with no hiding at all. So the life in them isn't a separate thing. It's one with His will, completely.

The Presence "resting" on something means G-d is revealed in it, and that means the thing is fully included in His light and given over to Him. Whatever is not fully given over, His light doesn't rest in it openly. Even the tzaddik who clings to Him in great love can't grasp Him with any thought. For the truth of G-d is His oneness: "He alone is, and there is nothing besides Him." So the one who loves, who is *something* and not nothing, can't grasp Him. His light rests on him only through the mitzvot, which are His will and wisdom with no hiding. (The chapter adds a teaching of the Maggid, the Alter Rebbe's teacher: this is why, in the Kabbalah, the Infinite unites even with the highest world only through wisdom.)

When a person learns Torah, his soul and its two inner garments, thought and speech, are included in G-d's light. That is the Presence resting on his soul: "Even one who sits and learns, the Presence is with him." But to draw it onto the body too, and onto the animal soul that lives in it, he needs mitzvot done with the body. Then the body's own strength in that deed is included in His light.

And then even the life force of the animal soul, which comes from the mixed "other side", turns from bad to good in that act, because it is the one doing the deed. Without it the holy soul couldn't move the body at all. The soul is spirit, the body is matter, and the animal soul in the blood is the go-between. Its bad traits are not yet changed. But they are held down, and they answer "Amen" whether they like it or not, and go along with the mitzvah, because the mind rules the heart. At that moment they are asleep, in exile. So they don't stop the Presence from resting on the body. From the deed, a light comes over the whole animal soul and the whole body, surrounding it from head to foot: "the Presence rests *upon* his head."

This doesn't change Him or make Him many. A heretic once asked Rabban Gamliel, "You say the Presence rests on every ten. How many Presences do you have?" He answered with the sun, which comes in through many windows.

Even love is still a self. Only His will, done with the hands, is wholly His, and so the deed carries what the self can't. The body is not in the way. It is the wick.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Whatever in you is still unchanged, let it be. The chapter was written for exactly that. - **The line, softly:** "My body is a wick, and the light is lit above." - **Think it through.** A wick alone won't burn. A soul alone isn't the oil. The oil is the deed. Your hands give, light a candle, help someone, and even the part of you that grumbled answers "Amen" and the light rests on all of you. - **Where the picture breaks.** A wick burns away. Here the wick is lifted into the light, not used up. - **From inside.** The life of the one in the middle makes sense. The deed carries what the self can't. - **The edge.** "He alone is, and there is nothing besides Him." Only what is given over holds His light. Your love can't. Your deed, His will, can. - **Into the day.** Do one mitzvah with your hands today, plainly: tefillin, a Shabbat candle, charity. Anyone can give with their own hand, and let that be the oil.
