Group-07 has twenty-five works: twelve by the Chabad Rebbeim, six modern histories and compilations from the Agudas Chassidei Chabad library, and seven from other schools. The idea is stated as the book's own subject in all four of the Mitteler Rebbe's works here. Imrei Binah opens by saying that all the Alter Rebbe's teaching was 'to fix the simple unity of God, the Essence of Ein Sof, in the mind and heart of each one', and it makes 'know today' a duty of knowledge, not faith alone. Ner Mitzvah VeTorah Or contains Shaar HaYichud itself ('the unity of Godliness in every single detail'), and its Shaar HaEmunah says that before the Essence spiritual and physical are 'equal without difference'. It has the highest rate of 'Essence' in the group. Ateres Rosh reads Rosh HaShanah as the Essence's 'I will reign', with 'Or Ein Sof below as above' at its end. Biurei HaZohar (OCR) argues the hiding ('no king without a people') and the two unities from the Zohar. The Rayatz's Kuntres Toras HaChassidus gives the Maharash's 'the physical comes from the Essence' and the method of force, light and life. His letters (OCR) state 'er iz altz un altz iz er' and insist that knowing God is a separate commandment from faith. The Rashab's Kuntres HaAvodah directs most people to contemplate the lower unity (memale) in prayer, and this is kept against Imrei Binah's demand on everyone. The Rebbe's early Reshimos show his method (halacha, then 'the life of the soul') with the tie mostly implicit. Outside Chabad the idea is stated with force by R. Tzadok (Machshavot Charutz: 'there is none besides Him even in the lowest Sheol', and no sin before Him; Sichat Malakhei HaSharet: 'while there is unity there is no room for creation'), by the Shem MiShmuel ('ein od... even sorcery', the sitra achra only through tzimtzum, pride as duality), by Kedushat Levi (all would be as if they had not been, were the vitality absent for a moment) and by the Degel ('the hiding is no hiding'). Shivchei HaBesht carries it in stories of providence. Thin, and said plainly: the Tzemach Tzedek's responsa and Seder Birkat HaNehenin (halacha), Piskei Admur HaZaken (halacha, with halachic 'bitul'), the Rashab's letters (vol. 6, communal), the Kuntres Umaayan OCR file (it holds only the historical introduction, not the maamarim), Chayei Moharan (centered on the tzaddik), and the library histories (Bukhara, Germany, the library mission, Avodas HaKodesh, a printer's memoir). Germania and Avodas HaKodesh each quote one or two doctrinal letters.

## Signature (reading, resting on stated passages)

The Reshimos in this file show the young Rebbe's method before his leadership: a strict halachic and textual analysis (the order of the Menorah lamps in the Rambam, Rashi and the Mishnah; a Gemara sugya) followed by a reading of the same subject 'al derech haderush' or 'in the life of the soul'. The one idea appears as the ground of that second step rather than as a thesis. The Temple is the world's 'head', through which Godliness is revealed in a world whose name means concealment (629, stated). A mitzvah is a joining of Creator and creature that no creature could achieve on its own (926, stated). Every Jew's inner soul is good, so even the wicked man can swear that his mitzvah came from within (1300, stated). What the work adds is the demonstration of G07 in miniature: the pshat is kept whole and then given life from within ('we will present the whole sugya as it bears on the life of the soul of a Jew', 1205). That this method is one expression of the unity is our reading; the text does not say so.

## Structure

Menorah treatise: halachic analysis of the order of lighting (1-560); 'the Temple and its vessels' al derech haderush (560-850), where the Mishkan mirrors the world and the person; 'the Menorah' al derech haderush (850-1000), on the heart's service, bitul and connection through Torah and mitzvot; later letters on the topic (to 1156). Shnayim Ochazin (1157-1465): the title page, the editor's preface and the sicha that maps finding, buying and oaths onto the good inclination, the evil inclination and the body.

## Signature (reading)

The She'elot uTeshuvot of the Tzemach Tzedek is the one idea's halachic shadow, not a statement of it. It shows the same author who wrote Derekh Mitzvotekha and Or HaTorah ruling on treifot, agunot, mikvaot and commerce with no chassidic vocabulary. The only reading under which the book belongs to the one idea is the spine's F10: halacha is His will and wisdom, one with Him. The book itself does not say this. Where the inner Torah surfaces, it does so as a source in the ruling, not as its meaning: the Zohar's praise of the beard is used as a halachic argument, and 'Atik Yomin' is glossed as a figure because God is 'above the bounds of time' (1694). We say plainly that the book as a whole does not carry the idea in any real way.

## Signature (reading)

Seder Birkat HaNehenin is a halachic code, and it does not state the one idea. It belongs to the index only through the premise of its first law: 'whoever benefits from this world without a blessing is as if he benefited from consecrated things, as it says: the earth and its fullness are the Lord's' (line 3, stated as halacha, from the Gemara). Read with the spine, this is the halachic face of 'the world is His': every pleasure is His property, and the blessing is the act that acknowledges it before use. That reading, and any tie from 'shehakol nihyeh bidvaro' to the continuous creation of F01, is ours. The Alter Rebbe draws no such line in this work.

## Structure

Introduction; ch. 1-8 blessings before and after food and drink, their order and precedence; ch. 9-11 fragrances and special cases; ch. 12 'hatov vehametiv'; ch. 13 thanksgiving (gomel) and blessings on sights (sages, kings, trees in Nisan, lightning, thunder). The structure is halachic.

## Signature (stated)

Biurei HaZohar takes the Zohar's images (the rose among thorns, the King, the moon, the beard, the wine) and reads each one as a statement about how the one Essence appears in a world that seems separate. Its angle is the Mitteler Rebbe's own: the unity in every detail, argued through the structure of lights and vessels. The lights of the ten sefirot are one with Him, 'as the Essence of the divine light is simple oneness', while all multiplicity and even 'providence to one man' comes through the vessels (3704-3710, stated). Its method is G06 and G04. 'From my flesh I see God' (3706) carries the argument, and each Zohar passage is traced from its outer image down through Malchut. 'No king without a people' is its recurring formula for the concealment. Malchut's outwardness is 'the divine tzimtzum to lower and hide the light' (173, stated), and kingship itself exists only where there is 'something separate from the Essence' (7643, stated). What it adds is the Zohar as a field for the idea: the spine's F02 and F03, the two unities and the hiding, argued from the Zohar's own text.

## Structure

Front matter and the Rebbe's editorial notes (1-30); maamarim in Torah order, Bereshit to Devarim (to about scan p. 560), each followed by the editor's source notes; appendices including the Rayatz's summaries of the first three maamarim and indexes. The notes (lines beginning with a number) inflate some term counts slightly.

## Signature (stated)

Imrei Binah is the only book in this group whose author states, in its first sentence, that it is an expression of the one idea. He says the Alter Rebbe's every discourse, public and private, was 'to fix the simple unity of God, which is the Essence of Or Ein Sof, in the mind and heart of each one', as the true unity of Godliness in the higher and lower unity, Havayah and Elokim, 'know today... that Havayah is Elokim' (3, stated). He then builds the whole book as a practice of that sentence through Kriat Shema, tzitzit and tefillin. Its angle is F12 as obligation: the command 'know today' and 'Shema' is knowledge, not faith alone, and even a simple man must toil at the unity of sovev and memale (5, stated). It rejects the kabbalists' many kavanot and yichudim in favor of 'one intention alone, to bind his soul to the truth of His Essence', because everything rises to one place, 'the simple unity' (6, stated). What it adds is the application of G05 to the lowest point: the earth, 'the end of all, beneath which there is nothing', holds the creative power of Ein Sof from the beginning, because 'their beginning is embedded in their end' (179, stated).

## Structure

Introduction (1-8): the book's thesis and the duty of hitbonenut. Petach HaShaar (9-91): the whole system in brief, ending in sovev/memale (90). Shaar Kriat Shema (92-465): Shema, Baruch Shem and ve'ahavta as the higher and lower unity, contemplated in detail, with nature, prophecy and the letters. Shaar HaTzitzit (466-545) and Shaar HaTefillin (546-603): the same unity drawn into the two mitzvot that 'surround' and bind the body.

## Signature (stated)

Ner Mitzvah VeTorah Or carries the one idea in its two most characteristic forms, as faith and as contemplation. The author states that the added gate speaks 'of the unity of Godliness in every single detail of the order of the ten sefirot of the four worlds', a digest of all that a person can bring into understanding, 'from the first tzimtzum to the end of Asiyah' (23, stated). This is the book the spine rests on for the claim that Chassidus is one point. Shaar HaEmunah gives the idea from the side of the Essence: sovev and memale are only concealment and revelation, while before the Essence spiritual and physical are 'equal without difference', 'there is none else, equally' (105, stated). Shaar HaYichud gives it from the side of the one who contemplates. It moves through every rung, from His simple will to the will for Asiyah, and shows that all of them 'rose at once in the essence of His simple will, without distinction of levels' (453, stated). What it adds is the grammar of G04 at its fullest: every particular is joined to the whole, and the whole to what is beyond whole and part.

## Structure

Introduction (1-23): forgetting and remembering, faith that includes all mitzvot, the plan of the book. Shaar HaEmunah (24-313): the Exodus and Splitting of the Sea read as the revelation of the Essence beyond sovev and memale; prophecy; the Song. Shaar HaYichud (314-459): chapter 1 on what hitbonenut is, then the chain from before the first tzimtzum through Atzilut, the screens, Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah, down to the constellations and the kelipot (455), with the aim of joining each detail to the whole.

## Signature (stated)

Ateres Rosh reads the Days of Awe as the renewal of the one Essence's will to be King over a world that seems separate. The book traces kingship through levels, each stated. First is the thought 'I will reign' in the Essence itself, 'Malchut of Ein Sof itself'. Then comes its extension to be 'King over a people'. Last is the present kingship that brings being 'continuously from nothing' in the divisions of time (51-52). What Rosh HaShanah renews is the first of these, above time. Its angle is F05 joined to F07: the future kingship 'yimloch' is the revelation of the Essence above time, when 'Or Ein Sof will be below as above' and souls in bodies will live eternal life (53, stated). It also states F09 with unusual sharpness. From the Essence's side, 'darkness is as light', small and great are equal, and the Shechinah could rest on the nations as on Israel. That is why the Ne'ilah seal asks that He choose Israel from their inwardness (86, stated).

## Structure

Rebbe's preface and table of contents (1-31); Shaar Rosh HaShanah (the four levels of kingship, then Zichronot as the ascent to the Essence's 'chafetz chesed', then Shofarot); Shaar Yom Kippur (teshuvah, Ne'ilah); the Shabbat Shuva derush on Ha'azinu (dew and rain as Torah); further derushim. Each gate moves from the liturgy (the effect) to the Essence and back to service.

## Signature (stated in places; reading for the whole)

The Rayatz's letters carry the one idea as instruction to individuals. Its center is F12: the Rayatz writes that there is no question whether there is Godliness in the world, because 'it is simple and visible to eyes of flesh'. One need only open the eyes of the intellect 'and then the seer will see that there is none besides Him, for all creation is He, er iz altz un altz iz er' (11634, stated). He insists that this is knowledge, not faith. Those who assign the knowledge of Godliness to faith 'are mistaken', since the two are separate commandments, and the knowledge is explained in Chassidus 'with instruction from daily life' (11635, stated). Its second face is providence read as instruction (G12). Everything a person sees and hears, even what seems ordinary, is holy and comes by particular providence (14099, stated, via the Besht). The purpose he gives is the home below: every worldly thing should arouse love and fear and the wish 'to make the lower a vessel for Godliness' (8933, stated). That the whole of this largely practical correspondence (fund-raising, yeshivot, rescue) is one expression of the idea is our reading.

Chronological letters by volume, each with editors' notes on sources and recipients; indexes at the ends of volumes. Letters of doctrine (e.g. vol. 3, scan pp. 178, 588; vol. 5, p. 296) sit among communal and rescue letters.

## Signature (stated)

The Degel carries the one idea as the Besht's teaching passed on by his grandson, and its angle is G03, the concealment that is no concealment. Whoever knows that everything hiding the King's face 'is no hiding, for the whole earth is full of His glory', and who cries out, knowing 'his soul was hewn literally from Him', breaks the walls of falsehood between us and God (695, stated). The spine quotes the same teaching from Keter Shem Tov: 'if one knows He hides there, it is no hiding'. Its method is G12. 'Know Him in all your ways' means that with every pleasure one sets the heart on the source of all pleasures, 'the cause of causes that gives life to all', and so pierces the physical to find its root and inwardness (192, stated). The book gives the unity a Besht-circle form that is devotional rather than systematic.

## Signature (reading, resting on two stated stories)

Shivchei HaBesht is a book of stories, not of doctrine; every unity term appears in fewer than 6 lines. It carries the one idea as it was lived by its source, the Besht, in two recurring forms. The first is particular providence: when the Besht wandered into a cellar and had the mezuzot checked, he answered the question whether a wrong turn might be chance, 'with me there is no chance', and 'let every man believe that everything comes by providence' (231, stated). The second is service in physical acts: at a pidyon haben he chewed a piece of bread with the kavanot of eating and reached what Moshe intended (250, stated). That these stories illustrate the spine's F01 and G12 is our reading; the book tells them as praises.
