This stretch is the **holiday talks** (derashot for the festivals), running from the close of Yom Kippur through Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret, Simchat Torah, Chanukah, Purim and Pesach, from 5686 to 5693 (1925-1933). Like the rest of the book they are homilies, not a manual. But the festivals push him again and again to the same subject: what the soul feels, how an arousal that comes from above can be kept, and how a person finds the self that is lost. The range does not use the word *hashkatah* (quieting) and has no instruction about melody. What it does have is a lot about **finding oneself**, **the inner will beneath the surface wants**, **arousal from below and arousal from above**, **hidden joy and revealed joy**, and two passages that tell the reader to **picture something** (1143, 1308).

## 1. What the range contains

- **Yom Kippur 1:10 (983).** The end of a talk: Israel "rose in thought" before the world was. Yom Kippur is the "seal," and "I have set God before me always" (*shiviti*) is possible only because our form is engraved above.
- **Motza'ei Yom Kippur 5690 (984-988).** Yom Kippur atones because Israel is a *small child*. A small child is loved because he knows he cannot lead himself. The more a person feels the holiness in him, the more he feels he has no wisdom of his own and prays "teach me Your way." Picture: a man in a dark cellar who sees light far away and only then learns he lacks light. The moon was made small so it could keep growing, and the righteous "will be called small" after it.
- **Motza'ei Yom Kippur 5691 (989-1009).** When Israel says "holy, holy, holy" they themselves become holy: parts of the soul rise and turn into praise. Someone who serves from love together with fear reaches "the spark of the essence" above the split between the traits. **True kindness is finding the purpose you were created for; if you have not found it, "he is not found."** Every person feels his particular purpose in his soul. The first thought of return is a light given without work; the tzaddik's work is to go "from strength to strength," which is *teshuvah* from good deeds, not from sin.
- **Sukkot 1, 5690 (1010-1020).** A real "first" is a real renewal. Anyone who fixes a trait in himself has made something new and is "first" to it. That makes him master of himself, and only the person who is master of himself can force his mind to go deep and keep stray thoughts out.
- **Sukkot, Isaac's day (1021-1027).** Everyone's work is to gather the sparks of his soul scattered through the world. There is *will* and there is *lust*: a thing you pursue for God's sake is the soul wanting its own parts back; pursued for itself it turns into bodily craving. **Check yourself always: have you found yourself yet? "The first work is to find ourselves and our soul scattered through the whole world."** Once the soul is found, one can reach *ruach*, the spirit, and holy inspiration.
- **Sukkot 2 and 3 (1028-1035).** Abraham was so nullified that everything God did felt like kindness to him. Isaac knew how to narrow himself to the level of ordinary people. Aaron: understand with the heart, not the head; faith comes from what the soul understands.
- **Hoshana Rabba (1036-1039).** God is "the cause of all causes" only because Israel cling to Him.
- **Shemini Atzeret 1, 5686 (1040-1050).** **Every desire is a prayer.** A want only arises because the soul knows inside that someone can fill it. Our work is to turn every desire of the day into a prayer to God and sanctify it. That is why prayer must be said aloud and with the whole body. The test of whether you prayed well: look at what you wanted all day afterwards.
- **Shemini Atzeret 2, 5690 (1051-1063).** The closer to God, the fewer words are needed: the Ari once grasped in one Shabbat nap what would take decades to explain. Speaking something aloud reveals what the mind already grasped. The prince far from his father, who asks only for shoes: "in the inner part of our soul we long for God," but we have grown used to exile and our longing comes out as requests for bodily things.
- **Shemini Atzeret 3 (1064-1074).** The lulav and the hakafot. "You have been shown to know": first see that "in ourselves there is nothing but Him," then make the seeing hold (*da'at* = binding). The body becomes "oil and wick" when service is fiery; cold service leaves it just matter. Rain and dew: God stirs us at times like rain, and we work to make the light stay, like dew. Someone who always goes about in holy thoughts has a kind of holy spirit.
- **Shemini Atzeret 4 (1075-1087).** In prayer God and the Jew pray in one place and their wills become one. **A request is outer will; prayer is the inner will coming up.** The inner longing for God dresses itself in bodily worries, as a prophecy dresses itself in the dreamer's own thoughts. The test: if, the more you pray, the inner will rises until you feel you want God.
- **Shemini Atzeret 5, 5691 (1088-1106).** God judges Israel "standing," lifting them above the world. Even an ordinary person can "throw himself" off the world for a day or an hour. Arousal climbs body to soul to *neshamah* up to the root. **Arousal that climbs from below keeps the body's marks; arousal that comes from above is pure.** Son and servant: our traits are "garments"; if a love has become one with its object (wine, money) we are servants. The test is to strip the love from the object. Someone who closes his windows with rubbish cannot see the sun. The highest longing: to grasp God with no garment at all, a "grasping of not-grasping."
- **Simchat Torah 1-2, 5686/5690 (1107-1115).** The Torah dances with us. Tzaddikim who built their bodies in holiness for years can make eating into service; aiming *kavanot* alone does not do it. The chassidic *tish*: those at the table feel "a holy trembling." Hunger is itself a spiritual demand. The soul is a river held back by a board; after the holidays it breaks through and even the body dances (*ratzo vashov*).
- **Simchat Torah 3, 5691 (1116-1126).** Two kinds of growth in feeling: **more** (*rabu*) and **deeper** (*amku*). "The feelings he feels now are not the same kind he felt last year." Learning out of longing to know the King touches His light directly. A talk from the speaker's own worked-through experience versus a "vision from afar."
- **Simchat Torah 4-5 (1127-1135).** The last letter *tav* reaches down to lost souls. Why a person sometimes feels great light and yet keeps the mitzvot no better: part of his soul is still held by another tzaddik who raised it. He must train himself until it comes back to him.
- **Chanukah 1, 5686 (1136-1156).** Holy will and thought stay above, and the whole work is to bring them down into action. The tzaddikim who rose "in thought" enter whoever serves in their ways and pray with him. **Picture yourself lost in a desert among beasts, your father close by and you do not know the way to him, and cry "Father, teach me Your way."**
- **Chanukah 2 (1157-1162).** A long passage (with OCR damage) on the "sling": a person who resists God's will is thrown out of the upper worlds and out of this world too. That is where stray thoughts in prayer come from. "Look inward at your soul, your thoughts and your wants." Doing mitzvot versus the self becoming good.
- **Chanukah 3, 5693 (1163-1181).** Torah as the *given* "I" (Anochi) placed inside us, which we only come to feel through study. The disciples of the prophets were taught to draw out their own spark of prophecy. Self-pity for one's fallen soul wakes the holy part. Arousal, even from whiskey, can wake the holy soul, but only if not "used for oneself." Depression is not good.
- **Chanukah 4, 5691 (1182-1202).** "If she is a wall": stand firm like a wall and let no obstacle stop you. A thought to fix in the heart and think again and again. God rests on the firm person always and on the loose one only for an hour. "The main thing is to make yourself."
- **Chanukah 5-6 (1203-1227).** Serving in "will be" (the future). The light of letters is higher than the light of intellect, so saying holy words joins you to the unity above division. Love that does not wait until it is worthy ("in her love you will always err"). Suffering from love: "who is hitting me? my father." The great pain of a pure soul sunk in the world's mud: "where am I?"
- **Purim 5690 (1228-1244).** Real joy versus revelry. Joy is extra vitality beyond what the body needs. Revelry is imagination and ends in sadness like waking from a dream. **Revealed joy** comes when light arrives beyond your vessels; **hidden joy** is holiness you have already absorbed, and it feels like a broken, "sealed" heart though it is full.
- **Pesach 1-4 (1245-1279).** Borrowed arousal: a person can pray with feeling and stay as low as before. The daily acceptance before Shema: "I bend and nullify my whole self to You." Faith made into a mitzvah. The surrounding light that is always outside a person keeps him longing and unsettled.
- **Pesach 5-7 (1280-1308).** Abraham tied a rope before going down into the pit: the faith you tie in the morning holds you at night. **Whatever a person adds of himself is his own oral Torah and "the core of his soul."** Guard your speech. Keep thinking, picturing and spreading through mind and heart that God is the only being, until the "iron wall" between you and God comes down.

## 2. The method: concrete instructions

The range has no timed sitting practice. What it has is a set of repeatable inner acts, most tied to prayer, to the start of the day, or to the moment a feeling or desire appears.

1. **Check whether you have found yourself (1025, 1027).** "לכן כל איש יהודי צריך להרגיש בעצמו ולבחון את עצמו תמיד אם הוא נמצא כבר" (1025). The work comes in order: first find yourself, then the higher levels: "וראשית עבודה היא למצוא את עצמנו ונפשנו הנפזרה בכל העולם" (1027), "כי אם מצאנו נפשנו נוכל לזכות לרוח" (1027).

2. **Turn every desire into a prayer (1044-1048).** When a want comes up, recognize it as prayer and sanctify it: "ובכל רצון שעולה על לבו צריך לדעת כי תפילה לה' היא והוא צריך לקדשו" (1044). The aim is "שכל רצון יהיה תפילה" (1048). The check comes afterwards: "וזה סימן אם רוצה האדם לדעת אם התפלל כראוי, יראה ברצונו של כל היום" (1045).

3. **Pray aloud, with the whole body (1046).** "לכן אנחנו צריכים לעבוד בתפילה עבודה קשה בקול. ובכל האברים" (1046). His reason: the lower a person's level, the more his prayer has to come outward (heart, then tongue, then lips, then mouth). Hannah could whisper because her want was already God's. So the loud, bodily prayer is for us, not for the great.

4. **Keep praying until the inner will surfaces (1080).** Start with whatever you want. Your real longing for God is already pushing those wants up and is dressed in them: "לכן כשרצון פנימי הזה מתחיל לעלות ולהעיק לו על לבו מתלבש הוא ברצונות אלו" (1080). Go on praying and watch for the change: "וזהו הסימן. אם אחר שמרבה להתפלל עולה בו הרצון הפנימי בכל פעם יותר עד שמרגיש כבר רצון לד'" (1080).

5. **See first, then bind (1068).** Before the hakafot: "וראשית צריכים לראות ולדעת שבנו בעצמנו אין עוד מלבדו והכל אלקות" (1068). Seeing is the gift; *da'at*, "binding," is the work of making the seeing hold.

6. **Picture yourself lost in the desert, then cry out (1143).** This is the one full imaginative exercise in the range. "יצייר האדם בעצמו כאלו תועה הוא במדבר" (1143): among wild beasts and robbers, every step taking you further in, your father near enough to save you, and you do not know the way to him. Then: "כמה יצעק האומלל התועה לאביו אבי אבי הורני דרכך" (1143). Before this comes a reckoning. Do not only ask whether you walk in God's way, "אלא אף יחשוב ויתבונן אם יודע הוא מהו דרך ד'" (1144).

7. **Think one truth over and over until it sets like a peg (1186).** "ואיש הישראל צריך לקבוע מחשבה כגון זו במוחו ולבו, ויחשוב כפעם בפעם" (1186). The content: "הנה ד' ית' אין סוף ואין תכלית לעילא מכל שמהן" (1186) … yet He "אוהב אותי כמו שאה"כ אהבתי אתכם אמר ה'" (1186), and so "וכמה צריך אני להתקשר בתורה וכמה צריך אני לקבוע את התורה בלבי ואת לבי בתורה כיתד שלא תמוט" (1186). This is the closest the range comes to hisbonenus as Chabad means it: one truth, repeated, until it reaches the heart. But notice that its object is *God's love for me*, not the order of the worlds.

8. **Decide who you are, and keep walking in that thought (1196).** "הוא צריך להחליט בדעתו אני יהודי ועבד ד'" (1196). After that the only choice left is *how* to come close, not *whether*: "ובמחשבות כאלו הולך תמיד" (1196).

9. **The morning acceptance before Shema (1255).** Every day, and above all at the morning Shema, say inwardly: "אני בא עתה לקבל עלי עול מ"ש, רבש"ע מכניע ומבטל אני את כל עצמי גופי ונשמתי אליך" (1255), and only then "Hear O Israel."

10. **Wake compassion for your own soul (1172).** Alongside the plain urge to return: "שכ"א צריך לעורר רחמנות על נפשו איך נפלה מאיגרא רמה ממקור מחצבתה לבירא עמיקתא" (1172). Compassion for yourself wakes the compassion of the holy part inside you (1171).

11. **Look inward at thoughts and wants (1160).** "לכן צריך האדם להסתכל ולהשגיח על נפשו מחשבותיו ורצונותיו פנימה" (1160). Stock-taking every hour: "וכמה צריכים לסייר א"ע בכל עת ובכל שעה" (1286).

12. **Strip a feeling from its object (1104).** When a love or fear arises for some thing, "יפשוט א"ע מהדבר, ותשאר אהבה או יראה סתם אשר יוכל להלבישה לד'" (1104). The fear of being seen by a person is really fear of God, dressed in a garment (1293); take it off and turn it toward Him (1294).

13. **Say holy words and cleave to the letters (1213).** The deepest way into the unity of Torah is speech: "ע"י שאומרים דברי קדושה, כי נודע שמקור אור האותיות גדול ממקור אור השכל" (1213), "וכשמתדבקים עם האותיות בקדושה, מתחברים אל פנימיות אור האחדות לעילא מן הפירוד" (1213). Hence saying *leshem yichud* before a mitzvah.

14. **Picture and spread the thought that God alone exists (1308).** The closing instruction of the range, and the most meditative. Knowing is not enough: "רק שיתמיד במחשבתו זו להגות בה לדמותה ולפשטה במוחו ולבו, שהוא ית' הנמצא היחידי" (1308). Three verbs: *meditate* on it, *picture* it, *spread* it through mind and heart. He calls this "וזו היא התחלה לראיה עליונה ג"כ" (1308), the beginning of the seeing that the great tzaddikim had "בעיני הבשר" (1308).

15. **Guard speech all day (1308).** "וצריך לקדש את פיו בכל דבוריו של כל היום" (1308), so that prayer and Torah words can carry holiness.

16. **Tie the rope in the morning (1283).** In a state of expansion, sing, contemplate God's greatness, tell others. That is the rope that holds you in the "night" of constriction: "ותלוי עד כמה התקשר בבוקר בלהגיד וכמה חוזק החבל ובכמה קשרים אתקשר" (1283).

17. **Small practical acts.** When a bad desire appears, "צריך להתחזק ולעשות מצוה או ללמוד ואז מהבחירה לא טובה תעשה טובה" (1268). When the evil inclination comes, hold out, "ואם יתאפק האיש ולא ישמע לו יפסק היצה"ר מעצמו" (1225). Before going to sleep, feel the obligation you are resting for: "למשל כשהולך לישון לא ילך לישון רק ללמוד או לעשות איזה מצוה" (1210). Make yourself master of your mind and order it to go deep: "כן צריכים להיות אדון ומושל על השכל לכפותו ולגער בו לך העמק והבין" (1018). Remember a teaching by the *impression* it left on you, not by an outside mnemonic: "רק מן הרושם שנעשה ונשאר בו" (1201).

## 3. The inner experience, in his own images

**The cellar and the far light (985).** A person sunk in the world thinks he knows how to run his life. Glimpse holiness, even from far off, and you discover your lack: "כי גם מי שעומד במרתף חשוך ולא ידע כל ימיו שחסר לו אור" (985). The glimpse shakes the soul: "ורק מרחוק ראה נפשו מזדעזעת" (985). And a paradox: "לכן כל מה שמרגיש האיש יותר את הקדושה עלאה שבקרבו מרגיש יותר שנעדר דעת" (985). The more holiness you feel inside, the less you trust your own judgment.

**Pieces of the self given away in praise (993-994).** In praise the person feels "ומרגיש בעצמו תשוקה לעקור חתיכה מן לבו ונפשו לה'" (993), as if saying "כאילו הי' אומר רבש"ע את כל עצמותי אני נותן בשירה וזמרה לה'" (993). That is why a Jew can go from weeping at Ne'ilah to dancing a moment later ("ברגע האחרונה של יוה"כ. בנעילה, צועק ובוכה", 994): his life force is not his own, and it follows what was made above.

**Rising above division (997-998).** In the mind's ascent everything becomes one: "בשעה שעולה למחשבה למעלה מן ההתחלקות" (997), just as "וכשעולים למקור הנפש כולן אחת הן" (997) (sight and hearing are one at the soul's source). A person whose fear includes love reaches "אז בא אל המקור שלמעלה מן ההתחלקות. אל ניצוץ העצם" (998), and "ויופי של מעלה נתגלה לו בחי' תפארת ומתפעל ומתלהב וכל המדות כלולות בו" (998). Beauty, excitement, and all the traits at once.

**The gathered soul (1023-1027).** The soul is scattered in things and places. What you want from the world for God's sake is the soul's own parts calling to it ("כי יש רצון ויש תאוה", 1023). Exile is simply not yet being found: "כי כל הגלות הוא אם אינו נמצא" (1025).

**The prince asking for shoes (1061-1062).** Our prayers for bodily things are the voice of a deeper grief: "כן בפנימיות נפשנו אנו מתגעגעים אל ד'" (1062). We cannot put it into words ourselves, so we ask God to hear it: "לכן מתפללים לד' בינה הגיגי שיבין ד' בתפלתנו את אשר אין מבינים גם אנו" (1062), "ויראה בתוך תוכיותינו את תפלת נפשנו שתשוקתה לד'" (1062).

**Inner will surfacing, outer wants melting (1080-1084).** Prayer is like a dream: "ניצוץ הנבואה מתלבש בהרהורי לבו" (1080). The real longing rises and puts on the wants that fill the mind. In heart-prayer "מפני שהרצוניות והתשוקות החיצוניות נמסות" (1082). At the end of the Amidah even your requests for yourself have become God's will: "ואמרי פי לעצמי ג"כ לפניך ד' הוא" (1084).

**The body as oil and wick (1068).** When a person serves with fire, "אז שאר גופו אף אכילתו שתיתו וכו' נעשה כשמן ופתילה" (1068). Dark by themselves, they catch and turn to light.

**Arousal that climbs, and the hunger that won't be filled (1098).** Excitement starts in the body, "אף אם מתחילה רק התעוררות גופנית היא" (1098), and climbs: "גופו מעורר את נפשו. נפשו את נשמתו" (1098). How far it reached shows in whether a mitzvah satisfies. If only the body or lower soul was stirred, a mitzvah settles it. If it reached the highest levels, "ואם כבר הגיעה ההתעוררות עד החי' יחידה שלו שלמעלה מגופו. אין עשיות גופו מוציאים אותה. ואינו שבע ותמיד משתוקק" (1098).

**Seeing through a crack in the door (1105).** Inner sight is the source of faith: "ראיה של הנשמה שממנה באה אמונה פשוטה" (1105). Doubts come from darkness, not from argument: "כי מי שראה את המלך ומשרתיו אף שאינו רואה כולו רק ע"י חרך וסדק הדלת והכותל לא יסתפק עוד" (1105).

**The grasp of not-grasping (1106).** At the top is a longing to reach God with no garment at all. Against that longing even the purest traits feel foreign: "לעומת תשוקתו גם הלבושים בעצמם הגבלת המדות כמו זר נחשב לו" (1106). The asking itself is the attainment: "לכן הוא השגה של אי השגה, השגה של תשוקה לראות" (1106).

**Holy trembling at the table; hunger as a summons (1113-1114).** At a real tish "מרגישים אז מין חרדת קודש" (1113). For a person trained in this, "ורעבון זה שהוא מרגיש מין תביעה רוחנית היא להיחוד" (1114).

**The dammed river (1115).** "כמו נהר שוטף שעוצרים אותו בקרש" (1115): "כן הנשמה משתוקקת תמיד לעבוד ולהתקדש והגוף עוצר בעדה" (1115). The force builds up quietly, and at the festival it breaks the board. Then even the body dances: "גם גופו רוצה להתעלות ולהתבטל כלו לד' ועולה גם בפועל למעלה ושב" (1115).

**More, and deeper (1120-1125).** Most of the year, "כי בכל השנה ע"פ רוב כל הרגשות אחת הן" (1125): joy or broken-heartedness, just more or less of it. Holiness can come as "האיש מרגיש בקרבו איזה רוח טהרה ומעט התפעלות של קדושה" (1120), and grow in quantity. Real growth is a change in kind: "שהרגשות שמרגיש עתה אינן מאותו המין שהרגיש אשתקד" (1120). Learning done out of longing touches something direct: "ונפשו שמשתוקקת ורצה נוגעת בזה בעצם הארת המלך" (1121).

**The sling and the stray thought (1160).** For someone who listens inside, "ואת נדנודי נפשו מרגיש הוא בכף הקלע הזה" (1160). The upper worlds push him out and this world pushes him back, "למה הוא פוסח אז מקודש אל החול ומחול אל הקודש" (1160). This is his account of the back-and-forth of stray thoughts in prayer.

**The pure soul in the mud (1225-1226).** The worst suffering is inner. "כאשר מסתכל בעצם נפשו כי קדושה וטהורה היא" (1225), and then sees how much baseness has left marks on him. He cries: "רבש"ע למה שיקעת אותי ברפש וטיט כזה" (1225), and "ונפשו אליו ית' תערוג, ותצעק איפה אני" (1226). The Rebbe's reading: "אז ידע שזה יסורים של אהבה" (1226).

**Joy as surplus life (1232-1233, 1239).** Real joy comes when there is more life than the body needs, as in a child. It lightens the body until one dances. Joy that comes from a light beyond the vessels: "נתפעל ממנה ומתלהב בשמחה שהיא האורה שיתירה מגופו וצרכיו" (1239). Such joy spills over to others: "ונפשו היתירה על גופו מתחברת עם נפשות חבריו ומשמחת גם אותן" (1233).

**The "sealed" heart that is full (1240).** Sometimes a person feels nothing: "שלבו נשבר בקרבו ואינו מרגיש אור וקדושה ונדמה לו שלבו אטום. ובאמת גם עתה מלא קדושה הוא" (1240). It is hidden joy, holiness already taken in, not extra. Tzaddikim who belittled themselves were in this state.

**Always drawn onward (1267-1268).** A light from beyond always shines on a person, "שתמיד באיזה מצב שהוא אפילו במצב הכי גדול תמיד אור שמחוצה לו מאיר בו" (1267), so "לכן כל תשוקות ישראל היא לעלות" (1267). That restlessness is also why one can go astray. Once a person begins to work he feels *where* he is going: "וכשמתחיל לעבוד ומרגיש בקרבו גם איפה. גם לאן לבו נמשך אינו תועה" (1268).

**The iron wall (1308).** The opposite state: "מי שחושב שהעולם עולם לעצמו והוא איש לעצמו" (1308). For him, when he studies, "מחיצה של ברזל עומדת בינו לבין אלקיו" (1308).

## 4. The self and God

This range supports the owner's thesis strongly, in several independent passages. It also sharpens it: for him, finding yourself is **the first step and the condition**, not the end, and the self you find is **God's thought about you**.

- **Your self is God's purpose for you (1003-1004).** "True kindness" is reaching the purpose for which God created you, and reaching it *is* reaching your soul: "אם האדם משיג זה התכלית אז השיג את נשמתו והשיג את החסד ואם לאו לא השיג את נשמתו" (1003). Without it you do not exist: "ואם לא מצא תכליתו אין הוא נמצא" (1003). And what that self is: "וחלק המחשבה זו זהו עצם האדם ומציאותו" (1003), the portion of divine thought you were made to complete. Everyone senses it: "והנה לכל אחד יש תכלית פרטי אשר כל אחד מרגיש זאת בנפשו מה תכליתו ולמה בא לעולם" (1004). This is almost exactly the owner's thesis: the true self is a piece of God's thought, so finding it is finding Him.

- **First find yourself, then the spirit (1025-1027).** "לכן כל איש יהודי צריך להרגיש בעצמו ולבחון את עצמו תמיד אם הוא נמצא כבר" (1025). The pattern is Esau, who "לא ימצא בעצמו את עצמו ונפשו" (1026). The order is explicit: "וראשית עבודה היא למצוא את עצמנו ונפשנו הנפזרה בכל העולם" (1027), then "כי אם מצאנו נפשנו נוכל לזכות לרוח" (1027), which leads to holy inspiration. Exile itself is not-being-found (1025).

- **What you add of yourself is the core of your soul (1284-1287).** God binds Himself to us through the written Torah; we bind ourselves to Him through what we add of our own. Every good thing a person takes on is his personal oral Torah, "והיא עיקר נשמתו וחלק התקשרות שבא בגללה לעוה"ז" (1284). "כי נשמת ישראל נכספה לד' ואינה מוצאת מרגוע בלתי שתוסיף מה" (1285). Without that, holiness only rests on you from above, like on Mount Sinai, which stayed dust: "אז האנכיות שלי אין" (1286), "אם אין איש ולא עצמות רק השראה ממרום כבהר סיני" (1287). So the self is not dissolved in God here. A real "I" is needed for there to be a bond at all.

- **"Make yourself" (1198).** Mitzvot done as tasks only keep God on a person "for an hour." The person who knows "הה העיקר אצלו לעשות את עצמו" (1198) is never idle: "ועם עצמו צריך הוא לעשות תמיד ואין לו רגע שיהי' לו מנוחה מעבודת עצמו" (1198). The test of goodness is the self, not the act: "ומתי קוראים לאיש איש טוב" … "אם באמת כל עצמותו נשתנה לטוב" (1160).

- **The "I" given within (1165-1173).** The deepest part of Torah is God's own "I" (*Anochi*) placed in the person as an inheritance. We cannot learn it; we can only learn to feel what we have been given: "וכל עבודתנו היא שעכ"פ בהלימוד ועבודה נדע מעט את הקדושה אשר בקרבנו" (1173), and we pray for mercy so "נבין ונדע ונלמוד יותר לדעת מה בקרבנו" (1170). Knowing oneself here means knowing the divine that was put inside. The prophets' disciples learned "למדם גם איך להוציא את ניצוץ הנביאות שבהם ולגלותו" (1166).

- **Self-renewal as mastery (1016-1018).** Someone who fixes a trait "אז כבר חידש בזה חידוש והוא הראשון לה" (1016), and "מי שחידש בקרבו איזה דבר והוא הראשון והמחדש בקרבו" (1017) becomes master of himself. Only then can light from above shine in him (1018).

- **The inner will is God's will (1079-1083).** At the bottom of what a Jew wants is God, and at the bottom of what God wants is Israel's good. In prayer the two meet: the inner will "is revealed and it is for God." This is the thesis in the language of desire: dig beneath your wants and you find Him.

- **The countervailing note.** He does not flatten the tension. The same range praises Abraham, who "had no feeling of his own self" (1030), the small child "whose soul is wholly nullified to his father" (987), and the Shema acceptance of "my whole self, body and soul" (1255). The self is found in order to be given. And finding it does not seem to be a private achievement: the tzaddikim "enter" the one who serves (1149-1150), and part of a person's soul can be held by another tzaddik until he earns it back (1130).

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

- **Imagining humility is not humility (985).** "Smallness" is a trait, and traits cannot be forced like acts: "הקטנות היא ככל מדה שאין האיש יכול להגיע אליה, בלתי שישנה א"ע ויהיה באמת כן" (985). Merely picturing oneself as nothing, "בזה בלבד שידמה האדם לעצמו שהוא אין ואפס" (985), does not help unless one really feels one cannot lead oneself. This is an explicit limit on imagination.

- **Arousal from below carries the body's traces (1098).** Starting from bodily excitement is allowed and it does work. But "מי שהתלהבותו באה ממטה למעלה. אז גם בעבודה אף כשהוא למעלה. מ"מ רשימות גופו נראות בה למעלה" (1098), whereas arousal from above "מין התגלות עליונה היא וטהורה היא" (1098). The test is satisfaction: an arousal that a mitzvah quiets went no higher than the body; one that leaves you always longing reached the soul's root.

- **The whiskey rule: use the flame, don't use its light for yourself (1178-1179).** He defends chassidim drinking whiskey, "למשל החסידים שותים י"ש" (1178), because "הרפוי' ובלא התעוררות לגמרי אין דבר טוב יוצא ממנה" (1178), and "מ"מ מן להב והתרגשות גופנית יכולים לבא להתרגשות דקדושה" (1179). But "כי אם חושב שהלהב הגופני לבד די לו ורק לצרכו ח"ו לא טוב" (1179). And "ומי שנופל ח"ו בדכאות רוח זה לא טוב" (1178).

- **Revelry is imagination (1233).** "ההוללות והתאוות בכלל רק דמיון הן כנודע" (1233). The sign is what comes after: "לכן אחרי שמחה של הוללות תוגה" (1233), "כמו אחר חלום טוב בשעה שמקיץ מחלומו ודמיונו ורואה שאין כלום" (1233). Real joy leaves its taste: "לא די שאינו בעצבות. אלא גם טעם השמחה נשארת לו" (1233).

- **Borrowed feeling (1249, 1130).** "רואים לפעמים איש מישראל מתפלל טוב בהתפעלות דחילו ורחימו ולומד טוב מ"מ הוא שוכב בנמיכיותו כמו שהיה" (1249). The arousal was lent to him and he did not raise himself to own it. Similarly: "לכן מרגיש האיש לפעמים רצון ואור קדושה שבא בלבו וחושב שעתה יקיים המצות כמו צדיק גדול" (1130), and he does not. The remedy is plain habit: "האיש הישראלי הזה צריך להתגבר ע"ע ללמוד ולעשות ולהרגיל א"ע בכל פעם יותר לעשותה טוב" (1130).

- **Kavanot are not a shortcut (1112).** Aiming mystical intentions over eating does not make it holy, any more than intending to be in Jerusalem puts you there: "כן בכיוון הכונות לבד לא יתעלה ולא יהיו עניני גופו עבודה" (1112). Only years of building the body in holiness do.

- **Don't wait to feel before you do (1169).** The given light is felt only after learning: "ומי שרוצה להרגישה מקודם לא ילמוד ולא יעשה רק מקודם ללמוד ומעט מעט ירגישה" (1169).

- **Feeling a mitzvah versus the mitzvah feeling empty (1260).** An added stringency born of real stirring, "לכן אם מרגיש איש הישראל בקרבו התעוררות קדושה ומגודל התעוררותו יראתו מהירותו מוסיף משמרת למשמרת" (1260), is a living mitzvah. Eve's addition, made from the serpent's persuasion and not from her own feeling, was an empty one.

- **Fear that paralyzes (1222-1224).** Fear alone can say "האם ראוי אני לעבוד ולגשת אליו ית', והאם אין אני מנאץ במצוותי" (1222) and stop doing mitzvot. That is fear from "the terror of the nights." Love answers: "הן אמת שאיני ראוי. אבל הלא שם יש מקום קדושה ושם נמצא מלכי וקדושי ואיך אתאפק שלא אתקרב אליו" (1223), "באהבתה תשגה תמיד" (1223). In suffering, the fearful man sinks; the lover says "ווער שמייסט מיך מיין טאטע" (1224).

- **Self-deception through excuses (1184-1187).** People who serve out of fear of punishment get skilled at excuses, and the one who does nothing always feels righteous. The real servant stands in awe before the King: "לבו רחוק מכל תרוץ ומכל ערמה לרמות א"ע ואת זולתו" (1187), "מתפחד ומשתוקק הוא לעבדו" (1187).

- **A love stuck to its object (1104-1105).** If love of wine or money has become "one essence" with its object, you are a servant, not a son. You have shut the windows "כמו מי שסותם את חלונותיו בדברים גושים וגסים שלא תעבור השמש" (1105), and doubts follow.

- **Feeling nothing is not proof of emptiness (1240, 1294).** The opposite danger: concluding from a numb heart that nothing is there. "ונדמה לו שלבו אטום. ובאמת גם עתה מלא קדושה הוא" (1240). But he also allows a real deafness, "ויכול להיות שהאיש לבו אטום ואינו שומע בקרבו את הקול מהר סיני" (1294). He does not fully reconcile the two.

## 6. Compared with Chabad hisbonenus

The Chabad method, as summarized in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`, is: take one divine truth (the order of worlds, God's unity, His greatness), unfold it in *Binah* with detail and analogy, hold it with *Da'at* and duration until it reaches the heart, and bring the result down into action. Arousal is valued but suspected; self-awareness in the state is the subtlest obstacle.

- **Thinking one truth over and over until it sets.** "ואיש הישראל צריך לקבוע מחשבה כגון זו במוחו ולבו, ויחשוב כפעם בפעם" (1186) is repetition and duration in plain terms.
- **Da'at as binding.** His reading of "You have been shown to know": the vision is given, and *da'at*, which he calls *hitkashrut* (binding), is what makes it hold (1068). That is the Chabad role of Da'at almost word for word.
- **Drawing down into action.** Chanukah 1 says the whole work is to bring holy will down into doing (1140, 1148); Pesach insists the vision must reach the body. Chabad's fifth stage.
- **Suspicion of self-referential arousal.** Arousal from below keeps "the marks of the body" (1098); feeling can be borrowed and leave the person unchanged (1249). This matches the Mitteler Rebbe's worry that arousal without self-refinement coarsens.
- **The test is change in the person.** "ומתי קוראים לאיש איש טוב" … "אם באמת כל עצמותו נשתנה לטוב" (1160) is close to the Rashab's test (has your dominant trait changed?).

**Where he differs (kept, not smoothed)**

- **Picturing.** Chabad's object is conceptual and its tool is *Binah*. He tells the reader to *imagine*: lost in a desert with robbers, father near (1143); and to hold "הוא ית' הנמצא היחידי" by picturing it ("לדמותה", 1308). The desert image is not a truth about God at all but a dramatized picture of one's own situation, aimed at making the heart cry out. Yet he also limits imagination: picturing yourself as nothing does not produce humility (985).
- **The object is often the self, not the cosmos.** Chabad lists "the practitioner's own soul" as one object among five. Here it is central: check whether you are found (1025); look inward at your thoughts and wants (1160); see your pure soul and its mud (1225); feel compassion for your fallen soul (1172). His favourite way into God runs through the self and its wants.
- **Desire as the doorway.** Chabad works on the mind so that it rules the heart. He starts from the heart's *wants*, even bodily ones, and says they are already prayers (1043-1048) with the true will dressed inside them (1080). You pray from what you want until the inner will comes up. This is a different entry point, not just a different emphasis.
- **Arousal from the body is legitimate.** He defends starting from bodily excitement, even from whiskey (1178-1179), and says flatness is worse ("הרפוי' ובלא התעוררות לגמרי אין דבר טוב יוצא ממנה", 1178). The Chabad sources are more guarded here (and the Chabad ontology records this as disputed within Chabad itself).
- **Speech and the letters over understanding.** "שמקור אור האותיות גדול ממקור אור השכל" (1213): cleaving to spoken holy letters joins one to unity "above division," which intellect cannot. Pray aloud with the whole body (1046). Chabad's centre of gravity is Binah; his, in these passages, is the mouth.
- **Joy and dancing as states in their own right.** Joy as surplus life, dancing as the body's own *ratzo vashov* (1115, 1232). Chabad treats *ratzo vashov* as a contemplative state; he locates it in the dancing body at Simchat Torah.
- **Descending light versus ascending effort.** He values the light that comes "from above" as purer (1098-1099), and describes sudden revelation beyond one's vessels, which one then works to absorb (1241). Chabad's process is mostly from below upward (contemplation produces the feeling). He keeps both, but the gift from above sets the direction.
- **The tzaddik inside you.** The tzaddikim enter the one who serves and pray with him (1149-1150). This has no real counterpart in the hisbonenus process.
- **Hidden joy in the numb heart.** Chabad's "dullness of heart" gets the prescription of more duration. He reads the same numbness, in a person who has worked, as hidden fullness (1240).
- **Absent here:** quieting (*hashkatah*) and melody do not appear in this range. Do not import them into this range's evidence.

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before anything higher, ask yourself honestly whether you are *found* yet, or scattered across the things you want. Finding yourself is the first work, and it is the doorway to more." (1025, 1027)
2. "Your true self is not what you happen to want. It is the part of God's thought you were made to complete, and you can feel something of it already, in what you know you came here for." (1003-1004)
3. "Start from what you actually want right now, even if it's small or bodily. Say it to God out loud as a prayer, and keep going until you notice a deeper wanting coming up under it." (1044, 1046, 1080)
4. "Close your eyes and picture yourself lost in a wilderness, your father close but you can't find the way. Let yourself call out to Him: *teach me Your way*." (1143)
5. "Take one sentence: God, who has no end, loves me. Think it again and again through the day, picture it, and let it spread from your head into your chest." (1186, 1308)
6. "If you feel nothing today, that does not mean nothing is there. A heart that seems sealed may be full of what it has already taken in." (1240)
7. "If your excitement came fast and a single good act satisfied it, it probably stayed on the surface. If you are left still longing, it went deep. Either way, don't use it for yourself; turn it toward Him." (1098, 1179)
8. "When you see how pure your soul really is and how much mud has stuck to it, and you cry *where am I?*, that pain is a love-pain, and it is already the soul reaching for God." (1225-1226)
