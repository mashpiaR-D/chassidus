<a id="c16"></a> ## C16 · Constant worry about money, health and family

*14 findings from Tanya, Likkutei Torah (part 2 of 3), Likkutei Torah (part 3 of 3), Sha'arei Teshuvah, Toras Chaim (part 1 of 4), Toras Chaim (part 3 of 4), Toras Chaim (part 4 of 4), Sefer HaSichos (part 1 of 2), Sefer HaSichos (part 2 of 2), Hayom Yom, Likkutei Sichos (part 1 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 3 of 3)*

> Worry about livelihood, the children, my health, wakes up with me and follows me into prayer. I scheme and plan as if everything depends on me, and I run from adviser to adviser for answers.

**What is really going on.** The library does not treat worry as prudence. It treats it as a burden carried in the wrong place. The work of earning belongs to the hands, and the worry has moved into the inner mind and heart. Tanya calls worry a heaviness that loses the struggle, and answers it by receiving events as hidden good. Likkutei Torah praises earning, but calls the worry 'strange fire' from the animal soul, and says that straining the mind too hard pushes out trust and dulls the mind. The Mitteler Rebbe (Toras Chaim, Sha'arei Teshuvah) calls the flood of livelihood pressure 'many waters' that make the means into the essence, but says it cannot reach the soul's hidden love. The Rebbe Rayatz, Hayom Yom and Likkutei Sichos turn this into a plain division of labor. Do your part fully and God does His. Trust is itself a kind of work. Worry harms body and mind. When there is illness, leave the medical reasoning to the doctors.

**The false conclusions, and what the works set against them**

**Where the works agree.** Every work separates the task from the worry. Earning is required and even praised, and doctors must be followed. None tells anyone to stop working or skip treatment. All place the remedy in taking the inner mind and heart off the outcome and returning them to God, to the mission, or to prayer. All agree worry costs something: heaviness, a dulled mind, weaker health, lost joy.

**Where they differ.** The Alter Rebbe (Tanya) accepts: what happens is hidden good. Likkutei Torah moves the worry away or burns it in prayer. The Mitteler Rebbe goes through the pressure, finding purification and a longing born of distance, and reads despair in toil as a seed rotting. The Rayatz and the Rebbe are gentler and more practical: joy first, trust as active work that brings the good ('think good'), not just acceptance, medical wisdom, and the turning wheel.

**Order and timing.** First the practical part is done in full (hands at work). When worry rises, turn the mind away or say it to someone who shares it. Daily, give the worry to morning prayer, if steady. At settled times, contemplate the hidden good, if able. With illness, the doctor's part runs alongside. In a downturn, the wheel.

- No program replaces practical action in a real financial or medical crisis, or professional help. The texts themselves insist on work, doctors and second opinions. - For clinical anxiety or OCD, 'don't worry' and 'put it out of mind' can become one more thing to fail at, or turn into suppression. Lead with speaking it to someone who shares it. - 'This too is for good' and 'one who complains is like a denier' must never be handed to someone grieving or in fresh crisis. - The counsel in Iggeres HaKodesh 22 to 'examine one's deeds and find sins that need cleansing' must never be used to link illness, loss or abuse to a person's sins. Its warning against running to advisers is not a warning against doctors, therapists or financial help. - The bitterness steps (burning worry in prayer, longing from distance) are not for the depressed. - 'Trust raises health' and 'think good and it will be good' can breed self-blame when an outcome is bad ('I didn't trust enough'). The guide must not imply that. - Reading despair in toil as a 'seed rotting before it sprouts' must not romanticise real despair.

<a id="c17"></a> ## C17 · Dread: my mind keeps picturing the worst

*5 findings from Toras Chaim (part 2 of 4), Sefer HaSichos (part 1 of 2), Likkutei Sichos (part 1 of 3), Likkutei Sichos (part 2 of 3), Sheeris Yehudah*

> I'm terrified, and my mind paints the worst for me and my family, a harsh decree, a hostile world. Some of the fear attaches to nothing real. Others tell me there was nothing to fear.

**What is really going on.** The evidence is scattered but consistent. The Rebbe Rayatz's prison diary shows the mechanism: pictures of his mother and wife, not the facts, were bringing his spirit down, driven by the feeling that everything depended on him. He stopped the pictures on purpose, handed the outcome to God after doing his part, avoided frightening details he didn't need, and filled his mind with verses. A letter in Likkutei Sichos gives a test: judge a dark thought by its fruit, and if it weakens good action and brings despair, it is groundless. Toras Chaim says some fear 'has no substance' and comes from emptiness of soul; unbroken fear confuses the mind, and tears carry the waste out. Sheeris Yehudah, a legal responsum, holds that fear is measured by the heart at that place and hour, and that even imagined danger was real fear. Likkutei Sichos also says longing for one's true place lifts a person above fear of a hostile world.

**Where the works agree.** Fear's pictures are not facts. A frightening thought need not be obeyed; test it by its effect. Do your part and hand the outcome over. Don't feed the imagination with needless detail; fill the mind with something steadier. The frightened person should not be shamed.

**Where they differ.** The Rayatz stops thoughts by will. Toras Chaim treats fear as waste to be released, through tears. Toras Chaim calls some fear 'without substance', while Sheeris Yehudah insists imagined fear was real fear. These can be reconciled: the threat may be unreal, but the experience is real. Likkutei Sichos cures fear through longing and pain, not calm.

**Order and timing.** In acute threat: notice the picture, stop it, hand over the outcome, do your part, settle, fill the mind. For lingering foreboding: test by fruit and replace with good. When it overflows, let tears come. Throughout, the helper validates the fear. The deeper remedy, filling the soul's emptiness, is named but not given a method.

- In real danger (violence, abuse, medical emergency), safety and practical help come first; 'trust God' must not replace action or leaving. - Thought-stopping in chronic anxiety, OCD or trauma can become avoidance and rebound; these need clinical care. - 'Fear without substance' must never be used to dismiss a real threat, especially one a person reports about someone else. - Don't call a trauma survivor's fear foolish; the sources themselves forbid it. - Curing fear through longing and pain at exile may deepen distress in someone depressed.
