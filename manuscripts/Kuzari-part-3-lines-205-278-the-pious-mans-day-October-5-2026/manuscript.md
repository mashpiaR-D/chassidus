## The shape of the range

The king has heard enough theory. He asks: what does the pious man among you do today? Part 3 is the answer, and it is the most practical part of the book: a whole day, a whole week and a whole year of a person who serves G-d without leaving the world. Then it turns to a long defense of the Oral Torah against the Karaites, and ends with the chain of the sages.

1. **Not a hermit** (205). Withdrawal was right for prophets when the Presence was in the Land. Today it brings sickness and regret.
2. **The pious man as a ruler** (207-209). He rules his powers like a just king rules a city, then gathers them all to rise to the divine level. Prayer, Shabbat, the months, the festivals, Yom Kippur.
3. **The eyes that see, and the body that is His** (215). Continuous creation felt in every movement. Blessings. Justice of G-d. "This too is for the good."
4. **Blessings double the pleasure** (217-221). The meaning of each blessing of the morning prayer, the Shema, the Amidah.
5. **The community** (223-225). Why pray with ten. The courtier who never asks for an escort.
6. **Oral Torah and the Karaites** (227-277). Nearness comes only by the exact deed He gave, so we need the tradition that tells us the exact deed. The chain from Sinai. The four who entered the orchard.

## The unity of G-d

**Creation is not craft: He holds every limb every moment.** This is the unity teaching of part 3, and it is given as something to feel in the body. The pious man thinks about his limbs: he wants to stand, and all his limbs have already raised him, though he does not know which muscle moved. A child speaks what it hears and does not know which nerve or cord makes the sound. "As if their Maker brings them into being and makes them serve him at every moment as he needs." Then:

> מפני שאין ענין הבריאה דומה לענין המלאכה, כי האמן כשהוא עושה רחים, על הדמיון, וילך לו, ויעשו הרחים מה שבעבורו נעשו, והבורא יתברך בורא האברים ונותן להם כחות וממשיך להם עם הרגעים (Kuzari 3:11; line 215)
> For creation is not like craft. A craftsman makes a mill, say, and goes away, and the mill does what it was made for. But the Creator creates the limbs, gives them powers, and draws them on moment by moment.

> ואלו היו מעלים על לב הסתלקות השגחתו והנהגתו רגע אחד היה נפסד העולם כלו (Kuzari 3:11; line 215)
> And if one could imagine His care and guidance withdrawn for one moment, the whole world would be undone.

*Ours.* This is almost word for word the argument of the Gate of Unity (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 2): the craftsman and the vessel, against the heretics who think the world stands like a finished thing. The Tanya uses it to reach "there is nothing but Him" (F01). The Kuzari uses it to reach "He is with me in every move" and stops there. It is the seed of F01, planted in the body rather than in the letters of speech.

**Therefore: as if the Presence is with him.** When the pious man thinks this in all his movements, every movement has "the Creator's share" in it:

> והוא לעולם כאלו השכינה עמו והמלאכים מתחברים עמו בכח (Kuzari 3:11; line 215)
> And he is always as if the Presence is with him and the angels join him in potential.

If his piety grows and he is in places fit for the Presence, they join him in fact, and he sees them "below the level of prophecy", as the best sages of the Second Temple saw forms and heard the heavenly voice. That is the level of the pious (חסידים); above it, prophets.

**The Torah is His will to show His kingship on earth.** In the blessing "with everlasting love", the pious man should think:

> ושהתורה מאצלו התחלת חפץ ממנו להראות מלכותו בארץ כהראותה בשמים, ולא גזרה החכמה שיברא מלאכים בארץ, אבל אדם מזרע ודם (Kuzari 3:17; line 221)
> And that the Torah from Him is the beginning of a will of His to show His kingship on earth as it is shown in heaven. Wisdom did not decree that He create angels on earth, but man, of seed and blood.

*Ours.* This is the closest the Kuzari comes to the Chassidic "home below" (F07): a will to have His kingship seen here, in flesh and blood, where natures clash. Chassidus will call that will "a desire" (תאוה) with no reason, and say it is the purpose of all worlds. The Kuzari calls it a beginning of will, and ties it to a people and a Torah. The step is the same.

**The Presence as rain.** Why pray with the many? "The divine matter is like rain that waters a land when the whole land is fit for it" (הענין האלהי כמטר, line 223). A few unfit people are included in the blessing; a few fit people can be held back with an unfit land. The one is a limb of the body: "the single person in the community is like one limb in the whole body" (היחיד בכלל הצבור כאבר האחד בכלל הגוף, line 223).

**The two pillars of the dwelling.** The Tabernacle is the model of every approach. Every part was made "as the Lord commanded Moses", no more and no less; and it was given "from every man whose heart moved him". When both were complete, the Presence came:

> האחד מהם שתהיה התורה מאת האלהים, והשני שתהיה מקבלת בלב נאמן מהקהל (Kuzari 3:23; line 227)
> One of them, that the Torah be from G-d; the second, that it be received with a faithful heart by the community.

## States of consciousness

**The ruler of his city.** The king asks about a pious man and the Chaver describes a ruler. The king objects, and the Chaver explains:

> החסיד הוא מי שהוא מושל, נשמע בחושיו וכחותיו הנפשיים והגופיים (Kuzari 3:5; line 209)
> The pious man is a ruler, obeyed by his senses and his powers of soul and body.

From inside: a quiet authority. Each power has had its due, food, sleep, the anger that fights for truth, and is content. Nothing is starved and nothing runs the house. Then he "calls his congregation, like an obeyed ruler who calls his army, to cling to the level above it, that is, the divine level which is above the level of mind" (line 209). It is close to S28 (mind over heart) and the Tanya's "the mind rules the heart by nature", but it is gentler: the powers are not beaten, they are fed and then called. A proposed state: **the obeyed ruler** (JSON in KUZARI.md).

**The heart of the day.** The prayer time becomes the center that everything else points toward:

> ותהיה העת ההיא לב זמנו ופריו, ויהיו שאר עתותיו כדרכים המגיעים אל העת ההיא (Kuzari 3:5; line 209)
> That hour will be the heart of his time and its fruit, and his other hours like roads that lead to that hour.

"The fruit of his day and night are those three times of prayer, and the fruit of the week is Shabbat, for it is set to cling to the divine matter, and his service is in joy, not in humbling" (line 209). This is a state of the whole life, not of a moment: **the day with a heart**. It is what Chassidus asks when it says the rest of the day should be lit by the morning prayer.

**Prayer as food: the blessing lasts, and fades.**

> והסדר הזה מהנפש כסדר המזון מהגוף, מתפלל לנפשו ונזון לגופו, ומתמדת עליו ברכת התפלה עד עת תפלה אחרת (Kuzari 3:5; line 209)
> This order is to the soul as the order of food to the body. He prays for his soul and eats for his body, and the blessing of the prayer stays with him until the next prayer.

And the farther the prayer, the more the soul "goes dark" (הולכת וקודרת) from the day's business, and more so if he must be among foolish people and hear ugly talk and songs that pull the soul. Then in prayer he cleans his soul of what came before and readies it for what comes. This is an honest picture: the light is real, it fades, and it is renewed on a rhythm. It fits C5 (running and returning) and S26 (it goes flat), and it tells the person that fading is expected and has a remedy on a timetable.

**The week, the month, the year as a healing rhythm.** Shabbat cleans what a week gathered: the body gets back what it lost in six days, "and the soul remembers what it lacked while the body was busy, and it is as if on that day she is healed of a sickness that came before, and readies herself for what will ward off the sickness later" (line 209). Then the new month, the three festivals, and Yom Kippur, the fast "close to becoming like the angels", when "all his bodily powers fast from natural things and busy themselves with Torah things, as if he had no animal nature" (line 209). S76 (Shabbos: the soul returned) is here in the Kuzari's words.

**Seen and seeing: awe and joy together.** The pious man neither acts nor thinks nor speaks until he believes that "with him are eyes that see and watch" (line 215). He walks "as one who fears and trembles and is ashamed of his deeds at times, as he rejoices and is glad" (line 215). This is S33 (before Whom) with a twist the guide should keep: being seen is also a joy. "His soul is precious to him in his time of good", and he remembers his good to his G-d even when he suffers in His service.

**Living sweetly.** One who has settled G-d's justice in his heart becomes like Nachum Ish Gamzu:

> כל אשר תמצאהו צרה אומר: גם זו לטובה, ויחיה חיים ערבים תמיד, ותקל עליו הצרות (Kuzari 3:11; line 215)
> Whenever trouble finds him he says: this too is for the good. He will live a sweet life always, and troubles will be light on him.

The word ערבים (sweet, pleasant) returns at the end of part 3 when the king says of R. Akiva's death: "such a man will live the sweet life, and die the sweet death" (line 270). A proposed state: **the sweet life** (חיים ערבים), trust grown into taste. It is S84 (trust) and S85 (seeing Him in what happens), with the Kuzari's stress that it is pleasant to live in.

**Pleasure felt twice.** Why bless? "Readiness for pleasure, feeling it, and thinking of its absence before, doubles the pleasure" (line 221). The drunkard who was given every delight while drunk would grieve when sober: he had it and did not feel it (line 220). One who never blesses has "an animal pleasure that does not understand" (line 221). So blessing is a state: **awake to the gift**.

> ההזדמנות להנאה והרגשתה, ושיחשב בהעדרה קדם לכן, כופל ההנאה (Kuzari 3:17; line 221)
> Readiness for pleasure, feeling it, and thinking of its absence before: this doubles the pleasure.

*Ours.* This is a real teaching about joy that the guide can use at once. It is not "be grateful" as a rule. It is a way of seeing: I am dust and could lack all of this; here it is. And it prepares for loss: one who has thanked for life "will find sickness and death light when they come", for he has already counted his life as profit, and says "the Lord gave and the Lord took" (line 221).

**The inner eye of the pious: forms and voices.** The best sages of the Second Temple saw forms and heard the bat kol (line 215). Some of their stories are visions "from the greatness of their thoughts and the purity of their minds", and some are forms that have real being outside (line 277). A careful statement that such things happen, and that not all are the same.

**The orchard: the one who looked at the minds and left the deeds.** Of the four who entered the Pardes, the third (Acher) "ruined the deeds because he looked at the minds". He said:

> אלה המעשים הם כלים ומשתמשים מגיעים אל המדרגה הזאת הרוחנית, ואני כבר הגעתי אליה ולא ארגיש על מעשה התורה (Kuzari 3:65; line 269)
> These deeds are tools and servants that bring one to this spiritual level. I have already reached it, and will not care about the deeds of the Torah.

"He was ruined and ruined others, went astray and led astray." R. Akiva "used both worlds without harm" (line 269). *Ours.* This is the Kuzari's sharpest guard for any path of inner experience, and the guide should hold it close: an experience never makes the deed unneeded. The one who says "I am past it" is the one who has fallen.

**Dying on "One".** R. Akiva at his death asked if it was time for the Shema, "and he drew out 'One' until his soul left" (line 269). It is S60 (the essential bond) as a death.

## Meditation and practice

**Against withdrawal today.** The Chaver's first word on the pious man is a warning:

> מי שהכניס עצמו להנזר בפרישות, כבר הכניס נפשו ביסורין וחלי נפשי וגשמי (Kuzari 3:1; line 205)
> One who has put himself into abstinence by withdrawal has put his soul into suffering and sickness of soul and body.

He is not joined to a divine light that keeps him company as the prophets were, and not a philosopher with his sciences to fill his days. His new private prayers "have sweetness only a few days while they are new", and when they grow stale "the soul is not moved by them". He regrets what he bound himself to, "and adds by his regret distance from the divine matter he labored to come near" (line 205). *Ours.* A guard for the guide: no one should be sent to long solitude or self-made vows as a path. Short solitude inside a full life (technique 20) is another thing.

**The pious man's prayer, step by step.** Line 209 is a full method, and it is the most exact meditation instruction in the Kuzari. We set it out in order, in the text's own words, so the guide can use it (technique proposed: **the ruler's gathering**, JSON in KUZARI.md):

1. *Give each power its due first.* Food, drink, sleep, movement, anger used for truth. Only when "he has done the needs of each of them" does he call them.
2. *Order the congregation like Moses around Sinai.* Command the will to receive and keep what comes to it and do it in its time.
3. *Do not heed the demons of thought and imagination until the mind approves.* He tells the will "not to turn to the demons of thought and fancy, not to accept them nor believe them, until it takes counsel with the mind".
4. *Clear the thinking.* "He straightens the tools of thought and empties it of all the worldly thoughts that came before" (ומפנה אותו מכל אשר קדם מהמחשבות העולמיות).
5. *Picture the divine scenes.* The imagination is told to bring up, with memory's help, the most glorious forms it holds, to picture the divine matter it seeks:

> ומצוה המדמה להמציא ההדור שבצורות הנמצאות אצלו בעזר הזכרון, לדמות אליו הענין האלהי המבקש, כמו מעמד הר סיני ומעמד אברהם ויצחק בהר המוריה, וכמו משכן משה וסדר העבודה וחול הכבוד בבית המקדש (Kuzari 3:5; line 209)
> He commands the imagination to bring forth, with the help of memory, the most splendid of the forms it holds, to picture to itself the divine matter it seeks: such as the standing at Mount Sinai, and Abraham and Isaac on Mount Moriah, and the Tabernacle of Moses and the order of the service and the glory resting in the Temple.

6. *Guard it.* He tells memory to keep this and not forget, and rebukes the thought that confuses the truth and the anger and desire that would pull the will. 7. *Then the body.* The limbs serve "with quickness and eagerness and joy": standing when it is time to stand, bowing when it is time to bow. "The eyes look as a servant looks at his master" (ומביטות העינים הבטת העבד אל אדוניו). Hands still, feet together, all limbs "as if frightened, afraid to do their leader's command", not feeling an ache or a loss. 8. *Tongue with thought.* "The tongue agrees with the thought and does not add to it, and he does not say his prayer by habit and nature like the starling and the parrot, but with every word a thought and an intention in it."

*Ours.* This is a meditation in the full sense, and it is centuries before Chassidus: quiet the stray thought, clear the mind, picture a real scene of revelation, hold it in memory, let the body take the posture, let each word carry its thought. The picturing is close to the Piaseczner's tziyur (technique 10), and it chooses its images from the great moments of the Torah. Chabad's contemplation builds the same thing from ideas rather than scenes. The guide can offer both, and the Kuzari gives the scene-picturing a source among the Rishonim.

**If the thoughts will not go.** On Yom Kippur the soul turns back from wrong thoughts and deeds. "If turning back from the thought is not possible, because the imagination is stronger, from songs and riddles it heard in youth, let it be cleaned of the deed, and confess the thoughts, and accept not to say them with its tongue, much less do them" (line 209). *Ours.* A mild and wise guard: do not fight what you cannot yet uproot; keep it out of speech and deed, and name it before G-d. It fits technique 12 and S34 (two voices).

**The fast of the pious.** Whenever he fasts, "he afflicts his sight and hearing and tongue" too, and does not busy them with anything but what brings him near; and the inner powers likewise, imagination and thought (line 209).

**Binding the heart by things.** Tefillin on "the place of thought and memory" in the head, with a strap reaching the hand "so he sees it through the hours"; the hand tefillin on the heart, "the spring of the powers"; tzitzit "so his senses do not busy him in the world" (line 215). The blessing after the washroom ("who formed man in wisdom") is a meditation on the wonder of the body. A hundred blessings a day, and more with smells and foods and sights: "every addition is an addition that brings near to G-d" (line 215).

**Love and awe with a measure.** Love and fear enter the soul with these things, "measured by a Torah measure": so that joy on Shabbat does not lead to lightness and laziness in prayer, and fear does not reach despair of forgiveness "and he stays worried all his days and transgresses the command to rejoice in what He gave him" (line 215). Then a line the guide should keep beside every joy teaching:

> כי השבח הולך אחרי השמחה (Kuzari 3:11; line 215)
> For praise follows joy.

**Zeal that does not become anger.** "Let zeal in rebuking his fellow and in words of wisdom not bring him to anger and grudge, so that his soul is troubled from being purified at the times of prayer" (line 215).

**Settling G-d's justice.** A contemplation for times of doubt. Think how every creature, small and great, has what it needs: strength and claws for the lion, flight for the hare, the web for the spider. "If the demon of thought comes to show him the wrong done to the hare" that is food for the lion, the mind should rebuke it: I have seen this Wise One's justice; this too is "for a wisdom I do not grasp" (line 215). *Ours.* A technique: **study His wisdom where you can see it, and lean on it where you cannot**. The Kuzari repeats it as a rule: "once the general rules are clear, do not set your heart on the details" (line 247).

**Consolation in exile, in steps.** When the length of exile troubles his thought, he consoles himself: first with justice, then with the paying off of sins, then with the reward stored up, then with "clinging to the divine matter in this world". And if his accuser says "can these bones live?", he thinks of the going out of Egypt, and finds it not hard that we should return "even if only one of us remained", as it is said "fear not, worm of Jacob" (line 215).

**Blessings, with the mind on each one.** In "who forms the lights" he pictures the order of the upper world and how, before their Maker, sun and spheres are "like the smallest of creeping things", for His wisdom in the ant and the bee is no less, and more wondrous for their smallness (line 221). So the lights do not grow great in his eyes and pull him to star worship. In the Shema he accepts the Torah; in "true and firm" he binds himself as by an oath and calls witnesses. In the Amidah: the fathers, His power over the world and the reviving of the dead, then His holiness above all that. Before "who returns His Presence to Zion":

> ויחשב בלבו שהשכינה נצבת נגדו וישתחוה לנכחה (Kuzari 3:19; line 223)
> Let him think in his heart that the Presence stands before him, and bow toward it.

*Ours.* Technique proposed: **the Presence before you at the bow**. It is technique 2 (the threshold: before Whom) at its peak moment in the prayer, given a picture and a posture.

**Pray for the many.** "The prayer that is answered is only for the many or among the many, or for one who stands in the place of many" (line 221). A private request may harm another; a prayer said by one person is rarely whole; with ten, "each completes what another lacks" (line 223). The courtier who comes to the king daily never asks for an escort for the road, and is surer of it than the townsmen who ask every day (line 225). *Ours.* Trust shown by not asking, because one is near. A quiet state for the guide (S84).

**Only the deed He measured.** The Chaver's whole case against the Karaites rests on this, and it is the same as parts 1-2:

> אין מתקרבים אל האלהים כי אם במצות האלהים עצמם, בעבור שהוא יודע שעורם ומשקלם וזמנם ומקומותם (Kuzari 3:23; line 227)
> One comes near to G-d only by G-d's own commands, for He knows their measure and weight and time and place.

"The deeds of the Torah are like the works of nature, all measured by the Creator" (line 257). You do not know why a hen must sit on an egg three weeks; if you had never heard of how a child is born, the act would look like madness until you saw the child. So with the offering: slaughter, blood, washing, fire; "if it were not a command of G-d you would mock these acts", until you see the heavenly fire, "or find in yourself another spirit you were not used to, or true dreams or great things", and know "they are the fruit of what you did before" (line 257). And one who has reached that level "has no going down from it", even in death.

**The Rabbanite's peace of heart.** The king ends the argument: the Rabbanite "is good of heart in his Torah" because it comes from tested sages, while the Karaite, however hard he tries, cannot have peace, "for he knows his effort is reasoning and guessing" and cannot be sure it is the deed G-d wants (line 254). *Ours.* A state: **at rest in what was given**. The one who receives is calm; the one who invents is always at war (line 241: the reasoners are like one who walks in a desert, armed, not knowing what will meet him; the receivers "dwell on their beds in an ancient fortified city").

## The big human questions

**Does serving G-d mean leaving the world?** No. The pious man "loves the world and long days, because they earn him the world to come" (line 205). Each good he adds raises him a level there.

**Suffering.** Settled by justice seen in the general rules, and by "this too is for the good". He may even rejoice in suffering: as one who pays a debt is glad it is lighter, as one who awaits reward, as one who teaches others patience (line 215).

**Pleasure.** Good, when felt and blessed. Pleasure without awareness is animal; pleasure with a blessing is human and doubled (lines 217-221).

**Death.** "Such a man will live the sweet life and die the sweet death" (line 270). R. Akiva died saying "One".

**The world to come in prayer.** Why do our prayers speak so little of it? Because one who prays to cling to the divine light in his life, to see it with his eyes, "has already prayed without doubt for what is greater than the world to come" (line 224).

**Joy and sadness.** Service "in joy, not in humbling" (line 209). Fear must not reach despair; joy must not reach lightness (line 215).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 228-266 and 271-277 argue for the Oral Torah: vowels and accents kept by tradition, the Karaites' failures with the calendar and the details of the laws (what is "work" on Shabbat, what is "his place"), the "eye for an eye" read as payment, impurity and the eruv, the chain from the Men of the Great Assembly to Rabbi and the Gemara. They were read in full. Their value for the guide is the frame already given: nearness comes by the exact deed, and the exact deed comes by a living chain. The chain passage also gives the four who entered the orchard (line 269), reported above.
