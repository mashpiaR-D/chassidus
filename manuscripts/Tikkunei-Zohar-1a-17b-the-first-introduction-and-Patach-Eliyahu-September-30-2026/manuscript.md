## What this range is

The Tikkunei Zohar is seventy readings of one word, בראשית, "In the beginning", given by Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai in a cave while Elijah and the souls of the heavenly academy come down to listen. The book opens twice. The first introduction (1a-16b) teaches on the verse "the wise will shine like the brightness of the sky" and on the mitzvah of sending away the mother bird. The second introduction (17a) is where Elijah opens his mouth with the prayer every Sefardi and Chassidic siddur prints before Minchah on Friday: **Patach Eliyahu**, "Elijah opened". This is the most quoted page of the Tikkunim in Chassidus.

Read as a whole, the range has one heartbeat. God is one and past all thought. His presence in the world, the Shechinah, is in exile, driven from her nest like a mother bird. And the human task is to make her a home: in the heart, in prayer, in Torah learned for its own sake, in the body's limbs.

## The unity of G-d

**Past every picture.** The Master of all has no shape at all, not even the shape of a letter or a vowel point:

> ומארי דכלא לית ביה ציור דאות ונקודה (TZ 6b:5, line 98)
> *The Master of all has in Him no form of letter or point.*

What the prophets saw were only pictures, like a king's likeness pressed in wax. Some names of God are like the king's seal; others are like the mark the seal leaves. People fear the wax mark as if it were the king. But

> אבל אדון על כלא לית ליה מכל אלין ציורין כלל (TZ 6b:8, line 101)
> *but the Lord over all has none of these forms at all.*

Whoever makes a picture of Him, even out of what the prophets saw, falls under "cursed is the one who makes a carved image" (line 103).

**One who needs no help.** Who stretched out the heavens? He alone:

> עלת על כלא לבדו, יחידא בלא תניינא למעזר ליה (TZ 7a:2, line 106)
> *The Cause over all, by Himself: One without a second to help Him.*

**Filling and surrounding, all in His one domain.** Two lines together give the Tikkunim's short creed on space and presence:

> איהו עלת על כלא לית אל"ה עליה, ולא תחותיה, ולא לארבע סטרין דעלמא, איהו ממלא כל עלמין (TZ 6b:3, line 96)
> *He is the Cause over all. There is no god above Him, nor under Him, nor to the four sides of the world. He fills all worlds.*

> וכלהו ברשו דיליה ברשות היחיד (TZ 6b:4, line 97)
> *And all of them are in His power, in the domain of the One.*

The phrase רשות היחיד is a pun on the Shabbat law of the "private domain": the whole universe is one single domain, and it is His. The verse before (line 97) adds that He "surrounds all sides" (אסחר לכל סטרין) and gives each thing its border. This is the source of the pair ממלא כל עלמין / סובב כל עלמין that the Tanya builds on (face F04).

**He and His life are one.** The line the Alter Rebbe quotes in the Tanya's introduction to Likkutei Amarim and again in Iggeret HaKodesh:

> דעשר ספירות דאצילות מלכא בהון, איהו וגרמיה חד בהון, איהו וחייוי חד בהון (TZ 3b:9, line 49)
> *In the ten sefirot of Atzilut the King is in them: He and His self are one in them, He and His life are one in them.*

In the world of Beriah this is not so; there the sefirot and their life are two. And yet the Cause of all shines in all the worlds down to the spheres of the sky,

> ולא אשתני בכל אתר (TZ 3b:10, line 50)
> *and He does not change in any place.*

**Light that takes no color.** The yud of the Name takes no color; it shines from inside the colors, the way the soul is in the body:

> לא ישתני בשום גוון כלל, אלא נהיר מלגאו דגוונין, דשם יהו"ה כנשמתא בגופא דאיניש, או כשרגא בהיכלא (TZ 10a:5, line 168)
> *He does not change into any color at all, but shines from within the colors: the Name is like the soul in a person's body, or a lamp in a palace.*

**Witness on six sides.** The vav, the letter of six, turns every way and in every direction bears witness:

> והכי לכל סטרא סהיד על יחודיה, דלית אל"ה אחרא עילא ותתא ולארבע סטרין (TZ 7a:10, line 114)
> *And so to every side it witnesses His unity: that there is no other god above or below or to the four sides.*

This is why, the text says, the sages taught to crown Him "over heaven and earth and the four winds" when saying Echad in the Shema.

**Above and below as one.** "The Holy One above is one with His Shechinah" (דאיהו קודשא בריך הוא לעילא חד בשכינתיה, line 255). When He is seen only in His servant, the angel Metatron, without His Shechinah, He looks changed, and that is how Elisha ben Avuyah fell into thinking there were "two domains" (line 256). The error of two powers comes from seeing the servant without the Presence.

## Patach Eliyahu (17a-17b)

Elijah's prayer is the range's peak and the Tikkunim's clearest word on the unity. It moves in four steps.

*First, He is one past counting and past thought:*

> פתח אליהו ואמר רבון עלמין דאנת הוא חד ולא בחשבן (TZ 17a:9, line 292)
> *Elijah opened and said: Master of the worlds, You are One, but not in number.*

> אנת הוא עלאה על כל עלאין סתימא על כל סתימין. לית מחשבה תפיסא בך כלל (TZ 17a:10, line 293)
> *You are highest of all the high, hidden of all the hidden. No thought grasps You at all.*

"One but not in number" means He is not the first of a series. There is no second to set beside Him.

*Second, He brought out ten "garments" (the sefirot) to lead the hidden and the revealed worlds, and through them He hides:*

> ובהון אתכסיאת (נ"א אתכסייא) מבני נשא (TZ 17a:11, line 294)
> *and in them You are hidden from human beings.*

The same sefirot that show Him also hide Him (face F03, hiding).

> ואנת הוא דקשיר לון ומיחד לון. ובגין דאנת מלגאו. כל מאן דאפריש חד מן חבריה מאלין עשר. אתחשיב ליה כאלו אפריש בך (TZ 17a:12, line 295)
> *You are the One who ties them and unites them. And because You are within, whoever separates one of these ten from its fellow, it is counted to him as if he separated You.*

> ולית מאן דאנהיג לך לא לעילא ולא לתתא ולא מכל סטרא (TZ 17a:14, line 297)
> *And no one leads You, not above, not below, not from any side.*

He is the flow that waters the tree, and that flow is like the soul to the body (line 304). Yet

> ובך לית דמיון ולית דיוקנא מכל מה דלגו ולבר (TZ 17a:21, line 304)
> *and in You there is no likeness and no image of anything inside or outside.*

*Fourth, He has no name of His own, because He fills every name:*

> ולית בך דידע כלל (TZ 17a:22, line 305)
> *and there is none who knows You at all.*

> ובר מינך לית יחידא (ס"א יחודא) בעלאי ותתאי. ואנת אשתמודע אדון על כלא (TZ 17b:1, line 306)
> *Apart from You there is no One (another reading: no unity) above or below, and You are known as Lord over all.*

> ואנת לית לך שם ידיע. דאנת הוא ממלא כל שמהן. ואנת הוא שלימו דכלהו וכד אנת תסתלק מנהון אשתארו כלהו שמהן כגופא בלא נשמתא (TZ 17b:2, line 307)
> *You have no known name, because You fill all names. You are the wholeness of them all, and when You withdraw from them, all the names are left like a body without a soul.*

> אנת חכים ולאו בחכמה ידיעא. אנת הוא מבין ולא מבינה ידיעא (TZ 17b:3, line 308)
> *You are wise, but not with a known wisdom; You understand, but not with a known understanding.*

Even justice and mercy, by which He "shows how the world is led", are not His: ולאו מכל אלין מדות כלל, "and not of these attributes at all" (line 310). They were set up "to make Your strength known to people" (line 308).

Elijah then asks Rabbi Shimon to rise, the righteous of Hebron to wake, and Moses with them, "to wake the Shechinah who sleeps in exile" (line 315). The dead tzaddikim "are not dead": they are of her who says אני ישנה ולבי ער, "I sleep but my heart is awake" (ולאו אינון מתים, line 314). And the Holy One tells her He has not entered His own house since the Temple fell:

> לאו הכי דלא אעלנא כל זמנא דאנת בגלותא (TZ 17b:13, line 318)
> *Not so: I have not entered, all the time that you are in exile.*

**Our reading.** Patach Eliyahu holds together the two things Chassidus most wants held together. He is utterly beyond (no thought, no name, no likeness, not even His own attributes are Him). And He is the inside of everything (the soul of the names; to cut one sefirah from another is to cut Him). The Alter Rebbe builds Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah on exactly this pair; the Tanya (ch. 4) quotes "no thought grasps You at all" and pairs it with "but He is grasped by the will of the heart". That second half is not in either of the two books read here as one line with the first. The will of the heart (רעותא דלבא) does appear often in Zohar Chadash as the way one binds the knots of faith in the Shema (see part4-zc reports).

## States of consciousness

**Before the one without a picture (ties S35, S36; the edge of knowing).** The mind reaches for an image and is told each image is only wax. From inside: every picture you make of God is set down gently, not in anger, because the text itself says the prophets saw only likenesses. What is left is not blankness but presence without shape. How one enters: by the via negativa of lines 98-103 and 305-310, saying "not this" to each image, including the good ones. Guard: the text does not say He is empty; it says He fills all names (line 307).

**The whole world in one domain (ties S63 The world held, S62 Only Him).** "All are in His power, in the domain of the One" (line 97). From inside: there is no "outside" to stand in. Wherever I am, I am in the one domain. Entry: the Shema, crowning Him on six sides (line 114).

**The still small voice where the King comes (new, proposed Z-S01).** The union of the two Names needs silence: חבורא דתרין שמהן צריכין בחשאי (line 42). Wind, earthquake and fire are heard; the King does not come in them. He comes in the fourth thing, the still small voice:

> קול דממה דקה דא י' מן הוה"י, ואיהי י' מן אדנ"י, תמן קא אתא מלכא (TZ 3b:3, line 43)
> *The still small voice: that is the yud of the Name, and it is the yud of Adonai. There the King comes.*

From inside: the moment in the standing prayer when the words go quiet and the wings of the living creatures "let down", so that nothing is heard (line 42). Not emptiness; arrival. How to enter: the Amidah said in a whisper, standing, after the noise of praise. Ties to S32 Quiet and S33 Before Whom, but the Tikkunim adds that this quiet is where the joining happens, not only where the self rests.

**A home for her (ties S82 The home below; new shade proposed Z-S02 "making her a nest").** The Shechinah is a mother bird driven from her nest (line 12), and the masters of Torah "make her a dwelling, and for the Holy One" (ואלין עבדין לה דירה ולקודשא בריך הוא, line 15). The chassid is one who makes a nest for his Maker, and what she finds in him depends on how he prepared it: כפום ההוא בר נש דתקין לה (line 17), "according to the person who readies it". From inside: a sense of hosting, of making room, of being the place where someone homeless can rest.

**Joy of a new secret (ties S53 Glad).** "There is no joy among them like the joy when a secret of the Torah is renewed among them" (דלית חדוה ביניהו כחדוה כד אתחדש ביניהו רזא דאורייתא, line 183).

## Meditation and practice

**1. Intend the joined Names in every mention of God (proposed technique Z-T01, "one voice in the words").** At every mention of God, in Shema, prayer, song and thanks, intend the speech in Adonai (the Name of the Presence) and the voice in the Name Havayah, and join them in the hidden One. Then:

> וביה צריך הכונה, דלא תליא למימר ביה קול ודבור אלא מחשבתא (TZ 3a:4, line 37)
> *And the intention must be in Him, of whom one cannot say voice or speech, only thought.*

What it is for: that no word of prayer be spoken to a half of God. How (ours, in plain terms, without letter work): when the lips say "God", know that the saying is His presence here, the voice behind it is His, and both are one before a Oneness no word reaches. Ties to technique 18 (saying it with the lips) and to 25 (letter methods). Guard (ours): the guide keeps the meaning, not the letter permutations of lines 117-137, which the text itself says "may not be spoken aloud, only looked at and read in thought" (line 136).

**2. Crown Him on six sides in the Shema (proposed Z-T02).** The vav "witnesses on every side" that there is no other (line 114). Practice: at Echad, turn the mind up, down and to the four directions, and in each find that He is there and nothing else is. Ties to technique 6 (walking the truth down) and 14.

**3. No talking between (a guard).** Talk in the middle of the Shema or between the hand and head tefillin is "a sin in his hand", because it separates what was being joined (lines 38, 146). What it is for: the unity is also a unity of attention.

**4. Silence in the standing prayer.** The union is made בחשאי, in whisper (line 42). See the state above.

**5. Awe in ten rungs, and the first is knowing whom you fear.** Lines 70-91 count ten rungs of awe. Fear of punishment "like fear of the strap" is the lowest (line 84). Higher is awe born of Torah: a person who does not know who gave the Torah and who is watching, "how can he fear Him?" (line 87). Shame before God is so great a rung that "it is as if the world was created for it" (line 79). And awe must come before wisdom: whoever puts wisdom first is like one handed the inner keys without the outer ones; "how will he get in?" (line 70). Ties to technique 16 and state S45/S46.

**6. Love that does not hang on gifts (ties S43, S60).** Love of God that depends on His keeping my body, soul or money safe is not the root of love:

> הא רחימו דיליה תלויה בההיא דיהיב ליה, ולאו עקר רחימו (TZ 10b:4, line 177)
> *His love hangs on what He gives him; it is not the root of love.*

Practice: whatever is dearest to you, give it over to Him in love, especially in a hard time:

> במה דחביב עלך מסור ליה ברחימו דמארך בעת צרה (TZ 10b:1, line 174)
> *With whatever is dear to you, hand it over in the love of your Master in a time of trouble.*

This is the source of the Mishnah's teaching read by Chassidus (Tanya ch. 41 and 44, love "with all your might") as a love not bound to any cause.

**7. Taking the fruit and throwing away the peel.** Four entered the orchard; three ate the peels and died; the fourth ate the fruit and threw the peel away, like Rabbi Meir: רבי מאיר רמון מצא, תוכו אכל קליפתו זרק (line 193). The questions that cover a law are like peels; one must throw them off to eat the bread of Torah (line 194). What it is for: learning that feeds, not learning that argues.

## The big questions

**The soul.** The body is a lamp. The wick is the nefesh (the life joined to the body), the oil is the ruach, the light is the neshamah, and the vessel is the body, all "one knot of unity", which is "the lamp of God is the soul of man":

> כגוונא דא נפשא דאיהי שתופא דגופא איהי פתילה, רוח דא משחא, נהורא דא נשמתא, מאנא דא גופא (TZ 14b:7, line 252)
> *So the nefesh, which is partner to the body, is the wick; the ruach is the oil; the neshamah is the light; the vessel is the body.*

Also: the letters of Torah are the nefesh, the vowel points the ruach, the cantillation (the motion of the words) the neshamah (lines 215-217). The soul is whole when a person "completes his soul so that the Name rests in him, and makes Him a throne":

> זכאה איהו מאן דאשלים נשמתיה, לשריא ביה שם יהו"ה, ועביד ליה כרסיא לגביה (TZ 3b:7, line 47)
> *Fortunate is one who completes his soul so that the Name rests in it, and makes Him a throne.*

**Suffering and the exile of the Presence.** The Shechinah is black in exile, her throne cracked by sin (line 46). The tzaddik who wanders from his home wanders like her, "it is enough for the servant to be like his master" (line 13). The great comfort of the range is that God shares the exile: "I have not entered My house as long as you are in exile" (line 318). He is bound in exile with Israel, and "in all their distress He is distressed" (line 151).

**Freedom.** In the last redemption the King's daughter will not leave like a runaway slave, because the Torah goes with her into exile, and "the Torah truly is freedom" (דאורייתא ודאי איהי חירו, line 259).

**The far who came near.** The range ends on "peace, peace to the far and the near": לרחוק שנתקרב, "to the far one who came near" (line 283). Teshuvah is counted as nearness.

## What Chassidus takes from here, and where it differs (ours)

1. Chassidus takes Patach Eliyahu whole as its model of the unity: beyond thought and within all. The Tanya adds the one thing Elijah's prayer only hints at: that what thought cannot grasp, the will of the heart can (re'uta deliba). Elijah's prayer stops at "no thought grasps You"; the Tanya makes the heart's will the door.
2. "He and His life are one" (line 49) is, for the Tanya, the reason Atzilut is Godliness and Beriah is not; the Alter Rebbe uses it to say that the ten sefirot of Atzilut are not created. The Tikkunim say it; Chassidus builds the whole order of worlds on it.
3. The Tikkunim's God is a lover in exile, bound with His people (lines 151, 318). Chassidus keeps this and turns it inward: the "exile of the Shechinah" becomes the exile of the divine soul in the body (Tanya ch. 17, 31, 45, the pity for the spark of God in oneself). The Tikkunim's own center of gravity is more outward: her exile in history, and our task to raise her.
4. What the guide should take: the still small voice as the place of arrival (Z-S01); making a home for the homeless Presence (Z-S02); love that does not hang on gifts (line 177); the lamp of the soul (line 252).
