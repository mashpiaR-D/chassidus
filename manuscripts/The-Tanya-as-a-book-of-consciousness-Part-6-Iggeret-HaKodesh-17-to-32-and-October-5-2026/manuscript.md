# The Tanya as a book of consciousness · Part 6: Iggeret HaKodesh 17 to 32 and Kuntres Acharon

---
These are the last pages of the Tanya, and here the states of consciousness come inside letters about money, prayer, grief and quarrels in shul. The Holy Letters 17 to 32 keep returning to one movement: a heart that wakes below wakes something above. Mercy for a poor man opens mercy on you. Giving often, in small coins, lifts a fallen presence little by little. Love that splits between people makes the Shechinah sick; love that joins them lets life go round again. Around that movement stand other states: the thirst that has no taste yet and the delight that falls as a gift; the quiet of taking a blow face to face; the calm that comes when anger learns whose breath is in the one who hurt you; the grief that finds a teacher's life still going on inside his students. The Kuntres Acharon ("the last notes") is a set of dense essays on why the mouth, the hand and the deed reach further than the finest feeling, why holding an etrog holds more than a thought can, and why the book ends on the quiet of Shabbat, the stopping of idle speech. Where a letter is thick with Kabbalah this reading says so and gives its point plainly. Each letter was read in the Hebrew before it was written, and each source line was checked against the Hebrew text at the line given.

## Letter 17 · Doing what He does

**The state.** Rachamim (mercy) woken in your own heart for someone who needs it, felt not as a good deed of yours but as joining the one thing G-d Himself does all the time. Beside it the letter sets two kinds of closeness: the measured delight of understanding, which always has an edge, and a light with no edge at all, which will shine in a body.

**The chapter as consciousness.** It starts with a law of the inner life. When a person wakes mercy in his heart for everyone who needs it, the rabbis call that "a stirring from below" (isarusa diltata), and it brings "a stirring from above": great mercy comes down on him from the Source of mercy. You eat the "fruits" of it in this world and the "principal" stays for the World to Come. So the first state is simple. The heart softens toward a person in need, and in that softening you are standing where mercy flows. You do not earn it. You open to it.

The fruits are the flow of life that comes down step by step through all the worlds until it dresses itself in plain things here: children, life, food. The principal is harder to see. The verse says, "Your commandment is very wide." Why "Your commandment," one, and not "Your commandments"? Because it means charity, and charity is G-d's own commandment. It is what the Holy One, in His glory and in Himself, does always: He keeps the worlds alive, for nothing, because He wants to. "Keep the way of G-d, to do charity." A way is a road you walk from one town to the next. Charity is the road on which the Infinite Light, the light that surrounds all worlds (sovev kol almin), will come all the way down into this world.

Now the letter separates two states of closeness. The first is Gan Eden, the garden where souls go after this life. Its light is the light that fills the worlds (memale kol almin). It comes through many narrowings, and each soul takes it in by grasping, "each according to his measure." The delight there is understanding: the secrets of the Torah the person learned here in its plain form. This is a real state and a high one. But it has edges. It is knowing, and knowing always has a size.

The second is what will shine at the resurrection, when souls come back into pure bodies. That light is not grasped. It surrounds. It has no measure. The rabbis said the righteous will sit "with their crowns on their heads," and a crown sits around the head, not inside it. They said the righteous will one day be called "holy," and holy means set apart, beyond any knowing. This is keser (the crown), called "nothing" in the Zohar because nothing can grasp it. And it shows itself exactly when the soul is in a body again, because "their beginning is lodged in their end": the highest comes out at the lowest.

But you cannot skip the garden. First each person must take in wisdom by his own measure. Only then the crown.

So "Your commandment is very wide": charity is a very wide vessel, wide enough for the edgeless light to dress itself in. "Very" (meod) means without limit. Against it the verse sets "to every end I have seen a limit." The Hebrew for "end" (tichlah) is read as klos hanefesh, the soul's melting longing in Gan Eden. Even that has a stopping point. There are levels upon levels of Gan Eden, and scholars "have no rest" there, always climbing. Rest comes only after the resurrection.

What is it like from inside? When you give from a soft heart you feel wider than yourself. There is no understanding in it, no light in the mind. There is only a hand opening. The letter says that plain, unlit opening is a wider vessel than the brightest understanding. Where the picture breaks: a road is walked by the traveller; here the Traveller comes toward you on the road you pave. The letter compresses the map of the worlds and I have compressed it further.

**The movement.** From a closed hand and a tight heart ("I have my own needs") to mercy woken, and from there to knowing that this mercy is His own act done through you. What carries a person across is the act itself, from a soft heart, and understanding that giving is the way of G-d.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "This is what He does, always." 2. Think of one person you know who is short of something real: food, heat, a way to pay rent. Picture their table tonight. Then picture the life that holds every table in the world, given for nothing, every moment. 3. The picture breaks: you give from what you have; He gives from nothing, and loses nothing. 4. Stop at the edge. The light your giving holds is wider than anything you will understand. Do not try to see it. 5. Say to Him: "Let my hand be part of Your way." 6. Give something today, to that person or to someone like them, with a kind face.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when giving feels like standing next to Someone, and the question "do I have enough" gets quieter. The fake is giving to feel generous, where the warm glow is about you and the poor man is a mirror.

## Letter 18 · Thirst before taste

**The state.** Two loves of G-d. Ahavah betaanugim (love in delights): the soul tasting how good G-d is, a joy that falls from above as a gift. And ahavah vetaavah (love as longing): the soul wanting to cling, hurting to be far, like thirst with no water yet. The second is hidden in every heart and can be brought out.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "How beautiful you are, how pleasant, love in delights." There are two loves. The first is love in delights. The soul delights in G-d with a wonderful pleasure, a great joy, "the joy of the soul and its melting," tasting that G-d is good and sweet. It is a taste of the World to Come itself. "Rejoice, righteous ones, in G-d." Not every person merits it.

This love is a gift. The verse calls priestly service "a service of gift." You cannot get it by the toil of flesh the way you get awe. Of awe the rabbis ask, "Did you toil in awe?" Awe has to be searched for like buried treasure. This love cannot be searched for. It falls on a person by itself, from above, when he did not prepare for it or aim at it. But only after one thing: he has worked at yiras haromemus (awe of His loftiness) to the full measure his soul can reach. Then the love in delights comes on its own, to rest with the awe and join it. "It is the way of a man to go looking for a woman": the gift seeks out the one who toiled.

So the first state has a strange shape. You cannot aim at it. You aim at awe, and the delight comes from the side, unasked.

The second love is longing. The soul wants to cling to G-d, to be bound up in the bundle of life. Being near G-d is very good to it, and being far is very bad, as if an iron wall of the outside forces stood between. This love is hidden in the heart of every Jew, even the wicked. Regret comes from it: the pang after a wrong is this love, pressing up.

But because it is hidden, in exile in the body, the husk (kelipah, the shell that covers holiness) can rule over it. That is "the spirit of folly" that makes a person sin. So a person's work is to grow strong against the husk in every way. First drive it out of the body completely, out of the thought, speech and deeds of the mind, the tongue and the limbs. Then, with a strong hand, "bring the prisoner out of the dungeon": let the hidden love come out openly in every power of the soul.

How? Mainly with the mind. The mind should think, always, as much as it can, about the Creator: how He is the life of all life in general, and the life of your own soul in particular. From that thinking the longing comes, a natural longing, "like a son who longs to be always with his father, like fire that always rises to its source." The more you keep thinking it, the more the longing grows and spreads into the mouth and all the limbs, to learn and to do, and so to cling to G-d Himself through them, "for the Torah and the Holy One are all one."

On this open longing it is written, "My soul thirsts for You": like a man thirsty for water who has no pleasure at all yet. That is the inner feel of it. Thirst, not sweetness. It is not a lesser state for being dry.

And for this hidden love we pray, that He help us bring it out, so the heart is full of it alone and its rival, the craving for this world, does not come into the house. The love should be mistress of the house, and the craving her servant, used only for what she needs, like eating and drinking. "Know Him in all your ways."

Where the picture breaks: a son longing for his father is apart from him; the soul longs for the One who is its very life, closer than any father.

**The movement.** From the love lying hidden under the husk, showing only as regret after the fact, to open longing in the mind, the mouth and the hands, and then, after awe, perhaps the gift of delight. What carries a person across is first pushing the husk out of thought, speech and deed, then steady thinking about G-d as the life of your life.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "He is the life of my life." 2. Think it through: your breath this minute, your wanting, your ability to think this very thought. Each is being given. Come down to one thing: the hand holding this page. 3. The picture breaks: a father is somewhere else; He is nowhere else. 4. Stop at the edge. You will feel thirst, maybe not sweetness. Stay with the thirst. 5. Say to Him: "I want to be near You." 6. Today, do one mitzvah with the wanting in it, and eat one meal as the servant of that wanting, not its master.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when being far from Him starts to hurt in a quiet, clean way, and a craving you used to obey feels like a guest, not the owner. The fake is chasing the sweet feeling itself, prayer measured by how good it felt, delight grabbed without the toil of awe.

## Letter 19 · The back that holds the face

**The state.** Knowing that every word of the Torah, and every mitzvah, has a wisdom inside it above your understanding: the achorayim (the back) of G-d's wisdom is what reaches you, and the panim (the face) is hidden in it. It is the state of respect before a verse you do not understand, and of doing what it asks anyway.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "He wraps Himself in light like a garment." The Arizal wrote that Moshe, greatest of prophets, did not grasp the inside of the highest wisdom. He grasped its back, clothed in lower levels, all the way down to the lowest ones, where he saw inwardly. So G-d told him, "You will see My back, but My face will not be seen." (The letter traces this through the sefirot, the ten levels of G-d's light, and I have left the map out.)

A hard question follows. No prophet ever rose like Moshe. So how could the Arizal teach about levels higher still? The answer is plain. There are two different states of knowing G-d. Prophecy is called "seeing." In it, the Hidden shows Himself openly. The prophet is shown. The sages of the hidden wisdom, like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and the Arizal, grasp with wisdom and understanding. Nothing shows itself to them. They grasp hidden things that stay hidden. That is why "a sage is better than a prophet": by wisdom a person can reach higher than what is able to come down and be seen. Only the lowest levels come down openly, because they are the channels through which every higher level gives life to the one below.

Inside those channels is understanding (binah). Inside understanding is the back of wisdom (chochmah), which is above understanding. Wisdom is the source the mind flows from. So "the Torah comes from wisdom," and that is why the reasons of the mitzvos were not revealed. Even where a reason is given that we seem to understand, that reason is not the end of it. Inside it is the inner, hidden wisdom above all understanding.

The same is true of every word G-d spoke through the prophets, "whether rebuke or stories." You can feel it in the words written one way and read another. The reading is what we understand. The writing stays above understanding; as written, the word has no garment we can hold. The large letters in the Torah shine from a higher world, with no garment at all.

What is face and what is back? The face of a level is the level itself, one with the Infinite. The back is the outer, lower part of it that can come down and dress itself in creatures. The letter gives a picture from us. A person has five layers: mind, feelings, thought, speech and deed. The life in the deed is as nothing next to the life in speech, and that is as nothing next to thought, feeling and mind. So all of His wisdom that spreads down into limited creatures is as nothing next to its face.

Then the turn. Because Moshe reached the back of wisdom, the Torah was given through him, "the fallen fruit of the wisdom above." What fell down into our physical Torah is meant for doing. "Great is learning that leads to doing." The Arizal taught that every soul must come back again and again until it has done all 613 mitzvos in the deed itself.

A short note closes the letter. Our letters come three ways: in the deed (the shapes in a Torah scroll), in speech (the twenty-two sounds, the same in every language, only combined differently), and in thought. Speech lives from the letters of thought. Even when you talk while thinking of something else, you only say words that were in your thought many times before; a trace of that thought stays in them. That trace is what the back of a higher level is: what enters the lower to give it life.

From inside, this is a humble state and a full one. You hold a story, a law, a verse. You understand part of it. You know the part you understand is a garment. Where the picture breaks: a person's back is the less important side, but here the back is the only way the face reaches you at all.

**The movement.** From "I understand this, so I own it" (or "I don't understand this, so it is empty") to "there is more in this than I can see, and it came down to be done." What carries a person across is looking at the reason given and knowing it is not the whole reason, then doing the deed.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "There is more in this than I can see." 2. Take one mitzvah you do, washing hands, lighting a candle, giving a coin. Say the reason you know. Then see that the reason is a garment, and inside it is wisdom that is one with Him. 3. The picture breaks: here the back is not a lesser side; it is the face bending down to you. 4. Stop where Moshe stopped: "My face will not be seen." 5. Say to Him: "I will do what I cannot see through." 6. Do that one mitzvah today with care, as if you were holding His hidden wisdom in your hands.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a verse you do not understand makes you quiet instead of restless, and doing it matters more than explaining it. The fake is a cozy mystery that excuses not learning and not doing, or cleverness that thinks its reason is the bottom.

## Letter 20 · Something from nothing

**The state.** Standing inside the double sight of a created thing: from His side it is nothing, melted into the power that makes it; from our side it is utterly separate and solid. Only He, whose being is from Himself, can make a real "something" out of nothing, and the deed on that solid something is why the soul came down.

**The chapter as consciousness.** This letter is long and full of Kabbalah. I have folded its maps into plain sentences.

The Zohar says of the highest world, "He and His life are one, He and His vessels are one." But vessels have limits. How can the Infinite be one with them? The letter's answer: those vessels are G-dliness, able to create something from nothing, just as the Infinite can.

Most things come from other things, the way an effect comes from its cause. An effect stays wrapped in its cause and melts into it, like sunlight in the sun. The ten sefiros unfold from one another this way. But no amount of narrowing, step after step, will ever turn spirit into thick matter like dust. Spirit only makes finer spirit. For a real "something," a different act is needed: something from nothing, called beriah (creation).

Now the two sights. "Before Him" a created thing is nothing, melted into the light that makes it, like sunlight in the sun. That is His knowing, from above looking down. But in the knowing from below looking up, the created thing is completely separate. The power that makes it is not grasped at all, and there is no likeness between them, not even the likeness of effect to cause. An effect at least knows something of its cause and melts because of it. A created thing does not. That is why it is called "something" from "nothing." This is the ordinary state of consciousness in the world: things feel solid and self-made.

Who can do this? Only He. His being is from Himself; nothing came before Him and caused Him. So He alone, by His power, can create something from absolute nothing, with no cause before it. His light is like its source, so where His own light is clothed in the vessels, they can do it too. (The letter spells out how souls of angels and of people stand between G-dliness and "something," and how the tenth vessel, malchus, the "kingship," is where the separate thing is born. I have compressed all this.)

Why make things that feel separate? "There is no king without a people." A king needs subjects who are not himself and who do his word by choice. So the created thing is made separate on purpose, that He be King over the separate by their keeping His mitzvos. "The end of the deed was first in thought." That is why the mitzvos, which are bodily, are the root of the Torah, which is wisdom. The letter's picture: the placenta forms first from the seed and is the whole of the child for forty days, before any shape appears. So the deed is the root and the wisdom grows from it. "Whoever learns without meaning to do, better his placenta had turned over on his face."

Then the most beautiful picture. Where does His power of something from nothing show most openly? In plain earth. The earth brings up grass and trees out of nothing, every year, forever. The stars cannot. The rabbis say each blade has a star that strikes it and says "grow," but only once it has sprouted, from small to big. Before it sprouts, to whom would the star speak? The growing power in the earth is "nothing," spiritual, and what comes up is solid. Why the lowest element? Because at the very bottom of all the worlds, under the "feet" of the first spiritual form, the surrounding Infinite Light shines with almost nothing in between.

A planted seed becomes a whole tree; one grain becomes hundreds. That too is like something from nothing. And what a person plants is far better than what grows wild, because the work below draws the highest lights down, which is the purpose of man. The lower feeds the higher. Man lives on animals and plants; animals on plants. "A child cannot say Abba and Imma until he tastes grain." Even angels "of the ox's face" are fed by the spirit of the animal rising from the altar.

So the letter ends: see from all this how great the mitzvos of the deed are. They are the reason souls came down. "Today, to do them." "One hour of return and good deeds in this world is better than all the life of the World to Come."

From inside: you stand on the floor, which feels like the most final thing there is, and you know it is the place where His own power shows most. Where the picture breaks: a seed is still a something; His nothing is not a small something.

**The movement.** From "the world is solid and made by itself" to "the solid world is His something from nothing, and it is the place of the deed." What carries a person across is understanding the two sights, and then the act on physical things.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Something from nothing. Only He." 2. Take a piece of bread. Go back: grain, seed, earth, the growing power in the earth. Each step came out of less than itself. At the bottom is nothing, and He is making it now. 3. The picture breaks: a seed has some of the tree in it; His nothing has none of the thing in it. 4. Stop at the edge. From His side the bread is nothing. From yours it is bread. Hold both. 5. Say to Him: "You are King here, in this." 6. Say a blessing over that bread today, slowly, and eat it to do His will.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when plain physical things feel close to Him rather than far, and a small deed feels weighty. The fake is "the world is illusion," which makes the deed seem pointless, or loving the earth itself and forgetting who says "grow."

## Letter 21 · Eager, and again, and again

**The state.** Zerizus (eagerness): doing His will quickly, gladly, because you want to give Him pleasure, not because you were pushed. And with it the state of the many small acts: the same good done again and again, each time lifting a little of a presence that lies in the dust.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter was written to people who gave each year to support the poor of the Land of Israel. It asks them to give every week, or at least every month, from the sum they already meant to give for the year.

First, eagerness. "A person should always hurry to a mitzvah." The eagerness of Avraham stands for us forever. The letter says something surprising about the binding of Isaac. The test itself was not so great for someone like Avraham. G-d had spoken to him directly, "Take your son." Many holy people gave their lives for G-d without G-d ever speaking to them. What made Avraham's act great was that he did it with wonderful speed, to show his joy and his wanting to fill his Maker's will and give Him pleasure. So the inner state the letter points to is not heroism. It is gladness that moves fast. The feet run because the heart is already there.

Eagerness matters in every mitzvah and most of all in charity, which weighs as much as all of them and shields a person in this world from harm. "Charity saves from death," all the more from lesser troubles. A person is judged every day, so it is good to give as early as you can.

Then a second thing. The Rambam taught that giving many times is better than giving once, even if the total is the same. "Everything follows the number of the deeds." His reason: many acts refine the soul. One big gift moves you once. A thousand small ones reshape you.

The letter adds a deeper reason. "The work of charity is for life." Its power is to draw higher life from the Life of all life down into "the land of the living," which is the Shechinah (the indwelling presence of G-d), of whom it says, "You give life to them all." She is "the fallen booth of David," fallen to the dust. "When Israel went into exile, the Shechinah went with them." A stirring below, giving life to "the spirit of the lowly" who have nothing of their own, brings a stirring above. Each gift draws life down and makes a union above. So many gifts make many unions.

And this too is the Rambam's "refining the soul," because the Zohar calls the Shechinah "soul": she is our life and our soul. "Our soul is bowed to the dust." That is why "charity brings the redemption near": it raises her from the dust little by little, "until Shiloh comes."

What is it like from inside? Two states sit together. One is the quick, bright wanting of Avraham: I want to do this, now. The other is patience: the same small act, week after week, knowing that each time a little is lifted and none of it is wasted. The first without the second burns out. The second without the first turns grey. Where the picture breaks: lifting something from the dust sounds like one great heave; here it is a thousand small ones, and the dust is inside you as much as outside.

**The movement.** From giving once, when moved, to giving often and quickly, gladly. What carries a person across is the plain act repeated, and knowing what each act does: it refines you and it lifts her.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "To raise her from the dust, little by little." 2. Take the sum you give in a year. Divide it by the weeks. Picture that small coin each week, going to a real person with nothing of their own. 3. Think of Avraham waking early, saddling the donkey himself. Speed, not strain. 4. The picture breaks: you do not lift her alone and you do not see her rise. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Let me be quick for You." 6. Put a coin in a charity box today, and set a day each week to do it again.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when giving becomes something you look forward to and miss when you skip it. The fake is one big gift that buys you the right to forget, or a busy rushing that is only nerves.

## Letter 22 · Face to face, and one body

**The state.** Two states in two short letters. First, taking hard things panim bepanim (face to face) with G-d, with love, instead of turning your back to run for help. Second, shiflus ruach (lowliness of spirit) before every person and love of your fellow, pushing any bad thought about another out of the heart like smoke, because we are one body.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "From hidden love, open rebuke." The Alter Rebbe scolds his followers. They have made a custom of travelling to him to ask advice about worldly matters: business, marriages, health. Where, he asks, was such a thing ever done? Even the greatest sages, to whom "no secret was hidden," were not asked about worldly things. Only prophets were, like Shmuel, whom Shaul asked about lost donkeys. The truth is that all of a person's affairs, except Torah and the fear of Heaven, are known only by prophecy. "Everything is in the hands of Heaven except the fear of Heaven."

Why do they come? "Love spoils judgment." They love the life of their bodies, for the sake of Heaven, to serve G-d with them, and that love blinds them. When the body suffers they cannot bear it. It drives them out of their minds, from town to town, to ask advice from far away. They do not turn to G-d with a low spirit and a bowed body, to take His rebuke with love. "Whom G-d loves, He rebukes."

The picture: a father who is merciful, wise and righteous strikes his son. A wise son does not turn his neck and run for help, or even find someone to plead for him. He stays "face to face" with his father, and bears the blows with love, for his good all his days.

What is "face" above? It is the will and wanting with which our Father gives His children every good, life of soul and body, with love, through the Torah of life, which is His will. "In the light of Your face You gave us a Torah of life." (The letter says the idol worshippers get their bodily life from above without that wanting, from the "back," which is why their gods are called "other gods.") So it is in a person. Will and wanting are the face. If he does not take what comes with love and will, it is as if he turned his back.

The advice for taking it with love is G-d's own advice through the sages: search your deeds. You will find things that need cleansing by suffering, and you will see with your eyes how much He loves you, a love that "spoils judgment," like a great and awesome king who washes the filth off his only son with his own hands. Then, "as water reflects a face," love will wake in the heart of anyone who understands how precious G-d's love for the lower ones is, "better than life." And then G-d gives the good. He shines His face in open love, the love that was first clothed and hidden in open rebuke, and the harsh judgments are sweetened in their root.

The second letter is short. He is overwhelmed, he says, and can only remind them in brief: stand firm on prayer, out loud, with all your strength, against every obstacle, with a simple will and "a generous spirit," which is above wisdom and understanding. Walk simply and straight, "G-d made man upright," and do not seek "many calculations." That is Heaven's work, not flesh and blood's. Believe fully in "be lowly of spirit before every person," for each one has something better than his friend. "All Israel are one man, friends." One body has many limbs, and when they are pulled apart it touches the heart, "for from it comes life." So since we are all one man, the service is set right in the heart. That is why it says "to serve Him with one shoulder."

So, beloved, work with all your heart to plant love of your fellow in your heart. "Do not think evil of your fellow in your hearts." Never let it come up, and if it comes up, push it away like smoke, like a thought of idolatry itself. Evil speech is as grave as the three great sins, and thought is stronger than speech, for good and for bad.

From inside, the first state is turning toward the One who hurts you, not away. It feels like staying in the room. The second is a heart with no corner kept for anyone's faults. Where the picture breaks: a human father's blows can be wrong; here the letter asks you to trust the Father whose will is life. It does not ask you to call a human cruelty love.

**The movement.** From turning your back (running, pleading, resenting) to facing Him and finding His love inside the hard thing; and from a heart that keeps accounts of others to a heart that is one with them. What carries a person across: searching your own deeds, simple will in prayer, and pushing out the first bad thought about someone before it settles.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Face to face with my Father." 2. Bring to mind one real pain you are carrying now. Notice where you have been looking for a way around Him: people, schemes, worry. 3. Turn. Search one deed of your own that needed cleaning. See the King washing His son with His own hands. 4. The picture breaks: you will not understand why. Stop there. Face Him anyway. 5. Say to Him: "I am not running. I am here." 6. Today, when a bad thought about someone rises, push it out at once like smoke, and say one good thing about them.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the pain stays but the resentment in it melts, and other people's faults stop feeling like your business. The fake is forced cheerfulness that denies the pain, or "accepting" that is really giving up on doing what you can.

## Letter 23 · Held from head to foot

**The state.** Two kinds of G-dly light in a person. The light that comes inside you by measure and is felt: the heart stirs, prayer sweetens. And the light that rests on you from above, makif (surrounding), too great to come inside, not felt at all, which rests where ten gather in Torah and prayer. The state is living under a presence you cannot feel, and not treating it lightly.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The sages taught, "Ten who sit and learn Torah, the Shechinah rests among them." This, the letter says, is the whole of man. The soul came down into this world for this rise, and there is no higher one: that the Shechinah, which "the heavens and the heavens of heavens cannot contain," should dwell among people through Torah and mitzvos done in a group of ten.

The sages had to find a verse to show that even one person learning alone matters. But they found one only for a reward, for the single person by his own measure. For the resting of G-d's holiness, one is nothing next to ten.

What is the difference? The reward is G-d shining "for the soul that seeks Him" through the light of His Torah, which is His garment. The soul has limits in all its powers, so the light that shines in it is limited, narrowed, clothed inside it. That is why the heart of those who seek G-d is moved in prayer and the like. They rejoice and delight in the sweetness of G-d as He shows Himself through His garment, the Torah, and it flashes out "like lightning." That felt delight is the reward, always kept in the soul that toils.

The resting (hashraah) is something else. It is an immense light with no limit. It cannot clothe itself in a limited soul. It only surrounds it from above, "from head to foot." "Let the pleasantness of G-d be upon us": upon us, from above. The pleasantness that shone in the work of our hands, in Torah and mitzvos, rests on us because it has no limit and cannot come inside our soul and mind. So we do not grasp it. This is why the sages said, "There is no reward for a mitzvah in this world." It can only be grasped when the soul leaves the body, and even then only by kindness.

Angels are different. The Alter Rebbe heard from his teachers: if an angel stood among ten Jews together, even if they were not speaking Torah, boundless awe would fall on it from the Shechinah resting on them, until it would cease to exist.

So it is terrible, he says, that among his own people, who draw near to G-d in prayer, before or after prayer a "seat of scorners" forms: idle talk, jokes. If two sitting without Torah are a seat of scorners, what is it among ten, where the Shechinah rests? There is no greater insult to the Shechinah. A secret sin "pushes the feet of the Shechinah"; a public sin pushes the whole stature, as it were.

His remedy: every weekday, between the afternoon and evening prayers, learn in a group of ten from the aggadah in Ein Yaakov, where most of the Torah's secrets are hidden and whose plain teachings are the ways of G-d a person can walk in. Also learn a little of the daily laws. On Shabbat, the laws of Shabbat, where a person can so easily stumble. And let no one separate from the group, even to learn something else, and certainly not walk out if there would not be ten without him.

From inside, the surrounding presence is not felt at all. That is the point. The felt sweetness is real, but it is the smaller thing. The greater thing is over your head, and you know it only by faith and by how you act in the room. Where the picture breaks: "surround" sounds like something far outside you, but it holds you closer than anything inside.

**The movement.** From measuring holiness by what you feel, and so treating the room lightly when you feel nothing, to knowing that the greatest presence is the one you cannot feel, and acting like it. What carries a person across: gathering with others in learning and prayer, and guarding speech in the place where ten gather.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Surrounded, head to foot, whether I feel it or not." 2. Picture the room where ten gather to pray. Picture each face. Over all of them, a light no one feels, as real as the floor. 3. Then picture one angel in that room, melting away. 4. The picture breaks: nothing touches your head; you are held all the same. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "You are here, over us." 6. The next time you are in a minyan or a class, keep your mouth from idle talk for that whole time.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when being with others in prayer carries a hush you did not make, and you stop needing to feel moved to know He is there. The fake is chasing a group high, the warm buzz of the crowd, and calling that the Shechinah.

## Letter 24 · The King's hour

**The state.** Prayer as the time the King shows Himself, wrapped in the words of the ordinary prayer book, open to each person by his own understanding. The state is standing there wanting to look; its opposite is standing there busy with your own affairs, which the letter calls the folly of not wanting to see.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "Beloved brothers, do not do evil." Let no one make himself wicked for even one hour before G-d, the hour He chose from the whole day for His people to gather and stand before Him. It is a time of favor, a time when He shows Himself. It is the time to come into the "small sanctuary," the shul, to visit the Shechinah that "dwells with them even in their impurity," and to be found by those who seek Him.

One who talks about his own needs then shows that he does not want to look, and to see the revealed glory of the King. He becomes a chariot for the "higher fool," of whom it says, "A fool does not desire understanding." He does not want to contemplate the glory of the King of kings, which is revealed above at that hour, and below too, to those who want to look: the King wraps and clothes Himself in the words of the prayer, the set prayer in everyone's mouth, and He shows Himself to each person by his understanding and the root of his soul. "A man is praised according to his understanding."

The picture: "The kingdom of heaven is like the kingdom of earth." A king usually hides his might in inner rooms, with guards at every door. People wait days and years to see him. One day he wants to show himself to all. He sends word through the whole kingdom: gather, stand before me, see the glory of my kingdom. If a man stands there and does not care to look, and busies himself with his own things, how foolish he is. He is like a beast in everyone's eyes. And he shames the king, showing that seeing the king's beauty means less to him than his business. He is guilty for showing that shame in public.

So the sages set the rule: pray "as if standing before the king." Even a fool who has no such thought can at least show it in his deeds and words, for every eye to see. All the prayers were set up for this, for one who looks into them well.

Then a decree. He takes on himself the sages' mission and forbids idle talk from when the leader starts the prayer until the final Kaddish, morning, afternoon and evening. Whoever breaks it on purpose should sit on the ground and ask three people to release him from the heavenly ban. Then "he returns and is healed," and no ban ever fell on him, because it only falls on rebels who do not care to seek forgiveness. It does not apply to one who forgot, or to a few words that slipped out without intent. G-d tests hearts.

From inside, this is the difference between being present and being in the room. The words in the book are the same for everyone. For one person they are paper. For another they are the robe of the King, and he is wrapped in them. Where the picture breaks: a king shows himself once and goes back behind doors; this King shows Himself three times a day, and the doors are the words themselves.

**The movement.** From standing in prayer busy with your own needs and talk, to wanting to see, and then to seeing Him wrapped in the words, as much as your understanding holds. What carries a person across: silence from idle speech, and looking into each word of the prayer.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "He is wrapped in these words." 2. Take one line of the daily prayer, like "Blessed are You." Read it slowly. The King who hides behind many doors is inside these very letters, now, for you. 3. Think of the subjects who waited years. You have waited since this morning. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see a face. Stop there. Want to see, and that is enough. 5. Say to Him: "I came to look at You." 6. At your next prayer, keep completely silent from the start until the final Kaddish.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the prayer book stops feeling like a list and your own business drops away on its own. The fake is a show of intensity for others to see, or a rule of silence kept with a mind full of your own affairs.

## Letter 25 · His breath is in the one who hurts you

**The state.** Anger (kaas) seen as a moment when emunah (faith) has left the mind, and its cure: seeing that the one who hurts you is alive this very second only by G-d's breath in him. And a second state: praying so deeply that a noise meant to break your prayer drives you further in.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter explains a saying from a collection of the Baal Shem Tov's teachings, and starts with the sages' words: "Whoever is angry is as if he served idols." Why? Because in the moment of anger, faith has left him. If he believed this came to him from G-d, he would not be angry at all.

It is true that a person with free choice who curses you, strikes you or damages your property is guilty by human and heavenly law for his bad choice. Still, what happened to you was already decreed in heaven, and "the One has many messengers."

More than that. At the very moment he strikes or curses you, the power of G-d and the breath of His mouth are clothed in him, keeping him alive. "For G-d told him, curse." When did G-d tell Shimi to curse David? The thought that fell into Shimi's heart and mind came down from G-d, and the breath of His mouth, "which gives life to all their host," gave life to Shimi's spirit while he spoke those words. If the breath of His mouth had left Shimi for one moment, he could not have said a thing.

The Baal Shem Tov taught on "Forever, G-d, Your word stands in the heavens": the letters of "Let there be a firmament" stand clothed in the heavens forever, keeping them in being. Not like the philosophers, who picture G-d as a smith: once the vessel is made it no longer needs his hands. A smith makes something from something; he only changes its shape. Heaven and earth are something from nothing. That is a greater wonder than the splitting of the sea. There the wind blew all night, and had it stopped for a moment the water would have run back down. How much more with something from nothing: if the Creator's power left it, the thing would go back to nothing. So even the dust and stones of the earth live by "the word of G-d," and the Arizal taught that even stones, earth and water have a kind of soul.

This word of G-d is called the Shechinah, because it dwells in creatures to give them life. (The letter here spends a long aside on the levels of this word through the worlds, and on how, outside the Land of Israel, life reaches the nations through heavenly "princes," with only a spark of the word shining on them from above; that this life is in exile in them, which is why their gods are called "other gods"; that the souls of the nations come from the husks, "the ten crowns of impurity," and draw only from the "back" of holiness; and that this is "the exile of the Shechinah," until "I will turn to the peoples a clear language, that all may call on the name of G-d." It notes that a person's power of speech mirrors the higher speech, which is why words of Torah and the Shema must be said aloud. I have compressed all of this.)

Then back to anger. All this is about worldly matters, where "everything is in the hands of Heaven." In matters of Heaven, to keep someone from sin, anger can be right. "Moshe was angry," because G-d put this mitzvah in front of him. But that is when you can protest. When you cannot, say a stranger talking loudly and confusing you in your prayer, then why did G-d do this? Only so that you would strengthen yourself in prayer, from the depth of the heart, with such intent that you no longer hear him.

That takes a great stirring. The advice for it comes from the thing itself. Think how the Shechinah came down, as it were, so that a spark of her light is clothed in exile, in general in the husks to keep them alive, and now in particular in this very man's speech that disturbs your prayer. "Man rules over man to his harm": through it a person is stirred to pray from the depth of his heart until he does not hear. The compiler wrote that the Shechinah "rests" there. He did not have the exact word. The Baal Shem Tov taught in Yiddish and meant "is clothed," in exile.

The Alter Rebbe then defends the teaching. Those who object do not really object to a word. They object to the idea that the Shechinah is clothed in the husks. But nothing is more physical than dust, and still the lowest level of the word of G-d is clothed in it. And this is not hidden Kabbalah. It is among "the revealed things," simple faith in a plain verse: "Do I not fill heaven and earth, says G-d." It is the simple faith of Jews, who walked wholeheartedly with G-d without trying to work out with human reason how He fills the earth. He cannot explain more in a letter. Send one of your own, he says, and I will speak to him mouth to mouth.

From inside: the anger has heat because the other person seems to stand alone against you. When you see His breath in him, the other person loses that hard separateness. The hurt is still real and the man is still guilty. But the heat has nowhere to land. Where the picture breaks: breath in a man sounds like approval of what he says; it is not approval. It is life, lent for the sake of a test.

**The movement.** From anger, where the world has an enemy in it and G-d has left the picture, to faith, where even this man at this moment is held by His breath, and from being knocked out of prayer to being driven deeper into it. What carries a person across: thinking in detail about the word of G-d that keeps each thing in being, and in the moment itself, praying harder.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "His breath is in him right now." 2. Bring to mind one person who hurt you. See the words leave his mouth. Now see that he could not have said one word if the breath of G-d left him for one moment. 3. Go further: the ground under you, the wall, all of it is said by Him this second, like the sea held up by the wind. 4. The picture breaks: His breath in the man is life, not approval. Stop at the edge. You cannot know why it came. 5. Say to Him: "This too comes from You. Show me what to do with it." 6. Today, when someone angers you, say the line inside before you answer. If noise breaks into your prayer, pray one line from deeper.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when anger cools without your pushing it down, and you can still act wisely, even firmly. The fake is swallowing anger and calling it faith, or "it's all from G-d" used to excuse the one who did wrong, or to stop protecting someone.

## Letter 26 · Bound and unbound

**The state.** Knowing that every permitted thing you eat or use waits to be lifted, and every forbidden thing is "bound" where it is; and knowing that clear, careful thinking about what is permitted and forbidden is itself the freeing of holy sparks. It is the state of birur (refining): living among mixed things and sorting them by wisdom.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter opens with a hard passage from the Zohar (the section called Raaya Mehemna). It seems to say that in the time of redemption Israel will taste of the Tree of Life, the Zohar's inner teaching, and that "the Tree of Good and Evil, which is forbidden and permitted, impure and pure," will no longer rule over them. At first glance this says that learning the laws of forbidden and permitted belongs to the Tree of Good and Evil.

That cannot be, the letter says, and it piles up proofs. The whole revealed Torah is called "a Tree of Life to those who hold it." The Kabbalah was hidden in the early generations. Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai himself learned the Mishnah in the cave for years. Since the Temple was destroyed, G-d has in His world "only the four cubits of halachah." In the days of Moshiach people will still need the laws of slaughter, of forbidden foods, of impurity from the dead and from birth. So what does the Zohar mean?

Look closely at its words. It does not say "the laws of forbidden and permitted." It says the Tree of Good and Evil "is forbidden and permitted." It means the things themselves. The forbidden object and the permitted object come from the Tree of Good and Evil, which is kelipas nogah (the "glowing husk," the mixed layer of the world where good and bad are tangled).

Here is the state. A forbidden thing is called asur, "bound." A husk rests on it and holds it, and it cannot rise. A permitted thing is mutar, "untied." It is not bound, and it can rise through a person who eats it for G-d's sake. Even plainly: whoever serves G-d, and with the strength of that food learns and prays, turns what was refined out of that food into letters of Torah and prayer rising to G-d. On weekdays this is so. On Shabbat the glowing husk itself rises, so it is a mitzvah to eat fine things and to have meat and wine, which on a weekday would be called gluttony. A forbidden thing cannot rise, not on Shabbat and not on a weekday, even if you learn and pray with its strength, unless it was eaten to save a life, and then it has become truly permitted.

But learning the laws, even the laws of forbidden and permitted, is the body of the Oral Torah itself, which is the kingship of the highest world, where "He and His vessels are one." (The letter maps Scripture, Mishnah and Talmud to the worlds, and I have folded that away.)

Why does the Zohar call it the Tree of Good and Evil at all? When that highest kingship clothes itself in the glowing husk, to refine the sparks that fell in Adam's sin and in the breaking of the vessels, it is called the tree of knowledge of good and evil. That is the exile of the Shechinah. In that time the main work of a person, and the main work of Torah and mitzvos, is to refine the sparks.

So the heart of learning now is careful analysis of the law, sorting permitted from forbidden, pure from impure, with wisdom, understanding and knowledge. "In wisdom they were refined." When you work through a law until it is clear, you lift it out of the husks that hid it, so that it was unknown or not well understood. The question that confuses it comes "from the side of evil." And the higher beings cannot do this. Only the lower ones can, because they are clothed in a physical body from that same husk, and they wear its strength down by breaking their cravings. That is why the higher beings come to hear new Torah from below.

Every person can uncover hidden wisdom and bring out a new thought in law or story, revealed or hidden, by the root of his soul, and he must, to complete his soul by lifting the sparks that fell to his share. Every word of Torah, most of all a law, is a spark of the Shechinah: "the word of G-d, this is halachah." So "whoever learns Torah, G-d says, it is as if he redeemed Me and My children from among the nations."

When the refining is complete and the good has left the husk, the work will change: not refining any more, but joining higher unions through the inner Torah. The revealed laws will be known to all without forgetting. (The letter says the "mixed multitude" will still need Mishnah, to weaken the other side in them. It ends by explaining the Zohar's whole passage this way.)

From inside, this is how an ordinary day looks with open eyes. The bread on your table is waiting. What you do with its strength decides where it goes. A clear line of law in your head is not dry. It is a freed spark. Where the picture breaks: "bound" sounds like the thing is bad in itself; the letter means it is held where it is, for now, not that it is outside His world.

**The movement.** From eating and learning as private matters to eating as lifting and learning as freeing, and from feeling the husk as something only outside to meeting it in your own cravings and wearing it down. What carries a person across: eating for His sake, and the hard, clear work of understanding a law.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "May this become words of prayer." 2. Look at your next meal. It came up through the mixed world. With its strength you will speak and think today. Picture those words. 3. Then take one law you keep. Think it through until it is clear, not foggy. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see a spark rise. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Let what I eat serve You." 6. Eat one meal today as a person who will pray with its strength, and learn one law until it is clear.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when food and learning start to feel like parts of one service, and a clear law brings a quiet joy. The fake is using "raising sparks" to excuse overeating, or treating learning as a cold contest of cleverness.

## Letter 27 · A teacher's life goes on in you

**The state.** Grief for a righteous teacher that becomes a bond. The tzaddik's real life was emunah, yirah and ahavah (faith, awe and love), and after his passing these are free of the body, so anyone who was truly close can take a part of them in. It is the state of receiving: love, preparation and toil to carry what he carried.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter was sent to the Chassidim in the Land of Israel to comfort them on the passing of Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk. He comes "to speak to the heart of the crushed," and to explain a saying of the sages: the tzaddik "left life to all the living."

"The righteous lives by his faith." "The fear of G-d is for life." And the fire of his love is more than life. These were the life of his spirit all his days. When G-d took his spirit up, rising and rising, he left behind the life of his spirit, his work in Israel, "the work of the righteous for life." It is bound to the souls of all who loved him with cords of great love. Each one has a share of it by how truly he was bound to him, "for as water reflects a face," and "spirit draws spirit." His spirit stands right inside us, when we walk in the straight path he taught.

The Zohar says a righteous man who has passed "is found in all the worlds more than in his life." In the higher worlds that is easy to see. How in this world? The letter explains.

The life of a tzaddik is not the life of flesh. It is spiritual life: faith, awe and love. While he lived on earth, these three were inside a vessel and a garment, in a physical place: his soul bound to his body. His students received only the light that shone out from that vessel through his holy words and thoughts. That is why the sages said a student does not grasp his teacher's mind for forty years.

After his passing, the part that stays in the grave separates from the spirit in Gan Eden, which is these three qualities. Because the spirit is no longer in a vessel or a physical place, everyone close to him can receive a part of his very spirit: his faith, his awe and his love with which he served G-d, not only their glow. The part of him that stays in the lower Gan Eden, even as he rises, spreads into his students, each by how bound and close he was to him in his life and in his death, with great love. Every drawing down of spirit comes only through great love. "The spirit of the heart's longing draws spirit from above." But only if a person prepares himself with great preparation and toil to receive these three, the way his teacher taught him. "You toiled and found: believe it."

There is another light too. It does not come inside the students' minds. It shines on them from above. When his soul rose to its source, all his learning and service were sown there, and very high lights grew from them. That light shines on all who became servants of G-d through his teaching. It puts thoughts of return and good deeds into their hearts. All the good deeds born of it are "growths of growths." It is very hidden, like the sun shining to the stars from under the earth. So Moshe's light spreads, after his passing, into every generation.

In worldly matters too, "the righteous protect the world more in their death than in their lives."

From inside, grief here does not end. It changes direction. At first it looks back, at the man you cannot see. Then it looks in: his faith, his awe and his love are no longer behind a wall of body, and you can take them in. That needs work. The letter is plain that it comes only through love and toil. Where the picture breaks: the sun under the earth is gone from sight; his light is hidden, not gone, and it is not in the sky but in your deeds.

**The movement.** From loss, where the teacher is gone and his light with him, to receiving, where his very faith, awe and love can enter you, more freely than in his lifetime. What carries a person across: great love for him, and the toil of living as he taught.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Their life was faith, awe and love, and it is not gone." 2. Bring to mind one teacher, parent or righteous person who has passed. Name one thing they believed with their whole heart, one thing they stood in awe of, one thing they loved. 3. Think: these were their real life, and they are no longer held in a body. 4. The picture breaks: you will not feel them come in. Stop there. Prepare anyway. 5. Say to Him: "Let their faith, awe and love live in me." 6. Today do one thing the way they would have done it.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when grief turns into a quiet strength to live as they lived, and you catch yourself doing their good deeds. The fake is holding on to their memory as a shrine while nothing in your life changes.

## Letter 28 · Hidden toil comes to light

**The state.** The inner work of a life, given over in secret day by day, most of all in the moments of mesirus nefesh (giving yourself over) in the Shema, which stays hidden while you live and shines openly when you leave. It is also the state of a mourner who learns that a righteous death is a time of will that atones.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter was written to Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev to comfort him on the passing of his son, Rabbi Meir.

"Why is Miriam's death placed next to the red heifer? To tell you: as the heifer atones, so the death of the righteous atones." But why next to the red heifer, which is burned outside the camp, and not next to the sin offering brought on the altar, which is atonement itself?

The Zohar and the Arizal explain the offerings on the altar. They lift the animal soul, which is from the glowing husk, the mixed layer, back to its root in the holy beings who carry G-d's throne, "the face of the ox, the face of the eagle." In return, a flow comes down from the "Man on the throne." That kind of lifting reaches only as far as the animal soul reaches. So offerings atone only for mistakes, which come from the animal soul getting the upper hand.

The red heifer is different. Cedar, hyssop and living water are put with its ashes, and the water is then called "sanctified." That holiness comes from the highest and most hidden wisdom, where "darkness is turned into light": the place where the broken world was set right. So it purifies even the impurity of death, the strongest impurity there is, lower than the mixed layer.

Now the passing of the righteous. There is a light above called "keeping kindness" (notzer chesed). The letters of notzer are the letters of ratzon, "will." It is a time of will, revealed and shining from above to below, at the passing of the highest righteous: those who served G-d with love and gave their souls over to Him in their lifetime, evening and morning, in the Shema. By that giving over they lifted something up each day, and something came down from "keeping kindness." For all of a person's toil that his soul worked at in his life, above, in a hidden and concealed way, is revealed and shines openly, from above to below, at the time of his passing.

Through that revealed light, "the kindness of G-d is forever upon those who fear Him," and He "works salvation in the midst of the earth," to atone for the sins of the generation, even the willful ones, which come from the three husks below the mixed layer. Because that light comes from the most hidden wisdom, the source of all refining, where the darkness of the broken world is turned into the light of the repaired one.

So the passing of the righteous is placed beside the red heifer.

From inside, this letter speaks to two people. To the one who serves: your giving over in the Shema each morning and night, the moment you say "One" and mean your whole self, is hidden. You feel little and see nothing. It is not lost. It is stored above and it will shine. To the one who mourns: the death you are grieving is not only a loss. It is a time of will. What he worked at in secret all his life is shining now, for the good of the whole generation. The letter does not tell the father not to grieve. It tells him where the grief sits. Where the picture breaks: a heifer is burned, a thing used; a righteous soul is not used, it rises, and the light that comes down is its own.

**The movement.** From toil that seems to vanish, and death that seems only to take away, to toil stored and revealed, and death as a time of will that brings kindness down. What carries a person across: giving yourself over each day in the Shema, and in grief, knowing what the passing of the righteous does.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Hidden toil is revealed." 2. Think of the moment in the Shema when you say "One." Picture giving yourself over there, fully, if only for a breath of time. That moment is stored. 3. Then think of someone righteous who has passed. All their hidden work shines now. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see the light come down. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Take what I give in secret." 6. Say the Shema tonight slowly, and at "One" give yourself over, and tell no one.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you stop needing your inner work to show, and grief carries a thread of peace. The fake is dramatic talk of "giving everything" that never reaches the plain daily Shema, or using "it was a time of will" to hurry a mourner past his pain.

## Letter 29 · A garment to bear the light

**The state.** The state of a soul before a light too sweet for it to bear, and the garment that lets it look without going out: the mitzvos, which are His will dressed in deeds, and the laws of the Oral Torah, which make that will plain. It is the state of being clothed in His ratzon (will), and through it, near His delight.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "A woman of valor is her husband's crown." The sages said that "one who uses the crown" means one who uses a person who learns halachos, the laws, "the crown of the Torah." And "whoever learns laws each day is promised the World to Come." Why are the laws called a crown? Why laws, and not other Torah? Why does a single chapter in the morning fulfill a person's duty to learn?

The Arizal taught that every soul must come again and again until it has kept all 613 mitzvos in thought, speech and deed, to complete the garments of the soul, so that no garment is missing. Why does a soul need garments at all?

Because the soul is created, and no created thing can grasp anything of the Creator, the Infinite. Even after He shone His light down through many levels, with great narrowings and many garments, which the Zohar calls "hairs," the soul still could not bear the light. The light is too good and too sweet. "To gaze upon the pleasantness of G-d." "Then you will delight in G-d." "He will satisfy your soul with brightness." The soul has no strength to take in that sweetness without leaving its sheath and ceasing to be, like a candle in a torch.

Here is the first state, and it is a strange one to picture: a sweetness so great that meeting it without a covering would end you. Not a pain. A delight you are too small for.

So from that light itself a little comes down, through many narrowings, until a garment is made from it, something of the same kind as the light, to clothe the soul. Through that garment the soul can enjoy the light and grasp it and not be undone. The picture is looking at the sun through clear glass, or Moshe, who "went into the cloud" and went up, clothed in the cloud, and saw through it.

The garment comes from the outermost part of that hidden light, its "back." What is that outer part? Look at a person. His soul has the power of delight: the pleasure of a new idea, for example. The outer side of delight is will. You want what you want because it is not pain, and pain is the opposite of delight. When a person grasps a new, wonderful idea, in that moment a wonderful delight is born in his mind. So delight is higher than the mind, only clothed in it. In the same way, as it were, His will is the outer side of His delight. (The letter is careful: He and His will are one, nothing like a person's will. The Torah speaks in human language so the ear can hear.)

His will is called keser (the crown), which sits around the head, above wisdom. And His will is clothed in the 613 mitzvos and the seven rabbinic ones, nearly all of them deeds. Even the mitzvos of speech count as deeds, since "moving the lips is a deed." Even the mitzvos of the heart were given only to a person in a body, who has choice. A soul without a body needs no command to love.

So the mitzvos are like pillars. They stand from the highest height, His will, down to this physical earth. And they are hollow pillars. When a person does a mitzvah they surround and clothe his soul, and through them his soul rises all the way up, to be "bound in the bundle of life with G-d": clothed in the light of the crown, which is His will. Through that garment the soul can gaze on "the pleasantness of G-d," which is higher than the crown, His inwardness.

His will is hidden in the Written Torah and revealed only in the Oral Torah. "Bind them as a sign on your hand, and as totafos between your eyes." What to bind? What are totafos? Only the Oral Torah says: one box on the arm, four on the head, square, of black leather straps, on the upper arm, above the hairline. "Do no work" on Shabbat. What is work? The Oral Torah says: the thirty nine kinds. So "do not forsake your mother's Torah": as a mother brings out into a whole child what was hidden in the father's seed, so the Oral Torah brings every mitzvah out from hiding. The Oral Torah is the "woman of valor," who gives birth to countless "worlds," the laws without number. They reveal His will, which is above wisdom like a crown above the head. That is why the laws are called the crown of the Torah, and why one who learns them is promised the World to Come: his soul is clothed in His will.

From inside, a mitzvah done in this light does not feel like an item on a list. It feels like stepping into a pillar that reaches up past sight. Learning a clear law feels like holding His will in your hands. Where the picture breaks: a pillar holds the roof up; these pillars hold you up, and they are hollow so you can be inside them.

**The movement.** From a soul that wants the light bare, and would be undone by it, to a soul clothed in His will through deeds and laws, able to stand near His delight. What carries a person across: doing the mitzvos in their exact detail, and learning the laws daily.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "A pillar from His will down to my floor." 2. Take one mitzvah you do with your hands. Picture it as a hollow pillar standing on your floor and rising past every sky. 3. Then learn one detail of it you did not know. That detail is His will made plain. 4. The picture breaks: you will not feel yourself rise. Stop there. The sweetness above is not for grabbing now. 5. Say to Him: "Clothe me in Your will." 6. Learn one law today, and do that mitzvah with that detail.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when small details of a mitzvah start to feel precious, like the hem of a robe, and you want them right. The fake is fussiness about details that has forgotten whose will they are, or skipping the details in the name of "the spirit of the thing."

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 29 (line 1494): וְדֶרֶךְ עַמּוּדִים אֵלּוּ עוֹלִין הַנֶּפֶשׁ רוּחַ נְשָׁמָה שֶׁלּוֹ עַד רוּם הַמַּעֲלוֹת*

## Letter 30 · Where the endless meets the counted

**The state.** Faithful giving that does not shrink: the G-d fearing heart that will not give less this year than last, and that knows each coin is where the endless joins the counted. The state is shalom (peace) in its root sense: being the joint between two opposite ends.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The sages said: "Whoever is used to coming to the synagogue, and one day does not come, the Holy One asks about him." You are missed. It is a gentle, strong thought. Your habit of goodness is not only yours. It has a place, and when it is empty, He notices.

So with every mitzvah, and most of all charity, which weighs as much as all the others. Maybe you never vowed to give. Still, anyone whose heart the fear of G-d has touched will find that his G-dly soul does not allow him to give less to holiness than he used to give every year. That giving revives "the spirit of the lowly and crushed, who have nothing of their own." It is "the fallen booth of David," to raise it up, "to be one with the One."

"Everything follows the number of the deeds." And the sages said, "each penny adds up to a great account." The letter reads this with another saying: when is "G-d great"? "In the city of our G-d." That is the place of the account, of numbers, as it says "your eyes are pools in Cheshbon" (a place whose name means "account").

What is meant is this. A stirring below, drawing life, grace and kindness to another in the act of charity, with good will and a kind face, brings a stirring above. "May G-d shine His face." Grace, kindness and higher will are drawn from the Life of all life, whose greatness has no end and cannot be grasped at all, into "Your kingship, the kingship of all worlds": the revealed world, which gives life to every creature in every chamber above and below, all of them in number and count. "A thousand thousands serve Him."

That is the "great account." Through many acts of charity comes "peace." Peace means something that joins and mediates between two opposite ends. One end is the height of heaven, "His greatness has no search," without limit. The other is the lower end, which clothes itself in the worlds of limit and number. The coin is the joint.

From inside: you hold a small, counted thing, a coin or a bill. You hand it over with a kind face. In that plain moment two things that cannot touch are touching through you: the endless and the numbered. The heart that does this again and again does not feel grand. It feels steady. Where the picture breaks: a joint in the body joins two bones of the same kind; here the two ends have nothing in common, and still they meet.

**The movement.** From giving when moved, and letting it shrink when times are hard, to faithful giving that keeps its place and grows by count. What carries a person across: the fear of G-d that touches the heart, and the many small acts, each with a kind face.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "My coin is where the endless meets the counted." 2. Hold a real coin. It has a number on it. Think of His greatness, which has no number at all. 3. Picture the person who has nothing of their own, and the coin reaching them, and His face shining in that. 4. The picture breaks: you cannot see how the two ends touch. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Let me be a place where You meet the world." 6. Give today no less than you gave on the same day last year, and give it with a kind face.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when your giving feels steady rather than dramatic, and you would feel the gap if you skipped it. The fake is counting your gifts to feel good about the total, or a big gesture that is then forgotten.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 30 (line 1510): הוּא דָּבָר הַמְחַבֵּר וּמְתַוֵּוךְ ב׳ קְצָווֹת הָפְכִיִּים*

## Letter 31 · The blood stops moving

**The state.** Pirud halevavos (the heart's separation from other hearts) felt as sickness in a body, and its opposite: souls bound together so that life goes round from the heart to the limbs and back. The state is knowing that your quarrel with another person blocks the flow of life for everyone.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Tikkunim say, "The Shechinah is sick in exile," as it were. The letter explains with a picture of the body.

What makes a body healthy or sick? Life spreads from the heart to all the limbs, clothed in the blood. It goes round and round, into every limb and every vein, and comes back to the heart. If this going round is as it should be, steady, in the order set for it by the Life of all life, the person is fully healthy, because all the limbs are bound together and each receives the life it needs from the heart. If there is a blockage somewhere that stops or slows the blood, then the bond joining all the limbs to the heart is cut or weakened, and the person falls ill.

In the same way, all souls are called "the limbs of the Shechinah," and the Shechinah is called "heart": "the Rock of my heart." The word Shechinah means that G-d's light dwells in the worlds to give them life. That life reaches creation first by clothing itself in souls. Created things have no likeness at all to the Creator; before Him they are as nothing, and they cannot take in His light except through souls, which came first in His thought, before the worlds were made by speech.

All flow from higher to lower goes like the book of Creation says: "their end is lodged in their beginning and their beginning in their end." The Arizal calls it direct light and returning light. "The living beings ran and returned." It goes out and comes back.

So the Shechinah is called "heart" and souls "limbs" to teach us this: when all the souls cling together and are bound to each other, then the flow of life goes round and round, and the end is lodged in the beginning, to bind them all to the One G-d and cling to Him. "You are standing today, all of you, before G-d": "all of you," exactly, and "before," exactly, "your heads, ... your woodchoppers." All of you together.

This explains what the sages said: the Second Temple was destroyed, Israel fell into exile, and the Shechinah left and went down into exile, all because of baseless hatred and the separation of hearts. That is why she is called "sick." "He supports the fallen and heals the sick": "sick" in the plural, meaning all the limbs.

From inside: a grudge feels private. It sits in your chest and seems to hurt only you and the other person. The letter says a grudge is a clot. It blocks the blood of the whole body. When you let it go and are joined again, it is not only a feeling of relief. Life starts moving in places you will never see. Where the picture breaks: in a body the limbs cannot choose to cut themselves off; here they can, and they can choose to join again.

**The movement.** From hearts separated, each kept apart by a grudge, a judgment, a coldness, to hearts bound together so that life flows round. What carries a person across: letting go of baseless hatred, and joining yourself to the others before G-d, "all of you."

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "When we are joined, the life goes round." 2. Picture a body with a blocked vein, and the limb below it going cold. Then think of one person you are cold toward. 3. See that coldness as a clot, not only in you but in the one body of all souls. 4. The picture breaks: you cannot see the flow start again. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Heal the sick, all of us together." 6. Today, take one step toward that person: a greeting, a kind word, a favor.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you feel another person's good as your own life going round, and a grudge feels like an illness you want gone. The fake is a warm group feeling that still keeps one person out, or peace made on the surface while the heart stays closed.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 31 (line 1518): כִּי כַּאֲשֶׁר כָּל הַנְּשָׁמוֹת דְּבוּקוֹת וּמְקוּשָּׁרוֹת יַחַד*

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this reading applies it to every person.*

## Letter 32 · A wick for the light to come

**The state.** Chesed (kindness) and nedivus halev (a generous heart), understood as the wick and the body that the endless light will need to shine in this physical world. Two states: charity that receives, kept and hidden until the resurrection; and kindness that wakes in the heart by itself, from love of G-d in the Shema, and leads His face down.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter blesses those who give, and those who move others to give. "His charity stands forever." The word "stands" is feminine. The charity receives, from the stirring of a pure heart, and it still stands forever.

It means this. All the charity and kindness people do in this world from the generosity of a pure heart live and last in this physical world until the resurrection. Then the G-dliness and the Infinite Light that surrounds all worlds will be revealed in this world.

But that light needs a vessel to clothe itself in, as the soul needs a body. "Is not My word like fire?" "Fire gives light in this world only when it takes hold of a wick and is clothed in it." The body and vessel for His light is the quality of kindness and a generous heart: giving life to one who has nothing. The Tikkunim say kindness is "the right arm," and the whole body is included in the right. As the poet said, "His garment is charity."

So the sages said, "Charity is repaid only by the kindness in it": "Sow for yourselves by charity, reap by kindness." Harvest reveals the seed hidden in the earth. So the charity and kindness done in exile are hidden and covered until the resurrection, when the Infinite Light will dress itself and shine in this physical world, at a level higher even than the highest world.

That is why this charity is called tzedakah, a feminine word: "his charity stands forever." It receives the light of the Infinite that surrounds all worlds, which will clothe itself in it in this physical world at the time of the resurrection.

But "tzedek walks before Him" uses a masculine word. That is the kindness that wakes in a person's heart by itself, through the stirring of love of G-d in the Shema, wanting to cling to Him and give his soul over in "One," and "with all your might," which plainly means with your money. And through that stirring below, "as water reflects a face, so the heart of man" to the Man above, comes a stirring above: the drawing of the Infinite Light that surrounds all worlds down into this physical world, openly, at the time of the resurrection. That is "walks before Him": it leads and draws the Face above, from beyond the highest world, down into the world of doing.

And here, he says, is a time to be brief.

From inside, there are two ways of giving and both are holy. In one you are moved by someone else, by the poor man, by the collector, by the one "great of deed" who stirs you. Your charity receives. In the other, the kindness rises in you on its own, out of love that woke in the Shema. That one walks ahead. Either way, the coin is a wick. You will not see it lit. It is being kept. Where the picture breaks: a wick burns up in the flame; this wick is kept whole until the light comes, and then it shines without being consumed.

**The movement.** From giving as a loss, something that leaves your hand and is gone, to giving as preparing a wick, and from giving because you were asked to giving that rises from love in the Shema. What carries a person across: saying the Shema with love until "with all your might" reaches the pocket, and the act of kindness itself.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "A wick for the light to come." 2. Picture a candle with no wick: fire has nothing to hold. Then picture your kindness this week, each act a wick waiting in the dark. 3. In the Shema, at "with all your might," think plainly: my money too. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see the flame. It is kept. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Let my kindness hold Your light." 6. Today give one gift that no one asked you for, that rose in you on its own.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when giving feels like planting, not losing, and kindness starts to rise in you before anyone asks. The fake is giving for a sure reward, or a glow of generosity that never becomes a real gift.

## Kuntres Acharon (the last notes)

The Kuntres Acharon is a set of short, dense essays. The first goes back over chapter 40 of the first part of the book; the rest work through passages of the Zohar and the Arizal. They are full of Kabbalah, and each one below gives its point plainly and says where it compresses. Almost all of them ask one question from different sides: why do the mouth, the hand and the deed reach where the finest feeling cannot?

## Kuntres Acharon 1 · Thought rises, the mouth draws down

**The state.** Two directions of the inner life. Haalaah (raising): the soul going up, which needs thought with love and awe. Hamshachah (drawing down): light coming down into this world, which needs the spoken word and the deed. The state is knowing that your soul came down for the second, and that silent thought, however lovely, does not draw anything down.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The note asks: how is a person who reads the stories of the Torah bound to the highest wisdom? (It goes back to chapter 40 of the first part.)

The Arizal's writings say that as a person acts below, so his "image," the higher man that is the root of his soul, acts above. That is true even of thinking over written letters. But speech goes further. The spoken word breaks through and rises: to the highest world, or to the world of understanding if it was said with love and awe born of the mind, or to the world of feeling if with natural love and awe. Even plain reading of Scripture, without these, rises from this world to the ten levels of the world of doing, because speech breaks through the air. Thought alone does not. It stays with the image, the root of the soul.

The Zohar says "thought does nothing," even for good. The note explains: thought does not stir anything above so that it flows down below. The thought stays there. It even adds great light up there. But the main union is above, and the "fruits" in this world come only by drawing a little light down through speech and deed. With thought alone, nothing is drawn down. That is why a person does not fulfill his duty of Shema or blessing by thinking it. His soul came down into this world only to draw the higher lights down.

The other direction is different. To raise something from below to above needs good thought. Without love and awe it does not fly up. And where the Zohar says even words without love and awe "break through the firmaments," it means the outer halls and houses, not the body of the higher Man itself, and certainly not its soul. "Without love and awe it cannot rise and stand before G-d."

From inside, this is a correction to a very natural feeling. The inner life feels most real in silent thought, where you are closest to yourself. The note says: that is where you rise. But you did not come down to rise. You came down to bring light into the room. For that, open your mouth. Say the words. Do the act. Where the picture breaks: "up" and "down" sound like places; they are two ways the one light moves, and you are the hinge.

**The movement.** From a silent inner life that feels complete, to speech and deed that bring the light down, with thought and love carrying you up. What carries a person across: saying the words of Torah and prayer aloud, and meaning them.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "My soul came down to draw light down." 2. Think one verse silently. Then say it aloud, slowly. Now the words are in the world, not only in you. 3. Then think of the love behind it. That love is what lifts it. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see the light come down. Stop there. 5. Say to Him, aloud: "I came here for You." 6. Say your blessings today with your lips, not only in your head.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when speaking the words of prayer starts to feel weightier than thinking them, and you want both love and lips. The fake is loud recital with no heart, or a "deep" silent inner life that looks down on the plain spoken word.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 1 (line 1536): מַה שֶּׁיָּרְדָה נִשְׁמָתוֹ לָעוֹלָם הַזֶּה רַק לְהַמְשִׁיךְ אוֹרוֹת עֶלְיוֹנִים לְמַטָּה*

## Kuntres Acharon 2 · The deed turns the face

**The state.** Panim bepanim (face to face): the state in which G-d turns to the world fully, which comes only through mitzvos done in action. And its opposite, a spiritual life that "wants to nurse from its mother and not give below": taking in light for itself without bringing it into the world.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Arizal writes that the higher levels turn face to face again only through mitzvos done in deed. Why deeds exactly?

Because first there must be a raising from below. (The note uses the Kabbalah's term mayin nukvin, "lower waters," for the stirring that rises from below and calls down a flow from above.) And that raising, at the lowest level, is the level of doing.

Good deeds are called "cutting away the thorns" that cling to the "back" of holiness. The thorns are the husks that feed on the outer side of holiness, the level of doing. When a person does a mitzvah with a physical thing, he lifts the good hidden in it back to its source, and the thorns fall away.

The Arizal also says Adam set things right through prayer. The note explains: that is through the letters of speech, for "moving the lips is a deed." Speech comes from the life soul in the body and its blood, which are rooted in the mixed layer of the world. So spoken prayer is a kind of deed.

The raisings from the level of doing go up from world to world. (The note traces this with names of G-d and gates of the Arizal. I have left it out.)

This explains why "thought does nothing." Without raising something up from the fallen parts in the mixed layer, you cannot draw down the flow that makes the higher union. Without it, the higher level "wants to nurse from its mother and not give below." It takes in and keeps.

Then a turn. The Zohar speaks of an order of "gazing" in prayer, the higher intentions and unions of those who know. For them it works, because their own souls are the "lower waters": they give their souls over for the Torah, and in the prayer of falling on the face.

From inside, the deed does something the heart alone cannot. You can feel very near G-d and still leave the world exactly as it was. The note calls that state nursing and not giving. The deed turns the face toward the world. The only other way is to give your whole self, which is itself a deed of the soul. Where the picture breaks: thorns are cut and thrown away; here the thorn falls off and the good that was inside it goes home.

**The movement.** From a spiritual life that takes in and keeps, to a life that turns the face toward the world through deeds, and from there, for those who can, to giving the self over. What carries a person across: the mitzvah done with a real, physical thing, and speech in prayer.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "The deed turns the face." 2. Think of one mitzvah you usually do only in feeling: love of a neighbor, thanks, kindness. Find the physical deed that would make it real. 3. See the good hidden in the object you use, going home as you use it for Him. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see a face turn. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Let me give, not only take in." 6. Do that deed today with your hands.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a plain act feels more real than a lovely mood, and you want your love to end in something done. The fake is busy doing with no inward turn at all, or a sweet inner glow that never leaves the chair.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 2 (line 1539): שֶׁאֵין הַחֲזָרַת פָּנִים בְּפָנִים כִּי אִם עַל יְדֵי מִצְוֹת מַעֲשִׂיּוֹת דַּוְקָא*

## Kuntres Acharon 3 · Breath without sin

**The state.** Learning Torah plainly, stam (simply), from the hidden natural love and not for any glory of your own, which rises like the breath of small children learning. Beside it, the state of ego in learning, which keeps the words "under the sun," and of prayer with stray thoughts, which can still be lifted.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Arizal writes that Torah learned without intention creates angels in the world of feeling. The Zohar says "no voice is lost except the voice of Torah and prayer, which rises and breaks through." Prayer with intention creates angels in a higher world. Prayer without intention is pushed down entirely. So what is the difference between Torah without intention and prayer without intention?

It is plain. When you learn Torah you understand and know what you learn. Otherwise it is not learning at all. Learning "without intention" means you learn simply, not for the sake of heaven out of an open love in the heart, but from the hidden, natural love. It does not mean learning not for its sake at all, to make yourself great. That kind does not rise "above the sun," because your thought and intent are clothed in the letters of speech and do not let them rise.

Prayer without intention, where the mind is full of strange thoughts, is also held down. But because its aim is for heaven, it can easily be fixed and rise again, when you pray even one prayer with intention. That one gathers up the prayers of the whole year.

(The note then settles which "firmament" each kind of prayer reaches in different passages of the Zohar, and says that even sins rise to certain halls, but the risings have nothing in common except the name. I have compressed this.)

So a disqualified prayer is better than Torah learned truly for one's own glory, which stays under the sun, while the prayer at least reaches a firmament. But plain Torah, learned from the hidden natural love, is no less than the breath of little schoolchildren. Their breath rises because it is breath without sin. It rises even if they learn not for its sake, only for fear of the teacher's strap. And the angels carry that breath of the children up to the highest world.

From inside, this is a mercy. Most learning is not full of open love. It is plain: you sit, you understand the words, you go on. The note says this plain learning is pure, like a child's breath, as long as your ego is not wrapped in the words. What holds words down is not dryness. It is self. Where the picture breaks: a child's breath is without sin because the child is small; ours is without sin when we make ourselves small.

**The movement.** From learning to be seen, or thinking plain learning is worthless because it is not on fire, to learning simply and cleanly, from the love that is already there. What carries a person across: removing the self from the words, and sometimes one prayer said with full intention that lifts the rest.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Breath without sin rises." 2. Picture a room of small children reading aloud, their breath in the air. Then picture your own learning today, plain and a little tired. 3. Ask: is there any self wrapped in these words? Set it down. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see the angels carry anything. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Take my plain words." 6. Learn a few lines today simply, with no one to see, and pray one prayer with your whole mind.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when plain learning feels clean and light, and you no longer need to feel on fire to know it counts. The fake is lazy learning excused as "simple," or learning to win the argument and calling it love.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 3 (line 1551): דְּסָלִיק לְעֵילָּא מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהוּא הֶבֶל שֶׁאֵין בּוֹ חֵטְא*

## Kuntres Acharon 4 · Holding what the mind cannot

**The state.** The difference between metzius (knowing that He is) and mahus (His very being). Love and awe from the mind touch only that He is; a mitzvah done with a physical thing, like shaking an etrog, holds the life of G-dliness itself. The state is humble and strong at once: my best understanding is outside, and my hand is inside.

**The chapter as consciousness.** This is the longest and densest note. I give its main line and fold away the maps.

The Arizal says that now, the main refining is done through prayer, even though Torah study is higher than prayer. Why? Torah and mitzvos add light in the highest world: Torah in its inner vessels, mitzvos in its outer ones. But prayer draws the Infinite Light down into the lower worlds themselves, not only clothed, but the light itself, to change creatures from what they are: so the sick are healed and rain falls. Torah and mitzvos do not change things that way. The tefillin parchment is not changed by being worn. To draw the light down that far needs a raising from below, and that raising in the mind and heart is "flames of fire" with no limit, called "with all your might," to wake the Infinite. So prayer is called "life of the hour" and Torah "eternal life."

Then the main point. Moshe begged to enter the Land to keep the mitzvos that can only be done there. For a mitzvah no one else can do, you stop learning Torah, even the deepest mysteries, and certainly stop praying. Why? Because the mitzvos done in deed, and their study, are far higher than love and awe born of the mind.

It says "to cling to Him": to His qualities. But even in the higher qualities a person clings only to that they are, not to what they are. "I am dust and ashes." How much more with the Infinite Light, of which "no thought can grasp Him at all." We can know that He is, that He gives life to all, but not what He is, not even the angels. "Holy, holy, holy." Only in prophecy, stripped of the body, did Moshe see "My back." Without grasping there is no real clothing or clinging.

But the mitzvos are "the works of G-d." They come down, level by level, from the very being of the outer vessels of the highest world. The etrog, for example: its life comes from the very being of the outer vessels of the highest kingship, which is G-dliness, one with the Infinite. When you hold the etrog and shake it as the law says, you hold, really, its life that is clothed in it from that highest level, which is one with the Infinite Light. Your intention, even if you know the secret, grasps only that it is, not what it is. Learning the laws of the etrog grasps the etrog itself and its mitzvah, in speech and thought.

Knowing the order of the worlds, knowing that He is, is also a great mitzvah, even above all others: "Know today," "Know the G-d of your father." It brings a whole heart, which is the main thing. But it is one of 613, and a person must keep all 613 in thought, speech and deed.

The note gives more reasons. The fallen sparks in the physical world are higher in their root than a person's own soul, which is why a person lives on food. The inner face of the higher cannot come down; only its outer side comes down. A seed carries the very being of the one who sows it, and so it can beget; speech carries only a glow of the mind. Love and awe from the mind are like the angels born of a "kiss," of an outer glow. And all love and awe from the mind make things rise. That is only a going up, a withdrawal. The drawing down comes only through mitzvos in deed. "The outer side of the higher goes down, the inner side of the lower goes up." Both are needed. But the purpose of all the worlds is that the higher light be revealed below, not that the lower go up, which is only for a time, like on Shabbat and Yom Kippur.

How can an etrog, a fallen thing not yet refined, draw light into the highest levels? Like a seed that wakes the earth's growing power, which is the word of G-d, "Let the earth sprout." So the parchment and the etrog wake a level higher than the breaking. And the laws themselves are not physical at all. A law is a will drawn from the highest wisdom, to permit or forbid, that shines openly in a physical thing, "like water coming down from a high place." Learning Mishnah and Talmud draws the Infinite Light into this world, to be included in His light. "This is the whole of man." That was the work of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and all the sages in the revealed Torah, all through the exile: that the Highest come down, and have a dwelling in the lowest.

From inside, this is a humbling and a relief. The mind's best love touches only the outside. The hand holding the etrog holds the inside. You do not need to understand what you hold to hold it. Where the picture breaks: "holding" sounds like having; you do not have Him, you are simply where He chose to be held.

**The movement.** From thinking your understanding is your closest contact with G-d, to knowing that the deed and the law grasp what understanding cannot, and that the goal is His light coming down, not you going up. What carries a person across: doing the mitzvah exactly as the law says, learning its laws, and knowing that He is, so the heart becomes whole.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Holding this, I hold what my mind cannot." 2. Take a real object of a mitzvah into your hand: an etrog, tefillin, a Shabbat candle, a coin for charity. 3. Think: my deepest thought about G-d touches only that He is. This, done as He asked, holds His life in it. 4. The picture breaks: you are not holding Him the way you hold a thing. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "I don't understand. I'm holding it for You." 6. Do that mitzvah today exactly by its law, and learn one of its laws.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a physical mitzvah feels like the closest you get, and you stop needing to feel exalted during it. The fake is magic thinking about objects, or contempt for understanding, as if the mind did not matter at all.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 4 (line 1574): הֲרֵי זֶה תּוֹפֵס מַמָּשׁ חַיּוּתוֹ הַמְלוּבָּשׁ בּוֹ מִנּוּקְבָא דַאֲצִילוּת*

## Kuntres Acharon 5 · A hair from the King's head

**The state.** Learning a detail of law, even one that never happened and never will, as touching a hair drawn from the highest wisdom. It is the state of refining by learning: the holy is separated from what clings to it, and the soul's full stature is completed detail by detail.

**The chapter as consciousness.** What about details of law that never come up, that may never have happened at all, and certainly will not in the future, like the laws of an offering made unfit by wrong intent (pigul)?

Every forbidden thing in the world has a root of life in the husks. Otherwise it could not exist, since nothing exists without a flow from above. Even someone who curls his hair in vanity gets that moment's life from the halls of the husks, the Zohar says. So even forbidden details that never happened in this physical world have roots that really exist in those halls.

And details that could not even exist, like a mistake (counting the ninth animal as the tenth), where no husk rests on a willful act, still exist in the highest wisdom. That wisdom spread itself into this very detail for Moshe at Sinai, "all that a faithful student will one day say," and all the hard questions the sages ever asked.

For the spreading of the highest wisdom is the Infinite actually clothed in it. Every detail of law is a hair drawn from the highest wisdom and clothed in it, and from there it is drawn down and clothed in the lower worlds.

The husks feed on the back side of holiness, above all on its garments in the lower worlds, where good and bad are mixed. When a person learns the laws, in speech and thought, the husks are separated from the holy and fall away, because he draws the Infinite Light that is in that wisdom. "In wisdom they are refined." And that drawing is done by the higher image of the person, which learns these laws above in his root.

This explains why every soul must complete all 613 in thought, speech and deed, which are the details of the laws, and must come back again and again to complete the whole Torah in its plain meaning, hint, teaching and secret, to refine all the sparks that belong to it. Together they are a whole human stature: 613 parts, general and particular.

In the time to come, when the refining is finished, the work of the Torah will be only "do good": raising the soul higher and higher without end, and taking the 365 prohibitions in their root, which are holy strengths, and sweetening them in the kindness of the 248 positive mitzvos, and joining them together. So the whole Torah is eternal, in general and in detail. Even the details of the prohibitions are branches of the general, and they have a root above in the holy strengths, like the blood that gives life to the limbs.

From inside: you are learning a law that you will never use. It seems like a waste. The note says it is one of the hairs of the King's head, and learning it separates holiness from what clings to it, in you and in the world. Your soul has a shape of 613 parts and this is one of them. Where the picture breaks: a hair is the least living part of a person; this hair carries the Infinite clothed in it.

**The movement.** From learning only what is useful, to learning every detail as part of the whole stature of the soul and a thread of His wisdom. What carries a person across: learning the laws in speech and thought, including the ones you will never need.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Each detail is a hair from His head." 2. Open to a law you will never need to use. Read it slowly, aloud. 3. Think: this very detail was given to Moshe at Sinai. The Infinite is clothed in it. Learning it separates the holy from what clings. 4. The picture breaks: you cannot see His head, or the hair. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Let me be whole in Your Torah." 6. Learn one such detail today, clearly enough to explain it to someone.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when learning an obscure law feels full rather than empty, and you feel your soul being filled out. The fake is collecting details to show off, or brushing off "impractical" learning as waste.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 5 (line 1613): וְכָל פְּרַט הֲלָכָה, הוּא שַׁעַר נִמְשָׁךְ מֵחָכְמָה עִילָּאָה*

## Kuntres Acharon 6 · All the worlds before one detail

**The state.** Awe and joy at the Torah's greatness: all the worlds hang on one small detail of a mitzvah, and are as nothing before it. And above that, the quieter state the note corrects toward: the inner Torah, which is one with Him and beyond any praise, His own delight, which a person does not grasp but joins the "back" to.

**The chapter as consciousness.** King David was punished for calling the Torah "songs." The Zohar speaks of "the praise and song of the Torah." But what praise is it to G-d that this is forbidden and that is permitted?

It is like "How great are Your works, G-d; how very deep Your thoughts." All the worlds, higher and lower, hang on the exactness of one mitzvah. If an offering is kosher, a higher union is made and all the worlds rise to receive their life. If something is changed, if the blood was taken in the left hand, or not in a proper vessel, or something stood in between, then the rising of the worlds and their life from the Life of all life is cancelled. Kosher tefillin reveal the higher mind that is the source of life for all worlds, and one detail makes them unfit and the light withdraws.

So one who thinks how great the works of G-d are, the many worlds and all their hosts, and how all of them are as nothing before one detail of the Torah, which is the depth of His thought and wisdom, sees how deep His thought is. It has no limit. All the life of all the worlds flows from one small detail of it, the way a hair grows from the head.

This was David's joy. He sang and made music to gladden his heart in the learning of Torah in his time of trouble.

Then why was he punished for praising the Torah this way, saying "Your laws were songs to me"? Because this greatness, that all worlds are nothing before one of its details, belongs to the back side of the depth of His thought. "The Torah is the fallen fruit of the wisdom above."

But the inner part of that depth, the inner Torah, is one with the Infinite Light clothed in it, completely. Before the Infinite, all the worlds are truly nothing. "You were the same before the world was created." So you cannot praise the inner Torah at all by the life of the worlds, since they count for nothing. In its inwardness it is not the joy and delight of a human heart. It is, as it were, the joy and delight of the King Himself, who delights in it. "G-d understood its way and knew its place," in knowing Himself. It is "hidden from the eyes of all living." "My face will not be seen."

That is why the verse says, "I was with Him, His delight every day, playing before Him": with Him, before Him, in its inwardness. And of the back it says, "playing in His inhabited world, my delights with the sons of men." The Torah was given face and back, "written on the front and the back."

Because David held on to the back, he was punished with forgetting, which comes from the back. For a moment he forgot the verse "the holy service they shall carry on the shoulder": to join the "shoulders," which are the back, to "the holy service," the highest wisdom, the face. That is where the tablets in the Ark came from, "written on both sides," with no front and back at all.

From inside, there are two joys. The first is huge and bright: everything depends on this detail, and I am holding it. That is true, and David sang it. The second is quieter and higher. The Torah's inside is not about the worlds at all. It is His own delight, which I cannot see. I carry the back on my shoulder, and I join it to the face I do not see. Where the picture breaks: "back" and "face" sound like two sides of a body you could walk around; you cannot walk around this one.

**The movement.** From awe at the Torah's power over all the worlds, and a joy in that power, to bittul (the self gone quiet) before the Torah's inside, which is His own delight, and carrying the outer side up toward it. What carries a person across: thinking in detail how all worlds hang on one detail, and then stopping, knowing that even that praise is only the back.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "All the worlds are nothing before one detail of Your Torah." 2. Take one detail of a mitzvah, like the exact place of the tefillin on the head. Think: all the worlds rise or fall on this. 3. Then go further: before Him, those worlds are nothing, and the inside of this detail is His own delight. 4. The picture breaks: you cannot see His delight. Stop there. Carry the detail on your shoulder. 5. Say to Him: "This is Yours, far beyond what I can praise." 6. Do that detail today with care, and sing one line of praise from your own heart.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when care for a small detail feels both huge and humble, and you stop feeling that your learning makes you great. The fake is spiritual grandiosity, "worlds depend on me," or anxious perfectionism about details with no joy in it.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 6 (line 1625): וְאֵיךְ כּוּלָּם בְּטֵלִים בִּמְצִיאוּת לְגַבֵּי דִּקְדּוּק אֶחָד מִדִּקְדּוּקֵי תוֹרָה*

## Kuntres Acharon 7 · Mercy on your own spark

**The state.** Rachamim rabbim (great mercy) woken for the G-dly spark in your own soul, which is in exile as long as it cannot shine into the nekudas halev (the inner point of the heart). And charity as the act that opens that point, a small taste now of what will come.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "Charity like a mighty stream" (nachal eisan). The note reads each word. Eisan ("mighty") points to the highest hidden point of the soul. Its letters also form the future tense, "I will be revealed." It means that in the time to come the Infinite Light and His oneness will be revealed inside the inner point of the heart, through the "mighty stream," the light of the highest wisdom, shining in the inside of the heart. Then a person will be nullified in His oneness completely, from the depth of the heart, after the covering of physical cravings is removed.

Now, in this exile, there is advice for bringing a little of that light into the inner point of the heart, like in the time to come. It is by waking great mercy above on the G-dly spark in one's soul.

For as long as a person does not merit that G-d's light shine into the inner point of his heart, to be nullified in His oneness from the depth of the heart until the soul melts away (klos hanefesh), there is truly great pity on the spark in his soul. The spark comes from the highest wisdom itself. When it cannot shine from where it is into the inside of the heart, where it is meant to show, it is truly in exile. Through great mercy from above it comes out of exile and captivity and shines this great love into the inner point of the heart. "To Yaakov, who redeemed Avraham": mercy (Yaakov) redeems love (Avraham), as chapter 45 of the first part explains.

And a stirring above depends on a stirring below: on waking great mercy in the hearts of merciful and kind people, to give a physical gift down below, gold and silver and the like. So the act of charity is exactly the work of the "mighty stream." "Skin for skin, all that a man has he will give for his soul": for his G-dly soul, to light it with the Infinite light of life.

From inside, this is a new direction for mercy. You know how it feels to pity a poor man. Now turn it inward. There is a spark in you from the highest wisdom, and it is locked out of your own heart. It cannot shine where it belongs. That is a real captivity, and it is yours. Seeing it is not self pity. It is mercy on something holy that is suffering in you. And the way to wake mercy above on it is to have mercy below on someone else, with your money. Where the picture breaks: a prisoner longs to get out; this spark longs to get in, into the middle of your heart.

**The movement.** From a heart where the spark is shut out, and a person hardly notices, to great mercy on that spark, and from there, through the act of charity, to a little of its light shining in the inner point of the heart. What carries a person across: thinking on the spark's exile until mercy wakes, and giving to another with a merciful heart.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Have mercy on the spark in me." 2. Think: in me is a spark from His highest wisdom. It is meant to shine in the middle of my heart. Most days it cannot. 3. Picture a precious thing locked outside a house in the cold. That is it. 4. The picture breaks: the spark is not outside you; it is shut out from your own center. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Great mercy, on this spark of Yours." 6. Give charity today with mercy, and let it be for your soul too.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you feel tender toward your own soul and that tenderness moves you to give. The fake is self pity that dwells on your troubles, or giving to feel spiritual that never softens the heart.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 7 (line 1637): יֵשׁ רַחֲמָנוּת גְּדוֹלָה עַל הַנִּיצוֹץ שֶׁבְּנַפְשׁוֹ*

## Kuntres Acharon 8 · The hidden love comes into the heart

**The state.** Prayer as the main service now: the hidden love (ahavah mesuteres) brought out through contemplation in the verses of praise and the blessings before the Shema, until it is revealed in the heart during the Shema itself. The revealed love reaches the life soul, and that is the refining of prayer.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Alter Rebbe heard bad news: in one congregation they were removing from leading prayers the man who prays at length, the one whose long prayer, the sages say, lengthens the lives of all. Even someone who is truly pressed for time, he says, and cannot wait to answer Kedushah in the leader's repetition, is better off missing Kedushah and Barchu than cutting into the life of those who want life. His need is excused, and the leader fulfills his duty for him as if he heard.

Even in the early generations of the sages, whose Torah was fixed and was their main service, and not prayer, this was so. How much more now, "in the footsteps of Moshiach," when our Torah is not fixed, because times are hard, and the main service in these days is prayer, as Rabbi Chaim Vital writes. So it is right to give our very souls for it.

And it is a duty of the Torah itself, for those who understand: the value of contemplating, and some depth of knowing, each by his own measure, in the order of G-d's praise in the verses of song and the two blessings before the Shema, "Who forms light" and "With great love." Their purpose is to wake the love hidden in every Jewish heart, and bring it into the open, revealed in the heart, at the moment of the Shema.

That is the mitzvah of love in "You shall love G-d with all your heart," counted first of the 613 by the Rambam, the foundation and root of all 248 positive mitzvos. For the hidden love that is in every Jewish heart by birth and nature, no command could apply. The command is to bring it out.

Understand this: while the love is hidden, it is in the G-dly soul alone. When it comes out into the life soul, the soul that runs the body, it is revealed in the heart, in the left chamber, where the life soul lives.

That is the refining of sparks that the Arizal connects with prayer, which is why prayer is the main service in the footsteps of Moshiach. It is the life soul being subdued, or transformed, into the G-dly soul (iskafya and ishapcha). "The blood is the soul," and the blood is renewed each day from food and drink, and it is affected by clothes and home. So each day there is new refining to do. In the early generations, when G-dly souls were very great, the refining happened in a moment, in the Shema and its blessings, with a short verses of praise. Now it takes time.

From inside: you are not being asked to invent a love. It is there. It is quiet, underneath. The long prayer, word by word, with thought in the praises and the blessings, is how that love walks out into the heart, into the part of you that eats, wants and feels. And then the heart itself, the animal part, changes a little. That is why long prayer is not indulgence. It is work. Where the picture breaks: "left chamber" sounds like anatomy; it means the place where your ordinary feelings live, and the love coming to live there too.

**The movement.** From a love hidden in the G-dly soul, which does not touch your ordinary feelings, to love revealed in the heart during the Shema, where it reshapes the life soul. What carries a person across: contemplation in the praises and blessings before the Shema, each by his measure, and patience with the time prayer takes.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "The love is already there. Let it come into my heart." 2. In the blessing before the Shema, stop on one detail of G-d's greatness: the light made new each morning. Think it through, slowly. 3. Then think of His great love for you, the blessing's own words. Let the two meet. 4. At the Shema, say "One," and let the hidden love show. 5. The picture breaks: you cannot force it. Stop at the edge and ask: "Open my heart to love You." 6. Take ten more minutes in prayer tomorrow, and never hurry someone who prays long.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when prayer changes how you feel about ordinary things for hours after, and your wants quiet a little. The fake is a long prayer to be seen, or a flash of feeling that leaves the day untouched.

*Source: Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 8 (line 1644): לְעוֹרֵר בָּהֶן הָאַהֲבָה הַמְסוּתֶּרֶת בְּלֵב כָּל יִשְׂרָאֵל, לָבֹא לִבְחִינַת גִּילּוּי*

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this reading applies it to every person.*

## Kuntres Acharon 9 · The quiet of Shabbat

**The state.** The last state in the book: praying as one congregation, word by word, and the inner Shabbat. Zachor (remember) is clinging to G-d in Shabbat prayer and learning; shamor (keep) in its inner sense is shevisah (resting) from ordinary speech, as G-d rested from the ten sayings that made the world.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "Rebuke your fellow," the Torah says, "even a hundred times." So he cannot hold back from crying out again, in a weak voice: I beg you, with great mercy, have pity on your souls. Guard the Torah and "the service of the heart, which is prayer," with intention. Begin all together, as one, word by word. Not one this way and one that way, one silent and another chatting. G-d keep us.

The main cause of the harm is the leaders at the prayer stand. It is a free for all: whoever wants to stretch his legs, whoever grabs first, or no one wants to. So here is a fixed rule, not to be broken: choose fixed, fitting men, by lot or by the will of most of the congregation, to pray word by word, at a middle pace, aloud, not too long and not rushing. They must lead each on his day, and gather the congregation around them, praying a little aloud, not in a whisper, not rushing, as the old rules in many towns say.

"Now I come to renew them and strengthen them, so they never slip again." (In the manuscript, the words "Gevalt! Gevalt!" are written here.) How long will this be a trap for us? Is all the rebuke and all the trouble we have had not enough? May G-d comfort us double, and purify our hearts to serve Him in truth.

Also: finish the whole Talmud every year in every town, dividing the tractates by lot or by choice. And each learner should finish each week the "eight faces," Psalm 119.

Because the generation is weak and cannot fast as much as it should, the advice is what the sages said: "Whoever keeps Shabbat by its law is forgiven all his sins." "By its law," exactly. So each person must be expert in the many laws of Shabbat. And be very careful not to speak any idle talk.

Then the last lines of the book. Everyone who knows the inner teaching knows that every mitzvah has an inside and an outside. The outside of Shabbat is resting from physical work, as G-d rested from making the physical heaven and earth. The inside of Shabbat is the intention in Shabbat prayer and Torah learning, to cling to G-d who is One: "a Shabbat to G-d your G-d." That is "remember." And "keep," in its inner sense, is resting from ordinary speech, as G-d rested from the ten sayings by which the physical heaven and earth were made. For one matches the other.

From inside, the book ends in quiet. Not an empty quiet. A room full of people praying one word at a time, together. Then a day on which the mouth, which all week built and bargained and chattered, stops making a world of its own, the way He stopped. What is left is clinging: prayer and learning, and One. Where the picture breaks: His rest is not tiredness, and ours is not only silence; it is speech turned from the world to Him.

**The movement.** From scattered prayer, each at his own pace, and speech that never rests, to one congregation praying as one, and a Shabbat whose inside is clinging and whose keeping is quiet from idle words. What carries a person across: fixed order in prayer, knowing the laws of Shabbat, and guarding the mouth.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "As He rested from His sayings, I rest from mine." 2. Think of the ten sayings that made the world, "Let there be light," and then the seventh day, when He stopped saying. 3. Think of your own week of words: what they built, what they spoiled. 4. The picture breaks: His rest is not needing rest; yours is turning your words to Him. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "This day is Yours, and my mouth with it." 6. This Shabbat, keep from idle talk for one full stretch, and pray with the congregation word by word.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when silence on Shabbat feels full rather than forced, and praying together feels like one voice. The fake is a stiff, cold silence that looks down on others, or keeping the outside of Shabbat while the mouth runs all day.
