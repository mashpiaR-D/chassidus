## Read this first

**The question in one line.** The G-dly soul hears "you are held" and recognises it. The animal soul hears the same sentence and goes on bracing. How do we say it so that the animal soul takes it in too?

**The answer in one paragraph** *(ours, built from the evidence below)*. The animal soul is not convinced by argument. It is convinced by a lived small proof, in its own terms, at the moment it is braced. Its terms are safety, rest, food, warmth, being looked after, not being alone. It believes what the body has just experienced, more than what it has been told. So the teaching has to reach it as a concrete, present fact it can check right now ("the breath came without you", "the bread in your hand was given"), pictured rather than explained, said while the old fear is awake but not overwhelming, and repeated across many ordinary moments until it becomes the body's new expectation. This is, almost point for point, what the Rebbe Rashab asks for: contemplation of the G-dliness that fills this world (memale), "which the natural mind can grasp", so that the animal soul itself understands and feels, and is turned, not only overpowered (Kuntres HaTefillah, line 44 and line 77.4 in our index).

**What is new here.** BH covers being held as felt security, attachment to G-d and the costs of over-control. It treats the body in one entry, as "description only" (BH 16). This file goes into the body's own system: threat and safety circuits, the body budget, prediction, wanting and habit. It then sorts the methods that change that system by how well they are supported, and turns them into rules for the guide.

**The owner's rules still bind** (claims.md D09, D11, D16, D25, D41, D42, D44). No breathing drills, body scans or relaxation offered to calm down. A *felt fact* (the floor holding their weight, a breath that comes by itself) or a *felt act* (a line said with the lips, a palm on the table) that carries the insight is allowed (D11). No questions about feelings or how it is landing (D25). Understand before teaching (D41, D42). The method is hisbonenus, not mindfulness (D16). Several findings below turn out to support these rules, not strain against them; that is noted where it happens.

**Marks.** *Ours* marks our synthesis. **[in our files]** means the reference is already checked in one of the files above. **[confirmed by search]** means we checked author, year, venue and the gist of the finding on 2026-10-01. Strength labels as in BH: *meta-analysis, review, RCT, experiment, imaging study, correlational, theory, clinical theory, replication problem.*

**The honest gap.** No study has looked at the animal soul as such, or at Chabad contemplation of memale. Everything here is evidence by analogy, and psychology can never test the claim that a person is in fact being given being right now. It can only say how a creature like us comes to feel safe.

## Part 1. What the animal soul is, in the sources

Short, since MECHANISMS §9 has the two-souls picture.

- **It is the life of the body.** The Tanya's first chapters describe a soul "clothed in the blood of a person to give life to the body" (Tanya ch. 1, Kehot text line 82). Its native wants are life, comfort, safety and pleasure. It is called the "vital animal soul" (נפש החיונית הבהמית).
- **It has its own intellect.** It is not a mute drive. Like the G-dly soul, it is made of ten powers, including "the intellect that gives birth to them, divided into three: wisdom, understanding and knowledge" (ושכל המולידן הנחלק לשלש, שהן חכמה בינה ודעת, Tanya ch. 6, line 143). Its feelings come out of its own way of understanding the world.
- **It is the person.** In the ordinary person, "it is the man himself" (הרי היא היא האדם עצמו, Tanya ch. 29, line 411). The guide may not tell anyone that the frightened part "isn't really you" (TM §6).
- **It is not evil in itself.** It comes from kelipat nogah, a mixed realm that can be raised. Its pull is to be refined and, at best, turned: "to change it and turn it" (לשנותה ולהפכה) toward love of G-d (Tanya ch. 9, line 185; TM §6).
- **Two ways to deal with it.** The Rebbe Rashab: when the contemplation is of things above the natural mind, the G-dly soul's feeling *overpowers* the animal soul and subdues it. When the contemplation is of things the natural mind can grasp (memale, the G-dliness that fills and gives life to each thing), the animal soul *itself* comes to understand and feel the G-dly matter and is moved (Kuntres HaTefillah, lines 44 and 77.4-77.5, in `../kuntres-hatefillah/lines-1-253.md`). General, vague contemplation "is no contemplation at all" and leaves the animal soul's traits as strong as before (lines 49-50).
- **The Rashab's own limit.** The animal soul "by its nature does not want Godliness"; all its rising is the G-dly soul working on it. He leaves the question open (Kuntres HaAvodah, lines 142 and 180, in `../KUNTRES-HAAVODAH.md`).

So the task the owner set is the Rashab's task. Find the form of "you are held" that the natural, self-interested mind can grasp *in its own terms*, so that it comes to feel it.

## Part 2. The animal soul in modern terms

No single system in psychology is the animal soul. Seven systems together cover most of what the sources describe. For each: the finding, the fit, and where it breaks.

## 2.1 The threat system and the defence cascade

**The finding.** The brain has circuits that detect danger and launch survival responses (freezing, racing heart, fight, flight) largely outside awareness. LeDoux and Pine argue that this defensive circuit should be kept apart from the conscious feeling of fear, which depends on other, mostly cortical, systems (LeDoux and Pine, 2016, *American Journal of Psychiatry*) [confirmed by search]. *Theory/review; influential, and contested by some.* The responses form a graded cascade: arousal, fight or flight, freezing, and in extreme threat, collapse and shutdown (Kozlowska, Walker, McLean and Carrive, 2015, *Harvard Review of Psychiatry*) [confirmed by search]. *Review.* One theory holds that the stress response is *on by default* and is only switched off when safety is perceived (Brosschot, Verkuil and Thayer, 2016) [in our files, BH 16]. *Theory.*

**Fit.** This is the animal soul at its most animal: the part that braces before it thinks. Brosschot's idea is the closest match to THE-ROOT's portrait. The person is not reacting to one danger. They have never received a clear "you can stand down" signal.

**Where it breaks.** The animal soul is not only the alarm. It plans, wants, reasons and loves. And the conscious feeling of fear is not the same as the alarm, so calming one does not guarantee calming the other (LeDoux and Pine).

## 2.2 The body budget: allostasis and interoception

**The finding.** The brain's main job is to run the body ahead of time: to predict needs and meet them before they arise (allostasis; Sterling, 2012, *Physiology & Behavior*) [confirmed by search]. *Theory, widely used.* Barrett calls this the "body budget". On her account, much of what we feel is the brain's *prediction* of the body's state, checked against signals coming up from the body (Barrett and Simmons, 2015, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*; Barrett, 2017, *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*) [both confirmed by search]. *Theory with anatomical support.* Seth makes the same move: feelings are the brain's best guess about the causes of signals from inside the body (Seth, 2013, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*) [confirmed by search]. *Theory.* Interoception is a sense in its own right, and its disturbances run through anxiety and depression (Craig, 2002; Khalsa et al., 2018) [in our files]; (Paulus and Stein, 2010, *Brain Structure and Function*; Harshaw, 2015, *Psychological Bulletin*) [confirmed by search]. *Reviews.*

**Fit.** Good. The animal soul "gives life to the body" and looks after it. Its first question about anything is: is the budget safe? Will I have enough? Is this costing me? Worry, from inside this picture, is the body budget forecasting a deficit.

**Where it breaks.** The body budget has no intellect of its own in the Tanya's sense. And the theory of constructed emotion is one theory among several.

## 2.3 Predictive processing: beliefs held as predictions

**The finding.** On the predictive view, the brain is a prediction machine. It holds models of the world and of the body and updates them when predictions fail (Clark, 2013, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*) [in our files]. *Theory; broad, partly tested.* Deep, heavily weighted predictions ("priors") are hard to move. They shape what we perceive before we perceive it (SCIENCE.md §1.5).

**Fit.** *Ours:* this is the best single language for the animal soul's "beliefs". It does not believe propositions. It *expects*. "No one is coming", "it all depends on me", "I will be dropped" live in it as bodily forecasts, not as sentences. That is why an argument does not reach them. An argument is not a prediction error. Only an experience that turns out differently from the forecast is.

**Where it breaks.** It is a framework, not a finding, and it describes everything, which makes it hard to test. It also says nothing about a soul.

## 2.4 The attachment system and felt security

**The finding.** Covered in BH 1 and 2. The core: a creature that feels a reliable other is near goes out and explores; one that does not, clings or shuts down. Felt security can be raised for a time by reminders of those who care for us (Mikulincer and Shaver, 2007) [in our files]. Others lower the cost of threat to the body: social baseline theory (Coan and Sbarra, 2015) [in our files].

**Fit.** Very good. The animal soul's deepest safety question is relational: am I alone with this? THE-ROOT puts the core pain in the same place.

**Where it breaks.** Psychology's holding is always two: someone holds someone. The teaching's holding is one: being given being. The animal soul can only take in the first form. *Ours:* that is exactly why the Rashab uses memale. It can be pictured as being looked after, which the animal soul understands, while it points to something more.

## 2.5 Two ways of knowing: the experiential system

**The finding.** Epstein's cognitive-experiential self-theory proposes two parallel systems. A *rational* system works by logic and words. An *experiential* system works by feeling, images, stories and lived example. The experiential system is moved by "concrete, imagistic and narrative representations" and explains "conflicts between the heart and the head" (Epstein, 1994, *American Psychologist*) [confirmed by search]. *Theory with lab support.* Dual-process theories more broadly (Evans and Stanovich, 2013) [in our files]. Teasdale's two kinds of meaning: knowing a sentence is true is not the same as its "feeling true", and the second shifts through rich, specific, personally relevant material, not through more argument (Teasdale, 1993) [in our files]. Haidt's moral intuitions come first, and reasoning usually follows to justify them (Haidt, 2001, *Psychological Review*) [confirmed by search]; his later image is a rider on an elephant.

**Fit.** Strong for the *method*. The Rashab's complaint about chassidim who learn and do not change (Kuntres HaTefillah line 50) is the rational system knowing and the experiential system untouched. Epstein even lists religion among the things the experiential system runs on.

**Where it breaks, and this matters.** - The G-dly soul is not the rational system, and the animal soul is not the experiential one. The G-dly soul has its own love; the animal soul has its own intellect (MECHANISMS §9). - *Ours:* Haidt's picture fits the Tanya better in one respect. The rider often serves the elephant: reasoning is put to work for what the animal already wants. That is the Tanya's "intellect that gives birth to the traits" (line 143). The animal soul is the elephant *and* the clever rider in its service. So its intellect can be engaged, but only by an argument that answers its own concern.

## 2.6 Wanting

**The finding.** "Wanting" (being drawn toward a reward, driven by dopamine systems and set off by cues) is separable in the brain from "liking" (the pleasure itself). In addiction, wanting grows huge while liking does not (Berridge and Robinson, 2016, *American Psychologist*) [confirmed by search]. *Review of a large animal and human literature; strong.*

**Fit.** The animal soul is above all a wanting. THE-ROOT's "the symptom is a solution" sits here: the drink is wanted far beyond how much it is liked, because a cue fires and the system expects relief.

**Where it breaks.** The Tanya does not reduce desire to reward circuits, and the Rashab speaks of the animal soul coming to *want* G-dliness (Kuntres HaAvodah line 142), which no reward model predicts.

## 2.7 Habit

**The finding.** Much of daily behaviour runs on habits: context cues trigger responses automatically, with little reference to current goals (Wood and Rünger, 2016, *Annual Review of Psychology*) [confirmed by search]. *Review; strong.* Habits form slowly and at different speeds in different people (Lally et al., 2010) [in our files]. People good at self-control mostly resist less; good habits carry them (Galla and Duckworth, 2015) [in our files].

**Fit.** The animal soul's traits are, in large part, its habits. They are why the Tanya's ordinary person still feels the pull after years of service.

## 2.8 The overall map, and the "lizard brain" mistake

| What the sources say of the animal soul | Closest modern system | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gives life to the body; wants life and safety | Allostasis, body budget (2.2) | Good |
| Its fears and traits | Threat system, defence cascade (2.1) | Good for the alarm, weak for the rest |
| Its "beliefs" about the world | Predictive processing (2.3) | Good as language; untested |
| Wants to be looked after, not alone | Attachment system (2.4) | Very good for feel |
| Moved by pictures and lived example, not argument | Experiential system (2.5) | Strong for method |
| Its cravings | Wanting (2.6) | Strong |
| Its strength "as at birth" in the ordinary person | Habit (2.7) | Good |
| Has its own intellect | Reasoning in service of intuition (2.5) | Partial |
| Can be turned to love G-d | Nothing | None |

Do not translate the animal soul as "the lizard brain". The picture of a reptile core wrapped in newer layers has long been discredited by neurobiologists (Cesario, Johnson and Eisthen, 2020, *Current Directions in Psychological Science*) [confirmed by search]. And the Tanya's animal soul is not a primitive layer. It is the whole self-preserving person, intelligence included. *Ours:* the right picture is a capable, frightened, self-interested creature that is looking after itself as best it knows how. It can be reasoned with only in its own terms, and it changes most by experience.

## Part 3. What changes the animal part's beliefs

Each entry: (a) the finding; (b) what it implies for how to say "you are held"; (c) the bridge to the animal-soul contemplation; (d) where the bridge stops.

## 3.1 Prediction error and memory reconsolidation

**(a)** In rats, a reactivated fear memory becomes changeable again and must be re-stored (Nader, Schafe and LeDoux, 2000) [in our files, TM §1]. In humans, this destabilising happens only when the reactivation carries a *prediction error*, something that does not go as the memory expects (Sevenster, Beckers and Kindt, 2013, *Science*) [confirmed by search]. *Experiment; strong for the lab procedure.* Lane, Ryan, Nadel and Greenberg propose that the shared ingredient of effective psychotherapies is this: old emotional memories are activated, met with new emotional experience, and re-stored changed (Lane et al., 2015, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*) [in our files; confirmed by search]. *Theory with commentary.* Ecker builds a clinical method on the mismatch requirement (Ecker, 2015, *International Journal of Neuropsychotherapy*) [confirmed by search]. *Clinical theory.* **Caution:** a critical review finds the human evidence uneven and the boundary conditions unclear (Elsey, Van Ast and Kindt, 2018, *Psychological Bulletin*) [confirmed by search], and a well-known human result failed a registered replication (Chalkia et al., 2020) [in our files]. *Replication problem.*

**(b)** For the animal part to update, three things must be present at once: the old expectation is *awake* ("it's all on me"); something *lived* happens that does not fit it; and the person *notices* the mismatch. A claim is not a mismatch. A small, checkable experience is. "Your heart has beaten all night without your help" is a mismatch for "everything depends on me", because the person can check it in the moment and it is true.

**(c)** This is hisbonenus as SCIENCE.md §2.4 already reads it: start from where you stand (wakes the old model), then set a charged, concrete reading beside it. Memale is the ideal mismatch material because it is in every object the person can touch: the bread, the breath, the light. *Ours:* the Rashab's "turned, not only overpowered" reads very naturally as updating the old learning, as opposed to stacking a rule on top of it.

**(d)** Psychology says the old learning changes. The teaching says the new reading is *true*. The research cannot say which counter-experience is true, only which ones move people. And "turned" in the Rashab is love of G-d, which no memory study measures.

## 3.2 Inhibitory learning: when the old fear stays underneath

**(a)** In ordinary extinction, the old fear memory is not erased. A new "safe" memory competes with it, and the fear can return in a new place, after time, or under stress (Bouton, 2004, *Learning & Memory*) [confirmed by search]. *Review of animal and human work; strong.* Craske's inhibitory-learning approach strengthens the new learning by: violating the person's expectation as clearly as possible; practising in varied contexts; giving retrieval cues that carry the new learning into new places; removing safety signals; and putting feelings into words (Craske, Treanor, Conway, Zbozinek and Vervliet, 2014, *Behaviour Research and Therapy*) [confirmed by search]. *Review of experiments; strong.* Naming the feeling during exposure helped (Kircanski, Lieberman and Craske, 2012) [in our files].

**(b)** Expect the old fear to come back, and plan for it. Practise "you are held" in many places (kitchen, car, office, bed at night), not only in a quiet sitting. Give the person a short line they carry into the hard moment: that is a retrieval cue. And let the expectation be stated before the small proof: "you're sure it all falls apart if you stop for ten minutes", then the ten minutes.

**(c)** *Ours:* the Tanya's two ways map neatly. **Itkafia** (subduing) is like inhibition: the old pull stays at full strength and a stronger learning rules it. That is the Tanya's ordinary person, whose animal soul stays "as at birth" (line 408). **Ishapcha** (turning) is like updating the learning itself. The Rashab's two contemplations fit too: what is above the natural mind subdues; what the natural mind grasps turns.

**(d)** The mapping is ours and loose. Inhibition in the lab is about one cue and one fear. Itkafia is a moral and spiritual act of will that the Tanya values in itself, even when nothing changes inside. The guide should never imply that subduing is second best; for most people, most of the time, it is the work.

## 3.3 Felt safety cues, and the polyvagal question

**(a)** If the stress response is on by default (Brosschot et al., 2016), safety has to be signalled. The clearest measured safety signals are relational. Holding a spouse's hand reduced threat responses in the brain (Coan, Schaefer and Davidson, 2006) [in our files]. Polyvagal theory (Porges, 2007) [in our files] is the most popular account of safety and the body, with its "neuroception" of safety. A detailed critique argues its basic premises are likely refuted (Grossman, 2023) [in our files]. *Theory versus critical review. We do not use its language, as BH 16 already decided.*

**(b)** The animal part responds to *cues*, not conclusions: a reliable presence, warmth in the voice, being addressed by name, an unhurried pace. In a chat, the guide's own manner is the main cue it has. Short sentences, no alarm, no rush, no lecture.

**(c)** The Tanya's own picture of kindness to the body (Hillel feeding "the poor one", ch. 29; TM §5) is a safety cue given by the person to their own animal soul.

**(d)** The teaching is not a calming cue, and being held is not a calm state (BH 16). A person can be held and tense.

## 3.4 Security priming: reminders of being cared for

**(a)** Reminding people of someone who cares for them (their names, faces or a memory) raised compassion and helping in five experiments (Mikulincer, Shaver, Gillath and Nitzberg, 2005, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*) [confirmed by search]. *Experiments.* Priming security three times over three days shifted self-views and relationship expectations two days later (Carnelley and Rowe, 2007, *Personal Relationships*) [confirmed by search]. *Experiment.* A systematic review of thirty studies found security priming raises positive feeling and lowers negative feeling, with the effect building up when repeated (Rowe, Gold and Carnelley, 2020, *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health*) [confirmed by search]. *Systematic review; mostly small lab studies.* G-d can work as an attachment figure in such experiments, moderated by a person's history (Granqvist et al., 2012) [in our files, BH 2]. **Caution:** the wider priming literature has had serious replication problems; these effects are probably real but small and short.

**(b)** The animal part is soothed by a *specific* memory of being looked after, not by "you are loved" in general. And repeated small reminders beat one big one.

**(c)** *Ours:* each memale contemplation is a security prime with a different object. "Someone made sure there was air for you this morning." The animal soul understands being provided for. The teaching then says who.

**(d)** For a person whose history makes closeness feel dangerous, "He is close" can land as a threat (BH 2 and BH "Misreadings": surveillance). Lead with care and with what is given, not with being watched.

## 3.5 Pictures move the body more than words

**(a)** Imagining a scene raises emotion more than processing the same content in words (Holmes and Mathews, 2005; 2010) [in our files]. Imagining positive outcomes lifted mood more than thinking about their verbal meaning, and protected against a later low mood (Holmes, Lang and Shah, 2009, *Journal of Abnormal Psychology*) [confirmed by search]. *Experiments; moderate.* Imagery rescripting, a clinical method that changes the images of painful memories, has a moderate-to-large effect across 19 trials (Morina, Lancee and Arntz, 2017, *Journal of Behavior Therapy and Experimental Psychiatry*) [confirmed by search]. *Meta-analysis of small trials.*

**(b)** Give the animal part one picture, not a paragraph. A mother bathing a crying baby. A hand under the person they cannot see. Then stop.

**(c)** The mashal is the tradition's own image tool, and the Rashab asks for the matter to be made graspable, with "garments" (Kuntres HaAvodah line 46: one "cannot grasp a divine matter with no garment at all").

**(d)** Imagery rescripting is a clinical protocol and stays out (TM §11). The guide does not rework anyone's memories. And the Rashab warns against a picture taken for the thing itself ("false imagination", Kuntres HaAvodah line 204). The picture has to be said with where it breaks.

## 3.6 The body: what holds up and what does not

**(a)** Several popular "body changes mind" findings have failed to replicate: power posing (Ranehill et al., 2015) [in our files]; warm objects making people warmer to others (Lynott et al., 2014, *Social Psychology*: three replications, total N = 861, no effect) [confirmed by search]. Facial feedback came out real but small in a large, adversarially designed study (Coles et al., 2022, *Nature Human Behaviour*) [confirmed by search]. *Large replications.* Interoception is real and central (2.2), but deliberate attention to the body is not always calming (Part 6).

**(b)** Do not sell a posture or a gesture as a mechanism. Use the body as a *place where a truth can be checked*: the chair is holding your full weight without your effort; your heartbeat is happening without your instruction. That is a felt fact (D11), and its power is that it is a true mismatch with "it's all on me" (3.1), not that it is a body trick.

**(c)** This is memale at its plainest. "The life in this body is given right now" can be checked on one's own pulse.

**(d)** "Your body is held, so you are safe" can become a promise of calm. It is not one. The point is the fact, not the feeling.

## 3.7 Self-affirmation: a wider self is less defensive

**(a)** Reflecting on one's core values makes a threat to the self feel smaller and reduces defensiveness (Cohen and Sherman, 2014, *Annual Review of Psychology*) [confirmed by search]. *Review.* Across 144 tests, it modestly increased acceptance of threatening health information and actual behaviour change (Epton, Harris, Kane, van Koningsbruggen and Sheeran, 2015, *Health Psychology*) [confirmed by search]. *Meta-analysis; small effects.* Repeating positive self-statements, by contrast, can make people with low self-esteem feel worse (Wood, Perunovic and Lee, 2009) [in our files].

**(b)** When the animal part is defending itself (against blame, failure, a hard truth), first widen the ground it stands on: what it already cares about, who it already looks after. Do not affirm it with praise.

**(c)** *Ours:* "You are held" is the widest possible affirmation: the ground under the self is not the self's achievement at all. That is why it can lower defences without flattering.

**(d)** Self-affirmation affirms the self's values. The teaching takes the weight off the self. They feel alike and are not the same thing.

## 3.8 Savoring

**(a)** Positive feeling can be deliberately extended by staying with a good moment, sharpening attention to it, counting blessings and sharing it (Quoidbach, Mikolajczak and Gross, 2015, *Psychological Bulletin*) [confirmed by search]; beliefs about one's ability to savour can be measured (Bryant, 2003) [in our files]. *Review.*

**(b)** When the animal part already feels a moment of ease (a good meal, warm water, a child asleep), that is the best time to name it as given. Lingering on a real good is easier than manufacturing one.

**(c)** The blessing before food is exactly this: savour, and name the Giver, at the moment the animal soul is pleased. *Ours:* this may be the most natural door the animal soul has, because it is pleased already.

**(d)** Savoring aims at more good feeling. The blessing aims at knowing where the good comes from. Never make the feeling the goal (Mauss et al., 2011, in our files: chasing happiness backfires).

## 3.9 Awe and the small self

**(a)** Covered in BH 9. Awe at vastness shrinks the felt self and can raise humility and generosity (Keltner and Haidt, 2003; Piff et al., 2015; Stellar et al., 2018) [in our files], though some downstream behaviour effects did not replicate (BH 9). A proposed pathway from awe to health (Monroy and Keltner, 2022, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*) [confirmed by search]. *Review/theory.* **Important for the animal part:** there is a *threat-based* awe, at vast things that endanger us, and it raises arousal and lowers well-being (Gordon et al., 2017) [in our files].

**(b)** Vastness frightens the animal soul unless it comes with being held. "The universe is huge and you are tiny" can produce threat-awe. "The One who holds the galaxies is holding this cup for you" puts the vastness on its side.

**(c)** This is why the Rashab's order matters: memale (what fills *this*) is graspable by the animal soul; sovev (what surrounds and exceeds all) is for the G-dly soul and subdues (line 44, line 77.5). *Ours:* start the animal part with the close and small, not the vast.

**(d)** Awe research studies a feeling of smallness. The teaching's "nothing apart from Him" is not a feeling of being small, and must not become self-erasure (BH "Misreadings").

## 3.10 The soothing system

**(a)** Gilbert's compassion-focused therapy describes three emotion-regulation systems: threat, drive (wanting and achieving) and soothing (safeness and affiliation). Some people cannot soothe because the threat system runs their inner voice (Gilbert, 2014, *British Journal of Clinical Psychology*) [confirmed by search]. *Clinical theory.* Compassion-based interventions show moderate effects (Kirby, Tellegen and Steindl, 2017) [in our files, TM §5]. *Meta-analysis.*

**(b)** Notice which system is speaking. A person on the drive system ("I must, I have to, it's all on me") needs the soothing system woken, not more drive. Address the tired one kindly, as one would a child or a working animal that has carried too much.

**(c)** Hillel and "the poor one" (TM §5). The animal soul is looked after because it is the vessel. *Ours:* "turning" the animal soul may need it to feel cared for first. A starved, frightened creature does not turn.

**(d)** CFT's breathing rhythms and compassionate imagery are out (D09, D11, D16). Only the stance carries over.

## 3.11 Coherence therapy and schema therapy: the old learning makes sense

**(a)** Coherence therapy (TM §1) and schema therapy (TM §11) agree that a symptom comes from an emotional learning that made sense when it was formed. Schema therapy has trial support for severe personality problems (Giesen-Bloo et al., 2006, in our files); coherence therapy has case material only.

**(b)** Before any teaching, say why the animal part learned to brace: "Of course you hold on; when you let go before, something fell." It cannot update a learning it feels attacked for having.

**(c)** This is THE-ROOT's first step: the symptom is a solution. It is also the Tanya's sympathy for the animal soul's *wants*: life, safety, comfort. The animal soul is not wrong to want them. It is wrong only about who supplies them.

**(d)** No digging into childhood, no reparenting (TM §11). Stay with the present sentence.

## 3.12 Felt sense

**(a)** Gendlin observed that clients who did well tended to attend to a vague, bodily, not-yet-worded sense of their problem, and he taught this as "focusing" (Gendlin, 1978, *Focusing*, Everest House) [confirmed by search]. A review of the "experiencing" research reports that a higher level of experiencing in sessions goes with better outcomes across many studies (Hendricks, 2001, in *Humanistic Psychotherapies: Handbook of Research and Practice*, APA) [confirmed by search, as a citation; we did not read the chapter]. *Correlational; the method itself has few trials.*

**(b)** The animal part changes when the truth meets the place where it is felt, not when it is filed. Let a teaching be checked against "the whole sense of it", not only against logic.

**(c)** "Knowing in the bones" (da'at) and "until the heart answers" (MECHANISMS §4; ONE-PROCESS step 6).

**(d)** Focusing is a guided inner-attention method; asking a person to scan for a felt sense is a body technique and a feelings question, both out (D09, D25). Borrow only the respect for that layer of knowing.

## 3.13 Repetition, spacing, cues and habit

**(a)** Spaced practice beats massed practice for durable memory, across hundreds of experiments (Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted and Rohrer, 2006, *Psychological Bulletin*) [confirmed by search]. *Meta-analysis; strong.* If-then plans tied to a cue help people act (Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006) [in our files]. *Meta-analysis.* Habits form by repetition in a stable context, slowly (Wood and Rünger, 2016; Lally et al., 2010). Repeated security priming builds (Rowe et al., 2020).

**(b)** One big moment will not convince the animal part. Many small ones, spaced through days, tied to cues it already meets (waking, washing hands, eating, the car door, lying down), will. The same short line each time.

**(c)** This is the daily structure of Jewish life: Modeh Ani on waking, blessings over food, the Shema at night. THE-ROOT's "Ways to feel held" already lists them. *Ours:* the tradition has built a spaced-repetition schedule for the animal soul, attached to its own needs (waking, eating, sleeping).

**(d)** The tradition does these things because they are commanded and true, not as a learning schedule.

## 3.14 Wanting: give the want its real object

**(a)** Cue-triggered wanting can run far ahead of liking (Berridge and Robinson, 2016). Fighting a want head-on by suppression tends to rebound (BH 7). Habits do more of the work than willpower (Galla and Duckworth, 2015).

**(b)** Do not tell the animal part to stop wanting. Name what it actually wants under the craving (rest, relief, not to be alone, to put the weight down), and show it where that is already given.

**(c)** THE-ROOT's drinking example. And the Rashab's "water love", in which the natural mind itself comes to want G-dliness (Kuntres HaAvodah line 142): *ours*, the want is not killed but redirected to its source.

**(d)** For a real addiction, the body may need a doctor first (THE-ROOT guards; SAFETY.md). No teaching replaces detox.

## Part 4. What convinces the animal part *(ours, a synthesis)*

Put together, the evidence suggests that the animal soul takes "you are held" in when the message has these eight features. They are listed with their main support.

1. **In its own currency.** Safety, rest, food, warmth, being looked after, not being alone. Not "unity", "nullification" or "infinite light". (Attachment 2.4; experiential system 2.5; the Rashab's memale.)
2. **Concrete and present.** This breath, this chair, this bread, this morning. Not the universe in general. (Teasdale; Rashab, "general contemplation is no contemplation".)
3. **A checkable proof, not a claim.** Something the person can verify right now that does not fit "it's all on me". (Prediction error 3.1; felt fact 3.6.)
4. **While the old expectation is awake, but not flooding.** At the moment of bracing, or just after, not in a crisis peak and not in an abstract calm. (Reconsolidation needs reactivation; exposure needs a tolerable level; safety rules below.)
5. **As a picture.** One image beats a paragraph. (Imagery 3.5.)
6. **After its reason is honoured.** Say why it braced. (Coherence 3.11; THE-ROOT step 1.)
7. **Repeated, spaced, in many places, with one carried line.** (Inhibitory learning 3.2; spacing and cues 3.13; repeated priming 3.4.)
8. **Through a presence.** Warm, unhurried, specific, and pointing also to real people. (Safety cues 3.3; social baseline.)

**Why argument alone fails** *(ours)*. An argument addresses the rational system, which already agrees. The animal part holds its belief as a forecast. A forecast is only revised by an outcome. So the guide's job is to *arrange small outcomes*: moments where the forecast "I must hold everything" is checked and found wrong, and to name those moments as what they are. The Rashab says the same thing in his terms: the contemplation must be in matters the natural mind grasps, and must be particular, or it stays "surrounding" and never reaches the traits.

**How this maps onto memale** *(ours)*. Memale is the G-dliness that fills and gives life to each thing in its own measure. For the animal soul it can be put almost entirely in its own currency: - The life in this body is given, now. The heart beats without you. - The breath arrives by itself. You did not have to make it. - The food on the table came to you; its life is His word in it. - The ground under you is holding your weight; it is being held in being too. - You woke up. Someone gave you back.

Each line is (1) in the animal soul's currency, (2) concrete, (3) checkable, (5) picturable, and (7) attached to a daily cue. That is why memale is the animal soul's contemplation.

## Part 5. How to speak to the animal part: rules for the guide

These sit inside the owner's rules (D09, D11, D16, D25, D41, D42, D44), THE-ROOT's guards and SAFETY.md. Each gives the evidence behind it.

1. **Understand before you teach.** Reflect the specific weight they are carrying first. *(D41, D42; coherence therapy's "pro-symptom" step, 3.11.)*
2. **Honour the brace.** Say why holding on made sense. Never scold the animal part. *(3.11; Tanya ch. 29: "it is the man himself"; shame research in BH 8.)*
3. **Speak in its currency.** Safety, rest, being fed, being looked after, not alone. Translate every teaching into one of these before saying it. *(2.4, 2.5; Rashab, memale.)*
4. **One concrete thing in front of them.** Not "everything is held" but "this cup, this hour, this child asleep down the hall". *(Teasdale 1993; Kuntres HaTefillah line 49.)*
5. **Give a fact they can check right now.** The chair holding them; the breath coming by itself; the heartbeat without their help. Say it as a fact, not as an exercise, and never as a way to calm down. *(Prediction error 3.1; D11 felt fact; 3.6.)*
6. **Set it beside the old sentence.** Their sentence ("it's all on me") and the checkable fact, together, in one breath of text. *(Juxtaposition, TM §1; 3.1.)*
7. **One picture, then stop.** Prefer an image over an explanation, and say where the image breaks if it matters. *(Holmes and Mathews; Kuntres HaAvodah lines 46 and 204.)*
8. **Close before vast.** Start with what fills this moment (memale), not the infinity that surrounds (sovev). Vastness without holding frightens. *(Threat-awe, Gordon et al. 2017; Rashab line 44 and 77.5.)*
9. **Catch it at the moment, or right after.** The best time is when the brace is on: the 4am worry, the urge, the email. Offer one carried line for that moment. *(Reconsolidation needs reactivation; retrieval cues, Craske 2014.)*
10. **Not at the peak.** If they are flooded, presence and one practical step come first; teaching waits. *(D41: never hand meaning to someone in the fire; defence cascade, Kozlowska 2015.)*
11. **Use the moment it is already pleased.** Food, warmth, rest: name the gift while the good is there. *(Savoring, Quoidbach 2015; blessings.)*
12. **Ask for small proof, not belief.** "For the next ten minutes, let the outcome be His and do only your piece; see what happens." The proof is in what happens, not in a feeling. *(Expectancy violation, Craske 2014; behavioural experiments, TM §2.)*
13. **Same line, many places, many days.** Tie it to cues they already meet: waking, eating, the car, lying down. *(Spacing, Cepeda 2006; if-then plans, Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006; repeated priming, Rowe 2020.)*
14. **Expect the old fear to come back, and say so.** A return is not failure; the new learning has to be found again in a new place. *(Renewal, Bouton 2004; Tanya on the ordinary person's lifelong work.)*
15. **Name the want under the craving.** Rest, relief, not being alone. Show where it is already given; do not demand the want stop. *(Berridge and Robinson 2016; THE-ROOT.)*
16. **No praise as affirmation.** Widen the ground (what they love, who they look after), never "you're amazing". *(Wood et al. 2009; Cohen and Sherman 2014.)*
17. **Be a calm presence yourself.** Short sentences, unhurried, warm, no alarm words, no lecture. In a chat, your manner is the safety cue. *(Safety signals 3.3.)*
18. **Point to people too.** Being held also comes through others; one person reached counts. *(Social baseline; THE-ROOT "Through people".)*
19. **Never promise the feeling or the outcome.** Held is true even when not felt; it does not mean things turn out as wished. *(BH "Where psychology and the teaching part ways" 1, 3, 6; D25.)*
20. **No body techniques.** No breathing drills, scans, relaxation, postures, or "notice where you feel it in your body". *(D09, D11, D16; relaxation-induced anxiety and interoceptive risks, Part 6.)*

## Example phrasings

Plain words, no jargon, no question about feelings, no promised outcome. Each is a sample for a specific moment, not a script; the guide reflects their situation first (rule 1).

1. *(4am worry about money)* "Your body has been keeping you alive all night without a single instruction from you. Your heart didn't wait for you to solve the rent. The One doing that is the same One the rent is with."
2. *(Can't stop working)* "Of course you keep going. Stopping has always felt like falling. Here's the thing to check: the chair under you is holding all of your weight right now, and you're not doing anything to make it."
3. *(Craving at night)* "The part of you reaching for the bottle wants to put something heavy down. That's a real want. The weight it's trying to put down was never yours to carry. For tonight, this one line: I'm held right now, even in this."
4. *(Over a meal)* "Before you eat, look at the bread for a second. It came to you. The life in it is being given to you, now, by the One who gives you yours."
5. *(On waking)* "You woke up. You didn't do that. You were given back this morning. Say it with your lips, the old words: thank You for returning my soul."
6. *(Alone)* "You've had to be your own only safe place for a long time. You're not, though. You're being kept in being right now, not watched from far away, held from inside."
7. *(Panic about a child)* "You love her so much it has nowhere to go but fear. She is His child before she's yours. Your part is one hand out to her today. The rest of her road is held."
8. *(After a fall)* "You slipped. That happened, and you can repair it. What didn't happen: you didn't fall out of being held. There's nowhere outside Him to fall to."
9. *(Control, rereading the email)* "You've checked it enough to have done your part well. Try this: send it, and for the next ten minutes leave how it lands with Him. Then see what actually happened."
10. *(Heavy, exhausted)* "That tired body has been carrying you a long way. It's allowed to be fed and to rest. Looking after it is not weakness; it's looking after something given to you."
11. *(Facing something huge, frightening)* "You don't have to hold the size of this. Just this hour. The One who holds the whole of it is holding this hour, and you in it."
12. *(Before sleep)* "You can put the day down. You weren't the one holding it up. Lying down is letting yourself be held for the night, as you were last night."
13. *(A good moment, already pleased)* "That warm water on your hands right now: that's a small piece of being looked after. Let it count."
14. *(When it isn't landing)* "It's fine if you can't feel this today. Being held doesn't wait for you to feel it. Keep the one line; it does its work over many days."

## Part 6. Red flags and safety: when body-focused work can backfire

The owner's rule already keeps body techniques out. The evidence below explains why, and also warns about the *felt facts* that are allowed. SAFETY.md and the danger protocol override everything here.

**What the research says.** - **Relaxation can raise anxiety.** In chronically tense people, relaxation training increased tension for a substantial minority (Heide and Borkovec, 1983, *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*: about 31% with progressive relaxation and 54% with a focused meditation reported more tension) [confirmed by search]. *Small experiment; a classic, often reproduced as a phenomenon.* - **Attention to the body can feed anxiety and panic.** Anxiety involves noisy, over-weighted signals from the body (Paulus and Stein, 2010). For someone afraid of their heartbeat or breath, "feel your heart beating" can trigger the very fear. D11 already says: a symptom the person fears is never made the thing to feel. - **Depression and the body.** Interoceptive disturbance is part of depression (Harshaw, 2015). Numbness and heaviness may make "feel the life in you" land as proof that something is wrong with them. - **Trauma and dissociation.** Under extreme threat the defence cascade can end in shutdown, and dissociation is part of that (Schauer and Elbert, 2010, *Zeitschrift für Psychologie*) [confirmed by search]; (Kozlowska et al., 2015). For people with trauma, body focus can bring up intrusive sensations or push them toward numbness and "unreality". - **Meditation has adverse effects,** including anxiety, re-experiencing of trauma and depersonalisation, in a minority (Farias et al., 2020; Britton, 2019; Britton et al., 2021) [in our files; part-b]. Self-loss is bliss to one person and illness to another, depending on whether they still feel in control (Deane, Miller and Wilkinson, 2020) [in our files]. - **Reassurance can become a ritual.** In anxiety and OCD, reassurance seeking is common and may keep the problem going (Kobori and Salkovskis, 2013, *Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapy*) [confirmed by search]. *Correlational.* *Ours:* "you are held", asked for again and again to quiet a doubt, can become one more reassurance ritual.

**What the guide does.** 1. **Fear of the body.** If they fear their heart, breath, dizziness, or a body symptom (panic, health anxiety), never use that sensation as the felt fact. Use something outside: the chair, the floor, the bread, the light. 2. **Trauma signs** (flashbacks, "my body isn't safe", past abuse, startle, being touched or held as a danger): no body facts at all. "Held" can itself be a trigger word for someone who was held down. Use "kept", "given", "not alone", "looked after"; stay with the present and with things outside the body. 3. **Dissociation or unreality** ("I don't feel real", "I'm watching myself", "nothing feels like mine"): no teaching about the self not being a separate thing, no "nothing but Him", no vastness. Ground in plain, ordinary, concrete things and practical next steps. This matches the part-b counterfeits and SCIENCE.md §5. 4. **Psychosis signs** (voices, special messages, being controlled, grandiose missions): do not frame inner experiences as souls, G-d's voice, or hidden worlds (MECHANISMS §9 contraindication). Keep to plain support; follow SAFETY.md. 5. **Depression, numbness, heaviness:** no "feel the life in you". Lead with one small act (MECHANISMS §8, behavioural activation), and "held even when you can't feel it". 6. **Repeated asking for the line to quiet a doubt** (scrupulosity, OCD-like checking): gently stop repeating it. Give one action, not another reassurance. Never feed checking which soul a thought came from (MECHANISMS §9). 7. **If something makes it worse, stop it.** Do not push through. Say it is all right to leave it today (D09: "not today" is not failure). 8. **Danger** (self-harm, not eating or sleeping for days, withdrawal, someone at risk): the safety protocol overrides all teaching (THE-ROOT guards; SAFETY.md).

## Part 7. Where the whole bridge stops

1. **Feeling versus fact.** Every mechanism above changes how safe a person *feels*. The teaching says they *are* held, whether they feel it or not. A guide that measures success by calm has turned the teaching into a technique (BH "Where psychology and the teaching part ways" 1 and 3).
2. **Turning is more than updating.** Reconsolidation can change a fear. The Rashab's "turning" ends in the animal soul's *love of G-d*. No study measures that, and the Rashab himself leaves open how far it goes (Kuntres HaAvodah line 180).
3. **Subduing is not second best.** Inhibition is weaker in the lab than updating. In the Tanya, subduing the animal soul is the ordinary person's whole service and gives "satisfaction above". The guide must never make a person feel that a fear that stays means failure.
4. **The animal soul is not a brain system.** The map in 2.8 is a set of analogies. The sources speak of a soul from kelipat nogah with its own intellect, which can be refined. Psychology speaks of circuits and learning. Use one to help speak about the other; do not identify them.
5. **Who was studied.** Almost all of this was done with Western, mostly non-Jewish samples, and much in laboratories with students. None with Chabad contemplation.
6. **Replication.** Priming, embodiment and human reconsolidation have all had replication trouble. The *conversational* moves borrowed here cost little if the strong claims fail: a true fact said kindly at the right moment does no harm even if it erases nothing.
