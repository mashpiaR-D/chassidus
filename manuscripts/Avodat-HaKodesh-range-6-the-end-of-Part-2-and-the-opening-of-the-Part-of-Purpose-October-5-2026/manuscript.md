## The shape of the range

This range holds the answer to the owner's main question: why was the world made? Ibn Gabbai gives it straight, against the Rambam.

1. **Why souls come back into bodies at the resurrection** (911-930). The task was blocked by the evil inclination. When it is gone, body and soul will serve with nothing in the way.
2. **The world to come is body and soul** (931-1014). Against the Rambam, who said souls only. As the service was in body and soul, so the reward. The righteous will live from the light, as Moses did on the mountain.
3. **Joy** (1015-1027). Joy is what happens when a will is fulfilled. So there is joy above when we do what we were made for. Serve in joy, and joy wakes above.
4. **The purpose of all that exists** (1029-1048). The Rambam said there is no final purpose, only "so He willed". Ibn Gabbai: there is a purpose, and it is the need on high. Man, the true servant, is the purpose of all worlds.
5. **Man is greater than the angels** (1049-1093). Not man stripped bare, but man completed by the Torah. The whole world serves him so that he can be free to serve.
6. **His service is dearer than the angels'** (1094-1115). Because only man's service is needed above. "The one who unites is the purpose of all existence."

## The purpose of creation: the heart of this range

**The question and the Rambam's answer.** "The perfect maker is perfect according to the perfection of his purpose" (line 1029). So we must look for the purpose of all He made, and "if we find it ... we will know how great our duty in holy service is, to complete the purpose we were formed for." The Rambam (Guide 3:13) said the question has no final answer: even if all is for man, and man is to serve G-d, "what is the purpose of his serving? He gains nothing if all He made serve Him." So the answer ends in "so He willed, or so His wisdom decreed" (line 1031).

**Ibn Gabbai's answer.** The Rambam's argument only works if service does nothing above. But it does:

> ויודה כי תכלית המציאות כולו עליונים ותחתונים הוא האדם האמיתי וזה וזה אמנם נעשו ובאו לצורך גבוה והוא התכלית האחרון (Avodat HaKodesh 3:1:5; line 1033)
> And he would admit that the purpose of all existence, upper and lower, is the true man; and both of these were made and came for a need on high, and that is the final purpose.

The world had to exist because man is a body and needs a place, and the upper world needs service, and service needs a doer (line 1033). So:

> שלמות העליונים, הוא העולם האחרון ההוא ובו נשלם הייחוד ... כן אין שלמות העולם ההוא כי אם בעולם הזה הגשמי, לפי שהעולם ההוא צריך אל העבודה (Avodat HaKodesh 3:2:4; line 1045)
> The completion of the upper ones is that last world [Malchut], and in it the unity is completed ... and so the completion of that world is only in this physical world, because that world needs service.

The Zohar calls this "the world of deed, to complete the end of the Thought" (line 1046). The chain is short: the world is for man; man is for service; service is for the unity; the unity is a need on high.

> צריך אם כן אל מייחד, והמציאות כלו לא נמצא כי אם לזה התכלית, נמצא אם כן שהמייחד והעובד השלם הוא תכלית המציאות (Avodat HaKodesh 3:6:17; line 1110)
> So there must be one who unites, and all existence exists only for this purpose; so the one who unites, the complete servant, is the purpose of all existence.

**All creatures serve man, and man serves his Maker.** The sun ripens his food so that "he will be free always and ready for the upper service and the repair of the Glory, which was the cause of his being" (line 1092). And:

> כל הנבראים לשמש את האדם והאדם לשמש את קונו (Avodat HaKodesh 3:5:12; line 1092)
> All creatures are to serve man, and man is to serve his Maker.

**But the will itself has no reason.** Here is a turn that matters very much for the comparison with Chabad. Ibn Gabbai does not deny that the will is above reasons. He says both things at once:

> כי עם שהרצון העליון היה זה ואין לשאול ולהשיב עליו כי אין טעם ברצון, הנה התכלית ידוע למבקשי יי' והוא ייחוד השם הגדול בכבודו שהוא צורך גבוה (Avodat HaKodesh 3:6:21; line 1114)
> For though the upper will was this, and one cannot ask or answer about it, for there is no reason in a will, still the purpose is known to those who seek the Lord, and it is the unity of the Great Name with His Glory, which is a need on high.

And the verses that say man's good and bad do not reach Him ("if you are righteous, what do you give Him?") are said "in relation to the single Master, root of all roots" (Ein Sof). But for the Glory, "to unite the head of the Thought with its end ... it is a full need, and good and bad all touch the upper heart, for as the awakening below, so it wakes above" (line 1114).

*Ours.* This is almost the Tanya's own shape. Tanya ch. 36 says the purpose of creation is that "the Holy One desired to have a dwelling place in the lower worlds", and adds "about a desire one does not ask for a reason". Ibn Gabbai: "there is no reason in a will", and still its goal is known. The difference is where each puts the goal. For ibn Gabbai, it is the unity of the sefirot, the Glory, which "needs" us; Ein Sof itself is untouched. For Chabad, the desire is in the Essence itself, above the sefirot, and what it wants is a home *here*, in the lowest world, not only a repair above. Ibn Gabbai's lower world is the workshop; Chabad's lower world is the home. Both agree the lower one's service is what the whole plan was for.

**Why knowing this changes a person.** The last lines of the range:

> ומי שהשכיל וידע כי נברא לזה התכלית והוא תקון הכבוד ... אז ישתדל להשלים הכוונה במציאותו ולא יסתפק בעבודתו (Avodat HaKodesh 3:6:22; line 1115)
> One who understood and knew that he was created for this purpose, which is the repair of the Glory ... will strive to complete the purpose in his being, and will not be satisfied with his service.

"His heart will be secure in his Torah and good deeds, and he will know that his duty is very great", unlike one who thinks "there is only will" and serves for no purpose (line 1115). *Ours.* This is why the guide should teach the purpose early. Knowing *why* makes service both steady and hungry.

## The unity of G-d

**The Rambam's unity is only negation.** The unity the philosophers spread is "the unity of Aristotle", and the Rambam wrote that unity is "to negate, not to affirm". But "the unity received from the prophets is known to be neither affirmation nor negation" (line 1041). The promised end: "they will all know Me, from small to great" (line 1041). *Ours.* This ties to Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah and to F-level teaching that the Essence is beyond both "is" and "is not". It will be treated in chapter 69 (range 11).

**Israel completes the Holy Name.** From the Zohar:

> ישראל שלימו דשמיה קדישא אינון וכד ישראל לתתא אשתלמאן בעובדיהו, כביכול שמא קדישא אשתלים (Avodat HaKodesh 3:6:20; line 1113)
> Israel are the completeness of the Holy Name; and when Israel below are completed in their deeds, so to speak, the Holy Name is completed.

**The tzaddikim join heaven and earth.** "The righteous are called by His Name ... for they are the ones who tie and unite heaven with earth" (line 962).

**"You redeemed Yourself."** The Exodus was also for a need on high: "Were it not written, one could not say it: Israel said to the Holy One, You redeemed Yourself" (line 1108).

## Joy

This short chapter is one of the most usable in the book for the guide.

> השמחה תאמר על ערבות הפקת רצון האדם והשלם כוונתו בקיום מעשיו, והעצב יאמר על הפך זה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:44:1; line 1015)
> Joy is said of the sweetness of a person's will being brought out and his intent being completed in the doing of his deeds; and sadness is said of the opposite.

This is said of G-d too. When man did not fulfill the intent, "He was saddened to His heart" (Gen. 6:6); when he does, "the Lord rejoices in His works" (line 1015). "Both sadness and joy, all of it touches this heart" (line 1015). The Midrash calls G-d "the heart of Israel": "Rock of my heart" (line 1016).

> כי העבודה בשמחה למטה מעוררת השמחה למעלה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:44:3; line 1017)
> For service in joy below wakes joy above.

Sadness comes from the bad side of the Tree of Knowledge; it comes to one walking to a mitzvah, or giving charity, "so that they will not do it, and if they do, they will be sad in it" (line 1017). Joy is "the completion of the service", and in it "the holy upper spirit comes down and sparkles in them, and the joy reaches the heart" (line 1020). So one does not stand to pray "from sadness or from laziness ... but from the joy of a mitzvah" (line 1022). Rav Beruna joined redemption to prayer once, "and laughter did not leave his mouth all day" (line 1023).

*Ours.* Chabad teaches the same at length (Tanya ch. 26, 31-33; "sadness is not a sin, but it closes the heart"). Ibn Gabbai adds a strong reason: joy *is* what fulfillment feels like, above and below. The guide can offer a state: **His joy in me**. When I do what I was made for, there is joy above, and I can feel a drop of it.

## The body shares the reward

**Body and soul toiled together.** "The body would be robbed of its due, for it toiled on the eve of Shabbat and did not eat on Shabbat" (line 1013). So the reward must be in body and soul (line 951). *Ours.* Chabad agrees (the resurrection as the height of reward, since the body is rooted in the Essence; Tanya ch. 36). The guide can use this to honor the body in service, not to fight it.

**Living from the light.** The manna was "an offspring of the light", and Moses on the mountain was fed "by the light itself, the radiance of the Shechinah" (line 967). "Not by bread alone does man live": life does not come from the bread "but from the will of the Lord that clings to it and keeps them" (line 968). *Ours.* Tie to technique "eating to raise" (range 5): what keeps me alive in the bread is His will in it.

**The dance.** "The Holy One will make a circle dance for the righteous in the Garden of Eden, and He sits among them, and each one points to Him with his finger" (line 997). A circle "has no head and no end", a picture of delight without end; "the Shechinah in the middle and they around" (line 997).

## Man and the angels

Man bare of Torah is lower than the stars, and the philosophers are right about themselves (line 1049). But man completed by Torah is above the angels (line 1101). Why?

- **His soul is from a higher place.** Angels come "from one place"; the souls of the righteous "from two levels joined in one" (line 1081).
- **Only he grows.** All creation stays at the level it was made with. Man, busy with Torah and good deeds, "acquires a new form at every moment" (קונה צורה חדשה בכל עת, line 1091).
- **Only he can change and still be steadfast.** The angel "has only one way ... so there is no possibility of change in him". Man holds both ways, good and bad, and can still be firm; "this is a wondrous level, in which the form resembles its Former" (line 1077).
- **Only his service is needed above** (line 1094).

**The dearest praise comes from the farthest place.** At midnight the King knocks at the palace gate; "and the praise that rises from this world, which is far, this is more pleasing to the Holy One than all" (line 1095). From the Heichalot:

> כי אין לי הנאה בכל עולמי שבראתי כאותה שעה שעיניכם נשואות בעיני ועיני בעיניכם בשעה שאתם אומרים לפני קדוש (Avodat HaKodesh 3:6:3; line 1096)
> For I have no pleasure in all My world that I created like that hour when your eyes are lifted to My eyes and My eyes to your eyes, when you say before Me "Holy".

*Ours.* This is a state the guide can offer directly: **eyes meeting**, at the Kedushah or any time. It ties to the Kuzari's "the Presence before you" and to Chabad's teaching that G-d's pleasure comes from the lower world (Tanya ch. 36; Torah Or).

**The angels need our hand.** Angels offered to lift Jacob's stone to Jerusalem "only if you put your hand with ours" (line 1066). "The upper ones need help from the lower ones."

**Children's breath.** "The world stands only on the breath of schoolchildren." Even though they cannot yet unite with knowing, "they give strength to the Assembly of Israel in exile by the breath of their mouth ... and each one checks up to where his hand reaches" (line 1100). *Ours.* No one's service is too small. This is a comfort for beginners.

## States of consciousness

- **His joy in me** (lines 1015-1020). Proposed below. Ties to Chabad's joy states (Tanya ch. 26, 31) and the guide's joy entries.
- **Eyes meeting** (line 1096). Proposed below.
- **The purpose held** (lines 1110, 1115). The steady and hungry heart of one who knows what he is for. Proposed below. Ties to "alive in the task" (range 5).
- **New form every moment** (line 1091). The sense of growing, of not being fixed. Ties to the Chabad teaching that the soul's walking (mehalech) is what sets man above the standing angels (Zechariah 3:7; Likkutei Torah). Kept as part of "the purpose held".

## Meditation and practice

- **Ask why, then serve.** Before prayer or study, recall the chain in one breath: the world for me, me for service, service for His unity, His unity His need. *Ours.* A short form of the book's argument (lines 1092, 1110). Ties to technique 22.
- **Stand to pray from joy.** Not from sadness or laziness (line 1022). Before prayer, find one thing that makes the mitzvah sweet.
- **Lift the eyes at "Holy".** At the Kedushah, raise the eyes, and hold that His eyes are on yours (line 1096). A proposed technique.

## The big human questions

**Why was the world made?** For the unity of the Name, which needs a doer; so for man; and so for me (lines 1033, 1110). And behind that, a will with no reason (line 1114).

**Is man small or great?** Small without Torah, greater than angels with it (lines 1101-1102).

**Will the body matter in the end?** Yes. It toiled, so it shares the reward (line 1013).

**Why be joyful?** Because joy is the feel of a purpose fulfilled, above and below (line 1015).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

They serve the arguments above.
