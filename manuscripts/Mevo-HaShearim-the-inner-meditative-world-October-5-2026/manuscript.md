# Mevo HaShe'arim: the inner meditative world

*Text read:* Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Mevo HaShearim (Piaseczno, 1931-1943).txt  
*Range:* whole file, lines 1-408

---
The Rebbe calls this the opening gate of *Chovat HaAvreichim* (The Obligation of Young Married Men). By his own title page (line 4) it is part three of the series, after *Chovat HaTalmidim* and *Hachsharat HaAvreichim*, and meant only for someone who has already worked through both. He wrote it in the 1930s, before his wife's death in 1937 (line 154). The Warsaw ghetto chapters were added later. Most of the book is a history of how God's light came down to Israel: prophets, the Talmud, Kabbalah, then Chassidus. Along the way it argues that the body, the feelings, and even the bad drives belong in the service of God.

The last two chapters say what the whole series is for. The goal is to learn "the laws and orders" of a person's own body, feelings and thoughts, so that a person knows himself well enough to turn all of it toward God (lines 370, 389, 397-398). The most practical material on the inner life is therefore in chapters 2, 3, 8, 9 and 10.

Line numbers refer to the source file. Hebrew is quoted as it stands, with abbreviations left in.

**What this file does not contain.** It has no method of *hashkatah* (quieting), and it does not name the group *bnei machshavah tovah*. Its nearest equivalent is the *chevraya kedoshah* (the holy fellowship around a rebbe). He treats that at length in chapter 8 and points back to *Hachsharat HaAvreichim* ch. 10 for how to set one up again (line 309). Melody gets one short passage (line 303). The book promises several times that the practical detail will come "in the body of the book" (lines 120, 125, 395, 396). This volume is the gateway to that detail, not the detail itself.

## 1. What the range is: its structure

- **Title page (lines 1-6).** Named as gate one of *Chovat HaAvreichim*, for those who have finished the earlier two books.
- **Ch. 1 (lines 7-95): what a prophet really was.** A prophet was not mainly a fortune-teller or a moral preacher. Prophecy was a level of service: the body refined until it becomes soul, so that nothing separates the person from the holy. God wants all Israel to be prophets. The "work of prophecy" continues now, even though prophecy itself has ended. Prophets for all generations drew the light down, and prophets for each generation prepared ordinary people to receive it. After them, the Tanna'im and Amora'im put the same light into a form the human intellect can hold. Torah thought is a real presence, like a soul, not an empty "shadow" like arithmetic (lines 83-93).
- **Ch. 2 (lines 96-130): Kabbalah as prophecy through the intellect.** The prophets saw visions in the imagination. The kabbalists received the same prophecy through understanding. To study Kabbalah truly, a person has to draw out of himself an "abstracted" knowing, his own inner *atzilut*, by stripping every idea of its physical clothing. This chapter has his clearest description of an inner experience of God's greatness (lines 126-128).
- **Ch. 3 (lines 131-170): what Chassidus added.** Before the Baal Shem Tov, light came down *inside* the vessels, and the body and feelings had to be subdued. The Baal Shem Tov revealed that the vessels themselves are light, so the body and its traits are raised up, not crushed. Bad traits have holy roots. Arousal matters more than *kavanot* (Kabbalistic intentions). The chapter ends with concrete sensory techniques: looking at the stars, picturing the Temple, picturing the grave (line 169).
- **Ch. 4 (lines 171-189): Kabbalah seen through Chassidus.** The Kabbalah's images of a divine body (head, arms, and so on) are not mere metaphor. The upper worlds really are in man. "Kabbalah bent the heavens down to a person's wisdom. Chassidus spread them in the street and enthroned the One who dwells in heaven inside man" (lines 188-189).
- **Ch. 5 (lines 190-222): the two paths within Chassidus, and the rebbe and his chassidim.** One path rouses the soul through the mind and contemplation (he describes it in the Tanya's own terms, line 195). The other works through plain, forceful service and simple faith (Karlin, Beis Aharon, line 196-198). An inserted essay (lines 205-222) describes the unspoken bond between a rebbe and his chassidim, and the rebbe's own inner life of self-doubt.
- **Ch. 6 (lines 223-254): the Zohar and later Kabbalah.** Later kabbalists measured and named what they saw. The Zohar tells the vision itself. He illustrates this with a parable of two men describing a king's palace (lines 230-233).
- **Ch. 7 (lines 255-277): what a chassid is.** *Chassid* comes from *chesed* ("the long line"): a person whose soul is not hidden in his body. Simple people become chassidim by attaching themselves to a rebbe. God is found "on the lowest earth" (line 271).
- **Ch. 8 (lines 278-331): the journey to the rebbe, then and now.** A long, moving portrait of the old chassid's journey and Shabbos with his rebbe, set against the exhausted chassid of the 1930s. The loss of the *chevraya kedoshah*, and a plea to restore *kest* (years of support for young married men) so that a core group can form again.
- **Ch. 9 (lines 332-370): raising the body's drives.** Fasting and self-mortification against "sweetening" the drives. A reading of the Talmud on *mazal* as temperament. A key point: suppressing a drive leaves it in the evil inclination's hands, where it flares up later, while using its energy for holiness takes it away from the evil inclination.
- **Ch. 10 (lines 371-408): the method for this generation.** Chassidus has to come down to where the generation is. There is a lament over the persecution of the 1930s (lines 377-383). There are "laws of the soul": habit and strong emotion engrave an idea, and gazing at a thought makes it melt. He solves the problem of being unable to picture God's Name. Self-knowledge is necessary for knowing God. The book closes with a prayer to see into the hidden places of one's own soul (line 408).

## 2. The method: concrete instructions for inner practice

**2.1 Sit silent for a moment and let the heart speak (line 106).** He gives this as a small taste, open to any learner, of how revelation comes through the mind. When two readings of a text both have strong proofs, the learner "sits silent for a moment and says: this is the meaning, my heart tells me so": > ואז יושב ודומם כשעתא חדא ואומר כך היא הכוונה, לבי אומר לי כך (106)

He explains what that "saying of the heart" is: > התנוצצות נפשו והסתכלותה בתורה למעלה מן הראיות וההוכחות (106)

A related point, from the *Me'or Einayim*: sometimes a person feels that a verse holds something new before his mind knows what it is. He should then study, and the new idea arrives. "That earlier feeling is a spark of the holy spirit": > הרגשה הקודמת ניצוץ רוה״ק הוא (106)

**2.2 The exercise of abstraction (hafshatah), for studying Kabbalah and Chassidus (lines 120-126).** Step one is to picture the concrete thing, for example kindness as one person giving another money: > לכן מצייר לעצמו חסד שעושה איש עם חברו בעולם הזה ונותן לו מעות (120)

Step two is to strip the money away and hold only the wish to do good: > לכן מפשיט את החסד לעצמו מן המעות (120)

Step three is repetition until a new faculty opens: > וכאשר מרבה האיש להרגיל את עצמו בהפשטה כזו (121)

What opens is "an unclarified grasp", which the person cannot inspect directly (line 121). He is told not to test it by asking himself "what is goodness in itself?", because that question pulls him back into ordinary thinking (line 121). Duration matters: after studying "for some time" in this knowing, which is "not locked in the limits of the bodily senses", a spark of prophecy may appear in it: > שעל ידי שמרבה ללמוד איזה זמן בהדעת הזה שאינו מסוגר ואסור בגבולות הגופניות החושיות (126)

The preparation comes first: the mitzvot in practice and simple Torah study (line 129).

**2.3 Rouse the soul through the senses (lines 169-170, 179, 303).** These are the techniques he collects from earlier masters: - Look up at the stars in their courses (Noam Elimelech): > וביותר יכול האדם לראות רוממות אל בהביטו השמימה ורואה הכוכבים במסילותם (169) - Picture, as though seeing it with your own eyes, the Temple built and yourself standing in the Holy of Holies: > שיצייר כאילו רואה בעיניו ממש שביהמ״ק בנוי והוא עומד בבית קדשי הקדשים (169) - Before prayer, picture yourself lying in the grave in great suffering, and being told "stand up and pray" (Beis Aharon): > שיצייר לו קודם התפילה ששוכב בקבר ביסורים גדולים ואומרים לו עמוד והתפלל (169) - Use bodily "stratagems" as well. The Baal Shem Tov commanded that one look for *tachbulot* (devices): > גם בתחבולות גופניות לעורר את חושיו ונטיות הגופניות לקדושה ישמש (170) - Drinking a little spirits with chassidim, dancing, and chassidic conversation are "causes that awaken the workings of Chassidus in a person" (line 305): > סבות הן לעורר את פעולות החסידות בקרב האיש (305) - He adds that one needs new devices from time to time, and if one does not work, try another: > ואם לא פעלה עצה זו ינסה בזו (302)

**2.4 Melody (line 303).** The Baal Shem Tov commanded singing before almost every teaching of Torah: > וכן הנגינה שהבעש״ט זצ״ל כמעט קודם כל אמירת תורה צוה לנגן (303)

The Beis Aharon (in Yiddish) says that the greatest faith a Jew has, singing is its head: > איז זינגען דער ראש דערפון (303)

That is the whole treatment of melody in this book.

**2.5 Practical rules for prayer from *Likkutim Yekarim* (line 304).** This is the closest the book comes to a timed, ordered procedure: - Seclude yourself (*hitboded*), alone or even with others in the room: > איך יתבודד אם לבדו, או אף כשישנם אנשים בחדר (304) - Before prayer, do not say a lot of Tehillim with *deveikut*, so that the body is not worn out before the prayer itself: > קודם התפילה לא יאמר הרבה תהלים בדביקות כדי שלא יחלש גופו (304) - On Yom Kippur, say the earlier prayers in a "small" state of mind so as to have strength for Ne'ilah. - Posture of the eyes: in a small state, pray from the siddur. When attached to the upper world, close the eyes: > כשהאיש בקטנות יתפלל מתוך סידור, אבל כשהוא דבוק בעולם העליון אז יותר טוב לסגור עיניו (304) - Pacing: hope to say half or most of the prayer in *deveikut*. When strength fails, continue with a smaller intention: > ואח״כ כשיחלש, יתפלל כפי יכולתו בכוונה קטנה (304)

**2.6 Start with the mouth. Don't wait for the heart (line 198, the Karlin / Beis Aharon way).** > האיש יכניס עצמו בהדיבורים ובעבדות, וההתלהבות תבא ממילא (198) > שלא ימתין עד שיהי׳ בהתעוררות לבו (198)

Even if a person feels no life at all and is "like an ox to the yoke and a donkey to the load", he should take pleasure in that, and the taste will come: > ואפילו אם אינו מרגיש חיות רק הוא כשור לעול וכחמור למשא (198)

**2.7 Kindle the great love with a small love (lines 166-168).** Following the Maggid, a person who cannot rouse love of God should start from what he already loves, such as his children or even his money, the way one lights straw to catch the logs: > אז מדליק מקודם קש או תבן (166)

A variant: if a bad love stirs, reflect that this love comes from the divine *chesed* and ask how you are dirtying it. That thought itself turns into love of God: > גם מזה יתעורר באהבה לד׳ (143)

**2.8 Put the drive's own energy into holiness (lines 353-364).** Do not only restrain the fiery temperament. Carry it into prayer: > ואם יסוד האש מתגבר בו לכעס מכניסו בהתלהבות הקדושה (364)

Reduce fasting and self-mortification step by step. The Noam Elimelech says to break desire "little by little": > צריך האדם להתאמץ במעט מעט לשבור כח התאות (337)

**2.9 Two "laws of the soul" to work with (lines 388, 390).** - *Strong emotion engraves.* Habit already bends our faculties toward holiness without our knowing it: a Jew grabs his falling hat without thinking. A great fright stays with a person after the danger is gone. So a good thought can be engraved "through much excitement": > שבאם יתרגש האיש מאיזה התרגשות יותר, יחקק הרעיון בו הרבה אף נגד רצונו (388) > היינו ע״י התרגשות מרובה (388) - *Looking at a thought makes it melt.* He refers back to *Hachsharat HaAvreichim* ch. 8. When a foreign thought grips the mind during prayer, look straight at it: > שכאשר מביטים על מחשבה אחת שבנו, היא נמסת (390)

**2.10 Picturing God's Name: the indirect method (lines 390-395).** The books praise picturing the Name YHVH before one's eyes ("I have set God before me always"). They even call it a bad sign if a person cannot: > וסימן לא טוב לו אם לא יוכל לציירו (390)

He looks at this with sympathy: the harder you try, the less you can see it, until the forehead sweats. The reason is a law of the imagination. Try to picture your own father "properly" and the picture fades: > וככל שתרצה להתאמץ יותר לדמות אותם בדמיונך יותר תחדל תמונתם ממך לשעה זו (390)

Effort strengthens the ego-intellect, and the imagination weakens: > שמגביר על ידי זה את דעתו ושכל האנכיות שלו, אז כח הדמיון נעשה כהה אף חולף לגמרי (390)

He says he has not found a remedy in any book: > לא ראיתי באיזה ספר עצה לזה (391)

His remedy is to start by thinking of something you have no particular wish to picture: > רק יתחיל לחשוב מדבר שאין רצונו בו (395)

For example, remember standing in shul while the Torah was read, and "suddenly they called you up": > והנה פתאום קראו אותו לתורה (395)

Recall the details: being a little startled, not knowing which side of the rebbe to walk up on, the reader beginning "And God spoke to Moshe". In this way the whole verse, the Name included, appears in the imagination without interference: > ובאופן זה יצייר את כל הפסוק וגם שם הוי׳ בדמיונו, מבלי אשר יפריעהו יצרו (395)

He adds a preparation for this: whenever you are called to the Torah, look carefully at the writing and the Names so that they are engraved in you. The imagination itself also needs training: > גם את הדמיון בעצמו לחזק צריכים לימוד (395)

**2.11 Observe yourself (lines 389, 397, 401).** The method under all of this is introspection, not a list of tips: > שיכיר את עצמו ואת פנימיות נפשו וגופו ויביט עליהם וידע את חקותיהם (389)

This covers the healthy soul too, not only its illnesses: > רק גם את הנפש הבריאה, הרגשותי׳, רחשי׳ וכל זעזועי׳ (397)

The soul can only be studied from inside: > לכן רק בהסכלות עצמית היא נחקרת (401)

He distinguishes this sharply from the secular psychology of his day (lines 401-403).

**2.12 Take small steps (line 386).** > יפסיעו נא פסיעה קטנה אחר פסיעה קטנה (386)

## 3. The inner experience, in his own images

**3.1 Glimpsing God's greatness during Kabbalah study (lines 127-128).** This is the fullest description of an inner state in the book. After long study in the abstracted knowing, a person sometimes gets a flash of God's greatness that he has never seen: > זוכה לפעמים שיתנוצץ לפניו מן גדלות ד׳, שלא ראה עד עכשיו (127)

He sees it beyond all the worlds he has studied, "with a sight that is not of the senses, and not imaginings": > ובראי׳ בלתי חושית ולא דמיונות (127)

His soul shakes and is annulled, to the point that he is embarrassed and afraid even to pray, and certainly to ask for his own needs: > ונפשו מזדעזעת ומתבטלת עד שיש שגם מתבושש וירא אף להתפלל (127)

He then strengthens himself to pray anyway, because God wants that bond. He adds that the experience cannot be produced at will. It comes by itself, and the person only prepares: > ולמה כמעט שאין בידו לראות זאת ורק כממילא באה לו והוא רק מתכשר שיזכה לה (128)

**3.2 The abstract knowing: certain but unclarified (lines 121, 125).** It is "an unclarified grasp". You cannot look at it and say "I think this". Yet it is more certain than any human reasoning: > רק ברורה וודאית לו יותר מכל שכליים האנושיים (125)

Without it, Kabbalah and Chassidus feel "dry": > רק דברים יבשים רואה לפניו (122)

**3.3 Seeing the world as God (lines 133, 348, 316).** The Baal Shem Tov's teaching: > ואז כשאתה מסתכל על העולם, אתה מביט על אלקים ואלקים עליך (133)

This is not an idea about the world. It is "actual sight", until the whole person trembles from it: > רק ראי׳ ממשית (348) > עד שכולו יזדעזע ממנה (348)

The rebbe sometimes tells his close group what he sees in prayer: saying "from world to world You are God", he sees the worlds with God filling them. Saying "this is my God", he feels or sees Him speaking to him face to face: > ממש רואה העולם והעולם והוא ית׳ מלא מהם (316) > מרגיש או רואה אותו ית׳ המדבר אליו לנוכח (316)

The group feels "his soul that went out as he spoke", and they begin to see something of what he sees (line 316).

**3.4 Carried into another world (line 228).** After studying the Zohar with others for a while: > מרגיש שגם הוא נעתק עתה אל עולם קדוש ומובדל (228)

**3.5 The palace flooded with light (line 232).** In the parable of the second visitor, who cannot keep to measurements because he is so amazed, the palace had so much light: > ארמון כ״כ יפה וכ״כ אור בחדריו עד שדומה הי׳ לו שהשמש בעצמה ירדה ונמצאה בהארמון (232)

**3.6 The chassid's journey as an inner arc (lines 282-286).** There is fear and longing on seeing the rebbe's town. In the rebbe's room, a dread so great the chassid does not know where he is. He then works out that this fear is really fear of God, who is present there: > אין זה כי אם ממציאת קדושת השם (284)

At the handshake, he wants to fold himself up and be the rebbe's footstool (line 285). His heart burns "like a furnace" (line 285). After two days: > כה עברו יומים קודם השבת וכבר ראה החסיד שנהפך הוא לאיש אחר (286)

On the way itself: "where is he going? to behold God's pleasantness. So he is returning to God" (line 282).

**3.7 The unspoken union (lines 207-209).** Between rebbe and chassidim, a union is made that adds joy and teshuvah "unknown even to them, what it is and where it comes from": > יחוד נעשה ביניהם ומוסיפים שמחה ותשובה, בלתי נודעה אף להם בעצמם מה היא ומאין באה (207) > וכל זה נעשה גם באין אומר ואין דברים (208)

**3.8 The deadened state (lines 294-295).** He describes the opposite state closely. It is the chassid of the 1930s, overwhelmed by poverty and by his children leaving observance: > ישנם זמנים שאין האיש ישראל יודע אם חי הוא (294) > איש בלא שאיפות ובלא כיסופים נשגבים (294)

At the rebbe's table his head is "like a roaring millstone", and the rebbe's words roll around in his mind: > דברים נשברים כחרסי כדים נשברים מתגלגלים במוחו (295)

He writes the same about his whole generation: "no mind and no flame" (line 382): > לא מוח ולא להב (382)

**3.9 The rebbe's emptiness (lines 214-219, 314).** When the rebbe has no close circle, he feels empty: > דומה לו כמה פעמים שאין בו לא תורה לא עבודה ולא שום דבר, ריק הוא ומאומה אין בו (314)

He explains that everything in him has contracted because he has no one to give to, like a heart that cannot rest unless it pours out its blood. The tzaddik joined to his chassidim has no separate "I": > לכן אין לו עצמיות ואנכיות, רק נמיכות רוח ושפלות (219)

**3.10 The prophet's inner fire (line 36).** He quotes Jeremiah: "it was in my heart like a burning fire, shut up in my bones" (line 36). His point is that the word of God fills a person as light, not only as information.

## 4. The self and God

This book supports the owner's thesis strongly, with one important qualification.

**4.1 You cannot reach God without first knowing your soul (line 398).** This is the most direct statement. From the Noam Elimelech: a person cannot walk constantly in *deveikut* and think of God's exaltation unless he first thinks about his own soul, "what is it". The soul resembles God in five ways, and without knowing it he will not know the Creator: > בלתי אם יתחיל לחשוב בהנפש שלו מ״ה הי״א נפשו (398) > וכשאין לו בה ידיעה גם בבורא ית׳ לא ידע (398)

He draws the conclusion himself. Self-knowledge is needed not only for practical advice, but because rising to a high grasp of God is impossible without it: > רק גם לעלות על ידה להשגה עילאה אי אפשר בלתי אם יכיר את נפשו תחילה (398)

**4.2 Know what is above: from yourself (line 187).** The Chassidic reading of "know what is above you" (Avos): > אם תרצה לדעת מה למעלה, ממך תוכל לדעת, כי במרום ואצלך הכל חד (187)

The worlds and *sefirot* are not "outside us but in us" (line 204), and Chassidus "enthroned the One who dwells in heaven inside man" (line 189).

**4.3 The true self is itself God's light (line 291).** What the chassid knows after a real journey to his rebbe: > וגם הוא החסיד הארת אלקותו ית׳ הוא, ולא שום מציאות אחרת בו זולתו ית׳ (291)

**4.4 Revealing the soul's root is the start of the holy spirit (line 262).** A chassid is a *ba'al nefesh*, "one whose soul is not buried and hidden in his body": > שאין נפשו טמונה ומוסתרת כ״כ בגופו (262) > כי זהו ראשית התקרבות לרוה״ק לגלות את שורש נפשו (262)

**4.5 "The main thing is that the person should *be*" (line 246).** Reading the Zohar's "why have I come and there is no man", he says: > העיקר הוא שהאיש יהי׳, כי בו הכל (246)

He means someone whose spirit is awake, not one who only says the words.

**4.6 Finding oneself in Torah (line 289).** The Maggid of Kozhnitz's grandson, R. Moshe of Mogielnica: > כאשר אני מסתכל בהתורה מוצא אנכי עצמי קצת (289)

(It goes on: "but when I look at the tzaddikim, I find no worth in myself at all.")

**4.7 Seeing oneself in the rebbe's words (line 209).** Because the chassidim's longings act on the rebbe's soul, each one hears the teaching as meant for him: > עד שכל אחד רואה את עצמו בהם (209)

**4.8 The closing prayer (line 408).** The book ends with a request to see into one's own soul, and through it to return to God: > האר נא את עיני, אשר בקרבי ובסתרי נפשי יסתכלון (408) > וכל אורך ממנה לפני יגלה, ואליך אותה ואותו אשובה (408)

("…and all Your light in it will be revealed before me, and to You I will return it and him.")

**The qualification.** The "self" he means is the holy soul, not the ego. The ego actively gets in the way. The *anochiyut* (the "I-intellect") dims the imagination (line 390), and the tzaddik's height is that he has no separate "I" (line 219). He also marks a limit on the comparison between soul and God. The soul fills the body as God fills the world, but God is not affected by the world, while the soul is affected by the body: > משא״כ הנפש נתפסת ומתעפלת מהגוף ומנפש הגופני (399)

So in his view you find God by finding the soul beneath the ego, and that soul has to be studied as it actually is, bodily conditioning included (line 400). Finding the self is not the same thing as finding God, but it is the required gate to Him.

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

**5.1 The doubt that says "it's only imagination" (lines 127-128).** After the glimpse of God's greatness, "the evil inclination sometimes tempts him to say this is only imagination": > הן מפתה היצר הרע אותו לפעמים לאמור שזהו רק דמיון (127)

His test is this: if it were self-made, a person could produce it whenever he wanted. It comes on its own, and one can only prepare for it (line 128). *Not being able to produce it* is the sign that it is real.

**5.2 He is lenient about "imagined" feeling (line 144).** This goes against the usual worry. A person who only *thinks* he is stirred with love or awe, where "it is only imagination", still has something good: > ורק דמיון הוא לו, ג״כ טובה היא (144)

The reason is that at the core of the Jew the stirring is real. Only the outer layers make it feel imaginary: > אבל לעצם הישראליות שבו בשעה זו התעוררות אמיתית היא (144)

**5.3 Thought without feeling is the real counterfeit (lines 156, 162).** Kabbalistic intentions held only in the mind, with no stirring of the traits, are like "intending that you are in Jerusalem while actually abroad": > ומה יועיל לאיש שיכוון שהוא בירושלים אם בפועל בחו״ל נמצא (156)

Someone who cannot do both should give up the *kavanot* and keep the arousal: > מוטב שיוותר על הכוונות ובשעת התפילה לא יכוון (162)

**5.4 Forcing the imagination backfires (line 390).** See 2.10. Straining to picture the Name strengthens the ego-intellect and makes the picture disappear. Also compare line 128: the highest states cannot be forced.

**5.5 Suppression is a false cure (lines 362-363).** Holding back a drive by force leaves it in the evil inclination's hands. A person may think he is pure, but the drive was only bent down and can flare up later, sometimes stronger: > ולימים יבואו שוב יכולים להתלקח ולהתלהב (363) > כטבעיות כל כחות האדם שכאשר מעצירים אותם זמן רב עוד מתרגזים ומתגברים יותר (363)

Or he is left with good will and nothing more, because his bodily forces all belong to the other side: > ונשאר לו רק רצון טוב לבד (363)

**5.6 Self-deception in fixing traits (line 396).** Traits have "mothers" and "offspring". Trying to fix an offspring trait before its mother is fixed fails, "or he only fools himself into thinking he fixed it": > או רק ירמה את עצמו, לחשוב שתיקנה ולא תיקנה (396)

**5.7 Sadness disguised as piety (lines 202, 336).** The Baal Shem Tov forbade fasting to the *Toldos*: "it is the work of black bile and sadness, and the Shechinah does not rest out of sadness": > כי זה מעשה מרה שחורה ועצבות ואין השכינה שורה לא מתוך עצבות (336)

Karlin allowed fasting only under three conditions, and warned against falling into sadness (line 202).

**5.8 Feeling worse is not failing (lines 211, 296-299).** A chassid may come home from his rebbe feeling heavier and suspect either that he did not explain himself properly or that the rebbe did not care. "Both thoughts are false": > אבל שתי מחשבות אלו כוזבות הן (211)

The heaviness comes from a deeper longing that was awakened. Having gone up, he now sees his lowness more clearly: > כיון שתשוקה יותר פנימית נתעוררה לו, ואחר עלייתו בנסיעתו, יותר מביט ורואה בשפלות (211)

**5.9 Kabbalah as intellectual speculation (lines 119, 122-124).** Anyone who approaches Kabbalah as a book of philosophy "does not know that he does not know". He sees only "dry words" and objections. He suspects that people who studied Kabbalah and went astray were students of this kind (line 124). What Kabbalah needs is to be "wholehearted with Him": > אם יהי׳ אלקים עמך בהיותך תמים לו (124)

**5.10 The tzaddik's suspicion of his own greatness (lines 214, 218).** The rebbe cries that he may be "deceiving himself and the world". When words that sound grand come out of him, he regrets them as "deceit" (line 218). He records this as part of the real inner life. It is not a problem to be cured.

**5.11 Knowing the soul without Torah (lines 401-403).** He warns that the psychologists of his day study a soul shaped by thoughts without Torah and see "a rubbish heap". A Jew who looks into his own soul will find holiness even in its animal part.

## 6. Comparison with the Chabad method of contemplation (hisbonenus)

He describes the Chabad way himself, in Tanya's terms. He sets it beside another Chassidic way, calls both "one way to the One God", and does not rank them (line 204).

**Where he agrees.** - *He gives a fair account of the mind-first path.* "Their way of arousing [the soul] is through thought and contemplation": > אופן התעוררותם בדרך קדשם הוא ע״י המחשבה והתבוננות (195)

He lists its claims: *da'at* is connection, the soul's seat is in the brain and only from there reaches the heart, and man is made so that the mind rules the heart: > כך נוצר האיש שיהי׳ המוח שולט על הלב (195)

He also cites Tanya ch. 16: love held in the mind alone counts as though felt in the heart. - *Feeling is the point.* Chabad's "contemplation that stops at feeling has not finished" has a parallel here. He insists that mental intention without arousal is empty (lines 156, 162), and that "arousal is the essence of Chassidus": > כי הוא עיקר החסידות ההתעוררות (246) - *Duration.* His abstraction exercise works only through repetition "for some time" (lines 121, 126), which is close to Chabad's most repeated instruction. - *The object shapes the state.* His palace parable says the measurer and the enthusiast describe the same palace. A listener with "a warm heart" is set on fire even by measurements (line 233), which is Chabad's claim that detailed understanding leads to the heart. - *Suppression makes things worse.* This matches the Chabad diagnostic ("suppression worsens it") at lines 362-363.

**Where he differs, kept as differences.** - *He puts the other path on equal footing: act first, feel later.* The Karlin and Beis Aharon path, which he presents with clear sympathy, says the reverse of "mind rules heart". Do not wait for the heart. Say the words with force, "like an ox to the yoke", and the fire will come by itself (line 198). His own era calls for "broadening more than deepening" (line 376), and for easier ways in, because people now have "no mind and no flame" (line 382). For this generation he does not make contemplation the main door. - *Picturing and imagination are central tools, not a lower rung.* Chabad works through *binah*, unfolding an idea. His recommended techniques are mostly *pictures*: the Temple, the grave, the stars, the written Name (lines 169, 390-395). Prophecy itself rested on the "picturing soul" (line 105). He says the imagination has to be trained, and gives laws for how it works. - *Effort can defeat itself.* Chabad's remedy for "grasp without heart" is more time on the same idea. His finding about the imagination is that direct straining *kills* the image, because it strengthens the "I-intellect" (line 390). The way in is sideways, through a memory you do not care about (line 395). No parallel to this in the Chabad map is apparent. - *Stray thoughts.* The Chabad diagnostic says "return without commentary". He says to *look straight at* the intruding thought, and it melts (line 390). - *Strong emotion as an engraving tool.* He deliberately uses heightened excitement to engrave a good idea "even against one's will" (line 388). Chabad is wary of self-generated arousal (the Mitteler Rebbe's warning), but he treats it as a legitimate lever. Even arousal that is "only imagined" counts as real at the core (line 144). This is a sharp difference over how far feeling can be trusted. - *Body and drives.* Chabad's Tanya-based path, as he himself describes it, keeps the call to fast (line 202), and treats the animal soul as *kelipat nogah* (line 200). His path follows Karlin and the Baal Shem Tov: no fasts, and the animal soul's roots are "the face of the ox in the Chariot" (line 347). You transform bodily forces rather than subdue them (lines 353-364). - *Melody and fellowship.* Chabad's method is solitary contemplation. His method is mostly *social*: the rebbe, the *chevraya*, drinking and dancing, singing before Torah (lines 303, 305-306). For him Chassidus "is not engraved in a book but in the chassidim themselves" (line 302). - *Quieting (hashkatah).* This is absent from this book on both sides, so there is no comparison to make here. The nearest thing is "sits silent for a moment" (line 106) and humility naturally lifting a person "a little out of physicality" (line 274).

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before you try to know God, get to know your own soul: what moves it, what blocks it, how it works. The Rebbe says you cannot reach the higher knowing without this" (line 398).
2. If a person cannot picture something holy (a Name, a place, a face), tell them to stop trying so hard. Have them remember an ordinary scene they don't care about, fill in its details, and let the holy image turn up inside it (lines 390, 395).
3. When a stray thought takes hold, suggest they look straight at it for a moment instead of pushing it away. He says a thought you look at melts (line 390).
4. If there is no feeling, don't wait for it. Say the words fully with the mouth, even like an ox pulling a cart, and let the warmth come on its own (line 198).
5. Start from a love the person already has, for a child or even for something small, and use it as straw to light the larger fire (line 166).
6. Don't tell them to crush a strong drive or temper. Help them find where its heat can go toward God, because force that is only held down comes back stronger (lines 363-364).
7. If they come away from a strong experience feeling worse, tell them this often means a deeper longing has woken up and they now see more, not that nothing happened (line 211).
8. If a real glimpse comes that they could not have produced on purpose, don't let them dismiss it as "only imagination". What you cannot manufacture is not something you made up (lines 127-128).
