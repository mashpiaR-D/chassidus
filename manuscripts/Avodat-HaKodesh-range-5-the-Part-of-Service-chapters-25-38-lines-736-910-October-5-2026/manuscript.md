## The shape of the range

The last range asked why the soul came down. This range follows the soul out of the body and back in, and then turns to return (teshuvah) and the end of days.

1. **The soul's garments and the Garden** (736-793). The soul wears a fine garment in this world and another after it. The Garden of Eden is a real place below with a Garden above it. Who is alive and who is dead.
2. **Gehinnom** (794-812). Free choice between two paths. The "spiritual body" (gviyah ruchanit, the fine body that joins the soul to the flesh) is what sin can grasp. The soul itself cannot be touched.
3. **Coming back (gilgul)** (813-850). A soul that did not finish its task returns, up to three times. The deep reason: the task is a need on high, and it must be done.
4. **Return (teshuvah)** (851-888). Return is the foundation of the world. It brings the Mother back to her children. One who returns stands higher than one who never sinned.
5. **The end of days** (889-910). All souls must pass through bodies; then new souls come and the Messiah among them. The world goes back to how it was before Adam's sin, and "the Lord will be One and His Name One."

## The unity of G-d

**Why I came: the task cannot be skipped.** This is the book's thesis (tzorech gavoah, a need on high) applied to the hardest question: why would a soul come back again?

> כי כבר התבאר בזה החלק כי ביאת האדם לעולם הזה צורך גבוה, והוא תקון הכבוד (Avodat HaKodesh 2:33:1; line 827)
> For it was already explained in this part that man's coming to this world is a need on high, and that is the repair of the Glory (Kavod, G-d's revealed presence).

If he did not finish it, punishment in Gehinnom can clean him, but it cannot do the work he was sent for. So he must come again:

> עד אשר יצליח אשר שלחו ונברא בעבורו שהוא תקון הכבוד ועקר התכלית (Avodat HaKodesh 2:33:2; line 828)
> until he succeeds in what He sent him for, and was created for, which is the repair of the Glory and the main purpose.

> וכל עוד שלא נשלם על ידי האדם הרי הוא כאלו לא בא ולא נברא (Avodat HaKodesh 2:33:2; line 828)
> And as long as it is not completed by man, it is as if he had not come and had not been created.

*Ours.* This is the strongest form of the purpose teaching in the book. A person is not measured by what he feels or knows, but by whether the thing he was sent to fix got fixed. Chassidus says the same about the soul's descent: every soul has its own portion of the world to refine, and it comes down again if it has not (this is a common theme in Chabad teaching on the soul's descent; we have not tied it to one source here). The guide can use this as a steadying thought, not a threat: *there is a job only I can do, and it matters above.*

**The servant's first request: the Shechinah's food.** Ibn Gabbai reads a line of the Tikkunei Zohar: people ask for their own food, clothes and home, "and there is no one who asks for food, which is the food of the Shechinah" (line 829). His comment:

> שעקר מה שיצטרך העובד השלם לבקש ולשאול ולכוין בעבודתו הוא צורך הכבוד והוא מזון השכינה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:33:4; line 830)
> that the main thing the complete servant needs to ask and request and intend in his service is the need of the Glory, and that is the food of the Shechinah.

The food is Torah study; the clothing is the mitzvot; and "what comes out between these two is the unity between the two Beloveds", which is the Shema (line 830). *Ours.* This matches the technique already proposed in range 3, **asking for His sake**. Here it gets a picture: a servant who brings food to the Queen.

**When deeds are straight, "the Lord is One and His Name One".** From the Zohar he brings:

> אתגלי עתיקא בזעירא ומסתכלן כל אינון אנפין באנפין סתימין ומתברכאן כלהו (Avodat HaKodesh 2:34:16; line 855)
> The Ancient One is revealed in the Small Face, and all those faces look at the hidden faces and are all blessed.

"Until all worlds are blessed and all the worlds are found as one, and then the Lord is called One and His Name One" (line 855). When sins multiply, the Ancient One hides and judgments wake. *Ours.* Unity above is not only a fact; it is also a state that opens or closes with how we live. Chassidus keeps both: G-d's unity is never broken (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7), and its *revealing* depends on us (Tanya ch. 36).

**The world goes back to one.** At the end, "all the worlds were level and rising on one reed, and the Great Name in one unity with His Presence" as before Adam's sin (line 910). The Messiah will "remove the dividing screen" and "all will turn to the unity" (line 910).

> ואז תשלם הכוונה בבריאה וזה שעור מה שרצינו לבארו בזה הפרק (Avodat HaKodesh 2:38:10; line 910)
> And then the purpose of creation will be complete; and this is the measure of what we wished to explain in this chapter.

## Return (teshuvah)

> התשובה היא קיום העולם ויסודו, ולולא התשובה לא נברא העולם (Avodat HaKodesh 2:34:12; line 851)
> Return is the keeping of the world and its foundation; without return the world would not have been created.

G-d "drew the world before Him and it did not stand, until He created return" (line 852). It is "the medicine that the upper Wisdom saw fit to put before the illness" (line 851).

**What sin does above, and what return does.** Sin sends the Mother (Binah, the upper Shechinah) away from her children and the Queen away from the King (line 853). Return brings her back, which is why it is called *teshuvah*, "returning": she returns to her children (line 856). "Returning the stolen thing" means giving back the unity and blessing he held back from above (line 856).

**Why one who returns stands higher.** The tzaddik is bound to Yesod (the channel of giving); one who returns is bound to "the upper Return", Binah, "the life of the King" (line 863). He draws water from a higher place down to the tzaddik's place. And there is a second reason: the sin he was used to came to him, and he beat it.

> הנה זה לו נסיון גדול ועומד בו בזה הוא מעלה ומכבד את הקב"ה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:34:29; line 868)
> This is a great test for him, and he stands in it; by this he raises and honors the Holy One.

The tzaddik "did not taste the sweetness of that sin" and never had the chance to humble that side (line 868). *Ours.* This is the Tanya's teaching that the one who struggles (the beinoni, the in-between person) gives G-d a joy that the tzaddik cannot (Tanya ch. 27, quoting the Zohar: when the other side is subdued, the glory of the Holy One rises in all the worlds). The Tanya uses almost ibn Gabbai's words. Compare:

> ויש לדעת כי בכניעת יצר הרע, הקב"ה מתעלה ומתכבד (Avodat HaKodesh 2:34:25; line 864)
> Know that when the evil inclination is humbled, the Holy One is raised and honored.

**Confession makes mercy win.** From the Zohar: one who hides his sins is not given the door of return; one who spells them out before the King, "and all the more if he weeps", opens every closed door (line 866). "'He who offers thanks (todah) honors Me': two honors, one below and one above" (line 866).

> ותשובה אתקין לעילא ותתא ואתקין לגרמיה ולכולי עלמא (Avodat HaKodesh 2:34:33; line 872)
> And return repairs above and below, and repairs himself and the whole world.

"Because he did return, it is as if he made the Lord, truly" (line 872).

**The whole Torah in one mitzvah.** The goal of all the Torah is the unity of the Great Name, and "that very goal comes from return" (line 873). The word *teshuvah* also means "rest", as in "in rest (shuvah) and quiet you will be saved": "the settled spirit, pure and inner, that comes from Wisdom and awe" (line 874). *Ours.* So return is not only regret. It is also the settled, quiet heart. This is a real state: **return as rest**, the calm that comes when one is back where one belongs.

**"You will bring it back to your heart."** Before "you will return to the Lord" comes "you will bring it back to your heart" (Deut. 30:1). We see that good and bad both came from our own choices, so the fixing is also in our hands:

> ונשוב בתשובה למטה ונשיב התשובה למעלה (Avodat HaKodesh 2:36:4; line 876)
> We will return below, and bring back the Return above.

**The best return is not after sin.** "You will return and listen to the voice of the Lord" (Deut. 30:8) is "not for a sin that came before", but being open and yielding to the Torah, and holding back from sin when it comes (line 884). By this "they keep the upper unity going with no break". *Ours.* This is the Chabad teaching of the higher return (teshuvah ila'ah) that is for tzaddikim too (Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 8-9; Likkutei Torah).

**Three returns for three letters.** The three times "return" appears in the passage stand for the three letters of the Name: the first Heh (Mother) returns to her children, the Vav returns to goodness, the last Heh rises out of exile to her Beloved (line 888). "Since return is a need on high, He warns them about it again and again."

## States of consciousness

- **The living one** (line 769, from range 5's opening). "The living one is he who takes it to heart ... and the man who spends all his time [in Torah and service] is alive even after his death; and the other is dead even in his life." A state of being really alive. Ties to S01-type wakefulness; we propose it as part of **alive in the task** below.
- **Two fires** (lines 800-801). "The Holy One gave them to people to test them": both inclinations are fires, and "with lights honor the Lord" means honoring Him with both. Ties to Tanya ch. 9 (two souls fighting over one city) and to the guide's struggle states.
- **Mercy won by open confession** (line 866). The loosening that comes when one names what one did before G-d, with tears. Ties to the Chabad teaching of bitter heart (merirut) and its release into joy (Tanya ch. 29-31).
- **Return as rest** (line 874). New, proposed below.
- **Having beaten the old habit** (line 868). The honor above that comes when the known test comes and one does not fall. New, proposed as **the test met**.

## Meditation and practice

- **Eating to raise.** On meat: "set your intention to raise your soul, which is the soul of what is eaten" (line 842). Only one who knows what eating is for should eat meat; one who eats only to fill himself does not raise it (line 844). *Ours.* This is the root of the Chassidic teaching of raising sparks by eating (Tanya ch. 7-8; Keter Shem Tov). A practice: before eating, one line of intent: *I eat to serve, and this food rises with me.* Ties to technique 22 (carrying a line through the day).
- **Kindness to animals.** Shechitah must be done so the animal does not suffer, "for it is written, love your neighbor as yourself" (line 848). Kept here as a matter of heart, not of Jewish law.
- **Asking for His sake** (line 830): see above. Already proposed in range 3.
- **Return as rest** (line 874): sit, name the place you left, and come back to it, letting the heart settle. Proposed below.
- **Seek the wisdom.** "Every one called by the name Israel must seek wisdom and pursue it ... for by it they will see light, and it will lead them to complete service" (line 850). Ibn Gabbai's reason to study the inner Torah: it makes service whole.

## The big human questions

**What happens after death?** The soul's garment from the lower Garden clothes it; it enjoys "the shine of the Shechinah" (line 810). Souls rise on Shabbat and new moons. "There is no love like the love of soul and body" (line 772), so the lowest part of the soul stays near the body for a while.

**What is hell?** Not a myth (line 807). Sin draws a spirit from the other side that wraps the soul's fine body; Gehinnom washes it off, and "everything returns to its root" (line 810). The soul itself, from "the holy face", cannot be ruled over (line 811). *Ours.* The guide stays with the teaching's core, which Chassidus shares: what I do clings to me, and return while alive removes it (line 812). We do not dwell on punishment.

**Why do good people suffer?** Ibn Gabbai's answer, from the Bahir, is past lives: "the tzaddik was once wicked before" (line 825), and it is "the secret of the upper Thought" that even Moses did not fully grasp (line 834). *Ours.* The guide should keep the humility of the second half more than the certainty of the first: "Be silent, so it rose in Thought."

**Is it too late for me?** No. Return came before the world (line 851). "Even if one person returns, he and the whole world are forgiven" (line 860). G-d "makes plans so that no one pushed away stays pushed away from Him" (line 818).

**Will the world be healed?** Yes. The world returns to its first nature; the vine gives its fruit as it was meant to (line 904); "all flesh will call on Your Name" (line 909).
