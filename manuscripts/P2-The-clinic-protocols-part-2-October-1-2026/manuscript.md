## Read this first

**What this is.** For each problem, a master's formulation (where in the soul, what state, how it traces to the root, what is blocked, what state is needed, what kind of healing) and a staged treatment path, each stage with its exact practice, signs, the fallback when stuck, and when to move on. Each stage ends with what the guide says to start the person on it (80-140 words, plain, no Hebrew or book names).

**Built on.** State ids are from `consciousness-map/MAP.md`. Method ids (M01-M71) are from `animal-soul/METHODS.md`; rules from `animal-soul/PSYCHOLOGY.md`; depth ids (MR1-C02, MR3-C10, AB2-C15 and others) from `depths/DIGEST.md`; vessels (VS-02) from `lights-and-vessels/VS.md`; claims (D03, D04, D05, D17, D41) and conditions (C07-C40) from `Tanya Guided Meditation/universal/engine/`. Each protocol names the engine conditions it deepens. Nothing here repeats those files; it cites them.

**How claims are marked.** In the practice text, *[stated]* means the source says it, with the source named; *[stated there]* means one of our files reports the source; *[ours]* means our application. Every Hebrew quote was checked with `find_he.py verify FILE "…" --line N` and came back FOUND; the ledger at the end gives file and line. *(OCR)* marks a scanned text.

**The body is part of the service.** On the owner's direction, the body is woven into the stages as its own act of avodah with its reason, never as a side note: the Maggid's word as the Rebbe cites it, *a small hole in the body makes a big hole in the soul*; the Rambam's *keeping the body healthy and whole is one of the ways of G-d*; Hillel going to eat as *a kindness to the poor, humble one, his body*. So: slowing the body before speech in anger; meals and sleep as birur in desire; no bodily self-punishment in shame; eating and sleep as caring for the vessel in grief; the doctor as *making a vessel* in fear of illness. Two limits kept from `PSYCHOLOGY.md` part 6: for health fear and trauma, no attention to body sensations; and no breathing drills or body scans as techniques. The one slow breath before speaking in anger is a way of slowing the body down, not an exercise. No supplements or doses anywhere; persistent symptoms go to a doctor, plainly. `clinic/BODY.md` (another strand) will hold the body's own teaching.

**The root, honestly.** Six of the eight trace cleanly to the root problem (the self that holds itself up, yeshus), each in its own disguise: ownership (anger), the wish to feel oneself alive (desire), judge-and-defendant (shame), *what does it give me* (emptiness), keeper of one's own life (fear), worth as a quantity (jealousy), and plain *I, and nothing besides me* (pride). Grief does not: it is love, not a fault, and we say so. Where the root shows in grief it is only *I'm alone with this*, and the sources answer with company, not explanation.

## P2-01 · Anger and irritability

*Presenting:* "I snap at people. My kids, a coworker, the driver in front of me. Small things set me off. Afterward I feel awful, but in the moment I can't stop it." *Deepens engine conditions:* C34, C35

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** Quick flare-ups at small things; a replay loop afterward ("why did he do that"); regret, then the same thing again.
- **Where in the soul.** The animal soul, in its middos: anger is a fallen gevurah, from the element of fire, which rises (Tanya 1) [stated]. Its first garment is speech, then deed; the thought garment keeps it alive afterward as replay. The faculty that fails is da'as: the mind knows better but is not bound to the heart in the moment, so the mind's natural rule over the heart (Tanya 12) lapses. Level: nefesh and ruach (the feelings).
- **State now.** S15 Angry at someone (often with S06 Self-enclosed when the sting is about honor).
- **Root.** Yeshus in the form of ownership: the day, my plans and my honor are mine, and this person got in the way. The Alter Rebbe names the hidden step: at the moment of anger faith has left; if he believed this reached him from G-d he would not be angry at all (Iggeret HaKodesh 25) [stated]. So the self stands apart from Him exactly at the trigger, as if the event came from the person alone.
- **What is blocked.** Da'as: the knowing that everything reaches me from Him is there but not bound in the moment (hesech hada'as). The eye narrows: one fault fills the whole picture (engine C34 reading) [stated there]. The heart is not dead (not timtum); it is too hot.
- **State needed.** First S28 Mind over heart (the word not said). Then S85 Seeing Him in what happens, and S86 The other held. Later S59 True lowliness (a sharp word stings less).
- **Kind of healing.** Iskafya first, in the garment of speech, because the heart cannot be ordered but the mouth can. Then hamtakah (sweetening in the root): the anger is not argued down but taken back to its root, where the event is seen as reaching me from Him and the other as kept alive by Him; the Baal Shem Tov's school: judgments are sweetened only in their root, and when joy and love come the anger leaves (Tzava'at HaRivash 132) [stated]. Last, is'hapcha in part: the fire is kept and aimed (Tzava'at HaRivash 14) [stated]. Not forced forgiveness.

## Stage 1 · Catch the first word

- **Aim.** The anger may still rise, but it stops reaching the mouth and hands. The person learns from experience that he can hold the anger instead of being held by it.
- **Practice.** 1) Write down the two or three usual triggers (a child not listening, a coworker's tone, traffic) [ours]. 2) At the trigger, before the first word, slow the body down as an act of service: sit if standing, both feet on the floor, lower the voice, let one slow breath out. Reason: speech goes out through the body; slowing the vessel gives the mind the moment it needs to rule the heart, which it can do by nature (Tanya 12) [stated: the rule; ours: the bodily means]. 3) Ask one question: will this sentence get me what I actually want here? (engine C34, 'hold the anger' program) [stated there]. 4) If not, say nothing now. If it must be said, say it later, calmly, once, in a soft voice: 'a soft answer turns away wrath' (Iggeret HaKodesh 22) [stated]. 5) Count each word held back. The Tanya counts the one who 'sat and did not sin' as having done a mitzvah (ch. 27) [stated], and each push away gives a delight above. 6) Notice the body's warning hours: hunger and short sleep make the fuse short. Eat before the hard conversation, go to sleep on time; Hillel called feeding his body a kindness to 'the poor, humble one' (Tanya 29) [stated], and the Maggid: a small hole in the body makes a big hole in the soul [stated]. When: all day, at the moment. How long: a few seconds each time; the practice for 7-10 days.
- **Signs of progress.** At least one word held back most days. The heat is noticed a beat earlier. Fewer things said that need an apology.
- **If stuck.** If he blew up: no self-punishment (it feeds the next blow-up; Tanya 26: heaviness loses the fight). One plain apology where it fits, then back to stage 1 the next hour. If the thoughts keep raging even when the mouth is closed, that is expected: the heart can hold back anger more easily than thought can (MR1-C27) [stated]; go on to stage 2, which works on the thought.
- **Move on when.** Holding the word works on most days, or the person says 'I don't say it, but I'm still seething inside'.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 1: «כעס וגאוה – מיסוד האש שנגבה למעלה»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 12: «כי המוח שליט על הלב»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 22: «״מענה רך משיב חימה״»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27: «ישב ולא עבר עבירה – נותנים לו שכר כאלו עשה מצוה»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 29 (Hillel, as the Tanya tells it): «שהוא הולך לגמול חסד עם העלובה ועניה – הוא גופו»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 7, letter 2055, citing the Maggid to his son (Yiddish): «אז א קליינע לעכעלע אין גוף גורם א גרויסע לאך אין דער נשמה»

> **The guide says:** Let's start with one small thing, and only this for the next week. Your heart may flare; that's not in your control yet. Your mouth is. So when the heat comes, before the first word, slow your body down: sit if you're standing, feet on the floor, let one slow breath out. Then ask one question: will this sentence get me what I actually want here? If not, say nothing for now. You can say it later, calmly, once. Your body is the door your words go through, and slowing the door gives your mind the moment it needs to be in charge. Every word you hold back counts as a real win. And eat before the hard moments. A hungry body has a short fuse.

## Stage 2 · Who sent this? (the morning contemplation)

- **Aim.** From 'he did this to me' to 'he chose it, and it reached me from Him'. The replay loop shortens because the event has a second, truer address.
- **Practice.** Morning, 10-15 minutes, after morning prayer or after the morning blessings; seated, a cup of tea in hand is fine [ours]. 1) Recall yesterday's one flare-up in detail: the room, the words, his face. The Piaseczner notes that a thought stays only if it is filled with detailed pictures, and gives as his example the person who replays an enemy's harm with every detail (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 4:20) [stated]; the grudge already uses this power, so use it on purpose [ours]. 2) Say the first truth: he chose what he did and is answerable for it, before people and before Heaven (Iggeret HaKodesh 25) [stated]. Your hurt is real. 3) Say the second truth: what reached you was still from Above, and even at the moment he spoke, the breath of G-d's mouth was clothed in him, keeping him alive; if it withdrew for an instant he could not say a word (Iggeret HaKodesh 25, on Shimei: 'G-d told him: curse') [stated]. 4) The mashal [ours]: a letter delivered by a rude mailman. The rudeness is the mailman's; the letter is addressed to you. Where it breaks: G-d does not send anyone's bad choice; the choice is wholly the person's, and this picture is never used about ongoing harm. 5) Ask: if this came to me from Him, what is it asking of me? The Alter Rebbe's own answer, for one he cannot stop: to strengthen himself more in prayer (Iggeret HaKodesh 25) [stated]. 6) Say it aloud if possible (M27) and carry one line into the day: 'He chose it. It reached me from Him.' Reason: anger is 'as if worshipping idols' because in that moment something other than G-d seems to run the world; the contemplation restores the one fact the anger forgot (Iggeret HaKodesh 25) [stated]. The Alter Rebbe limits this to worldly matters; where a real wrong needs stopping, one may act (Iggeret HaKodesh 25, 'everything is in the hands of Heaven except the fear of Heaven') [stated].
- **Signs of progress.** The heat still comes, but the carried line comes a moment later. Shorter replay. He can describe the incident without his voice rising.
- **If stuck.** If it feels like excusing the other: stay longer on the first truth (he is answerable) before the second. If it stays in the head: add detail, and say it aloud. If he cannot say 'from Him' honestly yet: say only 'he is being kept alive right now, while he does this' and stop there. If harm is ongoing: stop this stage entirely (guards).
- **Move on when.** The replay is shorter on most days and the carried line comes by itself at a trigger at least sometimes.
- **Sources.** Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 4:20 (the Piaseczner): «ואי אפשר להאריך איזה זמן במחשבה זו לבדה מן הרע שעשה לו שונאו בלתי אם גם יצייר בה»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 25: «ומתחייב בדיני אדם ובדיני שמים על רוע בחירתו»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 25: «אפילו בשעה זו ממש שמכהו או מקללו – מתלבש בו כח ה׳ ורוח פיו יתברך המחייהו ומקיימו»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 25: «לפי שבעת כעסו נסתלקה ממנו האמונה, כי אילו היה מאמין שמאת ה׳ היתה זאת לו – לא היה בכעס כלל»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 25: «במילי דעלמא, כי ״הכל בידי שמים חוץ מיראת שמים״»

> **The guide says:** Now something for the mornings, ten minutes. Take yesterday's one flare-up and replay it slowly, in detail. Then say two true things, out loud if you can. First: he chose what he did, and he's answerable for it. Your hurt is real. Second: what reached you still came from G-d, and even while he spoke, his breath and words were being given life by Him, moment by moment. Think of a letter brought by a rude mailman: the rudeness is his, but the letter was addressed to you. Then ask: if this came from Him, what is it asking of me? Carry one line into the day: he chose it; it reached me from Him.

## Stage 3 · Two knowings of the same person

- **Aim.** The other stops being an obstacle and becomes a person: truly separate, and held by the same One. The rivalry goes out of it.
- **Practice.** Evening, 5-10 minutes. 1) Picture the person who sets you off, but this time picture his day, not his fault: what he woke up worried about, what he carries (Tanya 30: 'do not judge your fellow until you reach his place') [stated]. 2) Hold two sentences together: 'From below, he is really separate from me, with his own mind and choices' (Iggeret HaKodesh 20) [stated]; 'From above, we are matched, and we have one Father' (Tanya 32) [stated]. 3) Within 24 hours do one small act toward him: a kind word, a favor, a held door (S86 'grows: one act') [ours: the timing]. Do it with the body, not only in the heart: walk over, hand it to him. 4) At bedtime, the Shema before sleep has the line 'Master of the world, I forgive anyone who angered or provoked me' (Siddur) [stated: the text]. Say it if you can mean it even a little; if not, say you wish you could [ours]. Reason: in the Baal Shem Tov's school, when love and joy come the anger leaves, because a judgment is sweetened only in its root (Tzava'at HaRivash 132) [stated].
- **Signs of progress.** He can name one good thing about the person. The act gets done. Less bracing before seeing him.
- **If stuck.** If no kind thought comes: skip the feeling and do the act anyway (Tanya 16 and 44: picture it in the mind and act) [stated in S29]. If the person is dangerous or controlling: this stage is not for this relationship (guards).
- **Move on when.** One act toward him has happened more than once, and the morning line holds.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 30: «״אל תדין את חבירך – עד שתגיע למקומו״»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 20: «אבל בידיעה שממטה למעלה – היש הנברא הוא דבר נפרד לגמרי»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 32: «שכולן מתאימות, ואב אחד לכולנה»; Siddur, the Shema at bedtime: «רבונו שלעולם הריני מוחל לכלמי שהכעיס והקניט אותי»; Tzava'at HaRivash 132: «בבוא השמחה ואהבה נסתלק הכעס»; Tzava'at HaRivash 132: «שהדינים אינם נמתקים אלא בשרשם»

> **The guide says:** Tonight, five minutes. Picture the person who sets you off, but not the fault this time. Picture their day: what they woke up worried about, what they carry that you don't see. You don't have to excuse anything. Hold two sentences together. From where I stand, he's really separate from me, with his own mind and choices. From above, we have one Father. Then, within a day, do one small thing for him with your hands: a favor, a kind word, holding a door. Not a speech about forgiving, just one act. Before sleep there's an old line: Master of the world, I forgive anyone who angered me. Say it if you can mean it, even a little.

## Stage 4 · Turn the fire

- **Aim.** The force is kept and aimed. The heat becomes energy for what is right, without contempt for people.
- **Practice.** 1) When anger rises at something truly wrong, stop and ask: what is the pain under this? Usually: something good is being hurt. The Rashab: anger is not needed even against evil, only the pain that evil exists (AB2-C15) [stated]. 2) Say that pain to G-d in your own words for a minute (S55). 3) Do one thing that actually repairs something, in a soft voice. 4) Each morning, before prayer, say the Siddur's line: 'I take upon myself the commandment, love your fellow as yourself' (Siddur) [stated: the text]. 5) If the fire is your nature, do not try to kill it. The Baal Shem Tov's will: anger is a fallen fear from the trait of severity; overcome the drive and make the trait itself a chariot for G-d (Tzava'at HaRivash 14) [stated]. Use the body: put the heat into a brisk walk, into hard physical work for someone, into standing up straight to pray with force [ours]. The engine's picture: a smith holds hot iron with tongs, not bare-handed (C34) [stated there].
- **Signs of progress.** Anger at wrongs turns into a step instead of a speech. Others notice he is calmer. He still has fire, and is not ashamed of it.
- **If stuck.** If the anger turns on himself ('I'm a monster'): stop this stage; shame is its own protocol (P2-03). Turning indignation against one's own evil is not for anyone depressed or self-hating (engine C34, careful) [stated there].
- **Move on when.** This is the ongoing shape. Return to stage 1 after any bad week.
- **Sources.** Siddur, before morning prayer: «הריני מקבל עלי מצות עשה של ואהבת לרעך כמוך»; Tzava'at HaRivash 14: «ואם בכעס שהוא יראה רעה נמשך ממדת הגבורה יגבור על יצרו ויעשה מהמדה הזאת מרכבה להשי"ת»

> **The guide says:** Your fire isn't the enemy. The same heat that blows up at people can push you toward what's right. Next time anger comes at something truly wrong, stop and ask: what's the pain under this? Usually it's that something good is being hurt. Tell G-d that pain, in your own words, for a minute. Then do one thing that actually fixes something, in a soft voice, because a soft answer turns anger away. If the heat is still high, walk it off or put it into hard work for someone. The fire stays yours. It's just aimed now, like a smith holding hot iron with tongs instead of his bare hand.

**Daily shape.** Morning: the Siddur's 'love your fellow' line, then 10-15 minutes of 'Who sent this?' (stage 2). Breakfast eaten, not skipped. During the day: the first word caught, the body slowed before speech, the carried line. Evening: 5 minutes on the person (stage 3) and a short look back: one word held, one missed, no penalty. Bed on time; the bedtime 'I forgive' line if it can be meant.

**Duration.** First change (words held) in 1-2 weeks. The morning contemplation takes hold in 3-6 weeks. The trait itself is the work of years; the Tanya allows that this war may last all one's days (ch. 27). Honest: some people will still flare, less often and shorter.

- Where harm, coercion or abuse is ongoing, none of stages 2-3 apply: no counsel of patience, forgiveness or seeing it as from G-d; the person's anger may be a true signal. A person today if in danger (D04, D03). - Never use 'it came from G-d' to tell a victim what to feel about what was done to them. - Anger with violence or threats of violence needs outside help and safety steps; the texts do not cover it (engine C34). - Turning anger against oneself is not for anyone depressed, self-hating or at risk of self-harm. - If irritability is new, constant, or comes with poor sleep, low mood, drinking or a medical change, say plainly: see a doctor. - 'Holding anger as a tool' is never a license to frighten family, students or employees.

## Worked example (assessment, formulation, stage 1, the push, stage 2 with substance)

**Person:** I yelled at my son again this morning. Over shoes. Shoes! I don't get it. I know it's stupid and I still do it. **Guide:** That sounds painful, especially the part after, when you see his face. Can I ask a few things so I understand it, not just fix it? When does it usually happen: what time of day, and what state are you in? **Person:** Mornings mostly. We're late, I haven't eaten, I slept badly. Also at work when my manager talks down to me. **Guide:** And in the moment, what's the thought? Not the words you say, the thought underneath. **Person:** Like, why is everyone in my way. I'm trying to get somewhere and they're blocking me. **Guide:** One more: afterward, what do you tell yourself? And is there anyone in your life who scares you or harms you, or anyone you're worried you might hurt? **Person:** No, nothing like that. Afterward I tell myself I'm a bad father. **Guide:** Here's how I see it. Anger is heat, and it rises fast; your mind knows better, but in that second it isn't connected to your mouth. Underneath is a feeling that the morning belongs to you and your son is in its way. An old teaching says that in the moment of anger we forget that what reaches us comes from G-d, and that is the real gap. So we start with the one thing you can control, the first word, and then we work on the gap. **Guide:** So for this week, only one thing. Your heart may flare; that's not in your control yet. Your mouth is. When the heat comes, before the first word, slow your body down: sit if you're standing, feet on the floor, let one slow breath out. Then ask: will this sentence get me what I actually want here? If not, say nothing now; say it later, calmly, once. Count every word you hold back. And tomorrow, eat something before shoes. A hungry body is a short fuse; feeding it is part of the work. **Person:** Ok, but in the moment I can't think. I'm not going to remember a question. How do I actually feel different, not just shut my mouth? **Guide:** Fair. Holding the word is the floor, not the house. The feeling changes somewhere else: in the morning, before the fire. Here's the practice. Ten minutes, with your coffee. Replay this morning in detail: the shoes, his face, your voice. You already do this at night, that's the replay loop, so use the same power on purpose. Then say two things out loud. First: 'He's seven, he chose to dawdle, and it really was a problem.' That's true. Second: 'This morning, with its lateness and his shoes, reached me from G-d. And while he dawdled, his life and every breath were being given to him by G-d, right then.' Then ask: if this morning came from Him, what is it asking of me? Maybe: get up earlier. Maybe: be the calm one. Carry one line out the door: 'He chose it; it reached me from Him.' **Person:** Isn't that just telling myself a story so I don't get mad? **Guide:** It's the opposite of a story. The story is the one the anger tells: that this morning is yours and he stole it. The teaching says the morning was given, every second of it, and so was he. You don't have to feel that yet. Say it for two weeks and watch one thing: how long the replay lasts after a flare-up. If it gets shorter, something real moved. If not, tell me, and we'll adjust.

*What the example shows:* the guide asks before teaching (when, what thought, what after, any danger), explains the formulation back in four plain sentences, starts with the garment that can be controlled, and when the person pushes for felt change, moves to the root contemplation with full content (detail, two truths, the question, the carried line) and a checkable sign (the replay gets shorter).

## P2-02 · Desire: a pull the person can't stop (scrolling, food, lust)

*Presenting:* "I can't stop. Every night I'm on my phone till 2 a.m. / I eat when I'm not hungry / I keep going back to porn. I promise, I fall, I promise again." *Deepens engine conditions:* C33, C32, C10

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** A repeated pull that wins; promises and falls; shame after each fall; the fight in the head makes it louder.
- **Where in the soul.** The animal soul: the craving for pleasures is from its element of water (Tanya 1) [stated]; the middah is love fallen into 'a bad love' (Tzava'at HaRivash 14) [stated]. It moves through the garments: first sight, then memory, then craving (Tzava'at HaRivash 6) [stated], then thought (the hirhur), then the deed. Level: nefesh. Under it the G-dly soul's hidden love (Tanya 13), and often the soul's own thirst mistaken for hunger.
- **State now.** S07 Restless wanting, or S09 The pull that keeps winning when there is a fall-and-promise cycle.
- **Root.** Yeshus in the form the Mitteler Rebbe names: what holds a person to a desire is not the desire but 'the soul's craving to feel its own self' (Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai; MR3-C10) [stated]. The self wants to feel itself alive, and the screen or the food is where it does. The specific form here: the want under the want (rest, relief, feeling alive, not being alone) [ours, with PSYCHOLOGY rule 15].
- **What is blocked.** Da'as not binding knowing to the moment: he knows it is bad for him and it does not hold. The thought is debated, which feeds it (Tanya 28) [stated]. After a fall, atzvus (heaviness), which loses the next round (Tanya 26) [stated]. The body is often run down: late nights, skipped meals [ours].
- **State needed.** S30 Holding back, then S28 Mind over heart. In time S31 Turned over, never promised. Along the way S13 named: the longing seen for what it is.
- **Kind of healing.** Iskafya with simcha: turning away without a fight, and being glad in each turn, which the Tanya says gives a delight above (ch. 27) [stated]. Birur of the want: finding what the craving really seeks and giving it its real object (M17) [stated]. Then is'hapcha begun: the bare power to desire is not evil and can want the good (Likkutei Torah, AR-D8) [stated]. The body is part of the birur, not suppression: food eaten 'to broaden the mind for G-d' is lifted (Tanya 7) [stated]; sleep and meals are the vessel.

## Stage 1 · Know the hour, close one gate, feed the vessel

- **Aim.** The pattern becomes visible and the openings shrink. The body stops arriving at the danger hour exhausted and hungry.
- **Practice.** 1) For three days, note only when the pull comes: hour, place, mood (tired, lonely, bored, stressed). Not why [ours]. 2) Close one gate: phone sleeps outside the bedroom; no snack food in the desk; a filter. Reason: 'seeing brings to remembering, and to craving' (Tzava'at HaRivash 6) [stated]; fewer sightings, fewer fights (M39). 3) Body as birur: real meals at regular times, a set bedtime, some walking each day [ours, on the principle that] the body is to be kept 'healthy and whole' as one of G-d's ways (Rambam, cited by the Rebbe) [stated], and 'a small hole in the body makes a big hole in the soul' (the Maggid, cited by the Rebbe) [stated]. Before sleep, the Baal Shem Tov's line: my mind goes to Him and will be strengthened for His service (Tzava'at HaRivash 23) [stated], so bed is a destination, not a defeat. When: 3-7 days.
- **Signs of progress.** He can name his danger hour. One gate is closed and stays closed. He gets to bed earlier at least some nights.
- **If stuck.** If he 'can't' close the gate: shrink it (phone outside the room only on weeknights). If the pattern is nightly and severe or involves a substance: a professional, plainly (guards).
- **Move on when.** The danger hour is known and one gate holds for a week.
- **Sources.** Tzava'at HaRivash 6: «ראי' מביאה לידי זכירה ולידי תאוה»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 7, letter 2055, citing Rambam, Hilchos De'os 4:1: «היות הגוף בריא ושלם מדרכי השם הוא»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 7, letter 2055, citing the Maggid to his son (Yiddish): «אז א קליינע לעכעלע אין גוף גורם א גרויסע לאך אין דער נשמה»; Tzava'at HaRivash 23: «וכשהולך לישן יחשוב הלא המוחין שלו ילכו להקב"ה ויתחזקו לעבודתו ית'»

> **The guide says:** Before we fight anything, let's look. For three days, just notice when the pull comes: what hour, where you are, how you feel. Tired? Lonely? Bored? Don't judge it, just note it. Then close one gate. If it's the phone at night, the phone sleeps outside your room. If it's food, it doesn't live in your desk. Seeing something wakes the memory, and the memory wakes the craving, so fewer sightings means fewer fights. And look after your body on purpose: real meals at real times, and a set time for bed. A lot of night craving is a tired body asking for rest. Feeding it well is a kindness to it, and it makes the fight smaller.

## Stage 2 · Don't argue: turn away and do

- **Aim.** The pull comes and is not obeyed. No debate. Hands on something good. Gladness at each turn instead of shame.
- **Practice.** 1) Prepare one 'instead' for the danger hour: a few chapters of Psalms, one page of a short learning text, a call to a friend, walking out of the room [ours]. 2) When the pull arrives, name it plainly: 'that's the pull'. It is one of two souls, not the whole of you (Tanya 28) [stated]. 3) Do not answer it. The Tanya's mashal: a man praying while a fool talks to him to distract him does not answer, but acts as if deaf; 'one who wrestles a filthy man gets filthy too' (Tanya 28) [stated]. Where the mashal breaks: the 'fool' is your own animal soul, so no hatred of it; it is to be handled, not despised [ours]. 4) Hands on the 'instead' for ten minutes. Get up physically: stand, leave the room, wash your face [ours]. 5) If it is very strong: 'lower yourself to G-d and plead in your thought for mercy' (Tanya 28) [stated], in plain words: Father, have mercy, help me. 6) After it passes, be glad. 'He should be glad in his portion: though they fall into his thought, he turns his mind away' (Tanya 27) [stated]; 'one who sat and did not sin is rewarded as if he did a mitzvah' (Tanya 27) [stated]. 7) If you fall: Tanya 26: now is not the time for sorrow; set the regret for a fixed time tonight, then remove it fully (see P2-03).
- **Signs of progress.** More turned-away moments than falls in a week. Less inner debate. Gladness after a win, even a small one.
- **If stuck.** If the debate goes on and on: shorten to two words, 'not now', and move the body. If the falls bring heavy shame: treat the shame first (P2-03), because heaviness loses the fight (Tanya 26) [stated]. If the war does not end: the Tanya allows it may last all one's days, 'perhaps this is what he was created for' (ch. 27) [stated]; that is not failure.
- **Move on when.** He has a working 'instead' and can turn away on most days, or asks 'but why do I want it so much?'
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 28: «ולעשות עצמו כחרש לא ישמע»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 28: «כי המתאבק עם מנוול – מתנוול גם כן»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 28: «רק יעשה עצמו כלא יודע ולא שומע ההרהורים שנפלו לו»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 28: «ישפיל נפשו לה׳ ויתחנן לו יתברך במחשבתו, לרחם עליו ברחמיו המרובים»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27: «יש לו לשמוח בחלקו, שאף שנופלות לו במחשבתו – הוא מסיח דעתו מהן»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27: «ישב ולא עבר עבירה – נותנים לו שכר כאלו עשה מצוה»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27: «גם אם יהיה כן כל ימיו במלחמה זו, כי אולי לכך נברא»

> **The guide says:** Now the moment itself. When the pull comes, don't argue with it. Arguing with it is like wrestling someone covered in mud: you end up muddy too. Name it plainly, 'that's the pull', and remember it's one voice in you, not all of you. Then get up and put your hands on what you prepared: a few psalms, a short page of learning, a call, a walk out of the room. Ten minutes. If it's very strong, ask G-d inside: please, have mercy, help me. When it passes, be glad. Holding back once counts as a real good deed. If you fall, don't sink. Put the regret aside for tonight and start again.

## Stage 3 · The want under the want

- **Aim.** The craving is understood as pointing at a real need through the wrong door. The need gets a real answer, and the deepest want gets its true object.
- **Practice.** Evening, 10 minutes, after an urge, whether won or lost. 1) Ask: what did I want it to give me? Rest, comfort, excitement, feeling alive, not being alone? 2) Name the root the Mitteler Rebbe names: the wish to feel myself alive (MR3-C10) [stated]. 3) The Piaseczner's mashal: a sleeper stung by a fly dreams a crate fell on him; the soul cries and 'he thinks it is hunger, thirst' (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim) [stated]. Where it breaks: not every hunger is the soul; real needs are real, and are to be met. 4) Contemplate the source: every pleasure comes down from Him; the faculty of desire 'will return and want nearness to G-d, for He is good, the source of all pleasures' (Likkutei Torah, Nasso) [stated]. If you want the gift, want the Giver (M17). 5) Answer the real need in the body and in life: if it was rest, an earlier bedtime tomorrow; if loneliness, a call to a person tomorrow; if boredom, something with your hands [ours]. 6) Tell G-d in your own words what you are really hungry for (S55).
- **Signs of progress.** He can name the want under the want most times. The real need is being met more often, and the pull has less to feed on.
- **If stuck.** If nothing comes up when he asks: answer for him with a guess and let him correct it [ours]. If this turns into endless self-analysis: stop and go back to stage 2 for a week.
- **Move on when.** The under-want is usually clear and is being met in a better way.
- **Sources.** Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai (the Mitteler Rebbe): «תאות ותשוקת עצמיות הנפש להרגיש עצמותה»; Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim (the Piaseczner): «ויחשוב שרעב הוא, צמא»; Likkutei Torah, Nasso: «ויחפוץ בקרבת אלקים כי טוב שהוא מקור כל התענוגים»

> **The guide says:** Tonight, ten minutes. Think back to today's pull, whether you won or not, and ask honestly: what did I want it to give me? Rest? Comfort? To feel alive? Not to be alone? Usually a craving points at a real need through the wrong door. There's an old picture: a man asleep is stung by a fly and dreams a crate fell on him. The soul aches, and we read it as hunger. So give the real need a real answer: sleep if it's rest, a person if it's loneliness. And remember where every good thing comes from. If you want the gift, you can want the Giver even more. Then tell Him, simply, what you're really hungry for.

## Stage 4 · Keep the force, change the form

- **Aim.** The energy of wanting goes somewhere good. A new appetite for something real.
- **Practice.** 1) Learn the fact: 'the essence of this power is not sin or evil; it is only the power to desire, and it can desire the good' (Likkutei Torah, Chukas) [stated]. Don't try to kill the drive (M53). 2) Choose one place for the heat: praying a few minutes with real hunger, learning something hard, a project that helps someone, a melody sung with your whole self [ours]. 3) Train the will in small permitted things: the Tanya's example is delaying a meal for an hour while learning, 'whenever a person sacrifices his drive even for a short while' (ch. 27) [stated], and 'a person makes himself a little holy below, and he is made very holy from above' (ch. 27) [stated]. 4) Eat as service: a blessing before, eat sitting down and slowly, eat to be strong for the day; food eaten 'to broaden the mind for G-d' rises as an offering (Tanya 7) [stated], and from food the heart is fired in prayer (Sha'arei Avodah) [stated]. Not suppression: the body is fed, not starved.
- **Signs of progress.** Sometimes the pull is weaker. A new appetite for something good. Small refusals feel like strength, not deprivation.
- **If stuck.** If the drive turns into self-denial for its own sake: stop the small refusals. Never promise that it will turn over; transformation usually comes slowly, if it comes, after long holding back (S31) [stated].
- **Move on when.** This is the ongoing shape.
- **Sources.** Likkutei Torah, Chukas: «אין מהות כח הזה חטא ורע מצד עצם עצמותו שהרי עצם מהותו הוא רק כח המתאוה»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27: «כל מה שהאדם זובח יצרו אפילו שעה קלה»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27: «אדם מקדש עצמו מעט למטה – מקדשין אותו הרבה מלמעלה»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 7: «להרחיב דעתו לה׳ ולתורתו»; Sha'arei Avodah (R. Aharon of Strashelye): «על ידי אכילה ושתיה של האדם נעשה דם בחלל השמאלי שבלב ומהדם מתלהב לבו להוי"ה בקריאת שמע ותפלה»

> **The guide says:** Here's what most people don't expect: the strength of your wanting isn't bad. It's just wanting, and it can want good things as much as bad ones. So choose one place for it to go: praying with real hunger, learning something hard, a project that helps someone, a song you put your whole self into. Train your will gently in small, allowed things, like waiting half an hour before a snack while you do something good. And eat as a kind of service: a blessing first, sitting down, slowly, so you're strong for the day. You're not killing the drive or starving your body. You're giving the drive a better home.

**Daily shape.** Morning: breakfast with a blessing; one line carried ('the pull is not all of me'). During the day: meals at their times, the gate kept closed. The danger hour: the 'instead' ready, phone outside the room. Evening: 10 minutes on the want under the want. Bed at a set time with the Baal Shem Tov's line.

**Duration.** Stage 1 a week. Stage 2 is the main work: give it 40 days before judging it (the inner-world 40-day arc). Many people need months. The Tanya allows the war may last a lifetime and still be the person's service.

- Never advise stopping a medicine or a substance; addiction to alcohol, drugs or gambling needs a professional (constitution; S09). - Eating disorders (restricting, bingeing and purging, very low weight): no delays of meals, no 'small refusals' around food; a doctor or specialist, plainly. - Compulsive sexual behavior that is harming work, marriage or the law: a professional as well as this. - Never shame; never a lecture on desire (S07 blocks). - Scrupulosity: do not feed checking which soul a thought came from (PSYCHOLOGY part 6). - If the person is a minor and the issue is sexual: point to a parent, rabbi or counselor and keep it general. - Danger signs override everything (D03).

## P2-03 · Shame and self-hatred after failure

*Presenting:* "I messed up again. I'm disgusting. Who am I kidding, I'm just a bad person and I'll never change." *Deepens engine conditions:* C19, C20, C12, C15, C23

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** After a fall, a verdict on the whole self; hiding or performing; heaviness; often a repeat of the fall.
- **Where in the soul.** The animal soul's sadness wearing the clothes of teshuvah. The faculty captured is da'as of self: the mind fixed on one picture of who I am. The middah is gevurah turned inward, harsh. The garment is thought: the replay and the verdict. The G-dly soul's real bitterness (alive, wanting to move) is missing or drowned.
- **State now.** S11 The verdict: I'm my mistakes (often with S04 Low and heavy).
- **Root.** Yeshus disguised as humility. The Tanya: this sadness 'comes from coarseness of spirit: he does not know his place' (ch. 27) [stated]; the Mitteler Rebbe: it is not about G-d but about not being the complete person I want to be honored as (MR1-C02) [stated]. The self stays at the center, as judge and defendant both, standing apart from the mercy that holds it.
- **What is blocked.** Atzvus in place of merirus: a heart dull as stone instead of a heart alive and pained (Tanya 31) [stated]. The knowing that 'inside me is a real part of G-d' is not bound in. The knowing that His mercy does not run out is missing.
- **State needed.** S05 Bitter with purpose, for a fixed time only; then S77 The lower return (repair); then S53 Glad, the joy that comes after. Along the way S54 Compassion for the soul.
- **Kind of healing.** Teshuvah (the lower return) at a set time, ending in repair and then joy (Tanya 26) [stated]. Rachamim: pity for the spark inside, the Tanya's own turn in ch. 31 [stated]. 'That was not you' (M14) [stated]. Not the self-abasement of Tanya 29, which is for a hard, coarse heart, never for a crushed one (S04, S56 safety) [stated in the map].

## Stage 1 · Not now: set the hour

- **Aim.** The verdict stops running all day. The regret is moved to a set, short time.
- **Practice.** 1) When the verdict comes, do not argue and do not reassure (D05) [stated]. Say: 'Not now. I'll look at this honestly tonight, for fifteen minutes.' 2) Reason: 'now is not the time for real sorrow, even for worry over grave sins; for this one needs fixed times, a fitting hour, with a settled mind' (Tanya 26) [stated]. And heaviness in the middle of the day is the drive's trick: the fight 'cannot be won with sluggishness and heaviness, which come from sadness' (Tanya 26) [stated]. 3) Do the next small thing in front of you. 4) Do not punish the body: no skipping meals, no staying up to stew, no harsh 'penance'. The Alter Rebbe allows even fasting over sins 'only as much as he judges will surely not harm him at all' (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 3) [stated]. Get up, wash your face, eat: the body has to carry you to the repair [ours].
- **Signs of progress.** The verdict comes and is postponed. The day goes on. He eats and sleeps.
- **If stuck.** If the voice says 'I deserve it' or 'I'm a burden': that is a danger signal, not a reading (constitution); ask directly about safety (S21). If the verdict will not wait: write it on paper and put the paper away until the hour [ours].
- **Move on when.** He has held off the verdict to a set time at least a few times.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 26: «אין הזמן גרמא כעת לעצבות אמיתית, אפילו לדאגת עונות חמורים»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 26: «רק לזאת צריך קביעות עתים ושעת הכושר בישוב הדעת»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 26: «אי אפשר לנצחו בעצלות וכבדות – הנמשכות מעצבות וטמטום הלב כאבן»; Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah 3: «אלא כפי אשר ישער בנפשו שבודאי לא יזיק לו כלל»

> **The guide says:** Right now that voice is calling you a bad person. I'm not going to argue with it, and I won't just tell you you're great. I'm going to ask you to postpone it. Say: 'Not now. I'll look at this honestly tonight, for fifteen minutes.' Then do the next small thing in front of you. Heaviness now doesn't fix anything; it just makes you lose the next round. And don't punish your body for it: no skipping meals, no staying up to stew. Even teachers who fasted over their sins said to do it only if it couldn't hurt you at all. Get up, wash your face, eat. Your body has to carry you to the repair.

## Stage 2 · The honest fifteen minutes

- **Aim.** From a verdict on the self to one real wrong, felt alive, repaired, and then put down.
- **Practice.** At the set time, seated, settled, 15 minutes, no longer. 1) Name what you actually did: one specific act, not 'I'm terrible'. 2) Let it hurt in the alive way: the test is whether it wants to move and repair. Bitterness has life in it; sadness has none (Tanya 31) [stated]. 3) Say the Tanya's two sentences: 'True, this is me: the body and the vital soul in it. Even so, inside me is a real part of G-d' (Tanya 31) [stated]. 'And the pity for it is very great' (Tanya 31) [stated]: feel sorry for the spark dragged through this, not for your image. 4) Choose one repair: apologize, give back, a concrete step for tomorrow. This is the lower return, 'to lift it from its fall' (Tanya 17) [stated]. 5) Tell G-d in your own words: I did this. I'm sorry. Help me. 6) Close: 'then let him remove the sadness from his heart completely, and believe with full faith that G-d has taken away his sin, and He forgives abundantly' (Tanya 26) [stated]. The Tanya's mashal (ch. 31) [stated]: a king's son held captive, grinding in a prison, let out to his father's house; there is no joy like coming out of captivity. Where it breaks: you are not only the captive but also the one who chose; the repair is still yours to do.
- **Signs of progress.** The wrong gets named in one sentence. A repair is chosen and done. The fifteen minutes end on time, and the evening is lighter.
- **If stuck.** If the time turns into a spiral: stop at 15 minutes no matter what, and do something with your hands. If no alive pain comes, only numbness: do the repair anyway; the deed carries it (S29). If it is the same fall over and over: also use P2-02 on the pull itself.
- **Move on when.** The fifteen minutes regularly end in a repair and in setting the sadness down.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31: «כי עצבות – היא שלבו מטומטם כאבן ואין חיות בלבו, אבל מרירות ולב נשבר, אדרבה, הרי יש חיות בלבו»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31: «אך כל זה הוא אני לבדי, הוא הגוף עם נפש החיונית שבו, אבל מכל מקום, יש בקרבי ״חלק ה׳״ ממש»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31: «והרחמנות עליה גדולה מאד»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 17: «והיא בחינת תשובה תתאה, להעלות ה״א תתאה, להקימה מנפילתה»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 26: «אזי יסיר העצב מלבו לגמרי, ויאמין אמונה שלימה כי ה׳ העביר חטאתו ״ורב לסלוח״»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31: «ואין לך שמחה גדולה כצאת מהגלות והשביה, כמשל בן מלך שהיה בשביה וטוחן בבית האסורים»

> **The guide says:** Now the honest fifteen minutes. Sit somewhere quiet. First, name what you actually did: one specific thing, not 'I'm terrible'. Let it hurt in a way that wants to move. Then say two true sentences. 'Yes, this is me, the part of me that grabs and slips.' And: 'Even so, inside me is a real part of G-d, and I feel sorry for it, dragged through this.' Next, pick one repair: an apology, giving something back, one step for tomorrow. Tell G-d in your own words: I did this, I'm sorry, help me. Then close it. Put the sadness down completely, and trust that He forgives, and forgives a lot.

## Stage 3 · Forgiven abundantly: the mercy that does not run out

- **Aim.** The person stops counting whether he has used up his chances.
- **Practice.** Morning, in prayer or alone, 5 minutes. 1) The Alter Rebbe's reasoning (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11) [stated]: between people, if one wrongs another, is forgiven, and goes back to it, it is very hard to forgive a second time, and harder a third. Yet three times every day the Amidah blesses G-d as the One 'who forgives abundantly', precisely abundantly. 2) In the 'Forgive us' blessing, stop at 'who forgives abundantly' and think: this was written for people who fall again [ours: the pause]. 3) The Mitteler Rebbe: even if the son sins until he is loathed, the essential mercy prevails (Imrei Binah; MR1-C22, MR1-E8) [stated]. The mashal: a father's mercy that is part of himself. Where it breaks: a human father can be harsh or absent; this mercy is not modeled on any person you know. And mercy does not cancel the repair. 4) Carry the line: 'He forgives abundantly. Today I start again.'
- **Signs of progress.** Less 'I've ruined it for good'. He returns faster after a fall.
- **If stuck.** If he cannot believe it for himself: let him say it for someone he loves who fell, then say it for himself [ours].
- **Move on when.** He returns from a fall within a day, not a week.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11: «שבמדת בשר ודם, אם יחטא איש לאיש וביקש ממנו מחילה ומחל לו ואחר כך חזר לסורו – קשה מאד שימחול לו שנית»; Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11: «״המרבה״ דייקא»; Imrei Binah (the Mitteler Rebbe): «דגם אם ירבה לפשוע עד שימאס וישנאנו בתכלית יגברו רחמים העצמיי'»

> **The guide says:** Here's something for the mornings. People forgive once. A second time is hard. A third time, almost nobody does. Yet three times a day, the daily prayer calls G-d the One who forgives abundantly. That word was written for people who fall again and again. There's a mercy like a parent's love that's part of who they are: it isn't earned, and it doesn't run out. So when you think, 'surely I've used up my chances', stop on that line. Say it slowly: He forgives abundantly. Let it be true for this morning. Then go and do your repair.

## Stage 4 · Joy after, and walking like it

- **Aim.** Acting from worth, not from shame. Gladness that came after sorrow.
- **Practice.** 1) The Tanya: true joy comes after the sorrow, like light that comes out of darkness (ch. 26) [stated]. Joy is not a reward for being perfect. 2) The Piaseczner: 'whoever feels himself empty and vile behaves empty and vile' (Esh Kodesh) [stated]. So today do one good thing as someone who carries a part of G-d: a kindness, a prayer, a kept promise. 3) Let the body say it too: get dressed properly, eat breakfast, stand up straight, show up on time. These are the body's way of saying this life is worth caring for, Hillel's 'kindness to the poor, humble one' (Tanya 29) [stated: Hillel; ours: the use here]. 4) Do not be wicked in your own eyes (Avot, as the Tanya opens) [stated]. When the old voice comes, answer once: 'I fell. I am returning.' Then keep going.
- **Signs of progress.** Small gladness after a repair. He does good deeds without first needing to feel worthy.
- **If stuck.** If joy feels fake: don't force it; do the act and let the gladness come late (VS-02).
- **Move on when.** This is the ongoing shape.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 26: «כדי ללמוד בשמחה אמיתית בה׳ הבאה אחר העצב»; Esh Kodesh (the Piaseczner): «כי מי שמרגיש עצמו לריק ומנוול מתנהג כריק ומנוול»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 29 (Hillel, as the Tanya tells it): «שהוא הולך לגמול חסד עם העלובה ועניה – הוא גופו»; Pirkei Avot 2:13 as the Tanya reads it (Likkutei Amarim 1): «ואל תהי רשע בפני עצמך»

> **The guide says:** The last step is joy, and it isn't a reward for being perfect. It comes after the sorrow, like light that's brighter because it came out of the dark. Someone who feels worthless acts worthless. So today, do one good thing as someone who carries a part of G-d inside: a kindness, a prayer, a promise kept. Let your body say it too: get dressed properly, eat breakfast, stand up straight, show up on time. Those are ways of saying your life is worth taking care of. When the old voice says you're a fraud, answer it once: 'I fell. I'm returning.' Then keep going.

**Daily shape.** Morning: the 'forgives abundantly' pause; breakfast; dressed properly. During the day: the verdict postponed; the next small thing done. Evening, a set time: the honest fifteen minutes, then closed. Bed on time, with no stewing.

**Duration.** The postponing works within days. The full turn (named wrong, repair, set down, joy) takes 3-6 weeks to become a habit. Honest: the voice usually comes back after big falls; what changes is how fast he returns.

- 'I deserve it', 'I'm a burden', 'everyone would be better off': danger signals; the safety protocol overrides everything (S21, D03). - No reassurance and no confirmation of the verdict (D05). - Never Tanya 29's harsh self-talk ('you are evil...') for this person; it is written for a hard heart, not a crushed one. - Scrupulosity or OCD-like confessing: the fifteen minutes are capped; no repeated confessing; a professional if it is constant. - Shame from abuse done to the person is not their sin; do not run teshuvah on it; a counselor. - Persistent low mood, poor sleep, not functioning: a doctor, plainly.

## P2-04 · Grief

*Presenting:* "My mother died three months ago. I still can't believe she's gone. Everyone tells me she's in a better place and I want to scream." *Deepens engine conditions:* C28, C26

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** A loss; love with nowhere to go; comfort that feels like a lecture; days that don't hold together.
- **Where in the soul.** Both souls. The G-dly soul's love (chesed) has lost its address; the animal soul has lost a part of its world. The middos are love and its pain; the garment that most needs an opening is speech, words to Him (S55). Level: ruach, the feelings; at times the yechidah's plain cry (S51). This is not a fault in the soul.
- **State now.** S14 Loss and grief (sometimes S10 Torn in two).
- **Root.** Ours, said plainly: grief is not yeshus and is not a problem to be traced to a root. Where the root problem shows here, it shows only in a disguise: 'I am alone with this', the self carrying the loss by itself, apart. The sources do not answer with an explanation of the death; they answer that He is with the one who weeps (Esh Kodesh) [stated].
- **What is blocked.** Not a blockage to fix. What is closed is the address for the love: no one to tell, nowhere to put it. Sometimes da'as of being accompanied is missing. Watch that grief does not slide into atzvus that stops functioning, or into danger.
- **State needed.** S55 Own words to Him; S81 The deed in the hand, done for the person; later and only if it comes, S49 Weeping and joy in one heart. Never S56 or any bittul teaching here.
- **Kind of healing.** Company and the deed: shefichas hanefesh (pouring out the heart) [stated: Tzav VeZeruz], being with Him in the weeping (Esh Kodesh) [stated], then giving the love a place in deeds done in the person's name. Comfort (nechamah) is His and the community's; the guide does not hand meaning to someone in the fire (D41). Not birur, not iskafya, not 'everything is for the good'.

## Stage 1 · Company, not answers

- **Aim.** The person is not alone with it: met by the guide, by people, and by G-d who is with the one who weeps. The body is kept going as the vessel the soul still needs.
- **Practice.** 1) The guide does not explain the loss, and never says 'the hiding is His' about a death (D03) [stated]. 2) Offer what the Piaseczner wrote in the ghetto, as his words, not as an argument: in the inner chambers He grieves and weeps (Esh Kodesh) [stated], 'and there He weeps and wails with him, as it were, together' (Esh Kodesh) [stated]. And the Mitteler Rebbe: He dwells with the crushed 'to revive the spirit of the crushed' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut) [stated]. 3) Daily, a few minutes at a fixed time: talk to G-d about the person in your own words, any language: what you miss today, what you wish you'd said. 'Pour out your heart before Him, as it rises in your heart, in any language you understand' (Tzav VeZeruz) [stated]. Out loud, or written as a letter [ours]. Tears are allowed and not timed. 4) The body as the vessel: let people feed you; eat something even without appetite; lie down at night even if sleep is thin; step outside once a day. Reason: Hillel called feeding the body a kindness to 'the poor, humble one' (Tanya 29) [stated], and the body is the home the soul still needs to carry this love [ours]. Jewish mourning itself begins with others feeding the mourner [custom, not quoted here]. 5) For Jewish mourners, the laws and customs of mourning (Kaddish, the year) go through a rabbi [ours].
- **Signs of progress.** The daily few minutes happen. He eats and sleeps somewhat. He lets one person in.
- **If stuck.** If there are no words: one word is enough, or just the name, or a cry (S51) [stated in the map: the simple cry counts]. If he is angry at G-d: that can be said to Him too; it is still speaking to Him [ours]. If he can't function (not eating, not getting up) for weeks: a doctor or grief counselor, plainly.
- **Move on when.** When he asks 'what do I do with all this love?', or after a few weeks of the daily minutes. Never rushed.
- **Sources.** Esh Kodesh (the Piaseczner): «ובבתי גואי מצטער ובוכה כביכול בצרות ישראל»; Esh Kodesh (the Piaseczner): «ושם בוכה ומילל עמו כביכול יחד»; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe): «אז ישכון בו ממקור כל החיים מקורא דכולא להחיות רוח נדכאים»; Tzav VeZeruz (the Piaseczner): «ושופך לפניו את לבך בשיח ותפילה כפי שעולה על לבך בכל לשון שאתה שומע»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 29 (Hillel, as the Tanya tells it): «שהוא הולך לגמול חסד עם העלובה ועניה – הוא גופו»

> **The guide says:** I'm so sorry. I'm not going to explain why this happened; nobody can. Let me just keep you company in it. One teacher, who lost almost everyone he loved, wrote that when a person weeps, G-d weeps with him, together. So you're not crying alone. Here's one small practice: each day, take a few minutes to talk to G-d about her, in your own words, any language. What you miss today. What you wish you'd said. If tears come, let them. And let people feed you. Eat something even when you don't want to, and lie down at night even if sleep is thin. Your body is still the home your soul lives in, and it needs care to carry this much love.

## Stage 2 · Give the love a place: a deed in their name

- **Aim.** The love gets an address in the world. The bond feels alive in something done.
- **Practice.** 1) Once a day or once a week, one good deed in the person's name: a coin to charity, a chapter of Psalms, a short passage learned, a kindness done the way they would have done it [ours: the shape; widely held Jewish custom that such deeds benefit the departed soul, not quoted here]. 2) Say their name when you do it. 3) Reason: in doing a commanded deed 'a person draws the revelation of the Infinite Light down, to clothe itself in the physical world' (Tanya 37) [stated]; and 'one particular movement of a creature bears on the whole purpose of creation' (Hayom Yom) [stated]. The deed is real, not only a memory [ours: the application to grief]. 4) Do it with the body: hands giving, feet walking to the person being helped, the voice saying the words. 5) Do not claim to know what the deed does for the person who died, beyond what the tradition holds [ours].
- **Signs of progress.** A deed in their name is part of the week. He speaks of the person with more warmth and less only-pain.
- **If stuck.** If deeds feel empty: keep them small and keep them anyway; the feeling is not the measure (S29).
- **Move on when.** The deed has become a habit, and some days have room for a good memory.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 37: «כי בעשייתה, ממשיך האדם גילוי אור אין סוף ברוך הוא מלמעלה למטה להתלבש בגשמיות עולם הזה»; Hayom Yom 28 Cheshvan: «אלא עוד זאת דתנועה פרטית של נברא יש לה יחס כללי לכללות כונת הבריאה»

> **The guide says:** The love you have for him didn't go anywhere. It needs a place to go, so let's give it one: a deed in his name. Once a day, or once a week, do one good thing for him. Give a little charity, say a psalm, learn a short passage, help someone the way he would have. When you do a good deed with your hands, something of G-d comes down into this world through it. It's a real act, not just a memory. Many people find the bond feels alive in these deeds. Say his name when you do it, and do it with your body: your hands giving, your feet walking to the person you're helping.

## Stage 3 · Two sides of one heart

- **Aim.** Weeping and gratitude live together without either cancelling the other.
- **Practice.** Only when the person is ready; never pushed, never in the first weeks [ours]. 1) The Zohar as the Tanya quotes it: 'weeping is lodged in my heart on one side, and joy on the other' (Tanya 34) [stated]. 2) Once a week at a quiet time (for many, Friday night), remember one good thing about the person: a laugh, something they taught. 3) Say thank you to G-d for that one thing, while the missing stays where it is. 4) The mashal: two rooms in one house, both lit. Where it breaks: grief is not tidy; the rooms leak into each other, and that is fine [ours]. 5) Not a goal of feeling better: the love is being allowed both its faces.
- **Signs of progress.** A thank-you can be said without feeling like betrayal. Memories bring warmth as well as pain.
- **If stuck.** If thanks won't come: just tell Him you miss them. That counts too.
- **Move on when.** There is no 'end'. Grief changes shape; the practices stay as long as they help.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 34 (quoting the Zohar): «בכיה תקיעא בלבאי מסטרא דא, וחדוה תקיעא בלבאי מסטרא דא»
- **Deliberately not used here.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 26: «כי ״גם זו – לטובה״, רק שאינה נגלית ונראית לעיני בשר» (for 'this too is for the good', only it is not revealed and seen by eyes of flesh). See guards.

> **The guide says:** Some day, maybe not yet, try this. Weeping and joy can live in one heart, on two sides of it. You don't have to choose between them. Once a week, at a quiet time, remember one good thing about her: her laugh, something she taught you, the way she said your name. Say thank you to G-d for that one thing, while the missing stays exactly where it is. You're not trying to feel better or move on. You're letting your love have both of its faces. If thanks won't come, that's fine. Just tell Him you miss her. That counts too.

**Daily shape.** Morning: something to eat, even small. A fixed few minutes of own words to G-d about the person. During the day: one person reached; a step outside. Weekly (or daily): the deed in their name. When ready, weekly: one thank-you. Night: lie down at a set time.

**Duration.** Months, and the first year is its own season. Honest: grief does not finish; it changes. The practices are company along the way, not a cure.

- Never explain the death, never offer a reason, never 'better place' or 'the hiding is His' about a death (D03; S14). - Grief is not a practice state: no bittul, no self-examination, no 'everything is for the good' (Tanya 26's 'this too is for the good' is never applied to a death by the guide). - Watch for 'I want to be with her', not wanting to be here, not eating or sleeping for days: the safety protocol (S21). - Grief that is not easing at all after many months, or that stops a person functioning: a doctor or grief counselor. - A child's grief: parents and a professional; keep it simple. - Mourning laws and Kaddish: a rabbi.

## P2-05 · Emptiness: "what's the point?"

*Presenting:* "I get up, I work, I eat, I scroll, I sleep. Repeat. What's the point of any of it?" *Deepens engine conditions:* C40, C22, C18

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** Life runs, but nothing in it seems to matter; success feels empty; a homesickness with no home in mind.
- **Where in the soul.** The G-dly soul's thirst, unheard: 'it is not the man who feels this pain, only the G-dly spark within him' (Derekh HaMelekh) [stated]. Faculties: chochmah and binah have no purpose to grasp; da'as has nothing to bind to. The garments (deeds) are done without intent. Level: the neshamah's longing. The animal soul is often worn down and flat as well.
- **State now.** S13 The unnamed longing (if heavy and slowed, S04 Low and heavy: use P1's heaviness protocol first). Always check S21.
- **Root.** Yeshus in the form 'what does it give me?': life is weighed by how it makes me feel, so when the feeling is flat, nothing seems to count. The self stands apart from the purpose it is part of. Together with S12, the flat world: it all looks like it runs on its own.
- **What is blocked.** Da'as of purpose. The thirst is mistaken for hunger or boredom (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim) [stated]. Meaning is sought in a mood instead of in a deed (S29).
- **State needed.** S81 The deed in the hand; S82 The home below; in time S85 Seeing Him in what happens. S13 named as the soul's.
- **Kind of healing.** Birur of the longing (naming it as the soul's, not a malfunction), then kabbalas ol in its plain sense: the deed done before the feeling (S83), then simcha after. The purpose is given in the deed's address, not felt first (Tanya 36) [stated].

## Stage 1 · Name the ache, and keep the vessel

- **Aim.** The emptiness is read as a sign of the soul, not proof that life is empty. Danger is ruled out first.
- **Practice.** 1) Ask once, plainly: 'When you say what's the point, do you mean you don't want to be here?' If yes, the safety protocol (S21) and nothing else. 2) If no: the Piaseczner's reading. It is the spark that feels this pain (Derekh HaMelekh) [stated]; a sleeper stung by a fly dreams a crate fell on him, 'and he thinks it is hunger, thirst' (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim) [stated]. The ache is the soul homesick. Where the mashal breaks: some flatness is the body's (no sleep, no light, illness) and needs the body's answer. 3) The body is the vessel the meaning must be poured into: sleep at a set time, daylight in the morning, a walk, real meals [ours, on] 'a small hole in the body makes a big hole in the soul' (the Maggid) [stated] and 'keep his health plainly, in eating, drinking and sleep, and it will be good for him spiritually too' (the Rebbe) [stated]. 4) Write down the hour the emptiness is worst [ours].
- **Signs of progress.** He can say 'this is the ache' without concluding 'life is pointless'. Sleep and meals are steadier.
- **If stuck.** If the flatness is heavy, slowed, lasting weeks, with poor sleep or appetite: a doctor, plainly, and P1 heaviness protocol.
- **Move on when.** Danger is ruled out and the ache has a name.
- **Sources.** Derekh HaMelekh (the Piaseczner): «לא האיש הוא המרגיש את הצער הזה, רק הניצוץ אלקי שבקרבו»; Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim (the Piaseczner): «ויחשוב שרעב הוא, צמא»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 7, letter 2055, citing the Maggid to his son (Yiddish): «אז א קליינע לעכעלע אין גוף גורם א גרויסע לאך אין דער נשמה»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 7, letter 2055: «הרי ישמור על בריאותו פשוט כפשוטו בעניני אכילה ושתי' ושינה וכו' אשר עי"ז טוב יהי' לו גם ברוחניות»

> **The guide says:** First, a question I ask everyone who says this: when you say 'what's the point', do you ever mean you don't want to be here? If so, tell me, and let's get you a person today. If not, here's another way to see the ache. One teacher said it isn't really you who feels this emptiness; it's the spark of G-d in you, homesick. Like a man asleep who's stung by a fly and dreams a crate fell on him: the soul aches, and we read it as boredom or hunger. So the ache isn't proof that life is empty. It's a sign something in you wants more. And look after the body that carries it: sleep, daylight, a walk, real meals.

## Stage 2 · Seventy years for one favor

- **Aim.** Purpose is found in a deed for another person, not waited for as a feeling.
- **Practice.** 1) The teaching, from the Baal Shem Tov through the Alter Rebbe: 'a soul comes down into the world and lives seventy, eighty years, to do another Jew a favor, in material things and above all in spiritual ones' (Hayom Yom) [stated]. (For a non-Jewish person, the guide offers it as 'another person' [ours].) 2) Each day, one deliberate favor for one person, for their sake: carry something, send the message they need, listen properly. Go there in body; hands and feet, not only a text [ours]. 3) At night, write it down: who, what. 4) Do not wait to feel meaning first; meaning lives in the deed (S29). 5) After three weeks, read the list back.
- **Signs of progress.** The list grows. He notices people more. Some days feel less flat, without being chased.
- **If stuck.** If it feels mechanical: that is allowed; the Tanya counts the deed done with understanding even without felt love (ch. 16, 44; S29) [stated in the map]. If it becomes a way to please everyone: one favor, not ten.
- **Move on when.** The daily favor is a habit.
- **Sources.** Hayom Yom 5 Iyar (the Baal Shem Tov, via the Alter Rebbe; Yiddish): «עס קומט אראפ א נשמה אויף דער וועלט און לעבט אפ זיבעציג אכציג יאהר, צוליב טאן א אידען א טובה בגשמיות ובפרט אין רוחניות»

> **The guide says:** Here's a practice from an old teaching: a soul comes down into the world and lives seventy or eighty years just to do another person one favor. Just one. So today, do one deliberate favor for one person, for their sake. Not a big project. Carry something for someone. Send the message they need. Listen properly. Go there in person if you can; let your hands and feet do it. At night, write it down: who, and what. Don't wait to feel meaning first. Meaning lives in the deed, not in the mood. You may feel nothing the first week. Keep the list anyway, and after three weeks read it back.

## Stage 3 · The ordinary day as His home

- **Aim.** The plain parts of the day get an address: they are where G-d wanted to live.
- **Practice.** Morning, 10 minutes, then three moments in the day. 1) Contemplate: 'the Holy One desired to have a dwelling in the lowest realm' (Tanya 36) [stated]: not heaven, here; kitchens, offices, traffic. 'One particular movement of a creature bears on the whole purpose of creation' (Hayom Yom) [stated]. And the Alter Rebbe: 'in Gan Eden one hears how precious this world is' (Hayom Yom) [stated]. 2) Choose three ordinary acts of today: the first email, lunch, the drive home. Before each, say quietly: 'This is part of His home.' 3) Before eating, a blessing; eat sitting, as if it matters, because the body is part of the home [ours]. 4) Mashal [ours]: a house is made a home not by its walls but by someone living there; the walls look the same. Where it breaks: He is not absent until we invite Him; the home is His already, and we make it shown.
- **Signs of progress.** The three moments happen. Occasionally a plain moment feels addressed.
- **If stuck.** If it feels silly: do it for two weeks anyway; the point is the address, not a feeling.
- **Move on when.** The moments happen without reminders on most days.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 36: «שנתאוה הקדושברוךהוא להיות לו דירה בתחתונים»; Hayom Yom 28 Cheshvan: «אלא עוד זאת דתנועה פרטית של נברא יש לה יחס כללי לכללות כונת הבריאה»; Hayom Yom 17 Adar I (the Alter Rebbe; Yiddish): «אין גן עדן דערהערט מען די טייערקייט פון עולם הזה»

> **The guide says:** Now let's look at your ordinary day differently. There's a teaching that G-d wanted a home not in heaven but down here, in the plainest places: offices, kitchens, traffic. And one small movement of one person matters to the whole purpose of the world. So pick three ordinary acts from your day, like the first email, lunch, the drive home. Before each one, say quietly: this is part of His home. Before you eat, say a short blessing, and eat sitting down, as if it matters, because your body is part of the home too. It may feel silly. Do it for two weeks anyway. The point isn't a feeling. It's an address.

## Stage 4 · Ask the One

- **Aim.** A personal purpose, found a day at a time.
- **Practice.** Evening, 3 minutes. 1) Speak to G-d in plain words: 'What do You want from me tomorrow?' (S55; an invitation, never demanded of someone unsure, D17) [stated in the map]. 2) Sit quietly for a minute. Do not expect a voice. Notice what comes to mind: a person to call, a thing to fix, something avoided. 3) Write one down and do it tomorrow. 4) Reason: 'every soul has its own particular work, in mind and in feelings, according to its nature' (Hayom Yom) [stated]. Yours is not anyone else's.
- **Signs of progress.** Small specific tasks keep turning up and get done. 'What's the point' is asked less, and with less weight.
- **If stuck.** If nothing comes: tomorrow's task is the daily favor from stage 2.
- **Move on when.** This is the ongoing shape.
- **Sources.** Hayom Yom 8 Nissan: «כל נשמה יש לה עבודה פרטית במוחין ומדות כפי טבעה וענינה»

> **The guide says:** When the day ends, try talking to G-d directly, in plain words, for two or three minutes. Not a formal prayer. Just: 'What do You want from me tomorrow?' Then sit quietly for a minute. You probably won't hear a voice. Listen anyway, and notice what comes to mind: a person to call, something to fix, a thing you've been avoiding. Write one down and do it tomorrow. Every soul has its own particular job, matched to its nature, and yours isn't anyone else's. You don't find it all at once. You find it one day at a time, by asking and then doing.

**Daily shape.** Morning: daylight, breakfast with a blessing, 10 minutes on the home below. During the day: the three marked moments; one favor done in person. Evening: the favor written down; three minutes of 'what do You want from me tomorrow?'. Bed at a set time.

**Duration.** Two to six weeks for the first lift. The purpose question returns at life changes; the practices are for years.

- 'What's the point' can mean not wanting to live: ask directly, once, at the start; the safety protocol overrides everything (S21, D03). - Persistent flatness with poor sleep, appetite, energy, or loss of interest for weeks: a doctor, plainly. Do not call depression a spiritual state. - Do not promise experiences or a felt sense of meaning (S13 safety). - Do not push G-d-talk on someone unsure; stage 4 is an invitation (D17, D27).

## P2-06 · Fear of death or illness

*Presenting:* "Every headache, I think it's a tumor. I search symptoms at night. And when I lie down I think about dying and I can't calm down." *Deepens engine conditions:* C17, C16, C26

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** Health worry that checks and re-checks; dread of dying, worst at night; reassurance that lasts an hour.
- **Where in the soul.** The animal soul's love of its own life, and the fear that guards it; the faculty running ahead is the imagination (koach hamedameh) picturing the worst; da'as does not bind the knowing that life is given each moment. Garments: thought (the scenarios) and deed (checking, searching). Level: nefesh, the life of the body.
- **State now.** S02 Holding it all up (if unreal or detached, S20; always check S21).
- **Root.** Yeshus in the form of keeper: 'my life is mine and held up by me; if I stop watching, I'm gone.' The Alter Rebbe's diagnosis: when a person pictures his coming into being from nothing every moment, how could he think it is bad for him? (Iggeret HaKodesh 11) [stated]. The self stands apart as its own keeper.
- **What is blocked.** Da'as of continuous creation (S65): known, perhaps, but not bound. The body is watched as a threat instead of cared for as a vessel. The checking feeds the fear.
- **State needed.** S65 Given right now, then S84 Trust (shoulders down, practical step still taken). At night, S55 own words, the handing over.
- **Kind of healing.** Bitachon through contemplation of being given each moment (hamtakah of the fear in its root, not argument). Kabbalas ol in the plain sense: do the practical part fully, then give the outcome over. The doctor is part of the healing, not opposed to it: the Torah gave the doctor permission, and power, to heal (the Rebbe) [stated]; 'by making a vessel in nature, success is drawn into it above nature' (the Rebbe) [stated].

## Stage 1 · The doctor, once, as making a vessel

- **Aim.** The real practical step is taken fully, and then the checking stops.
- **Practice.** 1) If something worries you, see a doctor and tell them everything; follow an expert's instructions (the Rebbe: 'permission was given to the doctor to heal; so one must follow the instructions of an expert doctor') [stated], 'knowing clearly that G-d is the healer of all flesh, who does wonders' (same letter) [stated]. 2) Reason: the Rebbe teaches that making a vessel in nature is how blessing comes into it (Igros Kodesh) [stated]; going to the doctor is that vessel, an act of service, not a lack of faith. The Rebbe also pairs it: a weak bodily limb goes to the doctor, and a weak spiritual limb needs its own care, 'seeing what comes into being from nothing every moment' (Igros Kodesh) [stated] (this is stage 2). 3) After the appointment: no more searching symptoms. When the urge to check comes, say: 'I've done my part. The rest is His.' The Baal Shem Tov: whatever comes, think that it is from Him (Tzava'at HaRivash 4) [stated]. 4) If the doctor finds nothing and the fear stays strong, that fear itself is something to see a professional about, plainly.
- **Signs of progress.** The appointment happened. Fewer searches. The 'my part / His part' line used at the urge.
- **If stuck.** If he keeps asking for reassurance (from the guide too): stop giving it; give one action instead (PSYCHOLOGY part 6, rule 6) [stated there]. Don't repeat the line endlessly; that becomes checking too.
- **Move on when.** The practical step is done and checking has dropped.
- **Sources.** Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 10, letter 2899: «שניתנה רשות לרופא לרפאות, ובמילא צריך למלאות הוראות רופא מומחה»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 10, letter 2899: «אשר השי"ת הוא הרופא כל בשר ומפליא לעשות»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 3: «אשר על ידי עשיית כלי בטבע נמשך בו הצלחה בדרך למעלה מן הטבע»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 4: «כשמרגיש חולשה באבר גשמי הרי הולך לרופא»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 4: «בענין הראי' את הנולד מאין ליש בכל רגע»; Tzava'at HaRivash 4: «שכל דבר שיזדמן יחשוב שהוא מאתו יתברך»

> **The guide says:** Let's take the fear seriously, which means doing the practical thing first. If something about your health worries you, see a doctor and tell them everything, then follow what a good doctor says. That isn't a lack of faith. The tradition says healing was given into the doctor's hands, and that making a vessel in the natural world is how blessing comes into it. G-d is still the healer; the doctor is the vessel. Then, once you've done it, stop checking. No more searching symptoms at midnight. Each time the urge comes, say: 'I've done my part. The rest is His.' If the doctor finds nothing and the fear stays strong, that's worth getting help for too.

## Stage 2 · Given right now (the morning contemplation)

- **Aim.** From 'I hold my life up' to 'my life is being given, this moment'. The shoulders come down a little.
- **Practice.** Morning, 10 minutes, eyes open. 1) Look at something outside the body: a cup, the light on the wall. Not the heartbeat, not the breath, not any body sensation (PSYCHOLOGY part 6, rule 1: for health fear, never use the body as the felt fact) [stated there]. 2) Ask: why is this here right now? Contemplate the Baal Shem Tov's reading as the Tanya brings it: if the letters that make it withdrew for an instant, it would be truly nothing (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1) [stated]. It is being said, now. 3) The Rashab: 'being renewed from its source is its cleaving to its source' (Samach Vav, OCR) [stated]. 4) Apply it gently to yourself: my life this minute is not held up by my watching; it is being given, now, and now. 5) Then the Alter Rebbe's question, left as a question: 'when a person pictures his coming into being from nothing at every moment, how can he think it is bad for him?' (Iggeret HaKodesh 11) [stated]; 'the nothing, His wisdom, is the source of life, of good and of delight' (same) [stated]. Do not force an answer; let it sit next to the fear. 6) Mashal [ours]: a song sung live, not a recording; each note is given as it is sung. Where it breaks: the singer can stop from tiredness; He does not tire, and the giving is not a performance for us.
- **Signs of progress.** A few moments of 'given, not kept'. The fear still comes, but sometimes the line comes after it.
- **If stuck.** If the contemplation itself frightens ('what if He stops?'): close before vast (PSYCHOLOGY rule 8); stay with the cup and 'given now', skip the 'nothing' language. If it stays abstract: say it aloud, and in detail: this cup, this table, this hand that holds it.
- **Move on when.** The morning line is said most days and is sometimes felt.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1: «כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש»; Hemshech Samach Vav 5666 (the Rashab): «דההתחדשות ממקורו זהו הדביקות במקורו» (OCR); Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 11: «וכשיתבונן האדם בעומק הבנתו, ויצייר בדעתו הווייתו מ״אין״ בכל רגע ורגע ממש, האיך יעלה על דעתו כי רע לו»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 11: «הרי ה״אין״, שהיא חכמתו יתברך, הוא מקור החיים והטוב והעונג»

> **The guide says:** Now a morning practice, ten minutes, with your eyes open. Look at something outside you: a cup on the table, the light on the wall. Ask: why is this here right now? Not because it was made once and left. It's being given being this moment, the way a voice keeps a word in the air. If that stopped for an instant, it would be nothing. Now apply it gently to yourself: my life right now isn't held up by my watching. It's being given, this moment and the next. One teacher asked: if a person really pictured that, how could he think it's bad for him? Don't force an answer. Just let it sit next to the fear.

## Stage 3 · Into Your hand (the night)

- **Aim.** The night stops being the fear's time. Sleep is protected as a vessel.
- **Practice.** 1) In bed, say the bedtime line from the Shema before sleep: 'into Your hand I entrust my spirit' (Siddur) [stated: the text]. Say it slowly, as a handing over: my body, my breath, my tomorrow, for the night [ours: the intent]. 2) Mashal [ours]: a child hands a heavy bag to his father and lets his arms rest. Where it breaks: the bag is not taken from you forever; you take up your part again in the morning. 3) If the fear wakes you, say it again. Don't argue with the fear; hand it over again. 4) Protect sleep as service: the same bedtime, no screens or symptom searches in bed, the room dark [ours, on 'a small hole in the body' (the Maggid) [stated]]. 5) The Baal Shem Tov's line for lying down: my mind goes to Him and will be strengthened for His service (Tzava'at HaRivash 23) [stated].
- **Signs of progress.** Falling asleep faster on some nights. Less 2 a.m. searching.
- **If stuck.** If panic attacks come at night: a doctor or therapist; the guide gives no breathing drills or body techniques here.
- **Move on when.** Most nights end with the line, not the search.
- **Sources.** Siddur, the Shema at bedtime: «בידך אפקיד רוחי, פדית אותי יי אל אמת»; Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 7, letter 2055, citing the Maggid to his son (Yiddish): «אז א קליינע לעכעלע אין גוף גורם א גרויסע לאך אין דער נשמה»; Tzava'at HaRivash 23: «וכשהולך לישן יחשוב הלא המוחין שלו ילכו להקב"ה ויתחזקו לעבודתו ית'»

> **The guide says:** Nights are hardest, so let's give the night its own practice. There's an old line said before sleep: into Your hand I entrust my spirit. Say it in bed, slowly, as a handing over: my body, my breath, my tomorrow, I give them to You for the night. Like a child who hands a heavy bag to his father and finally lets his arms rest. If the fear wakes you at two in the morning, say it again. You don't need to win an argument with the fear; just hand it over again. And protect your sleep as part of the practice: the same bedtime each night, no screens and no symptom searches in bed.

## Stage 4 · Live the day you're given

- **Aim.** The fear's energy turns toward living: today has a purpose, not a countdown.
- **Practice.** 1) If every moment is given, today is a gift with a job in it (S65 grows into S82) [ours]. 2) Each day, one thing you would want done if you knew your time mattered: call a parent, give something, finish what you promised, say the kind word (link P2-05 stage 2) [ours]. 3) Care for the body as something entrusted, not as a threat to watch: walk, eat well, keep the regular check-ups, and no more than those. 'Keeping the body healthy and whole is one of the ways of G-d' (Rambam, cited by the Rebbe) [stated].
- **Signs of progress.** The fear still visits but does not run the day. Small deeds pile up.
- **If stuck.** If the deeds become frantic ('I must do everything before I die'): one deed a day, no more.
- **Move on when.** This is the ongoing shape.
- **Sources.** Igros Kodesh (the Rebbe) vol. 7, letter 2055, citing Rambam, Hilchos De'os 4:1: «היות הגוף בריא ושלם מדרכי השם הוא»
- **Deliberately not used here.** Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 8:17 (the Piaseczner): «ובשעה שנמצא אתה בעת של כבדות הלב כזאת» (and at a time when you find yourself in such heaviness of heart). See guards.

> **The guide says:** If every moment is being given, then today is a gift with a purpose, not a countdown. Your fear has a lot of energy. Let's point it somewhere. Each day, do one thing you'd want done if you knew your time mattered: call your mother, give something away, finish what you promised, say the kind word. Today, not someday. Care for your body the same way, as something entrusted to you: walk, eat well, keep your regular check-ups, and no more than those. Not from panic, from respect. The fear may not vanish, but it can start working for your life instead of against it.

**Daily shape.** Morning: 10 minutes 'given right now', eyes on something outside the body; breakfast. During the day: the urge to check met with 'my part is done; the rest is His'; one deed that matters. Night: same bedtime, no screens in bed, 'into Your hand' said slowly.

**Duration.** The checking drops within 1-3 weeks once the doctor step is done. The fear softens over 1-3 months. Honest: fear of death is human and does not vanish; what changes is who holds it.

- Never tell a sick or dying person that nothing bad is happening, and never use Iggeret HaKodesh 11's 'whoever is sad is like a denier' line with anyone. - The Piaseczner does teach picturing death and the grave (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 8:17), but for a hardened heart 'at a time of heaviness of heart'; it is never used for someone already afraid of death. - Health anxiety and panic: no attention to body sensations, no breathing drills or body scans (PSYCHOLOGY part 6, rule 1); a doctor or therapist. - Real illness: medical care always; the guide never suggests faith instead of treatment, supplements or doses. - Terminal illness: company, not teaching; a rabbi or chaplain; never explain suffering. - Danger signs override everything (S21, D03).

## P2-07 · Jealousy and comparison

*Presenting:* "Everyone online is doing better than me. My brother got the promotion. I smile and say congrats and I feel sick inside." *Deepens engine conditions:* C38, C36

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** Constant measuring against others; a sting at others' good news; a quiet sense of being cheated.
- **Where in the soul.** The animal soul: love of self and its pride (fire, Tanya 1) [stated] turned into rivalry; its craving (water) for what the other has. The gate is the eye: seeing brings to remembering and to craving (Tzava'at HaRivash 6) [stated]. The garment is thought: the comparison loop. Level: nefesh and ruach.
- **State now.** S06 Self-enclosed (with S07 Restless wanting).
- **Root.** Yeshus as a quantity: my worth is a number measured against yours, and the supply is limited, so what is given to him was taken from me. The self stands apart from the Giver. The Baal Shem Tov's school: 'G-d gave his fellow a mind just as He gave him a mind' (Tzava'at HaRivash 12) [stated], 'all is from Him' (Tzava'at HaRivash 127) [stated].
- **What is blocked.** Da'as of the source of gifts: every gift, his and mine, is being given now (S65). The eye feeds the loop. Gladness at another's good is shut.
- **State needed.** S86 The other held; S84 Trust about my own portion; S53 Glad, even at another's good.
- **Kind of healing.** Birur: seeing each gift at its source. Ahavas yisrael in deed: blessing and helping the one envied, mouth and hands first. Simcha in one's own portion and task (Hayom Yom) [stated]. Gentle bittul of comparing; never 'you are nothing'.

## Stage 1 · Close the comparison gate

- **Aim.** Fewer triggers; each sting named without a fight.
- **Practice.** 1) For a week, cut what feeds it: mute the accounts that sting; no feed at the hours you are weakest (often tired, late, in bed) [ours]. Reason: 'seeing brings to remembering, and to craving' (Tzava'at HaRivash 6) [stated]. 2) When a sting comes anyway, name it, 'comparing', and do not argue with it (Tanya 28's method, as in P2-02) [stated there]. 3) Notice the body moments: the sting hits hardest tired, hungry, scrolling in bed. Eat, sleep, put the phone down [ours]: the vessel that is cared for has less room for the loop.
- **Signs of progress.** Fewer triggers in a day. The sting is named, and passes faster.
- **If stuck.** If it is about one person he sees daily (a sibling, a coworker): go straight to stages 2-3 for that person.
- **Move on when.** The gate is narrower and the stings are named.
- **Sources.** Tzava'at HaRivash 6: «ראי' מביאה לידי זכירה ולידי תאוה»

> **The guide says:** Comparison gets in through the eyes, so let's start there. For one week, cut down what feeds it: mute the accounts that sting, and stay off the feed at the hours you're weakest. Seeing wakes the memory, and the memory wakes the wanting. When a sting comes anyway, just name it, 'comparing', without a fight. Notice when it hits hardest: tired, hungry, scrolling in bed. Those are body moments. Eat, sleep, put the phone down, and the comparing gets quieter. You're not trying to stop feeling anything. You're just not feeding it extra.

## Stage 2 · Who gave it?

- **Aim.** Each gift, his and mine, is seen at its source. Nothing given to him was taken from me.
- **Practice.** Morning or evening, 10 minutes. 1) Picture the person envied and the thing they have, in detail. 2) Say slowly: 'This was given to him. The same One gave me a mind, a life, this breath right now' (Tzava'at HaRivash 12, 127) [stated]. 3) Contemplate: his gift is being given him moment by moment, and so is all of mine; each is a separate word, said now (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1) [stated]. 4) Mashal: souls 'are all matched, and there is one Father for all of them' (Tanya 32) [stated]; a father's gift to one child is not love taken from another [ours: the extension]. Where it breaks: a human father has limited means; He does not. And from below, each is truly separate (Iggeret HaKodesh 20) [stated]: your road is your own, and his is not a measure of yours.
- **Signs of progress.** He can say 'it was given to him' without bitterness, sometimes.
- **If stuck.** If it stays theory: make it concrete, one gift at a time, said aloud.
- **Move on when.** The line is available at a sting.
- **Sources.** Tzava'at HaRivash 12: «והשי"ת נתן לחבירו שכל כמו שנתן לו שכל»; Tzava'at HaRivash 127: «כי כן הדיבור ג"כ אצל חבירי כמו אצלי הכל מאתו ית'»; Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1: «כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 32: «שכולן מתאימות, ואב אחד לכולנה»; Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 20: «אבל בידיעה שממטה למעלה – היש הנברא הוא דבר נפרד לגמרי»

> **The guide says:** Now ten minutes, morning or evening. Picture the person you envy and the thing they have, in detail. Then say slowly: 'This was given to him. The same One gave me a mind, a life, this breath right now.' Everything he has is being given to him moment by moment, and so is everything you have. Nothing he got was taken from your account. Think of one father with many children: a gift to one doesn't mean less love for another. Here's where that picture breaks: a human father has limits, and G-d doesn't. And your road is truly your own. His isn't the measure of yours.

## Stage 3 · Bless him, by name, and act

- **Aim.** Mouth and hands go first; the rivalry loosens when one is on the other's side in deed.
- **Practice.** 1) Daily, ask G-d for good for the person you envy, by name, in your own words: success, health, joy [ours, drawing on S86 'grows: one act']. You do not have to feel it; say it. 2) Once this week, one real act: sincere congratulations, help, speaking well of him to someone. Do it in person where possible: shake his hand, show up [ours]. 3) Reason: the Tanya builds love of the other on the soul's root, 'one Father' (ch. 32) [stated]; the Alter Rebbe's morning acceptance 'love your fellow as yourself' (Siddur) [stated] can be said before this.
- **Signs of progress.** The blessing gets said. Some gladness at his good news, even small.
- **If stuck.** If it is someone who harmed him: this stage is not required; do stages 2 and 4 only.
- **Move on when.** A blessing is part of the day and one act has been done.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 32: «שכולן מתאימות, ואב אחד לכולנה»; Siddur, before morning prayer: «הריני מקבל עלי מצות עשה של ואהבת לרעך כמוך»

> **The guide says:** Here's the hardest step, and the one that works best. Each day, ask G-d for something good for the person you envy, by name, in your own words: give him success, health, joy. You don't have to feel it. Just say it. Then, once this week, do one real act for him: congratulate him sincerely, help him, speak well of him to someone. Do it in person if you can, a handshake, showing up. Something changes when your mouth and hands go first. The rivalry loosens when you're on his side in action. If it's someone who actually hurt you, skip this step and tell me.

## Stage 4 · Your own place

- **Aim.** Attention moves from his portion to my task.
- **Practice.** 1) 'Do not judge your fellow until you reach his place' (Tanya 30) [stated]: you will never stand in his place, so you do not know what his life costs; and he cannot stand in yours. 2) 'Every soul has its own particular work, in mind and in feelings, according to its nature' (Hayom Yom) [stated]. 3) Tonight: write three things given to you today, small and specific, and one thing only you could have done today. Do one of those tomorrow. 4) Over time the question changes from 'why does he have that?' to 'what is mine to do?'
- **Signs of progress.** Comparison loops are shorter. More attention on his own work.
- **If stuck.** If the list feels thin: include the body's gifts too (woke up, walked, ate) [ours].
- **Move on when.** This is the ongoing shape.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 30: «״אל תדין את חבירך – עד שתגיע למקומו״»; Hayom Yom 8 Nissan: «כל נשמה יש לה עבודה פרטית במוחין ומדות כפי טבעה וענינה»

> **The guide says:** Last, your own place. You can't judge someone until you've stood where they stand, and you never will, so you don't know what their life really costs. And they can't stand in yours. Every soul has its own particular job, matched to its nature. Tonight, write down three things given to you today, small and specific, and one thing only you could have done today. Then do one of those tomorrow. Over time the question changes from 'why does he have that?' to 'what is mine to do?' That second question has an answer every single day.

**Daily shape.** Morning: 'love your fellow' line; 10 minutes 'who gave it?'. During the day: feed muted; stings named; a blessing said for the person by name. Evening: three given things and one task; phone out of bed.

**Duration.** 2-4 weeks for the sting to soften; the trait is long work. Honest: some comparison is human; the aim is that it stops running the day.

- If the comparison is about a real injustice (unfair pay, discrimination), practical action is right; this is not a counsel to accept it. - Never 'you are nothing' or 'be humble' to someone already crushed (S06 blocks). - If envy comes with low mood or self-hatred, use P2-03 first; a doctor if persistent. - Not for blessing or helping someone who is harming him (D04).

## P2-08 · Pride: needing to be seen

*Presenting:* "I need people to notice what I do. If nobody says anything, I feel empty. And tiny slights sting for days." *Deepens engine conditions:* C37, C08, C07

## Formulation

- **Presenting.** Performing; keeping score; stung by small slights; tired from keeping up an image; empty without an audience.
- **Where in the soul.** The animal soul: pride is from the element of fire, which rises (Tanya 1) [stated]. Da'as of self is mistaken about where being comes from. Middos: love of honor. Garments: speech (talking about oneself), deed done to be seen. Level: nefesh and ruach.
- **State now.** S06 Self-enclosed.
- **Root.** Yeshus in its plainest form: 'to be a something, a thing in itself' (Tanya 19) [stated]; the voice that lifts itself like an eagle, saying 'I, and nothing besides me' (Tanya 22) [stated]. Being seen is how the self holds itself up; when no one sees, the support fails and it feels empty. The Piaseczner: 'a person's selfhood is what stands against inspiration from above' (Derekh HaMelekh) [stated].
- **What is blocked.** Da'as of the source of one's gifts. The person is not empty of good; he is full of himself, so there is no room to receive (MR1-C24's vessel) [stated].
- **State needed.** S56 The self quieted (bittul that is made, with joy); then S59 True lowliness: a sharp word stings less 'because he has nothing of his own' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut) [stated]; far off, S85's evenness under praise and insult (Tzava'at HaRivash 2) [stated].
- **Kind of healing.** Bittul hayesh, made, not felt first (S56), with simcha; the hidden kindness; lowliness before every person (Tanya 30) [stated]. Never shame and never 'you are nothing' (S06 blocks). The Mitteler Rebbe's nishkeit is 'another lowliness that does not come from his low worth or from bitter self-accounting' but from taking up no room in himself (Toras Chaim) [stated]: that is the aim, not self-hatred. A little healthy pride stays (MR1-C24) [stated].

## Stage 1 · Notice the stage; do one hidden good

- **Aim.** The performing becomes visible; the self tastes doing good without an audience.
- **Practice.** 1) For one week, count two kinds of moments: when you are performing (saying or doing something mainly to be seen), and when a small slight stings. No judging; just a tally [ours]. 2) One good deed a day that no one will ever know about: wash someone's dishes, give anonymously, fix something quietly. With the hands, in the body, told to no one (S06 'grows: a hidden kindness') [stated in the map]. 3) The Baal Shem Tov's will: 'when he sees that his service is greater than his fellow's, let him not grow proud' (Tzava'at HaRivash 48) [stated]. 4) Reason [ours]: the hidden deed trains the self to stand without its usual support, an audience. It may feel like nothing happened; that is the point.
- **Signs of progress.** The tally is honest. The hidden deeds happen. A moment of quiet satisfaction with no one watching.
- **If stuck.** If he tells people about the hidden deed: count it as a performing moment, smile, and do another one tomorrow [ours].
- **Move on when.** A week of hidden deeds and an honest tally.
- **Sources.** Tzava'at HaRivash 48: «כשהוא רואה שעבודתו גדולה מחבירו לא יתגאה»

> **The guide says:** Let's start by watching, not fixing. For one week, notice two kinds of moments: when you're performing, saying or doing something mainly to be seen, and when a small slight stings. Just count them, no judging. And each day, do one good thing that no one will ever know about: wash someone's dishes, give anonymously, fix something quietly. Do it with your hands, and tell no one. That hidden deed trains you to stand without an audience. It may feel empty at first, like nothing happened. That's the point. Notice what it's like to be good without being seen.

## Stage 2 · Where do my gifts come from?

- **Aim.** Gifts are seen as given, now; the self stops holding itself up.
- **Practice.** Morning, 10 minutes. 1) Take one thing you are proud of: a talent, your words, your work. 2) Ask: where did it come from? Your mind, your strength, this breath were given, and are being given right now (S65). 3) The Baal Shem Tov's school: 'when he speaks, let him not think that he is the speaker; the life in him is the Creator speaking in him' (Tzava'at HaRivash 127) [stated]. 4) Mashal [ours]: a lamp proud of its light. The light comes through it. Where it breaks: you are not a lamp; your effort and choices are real and count, and the Tanya treasures the in-between person's struggle (ch. 27) [stated]. You are not nothing; you just don't hold yourself up. 5) The sign it is working is the Mitteler Rebbe's: afterward, true lowliness; an insult does not move him 'because he has nothing of his own' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut) [stated]. 6) Before speaking about yourself today, pause one breath and ask: who is this for? [ours].
- **Signs of progress.** A sharp word stings less. Less need to mention his own part.
- **If stuck.** If this slides into 'so I'm worthless': stop; that is a different illness (P2-03). Lowliness is light; shame is heavy (S59) [stated in the map].
- **Move on when.** Some slights land lighter, and he notices.
- **Sources.** Tzava'at HaRivash 127: «וכן כשמדבר אל יחשוב שהוא המדבר אלא החיות שבו הוא הבורא ית' המדבר בו»; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe): «וסימנו שאחר כך יבוא לכלל שפלות אמיתית»; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe): «מפני שאין לו מצד עצמו כלום»

> **The guide says:** Now a morning practice, ten minutes. Take one thing you're proud of: your talent, your words, your work. Ask: where did it come from? Your mind, your energy, even this breath were given to you, and they're being given right now. One old teaching says: when you speak, don't think you're the speaker; the life in you is G-d speaking through you. Like a lamp proud of its light, when the light only comes through it. Where that picture breaks: your effort is real, and your choices count. You're not nothing. You just don't have to hold yourself up. You'll know it's working when a sharp word stings less.

## Stage 3 · Lowly before every person

- **Aim.** Making room for others, in the mind and with the body.
- **Practice.** 1) The Tanya: 'be lowly of spirit before every person: truly, before every person, even the lightest of the light' (ch. 30) [stated], on the ground of 'do not judge your fellow until you reach his place' (same) [stated]. 2) Today, with each person you deal with, find one real way they are above you: what they carry, tests they faced that you never had. Not pretend [ours]. 3) Let the body make room: turn your whole face to the one speaking, let them finish, hold the door, take the worse seat [ours]. 4) Not for anyone crushed or low; this is for someone used to taking all the space.
- **Signs of progress.** He listens longer. Others feel more room around him.
- **If stuck.** If it turns into silent judgment ('I'm humbler than they are'): that is the counterfeit (S06) [stated in the map]; go back to stage 1's tally.
- **Move on when.** Finding one way the other is above him comes easily most days.
- **Sources.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 30: «״והוי שפל רוח בפני כל האדם״. והוי – באמת לאמיתו, ״בפני כל האדם״ – ממש, אפילו בפני קל שבקלים»; Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 30: «״אל תדין את חבירך – עד שתגיע למקומו״»

> **The guide says:** Here's a practice for your day. With each person you deal with, find one real way they're above you. Not pretend. Maybe they carry something you couldn't. Maybe they've faced tests you never had. You can't judge anyone until you've stood in their place, and you never have. Let your body help: turn your whole face to the person talking, let them finish, hold the door, take the worse seat. Small physical ways of making room. This isn't putting yourself down, and it isn't for someone who already feels crushed. It's for someone used to taking up all the space.

## Stage 4 · Praise and insult land the same

- **Aim.** Worth no longer rides on others' words. Gladness in serving without credit.
- **Practice.** 1) The far sign: 'it is all the same to him whether people praise him or insult him' (Tzava'at HaRivash 2) [stated]. Not because he stops caring about people, but because his being does not depend on their words. 2) Evening look-back, 3 minutes: did a comment sting less today? Did I do something good and not need it noticed? ## Where this departs from earlier files, and open questions

- **Body techniques.** `PSYCHOLOGY.md` rule 20 says no breathing drills, scans or postures. - **Non-Jewish users.** Several anchors are Jewish practice (the Amidah's 'forgives abundantly', the bedtime Shema, Kaddish). The guide offers the plain-English line ('an old line said before sleep') to anyone; the halachic practices are pointed to a rabbi for Jews only.
- **Not yet sourced:** the custom that deeds in the name of the departed benefit the soul, and the mourner's first meal, are widely held and marked as custom; we did not quote a source for them.
