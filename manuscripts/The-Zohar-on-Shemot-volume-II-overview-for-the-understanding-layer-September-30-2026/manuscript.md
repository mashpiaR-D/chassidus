## 1. Short answer

Volume II of the Zohar teaches that G-d is one with no second, that everything we call many (names, lights, worlds, even judgment) is many "only from our side," and that the way to know this is through prayer: a Shema that gathers everything into One, a whisper that keeps Him near, tears that open locked gates, and a soul given over into His hands. It is less a book of ideas than a book of nearness. Its greatest gifts to the guide are a small set of states (He goes down with me; the cry without words; tears through the locked gates; given over; held and not held) and a set of prayer practices that Chabad later built on almost word for word.

## 2. The core unity teaching

1. **All is He.** "כלא קודשא בריך הוא. כלא הוא. כלא חד" (1731): "All is the Holy One. All is He. All is one."
2. **Many only from our side.** After describing the lights nursing one from another "until all is known to be one, and all hang from One, and all is found one" (3409: "עד דאשתמודע כלא דהוא חד, ומחד תליין כלהו, וכלא אשתכח חד"), the Zohar says: "כולי האי לא אתקרי אלא מסטרא דילן, ומסטרא דילן אשתמודע כלא" (3411), "all this is named only from our side, and from our side all is known." Above, "לא שני, ולא ישתני" (3411), "it does not change and will not change." This is the shortest Zohar text for what Chabad calls the two unities.
3. **Beyond will and light.** Ein Sof "does not stand to be known" (4610), and "לאו רעותין, לאו נהורין, לאו בוצינין, בההוא אין סוף" (4611), "there are no wills, no lights, no lamps in that Ein Sof." It is "אין," *Ayin*, Nothing (4611). The mind reaching there catches only a scent (4612).
4. **One without separation.** The Shema is said "לאשגחא דכלא איהו חד, בלא פרודא" (2738), "to see that all is one, without separation," and the Tabernacle is built "לאחזאה דכלא חד בלא פרודא" (2756).
5. **The world is hiding.** *Olam* (world) is read as *he'elem* (hiding): the world "is revealed in hiding" (5004). What does not shine shone in the hidden point (2499). Light is known best as "the light that comes out of darkness" (3574: "דלית נהורא אלא ההוא דנפיק מגו חשוכא").
6. **One voice, no helper.** At Sinai seven voices and ten sayings came "מחדא מלתא" (5214), from one word, so no one would think angels helped Him.
7. **He is with us below.** "ובשעתא דיהוי לך ירידה, כביכול אנכי ארד עמך" (268), "when you go down, as it were, I go down with you." The Shekhinah in exile was never divorced (4185), and the King dwells in broken vessels (1750).
8. **The worlds hang on Him each moment.** Without His flow the world "could not stand even one moment" (2853).

## 3. States and meditations added

Twenty two new states (ZS2-01 to ZS2-22) and sixteen practices (ZT2-01 to ZT2-16) are proposed, with sources added to many existing map ids (S04, S13, S15, S19, S34, S44, S49, S53, S60, S68, S73, S76, S83, S84). The full list is in the JSON below. The strongest:

- **ZS2-20 Given over** (3866-3870, 3908): after asking, hand the soul to Him.

- **ZT2-13 Thought, will, voice, word** (5082-5084): the four steps by which prayer is born.
- **ZT2-16 Setting the hidden thought** (5399-5404): the first private thought, turned to a good deed, fills the worlds with blessing.

## 4. Where it agrees and differs with Chabad

- *The two unities.* "Only from our side... it does not change" (3411) is the higher and lower unity of *Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah* and of the Gate of Unity (`GATE-OF-UNITY.md`, F02), where change is real only "from our side." The Zohar gives the claim; Chabad gives the argument.
- *The Essence above will.* "No wills, no lights" in Ein Sof, and *Ayin* (4610-4611), is the root of the Chabad teaching that *Atzmut* (the Essence) is above even the Will.
- *Olam from he'elem* (5004), *light from darkness* (3574), *lengthening "Echad"* (4181), and *mesirat nefesh at nefilat apayim* (3867, 3908) are all Zohar sources that the Tanya and the Chabad prayer order take up directly.
- *The Shekhinah in exile with us* (4185, 1750) is Tanya's "the Shekhinah in exile" and "a broken heart is a vessel."
- *Joy.* "A person's joy draws to him another, higher joy" (3582) and "serve G-d with joy" (5445) match Tanya's long war on sadness.

- *Tzimtzum.* Volume II has no worked doctrine of tzimtzum. Its language of hiding (5004), the hidden point (2499) and "only from our side" (3411) leans the same way as Chabad's reading that the contraction is not literal, but the Zohar never argues it. The Gate of Unity supplies what the Zohar leaves open (ours).
- *Sadness.* The Zohar is warmer to sadness brought to Him than the Tanya's warnings would suggest: "when the King comes to the Queen and finds her sad, whatever she asks is given" (3206). Chabad's line between *atzvut* (dull sadness) and *merirut* (bitterness that moves) is the bridge (ours).
- *Awakening from below and stillness.* The Zohar insists everything needs an awakening from below, and also says at the sea "be still, you do not need to" (923). Chabad stresses the first; the guide needs both (ours).
- *Nations and the other side.* Many passages are exclusivist or dwell on impurity and sorcery. Chabad softens some; the guide sets them aside (D28). We keep the Zohar's own universal lines, such as the pious of the nations (5183) and "one righteous person holds up the world" (5226).
- *Letter and name mechanics.* The Zohar uses 42 and 72 letter names and seeing letters at dawn (2565). Chabad keeps these to the few; the guide keeps them out (technique 25).

## 5. What the guide should take (ours)

1. Open the unity teaching with 1731 ("All is the Holy One. All is He. All is one.") and explain it with 3411 ("only from our side... it does not change").
2. For a person who cannot pray: the cry without words (356), the gate of tears (3205-3206), and the deed with a prayer for the heart (1898).
3. For a person who feels abandoned: "I go down with you" (268), the Shekhinah never divorced (4185), G-d sitting at the door to guard (683).
4. For the Shema: the closed-eye Shema (843-844) and Rav Hamnuna Sava's lengthened "One" (4173-4182).
5. For the start of prayer: thought, will, voice, word (5082-5084); ask first to know Him (5062).
6. For the end of prayer: given over (3867, 3908).
7. For the day: the daily asking (1233-1238), setting the face to light (3581-3582), the hidden thought (5399-5400), the evening four questions (3838).
8. For anger: anger as the test of who you are (3542) and the child who stills anger (5446).
9. Leave out: physiognomy, star omens, sorcery, name and letter mechanics, and passages against other peoples.
