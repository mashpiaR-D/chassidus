## The short answer

The Kuzari is a talk between the king of the Khazars and a Jewish sage, the Chaver. The king keeps dreaming that an angel tells him: "your intention is pleasing to the Creator, but your deed is not" (line 1). The whole book answers that dream. Its teaching on the unity of G-d has four parts.

1. **He is known first by meeting, not by proof.** The Chaver begins with history, not philosophy: "We believe in the G-d of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, who brought Israel out of Egypt" (line 17). A proof leaves doubts; what was seen and passed down "is the proof itself" (line 21).
2. **The G-d of Abraham is not the G-d of Aristotle.** Reason reaches "Elokim", a ruler and orderer of the world. Only prophetic seeing and "taste" reach the Name. The taste makes a person ready to die for His love; the proof holds only while it costs nothing (line 294).
3. **There is a divine level in man above the mind.** Above nature, soul and intellect stands the inyan haElokı (the divine matter), which differs from mind as mind differs from beast (lines 45-48). The world exists so that it can rest on a ready vessel (line 167).
4. **Only the deed He gave brings near, and it is done in joy.** "A person reaches the divine matter only by a divine thing, that is, by deeds that G-d commands" (line 104). And the pious man's service is "in joy, not in humbling" (line 209).

> אנחנו מאמינים באלהי אברהם יצחק ויעקב המוציא את בני ישראל ממצרים (Kuzari 1:17; line 17)

> כבר התבאר לי ההפרש בין אלהים ויי והבינותי מה בין אלהי אברהם ואלהי אריסטו, ושיי תכספנה לו הנפשות בטעם וראיה, ואלהים יטה אליו הקשה (Kuzari 4:16; line 294)

*Ours.* The Kuzari is the Rishon who speaks most directly to an experiential guide. It says there are two ways to hold the same One: by proof, which is true but cold, and by taste, which is sweet and makes a person give himself. The guide is on the side of taste. But the Kuzari's taste is never a private mood. It comes through a chain of those who saw, and through deeds whose measure only G-d knows.

## His core teaching on the unity

**The G-d of Abraham against the G-d of Aristotle.** The philosopher in the opening says G-d has no will, "for intention shows a lack in the one who intends", and does not know particulars: "He does not know you" (line 2). Once you have joined the active intellect, "do not care which Torah you follow" (line 2). The Chaver answers from history. You know a king by his gifts and healing that reached you, not by a report that he is just (the king of India, lines 25-31). So the Ten Commandments begin "who took you out of Egypt", not "who created the world" (line 31).

Part 4 gives the book's famous line. "Elokim" is a title of rule, grasped by reasoning. The Name is a proper name, grasped only by prophetic seeing (line 293). Then:

> והטעם ההוא יביא מי שיגיע אליו שימסר נפשו על אהבתו ושימות עליה, וההקשה הזאת היא רואה כי רוממותו חובה בעוד שלא תזיק ולא יגיע בעבורה צער (Kuzari 4:16; line 294)
> That taste brings whoever reaches it to give his soul for His love and die for it. This reasoning sees that exalting Him is a duty only as long as it does no harm and brings no pain.

Abraham bore exile, circumcision and the binding of Isaac "because he saw of the divine matter what he saw, taste, not reasoning" (line 295). "Taste and see that the Lord is good."

**The Names say what He does, or what He is not.** "The divine Essence is above joining and parting. We say 'One' to deny plurality" (line 125). He is "the essence of mind, and He is the mind, and mind is not a trait of His" (line 125). One sun, many sparks: "the traits are many and the Essence is one, because the dwelling that receives differs" (line 281).

**The sun of the Names.** One sun shines on all. On clear stones its light is "piercing"; on wood and earth it is only "seen" (line 293). Plain light is Elokim; the piercing light passing through the pure souls of the prophets is the Name.

**Creation is not craft.** A craftsman makes a mill and walks away. "The Creator creates the limbs, gives them powers, and draws them on moment by moment; and if one could imagine His care withdrawn for one moment, the whole world would be undone" (line 215). The king adds the blessing "who renews in His goodness every day always the work of creation", and the Chaver says: "Well said ... this is the truth" (lines 317-318).

**One in the many.** From Sefer Yetzirah: counting, speech and writing "are one thing in G-d, and three in man" (line 303). His word is the thing itself. He is "the place of the world, and His world is not His place" (line 316).

**The veil of bright light.** When reason reaches His Essence it meets "a curtain of bright light that defeats the sight", hidden not because it is dark but because our eyes are weak (line 343). "If we grasped His truth, that would be a lack in Him."

**Why a world.** "All of it came to be for that treasured one, so that the divine matter could cling to it" (line 167): elements for minerals, up to man, up to the prophets and the pious. And the Torah is "the beginning of a will of His to show His kingship on earth as it is shown in heaven. Wisdom did not decree that He create angels on earth, but man, of seed and blood" (line 221).

## The inyan haElokı (the divine matter)

The Kuzari's most original idea is a level of the person above the mind. Plants have nature, animals add soul, man adds intellect. Above that is a level that differs in kind:

> אבל המעלה הזאת אלהית מלאכותית אם היא נמצאת, וזה מדין הענין האלהי לא מן השכלי ולא מן הנפשי ולא מן הטבעי (Kuzari 1:48; line 48)
> This level is divine, angelic, if it exists. It belongs to the divine matter, not to mind, not to soul, not to nature.

Adam had it: "the divine power after the mind, by which he clings to G-d and knows truths without learning" (line 101). It is not earned by thought. It rests on a vessel that is ready:

> והענין האלהי כמו צופה למי שראוי להדבק בו שיהיה לו לאלהים (Kuzari 2:14; line 137)
> And the divine matter is as if it looks out for whoever is fit to cling to it, to be his G-d.

It comes "only according to his readiness for it, if little then little, if much then much" (line 147). It "does good and wants good for all", and does not hold back its light from what is ready (line 149). The readiness is made by the deeds G-d measured: "One comes near to G-d only by G-d's own commands, for He knows their measure and weight and time and place" (line 227). Human inventions to draw the divine are like a fool giving out medicines in a doctor's pharmacy (line 85).

*Ours.* This is very close to what the Tanya calls the G-dly soul, a part of G-d above, and its point above intellect (Tanya ch. 2, 18-19). The Kuzari gives it to a chosen line; Chabad gives it to every Jew as a hidden love that needs only to be woken. And both hold that the deed He gave, not a technique of the mind, is what makes the vessel.

## Prophecy

**The inner eye.** "He gave to those of His creatures He chose a hidden eye that sees things as they are" (line 281). One who has it "sees all people as blind". The forms the prophets saw are true "in relation to what imagination and sense seek, and not true in relation to the Essence" (line 281). The proof that the sights are real: the prophets agree, as all agree honey is sweet.

**What the seeing leaves.** "Awe and love enter, fixed in his soul all the days of his life, and all his days he goes on longing ... asking that He show Himself a second or a third time" (line 283).

**For us: lean on the seers, and keep the open hours.** "We are like people with weak eyes ... and we must lean on the seeing ones who came before us" (line 285). The one who sees by the divine light "has times and places when he sees that light. The times are the times of prayer, and more so in the days of return" (line 285).

**Meeting a prophet.** "Anyone who meets a prophet ... a new spirituality is born in him" (line 109). The promise is nearness in this life, "that we cling to the divine matter by prophecy and what is near it" (line 115), and one who has it "will not fear death".

## The pious man's day

Part 3 is the book's practical heart. The pious man is not a hermit; withdrawal today "puts the soul into suffering and sickness" (line 205). He is a ruler of his city:

> החסיד הוא מי שהוא מושל, נשמע בחושיו וכחותיו הנפשיים והגופיים (Kuzari 3:5; line 209)
> The pious man is a ruler, obeyed by his senses and his powers of soul and body.

He first gives each power its due, then gathers them "to cling to the level above it, the divine level" (line 209). His prayer is a full meditation (line 209): clear the mind of worldly thought; let imagination bring up "the standing at Mount Sinai, and Abraham and Isaac on Mount Moriah, and the Tabernacle of Moses and the glory resting in the Temple"; let memory hold it; let the body stand "as a servant looks at his master"; let every word carry its thought, "not like the starling and the parrot". Then:

> ותהיה העת ההיא לב זמנו ופריו, ויהיו שאר עתותיו כדרכים המגיעים אל העת ההיא (Kuzari 3:5; line 209)
> That hour will be the heart of his time and its fruit, and his other hours like roads that lead to that hour.

Prayer is to the soul what food is to the body, and "the blessing of the prayer stays with him until the next prayer" (line 209). Shabbat heals what the week gathered; the festivals and Yom Kippur carry him further (line 209). All day he feels that "with him are eyes that see", and he walks in awe and in joy (line 215). He feels continuous creation in every limb, "as if the Presence is with him" (line 215). He blesses, because "readiness for pleasure, feeling it, and thinking of its absence before, doubles the pleasure" (line 221). When trouble comes he says "this too is for the good, and he will live a sweet life always" (line 215).

The balance of the Torah is the key (Part 2):

> וכללו של דבר, כי תורתנו נחלקת בין היראה והאהבה והשמחה, תתקרב אל אלהיך בכל אחת מהנה (Kuzari 2:50; line 173)
> In short, our Torah is shared among awe, love and joy. You may draw near to your G-d by each one of them.

"Your humbling on fast days is no nearer to G-d than your joy on Shabbat and festivals, when your joy is with intention and a whole heart" (line 173). And if the joy moves into song and dance, "it is service and clinging to the divine matter" (line 173).

**Meditations.** The guest at His table (line 173); the ruler's gathering and scene-picturing before prayer (line 209); the Presence before you at the bow (line 223); study His wisdom where you can see it, lean on it where you cannot (lines 215, 247); "stop your mouth, stop your heart; if your heart runs, return" (line 318); "before One, what do you count?" ## Where he agrees with Chabad, and where he differs

- **Continuous creation.** Line 215 (the craftsman and the mill) is the argument of Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 2, and line 317 is its prayer-book source.
- **Speech is the thing.** "His word is the thing itself" (line 303) is the Sefer Yetzirah root of the Chabad teaching that the letters of speech give being.
- **Faith above reason.** Faith is higher than proof, and the simple believer who received is above the one who reasons (line 149; Tanya ch. 18, the faith above understanding).
- **A level above the mind.** The divine matter above intellect is close to the G-dly soul and its hidden love (Tanya ch. 18-19).
- **Man, not angel.** The will to show His kingship "in man, of seed and blood" (line 221) is the seed of the dwelling below (Tanya ch. 36).
- **The deed is essential.** The heart wants a deed, and the deed wants a heart (line 349). Chabad: the mitzvah is His inner will.
- **Joy is service.** Joy is service; sadness and asceticism are not the way (Tanya ch. 26, 31).
- **Running and returning.** Ratzo vashov from Sefer Yetzirah (line 318) is a Chabad term.
- **"Know today and return it to your heart."** Knowing must become feeling (compare the Kuzari's taste with the Tanya's da'at, ch. 3 and 42).

- **Proof and taste.** The Kuzari sets reason and taste against each other. Chabad joins them. Hitbonenut (contemplation) takes the proven truth and, through da'at, makes it felt. For Chabad, the G-d of Aristotle, understood deeply, becomes a door to the G-d of Abraham.
- **Who holds the divine matter.** For the Kuzari it passes through a chosen line and reaches its height in the prophets, and some are born with it. For Chabad every Jew has a G-dly soul whole, and the aim is to reveal it in everyone.
- **Providence.** The Kuzari keeps a real middle world of natural and chance causes, so that choice stays real (line 342). The Gate of Unity sees every cause as His speech at every moment. On this point Chassidus goes further than the Kuzari.
- **The Names.** For the Kuzari, Elokim is G-d reached through nature and the Name is G-d met in revelation. For Chabad, Elokim is the power that hides and limits, the Name the light that gives being, and "the Name and Elokim are one" (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 6).
- **Picture or idea.** The Kuzari's prayer uses scenes held in imagination. Chabad's contemplation works mainly through ideas understood in depth. The guide can offer both.

## What the guide should take (ours)

1. **Start from meeting, not proof.** "Let them seek no greater proof than My being with them" (line 281). The guide's first move is to notice where He is already present.
2. **Taste and see.** Name the difference between knowing G-d is and tasting that He is good, and aim at the taste, always through deeds and the chain of those who saw.
3. **The guest at His table** for Shabbat, meals and mitzvot: joy with intention.
4. **The day with a heart.** Make the prayer the heart of the day, and treat the fading of its light as expected, renewed at the next prayer.
5. **The ruler's gathering** as a full pre-prayer meditation: feed each power, clear the thought, picture a scene of revelation, let the body stand, let each word carry its thought.
6. **Awake to the gift.** Bless in order to feel: think of the lack, then the gift.
7. **"This too is for the good"** as the sweet life, not as a slogan to silence pain.
8. **Guards.** - No long withdrawal or self-made vows (line 205). - No experience makes the deed unneeded; that was Acher's fall (line 269). - Joy must not become lightness, and awe must not become despair (line 215). - Trust is not a license for danger (line 342). - Human techniques are servants, not ways to force a result from above (line 85).
