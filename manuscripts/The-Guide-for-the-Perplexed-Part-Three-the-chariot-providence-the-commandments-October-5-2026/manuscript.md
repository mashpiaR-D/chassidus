## The shape of the range

Part Three has five subjects. It climbs from the most hidden (the chariot) to the most practical (how to spend your waking hours).

1. **The chariot (III:1-7).** A careful, half-veiled reading of Ezekiel's vision. The living creatures "run and return" like lightning, moved by the divine intention. All that is pictured is the *kavod*, the glory, "the chariot, not the Rider".
2. **Matter, evil and the purpose of the world (III:8-15).** Matter hides. Evil is an absence, not a thing. Most evil a person meets he makes himself. Not everything exists for man.
3. **Providence and knowledge (III:16-24).** Five views of providence and his own: individual providence follows the intellect. G-d's knowing is not like ours. The book of Job and the trials of Abraham.
4. **Reasons for the commandments (III:25-50).** Every commandment has a reason. The Torah aims at the welfare of body and soul. Fourteen classes of commandments, matching the fourteen books of the Mishneh Torah. Most rituals uproot idolatry; many train character.
5. **The end of the book (III:51-54).** The palace parable; the worship of the one who has understood; the training of attention from the first verse of Shema to the whole day; standing always before the King; the four perfections; chesed, tzedakah and mishpat, "kindness, right-giving and judgment", as the way to be like G-d.

## 1. The unity of G-d in this range

**His knowing is His self (III:20-21).** This is the chapter the Tanya takes. "His self is His knowledge and His knowledge is His self", and so "there is nothing shared between our knowing and His, as there is nothing shared between our self and His self" (line 1123):

> ואין שיתוף בין ידיעתנו וידיעתו כמו שאין שיתוף בין עצמנו ועצמו (line 1123)

In III:21 the Rambam gives the image of the maker and the onlooker. The one who built a water clock knows it from the inside; one who watches it learns from it. G-d is the maker: "by knowing the truth of His own self, which does not change, He knows all that follows from His acts" (line 1128):

> שהוא בדעתו אמיתת עצמו אשר לא תשתנה ידע כל מה שיתחיב לפעולותיו כולם (line 1128)

This is the source, in the Guide, of the line the Tanya quotes as the Rambam's: "by knowing Himself He knows all created things" (Tanya ch. 42, line 614 of the Tanya file, which adds that the Ramak in Pardes agrees; also ch. 2 and 4, the gloss to ch. 48, and Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah 7). The Mishneh Torah form of it (Yesodei HaTorah 2:10) is read in `mishneh-torah-madda.md`.

**Only light, and our own cloud.** Of "cloud and thick darkness around Him": the cloud is our matter, "to point out that the barrier is our own dullness", "for there is no darkness with Him, only the great, strong, constant light" (line 1055):

> כי אין חושך איתו אלא האור הגדול (line 1055)

The same idea closes the book: the hiding of the face is something "we cause, and we make this screen that separates us from Him" (line 1442).

**Only good comes from Him.** Evil is absence. G-d "does only existence, and all existence is good" (line 1058):

> שהוא אינו עושה רק מציאות וכל מציאות טוב (line 1058)

**The world is not for man.** Against the view that all exists for us, "the rest of the beings are also intended for themselves" (line 1078). The person who knows his small place "rests, and his thoughts are not confused" (line 1082). The world as a whole is *chesed*: "the building of the world is kindness" (line 1451).

**Abraham's unity.** Abraham saw the sun as "an axe in the hand of the one who chops with it" (line 1184), and the aim of his whole life, and the Patriarchs' after him, was "to make the unity of the Name known in the world" (line 1439).

## 2. States of consciousness in this range

- **Knowing that holds, even in pain (Job).** Job, while he knew G-d only by report, was shaken. When he knew Him truly he said "I had heard of You by the ear, and now my eye has seen You" (line 1139). The true good, knowing G-d, "is ready for whoever knows Him, and none of these troubles can confuse it". The point of Job: "when a person knows this, every event is light to him, and events do not add doubts about G-d... but add love" (line 1146). He ties it to the Sages' "those who act from love and are glad in suffering" (line 1146). *Ties to:* S27 (Knowing that holds), S84 (Trust), S49 (Weeping and joy in one heart).

> וכשידע האדם זה יקל עליו כל מקרה (line 1146)

- **The limit of love (the Akedah).** The binding of Isaac shows "the limit of the love of G-d and the fear of Him, how far it reaches" (line 1155), done not in a rush of feeling but after three days of thought. *Ties to:* S60 (The essential bond), S61 (Will and delight above reason). The Rambam's reading is striking: the highest self-giving is *thought through*, not a flash.
- **The busy knower: the cloudy day.** One who has understood and then turns to business is not in the dark. He is like "a quick scribe when he is not writing" (line 1440); he is like "one on a cloudy day", the sun there but hidden by the cloud, while the one who never knew is in the dark and the one whose mind is on what he knows stands in bright sun. *Ties to:* S26 (It goes flat), C5 (Running and returning), C7 (From darkness to light). *Proposed new state* **R-S5 "The cloudy day"**: the knowing is still there, held in "near potential", but covered by the day's work.
- **Heart awake while the body works.** The Patriarchs and Moses spoke with people, herded, farmed and ran households, "but did these deeds with their limbs only", while "their hearts and minds did not move from before G-d" (line 1439). The Rambam reads "I sleep but my heart is awake" as this state. He says it is Moses' level and the Fathers', and not one he can teach; the level below it can be reached by training. *Ties to:* S63 (The world held), S29 (Good thought joined to deed), S82 (The home below). *Proposed new state* **R-S6 "Heart awake while the hands work"**. Chassidus claims this as the goal for every Jew in daily work; the Rambam keeps it for the few.
- **Passionate love, chesek.** "The difference between one who loves and one who longs (chosek, is passionately in love): love so great that no thought is left for anything but the beloved is *chesek*" (line 1443). *Ties to:* S43 (Love like fire), S44 (Love as delight).
- **Death by a kiss.** For the perfect person, as the body weakens the mind grows clearer, and joy in what he grasps and longing grow "until the soul parts from the body in that pleasure" (line 1444). Moses, Aaron and Miriam died "by a kiss" (line 1445). *Ties to:* S47 (Running), and the Chabad teaching on klos hanefesh, the soul's expiry in longing.
- **Always before the King.** III:52: a person does not sit and speak alone at home as he does before a great king. The King "always joined to him and hovering over him" is the intellect that flows from G-d, "and as we grasp Him by that light, so by that very light He looks upon us" (line 1447). From this come awe, modesty and few words. *Ties to:* S33 (Before Whom), S46 (Higher awe).

> כן באור ההוא בעצמו הוא משקיף עלינו (line 1447)

- **Moved to turn.** The scapegoat is a symbol "to bring fear into the soul until it is moved to return" (line 1358). *Ties to:* S31 (Turned over), S77 (The lower return).
- **Awe dulled by habit.** "Any honored thing that a person sees constantly, something of it lessens in the soul" (line 1363). That is why the Temple was not open to all at all times. *Ties to:* S26 (It goes flat). A plain warning for any daily practice.
- **Music and fragrance.** The Levites' song was there because "souls are moved only by sounds and pleasant tunes" (line 1331); incense because "the soul widens greatly at a good smell" (line 1333). *Ties to:* technique `niggun`, S52.

## 3. Meditation and practice in this range

- **The ladder of attention (III:51, lines 1437-1438).** This is the Rambam's one step-by-step meditation program, and the most important practice text in the Guide. 1. When you say the Shema and pray, empty your mind of everything. At first it is enough to hold full intention in "the first verse" of Shema and "the first blessing" of prayer (line 1437). 2. When that is firm "for many years", give all your heart to understanding whatever you read or hear of Torah. 3. Then keep your mind free in all the rest of the prophets and all the blessings. 4. Then guard your mind during all the commandments, and give worldly matters their own times: meals, the bath, talk with your wife and small children, and with people. "These are many wide times I found for you." 5. Then, when you are alone, and "when you wake on your bed" (line 1438), think of nothing but the intellectual worship: to come near to G-d and stand before Him in the true way, "not in the way of imagined feelings".

*Ties to:* techniques `choose_line`, `arrive`, `carry`, `before_whom`, `hold`. *Proposed new technique* **R-T2 "The widening circle of attention"**: start with one verse held fully; widen only when that holds for a long time; give worldly thought its own set times; keep the alone and waking hours for Him. - **Worship after knowing (line 1433).** "This kind of worship should begin after the intellectual grasp." Remembering G-d often "without wisdom, following only imagination", is to be outside the palace and "not truly remembering G-d", because what one pictures is "a made-up thing". After grasping, "give yourself over to Him, try to come near, and strengthen the bond between you and Him, which is the intellect". "Love is in proportion to knowing", and after love comes "the service of the heart", which for him is "that a person put all his thought on the First Intelligible, and be alone with this as much as he can" (line 1433). *Ties to:* techniques `depth`, `hold`, `quieting`; C3. Note for the guide (ours): this is the Rambam's version of "knowing first, then love"; Chassidus agrees on the order and differs on what the knowing is (see RAMBAM.md). - **The bond is yours to strengthen or weaken (line 1434).** "This intellect that flowed to us from G-d is the bond between us and Him." You may strengthen it or let it thin until it breaks. When even the wisest turns to food or business, "the bond is cut at that time". "I have set G-d before me always" means: like my right hand, which a person does not forget for the blink of an eye (line 1434). *Ties to:* technique `carry`, S33. - **Deeds without mind (line 1435).** Praying with moving lips and face to the wall while thinking of buying and selling, reading Torah with the tongue while the heart is on building a house, doing a commandment "like one who digs a pit or chops wood": do not think you have reached the goal; you are "near in their mouth and far from their kidneys". *Ties to:* S16 (Knowing it, not feeling it), technique `lips`. - **Count the hours with Him (line 1446).** The closing instruction: "try with all your strength to increase the times you are with G-d or trying to reach Him, and lessen the times you are with other things". *Ties to:* technique `cheshbon`. - **Solitude, with a guard.** "Every pious person should often be alone and seclude himself" (line 1433). The Guide's own balance (II:39, line 947) warns against the hermit's excess. Guard (ours): the guide may use short set times alone, not withdrawal from people. - **Imitate His acts (III:54).** The last word of the book is not contemplation but conduct: knowing G-d's providence, a person should always aim "to do kindness, right-giving and judgment, to become like G-d's actions" (line 1469). *Ties to:* technique `act`, S81. - **Cry out in trouble.** The command to pray and cry out in distress strengthens the belief "that G-d grasps our affairs and can fix them", and not that it is chance (line 1242). *Ties to:* S51 (The sudden cry), S85. - **Count the days (line 1314).** The Omer is counted "like one who waits for the most faithful of his friends, who counts the days and even the hours". A lovely image for preparation. - **Remember hard times in good times (line 1264, line 1317).** Part of service is to remember your times of trouble when G-d has widened your life, to learn humility and thanks. *Ties to:* technique `cheshbon`, S53. - **The cure is the opposite extreme (line 1340, line 1355).** Sacrifices "heal the bad opinions, which are illnesses of the human soul, by their opposite at the other extreme"; a sin in a trait is atoned by its opposite. This is the Eight Chapters' method (see `eight-chapters.md`).

## 4. The big questions in this range

- **Why do the good suffer?** His answer (III:51, lines 1440-1442) is his most personal. Providence follows the mind that is bound to G-d. When the mind turns away, the person is "open to every evil that may meet him". Any evil that befalls a prophet or a pious person came "in that time of forgetting". Earlier (III:17, line 1106) he limits individual providence to humans: a leaf's fall or a spider catching a fly is "pure chance, as Aristotle thinks". Suffering that is not deserved is not "sufferings of love" in the Torah's sense (line 1105). The whole of Job says: G-d's providence is unlike ours "except in name" (line 1145), and the one who truly knows G-d is not shaken.
- **Does G-d know me?** Yes, as a maker knows his work, not as a watcher (lines 1127-1128), and His knowing does not remove my choice (line 1124).
- **What is a human being for?** The four perfections (III:54): possessions ("pure imagination", line 1461), the body, good character (which is "for others", line 1463), and the knowledge of G-d, which "is his alone" (line 1464) and gives lasting life. The last line then turns outward: know Him, and act like Him in the world (line 1469).
- **Why so many commandments?** To heal the soul of false beliefs (idolatry), to order society, and to train character; and all practical commandments aim at fear, while the Torah's beliefs aim at love (line 1449).
- **Change and repentance.** One cannot pass "from one extreme to the other suddenly" (line 1209). Belief in repentance matters because a person who thinks he can never repair his error will stay in it; with repentance he returns "more whole than before he sinned" (line 1243).
- **Hunger for more.** "The necessary things are few and have an end; the extra has no end" (line 1068). Most evils come from the self (lines 1066-1068).

## 5. Where Chassidus reads this range, and where it departs

- **Knower and known (III:20-21).** The Tanya takes the Rambam's knowing-self-knowing-all as its model of unity (chs. 2, 4, 42, the gloss to ch. 48, and Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah 7), and then limits it (SY 9, gloss): it holds only after the light is clothed in the vessels of Chochmah, Binah and Daat; above tzimtzum it does not apply. The Gate of Unity says outright "not as the Rambam's phrase". Details in RAMBAM.md.
- **Providence.** The Baal Shem Tov's individual providence over every leaf is the exact view the Rambam rejects at line 1106 (he gives it to the Ash'ariyya, line 1100). Chassidus here departs from him fully.
- **Hester panim.** For the Rambam the hiding of the face is our own screen (line 1442) and it means being left to chance. Chassidus agrees that sin makes the screen, but holds that the hiding itself is also His act and His presence (S68, The hiding seen as His).
- **The world for man.** The Rambam denies that the world exists for man (line 1078). Chassidus teaches that G-d desired a dwelling in the lowest world, made through the human being (S82). A real disagreement.
- **Running and returning.** "Ratzo vashov" (line 1025) is, for the Rambam, the motion of the creatures in Ezekiel's vision. Chassidus makes it the rhythm of the soul (C5). The words are the same; the use is new.
- **The service of the heart.** The Rambam: the service of the heart is putting all thought on the First Intelligible (line 1433). The Tanya (chs. 41-50) keeps prayer as the service of the heart and makes the heart itself the place where the understanding must arrive (S80). The Rambam's III:51 program is closer to the Chabad demand that meditation continue through the day (Kuntres HaAvodah, carry) than to its prayer-length contemplation.
- **The imagination.** The Rambam treats worship "by imagination" as not remembering G-d at all (line 1433). Chassidus uses picturing (the `picturing` and `mashal` techniques) as a tool, while agreeing that a picture is never Him.

## What this range adds

- The only step-by-step training in the Guide: R-T2 "The widening circle of attention" (lines 1437-1438). - Two states: R-S5 "The cloudy day" (line 1440) and R-S6 "Heart awake while the hands work" (line 1439). - The image of the light by which we see Him being the light by which He sees us (line 1447). - A clear warning that awe is dulled by habit (line 1363). - A plain account of the four perfections, with its turn to conduct at the very end (lines 1460-1469).

## Where the thread is thin

- III:1-7 (the chariot) is deliberately veiled; its inner content is mostly the one idea that we see His glory, not Him. - III:37-50 are long lists of reasons for particular laws (idolatrous customs of the Sabians, damages, foods, family law). They were read in full; most carry little on inner life beyond the lines noted. - His view that evil comes to the pious only when their mind wanders (line 1441) is hard, and the guide should not use it to tell a suffering person that they caused it (ours).
