## The short answer

This is not a book about the unity of God. It is a book about how a person at prayer comes to feel what the Tanya and its Gate of Unity teach. Its heading gives the verse it lives by: the service of the heart is to bind the soul to Godliness and refine the animal soul, "as it says, know today and return it to your heart" (lines 23-25). Its answer: **prayer has two aims, to bind the essence of the soul to the Essence of the Infinite, and to refine the animal soul until it too wants Godliness. Awe rests on a hidden feeling of the Essence and is higher; love must be earned, by contemplation that is particular and not general, and only love binds and refines.**

The person's side leads again, as in the Mitteler Rebbe's Kuntres HaHitpa'alut: knowing, refining, bitul, the soul. The faces of the worlds come in as the content of contemplation. The text says what to think about in prayer (the life in each creature, memale and sovev, the line of light, creation from nothing) but rarely develops them for their own sake.

## How the book builds it, chapter by chapter

**Chapter 1 (lines 26-64): two aims.** All the toil of prayer is to reach love. The first aim is a ladder by which nefesh, ruach and neshamah rise until the soul's essence is bound to its source, "as its source is hewn in the Essence of the Infinite" (line 27, OCR אוא"ם for אוא"ס). The nefesh serves by acknowledgment: "Modeh ani" gives one's whole existence to Godliness, and "this is very near to every Jew" (line 29). The higher unity, all before Him as nothing, is truly grasped only by lofty souls, but "every Jew must have this bitul" at least as acknowledgment (line 32). Ruach contemplates the life clothed in the world, and that, not some higher level, is "know today" (line 38). Through memale one reaches sovev and the union of the soul with the Essence (line 51). The second aim is refining the animal soul: the words and powers used all day rise and become "a crown for their Maker" (line 60).

**Chapter 2 (lines 65-99): the yoke as the gate.** Mostly practical, on guarding the eyes, ears, speech and thought, and marked as no tie. Its point for the unity is the first act: "to give oneself to Godliness" (line 94). That self-giving is "the gate to God" (line 96).

**Chapter 3 (lines 100-127): awe in the Essence, love in the lights.** Love comes from particular knowledge, so no one is born with it. Awe can be inborn, because it rests on "truly a feeling of the Essence of the Infinite, above particular levels" (line 107). So "awe in general is bitul, and above love" (line 107). Higher awe comes from contemplating that "the Infinite is found below as above" (line 121, OCR לסטה for למטה): the Infinite as it is, below, not only watching from above. But binding the soul, which is prayer's task, is done by love (lines 122-125).

**Chapter 4 (lines 128-160): love like water, love like fire.** Love like water comes from seeing "how the world lives from the Godly light" (line 129), as a man feels the life in his own body. The natural mind itself comes to see that the physical "is not worthy of the name of a thing by itself" (line 142). Love like fire comes from His greatness and from seeing one's own distance, and "the fire of holiness burns and consumes the fire of the other side" (line 149). The animal soul turns "from the opposite to the opposite" (line 145).

**Chapter 5 (lines 161-199): the natural will and the yechidah.** Love (ahavah) is from avah, to will. The soul's own will runs to the Essence because "it is truly united always with the Essence" (line 163). The yechidah is an essential bond, shown in self-sacrifice, and when it is revealed it turns the animal soul "from the opposite to the opposite" (line 180). But the hidden love by itself changes nothing: the thief at the tunnel calls on God while he steals (line 184).

**Chapter 6 (lines 200-250): only particular knowing is knowing.** General contemplation, even of "He fills and surrounds all worlds", however excited, "is only surrounding, and not true arousal but a false imagination" (line 204). The right contemplation is particular: the life drawn to each creature by its nature, and "how the power of the Infinite is seen and revealed in the world" (line 211). Along the way the Rashab corrects a first-impression reading of the Tanya's Shaar HaYichud 4: its two levels are "the radiance of the nothing clothed in the something, and the essence of the nothing above clothing" (line 216). And the reason for the toil: the soul was bound to the body "to make everything one" (line 230).

**Chapter 7 (lines 251-271): truth comes by stages.** Mostly about a person who takes on stringencies he cannot keep. One point for the unity: when the divine soul's excitement "does nothing to the animal soul" (line 255), the two have not been joined. The close gathers it up: contemplate memale at your level, love with both inclinations, and know that after the desert generation love must be reached by one's own work (line 270).

**The Rayatz's letter (lines 272-306).** Three loves, and a plain statement of providence: "for a Jew there is no chance" (line 287). It adds little to the tract, and its reading ties are ours in five places.

## What it comes to, for a person

- **Start with "Modeh ani".** Giving your whole self to Godliness in general is truer than any particular insight, and "very near to every Jew" (line 29).
- **You need not grasp the higher unity to have it.** Only lofty souls see that all is nothing before Him. Everyone can acknowledge it, and must (line 32).
- **Contemplate the thing in front of you, in detail.** Not "He fills all worlds" in general, which only hovers, but how this creature lives, and how the power of the Infinite is seen in it (lines 204, 211).
- **Awe is already in you.** It rests on a hidden feeling of the Essence and can be simply remembered (lines 107, 204). Love has to be worked for.
- **Check whether it reached the animal soul.** If the excitement of prayer changes nothing in the rest of you, the work is not done (line 255). The aim is one self, not two: "to make everything one" (line 230).

## What it adds to the Tanya and the other sources

- **Awe is in the Essence, love in the lights.** The Tanya says one must first awaken the natural fear hidden in every Jew (Tanya line 598), and that the great love, love in delights, comes as a gift to one "complete in fear" (Tanya line 651). The Rashab says awe in general, even lower awe, rests on a feeling of the Essence and is above love (lines 107, 113), while love is still the whole work of prayer, because only love binds and refines (lines 122-125). He does not say the Tanya disagrees; the two orders are kept side by side.
- **Knowing must be particular.** The Tanya asks for long, deep contemplation, until the thought is bound to God "with a strong and firm bond" (Tanya line 629). The Rashab calls general contemplation, even of memale and sovev, a "false imagination" (line 204). He also marks the limit: "in truth he cannot grasp a divine matter with no garment at all" (line 46, OCR כאמת for באמת), and creation from nothing is beyond human grasp (line 201). This is the SPINE's F12 dispute, with the Rashab on the side of knowing.
- **The animal soul can come to love.** The Tanya's beinoni is to regard the evil in himself as still "in its strength and might" in its place (Tanya line 222). The Rashab describes water-love by which the natural mind itself comes to want Godliness (line 142), and fire-love that raises the natural fire into holiness (lines 149-150). He keeps the limit himself: the animal soul "by its nature does not want Godliness", and all its rising is the divine soul working on it. He leaves that as an open question (line 180).
- **The hidden love changes nothing by itself.** The Tanya grounds self-sacrifice in the hidden love that cannot be separated from His oneness, and line 204 repeats it. The Rashab adds that this love is surrounding and leaves the coarseness as it was (line 184).
- **"Below as above" given to awe.** The Tanya says He is found "equally" below as above (Tanya line 1090, Iggeret HaKodesh 6). The Rashab makes that the contemplation that yields higher awe: the Infinite "as it is in its essence", found below in revelation (line 121).
- **A reading of Shaar HaYichud 4** against its "first impression": the two levels are the nothing clothed in the something, and the essence of the nothing above all clothing (line 216).
- **Beside Kuntres HaHitpa'alut.** The Mitteler Rebbe's tract tests arousal by whether self is felt in it. The Rashab tests it by whether it is particular, and whether it reached the animal soul. Both call a kind of arousal counterfeit: "strange fire" there, "false imagination" here.

- **90 lines have no tie:** the front matter and table of contents (1-21), 35 editor's footnotes, most of chapter 2 on guarding the senses (66-88), the Days of Awe advice (191), most of chapter 7 on order in stringencies (252-265), and one example in the letter (286).
- **Six ties are our reading:** the crown "from nearness after distance" as opposites joined (60.2), the two senses of "olam" (275), the springs parable (282), the body hiding the soul (292), Moshe's good body as the home below (293), and providence toward the Ravad's teshuvah (305).
- **The hiding (F03) is almost missing.** The tzimtzum appears once, as what lets created minds know (line 48). Elsewhere concealment is the person's own crookedness covering the divine soul (line 92).
- **Opposites joined (F09) is mostly the two souls and two inclinations,** not opposites joined in God.
- **OCR.** The file is uncorrected. Quotes keep its slips; the notes give the intended letters.

## Where it differs (kept as disagreements)

- **With the Tanya, on rank.** The Tanya puts awe first and love in delights as the higher gift (Tanya lines 598, 651). The Rashab puts awe, as bitul from the Essence, above love (lines 107, 113), while love is the goal of prayer. The Rayatz's letter at the end keeps the Tanya's order of loves. Neither is smoothed.
- **With the Tanya, on transformation.** See above: lines 142, 149-150 and 180 against the beinoni's evil "in its place" (Tanya line 222).
- **Within the book, on the animal soul.** Line 142 says it too comes to want Godliness. Line 180 says it never does so by its own nature. The Rashab marks the question open.
- **With the SPINE's knowledge-or-faith dispute.** The book stands with knowing: nothing short of particular grasp counts (lines 204, 209-211). It also keeps the limit: no divine matter is grasped with no garment (line 46).
