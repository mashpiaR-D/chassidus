## Summary

Group-10 has fourteen works: seven Chabad and seven not. In the Chabad maamarim the one idea is the stated center, and each work gives it a different angle. The Alter Rebbe's Maamarei Admur HaZaken states the dwelling below as the purpose of all worlds, a will whose reason is unknown. Its content is turning darkness to light, and it defines a dwelling as what is batel to the one who dwells in it (F07 = F08). The Tzemach Tzedek's Or HaTorah (Bamidbar and Devarim I in this file) reads verse by verse through the two knowledges. It states the yachid/echad precision in his own words: 'yachid is His Essence... not in the category of number'. The Rashab's Sefer HaMaamarim separates 'all is as nothing before Him' (sovev, Malchus) from the Essence 'called yachid', where nothing exists at all to be 'as nothing'. It makes ein od an object of intellect ('the existence of the physical is Godliness itself') and of contemplation that grows into sight. The Rayatz's Sefer HaSichos tells the idea as particular providence and biography: the Besht revealed it, the Maggid explained it, the Alter Rebbe brought it into comprehension; providence is 'more tangible' than yesh me'ayin. Thin, and said plainly: the Rebbe's 1946 Haggadah (halachic and customs apparatus, with two or three Chassidic glosses), Reshimas Maamarei Dach (a dated bibliography of the Tzemach Tzedek's maamarim) and Sefer HaMafteichos (an index that records, but does not teach, 'Shaar HaYichud is the key'). Outside Chabad: Mekor Mayim Chayim carries the Besht's unity in its classical form ('if one knows He hides there, it is no hiding'), the priest's objection that rejecting idolatry rejects 'part of Him', and Magen Avos's ladder of Yisro, Rachav and Moshe on whether the tzimtzum is true. Chovat HaTalmidim makes 'even below, ein od' the student's daily discipline, and the Jew the place where His essence, not only His power, is revealed. Bnei Yissaschar reads the unity through sacred time (Shabbat testifies to it, Pesach nullifies idolatry) and takes the non-literal side of the tzimtzum. Beit Aharon makes it a task, 'to unify everything in this world to His simple oneness', in the register of 'echad' rather than 'yachid' (our reading). Avodat Yisrael gives it in Beshtian form: descent means revelation, and He gives power even to the sinner. Likutei Moharan states the unity (lessons 33, 51) but holds it through faith: the void must be described by two opposites and 'cannot be grasped until the future'. That is kept as the far end of the spine's knowledge-or-faith disagreement, and the book's organizing center is the tzaddik and emunah, not the unity. Divrei Emet is largely thin: its center is the tzaddik and the sweetening of judgments, and the unity appears only in fragments (tzimtzum for the sake of bestowal; after teshuvah 'all is one'). Counts caveats: א"ס is omitted as letters-only noise; the Rayatz's Yiddish sichos undercount; ביטול in the Haggadah and Bnei Yissaschar is often the legal sense.

## Haggadah shel Pesach im Likkutei Taamim uMinhagim (Brooklyn, New York, 1946) **R. Menachem Mendel Schneerson (Lubavitcher Rebbe) (1902-1994)** · Chabad · clean text · 15,500 words · 431 lines

The Rebbe's first book (1946): the Haggadah with collected reasons, customs and sources. Most notes are halachic and textual (Shulchan Aruch HaRav, Rishonim, variant readings of the Haggadah), with customs of the Rebbeim and a few Chassidic notes from Likkutei Torah, the Rashab's writings and the Rayatz's talks (some in Yiddish). It has 431 lines and about 15,500 words, so its rates are volatile.

- **F05 The Essence beyond both** (secondary): עצמות 2 lines, אין סוף 1. The one Chassidic gloss on 'I and not an angel' reads 'in His glory and in Himself' as 'Malchus of Ein Sof and the Essence of Ein Sof before the tzimtzum' (line 259, from the Rashab's writings).
- **F09 The unity of opposites** (secondary): Line 352 (Sichos Chag HaPesach 5702, via the Mitteler Rebbe): in Jews the physical is not separate from the spiritual; the spiritual is drawn into the physical, and the physical is turned into the spiritual. This is the counter-side to the Alter Rebbe's 'spirituality and physicality are opposites in essence', which the spine keeps.

**Signature** (reading; stated only in two or three glosses)

The Haggadah is a book of practice, not of Chassidus, and its tie to the one idea is thin. Where it does touch the idea, it does so in the Rebbe's characteristic way: at the point of action, in the objects on the table. The exodus 'by Himself' is glossed as the Essence before the tzimtzum (259); matzah is 'food of faith' and 'food of healing', a 'point-word' handed down from the Besht through the Maggid to the Alter Rebbe and re-explained by every Rebbe (349-351); and the Mitteler Rebbe's distinction is extended to the claim that in Jews the physical is not separate from the spiritual (352). Our reading: the book's own method (every custom traced to its source, halacha first) matches G11, 'end in action', but it never states that tie itself. The 3 hits for ביטול are almost all the halachic nullification of chametz (line 10), not bitul in the sense of F08.

**Structure.** Follows the order of the Seder, from the search for chametz through Nirtzah, each step with sources, reasons and customs (בדיקת חמץ, קדש, יחץ, מגיד, מוציא מצה...). The Chassidic glosses sit inside that order, in מגיד and מוציא מצה, and are few.

**Thin.** Largely yes. The halachic and textual apparatus, most of the book, has no stated tie to the unity. Only the few Chassidic glosses (259, 349-352) carry it, and even there the book reports rather than develops it.

## Or HaTorah (Lubavitch, c. 1828-1866) **R. Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (Tzemach Tzedek) (1789-1866)** · Chabad · clean text · 900,694 words · 5,854 lines

The Tzemach Tzedek's maamarim and notes arranged by the verses of Torah. This clean file covers only Bamidbar (vols. 1-4, with Shavuos discourses and 'additions') and Devarim vol. 1, 5,854 lines and about 900,000 words; the full series is in a separate OCR file. Many pieces are the Tzemach Tzedek's reworking of his grandfather's maamarim: the Balak maamar 'לא הביט און ביעקב' (line 3684-3686) opens with the same question and the same answer as the Alter Rebbe's 5564 maamar in Maamarei Admur HaZaken (line 13964-13965).

- **F02 The higher and the lower unity** (primary): The two knowledges are named and worked through verse by verse: 'ד"ע וד"ת' at 1158; Havayah as yichuda ila'ah and Elokim as the concealer at 708. אין עוד מלבדו 28 lines.
- **F05 The Essence beyond both** (primary): עצמות 469 lines (5.2/10k), אלקות 333. The Essence is marked as 'yachid', beyond number and beyond 'echad' (3621).
- **F04 Filling and surrounding** (primary): The abbreviations carry this face: סוכ"ע 440 lines (4.9/10k), ממכ"ע 284 (3.2/10k); the spelled forms are only 8 and 3.
- **F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity** (primary): ביטול 622 lines (6.9/10k), the highest term in the set; true bitul as 'koach mah' (452, 3619).
- **F07 The home below** (secondary): דירה בתחתונים 102 lines (1.1/10k); 3685-3686: the mitzvos are the 'fine vessels' that make the dwelling; 2569: drawing His dwelling below 'to be ein od milvado' is above ecstatic fervor.
- **F11 Darkness turned to light** (secondary): אתהפכא 126 lines.

Or HaTorah reads Scripture phrase by phrase and makes each phrase a case of the unity. The census of Bamidbar becomes the question of number itself: 'echad' is many powers joined, 'yachid' is His Essence 'which is not in the category of number' (3621). This is the precision the Rebbe later makes the key to the spine (Inyanah 5:5), stated here in the Tzemach Tzedek's words. The priestly blessing's 'light of the eyes' becomes the two knowledges, from nothing to something and from something to nothing, in which 'in truth there is nothing besides Him' (1158). 'He saw no iniquity in Jacob' becomes the dwelling below: nothing changes in Him, 'only before Him'; for us the world hides Godliness, and service means preparing vessels for the dwelling (3685-3686). What it adds: the unity as exegesis. No verse is too narrative or technical to be read as one face of it, and the method is to hold the higher and the lower knowledge side by side (G02).

**Structure.** By parsha, each parsha a series of maamarim on its verses, followed by 'additions' (הוספות) and 'hanachos': variant versions, notes and glosses, often on his grandfather's maamarim. The additions repeat a discourse at different lengths, so the same unity argument is often given two or three times.

**Thin.** No. The book is dense Chassidus throughout; some notes are purely Kabbalistic mechanics (partzufim, letter-counts) whose tie to the unity is stated only at the level of method (G04).

> **Or HaTorah, במדבר כרך ג / פרשת בלק / ביאור על פסוק מי מנה / תתקפח6:1** (line 3621 · stated · F05, F02)
> שאחד נק' כשהרבה כוחות מחולקים מחוברים יחד בתכלית דהיינו בבחי' כלים דאצי', אבל יחיד הוא בחי' עצמותו ית' שאין עוד מלבדו שהוא פשוט בתכלית הפשיטות ואינו בגדר מספר
> — On 'Who counted the dust of Jacob': 'echad' is many divided powers joined, as in the vessels of Atzilus; 'yachid' is His Essence, ein od milvado, simple and outside number. The yachid/echad precision of the spine, stated in the Tzemach Tzedek.

> **Or HaTorah, במדבר כרך ג / פרשת בלק / לא הביט און ביעקב / א'יג:4** (line 3685 · stated · F07, F02, F03)
> רק זהו קמי' דווקא אבל אצלינו העולם נחשב ליש ודבר בפ"ע ומסתיר אלקות שלכך נק' בשם עולם ע"ש ההעלם שמעלים אלקות ומסתירו, ותכלית בריאת העולם הוא שנתאווה הקדב"ה להיות לו דירה בתחתונים
> — Nothing changes in Him ('only before Him'), but for us the world is a thing in itself and hides Godliness ('olam' from 'he'lem'); the purpose of creation is the dwelling below. Three faces in one sentence; the next line makes the mitzvos the vessels of that dwelling.

> **Or HaTorah, במדבר כרך א / הוספות - פרשת נשא / כה תברכו / צא:1** (line 1158 · stated · F02, F12)
> שיש ב' בחי' דעות ד"ע וד"ת, דעת תחתון הוא מאין ליש … משא"כ ד"ע הוא מיש לאין שלמעלה הוא בחי' יש להיותו יש האמיתי ולמטה הוא בחי' אין להיות כל העולמות קמי' הם כלא וכאין נחשבו ובאמת אין עוד מלבדו
> — The two knowledges as the 'two eyes' of the priestly blessing: lower knowledge sees the creature as yesh and Godliness as hidden; higher knowledge sees Him as the true yesh and the worlds as nothing, 'in truth ein od milvado'. G02 stated.

## Reshimas Maamarei Dach (scans, OCR) **R. Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (Tzemach Tzedek) (1789-1866)** · Chabad · OCR · 63,039 words · 2,558 lines

A 1994 Kehot bibliography, edited by R. Yosef Yitzchak Keller: a chronological list of the Tzemach Tzedek's maamarim (5588-5626), each title with its manuscript locations and where it is printed (usually Or HaTorah), with appended lists for other Rebbeim (e.g. the Maharash's notes of 5622-5624). The introduction explains that Or HaTorah prints by verse order, and this list restores the order of saying and writing. OCR, 2,558 lines, about 63,000 words.

- None carried as the work's own teaching.

The book does not express the one idea; it is an instrument for reading the works that do. The introduction's stated purpose is historical and textual: dating a maamar separates its core from later additions and shows where the Tzemach Tzedek had a passage from an earlier maamar inserted (9-12). The unity appears only in titles, e.g. 'explanation of tzimtzum and empty space' in R. Hillel's notes (1016). Any claim that the ordering itself serves the unity would be forced, and we do not make it.

**Structure.** Introduction on the value of dating; then year by year, each maamar title with manuscript numbers, headings as recorded, and the printed location; appendices for later Rebbeim.

**Thin.** Yes. A bibliography and manuscript index. The counts are near zero (צמצום 9 lines, all in titles).

## Maamarei Admur HaZaken (scans, OCR) **R. Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe) (1745-1812)** · Chabad · OCR · 2,745,537 words · 43,951 lines

The Kehot series of the Alter Rebbe's maamarim as transcribed by his sons and chassidim, in OCR: year volumes 5562-5572, plus Es'halech Lozniah, HaKetzarim (short versions), Inyanim, Maamarei Razal and Parshiyos. 43,951 lines and about 2.75 million words, with an editors' apparatus of sources and variant readings that also carries the terms (e.g. 3086, 5978 are footnotes on 'dirah').

- **F07 The home below** (primary): דירה בתחתונים 96 lines and נתאוה 41, spread across 5563, 5564, 5566, 5567, 5571, HaKetzarim and Inyanim; תכלית 1,438 lines (5.2/10k).
- **F05 The Essence beyond both** (primary): עצמות 2,601 lines (9.5/10k), the most frequent term in the set; אלקות 1,154.
- **F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity** (primary): ביטול 1,601 lines (5.8/10k); 'yesh nullified to ayin' at 8510; the dwelling as what is 'batel to the one who dwells in it' (33128).
- **F11 Darkness turned to light** (secondary): אתהפכא 103 lines; the dwelling is defined as 'turning darkness to light' (1710, 13965).
- **F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation** (secondary): צמצום 1,160 lines, הסתר 530; 'olam' from 'he'lem' (13965); 'love compresses the flesh': the contractions as love (33127).

In these maamarim the Alter Rebbe states the purpose of all worlds as the dwelling below, over and over, and he says what the dwelling is. He keeps its root beyond reason: 'the purpose of this intention is not known', only that it is His intention (1710). This is the will without a reason that the Rebbe later reads as 'a desire has no question'. Its content is transformation: 'to turn darkness to light', so that 'this physical and material world, beneath which there is nothing' is joined to Ein Sof by Torah and prayer (1710). He also defines it: a dwelling is batel to the one who dwells in it and has no identity of its own (33128). So F07 and F08 are one idea here, and that is stated. The infinite light 'I have not changed' is to be revealed specifically among creatures created in contraction and concealment (13965). The Alter Rebbe teaches this orally and in context, maamar by maamar. What it adds to Tanya's statement of the idea (ch. 33, 36) is the working-out: the dwelling examined in each festival, verse and mitzvah.

**Structure.** Year volumes of oral maamarim (hanachos), each with editorial notes; HaKetzarim gives condensed versions; Inyanim collects topical pieces; Maamarei Razal and Parshiyos arrange by source. The same maamar often appears in several transcriptions, and some reappear in the Tzemach Tzedek's Or HaTorah (e.g. 'לא הביט און ביעקב', 13963ff. = Or HaTorah 3684ff.).

**Thin.** No. Only the apparatus (footnotes, variant readings, the OCR's page furniture) is thin, and it inflates the counts somewhat.

## Sefer HaSichos (scans, OCR) **R. Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (Rebbe Rayatz) (1880-1950)** · Chabad · OCR · 569,489 words · 15,081 lines

- **F09 The unity of opposites** (secondary): 1125: the priest's question, if no place is empty of Him, rejecting idolatry rejects a part of Him; the Besht's answer, a curtain that separates good from evil.
- **F01 The world said into being now** (secondary): יחוד 123 lines (8.5/10k), אין סוף 57; God in every movement and every thought, even alien thoughts (519, 547, 554).

This anthology shows the Besht's unity as it passed into the Polish and Galician schools. His glory fills the earth, no place is empty of Him, and He is present in every movement and thought, alien thoughts included. The practice follows: know He hides there, and the hiding ends (1872, the spine's own anchor for G03). Two points are kept, not smoothed. First, the unity raises a real objection: if He is in everything, rejecting idolatry seems to reject part of Him. The answer is a curtain that separates good from bad, opposites kept distinct in conduct (1125, G10). Second, it records the tzimtzum dispute as three levels of faith (from Magen Avos): Yisro, who gave idols some power; Rachav, who knew He alone gives life below as above but held the tzimtzum true; and Moshe, for whom 'ein od' fills even the void (1604-1607). That is the spine's disagreement, set out as a ladder. What it adds: the one idea sourced to the Besht, parsha by parsha.

**Structure.** Opening kuntres; then by parsha, each unit a quotation from a named source ('ובהיכל הברכה... כתב וז"ל'), often several sources on one teaching, with the compiler's cross-references.

**Thin.** No, though as an anthology it repeats one teaching many times from different sources.

> **Mekor Mayim Chayim on Baal Shem Tov, Vaetchanan 6:2** (line 1605 · stated · F02, F03, F05)
> אבל לגבי מהותו ועצמותו יתברך שמו, כמו שהוא קמיה, אין הצמצום אמתי כלל … ודעת רחב, שהוא לבדו מחיה ומהווה למטה כמו שהוא למעלה … אבל בענין הצמצום סברה שהוא אמתי
> — From Magen Avos: the tzimtzum is concealment only toward creatures, and toward His essence it is not true at all (1604); Rachav knew He alone gives life below as above but held the tzimtzum true, while Moshe said ein od (1607). The spine's tzimtzum disagreement as a ladder. Pieces at lines 1604 and 1607.

> **Mekor Mayim Chayim on Baal Shem Tov, Vayeilech 4:2** (line 1872 · stated · F03, F12)
> שמעתי ממורי שאם ידע אדם בידיעה ואמונה שלימה שבכל הסתרות ומחשבות זרות הקדוש ברוך הוא מסתתר שם אין זה הסתרה כלל
> — The Besht (via Toldos, in Leket Imrei Peninim): if one knows with full knowledge and faith that God hides in every concealment and alien thought, it is no concealment. G03 in the Besht's words.

> **Mekor Mayim Chayim on Baal Shem Tov, Yitro 8:2** (line 1125 · stated · F09, F01)
> אתה מזהיר את העם שידעו בכל רגע דלית אתר פנוי מיניה, ואין שום מציאות דבר קטן וגדול ופעולה קטנה וגדולה בלתי מציאות השם יתברך, אם כן אם אתה כופר בע"ז אל נכר, אתה כופר בחלק שהוא ממנו יתברך
> — A philosopher-priest's question to the Besht: if nothing exists without Him, rejecting idolatry rejects a part of Him. The answer (next words): a separating curtain between good and evil. The unity pressed to its hardest case, with opposites kept apart in conduct (G10).

## Beit Aharon (Karlin, Belarus, 1830-1870) **R. Aharon of Karlin II (1802-1872)** · not Chabad · clean text · 221,013 words · 2,388 lines

Teachings of R. Aharon II of Karlin, with the daily conduct and letters of R. Asher of Stolin, aphorisms and additions (Karlin-Stolin). Clean text, 2,388 lines, about 221,000 words; most of it is the 'Teachings on Torah and Holidays'.

- **F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual** (primary): יחוד 156 lines (7.1/10k), אחדות 55 (2.5/10k): 'to unify everything in this world to His simple oneness' is 'the main thing' and 'an obligation on every Jew' (762).
- **F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity** (secondary): R. Asher: one who fears anything besides God 'implies two domains and denies the root' (73); 'setting oneself as ayin' (74).
- **F07 The home below** (secondary): 1214: this is the lowly world, yet all ascents of all worlds come only from it, to unify everything to the simple oneness. 'Dirah' does not appear (0 lines); the link to F07 is our reading.
- **F01 The world said into being now** (secondary): השגחה פרטית 6 lines; the exodus is knowing 'that all events are in particular providence' (1245).

**Signature** (stated as a task; the yachid/echad precision is our reading)

Beit Aharon makes the unity a task. The main thing is 'to unify everything that exists in this world to His simple oneness', 'to be one in One', through deed, speech and thought, and every Jew is obligated (762). This world is lowly, yet every ascent of every world comes from it (1214). The Karliner voice is fervor: all service leads to mesirus nefesh 'until everything is unified to the simple oneness' (1241). R. Asher of Stolin's rules turn the same point into conduct: fearing any creature implies 'two domains' (73). On the spine's precision: the book's register is 'echad', joining many into one ('one in One', the thirteen attributes as the numeral 'echad'). The spine's 'yachid' names the Essence beyond parts. That this is a difference of emphasis, not a disagreement, is our reading.

**Structure.** Daily conduct (R. Asher), writings, then teachings arranged by the Torah and the festivals, then miscellany, letters, aphorisms and an addendum.

**Thin.** No, though much is devotional exhortation to prayer and joy whose tie is general.

> **Beit Aharon, Teachings on Torah and Holidays, R' Aharon (the Second) of Karlin, Genesis, Chanukah 52** (line 762 · stated · F10, F07, F08)
> הנה כבר דברנו מזה כמה פעמים שהעיקר הוא לייחד כל מה שנמצא בזה העולם לאחדותו הפשוט וכפי שאנו אומרים כגוונא דילי' למהוי אחד באחד
> — 'As we have said many times': the main thing is to unify all that is in this world to His simple oneness, 'one in One'. It continues: every person must correct deeds, words and thoughts so that His holiness is revealed in this world.

> **Beit Aharon, Teachings on Torah and Holidays, R' Aharon (the Second) of Karlin, Leviticus, Haggadah 1** (line 1214 · reading · F07, F11)
> כי זה העולם נקרא עולם השפל. עכ"ז כל העליות מכל העולמות הוא רק מזה העולם שצריך לייחד הכל לאחדות הפשוט
> — On 'this is the bread of affliction': this world is lowly and 'broken' like the poor man's bread, yet every ascent of all worlds comes from it. The highest-in-the-lowest (G05); the tie to 'dirah' is ours.

> **Beit Aharon, Daily Conduct and Exhortations, R' Asher (the First) of Stolin 46** (line 73 · stated · F08, F04)
> והאדם הירא מזולתו מורה ח"ו שתי רשויות וכופר בעיקר ח"ו
> — R. Asher of Stolin's rule, after Chovos HaLevavos and the Tikkunim ('no place empty of Him'): to fear anything besides God is to imply two domains. The unity as a rule of conduct (F08: separateness as idolatry).

## Avodat Yisrael (Kozienice, Poland, 1750-1810) **R. Yisrael Hopstein, Maggid of Kozhnitz (1737-1814)** · not Chabad · clean text · 153,423 words · 739 lines

The Maggid of Kozhnitz's discourses on the Torah (Sefer), with an anthology, a letter, a commentary on Pirkei Avot, and novellae. Clean text, 739 lines, about 153,000 words.

- **F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation** (primary): 263: 'He descended' cannot be said of Him 'who fills all worlds... no place is empty'; descent means revelation, departure means concealment toward creatures. הסתר 19 lines.
- **F01 The world said into being now** (secondary): 556: He 'gives power even to those who go to sin'; the sinner only imagines he hides from providence. לית אתר פנוי 8 lines, מלא כל הארץ כבודו 7.
- **F09 The unity of opposites** (secondary): 585: 'the power of the agent is in the thing made', and since the agent is simple oneness, creatures always yearn for their root.
- **F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity** (secondary): Avot 4:4 (555): lowliness before every person.

**Signature** (stated in the Avot commentary and scattered discourses; the book as one expression is reading)

The Kozhnitzer states the unity in its classical Beshtian form. He is present everywhere, so His 'descent' and 'removal' are only revelation and concealment toward creatures (263). He gives the power even to those who sin, so the sinner's 'secret' is a self-deception, a 'gap in His existence' that the sinner makes only in his own mind (556). Because 'the power of the agent is in the thing made' and the agent is simple oneness, every creature yearns for its root (585). That is his answer to why a good Creator's world holds the wicked. The book's center, though, is the tzaddik and prayer with kavanos (צדיק 215 lines, יחוד 103, mostly yichudim). The unity is its ground, not its subject.

**Structure.** Discourses by parsha, a short anthology, a letter, a commentary on Avot chapter by chapter, and novellae.

**Thin.** Partly. The Kabbalistic yichudim and the tzaddik discourses carry the idea only as background.

> **Avodat Yisrael, Sefer Bamidbar, Nasso 3** (line 263 · stated · F03, F04)
> כי לא יצוייר אליו ית' הממלא כל עלמין בכל עת וזמן ולית אתר פנוי מיניה כלל. לומר שירד על הר סיני. ע"כ תיקן זאת בפירושו שמלת הורדה הוא ענין התגלות
> — He who fills all worlds cannot 'descend' to Sinai; the Targum renders 'was revealed'. His 'departure' likewise is concealment from creatures, lifted when their deeds are refined. F03 stated.

> **Avodat Yisrael, Pirkei Avot, Chapter 4 4:2** (line 556 · stated · F01, F12)
> אך דנודע כי הוה הבורא ית"ש מלא כה"כ ולית אתר פנוי מיני' כלל והוא הנותן כח גם בהולכי' לחטוא. א"כ כאשר יחשוב האדם זאת בודאי לא יחטא
> — 'Desecrating the Name in secret': He fills all and gives power even to those who sin; one who thought this would not sin. The sinner imagines he hides from providence and so makes 'a gap in the Creator's existence'.

> **Avodat Yisrael, Pirkei Avot, Chapter 5 1:1** (line 585 · stated · F09, F01)
> ור"ל כי כח הפועל בנפעל וכפי כח הפועל שהוא אחדות פשוט יחויב בזה שגם הנבראים ממנו יסתכלו וישתוקקו תמיד לשורשם
> — On 'the world was created with ten utterances': why a good Creator's world holds wicked people and passions. His answer: the agent's power is in what is made, and since the agent is simple unity, creatures always look to and yearn for their root.

## Divrei Emet (Lublin, 1770-1810) **R. Yaakov Yitzchak Horowitz (Chozeh of Lublin) (1745-1815)** · not Chabad · clean text · 80,066 words · 561 lines

Teachings of the Chozeh of Lublin on the Torah, with his ethical instructions ('Ethics from the Author'), published by his grandson. Clean text, 561 lines, about 80,000 words.

- **F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation** (secondary): 150: the tzimtzum was 'for the benefit of bestowing'; judgments sweetened in their root bring judgment on Israel's enemies and to Israel sustenance and wealth. צמצום 10 lines.
- **F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity** (secondary): 119-120: the true tzaddik's sign is that his service brings no pride; 'the humility and the ayin' above.
- **F09 The unity of opposites** (secondary): 225: before teshuvah there are 'many loves', money, honor; after teshuvah 'all is one'.

The Chozeh's center is the tzaddik who unites 'the Holy One and His Shechinah' and draws down 'children, life and sustenance' for Israel (צדיק 112 lines against אחדות 28 and אין עוד מלבדו 0). The spine records the doctrine of the tzaddik as a place where the tie to the unity is thin or forced, and this book bears that out. The unity appears only in fragments. The tzimtzum exists for the sake of giving (150), which is G03 turned toward material blessing. The true tzaddik has no pride, a form of F08 (119-120). And teshuvah makes a person's scattered loves 'all one' (225). These are real, but we will not call the book as a whole an expression of the one idea.

**Structure.** Approbations and introduction; the author's ethical instructions (numbered); teachings by parsha; festival pieces.

**Thin.** Largely yes. The idea is present in a few passages but does not organize the book; the doctrine of the tzaddik and the sweetening of judgments do.

> **Divrei Emet, Divrei Emet on Torah, Chayei Sara 4** (line 150 · stated · F03)

> — Creation is for His glory; the great light could not be received, and choice would be impossible, 'so there was the tzimtzum; the tzimtzum was for the benefit of bestowal'. Concealment for the sake of giving (G03), in the register of blessing.

> **Divrei Emet, Divrei Emet on Torah, Noach 10** (line 119 · reading · F08, F07)
> דהנה יש צדיקים שיש להם תשוקה להיות תמיד יחוד קב"ה ושכינתי' הוא כנ"י שהשי"ת ישכון ביניהם
> — The tzaddik's longing is for the union of the Holy One and His Shechinah, His dwelling among Israel; the sign of truth is that it brings no pride. 'Yichud' here is the Kabbalistic union, not the spine's 'yachid'; the tie is our reading.
