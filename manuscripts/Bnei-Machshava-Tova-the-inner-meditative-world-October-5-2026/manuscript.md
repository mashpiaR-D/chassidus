This is a short handbook, not a book of sermons. Around 1917-1923 the Piaseczner Rebbe wrote it for a small, private circle of young men who wanted to meet in secret to work on their inner lives. It is the most practical text he wrote on meditation: a rulebook for a fellowship, a set of exercises, and an account of what those exercises open up inside a person. Every Hebrew line below was checked with `find_he.py verify` against the source file and came back FOUND at the line given.

## 2. The method: concrete instructions for inner practice

These are set out in roughly the order a person would take them up.

**A. Precondition: feel the distance, don't just know it.** - Only someone who truly feels pain at being far from God may join, "and not that he merely knows in his mind his low state": ולא שידע בדעתו לבד את מצבו הנמוך (21).

**B. Begin with thought, not with the body.** - לכן צריכים להתחיל את העבודה במחשבה, "therefore the work must begin with thought" (39). This reverses the older order of purifying the body first (36-38). - Thought is trained like walking: כמו שמוציאים כח ההליכה בהילד על ידי ההרגל, "as one brings out the power of walking in a child through habituation" (54). - What is being trained is simple faith, *thought* until it fills the body: שיהיה חושב באמונתו במחשבה רחבה שממלאה את כל הגוף (51).

**C. For beginners, a permitted picture.** - Relying on the Raavad, the person knows God has no image, but, being "formed of clay", pictures it anyway so thought has something to hold: הקרוץ מחמר איש מגשם בעל תמונה מציר לו זאת, כדי שתוכל מחשבתו להאחז ולהתגדל ולהתרחב (89). - The picture to use: ציר לך שאתה עומד לפני כסא כבודו ואתה מתפלל ומבקש ממנו ית' פשוט כבן שעומד ומתחנן מאביו, "picture yourself standing before His Throne of Glory, praying and asking Him simply, like a son standing and pleading with his father" (91). - It is temporary: אז התמונה הציור גופני הזה ממילא יתבטלו, "then this image, this physical picture, falls away of itself" (90).

**D. Using existing emotion as a door (the "key").** - כל התעוררות אפלו אם גופנית, היא מפתח לנפש, "every arousal, even a bodily one, is a key to the soul" (100). - *Exercise 1, a past worry.* Recall a real sorrow and picture it vividly: ומציר אותה בדמיון חזק כאלו היא לנגד עיניו לבו מתרכך ומתרגש אף בוכה (103). Once it moves you, redirect it: ולמה לי לשבר את לבי ולבכות לריק הלא ד' לפני, "why break my heart and weep for nothing? God is before me" (104). Then strengthen the Throne-picture (105). - *Exercise 2, the day of death, in detail.* Not יזכיר לו יום המיתה" אבל לא ברעיון גרידא וכללי, "not as a bare, general idea" (106), because ידיעה כללית שאינה שוברת את הלב בפרוטרוט, "general knowledge does not break the heart in its particulars" (107). Picture the last moment, the children, the funeral, and then: וסוף כל סוף כלם שבים לביתם והוא איפה נשאר, ועם מי נשאר, "and in the end they all go home, and where is he left, and with whom?" (115). Move from this bitterness into prayer (117).

**E. Catching the moment during the day (lines 119-124).** - The problem is that prayer and arousal rarely coincide: מפני שאין תפלתו והתעוררותו באות כאחת (118). - When a sorrow hits, even a business one: ילך תכף הצידה ויעמיד עצמו אל הכתל ויאמר איזה קאפיטלאך תהלים, "let him go aside at once, stand at the wall and say a few chapters of Tehillim" (119). The chapter should match the worry (Ps 3, 69, 121). - Not straight from the market "with words without heart and without mind", ולא שיבא פתאום מן השוק וידבר דבורים בלא לב ובלא מוח (120). First contemplate the worry and who alone can help. - Then free prayer in any language. Word by word he will feel the mind rise: ומן דבור לדבור ומן תבה לתבה, ירגיש שמוחו ומחשבתו מתחזקים ועולים (121). End with a verse of strength (Ps 23) and rejoice. - The same with joy: Psalm 18, then thanks in your own words. Keep it short: טוב שלא להרבות עתה בתהלים, "better not to say too much Tehillim now" (122). - Even a sigh counts (123).

**F. Histaklus: "giving birth to the form" of a feeling (lines 137-163).** - ואין הסתכלות ראיה על דבר הנמצא בלבד, רק מין לידה שמולידים ומוציאים דבר שיהיה לנו להסתכל בו, "looking is not only seeing what is there; it is a kind of birth, bringing out something for us to look at" (142). - With a feeling: להוציא בה צורה ולהסתכל בצורת הרגשה, "bring out a form in it, and look at the form of the feeling" (144). - Pace: כי אין המהיר מתבונן, "the hasty man does not contemplate" (148), but also שלא תגיע במתינותך עד קצה גבולה, ולא תגע בעצלות ועצבות, "don't let your slowness reach its limit and touch sloth and sadness" (148). - The worked example is Seudah Shlishis in the dark (153-160). Review the whole Shabbos you have climbed through, sit in the dark with friends, and notice the struggle between longing and the approaching weekday. Then: הסתכל והתבונן, כי כל זה עובר בנפשך רק אתה לא התבוננת ולא ידעת (160).

**G. Daily contemplation of "everything is God except me" (lines 182-191, 286).** - Think each day: וגם אתה מלא אלהות הנך, ואפלו גרגירי החול שאני דורך עליהם שמות הם, "you too are full of Godliness; even the grains of sand I tread on are Divine Names", and ורק אני ברצוני הפרטי הוצאתי את עצמי לדבר נמצא בפני עצמו, "only I, by my private will, took myself out to be a thing existing by itself" (182). - Repetition is a kind of self-compulsion that frees the inner will, as in the Rambam's כופין אותו עד שיאמר רוצה אני (183). - Walking in the street or at home: כל העולם אלקות הוא, גרגיר חול אשר תחת רגלי, האויר שאני נושם בקרבי (286), ending "Master of the world, bring me close".

**H. Mesirus nefesh in the Shema (lines 188-191).** At Shema, picture a real test of martyrdom until the longing is actual. It is לא מחשבה ריקנית לבדה, "not an empty thought alone" (191); כל גופו מתפעל מכח ההשלכה ואת הנפש עם הגוף מטהרת (191).

**I. Niggun (lines 231-245).** - קח לך איזה תנועה של נגון, הסב פניך אל הקיר, או רק תסגר את עיניך, "take some turn of a melody, face the wall, or just close your eyes" (237). Think again that you stand before the Throne, come in brokenness to pour out your soul. - There is no need for new tunes (236), and you do not have to sing loudly: כי יש מי שמנגן רק המיה בלחש וקולו נשמע במרום, "there is one who sings only a whispered hum, and his voice is heard on high" (245). - Sing with the chassidim at prayer or at a meal, and at home whenever you feel ready (244-245).

**J. Prayer posture and kavanah (Daily Instructions 4, 6).** - בתפלתך תעמד במקום אחד, אצל הקיר או בסגירת עינים, ותתפלל בקול, "in prayer stand in one place, by the wall or with eyes closed, and pray aloud" (275). - Merely translating the words does nothing: וכונה של פירוש המלות זו לא תעורר אותו מאומה (277). Mean them. In "Halleluyah", intend שהוא מדבר עתה אל העולם ומצוה אותו להלל את ד', "that he is now speaking to the world and commanding it to praise God" (278). - Against a stray thought: הבט השמימה, או סגור את עיניך, וכון "ד' ד' א-ל רחום וחנון..." (דוקא במחשבה ולא בדבור), "look up to heaven, or close your eyes, and intend the Thirteen Attributes, specifically in thought and not in speech" (285).

**K. Keep your own words.** Words that burst out in a moment of feeling should be kept and reused to wake yourself: כי דבורים אלו ניצוצי נפשך הם, ממנה נחצבו, "for these words are sparks of your soul, hewn from it" (274).

**L. How to read and how long.** - תלמדהו בנחת, ולא בפעם אחת תעבר עליו, "study it calmly, not all at once" (134). Split it over days and repeat it. - Read it רק כאדם שקורא את דברי עצמו, "as one reading his own words" (268), because כי אי אפשר לך להתרגש בשפלות, שמחה, שבירת הלב ועליה בפעם אחת (272). - Work on one item for a day or two or three (273). - Timing appears as limits on worldly activity (half or a quarter hour for a meal, 301), not as a set length for meditation. **No fixed session length for contemplation is given in this text.**

**M. Training the "spirit of rule" (lines 255-263).** Deliberately refuse small permitted things: לשתות לפעמים קאווע בלא צוקער, "sometimes drink coffee without sugar" (261). When two dishes are served, pass up the one your eye fell on first.

**N. The group (lines 314-332).** Meet at least three times a week. Learn guidance texts slowly and apply them to yourselves. Voice every good idea. A little drink, a song (Yedid Nefesh, Adon Olam, Ps 23), a dance if the soul flares. Take one confidant: יקח לו כל אחד מהם חבר לעצמו שלפניו יגלה את כל מצפוני לבבו (325). Meet once a year for several days of quiet work. Keep it secret.

**On hashkatah (quieting).** This text has no explicit instruction to quiet the mind. The nearest things are the dark and stillness of Seudah Shlishis (155-157), avoiding haste (148), and eyes closed or facing the wall. The full hashkatah technique belongs to his later writings (see Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim and Mevo HaShe'arim in this folder), not to this one.

## 3. The inner experience, in his images

- **The run and the melting.** ריצה ונמיסה, נמיסת הנפש בהשתפכותה אל חיק אביה שבשמים, "running and melting, the soul melting as it pours itself into the bosom of its Father in Heaven" (5). It is like a son meeting his father after years apart.
- **Seeing with the senses transformed.** וגם הם יראו את קדשת ד' פרושה על כל הנמצא, "they too [the senses] will see God's holiness spread over everything" (6), and ועין בעין יראה האדם אשר נמצא הוא בגן ד' בעדן (7).
- **Yom Kippur as proof.** At Kol Nidre and Ne'ilah everyone feels בטול כל תאוה, הרהור ורצון לא טוב, מפני שמחשבתו ברה חזקה אז בד' (33). Strong thought on its own dissolves desire.
- **Picturing the Throne as the heart's native sight.** In a flare-up the strong thinker ורואה במחשבתו ובדמיונו דמיון של ישראל בני נביאים (48). Afterwards he can hold it: יכול לאחז במחשבתו הקדומה לבלי להרפותה (48). In ordinary fervent prayer ורואה שכסא הכבוד נשתלשל אליו, "he sees that the Throne of Glory has descended to him" (94).
- **Pure thought, seen from inside.** Strip away the sense-images and אז תשאר מחשבה רוחנית שאין אנו רואים ומרגישים אותה (66). One whose clean thought is established חושב ומדמה ורואה לפי מצבו את הקדשה ואת כסא הכבוד של שרש וחצב נשמתו, "thinks, pictures and sees, according to his state, holiness and the Throne of Glory of the root and quarry of his soul" (77).
- **Arousal is the soul stepping outside.** כל התרגשות והתלהבות הישראלי מין התגלות הנפש חוץ מגופה היא (96).
- **The prisoner-son.** The door has been opened for another reason, but "my son is before me": אחבקהו ואנשקהו ואדבר עמו דברי אהובים כאב עם בנו (101). He calls the soul out לקרא אותה מחוץ לשערי הברזל אשר אסורה שם (102).
- **Rising word by word, then an afterglow.** נפשו היתה בגן עדן היום ומי ידמה לה ולשמחתה (121).
- **The blocked spring.** דומה הנפש אל מקור מים שנסתם (123). Every crack lets out water enough for your fields and your friends' too.
- **Tearing the curtain.** לקרע בקריעה אחת את כל המסך הפרוס על כל החיים, and suddenly you see yourself among the angels, "and you are one of them" (127).
- **A new thought you did not know you had.** רק שיתגלה בקרבך מין מחשבה חדשה שלא ידעת מקדם שהיא נמצאת בך ובה תראה הכל (130).
- **The soul reaching out a limb.** A vague unease or joy that one "treats" with food or vodka was really מין הושטת אבר מאברי הנשמה החוצה שרצתה להתפעל ולחשב מחשבה טהורה (139). Drowning it out is והיא מין הפלת נפלים של הנשמה, "a kind of miscarriage of the soul" (141). The parable is Molech's priests beating drums so the father cannot hear his son (141).
- **The chariot passing through you.** At Seudah Shlishis your heart and mind shake from גלגלי המרכבה שעוברים דרך נפשך (152). Then כל החדר מלא מפמליא של מעלה, "the whole room is full of the heavenly retinue" (157). You push through to the Holy of Holies: אחזתיו ולא ארפנו (157). And the dread of Havdalah: התרי גוני של חשוכא נאבקים עתה בך, "the two kinds of darkness are wrestling in you now" (158). It is like a prince clinging to his father at the last moment before exile (159).
- **Feelings as angels.** To contemplate one's feelings is שתביט בפני כל מלאכי מעלה מדרגות ובחינות שעוברות דרך לבך ונפשך (162).
- **Seeing the "You".** תראה את "האתה" ואת "מלך העולם"... ותתענג ותתמוגג, "you will see the 'You' and the 'King of the world'... and delight and melt" (177). It is a sense like smell: רק חוש כזה יש לך וחקה היא (178).
- **The worry that turns into longing.** Standing outside God's domain: תתעורר בך דאגה על עצמך, למה באמת אתה עומד חוץ לרשות (185). He envies even the river: אפלו הליכת ומשיכת הנהר גנאי עבודה ומחתל ברצון ד' הוא (186). A Yiddish sigh bursts out: לאמיר פארט נישט זיין פארווארפען פון דיר (187).
- **The leap.** In the Shema: ובשמחה אתה עושה קפיצה אחת דרך המוקד אל האין סוף, "joyfully you make one leap through the pyre into the Infinite", and the body sometimes shudders (188). The longing is to become again לאבר מאברין דמלכא (188).
- **The soul walking at the head.** בשעה שנפשו הולכת אצלו בראש, drawing the whole body after it (192).
- **The soul in song.** מעט מעט תרגיש שנפשך התחילה כבר לנגן בעצמה, "little by little you'll feel your soul has begun to sing by itself" (237). בקולה בוקעת לך נפשך דרך עד למרום (239). The melody carries you: את נפשך נושא הנגון במעיו לשפכה ולקרבה לד' (239). The more moved you are, ויותר תצא נפשך מנרתיקה לעוף אל על, "the more your soul leaves its sheath to fly upward" (240).
- **The wordless stage.** Sometimes no words and no request come, only a feeling you cannot picture. It is like a child calling "Father, Father", and asked what he wants, he answers "מה אתה רוצה בני?". "כלום"" (241). And then: רק נפשך מרננת ומשתפכת ומפלטת רק, "רבש"ע, רבש"ע" (242). Joy and tears cross over: הוא בוכה לפעמים בנגון שמח (243).
- **The trace afterwards.** Is there a longing for the prayer-state, a pain at having left the beauty "I glimpsed"? האם אני מרגיש עתה געגועים למצבי הקודם שהייתי בעת תפלתי (266).
- **The slow dawn.** Glimpses of the world's radiance will come: ראיות הללו אם כי מצערות תהיינה בראשיתן, "small at first", growing clearer and more frequent (338).

**The stages, as the text lays them out.** He does not give a numbered ladder, but the sequence is clear: (1) feel the pain of distance (21). (2) Rouse feeling by any door: worry, joy, sigh, melody (100-124). (3) Pour it into prayer before the pictured Throne (91, 104). (4) Learn to "look at" and give form to what passes through you (142-162). (5) Thought grows strong and holds after the flare (48); the picture falls away (90). (6) Thought without image, and a new sense that sees the "You" in everything (128, 177). He summarises it at 336-338: first your conduct and thoughts change, then prayer grows warmer, then you learn to use joy and brokenness, to "look" at each mitzvah, and to reach some mesirus nefesh in the Shema, and only then does higher sight begin to sparkle.

## 4. The self and God

This text supports the owner's thesis strongly, though in its own terms. Here "the self" means **the soul revealed (גילוי הנפש)**, and the goal is to uncover it. In Bnei Machshava Tova, finding the soul and finding God are the same movement seen from two sides.

- **Where to find Him.** איפה תמצאהו? בך ובכל סביבותיך, "Where will you find Him? In you and all around you" (147). "In you" comes first.
- **Shabbos as returning to oneself.** The ascent of Shabbos is described as והשתדלת לבוא עד נפשך שעזבתה ורחקת ממנה בכל ימות החל, "you tried to come back to your soul, which you had left and gone far from all week" (153). This return is what brings a person to "the cloud where God is" (157).
- **The goal restated as revealing the soul.** ולא גלוי המחשבה לבד הוא זאת, רק גם גלוי הנפש היא (192). The fellowship's whole aim of "good thought" turns out to be the revealing of the soul.
- **The soul already sees.** כי נפשך כשהיא לעצמה רואה היא ראיה קדושה ורק הגוף מעצירה, "your soul, as it is in itself, sees with holy sight; only the body holds it back" (184). Seeing God is not added from outside. It is what the true self does once it is released. The same logic runs through the Rambam's "compel him until he says I want": the inner will is already for God (183).
- **The new faculty was inside all along.** מין מחשבה חדשה שלא ידעת מקדם שהיא נמצאת בך (130). Its root is the soul's own source: כסא הכבוד של שרש וחצב נשמתו (77).
- **Love and fear are the soul's own.** כי באמת כל נפש ישראל יראה ואוהבת את ד' היא, ורק כיון שקבורה במחצלת של קנים, "every Jewish soul fears and loves God; it is only buried in a mat of reeds" (304). This is why he gives no separate method for love and fear: reveal the soul, and they appear (304).
- **Truth as being present in oneself.** Truth is "existence", the soul being in one's acts: והאיש שבכל עניניו מעשיו ומחשבותיו יש מציאות שנפשו נמצאת בהם זהו אמת ותמימות (200). The person who answers by social calculation is described as ואתה עם נפשך חסרים, "you and your soul are missing" (205). The cure is to be oneself before God: תציר בדעתך אלו היית אתה יחידי בעולם (209). Then אז תתגלה לבך ונפשך ורצון עצמי, "then your heart, your soul and your *own will* will be revealed" (211), and that own will is to be God's servant.
- **The false self is the separated self.** The counter-image is equally direct. The only thing outside God is the "I" that made itself separate: ורק אני ברצוני הפרטי הוצאתי את עצמי לדבר נמצא בפני עצמו (182). So the *ego-self* is what hides God, and the *soul-self* is what sees Him. The owner's thesis holds if "self" means the second. The Rebbe is emphatic about the difference: he wants a person to find the soul, and to lose the "thing existing by itself".
- **Words from the self are prayer.** The spontaneous words are מחצב הנפש (240) and ניצוצי נפשך... ממנה נחצבו (274), and each good idea is מין הד של קול נפשו היא (318). The truest prayer is simply the soul's own voice becoming audible.

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

He is careful to warn in *both* directions, and a guide should keep both warnings.

**Warnings against false fire:** - **The habitual self-deceiver cannot even repent**, שגם תשובתו בשקר היא; he imagines he became a tzaddik in an hour (28). He is barred from the group. - **Knowing is not feeling**: ולא שידע בדעתו לבד (21), and "general knowledge does not break the heart" (107). - **Sadness is not brokenness.** שאין זאת שבירת ומרירת הלב רק מין העדר וטמטום, "sadness is not a broken or bitter heart but a kind of absence and dullness" (46). On Yom Kippur night the broken heart is *open*, while sadness leaves one "without heart and without thought". - **Imagination can turn into fantasy.** The questioner's worry, that expanding thought להשליך את האדם לידי הזיה ולעשותו לבעל דמיון שוא (59), is taken seriously. The Raavad-picture is a crutch, never a claim that God has form (89), and it has to fall away (90). He even suggests the Golden Calf came from a wish for an image taken too far, into an actual one (83). - **Melody can open the soul the wrong way.** The same key opens toward frivolity or toward או בשבירת הלב של עצבות ויאוש (235). Great cantors can be far from God (233). - **Talking from the marketplace.** Words without heart are not prayer (120). - **The test that counts is self-rule, not the glow.** אם באמת מושל אני על עצמי עתה יותר מאשר לפנים... ואם לאו ח"ו טעיתי ורמיתי את עצמי (252). The second test is whether a fine purity and longing are left after prayer (266-267). - **Showing off and noise.** כן כל עבודה שיש בה התגלות הנפש מתנגדת היא לשאון ופרסום (326). Sharing ideas only to show cleverness is ruled out (319). He wants no drinking to get drunk (320). - **Pride dressed as holiness.** After the vow one must not think oneself exalted and grow proud, but instead break one's heart (312).

**Warnings against killing the fire (the opposite error):** - **The dry investigators.** Some check every stirring: אם אמת הוא ולא דמיון גרידא, "whether it is true and not mere imagination" (246). He calls them "wise in their own eyes". His answer: כי בין עשרה התלהבויות רמיות תמצא אחת אמתית, "among ten deceptive fervours you will find one true" (247). Those who never let feelings out stay וכל ימיהם רק ריקות ויבשות (247). He says openly that the evil inclination is behind this over-scrupulousness (251). - **Examining pure thought while it is happening is a fall.** ורק אם רוצה הוא לחקר אחרי מחשבתי מה אני חושב ורואה, חקירה זו נפילה היא (78). This is because the examining is done by the "critic" again. - **The inner critic.** יש בה מבקר שמבקר ומגיה את כל מחשבות האדם (74). It רודף ומגרש הוא את כל מחשבה טהורה ממך (76). Much of what feels like "nothing is happening" is actually this critic at work. - **Despair about high levels** comes from laziness (132-133). "From earth to heaven is five hundred years' walk", so don't fall because one leap didn't get you there (335). - **Haste and sluggishness** both block contemplation (148).

**How to tell real from false, in his terms:** 1. Arouse first, then test. The testing is part of the service, and it must never replace the arousal: גם הנסיון עבודה היא אצלו, מתלהב ומנסה... ומפריד את הפסלת מן הסלת (250). 2. The measure is fruit: more self-rule (252), and a trace of purity and longing that remains after prayer (266). 3. If the test fails, keep going, like a sick man with his medicine: כי האם יעזב החולה את תרופתיו אם לא פעלה בראשונה (253). 4. Brokenness is open, and sadness is closed (46).

## 6. Compared with Chabad hisbonenus

Reference: `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`. In brief, Chabad contemplation takes a truth (unity, the order of descent, God's greatness) and unfolds it slowly through Binah, holds it with Daas, and lets it reach the heart. It is addressed to the animal soul. It emphasises duration, and it is wary of feeling that is self-aware or self-generated.

**Where they agree** - **The mind rules the heart, and forgetting God is the root of falling.** For him the whole problem is "you forgot the God who formed you" (29-34), and the fix is strong, clear thought (35). This is close to the Chabad axiom. - **Knowledge is not enough; it must become felt.** "General knowledge doesn't break the heart" (107) matches the Chabad state "grasp without heart". - **One truth, held and repeated.** His daily "everything is Godliness except me" (182-186), repeated until it compels, is recognisably a hisbonenus in the unity of God. It also yields the Chabad-style bittul and its opposite, the pain of *yeshus* (the separate "I"). - **Slowness.** "The hasty man does not contemplate" (148), and read the booklet calmly and more than once (134). This echoes the Chabad emphasis on duration. - **The test is change in conduct.** His self-rule test (252) sits close to the Rashab's test of whether one's dominant trait has changed. - **Two dangers.** Like Chabad, he names sadness as a closing-off, distinct from bitterness (46).

**Where he differs (keep these distinct)** 1. **Imagination and picturing are central tools.** Chabad contemplation works through concepts and worked analogy. He deliberately uses *dimyon*, visualising the Throne, oneself as a son pleading (91), and a detailed death-bed scene (108-115). He defends this with the Raavad against the Rambam (87-90) and calls pure imagination a "spark of prophecy", ניצוץ נבואה (60). The aim is *seeing*, not understanding: לא שתבין בשכל בלבד רק שבאמת תראה (164). 2. **He explicitly downgrades intellectual contemplation as a way to get close.** ומה יועיל לו לאיש שיעצם את עיניו ויתבונן בעבים, "what use is it to close your eyes and contemplate the clouds — will the clouds come down to him?" (129), because "knowledge alone does not truly bring near". The target is a new faculty of sight, not deeper understanding. This is a real difference of aim from hisbonenus. 3. **Feeling first, and from anywhere.** Chabad is wary of arousal that doesn't come from the idea, or that one is aware of. He takes *any* arousal, whether business worry, joy, a sigh, a tune or a drink, as a "key to the soul" (100, 119, 123), and redirects it. He even defends fervour that may be self-deceiving (247). In the Chabad dispute he is nearer R. Aharon of Strashelye's defence of felt arousal than to the Mitteler Rebbe's caution. He goes further than either in making *non-contemplative* emotion the starting point. 4. **Thought and fervour are one.** מחשבה טהורה תלויה בהתעוררות והתלהבות, וההתלהבות במחשבה טהורה (97), and באמת שניהם שהם אחד הם (93). In Chabad contemplation causes the feeling. For him they are one faculty that grows together, and neither is prior. 5. **Don't analyse the state.** In Chabad, being aware of yourself in the state is the subtle obstacle. He agrees that examining pure thought is a fall (78), but his reason is different: the examiner is the worldly critic, not self-regard. And he *requires* "looking" at one's feelings (histaklus, 142-162) as a core practice. That is attention to the feeling's form, which Chabad would treat with more suspicion. 6. **Melody and voice.** Niggun is a primary method (231-245), prayer is aloud (275), and there is spontaneous Yiddish speech (187, 240) and the wordless "Father" or "Ribbono shel Olam" (241-242). Chabad also values niggun, but hisbonenus as a method is silent and conceptual. 7. **Body and posture.** Face the wall, close your eyes, go aside at once, sit in the dark (119, 155, 237, 275). The body is fitted to the state of the soul (155). 8. **Quieting is not the technique here.** Chabad's diagnostic says "settle first". This text does not teach settling. It teaches *rousing*. (Hashkatah appears in his later writings.) 9. **Group and confidant.** The method is built into a fellowship with a personal confidant, a vow, and secrecy (305-332). Hisbonenus is presented as an individual discipline. 10. **The object.** Chabad offers many objects (the lights, the order of descent, the soul). His recurring object is *relational and pictured*: I stand before the Throne as a son (91, 237). The "Seder Hishtalshelus" appears here only as Kabbalah learned *after* one sees (173-176).

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before we try to feel anything holy, tell me what you are already feeling right now, worry or joy or even a sigh. He says every feeling is a door to the soul" (100, 123).
2. "Let's not push the feeling away with food or noise. Stay with it and give it a shape, then look at it. It may be your soul reaching out" (139, 144).
3. "Close your eyes, or turn toward a wall. Picture yourself standing before God like a child before a father who loves him, and say to Him in your own words what is on your heart" (91, 237, 275).
4. "If a tune comes, hum it, even in a whisper. At first you are singing to wake your soul. After a while notice whether it has started singing by itself" (237, 245).
5. "If words stop and only 'Father, Father' or 'Ribbono shel Olam' is left, that is not failure. That is the soul pouring itself out" (241-242).
6. "Don't check in the middle whether it's real. Afterwards we'll ask one question: do you rule yourself a little more today than before?" (78, 250-252).
7. "The picture is a handrail, not the destination. As your thought grows strong it falls away on its own" (89-90).
8. "Say it slowly, several times a day: the whole world is God's, even the air I breathe, and I want to come back in. You are not looking for something far away. You are uncovering the part of you that already sees Him" (147, 184, 286).

**Summary** 1. This is the Rebbe's private rulebook and practice manual for a secret fellowship. It is organised as Goals, Membership, 20 Principles, 14 Daily Instructions and a vow with group rules. 2. Its method is to rouse the soul through any emotion, picture oneself before the Throne (with the Raavad's licence), sing wordless niggun at the wall, "look" at and give form to feelings, and repeat daily that "everything is God except me". 3. The inner states run from melting and running toward the Father, through the soul leaving its sheath in song and the chariot passing through the heart, to a new sense that sees the "You" in everything. 4. Self and God: the goal is גלוי הנפש, and "your soul in itself sees with holy sight" (184). God is found "in you and all around you" (147). Only the separated ego hides Him. 5. Compared with Chabad, it shares mind-over-heart and repetition of one truth. It differs sharply in making imagination, any-source emotion, melody and "seeing" (not understanding) central, and it teaches rousing rather than quieting.
