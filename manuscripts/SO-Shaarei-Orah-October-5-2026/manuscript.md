*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Discourses for Chanukah and Purim. The author's introduction states the aim: to 'light up the soul with the Divine light' ('the way of life'), so that each reader 'may find rest for his soul according to his measure'. The book is built on two kinds of mesirus nefesh (giving one's life for what matters most) and two kinds of light. Chanukah is light from below, from the receiver: the soul's own lamp. Purim is light from above, from the giver, as at Sinai. Around this frame, long Kabbalistic chapters on the menorah, oil, the wick, incense, sacrifices, and the royal garment and horse carry scattered but precise passages on how the soul's light catches, holds or leaps away in a person.

**How much of it is medicine.** some — Most of the text is Kabbalistic cosmology and exegesis, but the lamp/oil/wick psychology, the two wills and the balance of self-nullification with holy pride are real, practical soul-teaching.

**Its model of the person.** The divine soul is a lamp. The body and its natural mind and heart are the wick. Divine arousal is the flame, and the oil is koach mah (the soul's power of self-nullification, rooted in Wisdom). Without the oil, the flame catches and then leaps away. Emotions refine the bad traits only for a while; only Wisdom and self-nullification transform them. Will has two layers: composite will, meaning choice, which rises and falls with feeling, and simple will, meaning acceptance of the yoke (kabbalas ol), which does not change. Under both lies an essential point of Jewishness that is beyond reason and surfaces when needed. Change comes from above (a gift) and from below (the soul's own effort).

**Its philosophy.** Light exists to light up darkness. Before the Essence of the Infinite, darkness and light are alike, so the highest light can dwell exactly in the lowest and darkest place. Every soul is bound to that Essence through Torah and mitzvos. Its light is always present, hidden and surrounding, and never lost, even when it is not felt.

**What it adds.** The lamp-oil-wick psychology explains exactly why real inspiration fails to hold (a lack of self-nullification, not a false feeling). The two wills explain why the yoke is steadier than mood-driven choice. Hidden-not-lost light answers the person who feels nothing despite effort, with 'wanting to want' as the first step. 'Eyes down, heart up' warns that humility without the heart's lift paralyzes. The work also gives a pedagogy of beginnings (generous gifts beyond measure).

## My inspiration flares up and is gone a moment later

A real, bodily stirring toward God in prayer or thought. It even warms the natural heart, then vanishes almost at once, and the person returns to what he was.

**What is really going on.** The flash was genuine, but the oil is missing: koach mah, the self-nullification that binds the soul's flame to the wick of the body. Without it, the light does not take hold in the natural mind and emotions.

**Program: Oil for the wick: build the power of self-nullification so the flame holds.** (1) Accept that the flash of feeling is real but will leap away without oil. (2) Draw on koach mah, the soul's self-nullification rooted in Wisdom, through Divine understanding, so that it reaches the body. (3) Let it humble the heart's coarseness until the craving falls away, the broken heart. (4) Rely on Wisdom, not emotion alone, to transform the traits lastingly. *When:* Contemplation in prayer and Torah study as a steady practice; the work gives no fixed length. *For:* Someone whose inspiration is real but short-lived, or whose good traits vanish in daily business. *Not for:* Someone depressed or self-despising; 'breaking the heart' and 'soul like dust' can deepen harm. `SO·Q09` · `SO·Q10` · `SO·Q04` · `SO·Q03` · `SO·Q11`

**Reframe.** From *"My good moments don't last, so they were fake."* to **The flash was real. It leaps away for lack of oil, the self-nullification that holds the flame to the wick.** By locating the failure in the oil rather than the flame, the person keeps faith in his experience and works on the oil. `SO·Q09` · `SO·Q03` · `SO·Q04`

## I work hard at it and feel nothing; my heart won't move

Long effort to arouse love, fear or repentance yields no feeling at all. Yet at other times, without any preparation, strong arousal comes on its own.

**What is really going on.** All love, fear and repentance are present in the soul in hidden, surrounding form. The body's coarseness and sins act as a screen that keeps them from showing. The light is hidden, not lost.

**Program: When the heart stays closed.** (1) Do not conclude that hope is lost; the hidden light surfaces in its time. (2) Know that it may surface at set times, in moments of crisis, or suddenly without preparation. (3) Do a great kindness, such as reviving a poor and crushed person; the work says this merit brings the soul's light out of hiding. *When:* Over long stretches when effort brings no feeling. *For:* Someone who has tried hard and feels nothing. *Not for:* Not a technique to force feeling on schedule. `SO·Q15` · `SO·Q13` · `SO·Q16` · `SO·Q14`

**Program: Kindle the wanting first (the desire to desire).** (1) Do not wait for love or fear to arrive. First awaken a small longing to be close to God, even with no feeling yet. (2) If even that is absent, want to want: wish that you had real will and love. (3) Ask for it in prayer, in the sense of 'unite our hearts to love and fear Your name'. (4) Let this first warmth underlie every trait, even quiet love, so that it does not fall back into coldness. *When:* At the opening of prayer and whenever service feels cold; brief, repeated daily. *For:* Someone whose prayer or practice feels cold and lifeless. *Not for:* Someone who takes the lack of feeling as proof he is spiritually dead; lead with F01 first. `SO·Q17` · `SO·Q18` · `SO·Q19`

**Program: The yoke: act without feeling.** (1) Take on the yoke of Heaven as a servant does, apart from whether you want to. (2) Do the good deed even when the heart feels no drawing to God. (3) Hold back from wrong even without felt fear, out of not wanting to rebel. (4) Take the joy of the mitzvah from the completed act itself. *When:* Every day, especially on days with no inner arousal; continuous for life. *For:* Someone whose practice rises and falls with mood, or who feels only the bare act remains. *Not for:* Scrupulous or self-coercive people; the yoke steadies practice and must not suppress emotion or trauma. `SO·Q22` · `SO·Q23` · `SO·Q24` · `SO·Q21`

**Reframe.** From *"I've lost it. Nothing moves me anymore, so there must be nothing in me."* to **All of it is present in your soul, hidden and surrounding, lacking nothing. The body screens it; it has not gone out, and it surfaces in its time.** The soul's light rests on a person constantly, and its hiddenness comes from the body's screen, not from absence. That removes despair, which the work says is unwarranted ('not that hope is lost'), and keeps the person turned toward the light. `SO·Q13` · `SO·Q14` · `SO·Q15`

## My prayer is cold and dead, just words on my lips

Even calm, gentle love has no life in it and drops away at once. Praise said only 'from the lip outward' is called the prayer of the dead.

**What is really going on.** Every good trait needs a prior spark of desire, the gold of the menorah. Without that first longing there is no vitality at all.

**Program: Kindle the wanting first (the desire to desire).** (1) Do not wait for love or fear to arrive. First awaken a small longing to be close to God, even with no feeling yet. (4) Let this first warmth underlie every trait, even quiet love, so that it does not fall back into coldness. *When:* At the opening of prayer and whenever service feels cold; brief, repeated daily. *For:* Someone whose prayer or practice feels cold and lifeless. *Not for:* Someone who takes the lack of feeling as proof he is spiritually dead; lead with F01 first. `SO·Q17` · `SO·Q18` · `SO·Q19`

So the person can act from the level he can reach now. `SO·Q18` · `SO·Q22` · `SO·Q24`

## Some days I choose right and some days I don't; my commitment keeps shifting

His walk in God's ways rises and falls with how stirred his heart is. Sometimes he wants good, sometimes the opposite.

**What is really going on.** Choice is composite will, rooted in pleasure and reasons, so by nature it changes. There is a simple will beneath it, kabbalas ol, that does not change.

**Program: Steady the choice by toil in love and fear.** (1) See that choice depends on the heart's arousal and so shifts. (2) Set heart and mind to toil at arousing love and fear. This toil makes choosing good constant. (3) When desire for closeness weakens, strengthen yourself again, each time, for all your days. *When:* Ongoing, whenever resolve slackens. *For:* Someone who wants his choice for good to stop swinging. *Not for:* Someone exhausted by effort that yields nothing; pair with G03 and G05. `SO·Q20` · `SO·Q21`

**Program: The yoke: act without feeling.** (1) Take on the yoke of Heaven as a servant does, apart from whether you want to. (2) Do the good deed even when the heart feels no drawing to God. (3) Hold back from wrong even without felt fear, out of not wanting to rebel. (4) Take the joy of the mitzvah from the completed act itself. *When:* Every day, especially on days with no inner arousal; continuous for life. *For:* Someone whose practice rises and falls with mood, or who feels only the bare act remains. *Not for:* Scrupulous or self-coercive people; the yoke steadies practice and must not suppress emotion or trauma. `SO·Q22` · `SO·Q23` · `SO·Q24` · `SO·Q21`

## All that's left of my spiritual life is going through the motions

No light or living feeling of love and fear, only the bare deed and the joy of having done it.

**What is really going on.** The work reads this as the state of the last generation. It does not count it as failure: the bare act done under the yoke reaches higher than the good oil of feeling.

**Program: The yoke: act without feeling.** (1) Take on the yoke of Heaven as a servant does, apart from whether you want to. (2) Do the good deed even when the heart feels no drawing to God. (3) Hold back from wrong even without felt fear, out of not wanting to rebel. (4) Take the joy of the mitzvah from the completed act itself. *When:* Every day, especially on days with no inner arousal; continuous for life. *For:* Someone whose practice rises and falls with mood, or who feels only the bare act remains. *Not for:* Scrupulous or self-coercive people; the yoke steadies practice and must not suppress emotion or trauma. `SO·Q22` · `SO·Q23` · `SO·Q24` · `SO·Q21`

**Reframe.** From *"All that's left is empty deeds. So a deed without feeling is not empty; it completes what feeling only intended. `SO·Q24`

## Who am I to serve God? I'm too small for this

*Its own term:* שפלות ... באומרו מי אנכי

Humility turned into paralysis. He cannot bring himself to serve at all because of his own lowliness.

**What is really going on.** The mind's self-nullification ('eyes downward') without the heart's upward lift. The heart must rise in its own fire, or no service happens.

**Program: Eyes down, heart up: balance self-nullification with holy pride.** (1) Let the mind stay humble ('eyes downward'). (2) Let the heart rise in fire and longing ('his heart was lifted up in the ways of God'), or 'who am I' will stop all service. (3) Afterward, temper that lift through Torah study, which nullifies the self. (4) Keep only a small portion of self-feeling (the 'eighth of an eighth') as a crown; more becomes real arrogance. *When:* Fervour in prayer, then Torah study after it; daily. *For:* The paralyzed-humble person and the self-inflated person alike, each at a different step. *Not for:* Do not stress the humility steps to someone already crushed. `SO·Q25` · `SO·Q28` · `SO·Q27` · `SO·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"Real humility means feeling I am nothing, and who am I to serve?"* to **Humility of the mind must be joined by the heart's lift; without that lift, humility stops service.** Mind is water and heart is fire. The heart joins the mind only by first rising, so a measured holy self-feeling is needed for any service at all. `SO·Q25`

## Starting is so hard; I can't get myself going

He is small relative to what he is asked to hold, and the beginning feels beyond him.

**What is really going on.** Every beginning needs chinuch (education): gifts and strength beyond the normal measure, given from a higher place, until the receiver can go on his own.

**Program: Chinuch: generous gifts for beginnings.** (1) Accept that 'all beginnings are hard' and the beginner is small next to what he must hold. (2) Give, or receive, encouragement and gifts beyond the ordinary measure, from someone higher, as a father gives his son treats on the way to school. (3) Continue this for some days until the person goes on his own without them. *When:* At the start of any new path; temporary by design. *For:* Beginners, and those who guide them. *Not for:* Not a permanent crutch; the aim is going on alone. `SO·Q06` · `SO·Q07`

## I've been humiliated and brought down to the dust

Utter lowliness and exposure, like the Jews in Haman's year.

**What is really going on.** The lowliness itself is what let the Essence settle within them: the highest height rests exactly in the lowest depth.

**Reframe.** From *"I've been brought so low; I'm worthless."* to **The highest light, by its nature, dwells precisely in the lowest place.** Height seeks depth ('the depth above rests in the depth below'), so the low state becomes a vessel where the Essence settles. `SO·Q31`

## The pain floods my mind and I can't stop crying

Pain beyond measure makes thought rush uncontrollably until tears break out.

**What is really going on.** Such tears come from the absence of delight and union. So they come from the very essence of the soul's delight, in its inverted form.

**Reframe.** From *"My tears show I'm broken."* to **Tears of pain come from the very essence of the soul's delight, in its inverted form; and the pain that you have not yet reached the light is itself a reaching.** Pain beyond measure and delight beyond measure share one root. This lets the person hold grief as a sign of depth rather than of ruin. `SO·Q29` · `SO·Q32`

## I'm being battered by trouble with children, health and money

Blows to one's children, life and livelihood.

**What is really going on.** In one parenthetical passage the work says such blows can make the soul's light rise into the body, bringing repentance with a broken heart. It is an interpretation, not a prescription.

**Reframe.** From *"These blows mean I've been abandoned."* to **Blows to children, life and livelihood can make the soul's light rise into the body, in a broken heart that turns toward God.** Something that pounds the body can let the soul's light rise into it. Offer this only if the person himself reaches toward such meaning. `SO·Q12`

## Cautions

- Breaking the heart and 'my soul as dust' (G02, Q10-Q11) can deepen depression or self-hatred; the work itself warns that lowliness paralyzes (Q25). - Reading blows to children, health or livelihood as bringing repentance (Q12) must never be offered as an explanation to someone suffering. - Forcing oneself against one's nature under the yoke can feed scrupulosity or emotional suppression; use it to steady practice, not to override pain. - The mesirus nefesh passages concern martyrdom under religious coercion; never frame self-harm or self-erasure as devotion. - The work frames the essential point and soul qualities as belonging to Israel; adapt for non-Jews without falsely claiming the text says so, or denying them worth. - 'The last generation lacks light' (Q24) is a consolation for bare practice; do not use it to lower hope or excuse despair. - Tears that 'sweeten judgments' should not become encouragement to dwell in grief; refer anyone in severe distress to real help.
