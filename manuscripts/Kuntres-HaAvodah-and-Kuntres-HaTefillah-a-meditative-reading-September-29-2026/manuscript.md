## 1. The two books as one meditative journey

These two small books answer one question the Rashab's chassidim kept asking him: how do you pray? Kuntres HaTefillah (1900) opens with it in so many words: "each one of our people asks and seeks: how does one pray?" (Tefillah line 41). Kuntres HaAvodah, written ten years later, is the long answer. The same complaint stands behind both: "we learn a great deal of Chassidus and we pray, and nothing in our traits changes for the better; they are as strong as the day we were born" (Tefillah line 50). The books walk a person from that place, knowing much and unchanged, to the place the Avodah names in one phrase: the soul was bound to the body "to make everything one" (Avodah line 230). The Tefillah ends with the same wish in plainer words: "may our heart be whole and true with the Lord our God in every single detail" (Tefillah line 122).

The road has a clear order. First, a floor you do not have to earn: "Modeh ani", the thanks that hands over your whole self, "very near to every Jew" (Avodah line 29); and awe, which is a hidden feeling of the Essence itself that the soul "never forgets even for one moment" (Avodah line 107). Then the gate: giving yourself away, the yoke of heaven, "the gate to God" (Avodah line 96). Then the real work, which is love, because only love binds the soul to God and refines the animal soul, the self that wants food, comfort and honour (Avodah lines 26, 125). Love comes only from contemplation that is particular: the life in this creature, God filling and surrounding the worlds, creation from nothing, held in detail "an hour and more" until the light shines in the mind and flows by itself into the heart (Tefillah line 57). The love has two forms. Like water, it clings: you feel the life in your own body and see that "the world lives from the Godly light" (Avodah line 129). Like fire, it burns: you see how far you are, and the holy fire consumes the coarse one (Avodah line 149). Under both lies a will with no reason at all, the soul's nature to run home to the Essence it was never parted from (Avodah line 163). At the top of the Tefillah stands a person who gazes until "the inwardness of his heart sees and feels the essence of Godliness as it is, truly above comprehension" and he becomes "like a silent stone" (Tefillah lines 44-45). He stands before an Essence "found in every single place", where hiding and revealing do not apply (Tefillah line 91). And then the road turns back down, to the one trait that is yours to fix (Avodah line 239), the words said slowly (Tefillah line 108), the minute of prayer added before business (Tefillah line 115), and the truth that "comes by stages" (Avodah line 264).

The contemplation these books ask for is thinking-through, slow and exact. They trust detail and distrust heat. Their sharpest word is for the quick excitement of a general idea, "He fills and surrounds all worlds" felt all at once: "a false imagination" (Avodah line 204; Tefillah line 49). They name other counterfeits too: the loud cry that pulls out a flash of the Godly soul and changes nothing (Avodah line 253), the clever new reading of a verse during prayer (Tefillah line 54), wanting every high thing at once (Avodah line 264), and learning that swells the self until it becomes "a deadly poison" (Tefillah line 81). They also ask for other things besides thinking. The Avodah asks for remembering all day, for awe (Avodah line 101), and for the cry "like one fleeing from death to life" that comes out of the deepest narrowness (Avodah line 182). The Tefillah asks for bitterness only at the right hour, at midnight, in private, and then joy (Tefillah lines 89-90); for compassion on your own soul, "this is easy for a person" (Tefillah line 90); and for standing before Him in the Amidah and saying "You" (Tefillah line 91). The images are homely: a ladder on the ground, a servant used to his master's yoke, a polished mirror, a traveller whose every step toward is a step away, a fire eating a fire, two friends who meet after years, water under the earth, a king's house swept clean. The books are demanding, and they are also kind. The cold feeling when you start to think carefully is not failure, "let him not worry at all" (Avodah line 226). No one is unfit, "there is no such thing" (Avodah line 228). It is "near to anyone who has a mind and a heart" (Tefillah line 67), and "one who comes to purify is helped" in every detail (Avodah line 236).

## Part One: Kuntres HaAvodah (the Tract on Service)

*Kuntres HaAvodah has seven chapters. They are long, so this reading follows the movements inside each chapter, which the file's own summaries (kitzurim) and turns of argument mark: 26 units for the tract and 4 for the Rayatz's letter.*

*Skipped, with nothing to contemplate: the title page, garbled copyright page, the Rebbe's preface of 5706, the table of contents and the Rayatz's covering letter (lines 1-22); the editor's source notes scattered through the tract (for example lines 30-31, 35, 43, 52, 59, 67-68, 75, 79, 95, 104, 116-118, 130-131), which were read and passed over inside their units. Most of chapter 2's practical advice on guarding the senses is gathered into unit a7 rather than read line by line. The letter breaks off mid-sentence at line 306.*

## Chapter 1 (i): all the toil is for love; the ladder of prayer (lines 23-28)

**The movement.** It gives prayer one aim and a direction: up a ladder, from the ground to the soul's own root. A mind that feels prayer as a list of words to get through is turned into a mind that is climbing.

> היינו התקשרות עצמות הנשמה בחי' נשמתא לנשמתא כמקור חוצבה בעצמית אוא"ם ב"ה
> *That is, the binding of the essence of the soul, the soul of the soul, as its source is hewn in the Essence of the Infinite, blessed be He.*

*OCR: The scan reads בעצמית for בעצמות and אוא"ם for אוא"ס.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The heading says the whole service of the heart has two jobs: to bind the soul to Godliness, and to refine the animal soul, the self that wants food, rest and honour (lines 23-25). The first sentence of the chapter says that all the work of prayer is "to come to the trait of love" (line 26). Then step by step. Tefillah, prayer, comes from a word meaning to join, the way a broken clay pot is joined (line 27). Prayer is also a ladder, "set on the ground, and its head reaches heaven". The soul's three levels, nefesh (the life that acts), ruach (the heart) and neshamah (the mind), climb it one rung at a time (lines 27-28). Then to the root. At the top the ladder does not reach a place. It reaches the soul's essence, bound to where it was cut from, the Essence of the Infinite. The soul is not joining something foreign. It is going back to its quarry. Bring it home. Your siddur on the table, your feet on the floor. The ladder stands where you stand, "on the ground". Each rung is a real step you take this morning, not a leap. The picture holds and then breaks. A ladder is climbed and left behind. Here, the Rashab says, the lower levels "also rise and are included" in the binding (line 27). Nothing of you is left standing at the bottom.

**From inside.** A sense of direction. Prayer stops being a chore to finish and becomes a way up. And a quiet dignity: you were cut from something, and it is still where you belong.

**At the edge.** The top of the ladder is the Essence itself (line 27). Later the chapter says that only the soul's essence reaches there, in the bowing of the Amidah (line 53). Here it is named, not yet walked.

**Into the day.** Before you start to pray, set your feet flat and say once: "The ladder stands here."

## Chapter 1 (ii): nefesh, the thanks that gives everything (lines 29-32)

**The movement.** It lowers the bar and raises it at the same time. The first rung is only acknowledgment, yet it hands over the whole self, and so it is truer than many high insights.

> הנתינה לאלקות בכלל (שלמעלה ממדרי' פרטיות) זה קרוב מאד לכאו"א מישראל
> *Giving oneself to Godliness in general (which is above particular levels) is very near to every Jew.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The lowest level of the soul serves by acknowledging (hoda'ah) and by keeping the commandments in deed (line 29). The first words of the day, "Modeh ani lefanecha", "I thank You", belong to this rung (line 29). Then step by step. The Rashab notices something odd about this thanks. It is not about any particular Godly level. It is "that with the whole of his existence he gives himself to Godliness" (line 29). A particular insight may be above you. The whole of you is never above you. Then to the root. So the lowest rung has an advantage: the giving is "with all his being" (line 29), and this thanks is "the foundation of the service of the whole day" (line 94). Above it is the higher acknowledgment, that all before Him is as nothing. Only lofty souls truly grasp that, "but in every Jew there must be this bitul (self-setting-aside)", at least as acknowledgment (line 32). Bring it home. You wake. Before you know what day it is, before the phone, two words. You do not have to understand anything to say them. You only have to be there. The picture breaks. Thanks usually thanks for something. Here the thanks is not for a gift. It is the one who was given, giving himself back.

**From inside.** A small, clean feeling of being handed over. Less weight: you do not have to earn the first step. A nearness that does not depend on how clever or holy you felt yesterday.

**At the edge.** It points at the higher unity, "all before Him counts as nothing" (line 32), and says you can hold it without grasping it. True knowing of it is "the sight of the eye of the intellect" (line 32). Most people will have it only as acknowledgment, and that is enough to start.

**Into the day.** Tomorrow, say "Modeh ani" slowly and mean only this: here is all of me.

**Ladder.** L06 (Not a thing on my own) (on a low day, L01 (Under everything, the spark) instead)

## Chapter 1 (iii): ruach, the life in the world known in detail (lines 33-38)

**The movement.** It sends the mind outward to the world and then back into the heart. Knowing becomes work: slow, particular, until the heart wants what the mind sees.

> והוא בהאור וחיות אלקי הבא בכחי" התלבשות בעולם להוות ולהחיות את העולמות
> *It is the Godly light and life that comes clothed in the world, to bring the worlds into being and give them life.*

*OCR: כחי" is the scan's slip for בחי'.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The middle level of the soul, ruach, is the heart: love and awe you can feel, born of thinking about God's greatness (lines 37-38). Its object is what can actually be grasped: the life clothed in the world, memale, God filling all worlds (line 38). Then step by step. The work is "to weary oneself with toil of soul and toil of flesh" in this grasp (line 38). Not with general knowledge, but with knowledge "particular and deep", until the matter is felt well in mind and heart and the heart truly wants Godliness (line 38). Then to the root. The Rashab says that this, and not some higher rung, is the service of the heart of which it says "know today and return it to your heart", precisely (line 38). It is "the measure of every person": each one is obliged to it (line 38). Bring it home. Pick one thing on your table, a cup of water or a piece of bread. It exists right now. Something is keeping it in being. Stay with this one thing longer than you want to. The picture breaks. You can feel your own body's life from inside. You cannot feel a table's life that way. So the Rashab asks for grasp, thought through, not a feeling you make up.

**From inside.** The world stops being scenery. Things feel held. And the heart starts to lean toward what holds them, a want that is not for the thing but for its life.

**At the edge.** It does not go past its rung, and says so. Its bitul is "the bitul of the something" (line 38): you are still there, moved. The quieter bitul belongs to the next level.

**Into the day.** Once today, before you eat, look at the food for a slow count of ten and say: "This is being kept alive."

**Ladder.** L02 (Being said, right now); L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Chapter 1 (iv): neshamah, setting the pictures down, and the limit (lines 39-49)

**The movement.** It takes the mind to the edge of its own pictures. You understand through images, then set them down and reach for the thing itself, and find that even then a thin garment stays on.

> ואח"כ שולל ומפשיט ומניח את הענינים שבהם הוא משיג את הענין ובא אל עצם הדבר המושג
> *And afterward he negates, strips and sets down the matters through which he grasped it, and comes to the very thing he grasped.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Neshamah is the mind's own level (line 39). It grasps the Godly matter "stripped of the garments of understanding", the thing itself. The sign is that "all the natural senses fall away" from the cleaving (line 40). The heart moves too, but without noise; it follows by itself (line 40). Then step by step. How do you get there? First you learn the matter in its pictures: memale "like the soul that fills the body", sovev like a general will (line 42). You understand it in detail, with explanations and parables. Then you take the pictures away, one by one. Then to the root. What is left is not nothing. It is the matter as it is in Godliness, "which is not at all in such a form" (line 44). You know how it works, and you know it is not like that there. Bring it home. Think of someone you love. First the picture: the face, the voice, the way they laugh. Then set the picture down. Something that is them is still there, and no picture holds it. The picture breaks, and the Rashab breaks it himself: "in truth he cannot grasp a Godly matter with no garment at all" (line 46). A fine garment stays. So even this is not yet the light shining openly.

**From inside.** Quiet. A cleaner, thinner feeling than excitement. The body goes still without being told. A holding-on that does not shake.

**At the edge.** It walks right to the edge: the garment thins but does not go (line 46). Beyond it is the one who "has in his essence a sense for Godliness" (lines 47-48), and beyond him, the soul's essence (line 50).

**Into the day.** Take one idea from this week's learning. Say it once with its picture, then once without.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Chapter 1 (v): the soul's essence; 'within the thing' and 'the thing'; pouring out the soul (lines 50-55)

**The movement.** It carries the mind from the life inside things to what is lifted above them, and then past knowing, into a union where no one is left standing apart.

> ובכ"ז נעשה בחי' יחוד ודביקות נפשו בעצמות אוא"ם ב"ה
> *And through all this his soul comes to union and cleaving with the Essence of the Infinite, blessed be He.*

*OCR: אוא"ם is the scan's slip for אוא"ס.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The Rashab reads a line of the prayers: "put understanding in our hearts, to understand". To understand is "a thing from within a thing". "Within a thing" is memale, God filling each thing. "The thing" is sovev, God surrounding all (line 51). Then step by step. By contemplating how He fills, you come to how He is wondrous and lifted above all worlds. That is a knowing by negation, knowing what He is not, with the certainty of "the eye of the intellect" (line 51). Then to the root. It becomes "union and cleaving of his soul with the Essence", in "bitul in existence itself". That is "his head reaches heaven", in the bowing of the Amidah (lines 51, 53). Bring it home. Prayer is called "pouring out the soul" (line 54). Water poured from a cup does not keep the cup's shape. The picture breaks. Pouring goes down; this pouring goes up, "My soul I lift to You". And it is fire as much as water: "the flames of the fire of longing and thirst to be included in the Essence" (line 55).

**From inside.** A pull upward that feels less like effort and more like being drawn. For a moment the "I" is thin.

**At the edge.** This is the edge itself: bitul in existence (line 53). The Rashab says true knowledge of what surrounds belongs to souls of Atzilut, but in every soul it is in the soul's essence, "the Godliness in the soul, above understood intellect" (line 51).

**Into the day.** At the bow in the Amidah, or any time you bow your head, think only: "Head in heaven, feet on the ground."

**Ladder.** L11 (Where you and He meet) (stop if it brings unreality or fear)

## Chapter 1 (vi): the second aim; the day's words become a crown (lines 55-64)

**The movement.** It turns attention back down, to the ordinary speech and strength spent all day, and shows that prayer lifts them. Nothing of the day is wasted.

> והן אותיות הכאים מקרב ולב עמוק מהקירוב שאחר הריחוק … הנה אותיות אלו נעשים כתר לקונו
> *They are letters that come from the depth of the heart, from nearness after distance... these letters become a crown for their Maker.*

*OCR: הכאים is the scan's slip for הבאים.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Prayer stands in place of a sacrifice: "when a man offers from you", the offering of the animal soul (line 55). So prayer has two sides: the heart's intention, which is the Godly soul rising, and the speech, which is the refining of the animal soul (lines 55-56). Then step by step. Torah's letters come from above and draw the Infinite down (line 58). Prayer's letters are a person's own, like bricks made by hand (line 58). They come "from nearness after distance": all day you used your natural powers for your own needs, and then you come close in prayer with those same powers, "my heart and my flesh sing" (line 60). Then to the root. The sages say an angel ties crowns for God from Israel's prayers. The Rashab says the crown is made of these very letters, refined, like jewels that shine once they are polished (line 60). Bring it home. Your voice this morning is the voice that argued with a driver yesterday. The same throat. Now it says "Blessed are You". The picture breaks. Jewels are dug up already made. These are made out of ordinary stone by the work itself. And both aims come about through love (line 60).

**From inside.** Relief about the day: it was not a loss. A new respect for your own speech. A wish to bring more of yourself in.

**At the edge.** It does not go past; this is the way back down. It notes that prayer's bitul is above understood reason, so in the Amidah the words come "by themselves" (line 60).

**Into the day.** Pick one word you will say often today ("thank you", "yes") and say it once as a prayer.

## Chapter 2 (i): guarding the eyes, ears and mouth (lines 65-76)

**The movement.** It closes some doors so that a room can be quiet. The contemplating mind finds it has been flooded all day by what it saw and heard, and is shown that the eye can also lift.

> מראות עיניהם של הצדיקים מעלים אותס לגדולה והוא ענין לאפתכלא ביקרא דמלכא
> *The sights of the righteous raise them to greatness, and that is gazing at the King's glory.*

*OCR: אותס is the scan's slip for אותם; לאפתכלא for לאסתכלא.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Awe is the start of the service and its foundation; without it nothing holds (lines 65-66). Its first form is guarding: shutting the eyes from seeing evil, the ears from hearing it, the mouth from speaking it (lines 66, 74-76). Then step by step. The eye is where delight wakes (line 66). A person far from any wrong still looks, "like cold spirit", feels nothing, and yet the look leaves a mark (line 72). The Rashab says why: a hidden delight of the soul is tied to what it looks at. Then to the root. The same eye can do the opposite. The righteous look and it raises them: "gazing at the King's glory", the soul cleaving to the Essence of delight (line 72). The eye is not the enemy. It is a door. Bring it home. Your screen. Scroll for one minute, then notice what is still with you an hour later. Then look at a face you love, or at the sky out the window, with one thought: whose glory is this? The picture breaks. A door is shut once. This one is shut again and again, and the Rashab says the hard part is only at first: "in time he becomes used to it", and then "he finds rest for his soul from many evils and confusions, and then he can do his service" (line 70).

**From inside.** Less noise. A room of your own inside, with a door. Some dignity: you choose what comes in.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there by itself. The edge is only glimpsed, in the King's glory (line 72).

**Into the day.** Once today, when your eyes drift somewhere they have no need to go, lower them and say "the King's glory" instead.

## Chapter 2 (ii): the yoke; the servant who always feels his Master; the polished soul (lines 77-92)

**The movement.** It swaps a picture of freedom for a picture of belonging. The self that does whatever it likes is shown as a man lying on his side at home; the yoke is being always in the Master's room.

> וכמו"כ הוא בעול מ"ש שנרגש בו תמיד שיש לו אדון עליו
> *And so with the yoke of heaven: it is always felt in him that he has a Master over him.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Throwing off the yoke is being "free with oneself", as the Rashab puts it in Yiddish, "very free with himself, he feels no yoke on him" (line 76). His picture: alone at home a man lies on his side and says whatever comes to mind; sitting with great people he watches how he sits and speaks (line 76). Then step by step. A servant is always gathered in because of his master's yoke. Even far from the master he does not leave it, and he is so used to it that it is not heavy at all (line 78). The yoke of heaven is a hidden feeling of "the Master of all the earth, who makes His kingship one over us in particular" (line 76). Then to the root. The Godly soul is "like a polished thing that takes light inside and shines" (line 92). What dims it is crookedness, going the crooked way of the animal soul; that is what covers the mirror (lines 91-92). Bring it home. Notice where you "lie on your side" inside: the moment you are sure no one is watching. The yoke is knowing Someone is there. The picture breaks. A human master is somewhere else. This Master is felt from within, and the yoke frees: before the light of the kingship of heaven the shells (klipot) run away (line 89).

**From inside.** Being gathered. Less spill. Stray thoughts lose their grip: when one comes, you push it off "with both hands" and turn away (line 89).

**At the edge.** It does not reach there. This is the base of the ladder, not its top.

**Into the day.** When you are alone today and about to "lie on your side" inside, sit up once and say: "There is a Master here."

## Chapter 2 (iii): the gate; giving oneself (lines 93-99)

**The movement.** It says where to begin when nothing works: not with understanding but with giving yourself. That is the gate.

> וז"ש זה השער לה' דקבעומ"ש והו"עץ היראה היא השער והפתח לעלות אל ה'
> *This is "this is the gate to God": the acceptance of the yoke of heaven, awe, is the gate and the door to go up to God.*

*OCR: והו"עץ is the scan's slip for והו"ע.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A person whose habits have hardened cannot start with understanding and heart-feeling; he is not yet a vessel for them (line 94). So "the first of everything is to accept the yoke of heaven, to leave the matters he is used to by nature, and to give himself to Godliness" (line 94). Then step by step. The Rashab tells a story. One of the great chassidim, on first coming to the Alter Rebbe, made himself a rule: "whatever he wants by nature, he shall not do" (line 94). The leaving should be because it is against God's will, and it holds best when some light is felt in the soul. Then to the root. This giving is "the foundation of all the service", as "Modeh ani" is the foundation of the day (line 94). It makes a person fit for everything, even the highest levels. "With this Aaron shall come into the holy": through this gate one goes in, rising after rising (line 96). Bring it home. Pick one small thing you want right now and do not need: the coffee before prayer, the next episode. Not forever. Today. The picture breaks. A gate is passed through once and left behind. This gate stays with you: the yoke is constant, all day (line 127).

**From inside.** A strange lightness after the giving up. The animal soul's thickness lifts a little, so the Godly soul is less covered (line 94).

**At the edge.** It does not reach there; it is the door, and says so.

**Into the day.** Do not do one thing today only because you want it. Say inside: "For You."

## Chapter 3 (i): awe rests on a feeling of the Essence (lines 100-107)

**The movement.** It shows that the deepest thing is not earned but already there. Love has to be built by knowing. Awe is a feeling of the Essence that the soul never forgets for a moment.

> שהאלקות בכלל נרגש בנפשו תמיד שאינו נשכח ממנו אפילו רגעץ אחד … והוא באמת הרגש בחי' עצמות אוא"ס ב"ה שלמעלה ממדריגות פרטיות
> *Godliness in general is felt in his soul always, never forgotten even for one moment... and it is truly a feeling of the Essence of the Infinite, blessed be He, above particular levels.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Awe must be in everyone, but it is not the work of prayer (line 100). It comes from remembering all day, not from contemplating in prayer (line 101). Some people are simply born with it. They cannot look at what is forbidden: "it's not allowed", they say in Yiddish (line 105). No one is born loving God (line 105). Then step by step. Why? Love is closeness that comes from particular knowledge of Godliness (line 107). Awe does not hang on any particular knowledge. It hangs on a Godly feeling: that Godliness in general is felt in the soul always. Then to the root. That general feeling is "truly a feeling of the Essence", only hidden (line 107). Love is in His lights and revelations. Awe, even lower awe, is in the Essence. "So awe in general is bitul, and above love" (line 107). Bring it home. Think of the thing you would never do, even alone, even if no one would ever know. You did not reason your way to that. That "no" is older than your reasons. The picture breaks. We think the highest must be the hardest to reach. Here the highest is the plainest, and it hides by being plain.

**From inside.** A surprised respect for your own plain "no". Something solid under all the ups and downs. It is not a feeling that comes and goes.

## Chapter 3 (ii): no place empty of Him; He sees (lines 108-119)

**The movement.** It turns the gaze around. You are not only looking; you are seen, everywhere, and that feeling is near to everyone.

**At the edge.** It points to bitul "like the sun's radiance in the sun" (line 113), and leaves the step to the next passage.

**The movement.** It lifts awe to its top: the Infinite as it is, here, below. Then it turns back and says that this is above prayer's task, and prayer's own work is love.

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Higher awe is "the very bitul, bitul in existence", drawn from the Essence of Godliness, "great and complete, and all before Him counts as nothing" (lines 120-121). Then step by step. It also comes from a contemplation taught elsewhere: "the Infinite is found below as above" (line 121). Binding the soul and refining the animal soul come through love, so "all the work and toil of prayer is to come to love" (line 125). Bring it home. This floor, this hour, is not a lesser copy of somewhere better. The Infinite is here as it is there. The picture breaks. "Below" and "above" are words of place, and the Infinite has no place. **At the edge.** Here: bitul in existence, from the Essence (lines 120-121), tied to the Amidah (line 113).

## Chapter 4 (i): love like water; the world lives from the Godly light (lines 128-135)

**The movement.** It brings the mind close. Like water, this love runs down and clings. You feel the life in your own body, and then see that the world has that kind of life too.

> *It is felt and seen by him how the world lives from the Godly light.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There are two loves: one like water, "remember Abraham, who was drawn after You like water", and one like flames of fire (lines 128-129). Water-love is closeness and cleaving. It comes from contemplating the Godly light and life clothed in the worlds, "You give life to them all", in each creature in particular (line 129). Then step by step. "From my flesh I see God": every person feels in himself that he has a life keeping him alive (line 129). The world is called "a great body". Think this through deeply and "it is felt and seen how the world lives from the Godly light" (line 129). Then to the root. Then the preciousness of Godliness is felt, and the soul clings, until all it wants is Godliness. It grows when you see that Godliness is the life of the world and the life of your own soul: "My soul, I desire You", because "You, God, are my soul and my true life" (line 129). As a man longs for his life, "so I long for the Infinite light, to draw it into me through Torah" (line 132). Bring it home. Put your hand on your arm. It is warm. You did not make the warmth. Now look at the tree outside the window the same way. The picture breaks. A soul in a body is in one body. The light in the world is in each thing "according to its nature". And beyond both is the love "like a son", from "You are truly our Father", which is felt "in a wholly other way" (line 134).

**From inside.** Warmth, nearness, liking. A wish to learn more. Even in physical things "the end of his will is the Godliness in them" (line 134).

**At the edge.** It points to the Father-love, "inner and essential" (line 134), and does not go there.

**Into the day.** Drink one glass of water slowly today, thinking: "You give life to them all."

**The movement.** It shows what love does to the rest of life. Coming closer is leaving. The mind that was stuck on things watches them grow thin.

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Contemplate the higher worlds, where the light is less contracted, the way a deeper mind holds more light (lines 137-138). In each higher light the preciousness of Godliness is felt more, and the soul comes very near (line 138). Then step by step. "Love is moving from place to place" (line 140). Someone walking to a place gets farther from where he left with every step. So love weakens the animal soul, not by fighting it but by walking. After good prayer, says the Rashab in Yiddish, "he is a different person" for hours, even a whole day (line 140). Then to the root. Near Godliness a person feels the coarseness of things he did not feel while chasing them (lines 140-141). Then even the natural mind understands that "the physical is secondary, and does not come up to the name of a thing by itself" (line 142). The animal soul too comes to want Godliness, first by agreeing, and in time by living it "with great life" (line 142). Bring it home. Remember the last time you were truly absorbed in something good: a child's question, a page of learning. The pull of the phone was simply weaker. You did not fight it. The picture breaks. A traveller leaves a place once. This leaving happens "not once or twice, but in the steady service of every day" (line 140).

**From inside.** Distance from appetites without strain. Clarity: things show their true size.

**At the edge.** From the natural mind it touches the higher unity: the physical is not "a thing by itself" (line 142). It goes no further.

**Into the day.** After you pray, keep the first ten minutes away from your phone, and see whether you are "a different person".

## Chapter 4 (iii): love like fire; seeing your distance (lines 143-152)

**The movement.** It adds the heat of distance. The mind sees the greatness, then sees how far it is from it, and the gap becomes a flame that burns up the coarse fire.

> הנה האש דקדושה שורף ומכלה האש דלעו"ז
> *The fire of holiness burns and consumes the fire of the other side.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The animal soul is at the level of "the living", whose element is fire: the heat of desire (line 144). Fire-love comes from contemplating His greatness, filling and surrounding, "all before Him counts as nothing". It strips love of every garment, so that it wants nothing but God alone (lines 144-145). Then step by step. For most people it comes by adding to the contemplation "how far he is from Godliness", in thought, speech and deed and in his whole soul (line 147). Then "his soul becomes very bitter, and flames with the fire of thirst and longing" to come near, to leave whatever separates him (line 147). Then to the root. Water-love only weakens the animal soul. Fire-love burns up its selfhood (line 149). And more: the natural fire itself rises and is included in the love (line 149). That is the sacrifice of "fat and blood": the animal's delight and the boiling of its blood go up into holiness (line 150). Bring it home. Think of the temper that flares in you. It is fire. The Rashab does not ask you to pour water on it. He asks for a greater fire. The picture breaks. Lower repentance, bitterness over one's faults, breaks the animal soul but does not lift it. Fire-love lifts it, because although it has bitterness in it, the main thing is the running toward (line 150).

**From inside.** Heat, longing, thirst. A pain at the distance that pushes forward, not down.

**At the edge.** It points to the "fire above", love above reason (line 145). Kept with the manual's guard: if it flattens rather than lifts, stop.

**Into the day.** When anger rises today, turn it into one sentence of longing: "I want to be close."

## Chapter 4 (iv): two hearts (lines 153-160)

**The movement.** It digs under feeling to will, and under will to a will with no reason at all: the soul's own nature to run home.

A deeper will is above reason: the natural will (line 162), the soul's run toward the Infinite, in the Rashab's Yiddish "a tzi tzu", a pull toward (line 163). ## Chapter 5 (ii): love that hangs on nothing; two friends, father and son (lines 166-174)

**The movement.** It sorts loves by what they hang on. The love made by contemplation ends when the contemplation ends. The essential love was never caused, so it cannot end.

> כמו אהבת אב ובן שאינה מתעלמת לעולם וא"צ לשום דבר המעורר והיא בתמידות ממש
> *Like the love of father and son, which is never hidden, needs nothing to awaken it, and is truly constant.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A love that hangs on a thing is measured by its cause and ends when the cause ends (line 166). A love that hangs on nothing is like "two faithful friends": it can hide while they are apart and wake at some joyful event (line 166). When it wakes it has no limit, since the event never caused it (lines 168-171). Then step by step. Above that, beyond comparison: father and son (line 172). It never hides and needs nothing to wake it. Then to the root. In the soul: love from reason, born of contemplation, hides after prayer (line 174). The will of the chayah, the soul's surrounding level, needs waking by contemplating His wonder, but "in truth the contemplation is not the cause of the love, but what is in the soul essentially" (line 174). The yechidah is like father and son: no waking, no hiding. Bring it home. Think of a friend you have not seen for years. You meet, and in a minute it is all there. You did not rebuild it. It was waiting. The picture breaks. Friends drift and fathers fail. The Rashab takes only the best of the picture: what hangs on nothing cannot fall.

**From inside.** Security. Prayer stops being a test you pass or fail. Contemplation becomes an introduction, not a manufacture.

**At the edge.** The yechidah, "an essential and constant bond" (line 174).

**Into the day.** Send a message to someone you love for no reason, and think: "like this, and more."

## Chapter 5 (iv): the thief at the tunnel; the Days of Awe (lines 183-199)

**The movement.** It shows the gap between true feeling and true change, without contempt. Faith, and even real tears, can sit on top of a life that stays the same.

## Chapter 6 (i): general contemplation is a false imagination (lines 200-209)

**The movement.** It takes away a counterfeit. The quick heat of "He fills and surrounds all worlds", thought in general, is named a dream. The mind loses a comfortable excitement and gains a real road.

Creation from nothing in general, with a vague sense of wonder, without asking which Godly power, and how (line 201). Creation from nothing is truly beyond the human mind; its power is the Essence's alone (line 201). Then step by step. Or "He fills all worlds and surrounds all worlds" in general. Even if one boils up, in the Rashab's Yiddish "cooked up about Godliness", it is surrounding only, a false imagination (line 204). General contemplation is only a remembering. Remembering is enough for awe, not for love (line 204). Then to the root. Why awe and not love? Bitul is natural to Jewish souls, "you are the fewest", they make themselves small (line 204). Love is a revealing, so it needs real grasp. And general contemplation usually brings bitterness and a worried heart: the matter is wondrous and will not settle, so the mind contracts (line 206). "A worried heart is a vessel for the secrets of Torah" (line 209), but here it is a vessel with no light in it, and no joy in learning afterward. Bring it home. You know the feeling after a big talk: "wow". An hour later, what is left? Try to say one particular. The picture breaks. A surrounding light hovers over the head like a halo in a painting. It lights nothing inside.

**From inside.** Disappointment, then relief. You no longer have to chase the high. Contemplation that grasps particulars brings "joy of the soul" (line 209).

**At the edge.** It limits the mind: creation from nothing is beyond human grasp (line 201). And the hidden love it relies on for awe is the power of self-sacrifice (line 204).

## Chapter 6 (iii): the cooling at the start; to make everything one (lines 225-237)

**The movement.** It comforts the beginner who feels colder the moment he starts to think carefully. The cooling is not failure. And it gives the reason to go on: the soul came down to make everything one.

> והו"ע קישור הנפש עם הגוף בכדי לעשות הכל בבחי' אחד
> *This is the binding of the soul with the body, to make everything one.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. When a person moves from general excitement to a particular contemplation, he cools right down (line 226). "About the cooling of the earlier excitement let him not worry or fret at all", because it was never real (line 226). He was not hot; he imagined it. Better than it is one who prays with the plain meaning of the words and has life in them (line 226). Then step by step. Many beginners stumble here. They want the light at once, feel colder, their heart falls, and they say "we are not suited to this" (line 228). That is a great error and a trick of the inclination. "There is no such thing at all" (line 228). Then to the root. "All souls came down to refine the animal soul": "days were formed", from the word for shaping with a stylus, "the binding of the soul with the body, to make everything one" (line 230). The days and years are given to match the refining each needs, so the strength is given too. The cooling comes because the work feels heavy (line 232). Ask yourself: why am I in the world? To be like an animal, busy with eating and drinking? (line 236). Bring it home. The first minutes of real concentration are dull, like the first minutes of a run. You do not stop because your legs feel heavy at the start. The picture breaks. "Making one" sounds like gluing two things together. Here soul and body are bound so that the whole person, and through him "all the matters of the world", becomes one (line 236).

**From inside.** Permission. Patience. A settled purpose. And help: "one who comes to purify is helped", in every detail of the work (line 236).

**At the edge.** The unity made in a life: raising the animal soul "and all the matters of the world" (line 236).

**Into the day.** When you feel cold at the start of prayer, say "this is the start of the real thing", and keep going five more minutes.

## Chapter 6 (iv): your one trait (lines 238-250)

**The movement.** It narrows the whole service to one point: the trait that is yours to fix. Contemplation is tested by whether that one thing moved.

> כל אחד ואחד יש לו מדה פרטית מהמדות הטבעיות שעליו לברר ולזכך אותה
> *Each and every one has a particular trait among the natural traits that is his to refine and purify.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Souls now are old souls, come again to refine what they did not refine before (line 239). The animal soul comes whole, with every trait; the ones refined before are weaker, the one not yet refined is strong (line 241). Then step by step. General arousal helps the weak traits. The strong one is untouched (line 241). One is by nature "white bile": pleasures, lightness, mockery. Another "black bile": anger, cruelty, laziness, sadness (line 243). After a "hot" general prayer comes the same lightheadedness or anger, maybe stronger, because he is pleased with himself (line 243). Then to the root. The one who is not deceived knows his main traits are as they were (line 249). Let him give real time to prayer and contemplate something particular. Each time, the Yiddish says, "the matter becomes clearer to him", "it becomes one with him" (line 249). Then he keeps the command to love, and completes the purpose for which his soul came down. Bring it home. Name your one trait. Not three. One. The one your family would name without being asked. The picture breaks. A doctor treats the disease head-on. Here the medicine is not aimed at the trait directly. It is love of God, thought through in detail, and it weakens the trait from above.

**From inside.** Honesty without despair. Focus. Prayer becomes personal.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there; it is the return into one's own nature.

**Into the day.** Give the last minute of prayer to your one trait: "Today, less of this."

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light); L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 7 (i): truth comes by stages (lines 251-266)

**The movement.** It warns against a lovely false fire: wanting everything at once. It turns a mind that reaches too high toward one true step.

> כי האמת בא בהדרגה דוקא, וכל דרגא היא אמיתית בעצמה
> *For truth comes by stages, and each stage is true in itself.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There is one who looks refined and wants every good thing: the highest levels, many stringencies (line 252). Then in a real test he cannot stand (line 252). His Godly soul was drawn out a little by loud outer arousal, shouts and motions (line 253). Then step by step. Two lacks. The arousal does nothing to the animal soul (line 255). And wanting everything above one's level is not true (line 255). A person should know what to ask of himself (line 261). It is bad when nothing bothers you; it is also not good when everything bothers you (lines 261-262). Then to the root. In true service you stand somewhere; in Yiddish, "he holds by something" (line 263). What is at your level truly matters to you, you keep it, and that truth makes you ready for the next. "It is easier to want high things untruly than to hold a low thing truly" (line 265). As in learning: start from the simple, or you end "bald on both sides" (line 264). Bring it home. Last month's list of resolutions. Which one did you actually keep? That is where you stand. The picture breaks. A staircase is climbed step by step, but each step is also a floor. You live on it for a while.

**From inside.** Relief from grandness. Solid ground. Not changing with every mood.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there, and does not try.

**Into the day.** Choose one small practice at your level and keep it for a week without adding another.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 7 (ii): the close; love at your own level; the desert and after (lines 267-271)

**The movement.** It sends the reader home with a plain instruction: contemplate what is near you, in detail, until you love. The love that was given in the desert is now earned.

> אבל הדור שאח"כ נצטוו על האהבה דהיינו שע"י עבודתם יבואו לבחי' אהבח
> *But the generation after were commanded to love, meaning that through their own service they would come to love.*

*OCR: אהבח is the scan's slip for אהבה.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Do not go after great things, contemplating high levels, "only the levels near to him" (line 268): memale, the Godly radiance clothed in the worlds. Then there is nearness "truly, by his own truth, at his level" (line 269). Then step by step. "Hear, O Israel" is hearing and understanding. "With all your heart" is with both inclinations, "that the evil inclination too return to the love of God" (line 269). "My heart and my flesh sing." Then to the root. The command to love comes only in Deuteronomy (line 269). The desert generation saw Godliness from above and loved by itself, love in delights (line 270). The generation after were commanded: by their own work. That is the purpose of the toil. Bring it home. You were not in the desert. You were not given the sight. What you have is your own head and heart, and an hour. The picture breaks. What is given seems higher than what is earned. The Rashab does not say which is higher. He says which is ours.

**From inside.** Humility and dignity together. Your love is your own.

**At the edge.** It notes that even the desert generation's main gift was awe and bitul from seeing (line 270), and goes no further.

**Into the day.** Tomorrow, pray with one near idea, the life in one thing, and nothing higher.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## The Rayatz's letter (i): the love 'for He is your life' (lines 272-279)

**The movement.** The letter opens the word olam: it means hidden, and it means eternal. It sets side by side two loves that are opposites and share one name.

> וכשם שהארם אוהב חיי נפשו כן הוא אוהב את ה' להיות שזהו חיותו. ונקראת אהבת עולם דחיות העולם הוא אלקות
> *As a man loves the life of his soul, so he loves God, for that is his life; and it is called ahavat olam because the life of the world is Godliness.*

*OCR: הארם is the scan's slip for האדם.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There are three loves: ahavat olam, ahavah rabbah, and love in delights (line 274). "Olam" means hiddenness and also eternity, and these meanings are "truly opposites" (line 275). Then step by step. Love taken from the world: "to love the Lord your God, for He is your life" (line 276). And its opposite branch: the whole source of the world is "a mere radiance that takes no place" before the Infinite (line 276). One is nearness, one is wonder; both come by reason (line 277). Then to the root. Eternal love is natural and essential, without change, without time, the same in its essence in everyone; in it "cause and effect are one" (line 279). The first love has an advantage: it has results, the way love of life leads to eating, drinking and resting (line 279). Bring it home. You love being alive. You eat, you sleep, you step back from the kerb. That same love, the letter says, can be for God, because He is what you are alive with. The picture breaks. A man's life belongs to him; God does not. The picture holds only where "your life" means what lives in you.

**From inside.** Plain warmth. Gratitude for being alive at all.

**At the edge.** The eternal love, above cause (line 279).

**Into the day.** Next time you eat, think: "for He is your life."

**Ladder.** L02 (Being said, right now); L03 (Two ways of knowing)

## The Rayatz's letter (ii): the springs of the deep (lines 280-283)

**The movement.** It gives a picture of a love that works by being there. Hidden water under the ground keeps the earth moist without doing anything.

> אמנם מזה שהם שוכנים בהארץ הרי בהארץ דוקא יש בה טבע הרטיבות
> *But because they dwell in the earth, the earth, precisely, has the nature of moisture.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The springs of the great deep flow far down in the earth and never show (line 280). They do nothing, and yet the earth is moist, and so it can grow things. Rain grows things only once it is on the earth (line 280). Then step by step. Two neighbours in one house with nothing between them still affect each other by living together (line 280). Not cause and effect; just dwelling. Then to the root. So with the eternal love: by the very fact that it is there, it always works (line 281). And it is "essential in the soul" (line 282), natural and essential at once. Bring it home. The love you have for your children when you are not thinking of them. It colours the whole day while you do something else. The picture breaks. Springs can dry up. This love does not change. And the deep is essential only in raw creatures, while this love is essential to the soul itself (line 282).

**From inside.** Trust that the ground in you is wet. Growth feels possible.

**At the edge.** The essential love of the soul (line 282). The letter does not name it further.

**Into the day.** When you do something plain today, remember the water under the floor.

**Ladder.** L11 (Where you and He meet); L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## The Rayatz's letter (iii): great love surrounds him; there is no chance (lines 284-287)

**The movement.** It shows a love that fills every power and forgets itself, and then says that a Jew's every stop on the road is meant.

> בי באמת באיש יהודי אינו שייך ענין הזרמנות
> *For in truth, for a Jew there is no such thing as chance.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Ahavah rabbah shows in all a person's powers, not only in the heart (line 285). Like running to a mitzvah: will, life and delight at once. "In every time and place he is surrounded by this love and forgets himself entirely"; in Yiddish, "he doesn't feel himself" (line 285). Then step by step. The letter's example is teaching Torah in public. Some do it by habit. With great love, "when he walks and stands, it lies in his head and heart", and he does not care what the class is, who the people are, or where (line 286). Then to the root. "A man's steps are established by the Lord" (line 287). If a Jew on the road stops somewhere he did not mean to and prays there, or says a psalm or a verse, "for this he had to stop there". Every movement is providence, for "Torah-light and the work of refining" (line 287). Bring it home. The traffic jam, the missed train, the waiting room. You are there. The picture breaks. "No chance" could sound like a cage. The letter means an address: the place you stand is where you were sent.

**From inside.** Being surrounded. Less resentment at delays. A readiness to do something good where you are.

**At the edge.** The great love that surrounds him "from every side" and gives him inner life (line 285).

**Into the day.** The next time you are stuck somewhere, say one psalm or one blessing there.

## The Rayatz's letter (iv): love in delights; the righteous and those who return (lines 288-306)

**The movement.** It climbs to simple delight at the top of the soul, then sets two roads side by side, drawing down and rising up, and says that the returner's endless rising goes higher.

> כ"א ענין התשובה הוא כמ"ש והרוח תשוב אל האלקים אשר נתנה
> *Rather, teshuvah means, as it says, "and the spirit shall return to God who gave it".*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Love in delights is the highest (line 288). Delight is closest to the soul's essence, and highest of all is "simple delight, not compounded with anything" (line 288). Spiritual things are not divided by walls of stone, so each love has branches of the others in it (line 289). Then step by step. The righteous who serve with their body toil to remove what hides the soul. The righteous who serve with their soul, like Moses at his birth when "the house filled with light", need no toil (lines 292-293). Those who return from sin break their whole nature (line 294). Then to the root. But teshuvah is not only for sin. It is lifting the spirit above the level at which it was given, and "every level of service, even the highest, finds a lack" (line 295), like the high priest's immersions from holy to holier. The soul came down in order to rise (line 300). The righteous draw down; returners raise up; and the second kind of returner is higher even than the righteous (line 304). Bring it home. A good day that still has a lack in it. Not a failure. The next rung. The picture breaks. Returning sounds like going back. Here it means going higher than where you started.

**From inside.** A holy restlessness. No resting on yesterday.

**At the edge.** Simple delight (line 288). The letter breaks off in mid-sentence (line 306).

**Into the day.** At the end of the day, name one good thing you did and ask: what is the next rung?

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Part Two: Kuntres HaTefillah (the Tract on Prayer)

*Kuntres HaTefillah has an opening and eighteen numbered sections; this reading follows them, with section 11 (the five preparations) read as two units. The kitzur after each section is the Rayatz's summary and is not quoted as the Rashab's words.*

*Skipped, with nothing to contemplate: the front matter (lines 1-40: title page, forewords, table of contents, and facsimiles of handwriting that the OCR turned to noise); line 76, a garbled line inside section 8; and the subject index compiled for the reprint (lines 124-253).*

## Opening: 'how does one pray?' (lines 41-42)

**The movement.** It begins with a question many people asked, and answers with humility: this is not new, only a reminder, so that we can become vessels for light.

> ואנחנו צריכים להדבק בהם ובדבריהם חק' ולהיות כלים לקבל בנפשינו את האור כי טוב
> *And we must cleave to them and to their holy words, and be vessels to receive in our souls the light, that it is good.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "Each one of our people asks and seeks: how does one pray, and with what does one serve God in the service of the heart?" (line 41). The Rashab answers from what the earlier Rebbes wrote. Then step by step. Their words are "living and standing, now as then" (line 42). He only reminds, and adds a few things "for this time, when hearts have become fewer" (line 42). Then to the root. Their whole wish was to put true light and life in our souls, and they gave their lives for it (line 42). Our part is to cleave to them and to be vessels. Bring it home. You are asking the question too. The book was written for the one who asks. The picture breaks. A vessel just sits and receives. Here being a vessel is work, and the whole book is about that work.

**From inside.** Being spoken to. A student's openness.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there; it opens the door.

**Into the day.** Before you pray tomorrow, ask yourself honestly, "How do I pray?", and wait for an answer.

## Section 1: gazing at the King's glory (lines 43-47)

**The movement.** It lays out the whole field of contemplation, from what the natural mind can grasp to what is above grasp, and ends where the heart sees and the person grows still as a stone.

> שעצם פנימיות לבו רואה ומרגיש עצם האלקות כמו שהוא למעלה מהשנה ממש
> *The very inwardness of his heart sees and feels the essence of Godliness as it is, truly above comprehension.*

*OCR: מהשנה is the scan's slip for מהשגה.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Memale, "which any thinking person can understand, even with the natural mind of the animal soul"; sovev and what is above it; the first two verses of the Shema; particular contemplations for love or for awe (line 44). Then step by step. Then "gazing at the King's glory": the Godly mind looking at the preciousness of the Godly light, until "all his senses fall away in this gazing, and he does not feel himself" (line 44). There is great delight in it, sweetness, even "the soul's expiring" (line 44). Then to the root. It reaches the heart too, "my heart saw much wisdom" (line 44). There is no stirring at all, because the heart's inwardness sees the essence of Godliness as it is. He becomes "like a silent stone" (line 45). And the gaze proper to beauty (tiferet): opposites joined in one subject, up to "the perfection of His Essence bears all opposites, and all is truly yachid", alone (line 46). Bring it home. The moment in front of a great view when you stop narrating. No "wow". Only seeing. The picture breaks. A king's glory is looked at from outside. This gazing is inward, and the one looking drops out of the picture.

**From inside.** Stillness. Sweetness. A glad heart at "the beauty of God and the splendour of His majesty" (line 46). No motion at all.

**At the edge.** This is the edge: above comprehension (line 44), and the Essence that bears all opposites as yachid (line 46).

**Into the day.** Once today, look at one beautiful thing without saying anything, then say: "The King's glory."

**Ladder.** L09 (One, and alone); L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Section 2: study is the preparation (lines 48-51)

**The movement.** It moves the centre of gravity from the study hall to prayer. What you learned is not yet yours.

> כי כל הלימוד והידיעת הקודמת היא כהכנה אל העיקר
> *For all the learning and knowledge before is a preparation for the main thing.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Everything is laid out in the printed discourses (lines 48-49). "It depends on you": to know them, to understand them, and to contemplate them in prayer, "which is the main intent" (line 49). Then step by step. The contemplation must be particular: widen the explanation "in the multitude of its details" (line 49). General contemplation, even of something you know well, "is no contemplation at all", because the light shines only "surrounding, above the mind" (line 49). Then to the root. Then a person may force his heart to feel, and that is "a false imagination" that passes at once: "will you set your eyes on it, and it is gone" (line 49). Nothing changes in his traits. That is why many complain: we learn much and pray, and nothing changes (line 50). Bring it home. A recipe you have read is not a meal. You have to cook. The picture breaks. "Preparation" sounds lesser. The Rashab means that it is for something.

**From inside.** A sober sense of purpose. Prayer gains weight.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** Carry one idea from this week's learning into tomorrow's prayer.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 3: do not invent (lines 52-55)

**The movement.** It takes away the pleasure of being clever in prayer. The mind that wants to be original is asked to receive.

> ולא ירצה להיות ממציא ומשכיל כ"א ללמוד ולדרעת היטב
> *Let him not want to be an inventor and innovator, but to learn and to know well.*

*OCR: ולדרעת is the scan's slip for ולדעת.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Some people invent a new reading of a verse during prayer and are moved by it (lines 52-53). Most likely it is not true, and their feeling rests on it. Then step by step. Even when it is true, it is surrounding; it lifts the soul for some hours, a polishing of the outside of the vessel (line 53). "A man likes his own foot better than another man's head" (line 54): we like our own finds. Then to the root. For the young it should be "truly like a prohibition": their nature becomes imagination and false sharpness (line 54). "Flee from this a bowshot." Learn and know well; then, sometimes, what you find will be true (line 54). Bring it home. That flash in shul: "oh, so that's what it means!" Pleasant. Now notice whether you are thinking about God or about your idea. The picture breaks. Invention looks like depth. The Rashab says real depth is in receiving.

**From inside.** Humility. Freedom from the need to be interesting.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** If a clever idea comes during prayer, park it for afterwards and go back to the plain words.

## Section 4: the hour (lines 56-59)

**The movement.** It gives the true work its length: an hour or more, tied to one idea, until the light shines in the mind and flows by itself into the heart.

> ויאיר האור בגילוי ממש במוחו. ומהמוח נמשך אל הלב שנחקק ונרגש האור כלבו
> *And the light shines truly revealed in his mind, and from the mind it is drawn to the heart, so that the light is engraved and felt in his heart.*

*OCR: כלבו is the scan's slip for בלבו.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. True service is toil of the brain, in great study and strong attachment, in Yiddish "a strong attachedness" (line 57). "It needs a long time, an hour and more" (line 57). Then step by step. Not like someone learning a subject, but bound to the thing itself. Then you see more details than you knew, and bring it close with your own explanations (line 57). "The essence brings its own revelation", until it settles, in Yiddish, "very good and very clean" in the mind (line 57). Then to the root. The mind is moved. Da'at, the power that binds and feels, draws the heart after it by itself; first hidden, then the light comes from above into the heart, revealed (line 58). Bring it home. An hour with one sentence sounds impossible. But you have spent an hour on one worry. The mind can stay. The picture breaks. Water runs downhill by itself, and the heart follows the mind by itself (line 58). But someone has to carry the water up first.

**From inside.** Absorption. Then a warmth that you did not make.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** Once this week, set a timer for twenty minutes and stay with one idea.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 5: da'at is feeling (lines 60-64)

**The movement.** It explains why understanding is not enough. Knowing must become feeling, the way the merciful man feels another's lowliness.

> ולכן כתי' וידעת היום והשבות אל לבבך דהעיקר הוא ההשבה אל הלב
> *So it is written, "know today and return it to your heart": the main thing is the returning to the heart.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Da'at is a brain of its own (line 61), and its essence is feeling what was grasped. A clever child understands everything and feels nothing, which is why he is not yet punishable (lines 61-62). Then step by step. The cruel man understands the other's lowness and does not feel it. "The merciful one feels his lowliness, and so has mercy on him" (line 62). Then to the root. Feeling lives in the brain like nerves, and it reaches the heart. When you feel the Godly matter itself, the heart is moved by itself. Nearness brings love; greatness brings inner love; "all before Him counts as nothing" or "the Infinite is found below as above" brings awe (line 62). The main thing is the returning to the heart (line 62). Bring it home. News of a disaster far away, and the same news about a friend. The same knowledge. Different da'at. The picture breaks. The merciful man feels a person's lowliness. God has none. What you come to feel is His goodness and greatness.

**From inside.** Things landing. Tenderness.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** Take one fact you know about God and ask, "Do I feel it?" Stay until you feel it a little.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 6: the garments as a passage; near to everyone (lines 65-69)

**The movement.** It separates being moved by a conclusion from being moved by the thing itself. The ideas become a doorway you walk through.

> אבל הלבושים הן כעין מעבר והעיקר נרגש אצלו האור האלקי
> *But the garments are like a passage, and what he mainly feels is the Godly light.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Some heart-arousal comes only from the bottom line: "Godliness is good and wonderful" (line 66). The heart is moved, but not by the Godly light; a gap separates mind and heart (line 66). Then step by step. When the mind is bound to the Godliness it grasped, the light shines, still clothed, but "the garments are like a passage". The heart is moved of itself, and the arousal lasts (line 66). Then to the root. Do not say, "how would I ever come to this?" "It is near to anyone who has a mind and a heart" (line 67). The inclination throws in "a bitter drop of false lowliness" (line 67). A person's mind is in his power. The advice: set aside an hour or two each day for deep study (line 67). Bring it home. A window. After a while you stop seeing the glass and see the garden. The picture breaks. A passage is left behind once you are through. The ideas stay, but they are not what you are looking at.

**From inside.** Directness. A heart moved "not by a made-up arousal" (line 63).

**At the edge.** It points: He is "sought by all who seek Him in truth" (line 67).

**Into the day.** During the Shema, let the words be the window, not the view.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart); L05 (The hiding is Him)

## Section 7: the 'low' matters are the main work now (lines 70-73)

**The movement.** It corrects a spiritual snobbery. The mind that wants only the highest is sent back to creation, to God filling and surrounding.

> אשר באמת עיקר העבודה עכשיו הוא. בענינים אלו ביותר
> *For in truth the main service now is in these matters most of all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Some deal only with what is above Atzilut, the Essence before the tzimtzum, and call creation from nothing, God filling and surrounding, and the angels' "Holy" and "Blessed", "low matters" (line 71). Then step by step. They do not know the depth of these matters. They have no life in them because they do not give themselves to prayer (line 71). The high matters are above grasp; they are known "in the inner binah", "the heart's understanding" (line 72). Reaching for them with the mind, people turn them over and even coarsen them, "bringing them into the selfhood of grasp and garments of corporeality" (line 72). Then to the root. We should be given over to the teachings of our Rebbes, "only a vessel to receive" (line 72). Bring it home. For you, today, the glass of water in front of you is a deeper subject than the Infinite before the tzimtzum. The picture breaks. Low and high change places: the "low" matters are the main work.

**From inside.** Humility. Ground under the feet. Relief from straining upward.

**At the edge.** It says where the highest is known: in the heart's understanding, not the grasping mind (line 72).

**Into the day.** Contemplate one "low" thing today: how this chair is being made to be, now.

**Ladder.** L02 (Being said, right now); L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Section 8: prayer is the spine (lines 74-79)

**The movement.** It shows prayer as what holds everything else upright, and names the animal soul's three natures. Knowledge without prayer fades.

> דלכן נמשלה התפלה לח"י חוליות השדרה שמעמידים כל הגוף
> *That is why prayer is likened to the eighteen vertebrae of the spine, which hold up the whole body.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Even people who learn Chassidus well pray little (line 75). Love and awe are the source of all the commandments, and learning has life through the prayer that comes before it (line 75). "Why is there a price in the hand of a fool to buy wisdom, when he has no heart?" (line 75). Then step by step. What is learned lasts only when it is engraved in prayer. Otherwise it shortens and is forgotten "as if it never was" (line 77). Engraved, it lasts "with an eternal existence" (line 77). Then to the root. The soul came down to refine the animal soul (line 77), which comes in three kinds: the goat, stubborn and unmoving, whose strength shows only when you try to move it; the ox that gores; and the soft sheep (line 77). Refining is done by da'at, by feeling, which tells good from bad the way nerves tell pain (lines 77-78). Bring it home. Which one are you in the morning? Goat, ox or sheep? The picture breaks. A spine is bone. Prayer is a living line that keeps you upright.

**From inside.** Seriousness, and self-knowledge with a smile.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** Before any learning today, say one line of prayer first.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Section 9: without prayer, knowledge turns to selfhood (lines 80-83)

**The movement.** It lets the mind see its own danger: knowledge of God can feed the self. And it shows the cure: the more the height of Godliness is felt, the lower evil looks.

> ובהעדר העבודה בתפלה גם מה שלמר וידץ נהפך לו לפה"מ בקרבו
> *Without the service of prayer, even what he learned and knows turns into deadly poison within him.*

*OCR: שלמר וידץ is the scan's slip for שלמד וידע.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. When the light is felt in mind and heart he feels the good, the height of Godliness, and "the more he feels the height of Godliness, the more he feels the lowness and meanness of evil" (line 80). So the choice of good is fixed in his soul. Then step by step. Learning, even of Chassidus, does not refine the traits by itself (line 80). "One may learn much Chassidus and be entirely far from Godliness, and even the opposite" (line 80). Some learn, are moved, stop, and at once do the opposite. Then to the root. Without prayer it turns to poison: he becomes "a something", very coarse in spirit (line 81). Pride is the father of impurity. Better for him not to have bought wisdom at all. So do not excuse yourself with learning alone (line 81). Bring it home. The pleasure of knowing more than others at the table. Notice it. The picture breaks. Medicine and poison can be the same substance. The difference is whether it reaches the heart.

**From inside.** A healthy fear of self-deception. A turn toward humility.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there. Its opposite of bitul is "a something" (line 81).

**Into the day.** When you share a teaching today, check: am I giving, or showing?

## Section 10: the order of prayer as a ladder (lines 84-86)

**The movement.** For someone whose mind is not wide, it gives the prayer's own map: from greatness in creation, through the angels' song, to the Shema's One, to one will for God alone.

> איך שהוא אחד בז' רקיעים וארץ וד' רוחות העולם בבחינת יחיד ממש
> *How He is One in the seven heavens and the earth and the four directions of the world, truly as yachid, alone.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Even one whose mind cannot bind itself to the essence of the Godly matter can serve with what he knows, until it is felt (lines 84-85). Then step by step. Pesukei deZimra: His greatness in bringing things into being, and the praise and bitul of the outer side of the worlds. The blessings of the Shema: the angels' song, "Holy" and "Blessed", and how they are wholly set aside before Him (line 85). Then to the root. "Shema Yisrael", the song of the souls: His oneness from the side of the worlds, One in the seven heavens, the earth and the four directions, "truly yachid" (line 85). "Baruch shem": all the worlds come only from "the glory of His kingship", "a glow of a glow of a glow" (line 85). Then "one will for God alone", and a life near to Godliness "in all his affairs" (line 85). Bring it home. The Shema, with your hand over your eyes. Seven heavens above, the earth below, and the four walls of your room. The picture breaks. Seven, one, four: numbers of places. And He is One "truly alone" in all of them.

**From inside.** Order. A path through the siddur. A sense of going somewhere.

**At the edge.** Yachid (line 85), and the whole world as "a glow of a glow of a glow" (line 85).

**Into the day.** At "echad", let your mind go up, down, and to the four sides.

## Section 11 (i): clearing the room; the vessel and dwelling (lines 87-89)

**The movement.** It prepares a vessel. The mind is emptied of worry by plain reasoning, the body stands still, and the person looks honestly at what makes him unfit to be a dwelling.

> שכאו"א צ"ל כלי ומשכן לאור ה' וקדושתו
> *Each one must be a vessel and dwelling for God's light and His holiness.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Without a vessel, general contemplation excites and particular contemplation cools, because surrounding light needs no vessel and inner light does (line 88). Then step by step. First, clear away worry: wait "two doorways" before praying (line 88). If you say you cannot, remember that you manage it every night to fall asleep (line 88). Stand in one place; pacing is only "breadth of the outer spirit" (line 88). Then half an hour of Chassidus in tallit and tefillin. Then lowliness, by an honest accounting (lines 88-89). Then to the root. A king's house must be clean (line 89). "Make Me a sanctuary and I will dwell within them", within each one (line 89). Unrefined, he is no dwelling, and even the Torah he learns is in exile in him (line 89). Broken-hearted, "I dwell with the crushed" (line 89). The bitterness is only at times, best at midnight, in private, and after it comes joy (lines 89-90). Bring it home. Your room before a guest comes. You pick up the socks. Not because the guest is proud. Because you love them. The picture breaks. A house is cleaned once and then lived in. Here the cleaning, the lowliness itself, is what makes the room.

**From inside.** Order. Quiet. An honest lowliness that is followed by joy.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there; it readies the room.

**Into the day.** Before praying tomorrow, stand in one place and set down one worry the way you do at bedtime.

## Section 11 (ii): compassion on your soul; standing before the Essence (lines 89-93)

**The movement.** If the accounting did not break the heart, compassion may. Then it sets the person before the Essence found in every place, and he stands in bitul.

> ועצמות המאור הוא בהתגלות שנמצא בכל מקום ומקום ממש
> *And the Essence of the Luminary is revealed, found in every single place, truly.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Arouse great compassion on your soul: "from a high roof to a deep pit" (line 90). Its root is higher than the angels, "the root of its root in the inwardness and Essence of the Infinite". It came down into a body, and your own deeds bound it in exile (line 90). "In Your great mercies have mercy on us", because we do not know how great the pity is. "This is easy for a person" (line 90). Then step by step. Before the Amidah: you stand before the King and speak to Him face to face, "You" (line 90). All the highest lights are dark before Him; they are only light (lines 90-91). Then to the root. The Essence of the Luminary is revealed, found in every place, and hiding and revealing do not apply to it at all (line 91). Before Him he stands and prays. Great awe falls on him, inner bitul, "the main point of the Amidah" (line 91). Bring it home. Your place by the wall in shul, the corner of your kitchen. The Essence is there as fully as anywhere. The picture breaks. A light can be dimmed and a lamp can be hidden. Not the Essence of the Luminary.

**From inside.** Tenderness for your own soul, then awe. Quiet. Speaking to "You".

**At the edge.** The Essence, beyond hiding and revealing (line 91).

**Into the day.** Before you pray, say one sentence of pity for your soul, then step forward and begin with "You".

**Ladder.** L10 (The plainest thing); L11 (Where you and He meet)

## Section 12: 'whole in himself'; the broken heart (lines 94-101)

**The movement.** It diagnoses the person for whom the teaching stays outside, "whole in himself", and names the vessel: the broken heart.

> כי אמיתית הכלי לאור פניטיות אלקות הוא הלב נשבר דוקא
> *For the true vessel for the light of inner Godliness is the broken heart, precisely.*

*OCR: פניטיות is the scan's slip for פנימיות.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Some study Chassidus and are not vessels for it (line 94). The sign: they like to hear it but not to work at it; it is a burden (lines 95-97). Their good is by habit, and some event can bring the bad out in force (line 99). Then step by step. The cause: he came to Chassidus not from a heart's return but because he liked it (line 100). So he has no true brokenness; "he is a man wholly complete in himself" (line 100). The teaching passes him by like something outside him. Then to the root. The cure: a real return, a long honest accounting, fasting, accepting rebuke from others (line 100). "The true vessel for inner Godliness is the broken heart": "the secrets of Torah are given only to one whose heart worries within him" (line 100). Bring it home. A full cup cannot take more water. The picture breaks. A broken jug holds nothing. The Rashab's broken heart is the one that holds.

**From inside.** An opening. Less self-sufficiency. Room to receive.

**At the edge.** Inner Godliness dwelling within (line 100). Kept with the manual's guard: this brokenness is not being crushed; not on a crushed day.

**Into the day.** Ask someone you trust to name one fault of yours, and answer only "thank you".

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light) (not on a crushed day)

## Section 13: the particular trait (lines 102-106)

**The movement.** It explains why good prayer does not fix you: you resolved in general. The resolution has to be on the one detail.

> והסכם הכללי אינו מועיל על הפרט
> *The general resolution does not help for the particular.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Some pray with mind and heart and still have ugly traits (line 103). A true feeling of Godliness makes a person feel its opposite in every part of evil (line 104). Then step by step. A great rule: know every part of the evil in you and what you must fix (line 104). In prayer he resolves strongly "to be only good", as the Yiddish puts it; it never touches the particulars. Anger comes, and he forgets. Then to the root. When he knows the particular trait, and in prayer means to subdue this one, then when the moment comes he rules his spirit, and the desire itself weakens (line 104). Not once or twice; "many prayers on one trait" (line 104). Bring it home. "I'll be a better person" against "I will not raise my voice at dinner tonight." The picture breaks. The general looks bigger. The particular is what bears the weight.

**From inside.** Precision. Small victories that are real.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** Name one moment today when your trait usually shows, and bring that moment into prayer by name.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Section 14: the young; slow words, alive (lines 107-109)

**The movement.** It slows the eager down. Before long contemplation, let the words themselves come alive.

> ויהיו התיבות חיים ומאירים להם מהרגש פי' התיבות
> *And let the words be alive and shining for them, from feeling the meaning of the words.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The young who have not yet learned enough should not climb to contemplation before their time; they will fool themselves with imaginations (lines 107-108). Then step by step. Learn with friends who know more, be humble before the elders, and learn a few things in depth (line 108). Pray in a fixed place, from the siddur, very slowly, with the meaning of the words, a little aloud, with clear speech (line 108). Then to the root. The great ones too, who cannot always pray with a wide mind and heart, should pray this way at times, but not excuse themselves by it (line 108): love and awe are commanded, they hold up Torah and mitzvot, and they refine the animal soul. Bring it home. "Baruch" means blessed. Say it as if for the first time. The picture breaks. Slow looks small. It is the root of all the rest.

**From inside.** Calm. Clarity. Warmth in plain words.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** Say the first blessing of the Amidah at half your usual speed, feeling each word.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Section 15: the yoke, the same for everyone (lines 110-113)

**The movement.** It gives what everyone can have: knowing you have a Master who rules you in particular, and keeping watch over each deed.

> לידע בנפשו שיש לו אדון הוא ה' אלקינו שמייחד מלכותו יח' על כאו"א בפרט
> *To know in his soul that he has a Master, the Lord our God, who makes His kingship one over each one in particular.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "This is equal for every Jewish soul" (line 110): the yoke complete, a faithful servant, and not a servant to strangers even for one hour (line 111). Then step by step. Look at yourself always, each trait and each physical matter, with a settled Godly mind, "not to turn his mind away even one moment, to do a thing without da'at" (line 111). What wrong he did, let it be engraved and stand before his eyes. Then to the root. Fix its source, the coarse trait. "One who comes to purify is helped from above", and God opens an extra door for him (line 112). Bring it home. Every small act, under the eye of the Master who is One over you. The picture breaks. A master over many has less for each. This One makes His kingship one "over each one in particular".

**From inside.** Attentiveness. Being accompanied.

**At the edge.** The unity at the scale of one person: His kingship made one over each (line 111).

**Into the day.** Before one purchase or message today, pause: "Is this for my Master?"

**Ladder.** L06 (Not a thing on my own); L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Section 16: the businessman's minute (lines 114-116)

**The movement.** It has mercy on the busy. Every extra moment in prayer is life bought for the whole day, a trace that guards you.

> וכל מה שיאריך בתפלה אפילו רגץ יותר הרי זה כקונה חיים לנפשו על כל היום
> *And whatever he lengthens his prayer, even one moment more, it is as if he buys life for his soul for the whole day.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Quoting the Mitteler Rebbe: people in business, who cannot pray long on weekdays, have another way (line 115). Every morning, notice how business uses up mind and heart: "they surrounded me" (line 115). Then step by step. Let the heart break a little. Do not chat about business before prayer. Learn a little first. Pray slowly, with the words themselves (line 115). The yoke of business is called drunkenness, "and not from wine" (line 115). Then to the root. Arouse compassion; "let the weak say, I am strong" (line 115). The trace guards him while he is busy, "though I walk... I fear no evil, for You are with me", and brings him near whenever he is free, and at a mitzvah such as charity (line 115). Bring it home. Your commute. Your inbox. Before you open it. The picture breaks. Buying sounds like a trade. The point is that a moment of nearness keeps working after it is over.

**From inside.** Hope: enough is possible, even for the busy.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there; it keeps "You are with me" (line 115).

**Into the day.** Add one minute to morning prayer, and say nothing about business before it.

## Section 17: knowing what to do (lines 117-120)

**The movement.** A practical charge: learn the law you will need, and set a separate time to wake the heart. It gives the day's deeds a place in the mind. It does not reach an edge.

> לעורר את הלב לאהכת ה' ולתורתו ועבודתו ית'
> *To awaken the heart to the love of God, His Torah and His service.*

*OCR: לאהכת is the scan's slip for לאהבת.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Everyone, even a sharp learner, must learn the laws needed for practice: the Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim, which gives each law its reason briefly (line 118). Then step by step. A fixed time each day "to know the deed he must do in all matters touching the life of his soul" (line 119). Then to the root. And a separate hour, best at night after Maariv, for books of musar built on the Midrash, the Aggadah and the Zohar, to wake the heart (line 119). Bring it home. "How do I say this blessing?" is part of the service too. The picture breaks. Law and heart look like two subjects. Here they share one daily page.

**From inside.** Order. Confidence in the deed.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** Tonight after Maariv, learn one law, and read one page that warms your heart.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Section 18: learn in order to do (lines 121-123)

**The movement.** It gathers the book into one plea: learn to know God, let the knowing become prayer, and let prayer become deed, in every detail.

> והי' לבבנו שלם ואמת עם ה' אלקינו בכל פרט ופרט
> *And may our heart be whole and true with the Lord our God in every single detail.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. He has shown why prayer is necessary, "touching each one's very life of soul" (lines 121-122). Then step by step. Learn to know God, deeply. But learn "in order to do": to serve with mind and heart in prayer (line 122). Then to the root. To refine the traits, to add light and life to the mitzvot, and to have the heart whole with God "in every single detail" (line 122). Bring it home. Whole in every detail: the email, the dinner, the tone of voice. The picture breaks. "Whole in himself" was the sickness (line 100). "Whole with God" is the health. The same word, turned around.

**From inside.** Integration: fewer compartments.

**At the edge.** A person made one with Him in every detail (line 122). It claims nothing beyond that.

**Into the day.** Choose one detail today, your tone of voice, and make it whole with Him.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations

1. **Avodah line 107 (chapter 3 (i): awe rests on a feeling of the Essence):** שהאלקות בכלל נרגש בנפשו תמיד שאינו נשכח ממנו אפילו רגעץ אחד. *Awe is not built. It is a feeling of the Essence that the soul never forgets for one moment, only hidden; so it stands above love.* When you refuse something small and wrong today, notice the plain 'no' and know where it comes from.

2. **Tefillah line 44 (section 1: gazing at the King's glory):** שעצם פנימיות לבו רואה ומרגיש עצם האלקות כמו שהוא למעלה מהשנה ממש. *The top of prayer: the heart's inwardness sees the essence of Godliness as it is, above comprehension, and the person grows still as a stone.* Look at one beautiful thing without a word, then say 'the King's glory'.

3. **Avodah line 163 (chapter 5 (i): love is will; the soul runs to its root):** וכמו טבע כל דבר שנמשך לשרשו ומקורו כך הוא: טבע הנשמה לימשך ולהכלל בשרשה ומקורה. *Under all feeling is a will with no reason: the soul runs to its root like everything does, and yet it was never apart.* When you hold firm today, say: 'I can't be separated.'

4. **Tefillah line 91 (section 11 (ii): compassion on your soul; standing before the Essence):** ועצמות המאור הוא בהתגלות שנמצא בכל מקום ומקום ממש. *The Essence of the Luminary is revealed in every single place; hiding and revealing do not apply. You stand before it and say 'You'.* Before you pray, one sentence of pity for your soul, then 'You'.

5. **Avodah line 129 (chapter 4 (i): love like water; the world lives from the Godly light):** נרגש ונראה אצלו איך שהעולם חי מאור האלקי. *As you feel your own body's life, see how the world lives from the Godly light, and love clings like water.* Drink one glass of water slowly: 'You give life to them all.'

6. **Avodah line 230 (chapter 6 (iii): the cooling at the start; to make everything one):** והו"ע קישור הנפש עם הגוף בכדי לעשות הכל בבחי' אחד. *The soul was bound to the body to make everything one. So the cold start is not failure and no one is unfit.* When prayer feels cold at first, keep going five minutes and call it the start of the real thing.

7. **Tefillah line 66 (section 6: the garments as a passage; near to everyone):** אבל הלבושים הן כעין מעבר והעיקר נרגש אצלו האור האלקי. *The ideas are a passage; what is felt is the Godly light itself. That is the difference between a made-up arousal and a real one.* Let the Shema's words be the window, not the view.

8. **Avodah line 121 (chapter 3 (iii): below as above; and why love is still the work):** מה שהאוא"ם כמו שחוא בעצם ה"ח נמצא למטה בבחי' גילוי ממש כמו שהוא. *The Infinite as it is in itself is found below, revealed, as above. Awe from this is bitul in existence.* Stand still three seconds somewhere ordinary and think: 'as above'.

9. **Avodah line 172 (chapter 5 (ii): love that hangs on nothing; two friends, father and son):** כמו אהבת אב ובן שאינה מתעלמת לעולם וא"צ לשום דבר המעורר והיא בתמידות ממש. *The deepest love hangs on nothing, like father and son; contemplation only uncovers it.* Send a message to someone you love for no reason: 'like this, and more'.

10. **Tefillah line 100 (section 12: 'whole in himself'; the broken heart):** כי אמיתית הכלי לאור פניטיות אלקות הוא הלב נשבר דוקא. *The true vessel for inner Godliness is the broken heart; the one 'whole in himself' cannot take the teaching in.* Ask someone you trust for one fault, and answer only 'thank you'.

## 4. Where these books' way of contemplating differs

**Detail is the test of truth.** The hisbonenus ontology (`Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`) names duration as the instruction the sources repeat most and skip most, and lists "extension and breadth" among the techniques. These two books go further. For them particularity is the line between a real contemplation and a counterfeit. General contemplation, even of God filling and surrounding all worlds, "is no contemplation at all" (Tefillah line 49); its arousal is "a false imagination" (Avodah line 204). The Gate of Unity, by the Mitteler Rebbe, also asks a person to earn the whole through the detail. The Mitteler Rebbe's own Kuntres HaHitpa'alut tests an arousal differently: by whether the self is felt in it. The Rashab tests it by whether it was particular, and by whether it reached the animal soul (Avodah line 255). Both call one kind of arousal counterfeit, "strange fire" there and "false imagination" here. The two tests are kept side by side, not merged.

**The test is one trait.** The ontology already credits the Rashab with a practical test the earlier sources lacked: has your particular dominant trait changed? In these books that test sets the whole shape of the work. Each soul has one trait that is "his to refine" (Avodah line 239), and "the general resolution does not help for the particular" (Tefillah line 104). The meditation manual's after-test, "a little quieter, a little less full of myself, a little more my own master", is gentler and more general. This reading keeps both, and adds the Rashab's test where he gives it: one named trait, over "many prayers" (Tefillah line 104).

**Awe needs no contemplation; love does.** The ontology's process runs from contemplation to arousal. The Rashab splits that process. Awe can come from remembering alone, all day and outside prayer, because it rests on a hidden feeling of the Essence and bitul is natural to the soul (Avodah lines 101, 107, 204). Love can come only from real contemplation, in the hour of prayer (Avodah line 112). He also ranks awe, even lower awe, above love, as bitul from the Essence (Avodah line 107), while the Tanya (ch. 43) sets love in delights above awe. The Rayatz's letter at the end keeps the Tanya's order of loves. Neither ranking is smoothed away.

**Cold at the start: keep going, do not change course.** The ontology's diagnostic layer prescribes for coldness: "change the object, not the effort". For the particular coldness of the beginner who moves from a general high to a careful contemplation, the Rashab says the opposite of changing anything. "Let him not worry or fret at all" (Avodah line 226). The high was never real, the cooling is the start of the real work, and the way through is to enter the contemplation "by way of the yoke" (Avodah line 223). Both answers are kept, for different kinds of cold. Change the object when the practice has gone stale. Stay with it when the cold is only the loss of a false fire.

**Bitterness, but on schedule.** The ontology says of bitterness: keep the resolution if it sharpens you, stop if it flattens you. The Rashab is more exact about timing. Bitterness that comes from general contemplation is sadness in disguise (Avodah lines 206-209, 248). Bitterness over one's distance belongs "only at times", at midnight, in private, "like a foundation hidden in the ground", and is followed by joy (Tefillah lines 89-90). The fire-love of the Avodah uses the sight of one's distance as fuel, but its main force is the running toward (Avodah line 150). This reading keeps the manual's guard as well: not on a crushed day, and stop if it flattens.

**Reaching for the highest.** The meditation manual's ladder climbs to the Essence (L09-L11), and the Gate of Unity spends whole gates above Atzilut. Kuntres HaTefillah warns against doing this with the grasping mind. "The main service now is in these matters most of all" (Tefillah line 71): creation from nothing, God filling and surrounding. The highest matters are known "in the inner binah", the heart's understanding, and a mind that reaches for them coarsens them (Tefillah line 72). The Avodah says the same to its reader: "only the levels near to him" (Avodah line 268). This reading therefore marks rungs L09-L11 as things the heart knows and the grasping mind does not reach. It does not settle the tension with the Gate of Unity. The SPINE keeps this as anchor G21.

**Prayer above study.** The SPINE records a dispute between prayer, and action and spreading outward (the Rashab against the Rebbe). These books give the Rashab's side at full strength. Study is "only the preparation" (Tefillah line 49), and learning without prayer can leave a man "far from Godliness, and even the opposite" (Tefillah line 80), "a deadly poison" (Tefillah line 81). Tanya 41-42 place contemplation before every mitzvah and in da'at in general. Only one side of this dispute is here.

**Your own words, and new readings.** The manual's sitting ends with "your own words to Him". Kuntres HaTefillah forbids inventing new readings of the prayer's words during prayer, and says it is truly like a prohibition for the young (Tefillah line 54). These are not the same thing. Speaking to God in your own words is prayer; building a clever interpretation is the mind admiring itself. The tension is kept, with this reading: speak to Him freely, and do not interpret while you pray.

**Posture, place and preparation.** The ontology has no rule for the body. The Rashab has several. Stand in one place and do not pace; pacing is "breadth of the outer spirit" (Tefillah line 88). Wait "two doorways" before you begin. Set worry down by reasoning with yourself, the way you do every night to fall asleep (Tefillah line 88). Spend half an hour on Chassidus in tallit and tefillin first. None of this is relaxation or breath-work. It is clearing a room for a King (Tefillah line 89).

**The animal soul can come to love.** The Tanya's beinoni regards the evil in himself as still "in its strength" (Tanya ch. 12-13). These books describe water-love that brings the natural mind itself to want Godliness (Avodah line 142; Tefillah line 77), and fire-love that lifts the natural fire into holiness (Avodah lines 149-150). The Rashab keeps the limit himself: the animal soul by its nature does not want Godliness, and all its rising is the Godly soul working on it. He leaves this open (Avodah line 180). The reading keeps the question open too.

**Who can do it.** The Mitteler Rebbe's Kuntres HaHitpa'alut puts the simple man's plain joy at the deepest place, where "the unlearned and the scholars are exactly equal". The Rashab says the work is "near to anyone who has a mind and a heart" (Tefillah line 67), and that no soul is unfit (Avodah line 228). Yet the young should not climb to contemplation before their time (Tefillah lines 107-108), and people in business get a different, shorter way (Tefillah line 115). Both statements stand.

**The worlds as the working matter.** The Mitteler Rebbe's Kuntres HaHitpa'alut hardly contemplates the worlds; its object is the self's own movement. These books put the cosmology back as the content of contemplation: the life in each creature by its nature (Avodah line 211), the kav, the higher and lower unity (Avodah line 221), the order of prayer from Pesukei deZimra to Baruch Shem (Tefillah line 85).
