## The shape of the range

1. **More of the Bible's human words for G-d, read as unity** (1654-1751). Near, full, high, pass, come, go out, walk, dwell, sad, eat, face, heart, spirit, soul, alive, wing. Each is a name for His joining of His lights, and each depends on us.
2. **Deed is the heart of it** (1752-1787). The fruit of study is the mitzvah; the fruit of the mitzvah is the unity. A mitzvah is whole when it has act, knowledge and intention.
3. **Attributes** (1788-1802). Against the Rambam's "only negative attributes": His attributes are not added to Him; "You are wise, but not with a known wisdom".
4. **The true unity: the Shema meditation** (1803-1816). Ibn Gabbai's own instructions for uniting at the Shema, and three ways to unite: the Shema, Torah study, and the mitzvot.
5. **Happiness is knowing the unity** (1817-1825). The end of the Part of Purpose.

## The true unity: how to unite at the Shema

This chapter (3:69) is the practical peak of the whole Part of Purpose. The Shema's unity is "to bind and unite the emanation, which is the Godhead ... in One"; "Hear" means "gather" (line 1803). One must unite "with intention and with a thought clean of any other thought, not to show any cutting or separation", for "His witness is at His side": the large Ayin of "Shema" and Dalet of "Echad" spell *ed*, witness (line 1803). And *ed* backwards is *da*, "know": "know how to unite, for the unity is the true knowledge asked of every person" (line 1803).

**The steps.** Ibn Gabbai's instructions (line 1804), in plain words:

1. At the first Name (the Lord), intend the Yud with its thorn, "for the thorn is not separate from the Yud at all", and include the hidden root, "so that the inwardness of the thought be in it".
2. At "our G-d", intend Binah (the first Heh).
3. At the second Name, intend the six attributes of the heart (the Vav), "truth and peace with their branches, included and united".
4. At "One", intend Malchut (the last Heh), "the place of the completion of the unity".
5. Lengthen the Dalet of "Echad", and raise Malchut in thought, by the same path by which it came down, and bind it above "to Ein Sof".

> והרי הייחוד תקוע במקום נאמן שם תקיעת הרעיון והמחשבה כי הוא הכל, והכל הוא (Avodat HaKodesh 3:69:2; line 1804)
> And so the unity is fixed in a faithful place, the place where the mind and thought are fixed, for He is the all, and the all is He.

6. Then, with "Blessed be the Name of His glorious kingdom forever", draw the light down again from the head of the Thought to its end.

**The hidden root runs through all.** The one who unites must "draw in his thought that soul [the hidden root] from the head of the Thought to its end, so that it is found in all of them":

> להודיע ולהעיד שהוא הנעלם יתברך הכל ואין עוד מלבדו (Avodat HaKodesh 3:69:3; line 1805)
> To make known and bear witness that He, the Hidden One, blessed be He, is all, and there is nothing besides Him.

**Joy is waiting for one who unites every day.** From the Zohar: "whoever unites this unity every day, joy is prepared for him from above" (line 1806). Ibn Gabbai: "by the unity he unites he gladdens G-d, so joy is prepared for him" (line 1806). This ties back to the chapter on joy (range 6).

*Ours.* This is, in effect, a pre-Chassidic "Shema meditation" with the same end as the Chabad one. Tanya ch. 41-44 and Shaar HaYichud teach the Shema as *bittul*: "the Lord is One" means there is nothing but Him, and the soul is "included" (nichlal) in Him. Ibn Gabbai's version is more technical (it walks through the sefirot), but its last move is the same: "He is the all, and the all is He ... there is nothing besides Him". The guide can offer a simple form of it as a technique, **the Shema from root to kingdom**, and hold the sefirot map as optional detail for those who study it. The key is the direction: up to the Infinite with "One", down to the world with "Blessed be the Name".

**Three ways to unite.** (lines 1806-1811) - **The Shema at its time.** Said at its time it is "like building the world at its hour"; said later it is "like reading Torah", which keeps the unity going but does not make it (line 1807). - **Torah study, Written and Oral together.** It "makes peace between the two Beloveds and unites them" (line 1809), "especially if the one who studies intends this, for then his Torah is a Torah of truth" (line 1810). - **Doing the mitzvot.** Each mitzvah matches a limb of the upper Man; doing it "gives strength to that upper limb and unites it in its place" (line 1811). Tefillin make the one who wears them "a throne and dwelling for the Shechinah" (line 1811).

**"Unite our hearts."** In the blessing before the Shema, "unite our hearts to love and fear Your Name": "from this he will come to unite the evil inclination with the good inclination, until they become one, and the bitter in it turns sweet and the bad good" (line 1814). And this is the aim: "to set right all opposites, to straighten them as they were in completeness in the Thought before they came out" (line 1814). *Ours.* This is the Tanya's "with both your inclinations" (ch. 9) and the beinoni's work of turning the animal soul's force to holiness (itkafya, ithapcha; ch. 27). Ibn Gabbai names the goal as restoring the opposites to their state in the upper Thought.

**No shame at the end.** "That we not be ashamed or disgraced forever": one who leaves this life having done the repair he was sent for "will not be ashamed in the world to come, for he did his mission" (line 1815). *Ours.* This ties to "alive in the task" (range 5).

## Deed is the heart of it

> כי פרי התלמוד ותכליתו מעשה המצות, ופרי מעשה המצות ותכליתן הייחוד (Avodat HaKodesh 3:66:15; line 1766)
> For the fruit of study and its purpose is doing the mitzvot, and the fruit of doing the mitzvot and their purpose is the unity.

**A mitzvah has three parts.** Like the upper Glory, which has "creation, formation and making", a mitzvah is whole when it has three: the physical act (bringing it from nothing into being); knowing its law from the Oral Torah (forming it); and "the intention at the time of doing it toward the unity, for its need" (making it complete) (line 1772). Then "it rises in the same way it was and still is" in the upper Thought (line 1772).

**But even a simple mitzvah works.** If only those who knew the intentions could be completed, "the whole nation" would be left out; so even a mitzvah done without the full intention gives completion, "for the nature of the mitzvot is according to the place from which they were hewn" (line 1770). *Ours.* This protects the guide from a trap: intention adds, but it is not a gate. A plain mitzvah is already joined to its root. Chabad teaches the same (Tanya ch. 39-41: a mitzvah without intention still rises, though intention gives it wings).

**Do before you understand.** "We will do and we will hear": "love woke them" to put the deed first (line 1759), because the deed is what rises from below and repairs (line 1766).

**Love with both inclinations.** "We are charged with setting right all things, to straighten them and sweeten them, as they were in the upper Thought before they were separated from there; so we are obligated to love even with the evil inclination" (line 1753).

**Two ways for the mind.** The mind is completed in two ways, "both inside the King's palace": the give-and-take of Oral Torah, "so as not to let the voice that did not stop be silent", and adding branches to the received wisdom (lines 1786-1787). "All draw from the place of love and all go to one place" (line 1787). And:

> כי כל עסק שאין בו ייחוד הוצאת הזמן בו אסור (Avodat HaKodesh 3:67:15; line 1787)
> For any occupation that has no unity in it, spending time on it is forbidden.

*Ours.* The guide does not adopt this as a rule; Chabad teaches that every permitted act can be done for unity (Tanya ch. 7, "all your deeds for the sake of Heaven"; "in all your ways know Him"). The inner point is the same: time is for unity.

## His attributes

**Not "is", not "is not".** The unity of the Torah "is neither affirmation nor negation" (line 1790). The Rambam allowed only negative attributes. Ibn Gabbai: the attributes are allowed, for three reasons (line 1798): G-d wants praise and prayer, which need them; they are "the ladder to rise and reach Him"; and they teach us to be like Him. But they are not added to Him:

> והוא לא יתרבה בהם אבל הוא אחד בהם, ולזה הותר לתארו בהם לפי שאינו חוץ ממנו שהוא והם דבר אחד (Avodat HaKodesh 3:68:12; line 1799)
> And He does not become many in them, but He is one in them; so it is permitted to describe Him by them, for they are not outside Him; He and they are one thing.

The Tikkunim: "You are wise, but not with a known wisdom; You understand, but not with a known understanding" (line 1800). R. Chanina was angry at the man who piled up praises because each one he chose was a single bounded side of an attribute; one should praise with the attributes that include the whole (line 1801).

*Ours.* This is the Chabad teaching on the sefirot exactly: "He and His causes are one" (hu vegoramav chad, the Tikkunim line quoted in Tanya ch. 4), and Patach Eliyahu (the Tikkunim passage quoted here) opens the Chabad siddur's morning prayers. The guide can hold both halves: praise Him by His ways, and know He is beyond every way.

## The unity in the Bible's human words

A selection of the readings in chapters 48-65:

- **Near.** "As for me, nearness to G-d is my good" (Ps. 73:28): "this is the final good and the acquired completion ... for this purpose man was created" (lines 1655-1656). An offering (korban) is so called because it "brings near" His powers to each other (line 1658).
- **Full.** The Bahir's parable: a king placed seven sons one above the other; the lowest said, "I will not sit below and be far from you"; the king said, "I will go round and see you all day" (line 1662).
- **Come.** Hillel: "If I am here, all is here." Ibn Gabbai reads it as the Shechinah speaking, and G-d saying: "If you come to My house, I will come to your house" (line 1676). "The Holy One does not come into the upper Jerusalem until He comes into the lower Jerusalem" (line 1677).
- **Walk, and the first contraction.** "The main dwelling of the Shechinah was in the lower worlds", so that "the Name fills upper and lower, there is nothing besides Him" (line 1682). After Adam's sin she withdrew; Abraham drew her back, but not fixed:

> כי נאסף כבוד השכינה ונקמט מכלל העולם לסבת חטאו של אדם וצמצמה עצמה ושרתה על מקום מיוחד שהוא אברהם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:54:1; line 1682)
> For the glory of the Shechinah was gathered and folded from the whole world because of Adam's sin, and she contracted herself and rested on one particular place, which is Abraham.

"The world was created only for its walks, to be drawn from above to below, to unite upper and lower" (line 1683). *Ours.* Ibn Gabbai uses the word *tzimtzum* (contraction) here, forty years before the Ari, for the Shechinah narrowing her presence onto one tzaddik. It is not the Lurianic cosmic tzimtzum, but it is the same inner picture Chassidus uses for the tzaddik as the place where the Presence rests (Tanya ch. 23, "the patriarchs are the chariot").

- **Sad.** "It grieved Him to His heart" (Gen. 6:6): like a king whose palace, built by an architect, did not please him (line 1702). "There is no sadness before the Holy One" in the inner chambers, "no sadness and no worry" there, only in the outer chambers where other forces hold (line 1706). The upper eye "is always open, always laughing, always glad" (line 1712).
- **Eat.** "When there is food below, there is food above" (line 1716). Elijah told R. Shimon that G-d, who does not eat, "changed His ways out of love for the bride, and ate and drank" in her house, as a groom eats at the bride's table (line 1719).
- **Face.** "You shall have no other gods before My face": "but My face shall be your G-d" (line 1688). "He and His Name are one thing" (line 1723).
- **Alive.** "He is the true living one, for He is life itself"; "the good portion is clinging to His Name, which is the true life" (line 1740).
- **Wing.** "One who comes to shelter in the Great Name shelters under these wings, and that is the clinging to Him" (line 1743). "I have taken off My robe" (Song 5:3): "I am with you face to face with no partition, and no stranger with us" (line 1745).
- **Limbs.** "The Torah gave G-d hands and feet, eyes and ears" so that a person will "complete his hands ... so that hands resemble hands"; "limb strengthens limb" (line 1750). "The likeness of limb to limb causes the clinging, and the clinging is the cause of life" (line 1751).

## Happiness is knowing the unity

> ההצלחה והאושר האמיתיים אשר הם פרי כל הבריאה וכל חפץ וכל ישע הם ידיעת הייחוד (Avodat HaKodesh 3:70:1; line 1817)
> The true success and happiness, which are the fruit of all creation and all desire and all salvation, are the knowledge of the unity.

The inner wisdom "makes the heart of those who know it burn to do the mitzvot in their completeness" (line 1819). From the Bahir: "whoever turns his heart from the affairs of the world and gazes at the work of the Chariot is received before the Holy One as if he prayed all day" (line 1820). One will stumble in it, and still: "one who wants to merit the way of life should bear the rebukes" (line 1821). The Tikkunim: the masters of the inner Torah are "children of love", and "the Shechinah never moves from them" (line 1823).

The last lines: G-d's purpose in creation was "the knowing and the clinging to Him", not yet completed. He will open the eyes of the blind "to gaze at the upper wisdom and cling to what they did not cling to in this world", and "make the darkness before them into light" (line 1824). *Ours.* Chabad's claim is the same, down to the verse: in the days to come "the earth will be full of knowing the Lord" (Rambam, end of Mishneh Torah; Tanya ch. 36-37).

## States of consciousness

- **He is all, all is He** (lines 1804-1805). The end state of the Shema meditation. Ties to Chabad's ein od milvado and S36-type nullification states. Proposed below.
- **Joy prepared from above** (line 1806). The joy that meets one who unites daily. Ties to "His joy in me" (range 6).
- **Sheltered under the wings** (line 1743). Clinging felt as shelter. Proposed below.
- **Nearness is my good** (line 1655). A simple state of wanting only nearness. Ties to the Tanya's "and I, nearness to G-d is my good" (ch. 49-50).
- **Sweetened opposites** (line 1814). The inner work of making the two inclinations one. Ties to Tanya ch. 27 and ithapcha.

## Meditation and practice

- **The Shema from root to kingdom** (line 1804). Proposed below.
- **The whole mitzvah** (line 1772). Act, know its law, and intend the unity. Proposed below.
- **Do, then hear** (line 1759). When unsure, do the mitzvah first, with love; understanding comes after.
- **If you come to My house** (line 1676). Go to the synagogue or study house as coming to His house; He comes to yours.
- **Gaze at the Chariot** (line 1820). Study of the inner Torah "as if he prayed all day": set time for it.

## The big human questions

**What is happiness?** Knowing His unity (line 1817).

**What is my life's mission?** The repair I was sent for; done, there is no shame at the end (line 1815).

**Does G-d grieve?** Only in the "outer chambers"; within, "no sadness and no worry", and the upper eye is always glad (lines 1706, 1712).

**Is my plain mitzvah worth anything without deep intention?** Yes; it is joined to its root by its nature (line 1770).
