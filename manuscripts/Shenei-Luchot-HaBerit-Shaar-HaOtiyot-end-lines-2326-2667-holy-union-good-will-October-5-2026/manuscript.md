The last pages of the Gate of Letters close the letter *kuf* (holiness) and then give the last three letters: *resh* for *ratzon* (good will, being easy to please), *shin* for *shetikah* (silence), *tav* for *teshukah* (longing). The Shelah ends by joining the three into one word, *shevach*, praise: silence, the nightly check, and desire.

## 1.1 Good will is the sign of the unity

The Shelah's teaching on *ratzon* (line 2561) is one of his clearest on the unity. G-d made the world by His good will,

> בנדבה בלי הכרח, רק משלימות מדת טובו להטיב לזולתו (line 2561)

"freely, with no compulsion, only from the fullness of His goodness, to do good to another." He also made the bad, but the bad serves the good: without judgment the good would not last, and so judgment itself turns into mercy.

> והכל נכלל ברצון הטוב, ובזה מובן סוד האחדות (line 2561)

"And all is included in the good will; by this the secret of the unity is understood." No bad comes down from above except to one who wakes it and "separates himself from the unity". Then the conclusion that gives the whole letter its point:

> כי הרצון מורה על אחדותו יתברך, והכעס הוא נמשך מאל זר (line 2561)

"The good will shows His oneness, and anger comes from an alien god." That is why the sages said the angry man is like an idol-worshipper (line 2553): anger is the inner experience of a second power. The Zohar he quotes (lines 2554-2556) says the angry man "uproots" his holy soul and seats an alien god in its place. For the guide this is the unity felt as mood: calm good will is the One; anger is two.

## 1.2 The letters as a chain

On speech, the Shelah quotes Reshit Chochmah and the Ramak (line 2589). The Sefer Yetzirah says G-d "fixed" the 22 letters in the mouth. The word *fixed* means a chain:

> שהוא כעין התוקע השלשלת ראשה אחד במקום אחד וראשה השני במקום אחר (line 2589)

"Like one who fixes a chain with one end in one place and the other end in another." However far apart the ends, move one and the whole chain moves.

> כי בהזכירו למטה אותיות אלו בתורתו או בתפלתו, מנענע ומעורר למעלה השרשים העליונים (line 2589)

"When he says these letters below in his Torah or prayer, he moves and wakes the roots above." This is why the prayers of the early sages were answered at once: they kept their mouths clean. This is the root of the Chassidic teaching on the letters of prayer (the Baal Shem Tov's "enter into the letters"), here with the Ramak's image.

## 1.3 The soul of the soul

From the Zohar on "my soul longs for You at night" (line 2650): "G-d is the spirit and soul of all, and Israel say: You are my soul and my spirit", "for He is the soul of the soul". Love is as strong as death because parting from Him would be like the soul parting from the body.

## 1.4 He knew there was none besides Him

A sharp note on Elisha ben Avuyah, called *Acher* ("the Other"). Why was he told "return, except Acher"? Because unlike Menashe, who worshipped idols out of anger, Acher

> הכיר האמת שם עצמו וידע בבירור שהוא יתברך המלך הכבוד סלה ואין עוד מלבדו, ומכוון למרוד בו (line 2356)

"knew the truth there himself, knew clearly that He is the King of glory and there is none besides Him, and meant to rebel." To say "there are two powers" after seeing that there is none else is false witness against oneself. Even so, the Shelah adds, Acher "should not have held himself back from mercy" (line 2357).

## 3.1 Return, even for the worst

The Zohar says one sin has no return. The Shelah, fearing that a reader will despair and "say: I am lost anyway" (line 2349), spends forty lines proving the opposite: Menashe, for whom G-d dug a tunnel under the Throne (line 2355); Elazar ben Durdaya, who put his head between his knees and wept until his soul left him (line 2357). His reading of the Zohar is that return for this sin is *hard* (it must mend the whole body, line 2362) and must reach deep, to Chochmah, "the root of return" (line 2381). His guards: fast in measure, only a set number, once a season, "so as not to afflict too much and come to sickness and danger" (line 2378); and the traits matter more than the fasts (line 2380). The daily part is a broken heart: "this regret is what rises above" (line 2379). *Ours:* the guide does not prescribe fasts or self-affliction; it keeps the daily regret, the refusal of despair, and "G-d joins a good thought to the deed" (line 2375). *Tie:* `cheshbon`, `own_words`, S77.

## 3.2 Three ways not to be angry

From R. Yitzchak of Acco, who got the answer in a Sabbath-night dream (line 2569): - **Look at who, not what.** If a fool insulted you, it is "like the chirping of birds"; if a wise man, it is for your good; if it is true, whoever says it, learn from it. - **Expect it** (line 2570): before dealing with a difficult person, resolve fully to bear every insult; then nothing takes you by surprise. Then leave him. - **Welcome it** (lines 2567-2568): insults are the lightest sufferings, with no loss of Torah or prayer; pray each day for humility to be glad in them.

And from *Chovot HaLevavot* (line 2576): when shamed, say "what you saw is nothing to what He has hidden of me." A sage sent a gift to the man who slandered him, "for you sent me a gift of your merits." A father's last command (line 2578): delay your anger one night. *Proposed technique:* "Look at who, not what". *Tie:* S15, S86.

## 3.3 The nightly check and release

"All his days are in return" (line 2653): sleep is a sixtieth of death, so return before sleep each night fulfils "return one day before your death". Check the day, mend it, forgive everyone who hurt you (line 2583), so "the days be whole and joined in the secret of good will". Four times of return: each night, each Friday, each eve of the new month, Yom Kippur (line 2653). And the family form (line 2664): "all his life let a person gather his children and household to wake and search the day's deeds." *Tie:* `cheshbon`. *Proposed technique:* "The nightly check".

## 3.4 Speech: two mouths

"With the mouth that speaks Torah it is not fit to speak idle things" (line 2615); R. Levi wished he had two mouths. Idle speech is like wasted seed (line 2591). The four groups that do not receive the Presence: scoffers, flatterers, liars, slanderers (lines 2593-2601); flattery includes doing good to be seen (line 2602). Speak with honor even of a hard argument in the Talmud: "the words are too deep for me" (line 2608). Never swear; say "without a vow" (line 2618). Never curse, not even yourself (line 2623). *Tie:* `lips`, S30.

## 3.5 No complaint against Heaven

"Let him not hint anything upward", like saying "why is this so" or "how did He make that": "for all is in wisdom, uprightness, truth and justice" (line 2625). Even the comfort "what can one do?" to a mourner is a complaint, as if one would if one could; "a person must accept the decree of the Place with love" (line 2635). *Ours:* the guide keeps the acceptance and the love; it does not shame honest grief (the Shelah himself notes David wept and fasted before the child died, line 2635).

## 3.6 Mikveh

Immersion "returns the soul to its place" (line 2505); every Friday, to take off the weekday and put on the Sabbath. The Shelah copies the Ari's intentions and a long confession (lines 2506-2518). The short form the guide can use: "as I purify my body shaped from clay below, so may the angels above purify" my soul (line 2514); afterward, "a new creature" (line 2521). *Ours:* the parts that accept the four death penalties in intention are the Shelah's and are not carried.

## 3.7 Holy union

The Shelah gives the marriage blessings as the seven lower sefirot, the ring as the *yud* of the Name, the two hands as the two *heh*s, the finger as the *vav* (line 2440). Before the wedding, bride and groom fast and return (line 2433). He tells his sons to learn by heart the laws and Reshit Chochmah ch. 17 (line 2454). Union at midnight, after Torah, with prayer for a holy soul (lines 2458-2485). *Ours:* the guide carries the dignity and the holiness in general terms; the details belong to their halachic and Kabbalistic setting.

## 3.8 Seclusion in one's four cubits

For one who cannot live in the Land (line 2539): a place of seclusion "to be alone with his G-d". Two kinds of holy place: a place of Torah and prayer, or a place apart from people; best is both, alone in the study hall (line 2542). A friend helps too (line 2543). *Tie:* technique 20.

## 3.9 Longing and song

Do every mitzvah in great joy, as a gift (line 2645). "One who loves Him with a whole heart will sing before Him" (line 2646), as Moshe, Miriam, David and Yehuda HaLevi did. Wake at midnight in the sweetness of remembering Him (line 2647). *Tie:* `niggun`, S52.

## 3.10 *Shevach*: silence, check, desire

The closing (lines 2652-2665): **silence** of mouth and heart (not even to think the bad), the nightly **check**, and **desire**, which means serving with both inclinations: want the opposite, then give it up for His honor, "and seek peace: with all your heart, with both your inclinations, to join them together" (line 2663). These three bind a person to G-d "as a man cleaves to his wife" (line 2651).

## 4. The big human questions

- **Can I come back?** Yes, always. "There is nothing in the world that cannot be mended by return" (line 2349). The door that seems shut is a test (line 2359).
- **Suffering and loss.** Accept in love; do not ask "why" as a complaint (lines 2625-2635). A child's death is not always about the child's worth; He knows who is ready to carry what (line 2633).
- **Love.** Love of G-d is greater than the love of women (line 2646); G-d is the soul of the soul (line 2650).
- **The point of a life.** Days count by what is in them: the righteous man "adds life every day" (line 2654).
- **The body and holiness.** The body's union, eating and speech are the three places where holiness is made or lost (lines 2449, 2591, 2495). "No holiness like the holiness of union, when it is in holiness" (line 2449).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **Anger as idolatry.** The Tanya (Iggeres HaKodesh 25) takes the same Zohar and the same verse: anger is like idol worship because in that moment faith has left, since all comes from G-d. The Shelah's *ratzon* teaching (line 2561) says the same from the side of will: good will is the unity felt.
- **Letters and roots.** The chain image (line 2589) is the ground of the Chassidic teaching on the letters of prayer and Torah; the Maggid and the Tanya (Iggeres HaKodesh 5; Tanya ch. 37) build on it.
- **Return.** Tanya ch. 29-31 and Iggeres HaTeshuvah take up the same Zohar on wasted seed and the same Rokeach penances. The Alter Rebbe (Iggeres HaTeshuvah ch. 3) replaces the fasts with charity for his generation; the Shelah limits them to seasons and adds that the traits matter more. Both answer the Zohar's "no return" the same way: it means very hard, not impossible.
- **Nightly return.** The Tanya's *Kriat Shema she'al hamitah* practice and the Rebbe's teaching on the nightly *cheshbon* agree; the Shelah's "whole days joined in good will" (line 2583) is a fine phrase for it.
- **Longing.** Tanya ch. 9 and 50 (the love like fire that longs to leave the body) is close to the Shelah's lover who wakes at night; Chabad adds the return (*shov*) that brings the longing back into the body's service.
- **Not carried.** Some lines on women and on non-Jews in these pages (e.g. 2332-2333, 2404-2405) and the extreme penances (2372, 2509-2518) are kept in the reading and not carried into the guide.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **Good will as the unity felt; anger as a second power** (line 2561): a teaching the guide can use with anyone.
2. **Look at who, not what; expect insult in advance; delay anger one night** (lines 2569-2578).
3. **The nightly check**, alone and with one's household (lines 2653, 2664).
4. **The chain of letters** (line 2589): why careful speech makes prayer strong.
5. **Return is always open** (lines 2349-2376), with the Shelah's own guard against over-affliction.
6. **Each mitzvah a gift; longing that sings** (lines 2645-2647).
