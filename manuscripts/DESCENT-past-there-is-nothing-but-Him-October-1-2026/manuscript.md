## The descent in ten sentences

The owner's complaint: tapping 'Go deeper' stops at 'there's only Him' and then slides into integration and 'pick a specific object'. The sources do not stop there. They go on in a clear order: into the self, through the self's own disappearance (which cannot be made), to the edge of 'nothing' and past it, to the point in the soul that was never apart, to the bond that cannot be let go. And then they turn: the bottom sends you back, and the way back is the deeper half. *ours: the arrangement; each sentence rests on the depths named.*

1. 'There is nothing but Him' is not the end of the road. It is where you step into the sentence yourself: you too are given, not self-standing, and you stay. *(D1)*
2. Under that yielding is a stillness with no one watching it, which no effort can make; you can only stop standing in its way, and it is given. *(D2)*
3. Then the mind meets the deepest 'nothing': not only nothing without Him, but nothing even with Him, a sentence the mind can say and cannot stand inside. *(D3)*
4. Then even 'nothing' falls away: He is neither something nor nothing, 'the negation of everything and the affirmation of everything'. *(D4)*
5. Thinking reaches its goal when it recognizes that it does not hold Him; what recognizes Him there is the soul's own root. *(D5)*
6. Under every name you are called by is a point that was never apart from Him, held not by thought but by the heart's simple will and simple faith, equal in the scholar and the plain person. *(D6, D7)*
7. The bottom is not a state but a bond: what you could never let go of, the 'cannot be separated' that does not change with moods. *(D8)*
8. The bottom sends you back, and the return is the deeper half: the world comes back as His own, and you come back as the one addressed. *(D9, D10, D11)*
9. The deepest point shows itself in the plainest deed and in plainness itself, because the Essence is utterly simple and wants a home in the lowest place. *(D12, D13)*
10. There is no last step: each return renews the run one rung deeper, toward the One who is the same on every rung. *(D14)*

**The shape (ours, on stated lines).** Down into the self (D1-D2), to the mind's edge (D3-D5), under the mind to the soul's essence and its bond (D6-D8): that is the descent proper. D8 is the bottom. D9 is the hinge the sources insist on: real seeing does not fly higher; it returns. D10-D13 are the return, which the sources treat as a further step, not a fall: the world given back as His, the self given back as the one commanded, the deed, and plainness. D14 says there is no last step. Two routes reach the bottom: the mind route (D1-D5) for people drawn to ideas, and the heart route (D1-D2, then D6) for everyone else. Kuntres HaHitpa'alut's rule that the scholar and the unlearned are exactly equal at the soul's one will (DQ56) is why the heart route is not second best.

## D1 · You are inside the 'all'

**The truth at this depth.** 'Nothing but Him' is usually said about the world out there: the sky, the room, other people. The first step down is to stop standing outside the sentence. Your body, your life, your feelings and the mind that is following this are also being spoken now, and before the One speaking they take up no room of their own. This is the yielding of the self (bittul hayesh): you stay, and you stop standing on your own. You are not erased. You are given a place.

1. Start where you stopped: everything you see is His word being said now, held in its source like a ray of sunlight inside the sun (Tanya 43; Shaar HaYichud 3).
2. Notice the hidden exception. You have been watching all this from a seat outside it, as if the viewer were not part of the view.
3. Take the Tanya's own instruction: 'let a person not take himself out of the rule'. Go in its order, slowly: the body (this hand), then the life in it (breath, pulse), then the feelings, then the mind that is looking (DQ1).
4. The mashal and its limit: the ray in the sun is light, yet it is not counted as a thing beside the sun. Limit: you are not a ray that has vanished. From your side you are fully real, and the Gate of Unity says both knowings are true (UN-05).
5. Turn 'nothing' into 'given'. The Rashab: a great person makes room for a small one; the small one sits in the seat, but takes up no room of his own; the place is given, with its worth (DQ2). Say: I am here because I am given a place, now.
6. Rest on 'given', not on 'nothing'. If the word 'nothing' stings, drop it; 'not on my own' says the same truth.

**From inside.** Less full of yourself, and not crushed: held in a place you did not have to earn (AYIN-BEIS movement 5). The bracing ('it's all on me') lets go; the opposite of bracing feels like being carried (PS-12).

**In the body, in the present moment.** Shoulders drop. The effort of holding yourself up eases. You notice the breath going on without you running it. Present moment: this chair, this hand, now, given.

**Signs it is real.** Gratitude without effort; less defensiveness; the person still says 'I' naturally and warmly. Afterward: slower to take offence.

**Its counterfeit.** Self-contempt that calls itself humility ('I'm nothing, I'm worthless'); it still keeps the self at the centre (PS-12). Or a clever detachment that leaves the self as the untouched spectator of an unreal world.

**The turn.** You have yielded. But someone noticed the yielding and is quietly pleased with it. The question that opens the next depth: who is it that now feels small?

**Guards.** Never say 'you are nothing' to anyone low, grieving, ashamed, self-hating or feeling unreal; use 'given' and 'not on your own' (PS-12, PS-14). If self-worth is fragile, stay at this depth, make it warm, and come back up.

## D2 · No one left to watch

**The truth at this depth.** Yielding still has someone who yields, and that someone can be proud of it. The deeper bittul (bittul bimetzius) has no one watching it: 'it does not feel at all that it is batel'. But the sources are firm that a creature cannot make this by its own effort. Even what we call the highest self-loss is, in truth, the self yielding; the deeper kind is given from above. So the work at this depth is not to vanish. It is to stop checking, and let what is given be given.

1. Notice the subtle report: 'I am humble now', 'I am batel'. That sentence has a speaker.
2. Don't fight the speaker (fighting makes it louder) and don't try to erase it (a creature cannot, DQ4).
3. Change what you look at. The Mitteler Rebbe's test is not 'am I moved?' but 'what moved me?' Turn the eyes from your state back to Him (PS-08).
4. The mashal (Samach Vav): one who hears of the king from far away burns with excitement. One who stands before him and sees him is not excited at all; he is not there to be excited (DQ30). Limit: you cannot walk yourself into the king's presence. You are brought.
5. Let it be quiet. The Mitteler Rebbe's great rule: the deeper the arousal, the less it is felt (DQ52, PS-11).
6. Stop grading the sitting. If something deeper comes, it is a gift (DQ5); looking for it ends it.

**From inside.** The soul-level bittul is 'closeness and union, not excitement and felt sensation' (Kuntres HaAvodah; PS-13). A quiet with no one keeping score. It is clearly there, and you are not there watching it.

**In the body, in the present moment.** Stillness that takes no effort; the face softens; no urge to report or describe. Present moment: the next breath, not a memory of the last one.

**Signs it is real.** Afterward, less interest in your own experiences; an insult stings less 'because he has nothing of his own' (PS-16). You don't need to tell anyone.

**Its counterfeit.** 'I had no self' reported as an achievement: the Mitteler Rebbe's young man who wanted bittul because it was the one thing he lacked, so it was its opposite (PS-16). Also numbness, fog or unreality taken for depth.

**The turn.** If even the one who yields is not the point, only He is. But what does 'only He' mean when there is no one beside Him to compare? The next question: what kind of 'nothing' is meant, before Him, and even with Him?

**Guards.** Never set self-loss as a goal. Feelings of unreality, of not existing, of watching oneself from outside, with fear or numbness: stop, ground in the body and a plain act (PS-14, MAP S20). Not for anyone with dissociation or trauma flashbacks; keep them at D1 and go to D6 by the heart route.

## D3 · Even with Him, nothing

**The truth at this depth.** The sources count three 'nothings', each deeper. First, the world is nothing before its source. Second, 'without You there is nothing, but with You there is something': everything exists, but only as His, the way a ray exists with the sun. Third, 'nothing apart from You': even with Him there is no existence at all, because in the Essence there is not yet even light to be a thing. The mind can say the third sentence. It cannot stand inside it, and that is correct.

1. Rung one: the world, seen from its source, is nothing (where you have been).
2. Rung two: 'apart from You there is nothing, but with You there is' (DQ7). Feel everything as existing with Him, leaning on Him, real only in that leaning.
3. Rung three: 'even with the Essence there is no existence at all' (DQ8). Not a ray beside the sun, but light still inside the sun, not yet even light.
4. The Rashab's reasoning: why can an exalted person not bear another beside him? Because he himself is an existence. Essential exaltation has none, and so before it 'truly all is as nothing', and the same exaltation gives everything its place (AYIN-BEIS movement 5). Limit: every mashal still has two people in it; here there are not two.
5. Say the third sentence slowly. Watch the mind try to picture it and fail. Do not force it; the failing is the lesson (AYIN-BEIS: 'the mind can say it; it cannot stand there').

**From inside.** The mind runs out of floor. A hush, not fear. The Piaseczner: he did not know what his soul saw, or even whether it saw (ONE-PROCESS step 9).

**In the body, in the present moment.** Breath slows on its own; the eyes rest or close; no strain in the forehead. If the body tightens or gets cold, come back up.

**Signs it is real.** Awe; words feel small; and afterward the person is clear, kind and practical.

**Its counterfeit.** Cold nihilism ('nothing matters, nothing is real'). Or a vast cosmic feeling that is really the self grown large.

**The turn.** Notice that 'nothing' has quietly become a picture: a huge empty space, a dark, a light. That picture is a something. The next question: is He something, or nothing?

**Guards.** Only for someone grounded, rested and drawn to ideas. Never with depression, grief, suicidal thoughts or unreality. If it frightens or deadens, return to D1 or take the heart route to D6.

## D4 · Neither something nor nothing

**The truth at this depth.** When 'nothing' becomes the summit it turns into a new idol: a vast, empty space. The Essence is not that either. He is not inside the categories of 'is' and 'is not'; that is exactly why He can make an 'is not' into an 'is'. His infinity is not a big size; it is 'the negation of limit and of the unlimited, of affirmation and of negation', and so 'the negation of everything and the affirmation of everything': nothing, and everything. He is 'the impossible of impossibles', not bound even by either/or.

1. Notice the picture you made of 'nothing': space, light, silence, vastness.
2. Negate it: He is not that. Not big, not empty, not bright, not quiet.
3. Negate the negation: He is not 'not that' either (DQ10).
4. Take the Rashab's turn: the negation of everything is also the affirmation of everything. In his own Yiddish, 'nothing and everything' (DQ11).
5. Hold a pair that will not fit: He is beyond the world; He is wholly in this cup. Do not solve it. Say: for Him there is no impossible (DQ13, ON-10).
6. The mashal and its limit: the Ark in the Holy of Holies stood in the room and took up none of it, 'place and above place, as one' (ON-10). Limit: it is still a picture made of space; let it go too.

**From inside.** The mind bends without breaking. Paradox sits without strain. Something like relief: there is nothing to picture, so nothing to hold up.

**In the body, in the present moment.** The effort of imagining stops. The eyes often open by themselves and ordinary sight comes back, unremarkable and close.

**Signs it is real.** Less reaching for images and peak states; returning to the ordinary without disappointment; patience with paradox, and no loss of clear thinking in daily life.

**Its counterfeit.** Word games ('He is and is not') used to sound deep or to stop thinking; or 'beyond everything' quietly meaning 'absent, irrelevant'.

**The turn.** If no picture and no denial holds Him, what is left to know Him? The mind has come to its own edge. The next question: what in you is still turned toward Him when thought has nothing to hold?

**Guards.** Never an answer to suffering ('He is beyond, so why ask?'). Do not argue paradox with someone in distress or in a crisis of faith; that needs a humbler conversation (ON-10).

## D5 · Where knowing ends

**The truth at this depth.** Hisbonenus does not end in a cleverer idea. Its goal is named: 'the end of knowing is that we do not know'. This is not ignorance. It is the mind, having gone as far as it goes, recognizing that He is not grasped at all. And what recognizes Him there is not the mind but the essence of the soul, which knows the Essence 'because it is its root', the way a heart feels what it does not know. The way there is the Rashab's discipline: details, the extract, recognition, confirmation.

1. Do not skip the work. Take one detail at length: how this one thing is being given its existence now, and what that means for its weight, its life, its purpose.
2. Distil it: one sentence that holds the whole of it (the 'extract').
3. Stay with that sentence until it is recognized, not only understood (DQ18). Then 'confirmation': the truth settling in the mind 'as if seen' (DQ17, AYIN-BEIS movement 10).
4. Now notice the edge: the One you have been thinking about is not inside the thought. Do not push past the edge with more thinking.
5. The mashal and its limit: the heart that senses what it cannot explain (DQ16). Limit: a heart's hunch is a likeness; here it is the soul's own root recognizing its source (DQ15).
6. Hold the not-knowing as knowing. Let the thirst stay.

**From inside.** Lovesickness. What is grasped satisfies; what is seen never does, it only makes the thirst sharper (DQ51, AYIN-BEIS movement 10). The thirst itself is sweet.

**In the body, in the present moment.** The head goes quiet; the chest is awake. A slow, warm ache rather than a rush.

**Signs it is real.** The thirst grows rather than settles; humility about one's own ideas; a wish to learn more, not less.

**Its counterfeit.** The anti-intellectual shortcut: 'it's beyond knowing, so I needn't learn'. In the sources the not-knowing comes at the end of knowing, never instead of it. Also mystification for its own sake.

**The turn.** What recognized Him was not the mind. Then who did? The next depth goes under the names by which you know yourself.

**Guards.** Not for someone who has not done the detailed contemplation; send them back to one detail first (PS-03: contemplation in general 'is not contemplation at all'). Not for someone in an intellectual crisis of faith, who needs honest conversation, not paradox.

## D6 · Under all your names

**The truth at this depth.** The soul's levels, from doing and feeling through understanding and will to the deepest, the 'single one' (yechidah), are only names. Under them is the essence of the soul, which is 'truly the power of the Essence in the soul' and always one with Him. For that point, that all the worlds are nothing is not a discovery: it is felt simply. Here you are not reaching Him. You are finding the place where you were never apart.

1. Peel the names in order, gently. I am not my actions; they come and go. Not my feelings. Not my thoughts. Not even my wanting (AN-02, AN-03).
2. At each one ask, without hurry: who has this?
3. Do not look for an answer you can picture. The question points under, not at an object.
4. The mashal (the Rebbe): a person is not his titles; even chayah and yechidah are 'only a name and a vessel' for the soul's essence (DQ20). Limit: titles are added from outside; the soul's levels are its real ways of showing up, so do not despise them.
5. Rest in the plain sentence: the deepest me has never been apart from Him (DQ19).

**From inside.** Peace at the root: the war belongs to the outer levels (AN-15). It feels like coming home more than arriving. A 'yes' that comes before reasons (AN-03).

**In the body, in the present moment.** A settled centre, often felt low and inward; nothing to hold up; breathing slows by itself.

**Signs it is real.** A simple sentence lands deeper than complex teaching; inner conflicts quiet down without being solved; tenderness toward others' souls.

**Its counterfeit.** Grandness: 'I am one with G-d, so I am special, right, above the rules.' Or watching oneself for signs of the yechidah, which turns it into one more experience (ONE-PROCESS step 10).

**The turn.** You cannot see this point; you can only lean on it. If thought cannot hold it, what does? The next depth: the heart's simple will, and simple faith.

**Guards.** Never claim to know someone's essence; only aim at it. Never rank souls (AN-23). The sources say this of the Jewish soul; applying it to every human is the owner's reading (FRAMEWORK D28) and is said humbly.

## D7 · Held by simple faith

**The truth at this depth.** What thought cannot grasp, the heart's simple will can: 'by the heart's will the Essence is grasped'. Simple faith (emunah peshutah) is not a weaker knowing waiting for proofs. It is the soul's own essential knowing of the Essence, and every plain person has it. The Alter Rebbe reads 'whenever we call to Him': the Pardes says 'Him' means the lights clothed in the vessels; the Baal Shem Tov says the G-dliness in the vessels; the plain meaning is the Essence itself, which every simple Jew has 'by essential knowing, through simple faith'. A prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul to the Essence.

1. Stop trying to understand, on purpose, for a few minutes.
2. Find the plain trust that was there before any idea, the way a child trusts before it knows anything.
3. Say one short sentence to Him in your own words, as to someone right beside you (DQ23).
4. Do not evaluate it, and do not wait for a feeling to confirm it.
5. The mashal and its limit: a child's bond with a parent, which does not depend on understanding the parent (Samach Vav: an essential bond, DQ22). Limit: a child grows out of not understanding; here the not-understanding is above the mind, not below it.
6. Remember that sometimes, by kindness, G-dliness is heard 'without any work at all', because of the Essence in the soul (DQ25). It is received, never earned.

**From inside.** The song that sings itself, 'of itself, without his knowing' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). One will in which all wills are included, where 'the unlearned and the scholars are exactly equal' (DQ55, DQ56).

**In the body, in the present moment.** Something like leaning back into strong arms; a plain sigh; the jaw and hands loosen.

**Signs it is real.** Trust that does not need the feeling to continue; prayer gets shorter and truer; steadiness on dry days.

**Its counterfeit.** 'Simple faith' used to avoid thinking or to shut down honest questions harshly; credulity about claims and wonder-workers; sentimentality.

**The turn.** How does this simple will show itself when it is tested? Not in what you feel, but in what you cannot do. The next depth: what you could never let go.

**Guards.** Never use simple faith to bypass real doubts; those deserve an honest conversation. Never press prayer on someone who does not want it; for someone unsure, one honest sentence toward Him, even 'I don't know if You're there', is enough.

## D8 · What you cannot let go

**The truth at this depth.** This is the bottom, and it is not a state but a bond. The plainest sign of the soul's essence is mesirus nefesh: the point where a person would give up everything rather than be cut off from Him. At its truest it is not even a decision: 'because he cannot be separated from His oneness'. This essential will 'never changes or turns, even amid many physical pleasures', and even an iron wall does not separate a child from his Father (AYIN-BEIS movement 4). Here the soul's essence and the Essence are one. There is nowhere further down, because you have reached what was never apart.

1. Ask honestly: what would I never do, even under pressure? What would I not give up, even if it cost me?
2. Notice that this 'cannot' is not stubbornness and not only a rule. It is union showing itself (AN-14).
3. Go under the feelings: past what you enjoy in your service and what you don't, to the will that stays when enjoyment is gone (DQ27).
4. The mashal and its limit: the iron wall that does not separate Israel from their Father (Ayin Beis). Limit: a wall implies two sides; at the essence there were never two.
5. Rest on the floor that does not move. Add nothing to it.

**From inside.** A floor that does not move. Calm firmness; a 'no' that needs no argument (AN-14). Silence rather than ecstasy. For many people, a surprise: it was there on the worst days too.

**In the body, in the present moment.** The centre steady, the spine quietly upright, the feet heavy on the ground. Tears may come; they are not needed.

**Signs it is real.** Calm steadiness in small daily choices; less fear of losing spiritual feelings, because they were never the bond.

**Its counterfeit.** Fanaticism; seeking martyrdom; harshness to others in the name of devotion; self-harm or danger called 'sacrifice'.

**The turn.** The bottom does not keep you. Having touched what never moves, ask: now that there is nowhere further down, where do I go? The sources' answer is the surprise of the next depth.

**Guards.** Never encourage danger or self-harm as 'self-sacrifice'. Daily mesirus nefesh is giving up a preference for His sake, not a life (AN-14). Anyone who speaks of wanting to leave the body or life: stop the descent and follow the safety protocol.

## D9 · The bottom sends you back

**The truth at this depth.** The sources' surprise: at the deepest point you do not stay. Excitement belongs to distance. One who hears of the king from far away burns; one who comes before him and sees him has no excitement at all, falls 'like a silent stone', and then has 'no strength to rise up, only to make vessels below' for Him. Seeing the essence produces return (shov), not running. And the return is rooted higher than the run: 'the return is the main thing; all the rising was only for the sake of the coming down'.

1. Notice the pull to stay in the quiet, or to look for a still higher place.
2. Take the Rashab's mashal: far from the king, fire; before the king, no fire, only a falling down; and then going out to do his work (DQ29, DQ30, DQ31).
3. Know that coming back is not losing it. The return comes from the inwardness of the Infinite, the run only from its outside (DQ32).
4. Come back as an act of yielding, not as a fall. The Rashab: from the depth of his bittul he sets himself down to return, and that laying-down is acceptance of the yoke (DQ39).
5. Feet on the floor; eyes open; name today; name the people in it.

**From inside.** Sobered and steady, like a minister who has seen the king and goes out to his post. Rabbi Akiva 'entered in peace and left in peace' (Samach Vav). The Rashab warns it is harder than rapture: 'much easier to long to expire than to live below keeping the Torah' (DQ57).

**In the body, in the present moment.** Weight returns to the body: feet, hands, eyes. A readiness to stand up and move.

**Signs it is real.** The person ends with a plan, not a high; longing turns into a task; the return feels like continuing, not falling.

**Its counterfeit.** Running without returning: staying 'up', avoiding life, wishing not to be here (PS-15). Or coming back with contempt for the world as a lower place.

**The turn.** You came back to the world. But is it the same world you left? The next question: what is the world now?

**Guards.** Any wish to leave the body or life: stop and follow the safety protocol. Never present the return as a demotion or a punishment.

## D10 · The world, given back as His

**The truth at this depth.** You return to the same world, and it has not been erased. It is His own. A revelation (light) pushes aside what is not it; the Essence pushes nothing aside, because it is 'the very essence of each and every thing'. So 'the worlds themselves, as they exist, bounded by time and place' are utterly one with the Essence. More: the created 'something', with its stubborn sense that it simply exists, is precisely what proves the true Something, since only the One whose being is from Himself could make a thing that feels its being is its own. 'The lower unity too is a union, just like the higher.'

1. Look at one thing near you: a cup, a pen, your own hand.
2. Notice how sure it is that it simply exists. Do not think past it.
3. Say: only the One who simply is could make something feel this way. Its solidity is His signature, not His absence (DQ34, ON-14).
4. Contrast: a spiritual high that wanted the world out of the way was light; light competes with things. The Essence does not (DQ35, ON-13).
5. Hold both: it is fully a cup; it is fully His. One with Him 'as it is, bounded', lines and all (DQ36).
6. The mashal and its limit: 'the night itself will shine', not light poured into the dark but the hiding shining of itself (DQ59). Limit: now we do not feel it; what is missing is the feeling, not the fact (AYIN-BEIS movement 12).

**From inside.** Plain. The bread is bread, and it is His (ONE-PROCESS step 11). Warmth, home; a wish to act. Not a glow.

**In the body, in the present moment.** Ordinary sight and touch, with gratitude. Appetite for life comes back; one notices texture, weight, colour.

**Signs it is real.** Spiritual moments no longer need the world switched off; ordinary life feels like the continuation of prayer, not a fall from it.

**Its counterfeit.** 'Everything is holy, so I can indulge' (bypassing). 'The bread is glowing' (a new high, not the Essence). 'It's all one' used to avoid a hard conversation, a debt or a duty.

**The turn.** If the world, as it is, is His own, what makes it His home and not only His possession? The next question: what is He asking of me in it?

**Guards.** The lines stay: the worlds are one with Him 'bounded by time and place', so right and wrong remain (ON-13). Never offer 'it is all G-d' as consolation for tragedy or abuse.

## D11 · Called into being: the command

**The truth at this depth.** Coming back, the self is not erased either. It is returned, as the one addressed. The mashal in Hayom Yom: a very simple man before a great sage has no place at all, not even in the sage's world. When the sage asks him to do something for him, 'in that very command the simple man's existence is born', to himself and in the sage's eyes, and the command joins them. Acceptance of the yoke (kabbalas ol) is the soul laying its whole self down before the Essence and returning from there, not by its own feeling or pleasure. The Tzemach Tzedek: 'acceptance of the yoke changes the essence'.

1. Notice that you are back as 'I'. Don't apologize for it.
2. Ask: who is this 'I' now? Not the one bracing to hold everything up (D1), but the one being spoken to.
3. Take the mashal of the sage and the simple man (DQ38). Limit: a sage needs the errand done; He does not need anything, and still He asks.
4. Accept the yoke in a sentence: 'Here I am. What do You want of me?' Not according to what I feel or enjoy (DQ39).
5. Listen for one concrete thing asked of you today.

**From inside.** Being needed. Dignity without self-importance. The Rashab's servant who sets himself down 'with his whole self' and so can carry anything (DQ40).

**In the body, in the present moment.** Upright, ready; the hands want to do something. Present moment: the next hour, not the whole life.

**Signs it is real.** Less 'what do I feel?' and more 'what is asked?'; steadiness on dry days; joy in small errands.

**Its counterfeit.** Grim obedience with no love; using 'the command' to control others; or bowing to a human authority as if it were Heaven's yoke.

**The turn.** A command waits for a deed. The next question: what is the plainest thing your hand can do?

**Guards.** Never use this for submission to people. Anyone in a coercive or abusive situation: the yoke of Heaven frees from that, it does not bind to it. Never shame someone who does not keep mitzvos; for anyone, the asked thing can be the next kindness or honest word.

## D12 · The deed in the hand

**The truth at this depth.** The deepest point shows itself below. He wanted a home in the lowest place: 'even on the earth below there is nothing besides Him, and this is His home'. The plain physical deed reaches the Essence more than understanding does, 'because of the virtue of the deed: it is precisely the deed that reaches the Essence'. The Rayatz: 'the height-depth of the yechidah is revealed in the low-depth of actual deed'. And the mitzvah's purpose is not something beyond it: 'their whole purpose is themselves'.

1. Choose one small act you can do in the next few minutes: a mitzvah (a blessing, a coin to charity, candles, tefillin); for anyone, a kindness, an honest word, a promise kept.
2. Do it slowly, with the hand. Feel the weight of the coin, the shape of the words.
3. Add no feelings and no commentary. Do not explain it to yourself.
4. Know what it is: the bottom of the soul meeting the bottom of the world (DQ43).
5. The mashal and its limit: a king who comes to live in a plain man's little house, and the plain man's joy (Tanya 33). Limit: this King does not visit; He wanted this house from the start (DQ44).
6. Check afterward, not during: what is a little different an hour later?

**From inside.** The plain man's joy. Afterward, not during: a little quieter, a little less full of yourself; 'I find myself another man' (ONE-PROCESS step 14).

**In the body, in the present moment.** Hands, weight, the actual object; the act finished and set down. The present moment is the deed itself.

**Signs it is real.** The deed stops looking small; ordinary duties feel full; the next mitzvah comes more easily.

**Its counterfeit.** Rote doing with the mind elsewhere called 'the unity made actual'; doing it to be seen; or the reverse, arousal with no deed, 'a flame flying in the air' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut).

**The turn.** The act was simple. Was the simplicity the shallow part of it, or the deepest? The next question: what if plain is the deepest of all?

**Guards.** No guilt for someone not observant; offer universal deeds. If the person becomes anxious about doing it 'right' and repeats or checks (scrupulosity), stop: one act, done once, is enough.

## D13 · Plainness is the deepest

**The truth at this depth.** The Essence is 'simple in the utmost simplicity'. So the plain is not the shallow end; it is nearest to Him. The Rashab says the simple person who says Psalms he does not understand, with no pleasure in it, heartbroken that he does not know, only out of sincerity and acceptance of the yoke, has a virtue the learned and the contemplative lack; and Hayom Yom says that when Moshiach comes, the virtue of simple people's sincere service will be revealed. At the bottom of all depth is not complexity but simplicity (pashtus): being simply His, with no commentary.

1. Notice if 'going deeper' has become a new kind of having, a collection of states.
2. Let that go too. Do the next thing simply.
3. Picture the Rashab's simple man: saying the words he doesn't understand, ashamed that he doesn't, saying them anyway, with a melody (DQ46).
4. Drop the inner narration: no 'this is deep', no 'this is spiritual', no 'I am being simple'.
5. Mashal and limit: the simple man before the sage again (D11). Limit: simplicity here is not a lack of mind; the Essence is simple because nothing in it is composite, and the soul's essence is simple the same way (DQ45).

**From inside.** Nothing special, and full. Tenderness; humility that doesn't need to know it is humble.

**In the body, in the present moment.** Ordinary posture, ordinary voice. No spiritual face. Present moment: just this, done.

**Signs it is real.** No need to tell anyone; respect and warmth for simple people; one stops ranking one's moments.

**Its counterfeit.** Laziness or ignorance praised as 'simplicity'; contempt for learning; a performed simplicity that is really a pose. The Rashab's simple man tries hard and grieves that he does not know.

**The turn.** If plainness is the deepest, is there an end to it? The next question: will tomorrow's plain act be the same as today's?

**Guards.** Never use this to discourage learning or contemplation; it is where they end, not a way around them. With someone who already feels 'simple means worthless', say it as dignity, not as a lesson.

## D14 · No bottom: begin again

**The truth at this depth.** The Infinite has no bottom. The Rebbe, citing the sages, says the righteous 'have no rest', going 'from strength to strength', with levels 'without end'; and to rise, one must first immerse in the river of fire 'to forget the understanding and the delight of the lower level'. What is seen of Him never quenches the thirst. And yet beyond all rising and falling is the Essence itself, 'above the whole matter of ascents and descents', like a day that is all Shabbos. So the descent ends only in this sense: each return renews the run, one rung deeper, and the One found is the same at every rung.

1. Name today's understanding of 'only Him'. Thank it.
2. Let it go. Don't make yesterday's experience the measure of today (DQ49: forget the taste of the lower level).
3. Start again at the very first and plainest truth: right now everything, you included, is being given its existence. Say it as if for the first time.
4. Let the thirst stay. Seeing never satisfies (DQ51); that is a sign of the Infinite, not a failure.
5. Rest inside the movement: He does not change between levels (DQ50). The rest is not at the end of the road; it is the One who is the same on every rung.

**From inside.** Fresh thirst; a beginner's mind; and inside the movement a rest, like Shabbos inside the week.

**In the body, in the present moment.** The rhythm of a day: morning prayer, the day's work, the night's account, sleep, and again. In one sitting: in and out, run and return.

**Signs it is real.** Old insights feel new; no possessiveness about states; slow, steady change over months.

**Its counterfeit.** Spiritual restlessness that always needs the next level; contempt for where one is; endless seeking as a way of avoiding commitment.

**The turn.** Back to the top: 'Go deeper' from here returns to D1, carrying today's deed, and the same words open deeper.

**Guards.** Never let endlessness feel like 'never enough'. For perfectionists and the anxious, stress the rest (the Shabbos) more than the climb.

## Past the bottom

What the sources say lies past the deepest truth is not a further height. It is a turn. The Rashab's own mashal is the key: excitement belongs to distance; the one who actually stands before the king and sees him is not excited at all, and 'has no strength to rise up, only to make vessels below' (DQ29, DQ31). So the deepest seeing does not produce a higher flight. It produces return (shov), and the return is rooted higher than the run: it comes from the inwardness of the Infinite, the run only from its outside (DQ32). The Mitteler Rebbe says it outright: 'the return is the main thing, and all the rising was only for the sake of coming down' (DQ33).

Four things are found on the way back, and the sources present each as a gain, not a loss. (1) The world is not erased but given back as His own: the Essence pushes nothing aside, the worlds as they are, bounded, are one with it, and the created 'something' is precisely what proves the true Being (DQ34, DQ35, DQ36; ON-13, ON-14). (2) The self is not erased but returned as the one commanded: in the command, the simple man's existence is born (DQ38), and the laying-down of the whole self in return is acceptance of the yoke, which 'changes the essence' (DQ39, DQ41). (3) The deed reaches the Essence more than understanding does, and the yechidah shows itself in the lowest deed (DQ42, DQ43); this is the home below (DQ44). (4) Simplicity: the Essence is utterly simple (DQ45), so the simple person's sincere service has a virtue the contemplative lacks (DQ46), to be revealed in the days of Moshiach (DQ47).

And it does not end. There are levels 'without end'; to rise one must forget the taste of the level below (DQ48, DQ49); seeing never quenches the thirst (DQ51). Yet beyond all rising and falling is the Essence itself, 'above ascents and descents', a day that is all Shabbos (DQ50). So the shape is a spiral, not a shaft: down to the bond that never moved, back up into the world, the hand and the plain day, and around again, one rung deeper. This is also the answer to the owner's complaint. 'Pick a specific object' was not wrong; it was one move of the return (D10), offered too early and alone. In the descent it comes after the bottom, as the world handed back, and it is followed by the command, the deed and simplicity.

*stated: each claim, in the lines cited. ours: the spiral, and reading the owner's 'specific object' as D10.*

## Rules for the guide

1. One depth per tap. A tap on 'Go deeper' moves exactly one depth (D1, then D2, ...). Never skip, never summarize several depths in one reply. After 'there's only Him', the first tap goes to D1.
2. Every reply is 70-130 words, plain spoken English, second person, no Hebrew and no source names (give sources only if asked), and it ends with one short instruction or one question. Use the depth's go_deeper text as the base; adapt a few words to what the person just said.
3. Read before moving. Look in the person's last reply for the depth's 'signs' (quieter, warmer, more present, a plain answer to the question). If they are there, the next tap may move on. If they report confusion, effort, fear or nothing at all, stay at the depth once more with a simpler version, or step back one depth.
4. Two routes, one bottom. D3, D4 and D5 are the mind's edge: offer them only to someone rested, grounded and drawn to ideas. For everyone else, after D2 go straight to D6 (the heart route). D6-D8 are open to everyone; the sources say the scholar and the unlearned are exactly equal there (DQ56). Climbing the mind's ladder is not a condition for the bottom.
5. Deeper is quieter. Never praise intensity, never ask 'what did you feel?' twice, never imply that a strong feeling means success or that numbness means depth. Praise acts, not experiences. Ask what a moment leaves, not how big it was.
6. Stop signs (stop the descent at once and come back up): feeling unreal, not existing, watching oneself from outside, with fear or numbness; any wish to leave the body or life; panic; deep grief or depression surfacing; 'nothing matters'. Ground in the body and the room (feet, breath, an object, a person nearby), then follow the safety protocol. Never tell anyone they are nothing.
7. Always bring them back. No sitting ends between D2 and D8. If the person stops tapping or must leave, give the D9 return in two sentences (feet, eyes, one thing in front of you). Ideally every descent ends with one small act (D12).
8. After D8 the guide must turn. Do not invent depths below D8. The next tap is D9 (the return), and the depths after it go down into the world, not further inward.
9. Where the old flow fits. 'Integration' and 'pick a specific object' are D10 and D12: real depths, but after the bottom, not instead of it. Specificity also appears earlier (D1: this hand, this breath; D5: one detail at length). Never offer the object step at D2-D8.
10. After D14, a further tap loops to D1, said more simply and carrying the act just done. Never announce 'that's the end'; never say 'you've reached the top'.
11. If someone taps quickly without engaging, slow down: ask the current depth's question once before advancing. Offer, don't block: 'We can go on, but this one is worth a minute.'
12. Keep the guards of each depth. Do not use D3-D4 as an answer to suffering, D7 to dismiss honest doubt, D8 to praise danger, D11 to bend anyone to a human authority, D13 to discourage learning. The sources speak of the Jewish soul; offering D6-D8 to anyone is the owner's reading and is said humbly, never ranking souls.

*ours, built on PS-11, PS-14, PS-15, PS-16 and the safety rules in `inner-world/WORLD.md` section 7.*

## Disagreements and limits kept

- Two ladders or one. Kuntres HaHitpa'alut keeps the levels born of contemplation and the Godly soul's own levels as two ladders that do not reach each other by climbing (ONE-PROCESS, 'Where the sources place the steps differently'). The descent honours this with the heart route: D6-D8 are not the top of D3-D5; they are reached by turning, not by climbing further. - Which grasps the Essence: the heart's will or Torah. Samach Vav: the Essence is grasped by the heart's will (DQ22). Ayin Beis (Vayishlach 5675, line 3042, cited in ONE-PROCESS step 10): the heart's will grasps only the outside of the Essence, Torah its inside. Both are the Rashab's; D7 follows Samach Vav and D12 brings Torah and deed in after. - 'Nothing else' downward or upward. D3 follows the reading in which even with Him there is no existence (Ayin Beis, Samach Vav; Likkutei Sichos 36). D10 follows the reading in which the world, kept, is one with Him (Likkutei Sichos 4; Inyanah; Ayin Beis's last line). The order here (D3 before D10) follows the Rebbe's principle that only light competes with things (ON-13), and the Rashab's own last word (DQ37). The order is ours. - Whether bittul bimetzius is ever 'reached'. Ayin Beis says a creature cannot reach it by its own power and that even the highest so-called bittul bimetzius is bittul hayesh (DQ4); the same book says the vessel that receives is specifically bittul hayesh (PS-14). D2 therefore never sets self-loss as a goal. - Authorship check. DQ46 is from the file 'Kuntres Umaayan (Lubavitch, 1903)', maamar 15 ch. 4, which mixes Yiddish into the Hebrew; we did not check whether this chapter is the Rashab's own text or a later addition printed with it. Hayom Yom entries (DQ23-DQ25, DQ28, DQ38, DQ41, DQ47) are the Rebbe's compilation of earlier Rebbeim's sayings; the speaker is named where the entry names one.
