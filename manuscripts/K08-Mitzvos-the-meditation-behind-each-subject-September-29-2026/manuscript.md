## How to read these

Every concept in Chassidus points to the unity of God, which cannot be grasped by definition; it can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (*hisbonenus*). So each subject is given here as a meditation in the Chabad way, not as an idea. **Hold** is one line of teaching from the entry's own sources. **The move** thinks it through slowly: it opens the line detail by detail, gives the source's own picture and says where the picture breaks, and brings it down to you, here, now. It uses only what the entry's derivation and passages say. **Stay** binds the mind to the line, as Tanya 3 describes da'as: the pauses are for thinking, and a stray thought is not argued with, only left for the line. **From inside** describes the glimpse and never asks for it; if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts. **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, adds one mitzvah or act of Torah in the person's measure, and gives a line to return to.

**Tie** says whether the move rests on derivation steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our own reading; the steps are listed. Where the entry marks a subject's tie as thin or partly thin, the meditation is kept modest and says so in one line. Where the sources differ on what the move touches, the difference is kept, not smoothed. Anchors are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly, OCR spelling included.

## S089 · The mitzvah as bond with the supernal will · מצוה לשון צוותא

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F05, F07, F08. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** A mitzvah is not a good deed you offer Him. His command itself joins you to Him: not to a glow of Him, but to Himself.

**Hold.** 'Mitzvah is from tzavta, joining: through the mitzvah a person is joined with the Essence of Or Ein Sof.' (the Rayatz, Sefer HaMaamarim 5689)

**The move.** Open it: creation, the Maharash says, is 'only a mere radiance', but through the mitzvot there is a joining with the Essence itself; the Alter Rebbe says what is drawn down in them is not a glow but 'I Myself'. And Hayom Yom adds that it makes no difference whether the command is great or small. The Rayatz's picture is a king who commands a commoner who had no standing before him, and by that command gives him his very existence before the king; it is only a picture for the ear, since He is no king of flesh, but it keeps this: the command itself makes the bond. Bring it down to one small mitzvah you will do today, here, now, and the word 'vetzivanu', 'and commanded us', in its blessing: in that word you are joined to Him Himself.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'joined with the Essence', and in the pauses think of the one small mitzvah. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being wanted: the command itself is the closeness. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say one blessing today with your lips, slowly, stopping for a moment on 'vetzivanu'; then do that mitzvah; and return during the day to 'and commanded us'.

**Where the sources differ.** What the mitzvah joins is put differently: in Chabad, the person to the Essence, from above; in Chernobyl and Berditchev, the person's own cleaving, without which the mitzvah is a body without a soul; in Ger, the soul's life drawn into the limb. And the Maharash himself says elsewhere that 'the whole matter of Torah and mitzvot is only a tzimtzum', the Essence untouched by them; both of his statements stand.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K08.json`): - Sefer HaMaamarim (Rayatz), תרפ"ט / גדול יהי' / 240:1 (line 1616): דמצוה הוא ל' צוותא וחיבור, והיינו שע"י המצוה האדם מתחבר עם עצמות אוא"ס ב"ה The Rayatz: the mitzvah joins the person with the Essence; the parable follows of a king's command that gives a commoner his very existence, and aveirah as its opposite. - Likkutei Torah, Vaetchanan 7:7 (line 2949): מצוך לשון צוותא וחיבור במצות ה׳ שהם גילוי עצמותו ומהותו כביכול The Alter Rebbe: 'Anochi metzavcha' is tzavta; in the mitzvot, not ziv and radiance but 'I Myself', the revelation of His Essence, is drawn down in deed. - Toras Shmuel, תרכ"ז / מאמרים / הללוי' הללו כו' מן השמים / קעו:2 (line 537): כי בחי' בריאת העולמות הוא רק מהארה בעלמא אבל ע"י המצות נעשה צוותא וחיבור עם עצמיות אוא"ס ב"ה The Maharash: creation is only a mere radiance, but the mitzvot make a bond with the Essence of Or Ein Sof. The same 'mere radiance' formula the spine records at F05j (line 713, another maamar), here set against the mitzvah.

## S090 · Positive and negative commandments · מצות עשה ומצות לא תעשה

*Face F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; also touches F10, F09, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Every 'do' draws Him into you; every 'don't' keeps you from becoming a thing apart from Him. Both are His first two words, and in their root they are one.

**Hold.** 'The word Anochi includes all 248 positive commandments, and You shall have no other includes all 365 negative ones.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 20)

**The move.** Open it: Tanya goes straight from this to His unity. The positive commandments draw the light of Ein Sof down into you, and the negative ones guard you from what is 'utterly separated from His unity', which is why at its core every prohibition is idolatry. Likkutei Torah turns the ranking round, rooting the 'don'ts' so high that they have no vessel below and are kept only by not doing; and Kuntres Acharon says that in the future the 365 will be sweetened in the 248 and included together. Bring it down to your own day, here, now: the blessing you said and the word you held back are the same two words, 'Anochi' and 'no other', and in their root they are one.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'Anochi, and no other', and in the pauses think of one 'do' and one 'don't' from today. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Refraining as a way of holding on. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'I am Havayah your God; you shall have no other gods' with your lips once today; keep one 'do' and one 'don't' on purpose; and return during the day to 'Anochi, and no other'.

**Where the sources differ.** Which rank is higher is kept: Tanya 37 has the negatives removing impurity and the positives drawing the light, while Likkutei Torah, Pekudei roots the negatives higher. The Izhbitzer says reason would put 'turn from evil' first, but God gave 'Anochi' first, in mercy.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K08.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 20:1 (line 277): כי דבור ״אנכי״ – כולל כל רמ״ח מצות עשה, ו״לא יהיה לך״ – כולל כל שס״ה מצות לא תעשה Tanya 20: the first two commandments, 'Anochi' and 'no other gods', contain all 248 positive and 365 negative commandments; the chapter goes straight on to His unity. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 24:1 (line 316): הם נפרדים מיחודו ואחדותו יתברך בתכלית הפירוד ממש, כמו הסטרא אחרא והקליפה הנקראת ״עבודה זרה״ Tanya 24: every prohibition, even rabbinic, is 'utterly separated from His unity', like idolatry itself. - Likkutei Torah, Pekudei 1:7 (line 52): לפי ששרש הל״ת ממקום גבוה מאד מבחי׳ י״ה ע״כ אין להם כלים למטה רק ע״י שב ואל תעשה Likkutei Torah, Pekudei: the negative commandments are rooted higher (Y-H) than the positive (V-H); having no vessel below, they are kept by refraining. A counter-emphasis to Tanya 37 by the same author.

## S091 · Charity · צדקה

*Face F07 The home below; also touches F01, F04, F08. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4, 5, 6). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** When you give, you do what He does every moment: give life to worlds that have nothing of their own. And what you give lifts all your working hours with it, to be a place He lives in.

**Hold.** 'The mitzvah of charity is literally God's own mitzvah: what the Holy One Himself does always, to give life to the worlds.' (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 17)

**The move.** Open it: before Him every world is a pauper with nothing of its own. The money you give holds the life you put into earning it, the whole animal soul, so when you give it all of that rises to Him, which is why charity is simply called 'the mitzvah'; and the fifth you give lifts the other four fifths with it, 'to be a place for His dwelling', your whole working day included. The Yismach Moshe adds the other side: He gives to all creatures, and the giver is only His messenger. Bring it down to one coin in your hand, here, now, and the hours of work it stands for: giving it, you do what He does every moment.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'what the Holy One Himself does always', and in the pauses think of the coin and the hours in it. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The joy of the plain man whose king lives with him. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'to keep the way of Havayah, to do charity' with your lips; give one coin before you pray or before you start work; and return during the day to 'what He Himself does always'.

**Where the sources differ.** The Yismach Moshe's ground is faith and the humility of the messenger, not the animal soul's rising or the surrounding light; the two meet at bitul.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K08.json`): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 17:4 (line 1209): היא מצות הצדקה, שהיא מצות ה׳ ממש, מה שהקדושברוךהוא בכבודו ובעצמו עושה תמיד להחיות העולמות IH 17: charity is 'His own mitzvah', what He Himself does always to give life to the worlds; it reveals the surrounding light (sovev) down to this world. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 37:19 (line 513): וכשנותנן לצדקה – הרי כל נפשו החיונית עולה לה׳ Tanya 37: charity is called simply 'the mitzvah' because the whole animal soul, invested in the earning, rises to God and is included in the Or Ein Sof clothed in the mitzvot. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 34:7 (line 468): הרי החומש מעלה עמו כל הארבע ידות לה׳, להיות מכון לשבתו יתברך Tanya 34: the fifth given lifts all four remaining parts to become 'a place for His dwelling'; the whole business day becomes a dwelling.

## S092 · Tefillin · תפילין

*Face F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; also touches F10, F06, F05. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Tefillin bind your own mind and heart into His. And the sages say He wears tefillin too, and in His is written 'who is like Your people Israel'.

**Hold.** 'In tefillin in particular: to nullify and include the wisdom and understanding of the divine soul in the wisdom and understanding of Ein Sof.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 41)

**The move.** Open it: your wisdom and understanding are given over in two of the four sections, and your knowing, with its love and awe, in the other two, the Shema and its partner, 'to subjugate the heart and the mind', as the Shulchan Aruch says. The Rebbe brings the Maggid on the sages' saying that God wears tefillin in which is written 'Who is like Your people Israel': tefillin are the delight of both sides in their bond, and since they can subjugate mind and heart, they must be above mind and heart. The saying is only a picture for the ear, since He has no head and no arm; what it keeps is that the bond is His delight too. Bring it down to tomorrow morning, here, the moment the tefillin are tightened, or, if you do not wear them, the first line of the Shema: your mind and heart, now, given over into His.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'to subjugate the heart and the mind', and in the pauses think: into His wisdom. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being bound as a delight, not a burden; the delight goes both ways. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the Shema with your lips in tefillin tomorrow, or with the same thought if you do not wear them; do the mitzvah slowly, hand and then head; and return during the day to 'mind and heart, His'.

**Where the sources differ.** R. Tzadok's frame is psychological, tefillin as devekut and a remedy for stray thoughts; Chabad's is the nullifying of the mind into Ein Sof. The Lurianic anatomy of tefillin in Derekh Mitzvotekha states no tie until its last section, through bitul.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K08.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 41:6 (line 581): ודרך פרט בתפילין, ליבטל וליכלל בחינת חכמתו ובינתו שבנפשו האלהית, בבחינת חכמתו ובינתו של איןסוף ברוךהוא Tanya 41: the intention of tefillin is that one's own Chochmah, Binah and Da'at be nullified and included in those of Ein Sof clothed in the parchments. - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד. מוצש"ק פ' בשלח, י"א שבט (מאמר ב), ה'תשכ"ח:22 (line 1319): ויובן זה ע"פ תורת הרב המגיד על מארז"ל תפילין דמארי עלמא … הרי מזה גופא מוכח שהיא למעלה ממוח ולב The Rebbe (Yud Shevat 5728) on the Maggid: tefillin are the mutual delight of God and Israel in their cleaving; that tefillin subjugate mind and heart shows they are above both.

## S093 · Tzitzit · ציצית

*Face F04 Filling and surrounding; also touches F02, F12, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Wrapping in a tallit is being wrapped in what surrounds all things: His kingship, drawn around you from head to foot. And the threads remind the heart of the unity it keeps forgetting.

**Hold.** 'In wrapping in tzitzit, have in mind to draw upon oneself His kingship, the kingship of all worlds.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 41)

**The move.** Open it: the Tzemach Tzedek, after the Ari, says the tallit is the great surrounding light, around a person from head to foot. R. Tzadok says the evil inclination works by making 'the good thoughts of the Creator's unity' be forgotten from the heart, and tzitzit, which you see, are what remind. And the Tzemach Tzedek reads the white and blue threads as two kinds of bitul, and says: 'this bitul is the true life of creatures, not their end'. Bring it down to the moment you wrap yourself, here, now, or take the fringes in your hand: you are drawing His kingship around you, and remembering His unity.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the kingship of all worlds', and in the pauses think: around me, and I remember. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being carried, held in a way that has no edge. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'you shall see it and remember' with your lips when you look at your tzitzit today; wear them, or put on the tallit, with this line in mind; and return during the day to 'the kingship of all worlds'.

**Where the sources differ.** The colours are read two ways: for the Tzemach Tzedek white is the higher bitul and tekhelet the lower; for the Sefat Emet white and tekhelet are the positive and negative commandments, with tekhelet, holiness, above white, purity. Breslov reads tzitzit as a guard of the eyes against the serpent's counsel, with no tie to the unity.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K08.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 41:7 (line 582): ובעטיפת ציצית, יכוין כמו שכתוב בזהר: להמשיך עליו מלכותו יתברך, אשר היא ״מלכות כל עולמים״ Tanya 41: in wrapping in tzitzit one draws upon oneself His kingship, 'the kingship of all worlds'. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of Tzitzit 1 (line 36): שהטלית הוא אור מקיף הגדול לז"א שמקיפו מראשו עד רגלו The Tzemach Tzedek (after the Ari): the tallit is the great surrounding light, head to foot. - Tzidkat HaTzadik 220:1 (line 224): שהמחשבת הטובות של יחוד הבורא יתברך נשכחות מן הלב R. Tzadok: the evil inclination works by making one forget 'the good thoughts of the Creator's unity'; tzitzit is a surrounding light that reminds, tefillin the same light bound.

## S094 · Circumcision and covenant · ברית מילה

*Face F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; also touches F05, F06, F07. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5, 6). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** A covering that hid Him completely was put there so that taking it away would reveal more than if it had never been. And the covenant it seals belongs to the very essence of who you are.

**Hold.** 'We were commanded to remove from ourselves this concealment that hides entirely, for it does not belong to us at all.' (the Tzemach Tzedek, Derekh Mitzvotekha, Milah 8)

**The move.** Open it: the Name Elokim is the vessel that clothes the light, and in holiness Havayah and Elokim are one, but in the husk that vessel becomes a total hiding, and the foreskin is that husk. It was made so that removing it would reveal a new light from Ein Sof, far higher than there would have been without it. The Rebbe says the covenant belongs to the very essence of Israel's holiness, beyond being one of the 613, and the Sefat Emet says it is sealed in the soul but must be revealed by a person's deed. Bring it down to a bris you have been at, remembered here, now, and the blessing 'to bring him into the covenant of Avraham': what was cut away did not belong to us at all, and what was revealed was always ours.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'it does not belong to us at all', and in the pauses think: removed, and more revealed. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The relief of a covering taken off that was never really yours. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'to bring him into the covenant of Avraham our father' with your lips once today; do one mitzvah openly, in deed, as Avraham came to milah after thought and speech: a blessing said aloud, a kindness done in the open; and return during the day to 'not ours at all'.

**Where the sources differ.** The Tzemach Tzedek calls the light revealed by milah 'a new light, far higher'; the Rebbe says the revelations drawn by the mitzvot, milah included, are not truly a new light, as teshuvah's is. They use 'new' in different senses, and the difference is kept.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K08.json`): - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of circumcision 6 (line 12): שם אלקים שהוא הכלי המלבשת להאור רק שבקדושה כולא חד והוי' הוא האלקים ובקליפה נעשה העלם והסתר גמור The Tzemach Tzedek: in holiness Havayah is Elokim, one; in the husk (the orlah) the vessel becomes total concealment. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of circumcision 8 (line 14): שנצטוינו להסיר מעלינו ההעלם הזה שמעלים לגמרי כי איננו שייך אלינו כלל The removal of the foreskin is the removal of the concealment that hides entirely. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of circumcision 13 (line 19): כי ע"י העברת ערלה מתגלה עוד גילוי הארה חדשה מא"ס ב"ה מאד נעלה מההארה שהי' בלעדה Answer to 'why create the foreskin?': removing it reveals a new light from Ein Sof, higher than there would have been without it.

## S095 · Sacrifices · קרבנות

*Face F11 Darkness turned to light; also touches F08, F07, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 5, 6, 7). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** What is offered is the animal in you: its heat rises and is taken into the fire that comes down from above. Nothing in you is too coarse to be brought near.

**Hold.** 'Your offering from the animal: that is, to refine the animal soul, by subduing it and then transforming it.' (Likkutei Torah, Vayikra)

**The move.** Open it: 'from you' is the divine soul, and 'from the animal' is the animal soul, refined by contemplating His greatness until it too loves. The fire below in the offering rises and is included in the fire above that comes down onto the altar, and now prayer is its place; and Tanya says permitted meat and wine eaten for God's sake rise to Him like a burnt-offering. The Rebbe adds that the rising is not the goal: the aim is drawing Godliness down, a dwelling below. Bring it down to your own table at the next meal, here, now: your appetite is the animal being offered, and the table is the altar.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'your offering from the animal', and in the pauses think of the next meal. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The coarse part of you brought near. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over your food with your lips, slowly; eat that meal for His sake, to have strength to serve; and return during the day to 'an offering from the animal'.

**Where the sources differ.** Likkutei Torah puts the weight on the ascent, the fire below included in the fire above; the Rebbe calls sacrifice the 'outer will' and kindness, the drawing down, the inner. Both stand.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K08.json`): - Likkutei Torah, Vayikra 2:16 (line 205): אבל קרבנכם הוא מן הבהמה כו׳ היינו לברר הנפש הבהמית להיות בבחי׳ אתכפיא ואתהפכא Likkutei Torah, Vayikra: 'from you' is the divine soul; 'your offering from the animal' is refining the animal soul by subduing and transforming it through contemplating God's greatness. - Likkutei Torah, Pinchas 1:15 (line 2490): והנה יסוד האש שלמטה שבקרבן עולה ונכלל באש שלמעלה שיורד מלמעלה למטה על המזבח Likkutei Torah, Pinchas: the fire below in the offering rises and is included in the fire above that descends on the altar; prayer is its place now. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 7:4 (line 135): נתברר חיות הבשר והיין שהיה נשפע מקליפת נוגה, ועולה לה׳ כעולה וכקרבן Tanya 7: permitted meat and wine eaten for God's sake rise 'like a burnt offering and a sacrifice'.

## S096 · The Sanctuary and the Temple · משכן ובית המקדש

*Face F07 The home below; also touches F02, F05, F06. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 4, 5, 6). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** In the Holy of Holies the Ark had a measure and yet took up no space: a limit and the limitless in one place. Now that place is a person, and four cubits of halachah.

**Hold.** 'Since the Temple was destroyed, the Holy One has in His world no sanctuary and dwelling but the four cubits of halachah.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 34)

**The move.** Open it: the Mishkan and its Holy of Holies were for the resting of His Shechinah, 'which is the revelation of His unity'. The Ark had measurements and yet took no space, its bounds Elokim and its taking no space Havayah, so its place was the union of the two Names; the Rashab asks how a world of hiding can hold that, and answers that the contraction need not go away, only stop hiding. And one who learns, Tanya says, makes himself a sanctuary and a place for His dwelling. Bring it down to your own chair with a book of halachah open, here, now: the four cubits around you, while you learn, are His sanctuary.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the four cubits of halachah', and in the pauses think of the chair where you learn. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The joy of the plain man whose king lives with him. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'make Me a sanctuary, and I will dwell among them' with your lips once today; learn one halachah where you sit; and return during the day to 'four cubits'.

## S097 · Forbidden foods · מאכלות אסורות

*Face F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; also touches F11, F01, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** There is a gleam of His life in everything, but not everything can be raised now. 'Mutar', permitted, means untied: what you may eat can rise with you, and what is forbidden is still bound.

**Hold.** 'That is the meaning of heter and mutar: not tied and bound in the hands of the outside forces, so that it cannot return and rise to God.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 7)

**The move.** Open it: permitted food eaten for God's sake rises to Him, and even eaten for lust it can rise again with the person when he returns. Forbidden food comes from the three wholly impure husks and is 'bound' in their hands, and cannot rise until death is swallowed up, or by teshuvah from great love. The Sefat Emet says there is a gleam of life in everything, but in measure, and in forbidden food the holy spark is imprisoned and cannot be revealed. Bring it down to what is on your plate at the next meal, here, now: 'permitted' means untied, and what you may eat can rise with you.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'not tied and bound', and in the pauses think of the food on the plate. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The freedom of a table set before God. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing before you eat with your lips, slowly; check that one thing you eat today is kosher before you eat it; and return during the day to 'untied'.

**Kept modest.** The tie is stated for the category, permitted as untied and forbidden as bound, not for single laws like meat and milk or the sciatic nerve, so this meditation holds only the category.

**Where the sources differ.** The Sefat Emet frames it as freedom, 'no strange god in you'; the Chabad sources frame it as how far refinement can reach now.

## S098 · Supra-rational decrees · חוקים / פרה אדומה

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F05, F08, F04. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Behind every mitzvah is a will that no reason reaches. The red heifer is the one place where that will stands uncovered, and you meet it by accepting it.

**Hold.** 'The foundation of every mitzvah is that it is a statute: the mitzvot are the supernal will, above the intellect.' (the Rebbe, Chukat 5729)

**The move.** Open it: the Tzemach Tzedek says that in truth all the mitzvot are called statutes, because the reasons revealed to us are not their true reasons. In the others the reason covers the will, and in the red heifer the will stands revealed. The answer to a will like that is accepting the yoke, which is bitul, Moshe's 'and what are we'. Bring it down to a mitzvah you keep without knowing why, here, now, or one whose reason you were told and still do not quite feel: that is where His will is least covered, and you are nearest to it there.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the supernal will, above the intellect', and in the pauses think of that one mitzvah. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Lightness: nothing to defend, not even an explanation. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'this is the statute of the Torah' with your lips once today; keep one mitzvah whose reason you do not know, on purpose; and return during the day to 'His will, above reason'.

**Where the sources differ.** The Tzemach Tzedek tells that David called the statutes 'songs', praising their will-side, and was punished; will, he says, is outer relative to delight. The Rebbe's praise of the uncovered will seems to pull the other way; the two may mean 'will' differently, and the texts do not settle it.

## S099 · Sanctifying the Name · קידוש השם

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One; also touches F08, F12, F04. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** There is a point in every Jew that will not be separated from the One, not even for a moment. It does not argue and does not reason; it simply cannot deny Him.

**Hold.** 'Because of the light of God clothed in their soul, which is not in the category of time at all', a Jew will not deny Him even for a moment. (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 25)

**The move.** Open it: the light of Ein Sof is clothed in the soul's wisdom, above knowing, which is why all Israel believe, and why even the lightest and the sinners among them have given their lives rather than deny the One God, without knowledge or reason, as though it were impossible to deny. The Rebbe adds that this readiness is bitul, and since a person is a 'something', he needs to call it to mind, as Tanya advises at the Shema. Bring it down to tonight, here, at the first line of the Shema: the 'One' you say comes from that point in you, now, which does not bargain and does not wait for reasons.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'not in the category of time at all', and in the pauses think of the word 'One'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A core that does not bargain. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the bedtime Shema with your lips tonight, drawing out 'One', with this line in mind; keep one mitzvah tomorrow simply because He asked, without looking for a reason; and return during the day to 'One, not even for a moment'.

**Where the sources differ.** Why even sinners are ready is put differently: for Tanya it is the light of Ein Sof in the soul's wisdom; for Breslov, that 'Israel rose in thought' and He delights in anyone still called Israel; the Piaseczner, in the Warsaw Ghetto, speaks of sufferings borne only with Him. The Noam Elimelech makes it a daily practice of the imagination; that practice is his, and it is not the one given here.

## S100 · Priests and Levites · כהנים ולוים

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One; also touches F01, F08, F07. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 4). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** The priest's service is to cleave to Him from the essence of the soul, and to see that nothing happens by chance. And the Rambam opens it: not only Levi, but anyone whose spirit moves him.

**Hold.** 'The task of the High Priest is to be in constant devekut with the Holy One, beyond measure and limit, a devekut that comes from the essence of his soul, the yechidah.' (the Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos 33)

**The move.** Open it: Berditchev says the Levites stand for seeing how the flow comes at every moment from the Cause of causes. The Izhbitzer says a kohen is whoever serves God and sees that nothing in the world happens by chance, that all is providence from Him. And the Rebbe brings the Rambam: not only the tribe of Levi, but every person whose spirit moves him may stand before God to serve Him. Bring it down to one small thing that seemed to happen by chance this week, here, now, a delay, a meeting, a word someone said: it came from the Cause of causes, and whoever sees it so is standing, in that moment, where the priest stands.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'constant devekut, from the essence of the soul', and in the pauses think of the one small thing. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Standing at a post: a closeness that is a service, not a mood. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'I have set God before me always' with your lips once today; keep one mitzvah with the care of someone serving in the Temple, slowly and whole; and return during the day to 'constant devekut'.

**Kept modest.** The institution itself (lineage, blemishes, portions) carries no stated tie; the tie is stated only for what the priest and Levite stand for, devekut, love in bitul and seeing providence, so this meditation holds only those.

**Where the sources differ.** Whether the priest's holiness is his own is disputed: the plain sense and the Rebbe's High Priest speak of his own, from the yechidah; R. Tzadok says two of the priests' five holinesses derive from Israel's.
