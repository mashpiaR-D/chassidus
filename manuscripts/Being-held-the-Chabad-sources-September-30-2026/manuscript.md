*Part of the "being held" study. This file gathers what the seven Chabad Rebbes and their chassidim say about one fact from many sides: everything, you included, is being held in being by G-d right now. You are not the one holding it up. Each angle below says what the teaching is, what it adds, the source's own picture, how the source says to take it in, what it is like when it lands, and how it sounds in a chat.*

"Shaar HaYichud" below, with no other name, means the Alter Rebbe's Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, the second part of the Tanya (TANYA lines 766-883). The Mitteler Rebbe's book of the same name is called "the Gate of Unity" (GOU).

## The word is still being said

- **What it says:** The verse says "Forever, G-d, Your word stands in the heavens." The Baal Shem Tov read it plainly. The very words G-d said, "Let there be a sky," are standing inside the sky now, keeping it alive. If those letters left for one instant and went back to their source, the sky would be nothing, as it was before He spoke. The same holds for every created thing, down to a stone and a clod of earth. The reason: the world was made from nothing, so nothing in it has any hold on being except the word that says it. The Alter Rebbe adds that a thing's Hebrew name is the narrowed piece of that word that gives it its life. In Likkutei Torah he brings the Midrash: "On what do the heavens stand? On that word I said." And he adds a second point: that the world goes on being made depends on what people below do. Their mitzvot are like the wick that holds the flame so it does not fly off.
- **What it adds:** This is the root of every other angle. Being held is not a feeling or a belief about G-d's care. It is a claim about what a thing is: a thing is a sentence still being spoken. It also makes "held" something that happens now, not once in the past. And the Likkutei Torah line adds that the person being held is also, in a small way, holding: your deeds are the wick.
- **The picture:** A spoken word. It lasts only as long as the speaker keeps saying it. Where it breaks: a human word leaves the speaker and goes out into the air. His word does not leave Him at all (see "Nothing has changed for Him" below).
- **How the source says to realize it:** The chapter asks you to take the verse "Know today and bring it back to your heart" seriously: the problem is not belief, it is that the world feels like it simply stands. The move is to apply the Besht's reading thing by thing: the sky, the earth, a stone, and their names. (The Tanya does not give a set time here; its practice sections come in Iggeret HaKodesh 11 and chapters 41-42, below.)
- **From inside:** You look at a table and it is no longer only there. It is being done. The weight of things is still weight, but now it rests on something.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "Everything is just sitting there and I have to keep it all going." After: "It is all being said right now, me too. I am inside a sentence someone else is saying."
- **Sources:** - Shaar HaYichud ch. 1, TANYA line 785: כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש ("if the letters left for an instant and went back to their source, the whole sky would be nothing at all"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 1, TANYA line 786: ואפילו ארץ הלזו הגשמית ובחינת דומם ממש ("even this physical earth, even the lifeless"). - Likkutei Torah, Acharei Mot, LT line 674: כי דבר ה׳ שבו שמים נעשו נצב שם לעולם כי לעולם הוא מהוה מאין ליש ("the word by which the heavens were made stands there forever, for forever He brings them from nothing into being"). - Same line: אך להיות חיים וקיימים לעולם מאין ליש הוא ע״י מעשה התחתונים דוקא ("but that they live and last is through the deeds of those below"). - Same line: והם הם כדמיון הפתילה והעצים שבהם נאחז האור שלא יסתלק למעלה ("they are like the wick and the wood that the flame holds onto, so it does not leave upward").

## Not the silversmith's cup

- **What it says:** Some people deny that G-d watches each thing (hashgacha pratis, individual providence). The Alter Rebbe names the root of their mistake. They picture G-d's work like a craftsman's. When a silversmith finishes a cup, the cup no longer needs his hands. He can walk off to the market and the cup keeps its shape. But a craftsman makes something from something; he only changes its form. G-d made heaven and earth from nothing. What was made from nothing needs the Maker's power in it at every moment. The Alter Rebbe's Iggeret HaKodesh 25 repeats this almost word for word, and draws from it a way to meet people who hurt you (see "Anger is faith gone missing").
- **What it adds:** It names the hidden picture most people carry: G-d as a maker who finished and left, so now the world, and I, must keep it going. It also ties being held directly to providence: if He is holding each thing up, He is attending to each thing.
- **The picture:** The silversmith and the cup, used as the wrong picture. Where it breaks is the point: the cup stands without the smith because the silver was already there. Nothing was there before G-d spoke.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Catch the picture. When you feel that things run on their own, ask: am I imagining G-d as the silversmith who walked away?
- **From inside:** A quiet shift from "He made it and left it to me" to "He has not let go of it for a second."
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "G-d set things up. Now it's on me to hold it together." After: "He never put it down. I am not the one keeping this from falling apart."
- **Sources:** - Shaar HaYichud ch. 2, TANYA line 792: כי כאשר יצא לצורף כלי – שוב אין הכלי צריך לידי הצורף ("once the smith has made the vessel, the vessel no longer needs the smith's hands"). - Iggeret HaKodesh 25, TANYA line 1381, the same passage retold: כאשר יצא לצורף כלי – שוב אין הכלי צריך לידי הצורף. - Shaar HaYichud ch. 2, TANYA line 795: אלא צריך להיות כח הפועל בנפעל תמיד, להחיותו ולקיימו ("the power of the Maker must be in the made thing always, to give it life and keep it in being").

## The wall of water

- **What it says:** At the Red Sea, "G-d drove the sea with a strong east wind all night," and the water stood like a wall. Had He stopped the wind for one instant, the water would have run downhill as water does. A stone wall stands by itself; water does not. Creation from nothing is more wonderful than the splitting of the sea. So how much more, if the Creator's power left a thing, it would go back to nothing.
- **What it adds:** It gives a picture of what "held" feels like from the side of the thing held. You are not a stone wall standing by your own nature. You are water standing up, and you stand only because the wind is still blowing. Everything's "nature" is itself held, too: even the water's wish to run down was created.
- **The picture:** The wall of water held by the wind. Where it breaks: the sea stood against its own nature for one night. A creature has no nature to stand on at all apart from His word. The picture undersells it, and the Tanya says so ("a great wonder, more than the splitting of the sea").
- **How the source says to realize it:** No separate practice here; it serves chapter 2's argument. Our reading: hold the image of the wind still blowing, and feel yourself as the standing water.
- **From inside:** Relief with a little awe in it. The strain of "holding myself up" was never mine to carry. The wind is doing it.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "If I stop pushing for one minute, everything collapses." After: "I'm the water, not the wind. The wind hasn't stopped."
- **Sources:** - Shaar HaYichud ch. 2, TANYA line 794: ואילו הפסיק ה׳ את הרוח כרגע – היו המים חוזרים וניגרים במורד כדרכם וטבעם ("had G-d stopped the wind for an instant, the water would have run back down, its way and its nature"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 2, TANYA line 794: והוא פלא גדול יותר מקריעת ים סוף ("it is a greater wonder than the splitting of the Red Sea"). - Iggeret HaKodesh 25, TANYA line 1382, the same picture repeated: ואילו פסק הרוח כרגע ("had the wind stopped for an instant").

## Sunlight inside the sun

- **What it says:** Sunlight out in the sky and on the earth looks like a real thing. The same light inside the body of the sun counts as nothing, because there only the sun is seen. So too every creature before the divine flow that makes it. We see creatures as solid and separate only because our eyes do not see their source. "If the eye were allowed to see" the life from G-d's mouth inside each thing, its matter would not be seen at all. And here the picture fails in our favor: sunlight in the sky is far from the sun, but creatures are always in their source; only the source is not seen.
- **What it adds:** It explains why being held does not feel like being held. Separateness is a matter of what can be seen from where we stand. You are not far from what holds you; you are inside it, and it is hidden.
- **The picture:** Sunlight in the sky and sunlight within the sun. Where it breaks (the Tanya's own note): in the sky the sun is really absent, while creatures are always in their source.
- **How the source says to realize it:** "Everyone who thinks carefully will understand" (line 799): the practice is understanding, taking the picture slowly until it is clear. Chapter 33 (below) turns it into a practice for joy.
- **From inside:** The sense of being a small separate thing out in the cold softens. You are a ray, and the ray was never cut off.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I feel so alone in this, like it's just me out here." After: "Out here is how it looks. I'm still in the sun; I just can't see it from this angle."
- **Sources:** - Shaar HaYichud ch. 3, TANYA line 800: לא היה גשמיות הנברא וחומרו וממשו נראה כלל לעינינו ("the creature's physical matter would not be seen by our eyes at all"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 3, TANYA line 801: רק ששם, במקומו ממש, נחשב הזיו הזה לאין ואפס ממש ("only there, in its very place, this ray counts as nothing"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 3, TANYA line 803: מהשאיןכן כל הברואים, הם במקורם תמיד, רק שאין המקור נראה לעיני בשר ("but all creatures are always in their source; only the source is not seen by eyes of flesh").

## The hiding is a kindness

- **What it says:** The name Havayah (the four-letter Name) means "He brings all into being from nothing," every moment; it is not enough for things that they were made in the six days. The flow that makes the world comes from His "greatness," chesed, kindness: "to be brought from nothing and to stand, by free kindness." But to let creatures exist as creatures, He also holds back and hides that flow (gevurah, tzimtzum), so that "it is as if the creature were a thing on its own." The name Elokim, which hides, has the number of "hateva," nature: it makes the world look as if it runs by itself. It is a sheath for the sun, so creatures can bear it. And this hiding is itself kindness, "the kindness by which the world is built." The hiding hides only from us. Before Him, nothing is hidden.
- **What it adds:** It answers the obvious objection: if I am held, why do I feel unheld? Because the holding includes a hiding, and the hiding is for my sake. Without it, I would be swallowed up. The feeling of standing on your own is itself a gift from the one holding you.
- **The picture:** The sun and its sheath: "for the L-rd G-d is a sun and a shield." Where it breaks: a sheath is a separate thing from the sun. Here the hider and the hidden are one: "G-d (Havayah) is Elokim."
- **How the source says to realize it:** "Know today and bring it back to your heart that Havayah is Elokim": take to heart that the name that hides and the name that gives life are one. When the world looks like plain nature, say to yourself: this is the sheath, and the sheath is Him.
- **From inside:** The distance you feel stops being an accusation. It becomes a kind of shade over you.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "If He were really holding me, I'd feel it. I feel nothing." After: "Not feeling it is part of how He holds me. The hiding is for me, not against me."
- **Sources:** - Shaar HaYichud ch. 4, TANYA line 805: ומהוה אותם מאין ליש בכל רגע ("and brings them from nothing into being every moment"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 4, TANYA line 806: להיות ברואים מאין ליש וקיימים בחסד חנם ("to be created from nothing and to endure by free kindness"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 4, TANYA line 808: וכאילו גוף הנברא הוא דבר בפני עצמו ("as if the creature were a thing on its own"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 4, TANYA line 816: והרי – בחינת גבורה זו וצמצום הזה, הוא גם כן בחינת חסד שהעולם יבנה בו ("this holding back and contraction is also the kindness by which the world is built"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 6, TANYA line 822: אך אין הצמצום וההסתר אלא לתחתונים ("but the contraction and hiding are only for those below").

## Down here as much as up there (the lower unity)

- **What it says:** Why did He hide Himself so the world would seem separate? Because "there is no king without a people," and a people means others, those who are distant and different. His kingship (Malchut) is the power that makes and keeps the world as a real, separate-seeming world, and makes space (up, down, the four sides) and time (past, present, future). Since that kingship is one with His Essence, space and time themselves are held in Him. So He is above place and time, and also "found below, in place and time." The Infinite fills the whole earth "equally, for it is so on the earth below just as in the heavens above." This is yichuda tataah, the lower unity: G-d as He is inside the world, in each place and moment, not only beyond it.
- **What it adds:** Being held does not stop at "the world is kept in being." The very room you are in and the very hour you are living are being made now, and He is in them as fully as in heaven. There is no place or time that is thinner, lower or further from Him.
- **The picture:** A king needs a people who are "other." Where it breaks: a human king rules people who exist without him. His "people" and their space and time are made by His kingship, now.
- **How the source says to realize it:** The Zohar calls "Shema Yisrael" the higher unity and "Baruch Shem" (blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever) the lower unity. The Tanya's explanation is meant to be carried into those two lines, said twice daily. Our reading: at "Baruch Shem," think of the place you are in and the hour it is.
- **From inside:** The present moment stops being a thin slice between past and future. It feels full. "Here" is not second best.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "G-d is somewhere up there. This kitchen, this Tuesday, isn't holy." After: "This hour is being made right now, and He is as much in it as anywhere."
- **Sources:** - Shaar HaYichud ch. 7, TANYA line 824: כי תכלית בריאת העולם הוא בשביל התגלות מלכותו יתברך ("the purpose of creating the world is to reveal His kingship"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 7, TANYA line 830: כי כך הוא בארץ מתחת כמו בשמים ממעל ממש ("for it is so on the earth below just as in the heavens above"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 7, TANYA line 823 (the Zohar on "Shema" and "Baruch Shem"): ״יחודא עילאה״ and ״יחודא תתאה״ ("the higher unity," "the lower unity").

## Nothing has changed for Him

- **What it says:** "I, G-d, have not changed." He is alone after the world was made, as He was alone before. The worlds make no change in His oneness, because before Him they count as nothing. His speech is not like ours: a person's word leaves him, but His speech and thought stay one with Him even after they went out to make the worlds. All the contractions are "hiding of the face," so that what gives us life seems to be a thing apart from Him. But before Him, "darkness is like light." And He knows all creatures by knowing Himself.
- **What it adds:** It turns being held around. You are not a separate thing being carried by another separate thing. From His side there is only Him. The holding is not an effort He makes, and it will not tire.
- **The picture:** A person's single word compared to the whole soul that could speak endless words. And better: a word before it is spoken, while it is still one with the mind and the heart's wish. Where it breaks: our spoken words do leave us. His never do.
- **How the source says to realize it:** The Tanya points to the daily prayer "You were before the world was created, You are since it was created" (line 300: אתה הוא עד שלא נברא העולם, אתה הוא משנברא), said "without any change." Our reading: say that line slowly as a fact about now.
- **From inside:** Stillness. Nothing you do or fail to do shakes what holds you.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "What if I've worn Him out? What if I'm too much?" After: "He isn't straining to hold me. Nothing about me changes Him."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 20, TANYA line 301: שכמו שהיה הוא לבדו הוא יחיד ומיוחד קודם הבראם, כן הוא לבדו הוא יחיד ומיוחד אחר שבראם ("as He was alone and one before He created them, so He is alone and one after"). - Likkutei Amarim ch. 20, TANYA line 302: אינו אלא דבר ה׳ ורוח פיו יתברך המלובש בהם ("[their being] is only the word of G-d and the breath of His mouth dressed in them"). - Likkutei Amarim ch. 21, TANYA line 313: וכל הצמצומים הם בחינת ״הסתר פנים״ ("all the contractions are a hiding of the face"). - Likkutei Amarim ch. 21, TANYA line 314: אך לגבי הקדושברוךהוא, אין שום צמצום והסתר והעלם מסתיר ומעלים לפניו ("but before the Holy One, no contraction, hiding or concealment hides anything"). - Shaar HaYichud ch. 7, TANYA line 832: כמו שהיה לבדו קודם בריאת העולם – כך הוא לבדו אחר שנברא ("as He was alone before the world was created, so He is alone after").

## Around you, and truly inside you (filling and surrounding)

- **What it says:** Chassidut speaks of G-d as memale kol almin, "filling all worlds," and sovev kol almin, "surrounding all worlds." The Tanya explains that "surrounding" is not about space. What is shown openly inside a thing, the small measured life that fits it, is called "filling." What is present but hidden, too much for the thing to take in, is called "surrounding." So this physical earth shows only the life of stone and plant, yet the whole infinite light is there: "it is called surrounding it, though it is truly inside it." And the hidden is higher than the shown.
- **What it adds:** It corrects a picture of being held as something outside you, like arms around a body. The part of Him you do not feel is not further away. It is the larger part, and it is right here.
- **The picture:** The earth, which shows only a trace of life and yet is filled with the whole light. Where it breaks: "surround" makes us think of a circle around something. The Tanya says outright that it does not mean place.
- **How the source says to realize it:** This chapter leads into the next: since He hid His endless light for your sake, you can "leave and let go of all you have" to cling to Him (ch. 49, next angle).
- **From inside:** What you do not feel stops counting as absence. The unfelt is the most present.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "Maybe He's around, but not in my life, not in me." After: "The part I can't feel isn't far off. It's the biggest part, and it's in here."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 48, TANYA line 703: וכל אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא נקרא סובב עליה, אף שהוא בתוכה ממש ("the whole light of the Infinite is called 'surrounding' it, though it is truly inside it"). - Likkutei Amarim ch. 48, TANYA line 701: ואין הפירוש סובב ומקיף מלמעלה בבחינת מקום חס ושלום ("'surrounding from above' does not mean in space, G-d forbid").

## Shelter in the shade (you are held even when it does not reach your heart)

- **What it says:** "It is better to take shelter (lachasot) in G-d than to trust (mibtoach) in man." The Alter Rebbe reads "man" as the "supernal man," the measured light that can come into a person's mind and heart. "Trust" (bitachon) here is from "plastering": a teaching that sticks inside you, that you can feel. "Taking shelter" is sitting in a shade that surrounds you from above. There is a kind of contemplation that the heart can hold. It moves you, but after prayer only a trace is left. There is a higher kind that the heart cannot contain, and it stays as a surrounding presence. That shelter is better, "even though it is only surrounding and cannot come into the open heart," because it does not leave. It is like a person so taken up with one thought that it wraps him all day, even while he does other things. And the shade guards against what comes from outside.
- **What it adds:** Here the Alter Rebbe himself says you do not have to feel held for the holding to work. The felt kind comes and goes. The unfelt kind, the shade, stays.
- **The picture:** A shade: "G-d is your shade at your right hand; the sun will not strike you by day." And the man wrapped all day in one thought. Where it breaks: shade is the absence of light. This shade is more light, too much to see.
- **How the source says to realize it:** The passage teaches both: do the contemplation that enters the heart, and also rest in what is beyond you and will not enter. "In one way the surrounding is better, in another the inner one."
- **From inside:** Being covered. Not warm or excited, just not exposed.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I prayed and felt something, and an hour later it was gone. So it wasn't real." After: "The feeling faded. The shade didn't. I don't have to feel it for it to be over me."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Torah, Shemini Atzeret, "Tov lachasot," LT line 4309: ולחסות הוא שחוסה בצל ומקיף שמלמעלה ("taking shelter means sheltering in a shade and a surrounding from above"). - Same line: ומבטוח הוא מלשון וטח את הבית שהוא לשון טיחה ודיבוק ("'trust' is from 'plastering the house,' sticking and cleaving"). - LT line 4310: וזהו טוב לחסות בהוי׳ הגם שיהיה בבחי׳ מקיף מבחי׳ הוי׳ ולא יוכל ליכנס בגילוי הלב ("it is better to shelter in G-d, though it is surrounding and cannot come into the open heart"). - LT line 4311: אחר התפלה לא נשאר רק הרשימו ("after prayer only the trace remains"). - LT line 4311: וכמשל מי שטרוד מאד במחשבה אחת שמקיפתו ומלבישתו במאד מאד עד שלא יסיח דעתו ממנה כלל אף בעשותו דברים אחרים ("like one taken up with a single thought that surrounds and wraps him, so he never takes his mind off it even while doing other things"). - LT line 4312: שהצל שהוא בחי׳ מקיף הוא שמירה מהחיצונים ("the shade, the surrounding, is a guard against the outside forces").

## He set His light aside for you

- **What it says:** All the contractions, world after world, have one aim: to make a physical person who can turn his whole self, body included, toward G-d. "As water reflects face to face": just as the Holy One, as it were, "set aside and removed His great, endless light," hid it in three kinds of contraction, "all for the love of the lowly person, to raise him up to G-d," so it is fitting for a person to set aside all he has, from soul to flesh, to cling to Him. The Mitteler Rebbe gives the contraction a teacher's face. A great teacher who wants to reach a student far below him hides the depth of his understanding and says only a short rule. The depth has not gone anywhere: "not that it withdrew and hid completely." It is all still in his mind, folded up for the student's sake.
- **What it adds:** The tzimtzum is usually taught as the reason G-d seems absent. Here it is the shape of His care. The smallness of your world is not neglect. It is the size someone who loves you made it, so you could live in it.
- **The picture:** Face in water (Proverbs 27:19): love shown draws love back. The teacher who speaks briefly for a young student. Where it breaks: a teacher's patience has limits and his depth can fade. His does not.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Chapter 49 ties this to the blessings before the Shema: think of how the angels and worlds are nothing before Him, and that He set all that aside for you; then the love wakes, and "the soul is poured out" in the Shema. The Tanya's order: first the thought of His greatness and what He set aside, then the letting go of all you have.
- **From inside:** The narrowness of your life reads as a doorway that was made low so you could come in.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "My life is so small and limited. Where is He in any of this?" After: "He made it this size on purpose, for me. The smallness is how He reaches me."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 49, TANYA line 714: הניח וסילק לצד אחד, דרך משל, את אורו הגדול הבלתי תכלית ("He set aside, as it were, His great endless light"). - Same line: והכל בשביל אהבת האדם התחתון – להעלותו לה׳ ("all for the love of the lowly person, to raise him to G-d"). - Same line: כי ראוי לאדם גם כן להניח ולעזוב כל אשר לו מנפש ועד בשר ("it is fitting for a person, too, to leave and let go of all he has, from soul to flesh"). - Mitteler Rebbe, the Gate of Unity ch. 12, GOU line 678: ולא שנסתלק ונתעלם לגמרי ("and not that it withdrew and hid completely"); the teacher's picture at GOU line 676: והרי זה כמשל אור שכל עמוק ורחב שבעצמות המשפיע ("like the deep, wide light of mind within the teacher himself").

## The King who came down to the dunghill

- **What it says:** Love wakes love, "as water reflects face to face." Picture a great king who shows great love to the lowest, most despised man lying on a dunghill. He comes down from his place of honor with all his ministers, lifts him up from the heap, brings him into his palace, into the innermost room where no servant or minister goes, and is with him there in true closeness, embracing and kissing. Even a heart of stone would melt. "Just like this, and far more, our G-d has done for us." He did not send an angel to take us out of Egypt; "the Holy One Himself went down there." Torah study is the kiss, the mitzvot done with the body are the embrace.
- **What it adds:** Being held is not only being kept in existence. It is being picked up. It answers the shame that says "not me, I'm too low." The king chose the one on the dunghill.
- **The picture:** The king and the man on the dunghill. Where it breaks: the Tanya itself says the real thing is greater "doubled and redoubled without end," since before Him all the worlds are nothing.
- **How the source says to realize it:** "Think about" this (the chapter's opening) and let it rouse love; then carry it into Torah and mitzvot as the kiss and the embrace. The chapter's move is from picturing to doing.
- **From inside:** Being wanted, not only tolerated. A melting.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "Maybe He holds up the world. He wouldn't bother with someone like me." After: "He comes down to exactly the one who thinks he's not worth it."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 46, TANYA line 681: ויורד אליו ממקום כבודו עם כל שריו יחדיו, ומקימו ומרימו מאשפתו ("and comes down to him from his place of honor with all his ministers, and lifts him up from his dunghill"). - Same line: ואף אם לבו כלב האבן – המס ימס והיה למים ("even if his heart is like stone, it will melt and become water"). - Likkutei Amarim ch. 46, TANYA line 683: אלא הקדושברוךהוא בכבודו ובעצמו ירד לשם ("but the Holy One Himself went down there").

## The rope tied above

- **What it says:** The soul was breathed into a person from G-d's own inner breath: "He blew into his nostrils the breath of life." The Tanya calls this soul "truly a part of G-d above" (chelek Eloka mimaal mamash). And "Jacob is the rope of His inheritance": like a rope, one end is tied above and the other hangs below. The rope is woven of 613 thin strands, the mitzvot; a sin cuts a thin strand. And when someone pulls the lower end, the upper end moves too. A rope that was cut and tied again is doubled at the knot, so one who comes back must add to his learning and giving there.
- **What it adds:** Being held from the inside. The world is held by His word; the person is held by a rope whose top end is in Him. You are not only kept in being; you are still attached at your root, even when you feel far.
- **The picture:** A rope, tied above, hanging down. Where it breaks: a rope can be cut through. The Tanya's point is that the upper end is never untied; only what is between can be blocked, and it can be repaired.
- **How the source says to realize it:** In the Letter on Return, through teshuvah and an honest accounting; and in the morning blessing "My G-d, the soul You gave me" (see "Handing yourself back").
- **From inside:** A tug at the center: the sense that you belong somewhere and are still tied there.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I've drifted so far. I don't think there's a way back." After: "The top of the rope was never untied. I can pull myself back along it."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 2, TANYA line 86: היא חלק אלוה ממעל ממש ("it is truly a part of G-d above"). - Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 5, TANYA line 939: כמו חבל, על דרך משל, שראשו אחד קשור למעלה וקצהו למטה ("like a rope, one end tied above and the other end below"). - Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 5, TANYA line 945: מחבל עב שזור מתרי״ג חבלים דקים ("a thick rope twisted from 613 thin ropes"). - Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 6, TANYA line 953: אם ימשוך אדם בראשו השני – ינענע וימשך אחריו גם ראשו הראשון ("if a person pulls its other end, the first end moves and is drawn after it too"). - Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 9, TANYA line 987: כמשל חבל הנפסק וחוזר וקושרו ("like a rope that was cut and is tied again").

## The King in the field

- **What it says:** The month of Elul is like a king on his way to his city. Before he arrives, the people go out to meet him in the field. There anyone who wants may come out to greet him, and "he receives them all with a pleasant face and shows a smiling face to all." Later, in the palace, only the chosen may enter, and only with permission. In Elul G-d's "face" shines on the source of every Jewish soul, and the Alter Rebbe adds: every Jew has "a spark of G-d itself" that pulls upward and wants to give itself over, above reason.
- **What it adds:** Being held also means being met. You do not need to reach the palace, prepare yourself, or earn entry. He comes out to where you are, in your working clothes.
- **The picture:** The king in the field. Where it breaks: the parable is set in one month of the year. (Our reading: the Rebbeim use it for Elul; applying the feeling of it to any moment of turning is ours.)
- **How the source says to realize it:** "Ani ledodi" (I am my beloved's) is the awakening from below that belongs to Elul: to wish, from the depth of the heart, to cling to Him, and to set aside one's own wants before His ("nullify your will before His will").
- **From inside:** Being greeted before you have your act together.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I'll come to G-d once I've fixed myself up." After: "He's in the field. He's already smiling at me as I am."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Torah, Re'eh, "Ani ledodi," LT line 3427: משל למלך שקודם בואו לעיר יוצאין אנשי העיר לקראתו ומקבלין פניו בשדה ("a king who, before he comes into the city, the townspeople go out to meet in the field"). - Same line: הוא מקבל את כולם בסבר פנים יפות ומראה פנים שוחקות לכולם ("he receives them all with a pleasant face and shows a smiling face to all"). - LT line 3428: ואח״כ בבואו להיכל מלכותו אין נכנסים כ״א ברשות ("and afterward, in his palace, none enter without permission"). - LT line 3428: דהיינו שיש בכל נפש מישראל ניצוץ אלהות ממש המחיה נפשו האלהית ("in every Jewish soul there is a spark of G-d itself that gives life to the Divine soul").

## He lives here (joy as the sign)

- **What it says:** When a person sees that his soul needs to be cleared and lit with joy, let him think deeply about the true unity: He fills all worlds, the whole earth is His glory, all is as nothing before Him, "and in this very place where the world was made, He alone filled it, and now too." If he goes deep in this, his heart will be glad, "for this is truly nearness to G-d." How glad a lowly man is when a king comes to lodge with him in his house; how much more the King of kings. This is why we say each morning, "How good is our portion": like one who inherits a great fortune he never worked for, we inherited the knowledge that "even on the earth below there is nothing but Him, and this is His dwelling below."
- **What it adds:** It makes joy (simcha) the sign that being held has landed, and the tool for getting there. It also flips the direction: you are not only held by Him, you are His home.
- **The picture:** The king lodging in a poor man's house; the unearned inheritance. Where it breaks: a human king visits and leaves. "Now too, He alone."
- **How the source says to realize it:** "At set times," when you see your soul needs lifting, go deep into this contemplation. And the daily "Ashreinu" (how good is our portion) each morning.
- **From inside:** A rising, unearned gladness. The house feels occupied.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I don't feel joy. I feel like I'm carrying everything alone." After: "Someone moved in. It's His house too. I didn't earn it; it was handed to me."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 33, TANYA line 472: וגם במקום הזה שנברא בו עולם הזה, השמים והארץ וכל צבאם, היה הוא לבדו ממלא המקום הזה, וגם עתה כן ("in this very place where this world was made, He alone filled this place, and now too"). - TANYA line 475: ישמח לבו ותגל נפשו אף גילת ורנן בכל לב ונפש ומאד באמונה זו ("his heart will be glad and his soul rejoice in this faith"). - TANYA line 476: כמה גדולה שמחת הדיוט ושפל אנשים, בהתקרבותו למלך בשר ודם המתאכסן ודר אתו עמו בביתו ("how great the joy of a lowly man when a king lodges and lives with him in his house"). - TANYA line 477: אשר אפילו בארץ מתחת – אין עוד מלבדו, וזו היא דירתו בתחתונים ("that even on the earth below there is nothing but Him, and this is His dwelling below").

## Seen and known: the King standing over you

- **What it says:** Before study or a mitzvah, think at least briefly of His greatness: He fills and surrounds all worlds, "and here G-d stands over him, the whole earth is full of His glory, and He looks at him and searches his heart." In chapter 42: any person, whoever he is, who thinks about this "for a long while every day," how He fills the upper and lower worlds, "and watches and looks and searches his heart and all his deeds and words, and counts all his steps," will have awe fixed in his heart all day, and then even a brief thought at any hour brings it back. With a human king, the awe is of his inner life, not his body, "for when he sleeps there is no awe of him." We see only the body and the clothes and know the life is inside. So the heavens and the earth are the King's outer garments, and His life is dressed in them.
- **What it adds:** Being held includes being seen, watched and counted, "all his steps." In the Tanya this first wakes awe, and the Tanya does not soften that. Our reading: for a person who fears being unseen or forgotten, the same line answers that too. Nothing you do is unwitnessed.
- **The picture:** Standing before a king; the king's clothes and the life within them; the courtiers bowing to one man, so that even a stranger who never saw the king feels awe (the note at line 642: the stars bow westward each evening). Where it breaks: a king's gaze can be avoided. His cannot, and it is not hostile.
- **How the source says to realize it:** This is the Tanya's most precise instruction. First, a long contemplation, "a great hour every day," as long "as his mind can grasp and his free time allows." Second, short returns to it "at every moment and every hour," which work because of the long one. And third, the habit: "the main thing is habit," to train the mind to see everything as the King's outer garments. That training is what the word emunah (faith) means: "a word of practice," like a craftsman who trains (me'amen) his hands.
- **From inside:** You are not alone in the room. There is weight and there is company.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "No one sees what I'm carrying." After: "Every step is being counted by the One who's holding me. It's all seen."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 41, TANYA line 600: והנה ה׳ נצב עליו, ו״מלא כל הארץ כבודו״, ומביט עליו ("and here G-d stands over him, 'the whole earth is full of His glory,' and looks at him"). - TANYA line 601: ויעמיק במחשבה זו ויאריך בה כפי יכולת השגת מוחו ומחשבתו, וכפי הפנאי שלו ("let him go deep in this thought and dwell on it as far as his mind can reach and his free time allows"). - Likkutei Amarim ch. 42, TANYA line 639: כשיתבונן בזה שעה גדולה בכל יום ("when he contemplates this a great hour every day"). - Same line: וצופה ומביט ובוחן כליותיו ולבו וכל מעשיו ודיבוריו, וכל צעדיו יספור ("and watches and looks and searches his heart and all his deeds and words, and counts all his steps"). - Same line: כשיחזור ויתבונן בזה אפילו בהתבוננות קלה, בכל עת ובכל שעה ("when he returns to it even in a light contemplation, at every time and hour"). - TANYA line 641: כשישן – אין שום יראה ממנו ("when he sleeps, there is no awe of him"). - TANYA line 644: אלא העיקר הוא ההרגל, להרגיל דעתו ומחשבתו תמיד ("the main thing is habit, to accustom his mind and thought always"). - Same line: הכל הם לבושים החיצונים של המלך הקדושברוךהוא ("all are the outer garments of the King"). - Same line: וזה נכלל גם כן בלשון ״אמונה״, שהוא לשון רגילות ("this too is in the word emunah, which means habit").

## Handing yourself back

- **What it says:** Every morning we say, "My G-d, the soul You gave me is pure; You breathed it into me and You will one day take it from me." The Tanya reads it as a decision: since You breathed it into me and You will take it back, "then from now I hand it over and return it to You, to unite it with Your oneness." This happens by binding my thought to His thought and my speech to His speech, in the words of Torah and prayer, and especially when speaking to Him directly, "Blessed are You."
- **What it adds:** A practice of letting go that starts from being held. You do not let go into nothing. You give back what was lent to you, to the One who gave it and holds it anyway.
- **The picture:** A deposit returned to its owner. Where it breaks: a returned deposit leaves your hands. Here you give the soul back and keep living with it, now joined to Him.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Precise timing: with this preparation, begin the morning blessings; begin your set study right after prayer; and in the middle of the day, before study, renew it at least briefly.
- **From inside:** The grip loosens. You are still here, but not clutching yourself.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I have to hold on to everything or I'll lose myself." After: "I gave myself back this morning. I'm still here, and I'm not the one holding me."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 41, TANYA line 621: לכן, מעתה אני מוסרה ומחזירה לך לייחדה באחדותך ("therefore from now I hand it over and return it to You, to unite it with Your oneness").

## From hearing to seeing

- **What it says:** In Torah Or the Alter Rebbe separates two ways of knowing G-d's unity. "Hearing" is understanding it well, thinking it through deeply, even having faith fixed firmly in the heart; but it is still like news heard from others, so "when he takes his mind off it even for a moment he turns to his desires." "Seeing" is when the knowledge is so bound to the soul that it is as if the eyes saw it: "as if he saw with his eye the birth and coming-to-be of something from nothing." Then "there is no way he can take his mind off it," because "he sees the King with his eyes." The bridge between them is da'at, binding the mind to the depth of the heart. And he says plainly that the basic fact is open to all: "we see that the worlds live and endure, and this life in them is from the Infinite, flowing into them every moment. This understanding is grasped by everyone."
- **What it adds:** It explains why hearing "you are held" a hundred times does not work, and names what would: moving the same fact from the ears to the eyes. This is the Alter Rebbe's own diagnosis of the owner's complaint about a repeated phrase.
- **The picture:** Hearing that something will happen compared to seeing it coming; the soul known by seeing the body alive. Where it breaks: G-d cannot be seen with eyes of flesh. The seeing is the mind's eye, "like one who sees by his wisdom what will come."
- **How the source says to realize it:** Through da'at: bind the thought to the point of the heart until the whole self is taken up in it, so that the understanding (binah) takes on the quality of sight (chochmah). The Sages' "look at three things" is his model: look, don't only hear.
- **From inside:** The fact stops being a sentence and becomes a view. You cannot un-see it for the moment.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I know, I know, I'm held. I've heard it. It doesn't help." After: "I just watched it: the next breath came, and I didn't make it."
- **Sources:** - Torah Or, Mishpatim, TO line 2041: והרי חיות זה בהן הוא מא"ס ב"ה השופע בהן בכל רגע והשכלה זו מושגת לכל ("this life in them is from the Infinite, flowing into them every moment, and this understanding is grasped by all"). - Same line: איזהו חכם הרואה את הנולד כאלו רואה בעין ממש הולדת והתהוות היש מאין ("who is wise? one who sees what is being born, as if he saw with his eye the birth of something from nothing"). - Same line: ולפיכך כשיסיר מחשבתו אף לפי רגע פונה לדרכי תאותיה ("so when he removes his thought even for a moment, he turns to his desires"). - Same line: ואי אפשר לו בשום אופן להסיר מחשבתו ("and there is no way he can remove his thought"). - Same line: מפני היותו רואה המלך בעיניו ("because he sees the King with his eyes"). - Torah Or, Va'era, TO line 1521 (the same fact about all things, even the husks of evil): הם מקבלים חיות תמיד מקדושה בכל רגע ושבאמת אינם יש ודבר נפרד בפ"ע ("they receive life from holiness every moment, and in truth are not a separate thing on their own").

## Made from nothing this moment

- **What it says:** This letter (Iggeret HaKodesh 11) is the Tanya's direct teaching on how to live as one held. The light of G-d does not rest on one who is attached to "children, life and food" (the things people fear for). "Nullify your will." The explanation is true faith in the Creator: creation from nothing happens "at every time and moment." When a person "pictures in his mind his coming into being from nothing at every single moment," how can he think it is bad for him, or that he suffers over children, health or livelihood? The "nothing" he comes from is G-d's wisdom, "the source of life, good and delight." There is no bad coming down from above; "all is good, only it is not grasped because it is so great." So "first of all" a person should be glad at every hour and truly live by his faith in G-d, "who gives him life and does him good every moment." One who is sad and complains shows he feels something bad and lacking. The believer: "yes and no are equal to him in true equality." All his needs, in every detail, come down from Him. And by this faith itself, the seeming bad is lifted into the hidden good, and "all becomes good even openly."
- **What it adds:** It connects continuous creation to the most human fears (money, health, children) and gives the stance: joy, and equanimity (hishtavut). It also makes a daring claim, that the faith itself turns hidden good into seen good.
- **The picture:** Being made at each moment out of a "nothing" that is really the fullest good. Where it breaks: "nothing" suggests emptiness; the letter says it is fullness too great to grasp.
- **How the source says to realize it:** "Let him contemplate in the depth of his understanding and picture in his mind his being from nothing every single moment." Then the order: "first of all" joy, at every time; then living by the faith; the test is whether yes and no feel equal.
- **From inside:** The next moment arrives as a gift rather than a threat. A lightness about outcomes.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "If this job doesn't come through I don't know what I'll do." After: "I'm being made right now, from a place that only gives good. Yes or no, I'm held either way."
- **Sources:** - Iggeret HaKodesh 11, TANYA line 1143: שיהיה רצונו בטל במציאות ("that his will be nullified"). - TANYA line 1144: ויצייר בדעתו הווייתו מ״אין״ בכל רגע ורגע ממש ("and pictures in his mind his being from nothing at every single moment"). - Same line: הרי ה״אין״, שהיא חכמתו יתברך, הוא מקור החיים והטוב והעונג ("the 'nothing,' His wisdom, is the source of life, good and delight"). - Same line: אבל באמת אין רע יורד מלמעלה, והכל טוב, רק שאינו מושג לגודלו ורב טובו ("in truth no bad comes down from above; all is good, only not grasped for its greatness and abundant good"). - TANYA line 1145: להאמין ד״לית אתר פנוי מיניה״ ("to believe that 'there is no place empty of Him'"). - TANYA line 1146: ויחיה ממש באמונתו בה׳, המחיה ומטיב עמו בכל רגע ("and truly live by his faith in G-d, who gives him life and does him good every moment"). - TANYA line 1147: ובכל עניני העולם ״הן״ ו״לאו״ שוין אצלו בהשוואה אמיתית ("in all worldly matters, 'yes' and 'no' are equal to him in true equality"). - TANYA line 1150: וכל צרכיו וכל עניניו משתלשלים באמת בפרטי פרטיותיהם ("all his needs and affairs come down, in every last detail"). - TANYA line 1151: ובאמונה זו באמת, נעשה הכל טוב גם בגלוי ("by this faith, truly, all becomes good even openly").

## The good that is hidden

- **What it says:** Chapter 26 gives "counsel to clear the heart of all sadness and every flicker of worry about worldly things, even children, life and food." The Sages said to bless on the bad as on the good, "to accept it with joy," because "this too is for the good," only not seen by eyes of flesh. It comes from the hidden world, which is higher than the revealed world. Iggeret HaKodesh 11 adds that this hidden good is the higher good, and that faith draws it into the open.
- **What it adds:** It treats what hurts as held too, but at a level the eye cannot reach. The hidden is not less good; it is higher good.
- **The picture:** Two worlds, the revealed and the hidden, like two letters of G-d's Name, the lower and the higher. Where it breaks: the Tanya does not ask you to pretend pain is pleasant. It asks for joy in what you cannot yet see.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Take the Sages' rule as practice: bless and accept "with joy" the way you would the good, because it is good you cannot see.
- **From inside:** Not an answer, but a floor under the question.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "Nothing good can come from this." After: "I can't see the good in it. That doesn't mean it isn't held. It may be from higher up than I can see."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Amarim ch. 26, TANYA line 375: עצה היעוצה לטהר לבו מכל עצב ונדנוד דאגה ממילי דעלמא, ואפילו בני חיי ומזוני ("counsel to clear his heart of all sadness and flicker of worry about worldly things, even children, life and food"). - Same line: כי ״גם זו – לטובה״, רק שאינה נגלית ונראית לעיני בשר ("for 'this too is for the good,' only it is not revealed and seen by eyes of flesh").

## Anger is faith gone missing (and the one who hurts you is held too)

- **What it says:** The Sages said one who gets angry is like one who serves idols. The reason: "at the time of his anger his faith has left him, for if he believed that this came to him from G-d, he would not be angry at all." The other person has free choice and is guilty for it; still, what happened to the one harmed was already decreed, "and G-d has many messengers." More: at the very moment someone curses or hits you, G-d's breath is dressed in him, keeping him alive. "G-d told him to curse," said David of Shimi; had G-d's breath left Shimi for a moment, he could not have said a word. The letter then brings the Besht's "Forever Your word" again: the same continuous creation. And it adds that a spark of the Shechinah (the divine Presence) is "in exile" inside that very speech, as the Sages said, "when they went into exile, the Shechinah was with them." If you can do nothing about the one disturbing you, "what has G-d done this for? Only so that he will strengthen himself more in his prayer."
- **What it adds:** It carries being held into the hardest place: other people's harm. It is the one angle that holds both you and the person hurting you in the same hands. It does not excuse him; it frees you from being ruled by him.
- **The picture:** Shimi cursing David, and David's answer. Where it breaks: the letter itself limits it to worldly matters; where you can protest a wrong against Heaven, you should (Moshe "was angry").
- **How the source says to realize it:** The counsel for arousing yourself: think of how the Shechinah came down "wondrously" and is in exile in this very person's speech; then pray more deeply, until you do not hear him.
- **From inside:** The heat drops. The other person shrinks to a messenger, and you are still in good hands.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "He ruined everything for me. I can't stop being furious." After: "He's responsible for what he did. But he doesn't run my life. Even he is being held up right now."
- **Sources:** - Iggeret HaKodesh 25, TANYA line 1376: לפי שבעת כעסו נסתלקה ממנו האמונה, כי אילו היה מאמין שמאת ה׳ היתה זאת לו – לא היה בכעס כלל ("at the time of his anger his faith left him; had he believed this came from G-d, he would not be angry at all"). - TANYA line 1377: על הניזק כבר נגזר מן השמים, והרבה שלוחים למקום ("for the one harmed it was already decreed in Heaven, and G-d has many messengers"). - TANYA line 1378: מתלבש בו כח ה׳ ורוח פיו יתברך המחייהו ומקיימו ("the power of G-d and the breath of His mouth are dressed in him, giving him life and keeping him"). - TANYA line 1396: גלו לאדום שכינה עמהם ("they went into exile to Edom, the Shechinah with them"). - TANYA line 1405: אם כן מה זאת עשה ה׳ לו, אין זאת, כי אם – כדי שיתגבר ויתאמץ יותר בתפלתו ("then what has G-d done this for? Only so that he strengthen himself more in his prayer").

## Down to the very last detail

- **What it says:** The Mitteler Rebbe says the whole point of all contemplation of the chain of worlds, every detail of it, is "only that everything be included in the simple oneness," until "the order of divine providence in detail, down to the very last detail, is fixed in the soul," even the courses of the stars, which move "by His simple will in His very Essence." Hayom Yom gives the same as the service of Chassidut: "to train oneself to see individual providence, how in His goodness He renews the world and its creatures every moment by His individual providence, and this alone is their existence, life and being." And: not only is every movement of every creature watched, and is its very life; each small movement "has a relation to the whole purpose of creation."
- **What it adds:** It ties the two big ideas together. Being held (continuous creation) and being watched over (providence) are one fact. The same act that keeps you in being is the act that attends to you. And no detail is too small to matter to the whole.
- **The picture:** The stars kept in their watches "according to His will." Where it breaks: the stars seem to run on fixed laws. The Mitteler Rebbe says that even the fixed courses are His simple will now.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Contemplation that goes through detail after detail and ends in the one point; the goal is that providence "be fixed in the soul." Hayom Yom's word is "to train oneself" (lehargil): a habit, as in Tanya 42.
- **From inside:** The small things of the day (a missed bus, a word overheard) start to feel addressed.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "G-d handles the big picture. My little life isn't on His radar." After: "The little stuff is exactly where He's holding me. Each piece fits into the whole."
- **Sources:** - Mitteler Rebbe, the Gate of Unity ch. 53, GOU line 2673: רק להיות נכלל הכל באחדות הפשוטה ("only that all be included in the simple oneness"). - Same line: עד שיקבע בנפש גם בסדר השגחה אלהי״ת בפרטיות, עד הפרט היותר אחרון ("until the order of divine providence in detail, down to the very last detail, is fixed in the soul"). - Hayom Yom, 29 Sivan, HY line 380: להרגיל את עצמו לראות ענין השגחה פרטית, איך כי בטובו ית' מחדש את העולם והנבראים בכל רגע בהשגחתו הפרטית ("to train oneself to see individual providence: how in His goodness He renews the world and its creatures every moment by His individual providence"). - Hayom Yom, 28 Cheshvan, HY line 628: אלא עוד זאת דתנועה פרטית של נברא יש לה יחס כללי לכללות כונת הבריאה ("moreover, a single movement of a creature bears on the whole purpose of creation").

## The leaf, the wind, and how it fell

- **What it says:** The Baal Shem Tov taught that providence is not only over people but over animals, plants and even lifeless things. A leaf that falls and rolls in the street is under providence, "by judgment, as to the time and the place where it must roll." The Frierdiker Rebbe tells it of a straw in the marketplace: G-d brings a wind out of His storehouses to turn it from side to side, and "all this is a ring in the whole chain" that rose in His will. The Rebbe tells what happened when the Mitteler Rebbe reported this teaching to his father: the Alter Rebbe added that providence is not only over the leaf's rolling, "but also over the way it came to roll, whether by a person or by a gust of wind." The Rebbe explains: the Besht showed the thing itself; the Alter Rebbe showed the how. The Rebbe Rashab, at a farbrengen in 5654 (1894), explained that by Chassidut providence is "not only in matters of understanding and grasp, but even in simple things." And the Frierdiker Rebbe adds the purpose: the straw moves "for a higher intention, that a person needs to learn something from it for his life."
- **What it adds:** It extends being held to the route as well as the result: not only where you ended up, but which wind or which person brought you. Nothing is chance, including the means.
- **The picture:** The leaf in the wind; the straw in the market; the fish in the sea "set for death" and the bird that is caught or goes free (the Alter Rebbe's proofs from the Talmud and Midrash). Where it breaks: a leaf does not know it is held. A person can, and is asked to learn from it.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Look at what just happened and ask what it teaches. The Frierdiker Rebbe (5706): "a person needs to learn a lesson in life from it."
- **From inside:** Even the clumsy way something happened stops feeling random.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "It's so stupid how I lost it, just a careless mistake by someone else." After: "Even the way it happened was held. I can ask what it's here to show me."
- **Sources:** - Toras Menachem 5712, part 1 (a talk after 19 Kislev), TM line 8065: גם עלה נובלת המתגלגלת בחוץ הוא בהשגחה פרטית, בדין ומשפט על הזמן והמקום שעלי' להתגלגל ("even a fallen leaf rolling outside is under providence, by judgment, as to the time and place where it must roll"). - TM line 8067: לא מבעי שההשגחה פרטית היא על העלה המתגלגלת, כי אם גם על אופני סיבת גלגולה ("not only is providence over the rolling leaf, but also over the manner and cause of its rolling"). - TM line 8069 (quoting the Frierdiker Rebbe's maamar): הנה עוד זאת שההשגחה פרטית היא גם בהסבות וסבות דסבות ("providence is also in the causes, and the causes of the causes"). - TM line 8075: אלא גם האופן שבו נעשה הדבר ("but also the way the thing was done"). - Sefer HaSichos (Frierdiker Rebbe), 5680-5687, SS-RY line 1063: גם קש בודד המתגולל בככר השוק אומר מורנו הבעש"ט נ"ע כי זהו בהשגחה פרטית ("even a lone straw rolling in the marketplace, says the Besht, is by individual providence"). - Same line: ואשר כל זה הוא טבעת בכל עניני השלשלת שעלה ברצונו יתברך ("and all this is a ring in the whole chain that rose in His will"). - Sefer HaSichos 5710, second day of Chol HaMoed Sukkot, SS-RY line 9763 (the Rebbe Rashab, 5654): אז השגחה פרטית איז ניט נאר אין ענינים פון הבנה והשגה נאר אפילו אין דברים פשוטים ("providence is not only in matters of understanding and grasp but even in simple things"). - Sefer HaSichos 5706, 20 Kislev, SS-RY line 6254: צוליב א כוונה עליונה, וואס דער מענטש בעדארף פון דעם אפולע לערנען אין לעבען ("for a higher purpose, that a person needs to learn a lesson from it in life").

## Held, and also sent

- **What it says:** "One who believes in individual providence knows that 'a man's steps are set by G-d,'" that this soul needs to repair something in a certain place. For hundreds of years, or since creation, the thing that needs fixing "waits for that soul to come," and the soul has been waiting since it was made for its time to come down and do it. The Frierdiker Rebbe, on Simchat Torah 5710, applies the Besht's leaf to people: as a wind carries a leaf "until it comes to a place and there it stays," so here too everything is by providence, "to the moment, in that place and with that person."
- **What it adds:** Being held is also being placed. You are not only kept from falling; you are set down somewhere on purpose, and something there has been waiting for you.
- **The picture:** The leaf carried by the wind until it settles in its spot. Where it breaks: a leaf lies there. A soul is placed in order to act.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Our reading, from the Hayom Yom line: when you arrive somewhere, or meet someone, ask what here has been waiting for you.
- **From inside:** Where you are stops feeling like an accident or a detour.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "How did I end up here? This isn't where my life was supposed to go." After: "I was set down here. Something here has been waiting for me."
- **Sources:** - Hayom Yom, Elul section, HY line 478: המאמין בהשגחה פרטית יודע כי מה' מצעדי גבר כוננו ("one who believes in individual providence knows that a man's steps are set by G-d"). - Same line: מחכה לאותה הנשמה שתבוא לבררו ולתקנו ("waits for that soul to come and refine and repair it"). - Sefer HaSichos 5710, Simchat Torah, SS-RY line 9818: אזוי איז אויך דא אלעס בהשגחה פרטית, צו דער רגע, ובאותו מקום ובאותו האיש ("so too here all is by individual providence, to the moment, in that place and with that person").

## Worry is a kind of sleep

- **What it says:** In a Rosh Hashanah maamar the Frierdiker Rebbe reads Maimonides' shofar call, "Wake up, sleepers, from your sleep; slumberers, rouse yourselves." Sleep is when "the eyes are shut and he does not set his heart on individual providence," and takes a lie for truth, thinking "that because of his wisdom and good management G-d makes him succeed." Deep slumber is worse: forgetting the truth altogether, sliding into a frantic rush (in his Yiddish) where "he needs everything, everything is necessary to him," there is time for everything except prayer and learning. Sleep needs only a wake-up; slumber needs real treatment, an orderly path of return.
- **What it adds:** It gives the psychology of not feeling held. It is not wickedness; it is sleep. The proof is a certain frantic feeling that everything depends on me and everything is urgent. It also names a quieter form: taking credit for success.
- **The picture:** Sleep and deep slumber; the dreamer who takes the dream for real. Where it breaks: a sleeper cannot wake himself. The shofar, from outside, does it; the maamar then asks the person to take up the path himself.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Hear the shofar's call; then "look into your souls and improve your ways": fixed times for Torah and prayer with the community are named as what the rushing crowds out.
- **From inside:** Waking up: the rush drains out, and you notice you were asleep.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I have no time for anything, everything is urgent, it's all on me." After: "That rushing was a kind of sleep. Nothing actually depends on me racing."
- **Sources:** - Sefer HaMaamarim (Frierdiker Rebbe) 5692-5693, Rosh Hashanah maamar, SM-RY line 41: דהשינה היא שעיניו סגורות ואינו שם אל לבו עניני השגחה פרטית ("sleep is when his eyes are closed and he does not set his heart on individual providence"). - Same line: כי חושב שבשביל חכמתו וטוב הנהגתו ד' מצליח לו בעניניו ("for he thinks that because of his wisdom and good conduct G-d makes him succeed"). - Same line: אז אלץ דארף ער, אלץ איז אים נייטיג ("that he needs everything, everything is necessary to him").

## Worry is forgetting (does G-d need you to worry?)

- **What it says:** The Rebbe, many times, in letters: the known remedy for worry is trust (bitachon), as in Chovot HaLevavot's Gate of Trust. And it follows from the basics of faith: G-d watches over each person by individual providence, and He is the essence of good, "whatever the Merciful One does, He does for good." "So what room is left for worry? It can only arise when one forgets the basics of faith." To a man pressed by his troubles (Pesach 5711) he writes in Yiddish: faith must fill the whole of life, not stay a thought. If He can lead a billion and a half people, then surely for your affairs the verse holds, "I made, and I will carry; I will bear and I will save" (Isaiah 46:4). "Does the Almighty need you to worry" so that He can run your household and solve your problems? And he offers two roads: go around worried until the blessing comes true, and then regret "all the life-strength you wasted worrying for nothing"; or be strong in trust now, and later say, "see how well I did not to worry."
- **What it adds:** It names worry as a lapse of memory, not a lack of love or effort, and so it can be treated by remembering. And it puts Isaiah's "I will carry" into plain speech: the carrying is His job, and your worry adds nothing to it.
- **The picture:** "I made and I will carry," like one who carries a child he made (the verse speaks of carrying "to old age and gray hair"). Where it breaks: a human carrier tires. The Rebbe's point is that the One carrying runs the whole world and is not strained by you.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Learn some chapters of the Gate of Trust (IK, to a nervous man); think it through with the basics of faith; choose the second road, trust now.
- **From inside:** A loosening in the shoulders. The job you gave yourself is taken off you.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "If I stop worrying about it, who will?" After: "He said, 'I will carry.' My worrying doesn't help Him carry."
- **Sources:** - Igros Kodesh vol. 17, 15 Iyar 5718, IK line 74291: וא"כ מה מקום נשאר לדאגה? אין זה כי אם כששוכחים על עיקרי האמונה ("so what room is left for worry? It can only be when one forgets the basics of faith"). In the file the quotation marks are HTML-encoded (&quot;); the letters match. - Igros Kodesh, 16 Adar II 5714, IK line 33711 (to a man troubled by his nerves): כדאי הי' שילמוד איזה פרקים בשער הבטחון ("it would be worthwhile for him to learn some chapters of the Gate of Trust"). - Igros Kodesh vol. 4, letter 984, 13 Nisan 5711, IK line 17148: אני עשיתי ואני אשא ואני אסבול ואמלט ("I made and I will carry, I will bear and I will save"). - IK line 17149: צי נויטיקט זיך דער אויבערשטער אז איר זאלט דאגה'ן ("does the Almighty need you to worry"). - IK line 17150: וואס האט איר געדארפט פטר'ן אזויפיל לעבנסקראפט דאגה'נדיק אומזיסט ("that you had to waste so much life-strength worrying for nothing").

## Stop dwelling; kindness surrounds the one who trusts

- **What it says:** The Tzemach Tzedek writes to a relative who was sunk in worry over his poor health: "If you will listen to me, distance yourself from this to the very last extreme, to take your mind off it completely, and trust in G-d's kindness." Dwelling on it "ruins you with your own hands," in body (he says this is known by plain experience) and in spirit. Trust removes the accusations and judgments, as it says, "one who trusts in G-d, kindness surrounds him": the kindness wraps him and hides him from every accuser. This is the kindness "that shines without limit or measure, even though the person is not worthy at all." It is like a great river whose current runs so strong that no one can dam it to build a mill.
- **What it adds:** It is the Tzemach Tzedek's own written form of what he later said in four Yiddish words (next angle). It makes trust literally a form of being held: "surrounded" by kindness. And it treats rumination itself as harmful, not neutral.
- **The picture:** Kindness as a surrounding wrap; the river that cannot be dammed. Where it breaks: a river's force comes from the slope. This kindness comes from above all merit.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Deliberately take the mind off the fear "to the last extreme"; trust; and give charity beyond the usual measure, which he says makes a vessel for this unlimited kindness (drawing on the Alter Rebbe's Iggeret HaKodesh 10).
- **From inside:** Letting the mind rest away from the sore spot, and feeling cushioned rather than exposed.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I keep checking my symptoms and reading about it all night." After: "I'm going to take my mind off it on purpose. I'm wrapped in more kindness than I can earn."
- **Sources:** - Igros Kodesh of the Tzemach Tzedek (letter dated in the edition with a query, [5589?]), IK-TZ line 274: ואם לקולי ישמע יתרחק מזה עד קצה האחרון ממש להסיח דעתו לגמרי ("if he will listen to me, let him distance himself from this to the very last extreme, to take his mind off it completely"). - IK-TZ line 275: כי על ידי העמקתו בזה מקלקל את עצמו בידים ("for by going deep into it he spoils himself with his own hands"). - Same line: שהחסד יקיפנו ויסתירנו מכל בעלי דין ("the kindness will surround him and hide him from every accuser"). - Same line: דהיינו אעפ"י שאין האדם ראוי כלל ("that is, even though the person is not worthy at all"). - IK-TZ line 276: והוא כמשל מי נהר גדול שנמשך הזרם וההליכה שלהם בתגבורת גדולה ("like the waters of a great river whose current flows with great force").

## Think good and it will be good

- **What it says:** Someone begged the Tzemach Tzedek to pray for a dangerously ill person. He answered: "Tracht gut, vet zein gut," think good and it will be good. The Rebbe (Likkutei Sichos 36) builds a whole account of trust on it. First try: trust means knowing all is from Him, so even if I deserve the trouble, I am calm, since no one but Him has power over me, and even punishment is kindness. "But this explanation is not enough." Trust in its plain sense is being sure of good that is seen and revealed, not only calm about hidden good. Yet who can say he deserves it? The answer: trust is not a by-product of faith. It is its own work: to lean on G-d until you throw your whole lot on Him, "having no support in the world besides Him," like a prisoner whose only hope is his master. Trust is "work and toil in the soul." And when a person truly relies on G-d alone, so that he does not worry at all, "without making calculations whether he can be saved," then measure for measure G-d treats him that way, with open good, even if he would not otherwise deserve it. That is the Tzemach Tzedek's meaning: the trust itself brings the good.
- **What it adds:** It moves trust from a feeling that follows from belief to an act a person does. It also answers the fear "maybe I don't deserve good": the trust itself is what draws it.
- **The picture:** The prisoner in the pit, whose only help is his master (from Chovot HaLevavot). Where it breaks: a prisoner trusts because he has no choice. Here the person chooses to have no other support.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Do the work: lean fully, cast the burden ("Cast your burden on G-d"), stop the calculations of whether it can work out naturally. The sign that it is real: no worry at all.
- **From inside:** A choice made in the chest, then a quiet. Not certainty about the outcome, but leaning your full weight.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I'll believe it will be fine when I see it." After: "I'm choosing to lean on Him completely now. That leaning is my part."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Sichos vol. 36, Shemot, sicha 1, LS line 25032: טראכט גוט — וועט זיין גוט ("think good, it will be good"). - Same line: שעצם המח­שבה לטובה (הבטחון) תביא לתוצאות טו­בות ("that the very thought of good, the trust, will bring good results"). - LS line 25020: שהדבר וודאי אצלו עד שהוא במנוחה גמורה ואינו דואג כלל ("the thing is certain to him, until he is at complete rest and does not worry at all"). - LS line 25023: אין זה מפני שיש שליטה ח"ו למי שמציר לו באיזה אופן שהוא, רק הכל הוא מן השמים ("it is not that the one who distresses him has any power at all; all is from Heaven"). - LS line 25027: אבל אין ביאור זה מספיק ("but this explanation is not enough"). - LS line 25034: אלא חובה זו היא עבודה בפ"ע ("rather this duty is a service of its own"). - LS line 25035: שאין לו שום משען ("that he has no support"), continuing "in the world besides Him"; and כעבד האסור אשר הוא בבית הבור ברשות אדוניו ("like a prisoner in the pit, in his master's power"). - LS line 25038: בטחון הוא עבודה ויגיעה בנפשו ("trust is work and toil in his soul"). - LS line 25039: מבלי לעשות חשבונות, אם אפשר לו להנצל ("without making calculations whether he can be saved").

## Make the vessel, but do not lean on it

- **What it says:** The Rebbe Rashab (Hemshech 5672, section 82) ties trust to temimut, wholeness: "Be whole (tamim) with the L-rd your G-d," which Rashi explains as "trust in whatever comes to you," not going by schemes and excess cleverness. There are two ways. In the first, a person makes a vessel for G-d's blessing, "the L-rd will bless you in all that you do," so you must do; "but he should not think the vessel is the main thing, nor set his mind and heart on it." Then nothing stops him from fixed times of Torah and prayer, and when business turns up right at prayer time he can leave it, because he trusts that G-d is the provider. In the second way, for some things a person leaves it wholly to G-d and does nothing, and this needs more wholeness, a bitul (self-setting-aside) above what human reason requires. A person who is whole in his deeds does little business, "knows well that this is only a vessel for the flow from above, and trusts in G-d's blessing." R. Hillel of Paritch gives the same two kinds: one who plows and sows in season and trusts G-d for the blessing, and one who trusts G-d to send even the vessel, like those who kept the Sabbatical year. Hayom Yom gives the everyday form: on Wednesday, when a Jew starts to wonder where Shabbat will come from and turns gloomy, "the advice is: Lechu neranenah (come, let us sing), trust." By Thursday you understand you must also do something.
- **What it adds:** Being held does not mean doing nothing. It means doing without leaning on the doing. It gives a clear line between effort (hishtadlut) and worry: the effort is a vessel; the mind and heart do not live in it.
- **The picture:** A vessel for rain; a farmer who plows and trusts for the rain. Where it breaks: the bigger the vessel, the more it holds. The Rashab says the opposite of greed: a small vessel is enough, since the blessing is what fills it.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Keep fixed times for Torah and prayer even when business pulls; when it pulls exactly then, leave it, as the test of trust. Do what is needed, and "do not set mind and heart" on it.
- **From inside:** Working with open hands. Busy, but not gripping.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "If I don't take every call and every client, we'll go under." After: "I'll do my part properly and stop there. My part is the bucket, not the rain."
- **Sources:** - Rebbe Rashab, Hemshech 5672 (Ayin-Beis), section 82, AB line 636: להיות בוטח בכל קורות ונולדות הבאות לך ("to trust in all that happens and comes upon you"). - Same line: שיעשה כלי לברכת ה' ("that he make a vessel for G-d's blessing"). - AB line 637: לעיקר ולא יניח מוחו ולבו בזה ("[not to think the vessel] the main thing, and not to put his mind and heart into it"). - AB line 639: ויש כמה דברים שהאדם צריך להניח על הנהגת ה' לבדו ("there are some things a person must leave to G-d's guidance alone"). - AB line 3062: ויודע היטיב שזהו רק כלי להשפעה העליונה ובוטח בברכת ה' ("and knows well that this is only a vessel for the flow from above, and trusts in G-d's blessing"). - R. Hillel of Paritch, Pelach HaRimon, Vayikra, Bechukotai, PELACH line 1075: שהוא עושה כלי לברכת ה' היינו שחורש בשעת חרישה וזורע בשעת זריעה ("he makes a vessel for G-d's blessing: he plows in plowing time and sows in sowing time"). - Same line: שהוא בוטח בה' שישלח לו גם הכלי מלמעלה ("he trusts G-d to send him even the vessel from above"). - Hayom Yom, Sivan section, HY line 377: האדם עליו רק לעשות כלי לפרנסתו ("a person need only make a vessel for his livelihood"), and the vessel must be clean of any cheating, by the laws of the Torah. - Hayom Yom, Kislev section (from talks of the Rebbe Maharash, Elul 5635), HY line 29: איז די עצה, לכו נרננה, בטחון ("the advice is: Lechu neranenah, trust").

## Effort without worry works better

- **What it says:** The Rebbe writes to a man worried about livelihood: the effort must be made, "but not in the manner of worry; on the contrary, out of strong trust in G-d who feeds the whole world," and "then the doing is more active and more successful," and it even adds to bodily health and to how one meets what is around him. To new immigrants afraid they could not manage the language: "as we have seen plainly, the more the trust grows, the more the energy grows." To a woman asking about providence: G-d watches over each one "not only in general matters but in his particular ones," and so He is to be trusted as the essence of good; "the first result is the absence of worry and the absence of confusion," for even while you think it through and decide, "G-d is watching and helping." And when he prescribed rest by telegram: rest from worry is in fact commanded to everyone, since trust, "and the Besht's teaching of providence down to the finest details," leaves no room for worry at all.
- **What it adds:** It turns the classic worry, "if I trust I'll get lazy," on its head. The Rebbe says from experience that trust gives energy and worry drains it. Being held is what frees the hands to work.
- **The picture:** None given; the Rebbe points to what can be seen ("as we have seen plainly").
- **How the source says to realize it:** Do the effort, and do it from trust. Watch the result: more energy, better work, better health.
- **From inside:** Energy that is not anxious. Doing that comes from rest, not fear.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "If I stop worrying I'll stop trying." After: "Without the worry I actually have more in me to give."
- **Sources:** - Igros Kodesh vol. 18, 5 Tevet 5719, IK line 78551: אלא שלא באופן של דאגה, כ"א אדרבה מתוך חוזק הבטחון ("but not in the manner of worry; rather, out of strong trust"). In the file the quotation marks are HTML-encoded; the letters match. - Same line: שאז העשי' פעילה יותר ומצליחה יותר ("then the doing is more active and more successful"). - Igros Kodesh vol. 14, 14 Adar I 5717, IK line 62820: ככל שתתגדל מדת הבטחון גדל ג"כ המרץ ("the more trust grows, the more energy grows"). - Igros Kodesh vol. 18, 16 Sivan 5719, IK line 81799: העדר הדאגה, והעדר המבוכה ("the absence of worry and the absence of confusion"); and גם אז השם יתברך משגיח ועוזר ("even then G-d watches and helps"). - Igros Kodesh vol. 19, 17 MarCheshvan 5720, IK line 84027: וכידוע ביאור הבעש"ט בהשגחה פרטית ועד לפרטי פרטים ("as is known, the Besht's explanation of providence, down to the finest details").

## The bottom line: nothing but Him

- **What it says:** A great chassid of the Alter Rebbe, R. Binyamin Kletzker, a timber merchant, once made up the accounts of his business, and at the "total" he wrote: "Ein od milvado," there is nothing but Him. The Rebbe asks: if this was always engraved on his heart, why did he write it only on the last line, and fill the other lines with numbers and goods as usual? That is exactly the point. Anyone can feel "there is nothing but Him" while praying or learning, apart from the world. It is hard to feel it in the middle of business, which by nature hides Him. The chassid did his accounts precisely, as the Torah commands a person to earn a living, and because he was soaked through with this knowledge, it came out in the total: the sum and purpose of all his dealings is "nothing but Him." This, the Rebbe says, is what makes a Jew a "witness": to show, inside the world that hides Him, that everything exists only from His true existence.
- **What it adds:** It is the clearest Chabad picture of trust as calm action. You do not skip the details or neglect the work. You do every line exactly, and the bottom line is Him.
- **The picture:** The ledger with its totals. Where it breaks: nowhere, in a sense; that is why it is told. (Our note: the chassid's line is a story, not a method. The Rebbe draws the method from it.)
- **How the source says to realize it:** Be "soaked through" with the knowledge in quiet times, so that in busy times it surfaces at the "total." Our reading: at the end of a task or a day, ask what the total was, and write that.
- **From inside:** Doing everything carefully, then letting the result belong to Him.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "It's all on my spreadsheet, and it doesn't add up." After: "I'll get every number right. The bottom line isn't mine."
- **Sources:** - Likkutei Sichos vol. 34, Shoftim, sicha 2 (from Shabbat Shoftim 5725), LS line 15964: כתב: "אין עוד מלבדו" ("he wrote: 'there is nothing but Him'"). - LS line 15969: קשה לו להרגיש אז את הענין ד"אין עוד מלבדו" ("it is hard for him then to feel 'there is nothing but Him'"). - LS line 15970: שכתב כל פרטי חשבונותיו בדיוק ("that he wrote all the details of his accounts precisely").

## Hold someone, and you will be held

- **What it says:** R. Isaac of Homel told that when he came to Liozna he found old chassidim from the Maggid's time whose constant saying was: "Hold up a Jew, and G-d will hold you. Do a Jew a favor, and G-d will do you a favor. Bring a Jew close, and G-d will bring you close." Nearby in Hayom Yom, the Tzemach Tzedek: helping a Jew make a living, even a small coin's worth, opens all the gates of the heavenly palaces; and the Rebbe Maharash, retelling it years later to his son, added that what counts is helping with a whole heart and taking pleasure in doing a Jew a favor.
- **What it adds:** Being held is not only received. The old chassidim put it as a two-way thing: the way you hold others is the way you are held. It turns "rest, you are held" outward.
- **The picture:** Holding someone up (Yiddish "halten"). Where it breaks: G-d's holding does not wait for ours. The saying is about what we are shown, and our reading is that it is measure for measure, not a price.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Do it: hold up another person today, help with a whole heart.
- **From inside:** The sense of being held grows warmer when you are holding someone else.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I'm so busy trying to stay afloat I can't help anyone." After: "When I hold someone else up, I feel held too."
- **Sources:** - Hayom Yom, Cheshvan (the entry just before 28 Cheshvan), HY line 627: האב האלט א אידען, וועט השי"ת דיך האלט האבען ("hold up a Jew, and G-d will hold you"). - Hayom Yom, Sivan section, HY line 379 (the Rebbe Maharash's addition): העלפין בלבב שלם מיט א געפיל, האבען א געשמאק אין א אידען טאן א טובה ("to help with a whole heart, with feeling, to take pleasure in doing a Jew a favor").

## Standing tall in rough water

- **What it says:** The Frierdiker Rebbe reads "You gave those who fear You a banner (nes) to raise themselves." Trials (nisyonot) are for lifting up, like the mast (nes) of a ship that is seen from far away. "Even in the great waters of the sea that throw dread on those in the ship, just as the mast stands in trust and strength," so those who fear G-d stand strong in their service "from the greatness of their trust, which is by a power above nature." In another maamar he explains how a trial is met: a trial is not a thing in itself, only something set up to hide and block; the work is with oneself, to wake the essential bond of complete trust "until all obstacles are nullified," because "nature itself is above nature, only it is a hidden light."
- **What it adds:** It speaks to the moment when being held is hardest to feel: the storm. Trust is not the absence of waves. It is standing upright in them.
- **The picture:** A ship's mast in a stormy sea. Where it breaks: the mast is fixed to the ship. The person's steadiness is fixed higher, to "a power above nature."
- **How the source says to realize it:** Work with yourself, not with the obstacle: wake the bond of trust at your core.
- **From inside:** Fear still in the body, and a straight spine anyway.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "Everything is shaking. I can't hold steady." After: "The water is rough. I can still stand straight in it."
- **Sources:** - Sefer HaMaamarim (Frierdiker Rebbe), maamar for 12-13 Tammuz 5689, SM-RY line 1561: כשם שהנס של הספינה היא עומדת בבטחון וחוזק ("just as the ship's mast stands in trust and strength"). - Sefer HaMaamarim (Frierdiker Rebbe) 5692-5693, maamar "Natata liyerei'echa nes," SM-RY line 1051: היינו ההתקשרות העצמי בבטחון גמור עד שמתבטל כל המניעות ועיכובים ("the essential bond, in complete trust, until all obstacles and delays are nullified"). - Same line: כי האמת הוא דטבע עצמו הוא למעלה מן הטבע אלא שהוא אור מוסתר ונעלם ("for the truth is that nature itself is above nature, only it is a light hidden and concealed").

## Always before your eyes (the Rebbe Rashab's ways of return)

- **What it says:** The Rebbe Rashab explained the five letters of the word teshuvah (return) as five ways back. The shin is "I have set G-d (Havayah) before me always." The Name Havayah "points to the coming-into-being of the world and its creatures," which happens "from nothing to something" beyond all comparison, and this way of return comes from having "always before his eyes the being-made of the world and all in it." The bet is "Know Him in all your ways": one who sets his heart on everything that happens to him and around him "sees G-dliness palpably," as the Mitteler Rebbe said of the advantage businesspeople have over scholars, a return that comes from seeing individual providence. The first letter, tav, is "be whole (tamim) with G-d," which he calls earnestness of heart.
- **What it adds:** It makes seeing the world as held, and seeing providence in what happens, into ways of coming home (teshuvah), open to someone who is busy in the world, maybe more than to the scholar.
- **The picture:** The Name kept in front of the eyes. Where it breaks: a picture on the wall can be ignored. Here what is kept in view is the very making of the view.
- **How the source says to realize it:** "Always before his eyes": a steady keeping in view, and setting the heart on "all that happens to him and around him."
- **From inside:** The day becomes a string of small signs, and each one turns you a little toward home.
- **In a chat, before and after:** Before: "I'm too busy with work to be spiritual." After: "My workday is where I see Him holding things. That's my way back."
- **Sources:** - Hayom Yom, Tishrei section (the Rebbe Rashab's explanation, as the Frierdiker Rebbe summarized it), HY line 537: אשר תמיד לנגד עיניו ענין ההוי' של העולם ומלואו ("that the being-made of the world and all in it is always before his eyes"). - HY line 539: רואה הוא אלקות במוחש ("he sees G-dliness palpably").

## Our synthesis: one path through the angles (ours, not the sources')

The Rebbes do not give a single program for "being held." Putting their own instructions in order, this is how the Chabad angles line up as a path. The order and the grouping are our reading; each step uses only practices the sources give.

1. **Understand the fact.** Continuous creation: the word still said, not the silversmith's cup, the wall of water (Shaar HaYichud 1-2). The Tanya insists on understanding first (Iggeret HaKodesh 11: "in the depth of his understanding").
2. **Know why it does not feel that way.** Sunlight in the sun; the hiding is kindness (Shaar HaYichud 3-6). This takes away the argument "I don't feel it, so it isn't true."
3. **Take time daily.** A long contemplation, "a great hour every day," as far as the mind and the free time allow; then short returns at any hour (Tanya 41-42). Aim to move from hearing to seeing (Torah Or, Mishpatim).
4. **Start the day by handing yourself back.** "My G-d, the soul": from now I return it to You (Tanya 41).
5. **Meet the fears directly.** Picture yourself being made from nothing this moment, from a source that is only good (Iggeret HaKodesh 11); worry is forgetting and sleep (the Rebbe, the Frierdiker Rebbe); take your mind off the sore spot on purpose (the Tzemach Tzedek); choose trust as your own work (think good).
6. **Act, calmly.** Make the vessel and do not lean on it (the Rashab, Paritch); effort from trust works better (the Rebbe); write every line of the ledger carefully and let the total be Him (Likkutei Sichos 34).
7. **Read the day.** Providence down to the leaf and the way it fell; ask what it is here to teach; you are placed where something has waited for you (Hayom Yom, Sefer HaSichos, Toras Menachem).
8. **Turn outward.** Hold someone up, and be held (the old chassidim). Even the one who hurts you is held, and does not rule you (Iggeret HaKodesh 25).
9. **The sign.** Joy: the king has moved into your house (Tanya 33); yes and no feel equal (Iggeret HaKodesh 11). In the Mitteler Rebbe's words, "as much joy, exactly that much self-nullification" (the Gate of Unity, GOU line 215: ולפי ערך השמחה כך ערך הביטול ממש).
