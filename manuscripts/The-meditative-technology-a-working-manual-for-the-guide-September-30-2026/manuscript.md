## 0. What was missing

Read against the brief, the files we had give the ingredients well and the mixing poorly. The gaps, in order of weight:

1. **No single account of the mechanism.** ONE-PROCESS has da'at as binding; SCIENCE has loosen, install, consolidate, gate; the Kuntres files have makif and pnimi and "da'at is feeling". Nothing ties them into one page. *Filled in §1.*
2. **Techniques described, not specified.** CATALOGUE gives each technique's method, mechanism and cautions. It does not say who it is for, when not to use it, the dose, the exact steps, what the guide says, what to do if nothing happens, or how it grows. *Filled in §2 for 25 techniques.*
3. **Core Chabad moves never treated as techniques.** Choosing the line is a paragraph in the manual. Breadth, length and depth are lumped into one stage. Walking a truth down the worlds to one object exists only as ONE-PROCESS content and a proposal in ABULAFIA §6.3. Detail by detail exists only as a warning against the general. Stopping at the edge, the quiet after, and the two kinds of love are described as states, not as things to do. *Filled in §2 (choose_line, breadth, length, depth, walk_down, details, edge, hold, heart_after).*
4. **Everything assumed a solitary sitting.** Nothing said how a technique runs inside a text chat: what replaces closed eyes, how a minute of silence works when the next thing is a message, what the person types, what the guide never asks. *Filled in §2 (guide lines, what the person does) and §4.*
5. **The eight steps had one instruction each and no techniques.** The engine's WAY list says what each step does. It does not say which techniques live there, what to do for a scattered, cold, low or already close person, or how the second round goes deeper. *Filled in §4 and `step_map`.*
6. **Progression stopped at day 40.** Part III ends at day 40; DARKEI has durations but not what changes first, what months and years look like, or when to go deeper and when to go back. *Filled in §3.*
7. **The three garments were implicit.** Thought, speech and deed are the Tanya's own order, and they are the natural order of a round, but no file said so. *Stated in §1 and used in §4.*
8. **Review was not tied to the repeated line.** Chabad chazarah (going over a line again, knowing it by heart) and the Rayatz's "absorbed by lingering" were not connected to the manual's "same line for a week". *Filled in §2 (chazarah, carry).*
9. **Abulafia was known and excluded, but nothing positive was taken.** His exit, "leave it for the week", "if your heart runs, come back", and the companion rule were noted in ABULAFIA §6.4 but never placed in the guide. *Filled in §2 (letters) and §3.*
10. **Still open, not filled here.** The Rashab's order of prayer as a full method (Kuntres HaTefillah §10 to 11) and one-trait work in prayer (DARKEI step 6) are pointed to, not specified. Hiskashrus is not treated. Kaplan's own books are lending-restricted and were not read. Nothing here has been tested on people; the step map is a design, to be judged in live conversations.

## 1. The mechanism in one page

How does a truth that is understood become a truth that is lived? The sources give their own answer. The six moves are theirs; putting them in this order is ours.

**1. Knowing is binding.** Da'at is not information. It is tying the mind to a truth "with a strong, very firm bond", until it is as real as a thing seen with the eyes. When a mind grasps an idea, the Tanya calls it a union "like no other": the knower and the known become one. So to know the unity is to hold it. Without the binding, even a wise person gets "only empty imaginings".

> <small>**Sources.** «שמקשר דעתו בקשר אמיץ וחזק מאד» (*he binds his mind with a strong, very firm bond*) · Tanya, line 112<br>
> «כמו שהיא מקושרת בדבר גשמי שרואה בעיני בשר ומעמיק בו מחשבתו» (*as bound as it is to a physical thing he sees with his eyes and thinks deeply into*) · Tanya, line 629<br>
> «והוא יחוד נפלא שאין יחוד כמוהו» (*and this is a wondrous union, like no other union*) · Tanya, line 134<br>
> «לא יוליד בנפשו יראה ואהבה אמיתית, כי אם דמיונות שוא» (*without that binding he will not give birth to true awe and love, only to empty imaginings*) · Tanya, line 113</small>

**2. The binding has three dimensions, and it is slow.** Breadth (rochav): the idea to every side, in many details. Length (orech): brought down until a small child could grasp it, and into one's own life. Depth (omek): the idea's own point, reached only after the other two. All three are "the pausing, the opposite of speed".

> <small>**Sources.** «הרוחב הוא בחינת הסבר המושכל לכל צד בפרטים הרבה כרוחב הנהר» (*breadth is explaining the idea to every side, in many details, wide as a river*) · Gate of Unity (Sha'ar HaYichud), Mitteler Rebbe, line 17<br>
> «ואורך הוא עוצם ירידות ההשכלה להלביש את המושכל במשלים שונים עד להביאו בהשגות התינוק קטן» (*length is bringing the idea far down, clothing it in parables, until even a small child could grasp it*) · Gate of Unity (Sha'ar HaYichud), Mitteler Rebbe, line 18<br>
> «כך עומק המושכל הוא בחינת עצם נקודתו כמו שהוא» (*so the depth of an idea is its very point, as it is*) · Gate of Unity (Sha'ar HaYichud), Mitteler Rebbe, line 21<br>
> «ודווקא אחר האריכות והרוחב במשלים בעיון רב יבא לבחינת העומק» (*only after length and breadth, in parables and much looking, does he come to depth*) · Gate of Unity (Sha'ar HaYichud), Mitteler Rebbe, line 101<br>
> «שעומד על דבר המושכל ומעיין בו הרבה מאד, שהוא העיכוב היפך המהירות» (*he stands on the idea and looks into it very much: the pausing, the opposite of speed*) · Gate of Unity (Sha'ar HaYichud), Mitteler Rebbe, line 24</small>

**3. General hovers; detail enters.** A truth known in general stays makif, "surrounding": it hovers over the mind, and any excitement it gives is "a false imagination". Worked in detail it becomes pnimi, "inward": the ideas become "a passage", and what is felt through them is the Godly light.

> <small>**Sources.** «ה"ז בבחי' מקיף לבד ואין זה התפעלות אמיתי כי אם דמיון כוזב» (*that is only surrounding (makif); it is not true arousal but a false imagination*) · Kuntres HaAvodah, Rebbe Rashab (OCR), line 204<br>
> «אבל הלבושים הן כעין מעבר והעיקר נרגש אצלו האור האלקי» (*the garments are like a passage, and what he mainly feels is the Godly light*) · Kuntres HaTefillah, Rebbe Rashab (OCR), line 66</small>

**4. The mind rules the heart, by nature.** This is the ground of the whole method. What the mind truly holds, the heart in time answers, "as water gives back a face". But da'at is also feeling: a person can understand a thing well and not feel it, so "the main thing is the returning to the heart". The mind is in a person's hands and the heart is not, which is why the practice asks for the thinking and never demands the feeling.

> <small>**Sources.** «כי המוח שליט על הלב» (*for the mind rules the heart*) · Tanya, line 209<br>
> «בתולדתו וטבע יצירתו» (*by birth, by the nature of how he was made*) · Tanya, line 209<br>
> «וההרגשה זהו"ע הדעת, כי יכול להיות שמבין את הדבר היטב ומ"מ אינו במורגש אצלו» (*the feeling is what da'at is: a person can understand a thing well and still not feel it*) · Kuntres HaTefillah, Rebbe Rashab (OCR), line 61<br>
> «דהעיקר הוא ההשבה אל הלב» (*the main thing is the returning to the heart*) · Kuntres HaTefillah, Rebbe Rashab (OCR), line 62<br>
> «כי מוחו ברשותו ויכול להתבונן בו ככל אשר יחפוץ» (*his mind is in his own hands; he can contemplate with it whatever he wants*) · Tanya, line 265</small>

**5. Learned first, then brought into prayer.** The contemplation is prepared before prayer and then carried into it, where "the mind's grasp" is brought "into the heart's feeling, and both together into deed". Learning alone is only the preparation.

> <small>**Sources.** «טוב לחשוב קודם התפלה איזה ענין בדא"ח כחצי שעה או יותר» (*it is good before prayer to think over a subject in Chassidus for half an hour or more*) · Kuntres HaTefillah, Rebbe Rashab (OCR), line 88<br>
> «כי כל הלימוד והידיעת הקודמת היא כהכנה אל העיקר» (*all the learning and knowing before is a preparation for the main thing*) · Kuntres HaTefillah, Rebbe Rashab (OCR), line 49<br>
> «עבודת התפלה היא המביאה השגת המוח בהרגשת הלב, ושניהם יחד בעבודה בפועל» (*prayer brings the mind's grasp into the heart's feeling, and both together into deed*) · Hayom Yom, line 618</small>

**6. Clothed in three garments, then made habit.** The soul acts through three garments: thought, speech and deed. Thought holds the truth (the binding). Speech in words of Torah, and one's own words to Him, are its vessel. The deed is where it lasts; arousal that never reaches one is "a flame flying in the air". Then habit, and "the habit becomes nature".

> <small>**Sources.** «שלשה לבושים, שהם מחשבה דבור ומעשה» (*three garments, which are thought, speech and deed*) · Tanya, line 115<br>
> «והנה בחי׳ הכלי לאור דרצוא ושוב הוא הדבור בד״ת דוקא» (*the vessel for the light of running and returning is speech in words of Torah*) · Likkutei Torah, Alter Rebbe, line 119<br>
> «הרי זה כשלהבת הפורח באויר שאין לו קיום» (*it is like a flame flying in the air, which has no endurance*) · Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, Mitteler Rebbe, line 778<br>
> «וכשירגיל עצמו כן תמיד הרי ההרגל נעשה טבע» (*when he trains himself this way constantly, the habit becomes nature*) · Tanya, line 664<br>
> «שיאריך בההתבוננות ויתבונן כמה פעמים באריכות, ואז יוקלט הענין אצלו בכי טוב» (*let him dwell at length and contemplate several times at length; then it will be well absorbed*) · Sefer HaMaamarim, Rebbe Rayatz, line 435</small>

**The bridge to the science (ours).** SCIENCE.md reads Jewish meditation as four phases: loosen the habitual self-talk, install one high-level model of reality and the self, consolidate it from state into trait, and gate the dose throughout. The sources' moves sit on those phases like this. This is a bridge for the builders, not a proof, and nothing reaches the person in these words.

| The sources' move | Phase | What the phase is (SCIENCE §3.2) | Techniques in §2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start from what moves; the threshold; quieting; melody; the edge where the mind stops | Loosen | the habitual stream and self-model held less tightly | arrive, quieting, niggun, edge (letters: kept out) |
| Binding; breadth, length, depth; detail; walking down to one thing; the picture; one's own words; love and awe | Install | a new model built and made emotionally real by elaboration, imagery, relationship | choose_line, breadth, length, walk_down, details, depth, mashal, picturing, binding, own_words, heart_after |
| Speech; the deed; the line carried; the same line again; the evening look-back; the garment look | Consolidate | state becomes trait by repetition, cues and deeds | lips, act, carry, chazarah, cheshbon, garment_look, hold |
| Never force; test later, not now; low days; a device used daily stops working; stop-signs | Gate | arousal and loosening kept within what the person can hold | before_whom, discern, and the "not when" of every technique |

Three things follow. **Chabad mostly installs.** Its loosening is mild: it does not empty the mind but fills it with one meaning so large that the self it held gives way (ABULAFIA §6.1). **The closest scientific fit is elaboration.** "I knew this before too, but I only knew it" is the gap between knowing that and feeling that (SCIENCE §2.4); breadth, length and depth are a precise description of elaboration. **The weakest link is order.** That a little loosening before installing helps is theory, never tested on prayer (SCIENCE §3.4). So the guide keeps loosening short and optional.

> <small>**Sources.** «הן גם מאז זאת ידעתי, אבל רק ידעתי» (*I knew this before too, but I only knew it*) · Tzav VeZeruz, Piaseczner, line 72</small>

**What this means inside a conversation (ours).** A reply that speaks of "the universe" is makif; a reply about their cup is pnimi. A reply that explains is thought; asking them to say it to Him is speech; one act today is deed. Every round should pass through all three garments, and every reply should be as particular as the person's own day.

### 1. Start from what is already moving · מפתח לנפש (mafte'ach lanefesh, "a key to the soul") *id `arrive` · phase: gate · lives in: meet*

- **For.** Finding the door in what the person already brings, so nothing has to be made up. Any stirring at all, even a worry about money or a bodily want, is a key to the soul.
- **When.** The first reply of every round. Any time the person brings a mood, a worry, a want or a small joy. Also when they say 'not much'.
- **Not when.** In danger: the crisis protocol, nothing else. Do not start from the soul's pains when a clearer or gladder feeling is also there. Never make a feared body symptom the thing to look at (D11).
- **Dose.** One sentence from them. One reply from the guide.
- **Steps.** 1. Take what they said as the door. Do not ask for a better or holier feeling. 2. If a clearer or gladder feeling sits beside the pain, start from that one. 3. If nothing seems to be moving, borrow a love they already have (a child, a friend, even their work) and point it one step further. 4. Leave one small real thing from their own day to look at or answer. Never an interview.
- **What the person does.** Says where they are, in their own words, in a sentence or two.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "That worry is awake in you right now. Good. We start there, not somewhere holier." - "You said you love watching your son sleep. Hold on to that. It points further than him." - "Flat is a real place to start. Your heart kept going all night without your help."
- **Signs it is working.** They answer from their own life, not in general. A sigh or a small joy gets named plainly. The Piaseczner: any stirring is a key.
- **The counterfeit.** Waiting for a holy feeling before starting. Or the opposite: the trouble becomes the whole subject and the round never turns.
- **If nothing happens.** 'Not much' is an answer. Give them one small real thing to look at, so they have something to answer.
- **Over days and weeks.** Early on the door is a mood. After some weeks the person often arrives already carrying yesterday's line, and the door is the line itself.
- **For anyone.** The same for everyone.

### 2. The threshold: before Whom · לפני מי (lifnei Mi, "before Whom") *id `before_whom` · phase: gate · lives in: turn*

- **For.** Changing the frame before the content. The same words said 'before Him' are a different act. Awe is the gate.
- **When.** The turn step. The start of any sitting, on waking, before a hard call or meeting.
- **Not when.** If the person fears God as a judge or feels watched and condemned, do not use the King who sees; use the Throne as a child before a father (picturing). For someone unsure, say it as a fact they can check: they did not make this moment.
- **Dose.** One sentence. Seconds.
- **Steps.** 1. Stop at the door of what comes next. 2. Say in the heart before Whom you stand: the One whose word is making all this right now. 3. If awe does not come at once, go a little deeper into the same thought until it reaches you. Then begin. 4. If it still does not come, begin anyway. The frame counts.
- **What the person does.** Pauses for a few seconds before answering or before starting.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Before you answer me, one second. Whatever you do next, you do it in front of the One holding you up." - "Stop at the door of this. You didn't make this moment. Someone is making it, now."
- **Signs it is working.** A small sobering and straightening, like walking into a king's room. Speech slows down.
- **The counterfeit.** Awe used as a threat. Or a show of reverence.
- **If nothing happens.** The Alter Rebbe: go deep into it until it reaches you. If it still does not, the plain act of stopping is enough for today.
- **Over days and weeks.** First at set doors (waking, before prayer, before a sitting). Later, the Besht's aim: 'I have set God before me always' kept even going about one's business.
- **For anyone.** 'I have set God before me always' (Psalm 16:8), or 'I did not make this moment'.

### 3. Choosing the one line · ענין אחד (inyan echad, "one matter") *id `choose_line` · phase: install · lives in: turn, line*

- **For.** Giving the whole round one object. The Mitteler Rebbe calls knowing 'the gathering of all the power of his mind' into one idea.
- **When.** The guide does it silently before the line step. A person sitting alone does it before the sitting.
- **Not when.** No line of bitul ('not a thing on my own') on a low day or to someone ashamed. No line about the Essence to a beginner: the Rashab says the main service now is in the lower matters (creation, filling and surrounding).
- **Dose.** One sentence of about six to twelve words (ours). Kept for a week (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. Take it from something learned, not made up on the spot. Before prayer the point is to feel the life of what you learned. 2. Choose it for what the person needs now, using the rung table (ONE-PROCESS §4): worry gets 'being said now', fear gets 'held from inside and all around', 'I know but don't feel' gets 'know it today'. 3. Prefer the lower rungs the mind can check before the rungs above the mind. 4. Say it in plain words with its reason, at full depth (D13). 5. Do not switch in the middle. A better idea arriving halfway is the pull away, however lofty. 6. Keep it for days. Tomorrow goes one step deeper into the same line.
- **What the person does.** Receives one sentence and says it back in their own words, if they like.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Here's the one thing for today. The word that makes this table is saying it right now." - "One sentence, not a lesson. You're being given this moment. You're not holding it up." - "Stay with that one. A better idea will turn up soon. Let it go past."
- **Signs it is working.** The person repeats it in their own words. It fits what they brought at the start.
- **The counterfeit.** Collecting insights. Choosing the loftiest line to feel deep. The Rashab: reaching for the highest matters with the mind only coarsens them.
- **If nothing happens.** If a line stays dry for several days, change the rung, not the effort (manual Part III).
- **Over days and weeks.** First weeks: the lower rungs. After forty days: the rung the log shows is most alive. Over years: a few lines, each held deeper (the Rebbe's fold, ONE-PROCESS §3).
- **For anyone.** The lines are true of everyone. For fear of a person or a power, Nefesh HaChayim's line serves any faith: there is no other power besides Him.

### 4. Breadth · רוחב (rochav, "width") *id `breadth` · phase: install · lives in: line, look*

- **For.** Giving the line room. Many sides and many details, 'wide as a river', so it stops being a slogan.
- **When.** The line and look steps. The first minutes of thinking it through.
- **Not when.** With a scattered mind keep it to two or three things. Simple and broad, never clever.
- **Dose.** Three to six things, a few seconds each (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. Say the line one way, then another. 2. Go around the room one thing at a time. Each thing gets the line. 3. At each return add one thing you did not say before (manual Part III). 4. Go slowly. Looking is 'the pausing, the opposite of speed'.
- **What the person does.** Looks at a few real things in turn and thinks the line over each one.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Say it over the table. Then the window. Then your own hand. Same line, a little slower each time." - "The kettle, the chair, the light switch. Each one being said right now."
- **Signs it is working.** The line starts to widen. The Piaseczner: repeat it only while it adds and widens. The room seems to fill with it.
- **The counterfeit.** A list said fast. Reading through instead of looking.
- **If nothing happens.** Fewer things, slower. Or move to length: bring it into their own worry.
- **Over days and weeks.** From things in the room, to people, to the day's events, to oneself.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 5. Length · אורך (orech, "bringing it down") *id `length` · phase: install · lives in: line, look*

- **For.** Bringing the idea down 'until a small child could grasp it', and into the person's own life. The Tanya narrows the same way: His kingship over His people in general, and over him in particular.
- **When.** The look step, after breadth.
- **Not when.** Never bring a line down into someone's loss to explain it. Never use the hiding to account for grief.
- **Dose.** One or two sentences. Ten to thirty seconds of thinking (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. Clothe the line in one plain picture from their world. 2. Bring it into this room. 3. Bring it into the thing they named at the start: the meeting, the rent, the child. 4. Say it in the particular: not 'everything is made each moment' but 'Thursday's meeting is being given being now, and so am I' (manual).
- **What the person does.** Thinks the line into their own situation, in the first person.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Bring it to Thursday. The meeting, the people in it, you walking in. All being said right now." - "Not the world in general. Your rent, this month. Held by the same word as the stars."
- **Signs it is working.** They say 'me' and name their own thing. It comes close; it stops hovering.
- **The counterfeit.** It stays 'the universe'. A general excitement the Rashab calls a false imagination: it only surrounds.
- **If nothing happens.** Go smaller: the one object in front of them.
- **Over days and weeks.** In time the person can bring any line into any trouble in one sentence, without help.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 6. Walking the truth down to one thing · השתלשלות (hishtalshelus, "the chain coming down") *id `walk_down` · phase: install · lives in: look*

- **For.** Seeing one plain thing as the last link of a chain that comes down from His word. The Tanya's own example is a stone: its name is drawn down from the Ten Utterances, and that name is its life.
- **When.** The look step, for lines about creation and the lower unity. Good for worry and for 'the world feels heavy'.
- **Not when.** Not with someone who feels unreal or far away from their own life: it can feed 'nothing is real'. With them stress that the thing is real and is given. Not a cosmology lecture.
- **Dose.** Three or four links, then five to thirty seconds on the object (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. Start at the top in one plain sentence: His word. 2. Walk it down in a few links, not a lecture: the word, its letters, the name of this thing, the thing. 3. Land on one object within reach, and say its name. 4. Say what would happen if the saying stopped: not falling, just not being. 5. Look at it for a few seconds as being said now. 6. Stop there. Do not climb back up with more theory.
- **What the person does.** Looks at or touches one object and thinks of it as being said right now.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Look at the stone on your windowsill. The word that made light comes down, link by link, into that one name." - "If the saying stopped, the cup wouldn't fall. It just wouldn't be. It's being said now." - "Put your hand flat on the table. Under your palm is the last link of a very long chain."
- **Signs it is working.** 'For a second it is not an idea but so' (manual Part IV). The object looks thinner, like a garment with Someone inside.
- **The counterfeit.** 'Nothing is real, so nothing matters.' The world is real and really given. Or theory about worlds with no object at the end.
- **If nothing happens.** Shorten the chain to two links, the word and the thing. Or switch to the garment look.
- **Over days and weeks.** Day one: one object. Weeks: objects met through the day. Months: people and events. For a Jew, the same walk runs down the four directions in the Shema's 'One'.
- **For anyone.** Use the name in their own language. The Hebrew name of the thing is the Tanya's version and can be offered as something to look at, never as a formula (ABULAFIA §6.3).

### 7. Detail by detail · התבוננות פרטית (hisbonenus pratis, "contemplation in detail") *id `details` · phase: install · lives in: look, stay*

- **For.** Moving the idea from outside to inside. A truth known only in general stays makif, surrounding, hovering over the mind. A detail lets it in, pnimi. The Rashab: the ideas become a passage, and what is felt is the Godly light.
- **When.** Whenever a reply has gone general. The look and stay steps.
- **Not when.** Do not turn it into cleverness.
- **Dose.** One thing, three or four details, slowly (ours). The Rashab's long form is an hour or more.
- **Steps.** 1. Pick one thing. 2. Ask how, exactly, the power is seen in it: its life, its kind, its use, its warmth. 3. Go slowly. Each return to the line adds one new detail. 4. If it goes flat just as the details start, stay. That is where the work starts (manual Part IV).
- **What the person does.** Holds one thing and goes through its particulars with the line.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Just the cup. Its weight, the warmth, the chip on the rim. Each of those is being given." - "Stay on your daughter's voice on the phone. Not people in general. That one voice, being said."
- **Signs it is working.** Specifics show up in what they write back. It goes flat, then catches.
- **The counterfeit.** A warm general glow. 'Everything is God' said wide. The Mitteler Rebbe: in general, God appears to him from very far off.
- **If nothing happens.** Fewer, smaller details. Eyes open on the actual thing.
- **Over days and weeks.** From things, to people, to one's own particular trait, which the Rashab works on many times over.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 8. Depth · עומק (omek, "the point itself") *id `depth` · phase: install · lives in: stay*

- **For.** Reaching the idea's own point, 'as it is', after the pictures. Said in the fewest words, then held still.
- **When.** The end of the stay step, going into inside.
- **Not when.** Never first. The Mitteler Rebbe: only after length and breadth does depth come. Not for a scattered mind.
- **Dose.** Three to six words. Twenty seconds to a minute of stillness (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. After breadth and length, drop the examples. 2. Say the point in three to six words. 3. Stay with it without explaining.
- **What the person does.** Says the short form once and stays with it before writing back.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Drop the examples now. Just this. Being said, now. Stay there a minute before you write." - "Say it shorter. Held. Nothing else for a minute."
- **Signs it is working.** Fewer words. It is so, the way a thing seen is so.
- **The counterfeit.** Deep-sounding words. Talking about depth instead of staying.
- **If nothing happens.** Go back to one detail. Depth only comes after length.
- **Over days and weeks.** The short form arrives faster, and the stillness gets longer.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 9. The picture, and where it breaks · משל ונמשל (mashal venimshal, "the picture and the thing") *id `mashal` · phase: install · lives in: line*

- **For.** A picture to carry the idea, and saying where it breaks, so God is never pictured with a body. The Alter Rebbe breaks his own: a parable 'only settles the ear'.
- **When.** The line or look step. One picture, two at most.
- **Not when.** Once a feeling is there, stop looking for pictures. Never picture God as a soul in a body.
- **Dose.** One picture, one sentence where it breaks.
- **Steps.** 1. Give one picture, from their world if possible. 2. Let it land. 3. Say where it breaks, every time. 4. Drop it when the line holds without it.
- **What the person does.** Takes the picture, then lets it go.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Think of your own words. Once spoken, they leave you. His never leave Him. That's where the picture stops." - "A ray inside the sun has no life of its own. That's the picture. He isn't a sun."
- **Signs it is working.** The picture helps once and then is not needed.
- **The counterfeit.** The picture taken as the thing. Hunting new pictures after the feeling has come.
- **If nothing happens.** A picture from their own trade or family. Or skip to details.
- **Over days and weeks.** Fewer pictures, faster breaks, and in time none.
- **For anyone.** The same. The guide marks its own pictures as its own (D08b).

### 10. Picturing, the Piaseczner's way · ציור (tziyur, "picturing") *id `picturing` · phase: install · lives in: look*

- **For.** Moving the traits when thought alone stays in the head. The Piaseczner: strong thought is impossible without pictures, and pure thought without imagination cannot wake the traits.
- **When.** The line has stayed dry for days. The person thinks in pictures. The speak step (the Throne) or the look step (the sky, a holy scene).
- **Not when.** Never a picture of God's form, of light entering the body, or of one's own death (the Lizhensk fire is not offered by the guide). No visions sought. Careful with people who report unusual experiences (SCIENCE §5.1). Never strain: the harder you try, the more the image leaves.
- **Dose.** One picture, under a minute (ours). Not every day.
- **Steps.** 1. Choose one: standing before the Throne asking simply, like a son before his father. Or a holy scene 'as if you were there'. Or the sky and what is past it. Or, when nothing moves, yourself as a tzaddik with your soul in its root. 2. Keep it simple and broad. 3. If the holy picture will not come, come at it sideways: start from an ordinary memory you do not care about and let the holy thing appear inside it. 4. Once a feeling comes, stop hunting pictures and stay in the feeling. 5. Let the picture fall away by itself.
- **What the person does.** Holds one simple picture for a short while, then lets it go.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Picture yourself standing before Him like a kid asking his father. Simple. Ask for what you need." - "Look at the sky out your window. Think past it, and past that. The same One is holding your street." - "Don't strain for it. Start from an ordinary memory and let Him be in the room of it."
- **Signs it is working.** Warmth, nearness, a child's plea. The picture falls away of itself.
- **The counterfeit.** Seeking lights or presences. A picture that makes the person feel special.
- **If nothing happens.** Switch to the lips or to their own words.
- **Over days and weeks.** The Piaseczner: once thought is strong, the bodily picture falls away. Over months the handrail is needed less.
- **For anyone.** The Throne works for any faith that speaks to God. For someone unsure, the sky.

### 11. Seeing the garment · הסתכלות (histaklus, "looking through") *id `garment_look` · phase: consolidate · lives in: turn, look*

- **For.** Training the eyes so that whatever they see, heaven and earth and everything in them, is the King's outer clothing, and so the life inside is remembered. Eyes open.
- **When.** The look step, and through the day.
- **Not when.** Not an excuse to stare at what one should not look at. With someone who feels unreal, keep the thing solid and given.
- **Dose.** Seconds, many times a day.
- **Steps.** 1. Eyes open on a real thing. 2. Say to yourself: clothing, with Someone inside. 3. Let the thing stay, but thin. 4. Do it again later. The Tanya's word for it is habit, like a craftsman training his hands.
- **What the person does.** Looks at the world for a moment as a garment.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Look out the window for a minute. Trees, cars, people. All of it clothing, with Someone inside." - "The face across the table is a garment too. Look at it that way once today."
- **Signs it is working.** The world stays, but looks thin. A pull toward a thing turns into longing for its root.
- **The counterfeit.** Cold detachment: 'the world doesn't count'.
- **If nothing happens.** One object only. Or walk the truth down to it.
- **Over days and weeks.** The Tanya: habit, until it becomes the mind's default.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 12. Returning the wandering mind · קשר הדעת (kesher hada'as, "tying the mind") *id `binding` · phase: install · lives in: stay*

- **For.** This is da'at itself: holding the mind on the line and bringing it back, 'with a strong, very firm bond'. Without it, the Tanya says, even understanding gives only empty imaginings.
- **When.** The stay step, and all through any sitting.
- **Not when.** Never fight thoughts, never scold. Never sadness over wandering.
- **Dose.** As many returns as it takes. A minute of staying is a real start (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. When you catch the mind gone, come back to the line by way of the one thing. 2. Do not answer the stray thought. Do not ask why it came. Act as if you did not hear it, and add a little strength. 3. Do not be sad about it. Wandering is not failure. 4. If one thought keeps coming back, look at it from the side for twenty seconds ('so this is what I'm thinking'), then return. A thought looked at melts.
- **What the person does.** Stays a minute with the line and the one thing, coming back when they drift.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Give it a minute with the cup and the line. When your mind wanders, come back through the cup. No scolding." - "One thought keeps pulling? Twenty seconds on it. 'So that's what I'm thinking.' Then back to the cup."
- **Signs it is working.** Returns get quicker. The line is there when you come back.
- **The counterfeit.** Arguing with thoughts. Being sad over wandering. Analyzing why.
- **If nothing happens.** Shorten it. Say the line aloud. Or quiet the stream first.
- **Over days and weeks.** Many returns in a minute at first. Longer holds over weeks. The Rashab's hour is a long road, not a start.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 13. Quieting the stream (a door only) · השקטה (hashkatah, "quieting") *id `quieting` · phase: loosen · lives in: turn, stay*

- **For.** A short door for a racing head, so the line has somewhere to land. The Piaseczner: a person's own busy self-ness is what blocks what comes from above.
- **When.** Only when the person says their head is racing, before the line.
- **Not when.** Never as the goal and never for long. Not with anyone who feels unreal or far from their body. No breath, no body, no relaxing. The guide does not say 'notice'.
- **Dose.** One or two minutes, then the line (manual). Practised for some weeks, then often not needed.
- **Steps.** 1. Look at your thoughts for a short while with one question: what am I thinking now? 2. With a restful heart, not agitated, not straining. 3. Little by little the head empties and the flood stops. 4. Then say one verse or the line, softly, never forcefully. 5. Say it as a yes, not a no: 'I want to be close to You', not 'I must stop drifting'.
- **What the person does.** Asks 'what am I thinking now?' for a minute, then reads the line.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Your head's loud. For one minute just ask, what am I thinking now? Don't chase the answer. Then read the line." - "Say it softly, not hard. 'I want to be close to You.'"
- **Signs it is working.** The head feels roomier; the usual flood stops.
- **The counterfeit.** Staying blank. Chasing emptiness. Forcing.
- **If nothing happens.** Skip it. Mouth first: say the line aloud and let the thinking follow.
- **Over days and weeks.** Shorter over weeks. Many people never need it.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 14. Stopping at the edge of understanding · ביטול השכל · השגת השלילה (bitul hasechel · hasagas hashlilah, "the mind giving way · knowing by 'not this'") *id `edge` · phase: loosen · lives in: inside*

- **For.** Saying plainly where the line stops being graspable, and staying a moment there, so the round ends open, not as a slogan. The Rashab's order: grasp by negation, then wonder, then recognition. Even that sees Him only from behind; its use is the thirst it leaves.
- **When.** The inside step, after depth. When the person reaches for 'there must be more'.
- **Not when.** Not to someone who feels unreal or spacey. Not as a trick for mystery. Not before any rung has actually been held (ours).
- **Dose.** Ten to thirty seconds (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. After the line has been held, say where it stops: how something comes from nothing no human mind truly grasps. 2. Go by 'not this': He is not the cup, not the sky, not the light. 3. Say 'I don't know how' plainly. 4. Stay a moment with not knowing. Faith carries past the mind. 5. Then come down: a plain act, a word to Him.
- **What the person does.** Stays a few seconds where the thinking runs out.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Here the thinking runs out. How it's being said, no mind holds. Stay at that edge a moment." - "Not the cup, not the sky, not the light. Past that you don't know. That's all right."
- **Signs it is working.** A hush. Wonder. A thirst. Afterwards, less full of oneself.
- **The counterfeit.** Thinking you reached the top. A spacey, unreal feeling: stop and do something plain with your hands (ONE-PROCESS step 9).
- **If nothing happens.** Nothing to grasp is the edge. Go to the lips.
- **Over days and weeks.** Over years the edge is reached sooner and held longer. Where knowing ends, faith goes on. The Rashab: the end of knowing is that we do not know.
- **For anyone.** For someone unsure, say plainly: this is where reasoning stops, and past it is an invitation (claims F44).

### 15. The quiet after · התעכבות (hisakvus, "lingering") *id `hold` · phase: consolidate · lives in: inside*

- **For.** Letting what opened stay, without checking it. The Rayatz: the grasp is absorbed by lingering. The Piaseczner: this time of stopping is also service.
- **When.** Right after the heart answers, or after the edge. The inside step.
- **Not when.** Not blankness for its own sake. Not a time to examine the experience.
- **Dose.** About a minute (ours). The Piaseczner's long form is an hour after prayer for what rises by itself.
- **Steps.** 1. Once a feeling is there, stop looking for images and pictures. Linger in it. 2. Do not ask what it is or whether it is real. Examining it spoils it. 3. Let it be the pause before the next thing. 4. Then go on to speech.
- **What the person does.** Waits a minute before writing anything back.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Leave it there. Don't look at it or name it. A minute, then write whatever you like." - "No need to do anything with that. Staying is part of it."
- **Signs it is working.** Felt, and yet not felt as one's own. The deeper, the less it is felt.
- **The counterfeit.** Analyzing ('am I in it now?'). Stretching it out for the high.
- **If nothing happens.** Nothing is needed. The thinking already counted.
- **Over days and weeks.** The hold grows from seconds to minutes. Later it becomes the stillness at the end of every prayer.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 16. The path of the heart: love and awe · אהבה ויראה (ahavah veyirah, "love and awe") *id `heart_after` · phase: install · lives in: inside*

- **For.** Knowing what the heart does when a truth lands, so it can be described and never demanded. Love like water: seeing how the world lives from the Godly light. Love like fire: from His greatness and one's own distance; it burns up the other fire. Awe under both. Joy and bitul together.
- **When.** The inside step, as description only.
- **Not when.** Never ask for a feeling, promise one, or ask how it landed (D25). Never force it.
- **Dose.** One or two sentences of description.
- **Steps.** 1. Describe what this truth is like from inside, in the sources' images, lightly. 2. If something moves, do not grab it or push it away. Do not make a 'something' of it. 3. Let the feeling add its strength back into the understanding. 4. If nothing moves, say plainly that the mind's own love counts. The mind is in your hands; the heart follows in its time. 5. If weeping and joy come together, keep both.
- **What the person does.** Reads a short description and goes on, or says what came.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "When this lands, people often say it feels like coming home. Some feel nothing at all. Both count." - "If something warm came, don't hold it up to look at. Take it back to the cup."
- **Signs it is working.** As water gives back a face, the heart answers. Often only a small stir. Afterwards, true lowliness; an insult stings less.
- **The counterfeit.** Arousal made by wanting arousal. Feeling oneself 'from that very experience'.
- **If nothing happens.** The heart may answer late, by degrees. The Tanya: contemplation gives birth to love 'in the mind, at least'.
- **Over days and weeks.** Quiet love like water tends to come first. Fire and the fuller awe come later, if at all, and matter less than the change in the day.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 17. Telling real from counterfeit · הבחנת ההתפעלות (havchanas hahispa'alus, "testing the arousal") *id `discern` · phase: gate · lives in: inside*

- **For.** Asking where a movement came from: the idea itself, or the wish to be moved. The Mitteler Rebbe calls the second 'serving oneself'.
- **When.** The guide, silently, at every inside step. The person, later, at a set time, never in the middle.
- **Not when.** Never during the sitting. Never as an interrogation of the person (D25).
- **Dose.** One plain question, later.
- **Steps.** 1. Do not test in the moment. 2. Later, look at the fruit: lowliness, an insult that stings less, being more one's own master. 3. Test only at times. 4. Aim to give Him pleasure, not to reach a level.
- **What the person does.** Asks once, later: am I a little more my own master than before?
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "The test comes later, not now. Tonight, check one thing. Did anything sting less today?" - "Big feelings aren't the measure. Being a bit more your own master is."
- **Signs it is working.** Afterwards true lowliness; 'he has nothing of his own'.
- **The counterfeit.** Arousal from the wish to be aroused. The self felt more, 'from that very experience'.
- **If nothing happens.** No arousal is not failure. The test is the day.
- **Over days and weeks.** The same test for years; it only gets more honest.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 18. Saying it with the lips · דבור בדברי תורה (dibbur b'divrei Torah, "speech in words of Torah") *id `lips` · phase: consolidate · lives in: speak*

- **For.** The second garment. Speech in words of Torah is the vessel that holds the light. The voice wakes the intention. Mouth first, the heart follows.
- **When.** The speak step. Any time the heart is shut and the mind will not hold.
- **Not when.** No saying, chanting or permuting of Divine Names. For a Jew, Echad is lengthened in thought, not in sound. Not rattled off, not loud for show.
- **Dose.** One line, or the Shema's first verse. Under a minute.
- **Steps.** 1. Say the line, or its verse, aloud or under the breath. Slowly. All your strength in each word. 2. For a Jew, the Shema: cover the eyes, say it aloud as a new decree never heard before; hold the ches a little and the dalet longer, in thought: He alone, over the four directions. 3. Then 'Blessed be the name...' quietly, and let the world come back, full of Him. 4. If nothing is felt, say it anyway. The heart wakes through the mouth.
- **What the person does.** Says one line aloud or under the breath.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Say it out loud, even quietly. 'There is nothing besides Him.' Once, slowly, like you mean it." - "Cover your eyes and say Shema. Hold the 'One' in your head, not your voice." - "Mouth first. The heart catches up."
- **Signs it is working.** Fervour comes by itself. The words start to come out by themselves.
- **The counterfeit.** Rattling off. Loud for show.
- **If nothing happens.** Say it anyway, with the mouth alone.
- **Over days and weeks.** From one line to a prayer said word by word. The Piaseczner keeps his fiery Shema to three times a week, since a device used daily stops working.
- **For anyone.** 'I have set God before me always' (Psalm 16:8), or the line itself, or their own faith's words.

### 19. Melody · ניגון (niggun, "a tune") *id `niggun` · phase: loosen · lives in: stay, speak*

- **For.** Carrying the line in a tune. The Rashab: through melody specifically the soul is moved, for the voice arouses the intention of the heart.
- **When.** The stay or speak step, especially when cold or low.
- **Not when.** Not in place of the work. The Rayatz: a niggun is a preparation, not yet the work. Not mood music.
- **Dose.** A minute or two. A whispered hum is enough.
- **Steps.** 1. Take a tune you know, or any turn of melody. 2. Face the wall or close your eyes. 3. Put the line into the tune. 4. At first you sing to wake the soul. Then it sings by itself.
- **What the person does.** Hums the line into a tune they know.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Hum something you know, even under your breath. Put the line inside it." - "No good voice needed. A hum in a whisper is plenty."
- **Signs it is working.** The soul starts to sing by itself. Felt, and not felt as one's own.
- **The counterfeit.** Performance. A pleasant mood with no line in it.
- **If nothing happens.** Skip it.
- **Over days and weeks.** The same tune becomes a key back to the line.
- **For anyone.** Any tune.

### 20. Your own words to Him · שיחה · התבודדות (sichah · hisbodedus, "talk · being alone with Him") *id `own_words` · phase: install · lives in: speak*

- **For.** Turning the line into address. Speech itself loosens the knots in the soul. Breslov: one cannot come to self-nullification except through hisbodedus. The Piaseczner: pour out your heart in any language you understand.
- **When.** The speak step. Any time something stirs.
- **Not when.** Not to the guide: to Him. Heartbreak is not sadness; if it turns into despair, stop. For someone unsure, an honest try they can skip.
- **Dose.** A minute in the conversation. The Breslov custom is a set time daily, best at night.
- **Steps.** 1. Say it to Him in the language you actually speak, as to a teacher or a friend. 2. Name the thing itself. 3. Lean on the word 'You'. 4. If words will not come, say only 'Master of the world'. Speaking without heart is still very good. 5. Do not wait for tears. 6. Make the teaching itself into a prayer. Keep the words that burst out; use them again.
- **What the person does.** Speaks to Him, out loud or under the breath. Does not report it.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Say it to Him, not to me. You could start with 'You're holding the rent too.'" - "If words won't come, just 'Master of the world.' That's a whole prayer." - "You don't have to tell me what you said."
- **Signs it is working.** Knots loosen. Words come that surprise. Stone some days, open others.
- **The counterfeit.** Waiting and watching for tears. Performing. Self-pity going round.
- **If nothing happens.** One word. Or the still, small scream no one hears.
- **Over days and weeks.** A minute a day, to a set daily time. Sefer Charedim's older form: one day a week alone with Him, as if already standing before Him.
- **For anyone.** The same, for any faith. For someone unsure: 'if anyone is listening'.

### 21. The act that closes · מעשה (ma'aseh, "the deed") *id `act` · phase: consolidate · lives in: day*

- **For.** The third garment. Light holds only in a vessel. Arousal that does not reach a deed is 'a flame flying in the air'. The rising was for the sake of coming back down.
- **When.** The day step, at the end of every round.
- **Not when.** No vow made in the glow. Nothing big.
- **Dose.** One small act with a when.
- **Steps.** 1. Choose one small act tied to the line, at a real moment of the day. 2. Say it aloud from the heart once; the soul bows to words said strongly. 3. Do it at once if you can.
- **What the person does.** Names one act and does it today.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "One thing today. Pay that bill at lunch, and before you click, say 'being said'." - "Put a coin in a box on your way out. That's where this lands."
- **Signs it is working.** Settled. Something to show. Better one act than a thousand sighs.
- **The counterfeit.** A grand resolution. A high with nothing after.
- **If nothing happens.** A smaller act.
- **Over days and weeks.** From one act to the mitzvah or kindness one usually rushes, done slowly (manual L14).
- **For anyone.** A kindness, or a coin given.

### 22. Carrying a line through the day · התבוננות קלה (hisbonenus kalah, "a light contemplation") *id `carry` · phase: consolidate · lives in: day*

- **For.** The Tanya: one who contemplates at length once a day can bring it back with a light contemplation at any hour.
- **When.** The day step.
- **Not when.** Not a mantra said without the thing. Not a burden.
- **Dose.** Three to six words, seconds at a time, at a set cue.
- **Steps.** 1. Take a few words of the line. 2. Tie them to a cue: a door, the kettle, opening the phone. 3. At each cue, seconds only. 4. Know a few lines by heart, so they are there in the street.
- **What the person does.** Says three words at a set cue during the day.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Take three words with you. 'Being said now.' Every time you open the fridge today." - "You don't need the whole thing later. The three words bring it back."
- **Signs it is working.** The line comes back at the cue without effort.
- **The counterfeit.** Words said with nothing behind them.
- **If nothing happens.** A new cue.
- **Over days and weeks.** From one cue a day to 'at least every hour' (Tanya ch. 41), and the Besht's small thought kept alight while going about one's business.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 23. Coming back to the same line · חזרה · הרגל (chazarah · hergel, "going over it again · habit") *id `chazarah` · phase: consolidate · lives in: day*

- **For.** The same line day after day, one step further each time, until habit becomes nature. The Rayatz: contemplate it several times at length, and then it is well absorbed.
- **When.** The next day's round, and any sitting on the same line.
- **Not when.** Not bare repetition of the general idea: the Mitteler Rebbe says that has no life of Godly light in it.
- **Dose.** The same line for about a week (ours). A special device (the fiery Shema) only a few times a week.
- **Steps.** 1. Start from yesterday's line. 2. Go one step further: a new detail, a harder place in the day, less explaining, more staying. 3. Know it by heart. 4. If it has gone cold for several days, change the rung, not the effort.
- **What the person does.** Meets the same line again, and takes it one step further.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Same line as yesterday. Today take it one step closer, into the hardest part of your day." - "You know this one already. So less explaining today, more staying."
- **Signs it is working.** 'I knew this before too, but I only knew it.' Then it is so.
- **The counterfeit.** Reciting. The general idea remembered but not lived.
- **If nothing happens.** Change the rung, not the effort.
- **Over days and weeks.** See Progression, below.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 24. The evening look-back · חשבון הנפש (cheshbon hanefesh, "the soul's accounting") *id `cheshbon` · phase: consolidate · lives in: day*

- **For.** Going over the day's thoughts, words and deeds honestly and briefly, ending in joy, and finding the fruit of the line in the day.
- **When.** Bedtime. Offered by the guide only now and then, as a day-step option.
- **Not when.** Not on a low day. Not for someone scrupulous or self-punishing. Never endless: the Besht calls over-scrupulous reckoning a trick, and sadness the enemy's aim.
- **Dose.** A few minutes (ours).
- **Steps.** 1. Go over thoughts, words and deeds, without defending yourself. 2. Be sorry, briefly, about one thing. 3. Then remove the sadness from your heart entirely. 4. Find a good point still in you, and gather it. 5. Ask one question: am I a little more my own master than before? 6. If a heavy mood has no name, ask 'since when?' and trace it to its hour.
- **What the person does.** Two minutes at night: one thing right, one thing not, then put it down.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "Tonight, two minutes. One thing that went right with this, one that didn't. Then put it down." - "Be sorry about one thing, briefly. Then find one good point in yourself and sleep on that."
- **Signs it is working.** Sobering, then relief. It ends in resolve and joy.
- **The counterfeit.** A broken heart turned into sadness. Endless review.
- **If nothing happens.** One good point is enough.
- **Over days and weeks.** The Alter Rebbe's full form is an hour; the Rebbe tells a person stuck in endless review to stop thinking about his state for some weeks (Darkei §7). Short and regular is the rule here.
- **For anyone.** The same.

### 25. What letter methods add, and what the guide keeps out · צירוף אותיות (tzeruf osiyos, "joining letters") *id `letters` · phase: loosen · lives in: outside the round*

- **For.** A comparison. Abulafia loosens meaning by moving letters until no picture can settle; Chabad fills one meaning until the self it held gives way (ABULAFIA §6.1). Both show the engine is one-pointedness plus time. Some of his safeguards are useful; his core practice is not for this guide.
- **When.** What the guide takes: the exit (come back into the body through the plainest acts), 'if nothing comes, leave it for the week', 'if your heart runs, come back to the place', character before practice, joy as a precondition, a companion nearby. The Chabad-safe form of letters: the Tanya's name of a thing as its life (walk_down), and the Besht's full strength in each word of prayer (lips).
- **Not when.** Never: saying, chanting or permuting Divine Names; breath counts or head movements; night-long solitary intensive practice; picturing angels or a court; seeking visions or voices. Abulafia himself: in exchanging letters is the place of danger; do not say the Name with a weak or busy mind; Sha'arei Kedusha warns that forcing brings mixed, external things. His peak signs (soul leaving the body, thinking one will die, forms, voices, limbs going weak) are stop-signs here (SCIENCE §5.3).
- **Dose.** None. Not practised.
- **Steps.** 1. If a person asks for Abulafia or Kabbalistic letter practice: explain, honestly, that the tradition itself guards it with a teacher, character and halachah, and that this guide does not lead it. 2. Offer what is safe: the letters of a thing's name as its life (walk_down); full strength in each word of prayer (lips). 3. If a sitting leaves someone frightened or unreal: stop, bow or simply stand, eat or drink a little, do something plain with the hands. 4. If nothing comes: leave it for the week.
- **What the person does.** Nothing from this family, except the safe forms named.
- **What the guide says** (ours): - "That practice was always taught face to face, with a teacher watching. I won't lead it here." - "The Tanya has a gentler one. The Hebrew name of a stone is its life. Look at yours." - "That feeling of floating is a stop-sign. Stand up, drink some water, wash a cup."
- **Signs it is working.** Not applicable: this family is not practised here.

## 3. Progression

*The stages are ours; the signs and warnings in them are the sources'.*

## Day one

One round of the eight steps, in one conversation, with one line. The person learns three things by doing them, never by being told: one line, one real thing, and coming back. The real holding may add up to five or ten minutes spread across the replies (ours). Expect little. Often nothing moves, or one detail catches for a second ("this cup is being said"), or it goes flat just as the details start. All three are normal. It ends in one small act. The guide does not praise, grade or ask how it went (D25).

## The first forty days

Manual Part III's course is the spine: the fourteen rungs, each sat two or three times, Shabbos lighter, low days on the first rung. In the conversation, one round per visit, and the ladder log moves the line.

| Days | What is added (ours) | Techniques |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 7 | The lower rungs. One line, a few things, a minute of staying, a line said aloud, one act. | arrive, choose_line, breadth, length, walk_down, binding, lips, act |
| 8 to 15 | Detail, and the picture broken. Staying grows to two or three minutes. The evening look-back offered once. | details, mashal, garment_look, cheshbon |
| 16 to 21 | The hinge: know it today. The same line again, shorter, and the quiet after. A fixed moment chosen. | chazarah, depth, hold, carry |
| 22 to 28 | Above the mind. The edge, said plainly; longer words to Him; a tune if one comes. | edge, own_words, niggun, heart_after |
| 29 to 40 | Coming down. The act slowed; the line carried at a cue; the hardest room of the home. Day 40: the manual's check. | act, carry, garment_look, discern |

**What changes first.** Not feelings. In the order people tend to meet them (ours, from the sources' signs): 1. Coming back gets quicker. The line is there when the mind returns. 2. The line comes back by itself at the cue. The Tanya: a long contemplation once a day lets "a light contemplation" bring it back at any hour. 3. One reaction that would have happened, doesn't. The Piaseczner's only question: am I more my own master than before? 4. It goes flat. Just as the details begin, it goes cold. That is where the work starts (manual Part IV, state 3). 5. The heart answers late, and by degrees.

## Months

- **A fixed time.** The line moves into prayer for a Jew (the Rashab's half hour before it), or a set daily time for anyone. If you cannot be aflame all day, the Piaseczner says, at least set times.
- **Longer staying.** From two minutes toward ten or twenty (ours). The Rashab's "hour or more" is a road, not a demand.
- **Pictures needed less.** Once thought is strong, the bodily picture falls away by itself.
- **One trait at a time.** The line starts to be brought into one particular trait, many times over (DARKEI §2, step 6).
- **Up and down.** "A person must always be going up and coming down." When the heart seems sealed, "in truth, even now it is full of holiness".
- **Own words daily.** A set time to talk to Him, in one's own language.

## Years

- **The same few lines, deeper.** The Rebbe's fold: the first rung is held simply only at the deepest level (ONE-PROCESS §3). Nothing is left behind.
- **The edge reached sooner.** Where knowing ends, faith carries.
- **The small thought kept lit.** The Besht's "even when he goes about his business"; the letters of Torah as the handle that is always there.
- **Steady, not up and down in extremes.** Praise and insult land the same: the Besht's reading of shiviti as evenness. This is the fruit of long practice, never a pose.
- **The durations the sources give** (one trait, forty days, three to four years, a lifetime) are gathered in DARKEI §5.

## When to go deeper, when to go back

**Go deeper** (one step, on the same line) when: the line came back by itself at the cue; coming back is quick; yesterday's act got done; the person asks to go on. **Not** because they had a big feeling. Deeper means: a new detail, a harder place in the day, less explaining and more staying (chazarah); after two or three rounds, the next rung.

| State | What the guide does |
| --- | --- |
| Low, heavy, sad | The first rung or the plainest thing, the three-minute shape. No bitul, no edge, no look-back. Melody, a good point, a son before his father. |
| Scattered | A minute of quieting, or mouth first: the line aloud. Fewer things in breadth. |
| Cold for days | Change the rung, not the effort. A picture, a tune, the lips. |
| Frightened, floating, unreal | Stop. Stand, drink a little water, do something plain with the hands. Next time, the first rung or the home below. |
| High and floating | Straight to the act, today, before it becomes a flame in the air. |
| Danger | The crisis protocol. Nothing else. |

The sources expect the going back. The descent is for the sake of the ascent; one must come down several times a day to rest; a device used every day stops working; if nothing comes, leave it for the week.

## 4. The map into the conversation

*All of this section is ours: the sources give the techniques, not the chat.*

**The rules over every step** (the owner's calls, restated as they bear on technique). Answer what the person actually said first. One step per reply, never named; never "now we'll meditate", never "would you like to". No questions about feelings or how it is landing; after a short answer, give, don't ask. Replies short, like texts from a friend who gets it. No breath, body scan, "notice" or relaxing (D11, D16); a felt act (a palm on the table, the line said aloud, a coin in a box) is fine. Heavy things first: if they bring something heavy or new, meet it fully and step back.

**What changes in a chat.** Closed eyes become "before you write back". A minute of stillness becomes "take a minute before you answer". Speech to Him is "out loud or under your breath; you won't see it, and you don't have to tell me". The guide never checks whether the person did it.

**The three garments across a round.** Steps 1 to 6 are thought (the binding), step 7 is speech, step 8 is deed. Every round passes through all three.

**The second round.** After "into the day", the engine returns to "look" on the same line. The second pass adds what the first did not have: details, depth, the edge, the hold, chazarah. The next day's line goes one step deeper (the ladder log).

### 1. Meet (`meet`) **Techniques:** Start from what is already moving. **One reply:** Take what they brought, at their size, as the door. Answer any question first. Leave one plain fact from their own day that shows more is holding them than they think, said lightly. If they give little, give them one small real thing to look at. **If they are:** *scattered:* Meet it; no technique yet. *low:* Stay with them; the gladder or plainer thing, never a lesson. *close:* Meet them where they already are; they may bring yesterday's line.

**Sample lines** (ours): - "That worry is awake right now. Good. That's where we start." - "Your heart kept going all night while you worried. You didn't do that." - "Tired and flat counts. Something got you through last night."

### 2. Turn toward what holds (`turn`) **Techniques:** The threshold: before Whom, Seeing the garment, Quieting the stream (a door only), Choosing the one line. **One reply:** Turn their attention, lightly, from the trouble to what is under it: what keeps going without their effort. One sentence that makes them look, or one real question about their life. The guide chooses the line silently here. If their head is racing, a minute of quieting belongs here, then on. **If they are:** *scattered:* One minute: what am I thinking now? Then the line next reply. *low:* What held them today, plainly. No threshold of awe. *close:* The threshold: before Whom.

**Sample lines** (ours): - "You didn't make this moment. Something is making it, right now, with you in it." - "Before the worry, one question. What's still standing today that you didn't hold up?" - "Whatever you do next, you do it in front of the One keeping you here."

### 3. One true line (`line`) **Techniques:** Choosing the one line, Breadth, Length, The picture, and where it breaks. **One reply:** Give today's line in their words, at full depth, with its reason and one picture from their world. Say where the picture breaks only if it helps. One teaching, exact. Not the lesson around it. **If they are:** *scattered:* The line in eight words, nothing else. *low:* The gentlest rung: the spark under everything, or the plainest thing. *close:* Yesterday's line, one step deeper.

**Sample lines** (ours): - "Here it is. The word that makes this table is saying it right now. If it stopped, the table wouldn't fall. It wouldn't be." - "Your words leave you when you speak. His don't leave Him. So nothing is ever outside Him, you included."

### 4. Try it on something real (`look`) **Techniques:** Walking the truth down to one thing, Detail by detail, Length, Breadth, Seeing the garment, Picturing, the Piaseczner's way. **One reply:** Have them test the line on one real thing in front of them, or one person in their life. Give the doing first. Walk the truth down to that one thing if it helps. Particular, never general. **If they are:** *scattered:* One object, two links. *cold:* A picture: the sky past the window, or the Throne. *low:* The plainest thing: a cup of water, a slice of bread. *close:* The hardest thing in their day, held the same way.

**Sample lines** (ours): - "Pick up the cup nearest you. The word comes all the way down into that one cup. It's being said now." - "Put your hand flat on the table. The last link of a long chain is under your palm." - "Now Thursday's meeting. The room, the people, you walking in. Same word, same moment."

### 5. Stay with it (`stay`) **Techniques:** Returning the wandering mind, Depth, Detail by detail, Melody, Quieting the stream (a door only). **One reply:** Ask them to stop for a minute with the line in their own words and the one thing, eyes closed if they like, and to come back through the thing when the mind wanders. Then to write whatever is there. Don't call it meditation. **If they are:** *scattered:* Thirty seconds, said aloud. *cold:* Hum the line into a tune. *low:* Shorter, softer, the one plain thing. *close:* Drop the examples. Three words, and a longer stillness.

**Sample lines** (ours): - "Take a minute with the cup and the line before you answer. When your mind wanders, come back through the cup." - "A thought keeps pulling? Twenty seconds on it, from the side. Then back to the cup." - "Drop the examples. Just 'being said, now'. A minute, then write whatever's there."

### 6. From inside (`inside`) **Techniques:** The path of the heart: love and awe, The quiet after, Stopping at the edge of understanding, Telling real from counterfeit. **One reply:** Meet what they bring back in a sentence. Then say, in one or two lines, what this truth is like from inside when it lands, in the sources' images. Described, never promised. If nothing came, that is normal and the thinking counted. On a second round, say where the mind stops and leave a moment there. **If they are:** *low:* No edge. Only: the thinking counted. *close:* The edge, then the quiet after. *floating:* Stop. Something plain with the hands.

**Sample lines** (ours): - "People describe it as being held rather than holding. Or nothing at all. Both count." - "If something warm came, don't look at it. Leave it a minute." - "Here the thinking runs out. How it's being said, no mind holds. Stay at that edge a moment."

### 7. Say it to Him (`speak`) **Techniques:** Your own words to Him, Saying it with the lips, Melody. **One reply:** Ask them to say it to Him, not to you, in their own words, out loud or under their breath, and give the first few words. For a Jew, the Shema's first verse fits here. For someone unsure, one light sentence makes it an honest try they can skip. **If they are:** *cold:* Mouth first: one line aloud, the heart follows. *low:* As a child to a father. Or only 'Master of the world'. *unsure:* 'If anyone's listening...'

**Sample lines** (ours): - "Say it to Him, not to me. You could start with 'You're holding the rent too.'" - "Cover your eyes and say Shema. Hold the 'One' in your head, not your voice." - "If words won't come, just 'Master of the world.' That's a whole prayer."

### 8. Into the day (`day`) **Techniques:** The act that closes, Carrying a line through the day, Coming back to the same line, The evening look-back. **One reply:** Land it in one small act at a real moment of their day, and a few of their own words to carry there. Then let them go warmly. Tomorrow the same line goes one step deeper. Offer the evening look-back only now and then, never on a low day. **If they are:** *low:* The smallest act. No look-back. *high:* The act now, before the glow fades. *close:* The act they usually rush, done slowly.

**Sample lines** (ours): - "One thing today. Before you pay that bill, say 'being said' once." - "Take three words with you. Every time you open the fridge." - "Tonight, two minutes. One thing that went right with this. Then put it down."

## 5. The five that matter most for the guide (ours)

1. **Binding (returning the wandering mind).** It is da'at itself; without it every other technique gives "empty imaginings". In the chat it is one sentence: come back through the one thing, no scolding.
2. **Detail by detail, with walking down to one thing.** The difference between makif and pnimi is the difference between a slogan and a meditation. One cup, being said now.
3. **Choosing the one line.** Everything rests on the right line for this person today, from the lower rungs, kept for days.
4. **Your own words to Him, then the lips.** Speech is the vessel, and in a text conversation it is the one moment the person turns from the guide to Him.
5. **The act that closes, with the line carried.** Light holds only in a vessel; the day is where the round is judged.
