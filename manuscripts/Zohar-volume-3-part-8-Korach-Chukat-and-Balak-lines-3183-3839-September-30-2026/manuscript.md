## The shape of the range

- **Korach** is about peace and division. The Name of G-d is "Peace," and whoever splits peace splits the Name (3187-3190). It also teaches how the priest's quiet incense calms anger (3202-3209), and how to include the left in the right (3210-3216).
- **Chukat** holds the red heifer (3260-3276), the deaths of Miriam, Aaron and Moshe as moon, arm and sun (3277-3310), and a sharp teaching on who knows how to "call" G-d in prayer (3317-3325).
- **Balak** is one of the longest sections in the volume. It is built of story after story: - The Yenuka, the wonder-child of Rav Hamnuna Saba (3361-3464). - The long teaching on the prayer of the poor and the four postures of prayer (3518-3543). - Rabbi Pinchas ben Yair's donkey and the birds that shade Rabbi Shimon's circle (3625-3667). - The father who pleads with his son (3668-3669). - The boy whose tears bring his dead father back to life (3689-3715). - Balaam, his "evil eye," and the ways it was turned (3729-3825).

## The unity of G-d

**1. The Name is Peace.** "The world stands only on peace." When G-d made the world "it could not stand until He came and rested peace on it. And what is that? Shabbos" (3188).

> ומאן דפליג על שלום, פליג על שמא קדישא, בגין דשמא קדישא, שלום אקרי (Zohar, Korach 1:5; line 3187)
> Whoever divides against peace divides against the holy Name, because the holy Name is called Peace.

*Ours:* this ties unity to relationships. Splitting people apart is treated as splitting G-d's Name. The guide should keep this: the inner unity and the outer peace are one teaching.

**2. "And this is the Torah": all one with no separation.** The extra letter *vav* in "וזאת" is there "to show that all is one, without separation" (לאחזאה דכלא חד, בלא פרודא, line 3244). Law without the *vav* is only a decree. Torah with it is the joining of the Holy One and the Assembly of Israel.

**3. "He" is below and above, revealed and hidden.** On the verse "the Levite, *he* shall serve," the Zohar says: *He* completes, so that all is one. "*He* below, *He* above, *He* revealed, *He* hidden, *He* is G-d" (הוא: לתתא. הוא: לעילא. הוא: אתגלייא. הוא סתים. הוא אלהים, line 3222). And "if judgment were not found in the world, people would not know the higher faith" (line 3221). *Ours:* even the side of judgment serves the knowing of G-d.

**4. The complete unity in the Shema.** "All the complete unity is here: Hashem, our G-d, Hashem." The first is the head, the second the trunk, the third the path below (line 3676). Israel "dwells alone" means "in unity, without mixture" (ביחודא, בלא ערבוביא אחרא, line 3675).

**5. He is found in the smallest mercies, and only He knows them.** Rabbi Pinchas weeps when he learns that his donkey's braying once saved travelers: "Master of the world, You did this miracle for me and I did not know" (line 3626).

> כמה טיבו עביד קודשא בריך הוא עם בני נשא, וכמה נסין ארחיש לון בכל יומא, ולא ידע אלא איהו בלחודוי (Zohar, Balak 24:2; line 3626)
> How much good the Holy One does with people, and how many miracles He works for them every day, and none knows it but He alone.

A person steps on a snake in the morning and never knows. "He alone does, and knows, and another does not know." This matches S63 (the world held) and adds a warm note of hidden care.

**6. "Hashem kills and gives life," read as one act.** The Name is "an elixir of life for all," and death does not rest in it. So what does He "kill"? The pull of the other side, which "the moment it sees the radiance of His glory, dies," and in that same moment He revives the holy spirit. "The Holy One does all at one time" (line 3702). *Ours:* the unity here is inside time. Destruction and revival are one act seen from two sides.

## States of consciousness

- **The poor man's prayer, alone with Him.** This is the richest teaching on prayer in this part of the volume:

> לא בעינא הכא בי דינא דידונון ביננא, קמאי ליהוו תרעומין דיליה, ואנא והוא בלחודנא (Zohar, Balak 14:4; line 3521)
> I want no court here to judge between us. Let his complaints be before Me, I and he alone.

The poor man's prayer goes before Moshe's and David's. "He picks a quarrel with the Holy One, and the Holy One listens" (line 3520). The heavenly hosts ask what G-d is busy with, and are told He is alone "with desire, with His vessels" (line 3522). Then:

> דלית תיאובתא למסכנא, אלא כד שפיך דמעוי בתורעמו, קמי מלכא קדישא. ולית תיאובתא לקודשא בריך הוא, אלא כד מקבל לון (Zohar, Balak 14:5; line 3522)
> The poor man has no desire except when he pours out his tears in complaint before the Holy King. And the Holy One has no desire except when He receives them.

This maps to S77 (lower return) and S60 (essential bond), but it is its own state: the right to complain, face to face, with no one between. **Proposed state: the poor man's complaint.** *Ours:* it gives the guide an ancient source for honest, even angry, prayer as closeness, not as rebellion. - **Making oneself poor.** David took off his royal robe and sat on the ground (line 3523). "A person should make himself poor so that his prayer enters with all the poor" (line 3524). Maps to S57. - **Found when small.** When the Shechinah's love is more than she can bear, she makes herself small "until only one point is seen," and says "do not look at me, for I am dark." Her hosts roar. The Beloved hears, comes out with gifts, "finds her small and dark, without form," and "embraces her and kisses her until she wakes little by little" and is rebuilt (3446-3448). The same happens below in a bad generation: she shrinks to a point, and the righteous "make her" again (line 3450). **Proposed state: found when small.** *Ours:* this is a picture for the person who has shrunk to almost nothing inside. It says that such a person is not lost; they are waiting to be found. - **The heart that turns.** "When a person sets his heart and will to return before his Master," G-d gathers his spirit into the bundle of life (line 3199). The level called *Mi* ("Who") is where "all the paths of return are open" (line 3350). Maps to S77 and S78. - **"Our Father" said from pain.** The long note on *Avinu* in "our father died in the desert": "there is no pain of spirit and soul except when they call out *Avinu* with pain from the heart" (line 3706). Grief that turns to G-d as Father. Near S49. - **Anger as a fire of the other side.** "Whoever is angry is as if he serves idols, because the other side burns in the person" (line 3229). The cure is "in anger, remember mercy." Maps to the map's teaching on anger. - **Morning, the hour of favor for all.** "When morning comes, that is a time of favor for all, to do good to the whole world, to the righteous and to the wicked" (line 3682). "All the King's prisoners find rest" (line 3683). Maps to S53 and S84.

## Meditation and practice

- **The four postures of prayer.** Rabbi Elazar maps one morning service onto four postures from the Psalms: 1. **Servant.** Take this posture three times: in the praises before the Amidah, after the Amidah, and at the end of the whole prayer. 2. **One who gives his soul for the Name.** This happens in the unity of the Shema: "whoever sets his will this way on this verse is counted as if he gave his soul for the sanctification of the Name" (line 3531). 3. **Poor.** Take this posture in the Amidah, right after "true and firm": "broken-hearted, poor and needy, and set his will to be included among the poor, in brokenness of heart, in lowering of soul" (line 3532). 4. **Chasid.** Take this posture in "who hears prayer," where a person names his sins "to cleave to the right hand stretched out to receive those who return" (line 3533).

Whoever does this "with the will of the heart," G-d spreads His right hand over him and says "you are My servant, Israel, in whom I glory," and his prayer "will never return empty" (line 3535). Ties to `before_whom`, `own_words`, `heart_after`. **Proposed technique: the four postures of prayer.** - **Only the sins you remember.** A person does not need to list every sin since birth, "only those he remembers; and if he sets his will on them, all the others are drawn after them" (line 3526), just as one searches for chametz only where the eye can see. Ties to `cheshbon`. *Ours:* a kind limit on self-examination. - **The nightly reckoning.** "Every night before he lies down and before he sleeps, a person must make a reckoning of his deeds of that whole day, return from them, and ask mercy for them," because sleep is "the hour of the tree of death" (line 3215). Ties to `cheshbon`. - **Knowing how to call.** "Everyone goes to the synagogue to awaken something above, but few know how to awaken" (line 3320). Those who only keep the order draw from "behind His shoulders." Those "who know the root of the word and the deed, and know how to direct heart and will," bring blessing "from the place of Thought" (3324-3325). Ties to `before_whom` and `binding`. - **Include the left in the right.** "Whatever your hand finds to do with your strength, do": let everything be done on the side of the right, including the left in it, "and then the Holy One dwells in him in this world" (line 3212). Do not put it off: "do not say, when I come to that world I will ask mercy there" (line 3213). Ties to `act`. - **Guarding the mouth.** Esther was clothed in the holy spirit "because she guarded her mouth. Whoever guards his mouth and tongue merits to be clothed in the holy spirit" (line 3312). Ties to `lips`. - **Speaking only after hearing fully.** "It is forbidden to rush ahead in a word of Torah until one has heard the word and knows it as it is" (line 3747). Ties to `discern`. - **Comforting a mourner.** Job's friends said true things, but they did not comfort. A comforter must "arrange words" that the mourner can agree with, until he accepts the judgment himself (line 3197). *Ours:* this is a clear teaching for anyone who sits with someone in grief. - **The Shema in bed.** "When a person wants to lie on his bed, he sanctifies the high Name, makes Him King above and below," and asks mercy with verses of mercy (line 3808). Ties to the nightly handing over (part 1). - **Care for animals.** "Trouble to living creatures we do not want, for His mercies are on all His works" (line 3641). Ties to `act`.

## The big human questions

- **Why suffering?** The Zohar gives three answers in this range. It does not hide how hard it is.
- **Small, now.** G-d gives Israel small sufferings "little by little in every generation," so that at the great day of judgment there is "a note of sufferings" to set against every note of sins (3605-3607).
- **Here, not there.** David says "punish me here, to cleanse me, and do not leave my punishment for that world" (line 3705).
- **Narrowness as a bride's crown.** "The narrowness and distress of Israel, how much good it brings them" (line 3603). The word *kalah* is read as both "an end" and "a bride."
- **Does G-d hurt when He punishes?** Yes. The parable of the father:

> אמר קודשא בריך הוא, חמינא בברי, דבגין מלקיותא דלקינא לון, אינון חששו ברישיהון. ווי, דהא מגו כאיבא דלהון, חשישנא אנא (Zohar, Balak 30:2; line 3669)
> The Holy One said: I see My children, that because of the blows I struck them, their heads hurt. Woe, for from their pain I hurt.

So He stops striking and pleads: "My people, what have I done to you? My only son, beloved of My soul... answer against Me" (line 3669). The Zohar is saying G-d is not cold. *Ours:* this is one of the strongest pictures in the whole volume of G-d's pain for us, and the guide should use it. - **Can tears change a decree?** A small boy lies with his mouth on his dead father's mouth and argues from the Torah: "You wrote to send away the mother and spare the young. You took our mother; now You took our father too, who covered us. Where is the law of the Torah?" (line 3693). A voice says his words and tears rose to the Throne; twenty-two years are added; Rabbi Yosi opens his eyes (3695-3696). Later he says his son's 365 tears "were counted before the Holy King" (line 3698). - **Who is near, and who is kept away?** When a leper knocks at the palace gate, the king himself goes out to warn him off from his son. When the beloved knocks, the king says "no other voice will call him but mine: come in, beloved of my soul" (3621-3622). - **Is the world ruled by chance?** No. Even a daily miracle goes unseen (line 3626).

## For the guide (ours)

1. The poor man's prayer (3518-3524) and the four postures (3525-3535) together are a full, ancient teaching on how to pray from the heart. The guide should offer it as a real practice. It permits complaint and tears, and it builds the whole service out of four simple inner stances.
2. The father who says "from their pain I hurt" (3669) should enter the guide as the answer to "Is G-d angry at me?" It pairs with the father who holds his son to his chest (3634).
3. "Found when small" (3446-3450) is a state for the person who feels they have shrunk to a point. It is found, embraced, and rebuilt "little by little."
4. "The Name is Peace" (3187) links unity to how we treat people. It belongs in the guide beside the teaching on anger (3229).
5. Two kind limits belong in the practice: confess only the sins you remember (3526), and comfort mourners with words they can say yes to (3197).
