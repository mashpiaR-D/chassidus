## What this part is

Fourteen sittings, in order. Each one takes one face of a single truth, that there is nothing besides God, even here, and gives it to you as something to sit with: one line, a way to think it through, a picture, words to say, and one thing to do today. You go up the ladder from what is nearest (yourself), through the world and the rungs of the unity, as far up as anyone can reach, and then back down into your kitchen, your habits and your hands.

The owner of this project put the whole idea in one sentence: people aren't looking for God, they're looking for themselves, and in finding who they really are, they find Him. So the ladder starts with you and, near the top, comes back to you.

This is hisbonenus, the Jewish way of contemplating as Chabad teaches it. It is not mindfulness. You won't be asked to watch your breath, scan your body, relax, or empty your mind. The object of every sitting is a line of teaching, thought through until it becomes real. Calmly watching your thoughts for a minute to let them slow down, as the Piaseczner Rebbe teaches, is allowed at the start. It is only a way to get quiet enough to begin.

## Before you start

- **If you are in danger**, or having thoughts of not wanting to be alive, stop here and reach a person now: your local emergency number, or a crisis line where you are. This manual is not for that moment.
- **On a low day**, use sitting 1 and the 3-minute versions. Skip sitting 6 (bitul) and sitting 12 (the dark place). They are for a self that is too big, never for a self that has been crushed.
- **If a sitting leaves you worse**, frightened, or feeling that things are unreal, stop it. Go back to sitting 1 or sitting 13 and do something plain with your hands. That is not failure. It is the right move.
- **Who it is for.** Everyone. Every sitting has words for a Jew, for a person of another faith, and for someone unsure about God. Jewish-only practices (the Shema, the mezuzah, blessings in Hebrew, Shabbos) are offered to Jews only, and each has a version for everyone else.

## The order, and why

The unity has rungs. On the first, the world is real and exists only because God is giving it being this moment. On the second, before Him it counts for nothing. Above both, before God as He is, the word is "alone" (*yachid*). Yet the Shema says "One" (*echad*), with the world held, and that is the deepest unity of all. The sources this index read line by line agree on the ladder and on one warning above all: never teach one rung as the whole. "Created every moment" is the first rung, not the top (Sefer HaArachim, the unity volume). So the sittings climb in order, reach as far up as a person can reach "a little, even in imagination," and come back down. The rising, Imrei Binah says, is only for the sake of the return.

**Three movements.** Sittings 1 to 7 are the rungs you can think: you can check each one against your own table and your own day. Sittings 8 to 11 are the rungs you can only glimpse, where the mind runs out and, as the Tanya says, faith holds what is above it. Sittings 12 to 14 bring it all back down. The shape follows the sources' own "running and returning": rising is good, but the return is the main thing, and a sitting that ends in the clouds has not finished.

**Why the self comes first, and again near the top.** The Tanya reads "from my flesh I will see God" as coming to understand a little of His Godliness from the soul clothed in a person's body. So sitting 1 finds the spark under what you carry, and sitting 11, after the world and the Essence, finds that the bottom of that spark is joined with Him. In between, the world is learned. The self is the door in and the room at the end.

**Why "being said" is second, not first or last.** It is the Tanya's own opening move in the Gate of Unity, and it is the rung you can see. But Sefer HaArachim is plain that continuous creation is, within Chassidus, the lower unity. So it gets its own sitting, and the next sitting keeps it from being mistaken for the top.

**Why bitul comes after the world, not before.** Bitul taught first, to a person who hasn't yet found the spark, lands as "you are nothing." Taught after the world is seen as held, it is only the natural last step: if nothing else stands on its own, neither do I. And it comes with a guard: never on a low day.

**Why knowing is the hinge.** Everything up to sitting 7 can be understood. From sitting 8 on, it can't. The Tanya's knowing is binding, holding a truth until it is as plain as a thing you see, and its limit is faith. Both are needed before going higher.

**Why the Essence is reached through bread.** The Rashab warns against reaching for the Essence with the mind; the work now is with what is near. The Alter Rebbe says only He, whose being is from Himself, can make something from nothing. So the plainest "just there" points straight to Him, and the sitting on the Essence is a blessing over bread.

**Why the ladder ends with a deed and not a vision.** The sources say the goal is not to leave the world but to find Him in its lowest place, and that bitul should show inside the solid thing itself. So the last three sittings go down: into the dark habit, the ordinary house, and the hand doing a mitzvah.

## How each sitting is built

Every sitting has the same parts, in the same order, so that after a few days you know your way around.

- **What it's for** in your life, not in theory.
- **The line**, in plain words, with the source line it comes from in a small note.
- **Think it through**: breadth (the idea opened in details), length (brought down into your room, your hands, your street, your people), depth (the point, in a sentence), then into your own life.
- **The picture** the sources use, and where it breaks. Every picture breaks; saying where keeps you from mistaking the picture for God.
- **From inside**: what people describe. It is described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.
- **Your own words**: a sample. Use your own.
- **The lips**: words to say aloud, for a Jew, for someone of another faith, for someone unsure.
- **The act for today**: one small, specific thing.
- **Signs it's taking hold**, and **the counterfeit to watch for**.
- **If nothing moves**, and **across the days**: the first, third and seventh time you sit with the rung. These are counts of sittings, not calendar days. By the seventh time, the test is outside the sitting: what changed in your week.
- **When you're ready for the next rung**, and **the 3-minute version**.

How long? On a working day, about ten minutes (Part III gives the shape). On a free morning, twenty to forty. On a busy day, the 3-minute version counts. The Tanya says to stay with a thought "as far as your mind can and as your time allows," and the Rashab, passing on the Mitteler Rebbe, says every moment longer is like buying life for the whole day. Never shame a short sitting. Better a short one that truly stays than a long one that recites.

What counts as success is not how the sitting felt. It is the aftermath, over weeks: a little quieter, a little less full of yourself, a little more your own master. Part III (e) shows how to keep track.

## The fourteen sittings

### Sitting 1 · Under everything, the spark *F06 The soul as part of the One · The door: through yourself · claims Y01, Y02, Y03, B02, B16*

**What it's for.** For the days you have decided something about yourself: I'm a mess, I'm my mistakes, I'm just an anxious person, I don't even know who I am anymore. This sitting finds the floor under all of that. It is the door to the whole ladder, because people don't set out looking for God. They set out looking for themselves, and the bottom of the self is where He is.

**Before you start.** Never take 'the part nothing reached' to mean 'nothing happened.' If something was done to you, it was real and it was wrong. This sitting does not erase it. It finds who was there before it and is still there after it.

**The line.** *Under everything I carry, the deepest me is a part of God above.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 2, line 86: ונפש השנית בישראל, היא חלק אלוה ממעל ממש — *The second soul in a Jew is truly a part of God above.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Go through what you carry, one layer at a time. What you do all day: the job, the errands, the kids, the screen. What you feel: tired, tense, flat, annoyed. What you believe about yourself: I'm lazy, I'm too much, I'm not enough. What you want. Each layer is really yours. And each one changes. Last year's big worry is gone, and you are still here. So none of the layers is the bottom.
2. **Length.** Sit at your own table and put your hands on it. These hands have done good things and things you regret. Now think about the one who is looking out through your eyes right now. That one was there when you were eight, in a different body and a different mood. It was there on your worst day and on your best. The Tanya says that one, the deepest you, is not something made from outside. It is a part of God above.
3. **Depth.** A part is not a copy and not a gift left on a doorstep. It is of the same stuff as the whole. So the bottom of you is not a verdict, and it is not alone.
4. **Into your life.** Take the one sentence you say about yourself most often. Say it once, quietly. Then put it where it belongs: it is a layer. Say, 'That is something I carry. It is not who I am.' The Tanya's first chapter says the same thing in four words: don't be wicked in your own eyes. A person who believes the verdict grows sad, and the sadness takes the life out of every good thing he tries.
5. **For anyone.** If you aren't Jewish, the sources speak of every person as made in the image of God. Walk the same layers down. What you find at the bottom is not your own invention either.

**The picture.** A candle flame always leans upward. The Tanya says that is the soul: fire wants to leave the wick and go home to its source. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. A flame that reaches its source goes out, and you don't. And God is not a bigger fire. The picture shows one thing only: something in you keeps reaching home.

**From inside.** People describe finding a floor under the floor. The verdict about yourself goes quiet. Nobody argued it away. You just see it is a layer. Sometimes there is a homesickness, which turns out to be love. Sometimes there is nothing dramatic at all, only a steadier sense of 'I'm here.' Any of these is fine, and so is none of them. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "I've been calling myself my mistakes. There's more of me than that. The part of me that wants to be good came from You. Help me find it today and act from it once."

- *For a Jew:* Modeh Ani, slowly: 'I thank You, living and lasting King, for giving my soul back to me with compassion.' Say it now, and again tomorrow before anything else.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'I will thank You, for I am awesomely and wonderfully made' (Psalm 139:14). Or thanks in your own words for the one who looks out of your eyes.
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'If there is a source for whatever is looking out of my eyes, I would like to know it.' Say it honestly, as a question.

**The act for today.** Tomorrow morning, before you touch your phone, say Modeh Ani (or thank Him in your own words). Today, do one good thing you would do if you believed the good in you was the real you.

- The verdict sentence comes less often, or with less weight. - You catch yourself saying 'I did' instead of 'I am.' - A small good act feels natural instead of fake. - You need other people less to tell you you're okay.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Two fakes. The first is inflation: 'I'm a divine spark, so whatever I do is fine.' The Tanya keeps the other half of its sentence too: a person whose heart is never pained by what he did can slide into carelessness. The second is a warm glow about being special. The real thing makes you quieter and kinder, not grander.

**If nothing moves.** That is normal on the first day. The thinking still counts. Keep one sentence for the day: 'That's something I carry, not who I am.' Say it the next time the verdict comes.

- *The first time:* Walk the layers one by one, slowly. Stay a little at the bottom.
- *The third time:* Go through the layers faster and stay longer at the bottom. Bring one hard memory and set it where it belongs, as a layer, without reliving it.
- *The seventh time:* The test is outside the sitting now. This week, did the verdict run your day a little less?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you can say, and mean a little, 'there is a me under what I carry.' And when your worst sentence about yourself can be said without swallowing you. Then look outward: the next sitting asks what is holding up everything else.

**The 3-minute version.** Hands flat on the table. Say the line once. Name one layer you carry today and say, 'That's something I carry.' Say Modeh Ani or a thank-you. Do one good thing.

### Sitting 2 · Being said, right now *F01 The world said into being now · Rung one: the lower unity · claims A10, A03, E09, E15*

**What it's for.** For when the world feels heavy and solid and you feel you are holding it all up. For worry about tomorrow, the 4 a.m. math, the sense that things just happen at random. It gives you the first rung of the ladder: nothing you see is holding itself up.

**The line.** *Everything I see is being said into being right now, and so am I.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 1, line 786: אילו היו מסתלקות ממנה כרגע חס ושלום האותיות מעשרה מאמרות … היתה חוזרת לאין ואפס ממש — *If the letters of the Ten Sayings were to leave it for one instant, God forbid, it would go back to absolute nothing.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Start with the ceiling. Then the cup, the window, the street, a tree, the sky. The Baal Shem Tov read the verse 'Forever, God, Your word stands in the heavens' in the present tense. The words 'Let there be a sky' are standing in the sky right now, saying it. Go object by object. The Tanya adds: stone and dust too, the most lifeless things.
2. **Length.** Your chair was built in a factory years ago. But the chair being here at all, this second, is not the factory's doing. If the saying stopped, the chair wouldn't fall or crack. It simply wouldn't be. Hold up your own hand. Same thing. It is being said.
3. **Depth.** Not 'was made' but 'is being made.' Nothing in this room is holding itself up. That includes you, and it includes every one of the things you worry about.
4. **Into your life.** Name the thing you are carrying: the bills, the child, the job, the diagnosis. It is being said this second too, by the One who is saying you. You are not the floor it stands on.
5. **Know which rung this is.** Sefer HaArachim says plainly that 'created every moment' is the lower unity, the first rung. It is true and it is not the top. Hold it as a door. The next sitting shows what is above it.

**The picture.** When we speak, the words leave our mouths and go off on their own. His word never leaves Him (Tanya 21). So nothing that He says is ever outside Him. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. He has no mouth and no voice. And don't picture the world as His body with Him inside it like a soul. The Tanya refuses that picture, because a soul and a body really are two things at their root, and He and the world are not.

**From inside.** Being held instead of holding. The shoulders of the mind come down a little. Things can look new for a moment, as if you had just walked into the room. The worry is still there, but it isn't standing on you anymore. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "Right now You're saying this table, this room, and me. I don't have to hold it all up. Help me act like it today."

- *For a Jew:* 'Ein od milvado,' there is nothing besides Him. In the morning, mean the words of the blessing before the Shema: He renews in His goodness, every day, always, the work of creation.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'Forever, Lord, Your word stands in the heavens' (Psalm 119:89).
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'Nothing I can see is holding itself up. If Someone is holding it, I'm listening.'

**The act for today.** On your way out, touch the mezuzah with the line in mind. (Not Jewish: say the line quietly at the door.) Once today, at a moment of worry, look at one plain object and say, 'Being said.'

- Worry has a little less grip, especially at night. - 'Being said' comes back by itself in the middle of the day. - Less sense that everything depends on you. - Plain things (water, a chair, a morning) start to look given.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** 'Nothing is real, so nothing matters.' That is the opposite of this sitting. The world is really there, and it is really being given. A dreamy, spaced-out feeling that things are unreal is not this either. If things start to feel unreal or frightening, stop, and go back to the first sitting or to something plain and physical, like washing a dish.

**If nothing moves.** Pick one object and stay with that one only. Say the line aloud more slowly. It still counts.

- *The first time:* Object by object, in the room you are in.
- *The third time:* Go outside in your mind: the street, the people passing, the traffic, each being said. Stay longer on 'if it stopped, it wouldn't be.'
- *The seventh time:* The test is the day. At the moment of worry this week, did 'being said' come back by itself?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you can look at one object and 'being said' is almost as plain as its color. And when a question starts to itch: if it's all Him, why does it look so completely like itself? That question is the next rung.

**The 3-minute version.** Look at one object: 'Being said, right now.' Look at your hand: 'Me too.' One sentence to Him. Touch the mezuzah, or say the line at the door.

### Sitting 3 · Two ways of knowing *F02 The higher and the lower unity · Rungs one and two, held together · claims A02, A03, A12, E17*

**What it's for.** For when 'God is everything' makes you feel you don't count, or that the world is a dream. And for the opposite, when the world is so real that God feels like a nice idea. This sitting keeps both. It is the second rung, and it keeps the first rung from turning into the whole story.

**The line.** *From His side there is only Him; from my side the world is really here, and He fills it.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 20, line 1290: אבל בידיעה שממטה למעלה – היש הנברא הוא דבר נפרד לגמרי — *But in the knowing from below upward, the created thing is a completely separate thing.*</small>

1. **Breadth, from below.** Your kitchen is real. Your bills are real. Other people are really themselves, with their own will and their own choices. The Alter Rebbe says so outright: seen from below, a created thing really is a separate thing.
2. **Breadth, from above.** Now look from His side, 'before Him.' A ray of sunlight on your floor is called light. The same light inside the sun isn't called anything. It's just the sun. From there the world adds nothing and takes up no room.
3. **Length.** The Shema does exactly this, every morning and night. Cover your eyes. 'Hear, Israel, God is our God, God is One.' Draw out 'One.' On its first letter, the ches, think that He is King in the seven heavens and on the earth. On its last letter, the dalet, think that He alone is in His world, in all four directions. Everything folds in. Then quietly: 'Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever.' And the world comes back, real, full of Him, His.
4. **Depth.** Both are true at once. Neither swallows the other. And the lower is there for the sake of the higher.
5. **Into your life.** Someone who hurt you is really a separate person, seen from below, and the wrong they did is really a wrong. Seen from above, nothing stands outside Him, this included. You don't have to choose one view and throw the other away.

**The picture.** Heart above, eyes below. Sefer HaArachim says that in prayer the heart must be above and the eyes below. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. These are not two places, and He is not 'up.' They are two ways of knowing one truth.

**From inside.** First a lightness, as the world grows thin inside the 'One.' Then the world coming back full, like walking back into your house after a long walk and seeing that it is all still here, and all yours. Not dreamy. If anything, clearer. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "From where You are, there's only You. From where I am, there's this whole life. Let me hold both today and not drop either one."

- *For a Jew:* The Shema's first verse, aloud, eyes covered, 'One' drawn out. Then 'Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed' quietly.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'The Lord has set His throne in the heavens, and His kingdom rules over all' (Psalm 103:19).
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'Maybe from the widest view all this is one. From here it's many. I'll try to keep both in mind today.'

**The act for today.** The bedtime Shema, with these two thoughts. (Not Jewish: the verse above and a thank-you before sleep.) And once today, in a disagreement, remember that the other person is really separate and really held.

- Less swinging between 'nothing matters' and 'everything is on me.' - You can take the world seriously without it being all there is. - A disagreement feels less like a war.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** 'It's all God, so my choices don't matter, and right and wrong melt away.' Sefer HaArachim calls that Korach's error: seeing a real unity above all lines and using it to erase the lines God drew. The other fake is keeping only the high view and treating the world as a dream. The real thing makes the world more solid and more His at once.

**If nothing moves.** Do only the Shema part, once, slowly, with the two thoughts. Let that be the whole sitting.

- *The first time:* The two looks, one after the other.
- *The third time:* Switch between them faster, then hold both together for a few seconds without choosing.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the day. In a conflict or a chore this week, could you hold both?

**Ready for the next rung?** When 'He fills the world' and 'before Him it counts for nothing' stop feeling like a contradiction you must solve, and start feeling like two windows onto one house.

**The 3-minute version.** Cover your eyes. The Shema's 'One,' drawn out (or Psalm 103:19). Then 'Blessed be the name…' and open your eyes on the room. One sentence: 'From Your side, only You. From mine, this life, full of You.'

### Sitting 4 · Held from inside and all around *F04 Filling and surrounding · Rung one widened: held from inside and all around · claims J01, B11, B12*

**What it's for.** For fear, anxiety and dread. For feeling exposed, small and alone in a big world. For the thing you keep picturing going wrong.

**The line.** *He fills each thing to its own size, and surrounds all things the same.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7 (quoting the Zohar), line 839: איהו סובב כל עלמין כו׳, ולית מאן דנפיק מרשותיה לבר, איהו ממלא כל עלמין — *He surrounds all worlds… and no one leaves His domain… He fills all worlds.*</small>

1. **Breadth, filling.** The life in a leaf is leaf-sized. The life in a dog is dog-sized. The life in you is you-sized. The Tanya says the life in each thing is contracted to fit it, to its measure.
2. **Breadth, surrounding.** Then there is the light that no thing can hold. It isn't 'up above.' The Tanya says surrounding has nothing to do with place. It is around the near and the far the same: around the thing you fear, and around you.
3. **Length.** Take one particular thing, not 'all worlds.' The Rashab warns that 'He fills and surrounds all worlds' said in general only floats over the mind. Take the mug in your hand. The life in it is mug-sized: its being here. And around it, holding it and you equally, is the light that fits nowhere. Look at how the mug lives from that.
4. **Depth.** Nothing you fear stands outside this. It is in the same hold you are.
5. **Into your life.** Name the thing you dread. Put it inside the picture. It too is filled to its size and surrounded. It doesn't hold you. You are both held.

**The picture.** Think of something in your mind, a house, say. Your thought surrounds it. The Tanya uses this picture for 'surrounding.' *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. Our thought surrounds only the idea of the house. His knowing surrounds the thing itself and is its very being. And He has no inside and no outside.

**From inside.** Being carried, with no edge. The fear doesn't always leave, but it gets smaller than what holds it. There is a kind of floor that goes all the way down. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "The thing I'm scared of is in Your hands too. So am I. Let me do the next right thing."

- *For a Jew:* 'Shivisi Hashem lenegdi tamid,' I have set God before me always. The Alter Rebbe calls it a great principle of the Torah.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'I have set the Lord before me always; because He is at my right hand, I will not be shaken' (Psalm 16:8).
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'If something holds all of this, it holds what scares me too.'

**The act for today.** Say the verse with your lips right before the thing you fear: the call, the appointment, the email, the drive.

- The fear comes, but now it has something bigger around it. - You do the feared thing anyway, and a little sooner. - Night thoughts loop a little less.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** A cozy, vague 'everything is surrounded by light' feeling. The Rashab calls general contemplation like that a false imagination. Or 'nothing bad can happen to me now,' which no one promised. The real thing is particular (this mug, this call) and it makes you able to act.

**If nothing moves.** Stay with one small object and its 'filled to its size.' That is enough for today.

- *The first time:* One object, filled and surrounded.
- *The third time:* The feared thing itself, filled and surrounded. Less explaining, longer staying.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the day. This week, did you say the verse before the fear, and act?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you can hold the feared thing inside the picture without the picture breaking. And when the question comes: if He fills everything, why does it so often feel like He isn't here?

**The 3-minute version.** Pick up one object: 'Filled to its size.' Think of the feared thing: 'Surrounded, the same as me.' Say 'I have set God before me always.' Go and do the next thing.

<small>**Also behind this sitting.** Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7, line 842: היא בחינת החיות המתלבשת תוך עצם הנברא, שהיא מצומצמת בתוכו בצמצום רב — *['Filling'] is the life clothed inside the creature itself, contracted inside it very much [to its measure].*<br>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 48, line 701: ואין הפירוש סובב ומקיף מלמעלה בבחינת מקום — *'Surrounding' does not mean above, in the sense of place.*<br>Kuntres HaAvodah (the Rashab), scan p. 38 (OCR), line 204: ואין זה התפעלות אמיתי כי אם דמיון כוזב — *[General contemplation of filling and surrounding] is not true arousal but a false imagination.*<br>Kuntres HaAvodah (the Rashab), scan p. 23 (OCR), line 129: נרגש ונראה אצלו איך שהעולם חי מאור האלקי — *It is felt and seen by him how the world lives from the Godly light.*<br>Shulchan Arukh HaRav, Orach Chayim 1:5, line 71: שויתי ה' לנגדי תמיד — *I have set God before me always.*</small>

### Sitting 5 · The hiding is Him *F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · Why the first rungs look like nothing: the hiding · claims E18, E12, A03*

**What it's for.** For when God feels absent. For when life seems to run by itself: the market, the doctors, the traffic, the rules of nature. For the hard thing that seems to prove nobody is there.

**Before you start.** If you are grieving or in real pain, don't use this sitting to explain it. Use it only to know you are not alone in it.

**The line.** *The hiding is Him hiding, and He hides out of love.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 4, line 809: הן הן גבורותיו של הקדוש ברוך הוא, אשר כל יכול, לצמצם החיות והרוחניות — *These are the very mighty acts of the Holy One, who can do anything: to contract the life and the spirit [and hide it].*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Nature runs by rules. Your job runs by the market. Your body runs by biology. Each one looks like it runs itself. The Alter Rebbe says the hiding is not His absence. It is His power, one of His mighty acts. It takes more power to hide than to shine.
2. **Length.** Find the part of your day that feels emptiest of Him: the commute, the spreadsheet, the waiting room, the dishes. There, the life is hidden inside the thing, so that the thing seems to stand on its own.
3. **Depth.** Why hide? The Tanya's answer: He set His great light aside and hid it, all for love of the lowly human being, to lift him up. The hiding makes room for a 'you' out here who can find Him.
4. **Into your life.** The hard thing. This sitting does not say what your trouble is for. Nobody can tell you that. It says only this: the hiding in that place is His hiding. And the Baal Shem Tov says once you know He is hiding there, it is no hiding.

**The picture.** A great king comes down from his palace to a man lying in the dust, lifts him up, and brings him inside, room after room (Tanya 46). The distance was covered from His side. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. It shows that the hiding is His and is for us. It does not explain any particular pain, and it does not say He sends pain to teach a lesson.

**From inside.** Finding what was never gone. A quiet 'oh, You were here.' The Tanya describes the heart answering back, the way a face answers its reflection in water. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "I can't see You in this. Tell me You're hiding here and not gone. I'll keep looking."

- *For a Jew:* 'Hidden, not gone.' Then a blessing said slowly over something ordinary, like water: 'shehakol nihyeh bidvaro,' that all came to be by His word.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'Truly, You are a God who hides Himself' (Isaiah 45:15). Then a thank-you to Him before you eat or drink.
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'If this is a hiding and not an emptiness, I'd like to see behind it.'

**The act for today.** Say a blessing slowly over the thing that seemed emptiest of Him today. (Not Jewish: a thank-you to Him before eating.)

- Dry stretches feel less like being abandoned. - You start to look for Him in ordinary places without being told to. - 'Why is this happening?' loosens its grip a little, even without an answer.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Explaining your suffering: 'He's hiding to teach me X.' That is a reading a person may someday find about his own life. It is not the point of this sitting, and never hand it to someone else. The other fake is painting a bright 'He's here!' over real pain. The real thing sees the cover and stays honest about how dark it is.

**If nothing moves.** Carry three words for the day: 'Hidden, not gone.' Say them at the empty spot. That is the sitting.

- *The first time:* Ordinary empty spots in the day.
- *The third time:* One harder spot. Stay with 'His power, His hiding' longer, and explain less.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the day. Did an empty stretch this week feel a little less empty?

**Ready for the next rung?** When the empty place begins to look like a covered place. And when you notice the last thing in the room that still seems to stand on its own is you.

**The 3-minute version.** Name the emptiest spot of today. Say, 'Hidden, not gone. His hiding, out of love.' Say one blessing slowly.

<small>**Also behind this sitting.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 49, line 714: והכל בשביל אהבת האדם התחתון — *And all of it for love of the lowly human being.*<br>Keter Shem Tov 1:55 (the Baal Shem Tov), line 93: אחר שידע אדם שהקב"ה מסתתר שם אין זה הסתרה — *Once a person knows that the Holy One is hiding there, it is no hiding.*</small>

**From Samach Vav** *(a note added 2026-09-29; the sitting above is unchanged).* The Rebbe Rashab takes this sitting's line one step further. Hiding is not only one of His mighty acts; it is the chief of His powers. And it does not hide Him from Himself: "essence does not hide essence." A hiding He chooses, he says, is no hiding, which is close to the Baal Shem Tov's line above. He tells the Baal Shem Tov's parable of the father who hides to test his child: the wise child runs after him, and the father's love comes out "from darkness to light." He does not use it to explain anyone's pain, and neither does this sitting.

<small>Hemshech Samach Vav (the Rashab), Adam Ki Yakriv, line 2503: ואדרבה, זהו עיקר בחי' הכח והגבורה להעלים ולהסתיר — *On the contrary, this is the chief power and might: to conceal and to hide.*<br>Ki Tavo'u el HaAretz, line 2891: ועצם אינו מסתיר על עצם — *And essence does not hide essence.*<br>Panim BePanim, line 5684: והסתר רצוני אינו הסתר — *And a willed hiding is no hiding.*<br>HaShamayim Mesaprim, line 3261: ועתה נתגלית מן חשך לאור — *And now it is revealed, from darkness to light.*</small>

### Sitting 6 · Not a thing on my own *F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · Rung two, from the person's side · claims B05, B07, B08, E17, A07*

**What it's for.** For touchiness, pride, being easily insulted, needing to be right, and the tiring job of defending yourself all day. Once the world is seen as held, the last thing in the room that still seems to stand on its own is the self. This sitting lets it rest.

**Before you start.** Not for a low day. If you already feel like nothing, skip this sitting and do the first one instead. Bitul is for the self that is too big, never for the self that has been crushed.

**The line.** *I'm not a thing on my own; I'm like a ray still inside the sun.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 22, line 323: אין קדושה עליונה שורה אלא על מה שבטל לו יתברך — *The higher holiness rests only on what is nullified to Him.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Sunlight on your floor is called light. The same light still inside the sun isn't called anything. It is just the sun. The Tanya says every creature stands before its source like that. And it says where separation starts: in taking yourself as a thing on your own.
2. **Length.** Think of the last time you were insulted. Where did it sting? In the 'me' that stands up by itself and needs to be respected. Now think of your hand. The moment you want to move it, it moves, with no argument. The Tanya uses that picture for a self given over to Him.
3. **Depth.** This is not 'I am worthless.' The ray is real light, the very same light as the sun. Bitul is not being less. It is not being separate.
4. **Into your life.** Name one place where you defend yourself all day: at work, in the family, in your own head. Picture it with nothing to defend. What would you do there if you didn't need to win?

**The picture.** A ray still inside the sun (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud 3 and 10). It has nothing of its own apart from the sun, and it is fully light. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. You are not dissolved. You still choose, act and answer for what you do. And He is not a sun. The picture says one thing only: the ray has nothing apart from its source.

**From inside.** Lightness, with nothing to defend. An insult lands on less surface. Often you don't notice this while it happens. The Mitteler Rebbe says the deepest states are the least felt, and you find out afterward that you were calmer than usual. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "What I am comes from You. I don't need to guard it. Let me be light and useful today."

- *For a Jew:* 'Ein od milvado,' there is nothing besides Him (Deuteronomy 4:35).
- *For someone of another faith:* 'Not to us, Lord, not to us, but to Your name give glory' (Psalm 115:1).
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'Maybe I don't have to hold myself up all the time.'

**The act for today.** One mitzvah, or one kindness, that no one will ever know about.

- An insult stings a little less. The Mitteler Rebbe gives this as the real sign: it doesn't bother him, because he has nothing of his own. - You need less to be seen and credited. - It is easier to say 'I was wrong.'

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Two fakes, and they are opposites. The first is being proud of your bitul ('I'm so humble now'). The Mitteler Rebbe says bitul wanted as a prize turns into its opposite, and its sign is feeling yourself afterward. The second is self-hatred dressed up as humility ('I'm nothing'). That is not bitul. It is the verdict from the first sitting wearing religious clothes. If it shows up, stop and go back to the first sitting.

**If nothing moves.** Good. Bitul is not a feeling. Do the hidden act. That is the sitting.

- *The first time:* The ray inside the sun, and the hand that moves at once.
- *The third time:* The insult, looked at calmly from the ray's side. Shorter picture, longer staying.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Was there an insult or a slight that didn't sting as much as it would have?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you stop checking whether you are humble yet. And when you notice you know all this and still act the old way. That gap is what the next sitting is for.

**The 3-minute version.** Say, 'A ray still inside the sun.' Bring to mind one place you defend yourself, and set it down for a minute. Do one hidden good act today.

### Sitting 7 · Know it today, bring it to the heart *F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · The hinge: from understanding to 'it is so' · claims E01, E02, E03, E11, J01*

**What it's for.** For 'I understand it, but nothing changes.' For a heart that feels nothing while the head agrees. It is the hinge of the ladder. The first six rungs can be understood. This one binds them until they are simply so, and it shows where the mind stops and faith carries on, which is what the higher rungs need.

**The line.** *He is here, and I'll hold that until it's as plain as what I see.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 1 (Deuteronomy 4:39), line 784: וידעת היום והשבות אל לבבך — *Know today, and return it to your heart.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Look back over the ladder so far: the spark, the saying, the two knowings, filling and surrounding, the hiding, the ray. Pick the one that touched you most. That is today's object. Nothing new.
2. **The question.** The Alter Rebbe asks why the verse has to say 'and return it to your heart.' Would anyone think there is a god in the water under the earth? His answer is the whole sitting. The mind can know a thing while the heart goes on seeing a world that stands by itself.
3. **Length.** Knowing, da'at, the Tanya says, is binding: joining the mind to a thing and not letting go. Look at your coffee cup. See how plain it is that it's there. You don't have to believe in the cup. Now take your line, 'He is here,' or the rung you picked. Hold it in the same way, with the same plainness. When the mind drifts, come back without arguing.
4. **Depth.** At some point the mind runs out. How He knows everything and stays simply One, the Alter Rebbe says, can't be grasped, only believed, with a faith above the mind. Don't push the mind past its edge. Let faith hold what the mind can't.
5. **Into your life.** Choose one fixed moment in your day, the kettle, a doorway, a red light, and come back to the line there, lightly. The Tanya says a long sitting once a day lets even a light thought at any hour bring it back.

**The picture.** A face in water. The Tanya says love shown to a person wakes love in his heart, the way water gives back the face that looks into it. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. The heart doesn't answer on command. The picture says only that love shown wakes love. It may come later, or quietly.

**From inside.** The move from 'I understand' to 'it is so.' It is often quiet. The line stops being an idea you hold and becomes a fact you see. Some people say it feels less like learning and more like remembering. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "I know You're here. I want to know it the way I know this cup is here. Where my mind stops, I'll trust."

- *For a Jew:* 'Veyadata hayom vahasheivosa el levavecha,' know today and return it to your heart (Deuteronomy 4:39). And 'Shivisi Hashem lenegdi tamid.'
- *For someone of another faith:* 'I have set the Lord before me always' (Psalm 16:8).
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'I'll hold the thought. If it's true, I'd like it to reach my heart.'

**The act for today.** Set Him before you at your one fixed moment today. Just the line, once.

- The line comes back by itself. - It is harder to act as if He isn't there. - You understand less and know more.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Forcing a feeling: 'I must feel it now.' The Tanya warns that without binding you get only vain imaginings, and the Rashab calls feeling squeezed out by force empty imagination. The other fake is collecting insights instead of binding one. Knowledge that is never taken in turns into self.

**If nothing moves.** This is exactly the sitting for that. The binding counts even when the heart is slow. Some hearts answer days later.

- *The first time:* The cup, then the line, with the same plainness.
- *The third time:* Explain less. Stay longer. Go back to the line faster when the mind drifts.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the day. Did the fixed moment happen by itself this week?

**Ready for the next rung?** When 'He is here' has become, even a little, a fact you see and not an idea you hold. Then you are ready for rungs the mind can't hold at all, which you only glimpse.

**The 3-minute version.** Look at one plain thing and say, 'It's there.' Say, 'He is here,' in the same voice. Keep your one fixed moment today.

### Sitting 8 · What can't meet in me meets in Him *F09 The unity of opposites · Above the mind: opposites held in Him · claims A12, B09, E16, J04*

**What it's for.** For a split heart: grief and gratitude, love and anger at the same person, guilt and hope, wanting and not wanting. For when two people you love are fighting. For feeling close to Him and far from Him at once. It is the first rung above the mind, and it teaches you not to panic when a thing won't come out even.

**The line.** *What can't meet in me meets in Him.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 34 (quoting the Zohar), line 492: בכיה תקיעא בלבאי מסטרא דא, וחדוה תקיעא בלבאי מסטרא דא — *Weeping is lodged in my heart on this side, and joy is lodged in my heart on that side.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Fire and water. Love and fear. Kindness and strictness. In created things they cancel each other. The Rashab says the perfection of His Essence carries all opposites. They sit together in Him because He isn't any one of them.
2. **Length.** Name your two feelings that won't fit together. Say both, plainly. Don't solve them. The Tanya says a person can be broken-hearted and glad in the very same moment: weeping lodged on one side of the heart and joy on the other.
3. **Depth.** You don't have to become one simple feeling to be whole. The oneness is in Him, not in squeezing yourself into one shape. The Mitteler Rebbe even says the weeping over being far and the joy of being near are one movement of the same soul.
4. **Into your life.** Two people, two duties, two truths pulling at you. Hold both toward Him. But notice what this does not mean. Opposites meeting in Him does not make right and wrong melt. In how you act, they stay opposites. Sefer HaArachim: the unity does not cancel the lines God drew.

**The picture.** One heart with two sides, weeping lodged on one and joy on the other (the Zohar, in Tanya 34). *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. The heart isn't really two rooms, and He isn't a bigger heart. The picture shows only that both can be true at once.

**From inside.** A heart wide enough for both. The fight between the feelings stops, even when the feelings stay. The tears and the gladness turn out to be the same soul moving. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "Say both to Him, in your own words, for a few minutes. For example: 'I'm so angry at him, and I love him. I'm grateful and I'm grieving. I can't make these fit. You can hold them. Hold me with them.'"

- *For a Jew:* Both, to Him, in your own words. Then: 'Min hameitzar karasi Kah, anani vamerchav Kah,' from the narrow place I called to God, and He answered me with wide space (Psalm 118:5).
- *For someone of another faith:* 'Out of my distress I called on the Lord; the Lord answered me and set me in a broad place' (Psalm 118:5). Then both, in your own words.
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'I feel two things that can't both be true. If there's a place where they can, I'd like to rest there.'

**The act for today.** Pour out both to Him in your own words for a few minutes today. R. Nachman says there is no bitul without it.

- You need less to settle your feelings before you can act. - More patience with other people's mixtures. - Fewer either/or decisions made in a panic.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** 'It's all one, so nothing is really wrong.' That is Korach's error. The other fake is numbing both feelings and calling it peace. The real thing holds both awake.

**If nothing moves.** Just say the two feelings aloud to Him, one sentence each. That is the sitting.

- *The first time:* Name the two, and give them both to Him.
- *The third time:* A harder pair, such as anger and love for one person. Stay longer with 'both, in Him.'
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Was there a moment you held both without forcing a choice?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you can hold two opposites without panic. Then the next rungs, where the mind stops altogether, won't frighten you.

**The 3-minute version.** Name the two things. Say, 'Both, in You.' Tell Him each one in a single sentence.

### Sitting 9 · One, and alone *F05 The Essence beyond both (echad and yachid) · Rung three: 'alone', and the Shema's 'One' · claims A04, A06, A08, A11, A09*

**What it's for.** For when you want to go higher. For when life on the first rung has gone flat, or quietly turned back into being about you. Sefer HaArachim says everyone, not only the lofty, should reach now and then for a little of the higher unity, even in imagination, and that without it the lower is not true. This sitting also guards the reach, so that it comes back holding the world and not pushing it away.

**Before you start.** If you start to feel unreal, scared, or far from your own life, stop. Go back to a lower rung (the second or the thirteenth sitting) and do something plain with your hands.

**The line.** *Before Him as He is, He is alone; and the Shema still calls Him One, with the whole world held.*

> <small>**Source.** The Gate of Unity 10:12-13 (the Mitteler Rebbe), line 494: והוא למעלה מהתאחדות נפרדים שנקרא אחד — *And it ['yachid,' alone] is above the joining of separate things, which is called 'echad,' one.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** 'One' can mean a sum: one family, one team, one pile of laundry. That is many things joined. 'Alone,' yachid, is different. It isn't the first number in a count. It is just alone, with nothing beside it. Imrei Binah: like the single one that isn't in the count at all. The Mitteler Rebbe says that before God as He is in Himself, this is the word: alone, above any joining of separate things.
2. **Length, reaching a little.** For one minute, let the whole room, the city, the stars count for nothing. Not destroyed. Just not counting, the way numbers don't apply to something that isn't a number. Sefer HaArachim says every person should reach for this a little, a tiny bit, even if only in imagination.
3. **Then come back.** The Shema doesn't say 'God is alone.' It says 'God is One,' and Sefer HaArachim says it does so on purpose. Its news is that the heavens and the earth and all four directions, many and even opposite, are one with Him while they stand. Imrei Binah goes as far as to say there is no difference at all between 'alone' and 'one.' So you come back into the room holding it, not pushing it away.
4. **Depth.** Even 'alone' is our word. R. Aharon of Strashelye says it too is said only from our side. No word catches Him. Say less.
5. **Into your life.** Come back into your room. Touch the table. It is still here, and it is one with Him.

**The picture.** 'One' said before counting starts, the single one that is not part of any number (Imrei Binah). *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. He isn't a number at all, not even the first. The picture only points past counting.

**From inside.** A brief stillness where there is nothing to do and nobody to be. Then coming back and finding the room still here, lighter, and yours. Many describe it as a hush more than a feeling. Often it isn't felt at all while it happens. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "You're more than I can say. Even 'alone' is too small for You. And You made this whole world one with You. Let me come back into it holding it gently."

- *For a Jew:* The Shema's 'Echad,' drawn out, with the thought that everything is one with Him, held and not pushed aside.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'On that day the Lord will be One, and His name One' (Zechariah 14:9).
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'Whatever is at the bottom of everything, I think it's one. I'd like to know it.'

**The act for today.** Right after the sitting, do one ordinary thing, such as washing a dish, slowly, as the world held and not pushed aside.

- A taste of quiet above the usual noise. - Coming back to people more gently, not more coldly. - Holding things a little more loosely.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Three fakes. Thinking you've reached the top, when even the word is ours. A cold detachment, 'the world doesn't count, so people don't matter,' when the Shema says 'One,' with the world held. And a spacey, unreal feeling. The real reach comes back warmer to the world, not further from it.

**If nothing moves.** Expected. The top rungs are not felt. The reach counts, even in imagination. Sefer HaArachim says so.

- *The first time:* A short reach, a careful return.
- *The third time:* Reach a little longer. Come back more slowly, touching each thing as you return.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Did 'held, not pushed aside' change how you treated one plain thing or one person?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you come back from the reach more at home in the world, not less. Then the next sitting shows where the highest is actually found: in the plainest thing.

**The 3-minute version.** Say, 'Alone, before Him.' Then, 'One, with all this.' Cover your eyes for 'Echad' (or Zechariah 14:9). Open your eyes and put a hand on the table.

**From Samach Vav** *(a note added 2026-09-29; the sitting above is unchanged).* The Rebbe Rashab sharpens both halves. ### Sitting 10 · The plainest thing *F05 The Essence beyond both · The Essence, found in the plainest thing · claims A09, A11, B14*

**What it's for.** For when spiritual life has become chasing highs. For when you think He is only in special moments. For when ordinary things feel empty. And for anyone who wants Him as He is: you don't reach Him by climbing higher. You find Him in the plainest thing.

**The line.** *Only He simply is, so the plainest thing points straight to Him.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 20, line 1295: ולכן הוא לבדו בכחו ויכלתו לברוא יש מאין ואפס המוחלט ממש — *Therefore He alone, by His power and ability, can create something out of absolute nothing.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Lights, levels, insights, uplifted moments: all of these are His radiance. But bread doesn't feel like radiance at all. It is just there. The Alter Rebbe says only He, whose being is from Himself, can make a 'just there' out of absolute nothing.
2. **Length.** Hold a piece of bread, or a cup of water. It isn't spiritual. It doesn't glow. It is just here. That plain being-here is His fingerprint, not anyone else's. The Alter Rebbe once said he prayed 'with the lectern': the physical comes from the Essence.
3. **Depth.** The highest is found in the lowest. Sefer HaArachim says the truest unity is not the world erased. It is the world's own existence, one with Him. Only the Essence, which carries opposites, can do that.
4. **Into your life.** Don't try to picture the Essence. The Rashab warns against reaching for it with the mind. The main work now is with what is near. Go through the plain things: your shoes, your front door, the bread. You don't reach Him here. You are found by Him here.

**The picture.** Chosen, not reached. Like being loved, not for anything you did, just because you are you. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. He doesn't choose the way we do, from options in front of Him. The picture shows only that this is given, not earned.

**From inside.** Being chosen. A plain, solid peace. Nothing special is happening, and that is the point. Some people feel their need for a high quietly let go. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "I don't have to climb to You. You're here in the bread. Thank You for this plain day."

- *For a Jew:* The blessing over bread, slowly: 'hamotzi lechem min ha'aretz,' who brings forth bread from the earth.
- *For someone of another faith:* A thank-you to Him before eating, in your own words.
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'This is simply here. I didn't make it be. Thank you.'

**The act for today.** Eat one thing slowly, with its blessing (or your thank-you).

- Less craving for spiritual highs. - Plain duties feel less like a waste of time. - A steadiness that doesn't depend on mood.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Turning the plain thing into a new high: 'The bread is glowing!' That is excitement again, not the Essence. Thinking you have grasped the Essence. Or 'everything is holy, so I can indulge.' The real thing is plain and makes you plainer.

**If nothing moves.** Exactly right. This rung isn't felt as a feeling. The blessing is the sitting.

- *The first time:* Bread, or water.
- *The third time:* Something you dislike: your desk, the laundry pile. Just there. Only He simply is.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Did plain tasks feel a little less empty?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you stop needing the sitting to feel special. Then turn the same plain look inward, to the bottom of yourself.

**The 3-minute version.** Hold bread or a cup of water. Say, 'Just here. Only He simply is.' Say the blessing slowly, and eat or drink.

**From Samach Vav** *(a note added 2026-09-29; the sitting above is unchanged).* The Rebbe Rashab says why the plainest thing points to Him. From all the light, the levels and the insights, we know nothing of Him as He is. From creation out of nothing we know one thing only: that His being is from Himself, which is this sitting's line. And he finds "there is none else," the highest rung, precisely in the making of a physical "something." His warning matches the one above: the work is not to contemplate Him as He is.

### Sitting 11 · Where you and He meet *F06 The soul as part of the One (its essence, the yechidah) · The bottom of the self, at the Essence · claims B01, B02, Y04, B06*

**What it's for.** For the question 'who am I, really?' answered at its deepest. For feeling lost to yourself. It closes the circle the first sitting opened: the bottom of the self turns out to be where He is. Finding yourself and finding Him are one finding.

**Before you start.** If you feel a special mission, or you are too excited to sleep, or your thoughts are racing and your speech is getting grand, stop the ladder and talk to a person you trust today.

**The line.** *At the very bottom of me, I and He are joined.*

> <small>**Source.** Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus 11:5 (the Rebbe), line 64: מצד היחידה שבנשמה שמיוחדת תמיד בעצמותו ית' — *From the side of the yechidah in the soul, which is always united with His Essence.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** Go back down the layers from the first sitting: what you do, what you feel, what you believe, what you want. Then the spark under them, reaching home. Now one step further. Under the spark's reaching, the Rebbe says, is the yechidah, 'the single one' of the soul, and it is always united with His Essence. Always. Not after you get better. Now.
2. **Length.** This is why simple things reach so deep. The Alter Rebbe, as Hayom Yom records, says prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul with the Essence of the Infinite. Not learning, not a feeling. A plain 'I'm Yours,' said by anyone.
3. **Depth.** The Mitteler Rebbe says the soul, in itself, is literally a Godly power, and that clinging to God is not something you produce. It is the soul's own life. You don't build this bond. You uncover it.
4. **Into your life.** Think of a time you did something good for no reason. You just couldn't have done otherwise. That 'couldn't do otherwise' is this point showing through.

**The picture.** The name itself: yechidah, 'the single one,' the same root as His name Yachid, alone. Or a child who is his parent's child without doing anything to earn it. *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. You don't become God. You and He are joined; you are not the same thing, and you never stop being the one who answers for your life.

**From inside.** Rest. Not arriving somewhere new, but being home. A simplicity with nothing to add. The Mitteler Rebbe says the deeper it is, the less it is felt, so many people only notice it afterward, in a calm they can't explain. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "Under everything, I'm Yours, and You're here. I don't have to earn this. I just want to live from it."

- *For a Jew:* 'Elokai, neshamah shenasata bi tehorah hi,' my God, the soul You placed in me is pure. Then 'Modeh Ani' again, meaning more by it now.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'My soul thirsts for God, for the living God' (Psalm 42:3). Then a plain 'I'm Yours' in your own words.
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'If there's a place in me that's joined to what's most real, I'd like to live from there.'

**The act for today.** One act of simple faith: a short prayer in plain words, or a mitzvah or kindness done simply, without explaining it to yourself.

- Less need to 'find yourself.' - Less watching yourself. - Decisions come from a quieter place, and you are a little more your own master.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** 'I'm one with God' turning into grandness, or a sense of a special mission. The real thing is humble and plain. The other fake is watching yourself for signs of the yechidah, which puts the self right back in the middle.

**If nothing moves.** This point isn't felt. It is lived. The simple act is the sitting.

- *The first time:* Down the layers to the bottom.
- *The third time:* Go down faster and stay longer at the bottom, in plain, simple words.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Did you act once from simplicity, without a speech to yourself?

**Ready for the next rung?** When 'who am I?' has a quiet answer you don't need to say out loud. Now the ladder turns and goes back down, starting with the hardest place in you.

**The 3-minute version.** Say, 'Under everything, I'm Yours.' Say a short prayer in your own words. Do one simple act.

### Sitting 12 · Darkness turned to light *F11 Darkness turned to light · Coming down: the dark place · claims B09, B10, B17*

**What it's for.** For a habit or pull you keep falling into: the late-night snacking, the scrolling, the sharp word, a bitterness you can't put down. For the parts of you that you're ashamed of. It is the first step back down the ladder, and it starts where it's hardest.

**Before you start.** Not for crushing shame. If you feel bad at the core, start with the first sitting, and come here later.

**The line.** *The dark I turn becomes the brightest light.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 33, line 480: דאתכפיא סטרא אחרא ממש ואתהפך חשוכא לנהורא — *The other side is truly subdued, and darkness is turned to light.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** The unity isn't shown by never having darkness. It is shown by turning it. The Tanya says that each time the pull is refused, the other side is truly subdued, and darkness is turned to light. The crowded public street of the world becomes a private house of the One.
2. **Length.** Find the exact moment: the hand reaching for the phone, the mouth opening to say the thing. Right there is the smallest pause. That pause is the place where the dark turns. Don't argue with the pull or wrestle it in your head. The Tanya says to act as if you don't hear it and turn back to what you were doing. The Piaseczner adds that whatever you push against grows stronger.
3. **Depth.** Imrei Binah says true teshuvah makes good out of the evil itself. The dark place is where He is shown most, and He is shown through you.
4. **Into your life.** One pull, one small 'no' today. Or a 'yes' of a different kind: take the heat of the anger and put it into a good act, done hard.

**The picture.** The light that comes out of darkness shines more. And the noisy public street that becomes the private house of the One (Tanya 33). *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. Darkness is not needed to make light. Never go looking for it. The picture is for the dark that is already there.

**From inside.** The dignity of the struggle. Not a victory lap. A quiet self-respect. The Mitteler Rebbe says the one who comes low and broken becomes a place for the Source of all life to live in, to revive the spirit of the crushed. Sometimes the craving itself turns into a longing for Him. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "This pull is strong. I'm giving You this one 'no.' Turn it into light."

- *For a Jew:* One small 'no,' told to Him. Then the morning blessing, meant: 'hanosein laya'ef koach,' who gives strength to the weary.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'The Lord is my strength and my song' (Psalm 118:14). Then the 'no,' told to Him.
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'I'd like this struggle to be worth something. Here is one no.'

**The act for today.** One small 'no' today, told to Him in your own words.

- A pull loses a little of its grip. - Falling no longer means quitting. - Less shame, more work.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Pride in the win. The idea 'since darkness turns to light, let me go into the dark,' which is the pull talking. Fighting the thought head-on in your mind. And, on a low day, a crushing self-inspection. That is not this sitting.

**If nothing moves.** Count the 'no,' not the feeling.

- *The first time:* One pull, one 'no.'
- *The third time:* Catch the pull's moment a little earlier.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Was there a pull that had less grip?

**Ready for the next rung?** When you can look at your dark spot without hiding it and without drowning in it. Then look up at the rest of the house.

**The 3-minute version.** Name the pull. Say, 'The dark I turn becomes light.' Give Him one 'no' in your own words.

### Sitting 13 · The home below *F07 The home below · Coming down: the home below · claims A01, A07, E08, E10, B17*

**What it's for.** For 'what's the point?' For an ordinary life that feels empty. For work that has swallowed your life. For feeling that your day doesn't matter.

**The line.** *He wanted a home in the lowest place, and that means here, in my ordinary day.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 33, line 477: אשר אפילו בארץ מתחת – אין עוד מלבדו, וזו היא דירתו בתחתונים — *That even on the earth below there is nothing besides Him, and this is His dwelling in the lower realms.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** The whole chain of worlds, the angels and the heavens, wasn't the goal. The Tanya says the purpose is this lowest world. Not in spite of being low. Because it is low.
2. **Length.** Your kitchen. This Tuesday. The laundry, the office, the car. Each is a room He wanted to live in. The goal isn't that the solid world disappear. R. Aharon of Strashelye says the aim is that bitul show inside the solid thing, precisely there.
3. **Depth.** Imrei Binah: the earth is the end of everything, with nothing below it, and so the power of the very beginning is found in it. The lowest place carries the first power.
4. **Into your life.** Pick one ordinary job you have to do today anyway. Do it as setting up His home.

**The picture.** A king comes to live in a plain man's little house, and the plain man's joy (Tanya 33). *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. He doesn't move from place to place. The Tanya says nothing changes in Him; the change is in us, the ones receiving.

**From inside.** That joy. The plain day stops being filler between the things that matter. Things you do 'for no reason' get a reason. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "You wanted to live here, in this messy place. Come in. Let me make one corner ready today."

- *For a Jew:* 'Ashreinu, mah tov chelkeinu,' how fortunate we are, how good is our portion. The Tanya says this is said for exactly this: even here below there is nothing besides Him.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'This is the day the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it' (Psalm 118:24).
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'If this ordinary day is the point, I want to live it well.'

**The act for today.** A coin in the charity box before breakfast, as the first thing done in His house. (Anyone: a coin given, or a kindness.)

- Less boredom-despair. - Less waiting for life to start. - Small tasks carry a quiet gladness.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** 'Everything I do is holy, so it doesn't matter what I do.' The home is built by deeds done with Him in mind, not by everything as it is. The other fake is escaping upward: spending your time on 'spiritual' things and neglecting the people and the house right in front of you.

**If nothing moves.** Give the coin. The home is being built whether you feel it or not.

- *The first time:* Room by room through your real home.
- *The third time:* The hardest room: the office, the argument, the hospital corridor.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Did one ordinary task feel like it mattered?

**Ready for the next rung?** When the next thing to do is obvious, and it is a deed.

**The 3-minute version.** Look around and say, 'A room He wanted.' Put a coin in the box. Do the next plain task for Him.

<small>**Also behind this sitting.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 36, line 506: שנתאוה הקדוש ברוך הוא להיות לו דירה בתחתונים — *That the Holy One desired to have a dwelling in the lower realms.*<br>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 36, line 510: אלא התכלית הוא עולם הזה התחתון — *Rather, the purpose is this lowest world.*<br>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 33, line 476: כמה גדולה שמחת הדיוט ושפל אנשים — *How great is the joy of a plain, lowly person [when a king comes to live in his house].*<br>Sha'arei HaYichud VeEmunah, Third Gate 27:1 (R. Aharon of Strashelye), line 715: הכוונה שיהיה נגלה הביטול בבחינת היש דייקא — *The intention is that bitul be revealed in the 'something' itself, precisely there.*<br>Imrei Binah, the Shema (the Mitteler Rebbe), line 179: כי הארץ סוף הכל שאין עוד למטה הימנה לכך נמצא בה כח התחלה — *For the earth is the end of all, with nothing lower, so the power of the beginning is found in it.*</small>

<small>Full path: the_hidden_place · Subject meditations: S013 A dwelling in the lowest realm, S186 Serving God in the physical, S058 The descent of the soul into the body</small>

### Sitting 14 · The unity in your hand *F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · Coming down: the deed · claims E07, E14, B13, B17*

**What it's for.** For after the ladder: bringing all of it into a deed. For when prayer feels far but you can still do something. For the question 'what, practically, do I do with all this?'

**The line.** *His will is in this, and He and His will are one, so holding this is holding Him.*

> <small>**Source.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 4, line 126: כי אם כאשר תפיסא ומתלבשת בתורה ומצותיה, אזי היא תפיסא ומתלבשת בהקדוש ברוך הוא — *Only when [a thought] grasps and clothes itself in the Torah and its mitzvos does it grasp and clothe itself in the Holy One.*</small>

1. **Breadth.** The coin. The Shabbos candle. The tefillin. The bread before its blessing. A promise kept. A kind word to someone who annoys you. His will is in each one, and the Tanya says He and His will are one.
2. **Length.** The hand. The Tanya says the hand that takes the etrog grasps its very life, one with the Infinite. Hold the coin before you give it. Feel its weight. That weight is where His will is, right now.
3. **Depth.** Everything you reached for going up the ladder comes back down to this. Imrei Binah says all the rising was only for the sake of coming back down: the return is the main thing.
4. **Into your life.** The next mitzvah or kindness on your list. Do it slowly, with the hand, with the line.

**The picture.** Embracing the king. The closeness is the same whether he is wearing one robe or many (Tanya 4). *Where it breaks:* Only a picture. He has no body to embrace. The picture says only that the layers of the physical world don't make the closeness any less.

**From inside.** An embrace. Being close without needing to understand. For some, the deed feels heavier and more real, like it counts for more than they thought. (Described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.)

**Your own words** (a sample; use yours). "This is what You want, so this is where You are. Let my hands do it."

- *For a Jew:* The blessing of the mitzvah, slowly, before you do it.
- *For someone of another faith:* 'Establish the work of our hands' (Psalm 90:17), and a thank-you before the kindness.
- *If you're unsure about God:* 'If doing good is where the good is, I'll start with this one.'

**The act for today.** The next mitzvah, slowly, with the hand. (Anyone: the next kindness, an honest word, a coin given.)

- Less of a gap between what you believe and what you do. - Deeds start to feel like prayer. - You worry less whether you 'feel it,' because the deed holds it.

**The counterfeit to watch for.** Rote doing with the mind somewhere else, and calling it 'the unity made actual.' Or doing it to be seen. The real thing is done with the line, and often no one sees it.

**If nothing moves.** The Tanya says the deed holds more than the thought. It counts.

- *The first time:* One mitzvah or kindness, slowly.
- *The third time:* One you usually rush through.
- *The seventh time:* The test is the week. Did you do one thing a day with 'His will is in this'?

**Ready for the next rung?** Go back to the first sitting, one rung deeper. The ladder is a circle: you go up to come down, and you come down to go up again.

**The 3-minute version.** Pick up the next mitzvah or kindness. Say, 'His will is in this.' Do it slowly.

<small>**Also behind this sitting.** Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 4, line 127: הרי זה כמחבק את המלך דרך משל — *It is like embracing the king, as a parable.*<br>Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 23 (quoting the Zohar), line 332: אורייתא וקודשא בריך הוא – כולא חד — *The Torah and the Holy One are entirely one.*<br>Tanya, Kuntres Acharon 4, line 1574: הרי זה תופס ממש חיותו — *[One who takes the etrog in hand] grasps its very life.*<br>Imrei Binah, the Shema (the Mitteler Rebbe), line 283: והשוב הוא העיקר — *The return is the main thing.*</small>

**From Samach Vav** *(a note added 2026-09-29; the sitting above is unchanged).* The Rebbe Rashab says why the deed holds Him. God's will has two layers. The will that made the world has reasons, and it is the root of space and time. The will in a mitzvah has no reason at all. So only the mitzvah brings Him as He is into time and place, without their limits. And a will with no reason cannot be cut into pieces: hold part of it and you hold all of it. That is why it rests only in a physical thing, which is not a vessel at all. The Rashab also says the Essence is drawn by the deed and not by the intention. That does not undo this sitting's counterfeit (rote doing with the mind elsewhere). The line is for you; the deed is what carries Him. Both are kept.

<small>Hemshech Samach Vav (the Rashab), BaYom HaShemini Atzeret 5666, line 596: ואמיתית בחי' פנימיות הרצון הוא שאין טעם לרצון כללא — *The true inwardness of the will is that there is no reason for the will at all.*<br>VaYechulu, line 740: דהיינו שגם בבחי' מקום וזמן לא יהי' ההגבלה דמקום וזמן — *That even in place and time there be no limit of place and time.*<br>HaShamayim Kis'i, line 6189: שכשאתה תופס במקצת מן העצם אתה תופס בכולו — *When you grasp part of the essence, you grasp all of it.*<br>HaShamayim Kis'i, line 6189: כ"א בא בדבר גשמי דוקא, שאינו בגדר כלי כלל — *It comes only in a physical thing, which is not in the category of a vessel at all.*<br>HaMechaseh, line 4887: דהמשכת העצמות הוא דוקא במצות מעשיות, לא בכוונת המצות — *The Essence is drawn precisely by practical mitzvot, not by the intention of the mitzvot.*</small>
