## The shape of the range

- **Iggeret Teiman (1172).** To the Jews of Yemen under forced conversion and a false messiah. Why the Torah is attacked (by the sword, by argument, by imitation); why it cannot be destroyed; Sinai as the ground of faith; against calculating the end and against astrology; how to treat the false messiah.
- **Treatise on Resurrection (1191).** Written because readers took his teaching on the World to Come as a denial of resurrection. He affirms that the soul returns to the body, explains why he wrote little about it, and why the Torah does not state it openly.
- **Epistle on Martyrdom (Kiddush HaShem, about 1165).** Against a rabbi who told forced converts that their prayers were sins. Three kinds of commandments under duress, what profaning and sanctifying the Name are, and what to do now.
- **Pirkei HaHatzlachah.** Two chapters on the soul's happiness, with a reading of Psalm 45 as the soul's marriage to G-d. Its attribution to the Rambam is doubted by scholars (ours: treat as "attributed").
- **Sefer HaMitzvot.** The count of the 613. The first commandments are to believe in G-d, His unity, to love Him, fear Him, serve Him, cleave to the wise, swear by His Name, walk in His ways, sanctify His Name, and read the Shema.

## 1. The unity of G-d

**Belief and unity as commandments (Sefer HaMitzvot, lines 74-75).** The first positive commandment is "to believe in the Godhead, that there is a cause and reason that acts for all that exists" (line 74). The second is to believe the unity; the Sages also call it "kabbalat ol malchut shamayim", accepting the yoke of the kingdom of heaven (line 75). The Tzemach Tzedek explains why the Rambam could count belief as a commandment at all: "the command is for deepening the mind and contemplating this known thing" (Derech Mitzvotecha, Mitzvat Ha'amanat Elokut, line 99 of that file; he cites Eight Chapters ch. 2 there).

> היא הצווי אשר צונו בהאמנת האלהות (sefer-hamitzvot line 74)

**Love as contemplation, and love that reaches others (line 76).** Love is "that we think and contemplate His commandments, His words and His acts, until we grasp Him and take pleasure in the grasp to the utmost pleasure". "By contemplation the grasp comes, pleasure arrives, and love comes of necessity." And the commandment "also includes calling all people to His service", as a lover praises his beloved; "love Him to the creatures, like Abraham your father" (line 76). The Rebbe discusses this addition (Likkutei Sichos vol. 34, Va'etchanan 2, p. 36; line 15391 of that file).

> שבהתבוננות תעלה בידך ההשגה ותגיע ההנאה ותבא האהבה בהכרח (line 76)

**One, not like other ones (Iggeret Teiman, line 206).** The message for every community: "to make known to the many that G-d is one, not like other ones". And the people's survival is tied to His: "as it is impossible that G-d's existence be cancelled, so it is impossible that we be lost" (line 206).

> והוא להודיע לרבים שהקב"ה הוא אחד ולא כשאר האחדים (iggerot line 206)

**Against the body and the stars.** In the Treatise on Resurrection he tells of a scholar who doubted whether G-d has a body, and others who called the non-believer in a body a heretic (line 212). In Iggeret Teiman he tells the questioner to "wash your mind as one washes soiled clothes" of astrology (line 208). The unity is guarded from the imagination and from fate.

## 2. States of consciousness

- **Hope that grows with delay (line 208).** "Though it tarry, wait for it"; "the more it is delayed, the more hope you should add". *Ties to:* S84 (Trust), S36 (Faith above knowing).
- **Suffering as a test that shows love (line 206).** The persecution "is only a test and trial, to show your faith and your love to the world"; "we should rejoice in what we bear" (line 206). *Ties to:* S49, S60. The guide should use this for the person's own chosen hardship, not to explain another's pain (ours).
- **Held though forsaken (line 208).** "Zion said, G-d has forsaken me", answered by "Can a woman forget her nursing child? ... I will not forget you." *Ties to:* S68 (The hiding seen as His), S67.
- **The hidden commandment (lines 226, 232).** Those who were forced "are not rebels by will, and G-d will not forsake them" (line 226). A commandment done in secret at risk earns double: "G-d doubles its reward" (line 232). Those who come to pray after sinning "come in secret to steal commandments" and must not be shamed (line 232). *Ties to:* S41 (The hidden love wakes), S86. This is the Rambam's most tender teaching, and it is very close to the Chassidic view of every Jew's inner will (ours).
- **The soul's lamp (Pirkei HaHatzlachah, line 371, attributed).** "The seat of your heart is the Ark in which the tablets of testimony are hidden"; "every person, if he wills perfection, can be near to Moses". *Ties to:* S66.

## 3. Meditation and practice

- **Remember Sinai (line 206).** Raise children on the Sinai event and tell it in public; "it is the pillar around which faith turns". *Ties to:* technique `picturing`, `carry`.
- **Strengthen each other (line 208).** "Strengthen one another; plant faith." *Ties to:* S86; the guide's group practice.
- **Do not awaken love before its time (line 209).** On false messiahs: "do not arouse the love until it desires". Guard against forced spiritual urgency. *Ties to:* technique `discern`.
- **Speak to each by his measure (line 214).** "One should speak to each group according to what it can bear." *Ties to:* the guide's own method (ours).
- **Sanctify the Name in the ordinary (line 229).** Sanctifying the Name is doing a commandment "with no motive mixed in but the love of G-d and His service alone", and conduct that makes people praise the Torah. *Ties to:* S81.
- **Guard speech (line 226).** "A person should spare his speech more than his money." *Ties to:* technique `quieting`.
- **The commandments do not wait for the Messiah (line 231).** "The obligation of the commandments does not depend on the Messiah's coming"; do what you must now. *Ties to:* S81, technique `act`.

## 4. The big human questions

- **What if I was forced, or failed?** The forced are not sinners by will (line 226); keep what you can, and do not say "since I broke the big thing, the small things do not matter" (line 231). Draw the sinner near (line 232).
- **Will the body rise?** Yes: resurrection "is a foundation of the Torah: the soul's return to the body", and after it comes the life of the World to Come (line 212). It is a miracle, beyond nature, accepted from the prophets. "We strive to bring together the Torah and reason" (line 212), and he would rather one wise person follow him to the truth than a thousand fools (line 212).
- **Why is Israel's fate not by nature?** "There is no mazal (astral fate) for Israel": its welfare and its loss hang on service and sin, "a greater sign than any sign" (line 213).
- **Is the world ruled by chance?** No; to call trouble "chance" is to walk with G-d in *keri* (line 208, and Taaniyot 1:3).
- **What should I do when the world is against me?** Leave the place of forced conversion if you can (line 231); and even between two Jewish towns, go to the better one (line 231).

## 5. Where Chassidus reads this range, and where it departs

- **Belief as a commandment.** The Tzemach Tzedek reads the Rambam's first commandment as a command to *deepen* the mind in what is already known (line 99), which is the Chabad definition of hisbonenus. Agreement, with a Chabad turn.
- **Love that spreads.** The Rebbe's reading of positive commandment 3 (line 15391) made "love Him to the creatures" a pillar of outreach. The Rambam's source text is here.
- **Resurrection and the World to Come.** Here the Rambam says the World to Come comes *after* the resurrection (line 212), but it remains bodiless; Chassidus places the fullness in the resurrected body (Tanya ch. 36-37). The Treatise is the Rambam at his closest to the later view, and still apart from it.
- **The hidden Jew.** The Epistle's defense of the forced converts and of the sinner's secret mitzvah (lines 226, 232) fits the Chabad teaching that no Jew can be cut off from G-d (Tanya ch. 18-19, 24).

## What this range adds

- The commandments of belief, unity and love in the Rambam's own words, with the Chabad reading of each. - A theology of suffering for a community under pressure: hope, holding, the double worth of the hidden mitzvah. - The Rambam's own account of resurrection.

## Where the thread is thin

- Much of Iggeret Teiman is polemic against Islam and Christianity and a critique of astrology and messianic calculation; its inner content is the lines above. - The Treatise on Resurrection is mostly defense of his earlier writing. - The rest of Sefer HaMitzvot is a technical count (read at scan level). The other letters (Chiddushim, Teshuvot, the calendar, Galen, the controversy letters) were not read in full.
