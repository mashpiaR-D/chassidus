## 1. What the range is

**Sifra DiTzniuta**, "the Book of Concealment" (lines 3416-3488), sits in this volume at the end of Terumah. It is five short chapters in a compressed, riddling Aramaic, the seed text that the Idra Rabba and Idra Zuta later open. It speaks of the "scale" that was set up before the worlds, the "skull" of the Ancient One, the thirteen features of the beard (the thirteen attributes of mercy), the letters of the Name, and the "kings who died". Most of it cannot be taught as it stands, and we do not try. A few lines are plain enough to use, and they are important.

**Tetzaveh** (lines 3489-3634) opens with "and you" (*ve'atah*) as the upper and lower light joined, then gives a long letter teaching on *yod*, *heh* and *vav* (lines 3496-3525), which we summarize. Then: Job's offering and why he was handed to the accuser (lines 3529-3536), leaven as the evil inclination (lines 3537-3538), a strong passage on anger (lines 3539-3549), the counting of the Omer and Shavuot as freedom (lines 3550-3566), and the long story of an old hermit, Nehorai Sava, who comes out of the desert for the High Holy Days and teaches on light out of darkness, the shofar, Yom Kippur, and Sukkot (lines 3567-3634).

**Ki Tisa** (lines 3635-3754) covers the census and the half shekel, the dawn worshippers, a Roman official who asks about "the lip of truth" and later converts, the exile and the promises that let Israel bear it, the parable of the king's son sent out with his mother, God's mercy that holds back the strap, the love between the companions, and a long reading of the golden calf.

## 2. The unity of G-d

**He is the one who is not named.** Sifra DiTzniuta on the word "He" (*hu*): הוא אקרי מאן דסתים ולא שכיח, הוא מאן דלא אזדמן לעינא. הוא מאן דלא אקרי בשמא, "'He' is called the one who is hidden and not found; 'He' is the one who is not present to the eye; 'He' is the one who is not called by a name" (line 3445). **(ours)** This is the same "He" that Yitro set against "You" (part2-yitro.md, line 1822). The pronoun itself is the name of what has no name.

**Hidden, and shown and not shown.** עתיקא, טמיר וסתים. זעירא דאנפין, אתגלייא ולא אתגלייא, "the Ancient One is hidden and closed; the Small Face is revealed and not revealed" (line 3464). The scale by which the worlds were weighed "hangs in a place that is not," האי מתקלא תלי באתר דלא הוה (line 3420), and between the hidden and the shown there is a firmament that divides, yet לא אתפרש ממש, "it is not really separated" (line 3474).

**The open eye that does not sleep.** Of the Ancient One: אשגחא פקיחא דלא נאים, ונטיר תדירא, "an open eye that does not sleep and guards always" (line 3421). And the will of all wills is revealed דרעוין אתגליא בצלותא דתתאי, "in the prayer of those below" (line 3421). **(ours)** The most hidden level is the one that answers prayer.

**One simple thing.** After the long letter teaching, the *vav* holds right, left and middle, and so ולא תשכח בר פשיטו חד בלחודוי, ודא הוא חד, "you will find only one simple thing alone, and that is One" (line 3523). Unity here is not many parts glued together but one simple thing that was never divided.

**"And You" is both lights.** נהורא עלאה, ונהורא תתאה כליל כחדא, אקרי ואתה, "the upper light and the lower light joined as one are called 'and You'" (line 3490), as in "and You give life to them all" (Nehemiah 9:6).

**Unity is made from below.** Why does it say "from among the children of Israel"? Because nothing is made one except from among those below, who "open paths, light the lamps," ולקרבא כלא מתתא לעילא, למהוי כלא חד, "and bring everything near from below to above, so that all is one" (line 3527). כלא איהו קריבה, למאן דידע ליחדא יחודא, "everything is an offering, for one who knows how to make the unity" (line 3528).

## 3. States of consciousness

**Prayer of heart without words (fills in ZS2-05).** Above there are holy names דקיימין ברעו דרוחא ולבא בלא מלולא כלל, "that stand in the will of the spirit and the heart, with no speech at all" (line 3510); below are names that stand in speech and in the drawing of thought. This adds a second source to ZS2-05 (*Voice without its word*, part2-vaera-bo.md): the highest prayer is will of the heart with no words.

**Light that comes out of darkness (fills in ZS2-17).** The old hermit explains why he lived in the harsh desert: דלית נהורא אלא ההוא דנפיק מגו חשוכא, "there is no light except the light that comes out of darkness" (line 3574). ולית פולחנא דקודשא בריך הוא, אלא מגו חשוכא, ולית טובא אלא מגו בישא, "there is no service of the Holy One except from within darkness, and no good except from within bad." When a person enters a bad road and leaves it, God is raised in His glory, ודא איהו פולחנא שלים, "and this is the complete service" (line 3574). R. Shimon answers with Daniel 2:22: דאלמלא חשוכא לא אתידע נהורא, "if not for darkness, light would not be known," and "the light that dwells with Him" is נהורא דאתגלייא מגו חשוכא, "the light revealed out of darkness" (line 3633). This is the Zohar root of the Tanya's teaching that the light from darkness (*ohr mi-toch choshech*) is greater, and it fills in ZS2-17 (*The dark light at dawn*) and S68 (*The hiding seen as His*).

**The face that draws the face.** The lower world "receives always," and the upper world gives it only as it stands: אי איהו קיימא בנהירו דאנפין מתתא, כדין הכי נהרין ליה מלעילא, "if it stands with a shining face below, so they shine to it from above"; if it stands in sadness, it is given judgment to match (line 3581). So "serve God with joy": חדוה דבר נש, משיך לגביה חדוה אחרא עלאה, "a person's joy draws to him another, higher joy" (line 3582). Tie to S53 (Glad). **(ours)** Compare Terumah's gate of tears (part2-terumah-2.md): sadness kept in closes, tears given to Him open, and a face set to light draws light.

**Anger as a stranger in the soul.** How do you know who a person is? ברוגזיה ממש, ידע ליה בר נש, וישתמודע מאן איהו, "by his anger itself you know a person and recognize who he is" (line 3542). If he guards his holy soul in the hour of anger, he is a whole man. If not, איהו טריף ועקר נפשיה, בגין רוגזיה, ואשרי בגויה אל זר, "he tears and uproots his soul because of his anger, and sets a strange god inside it" (line 3543). The exception: רוגזא דרבנן במלי דאורייתא, "the anger of the sages in words of Torah" (line 3545), for the honor of Torah, is good. Tie to S15 (Angry at someone). **(ours)** The guide can use the test without the harsh rulings that follow it (lines 3546-3548): in the moment of anger, the question is whether I keep my soul in its place.

**The Holy Spirit whispering.** The wise-hearted who built the Tabernacle could not work עד דרוח קדשא ממללא בגווייהו, ולחש לון בלחישו, "until the Holy Spirit spoke inside them and whispered to them in a whisper" (line 3492). A picture of inspired work: the doing comes after the whisper.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Setting the face to light (proposed ZT2-10).** From lines 3581-3582: before prayer, study, or a hard meeting, set your face to light on purpose, a small real gladness, because the face below draws the face above. Not forced cheer; a turning of the face. Builds on technique 2 (Before Whom) and 19 (Melody). **(ours)**

**Murmuring lips, then the knot.** Sifra DiTzniuta reads "let the waters swarm" as prayer: כד מרחשין בשפוותיה פתגמי צלותא, בזכותא, ובנקיות דעתא, "when his lips murmur the words of prayer with merit and a clean mind" (line 3454). It gives three movements: lift the honor from below to above, draw the flow down from above to every level, then "tie the knot of the intention of faith." It lists nine ways a request can be arranged (the alphabet, the attributes of mercy, the names, the sefirot, the fathers, songs, and more), each needing great intention. And a comfort: one who intends properly will stand among the pious אף על פי דלא קרי כל צורכיה, כיון דזכה לאשגחא ידיעת מאריה, "even if he did not read all he should, since he merited to perceive the knowledge of his Master" (line 3457). **(ours)** A frame for technique 18 (Saying it with the lips) and 20 (Your own words to Him).

**Matzah as medicine.** Leaven is the evil inclination: עאל במעוי דבר נש זעיר זעיר, ולבתר אסגי ביה, "it enters a person little by little, and then grows in him" (line 3538). Matzah is the medicine a sick prince must eat first, כיון דאכלו מצה, דאיהי אסוותא למיעל ולמנדע ברזא דמהימנותא, "once they ate matzah, which is the medicine for entering and knowing the secret of faith" (line 3563), leaven can no longer harm. **(ours)** A picture for any practice: first the simple, flat, humble thing; then the rest of life can come back without harm.

**Do not tell your sins to another on Yom Kippur.** לא אצטריך בר נש לפרשא חטאוי קמי אחרא, "a person need not spell out his sins before another" (line 3609), because the words are taken up as witnesses. Confession is to God.

**Sitting in the shade (fills in ZS2-16).** The hermit comes out of the desert למיתב בצלא דקודשא בריך הוא, "to sit in the shade of the Holy One" in the seventh month (line 3568). The sukkah is the "shade of faith," ומאן דיתיב בצלא דא, יתיב באינון יומין עלאין, "and whoever sits in this shade sits in those high days" (line 3624). This adds to ZS2-16 (*Sitting in His shade*, part2-terumah-1.md).

## 5. The big human questions

**How did they bear the exile?** Without the good promised in the Torah, Israel could not have borne it. They go to the houses of study, open the books, וחמאן כל אינון טבאן, דקא מחכן, "and see all the good they are waiting for" (line 3651), and are comforted. ואלמלא דא לא יכלין למסבל, "and if not for this, they could not bear it" (line 3655). **(ours)** Reading hope in a text is a real way through suffering.

**Must I suffer all of it before it ends?** A woman who reaches even one day of the ninth month, אתחשיב עלה כאילו אתעברו כל תשיעאה שלים, "it is counted for her as if the whole ninth month had passed" (line 3658). So if Israel return, one pain counts as all. וכל דא, בתיובתא תליא מילתא, "and all of it hangs on return" (line 3670).

**Does God leave us in our sins?** The parable of the king's son (lines 3659-3669). The king gives his son to his mother, the queen, to raise. The son sins, is struck, sins again, is sent out. He does not go the straight way but joins harlots. The mother weeps. The king says: אמר מלכא, בגינך אהדר ליה, ואנת ערבא דיליה, "for your sake I will bring him back, and you are his guarantor" (line 3661), but quietly, not with trumpets, since he shamed himself. The third time the king sends the mother out with him: you will both bear exile together. **(ours)** The Shekhinah goes into exile with us (דגרמו לשכינתא דאתגלי בגלותא עמהון, line 3690). A person far away is never alone there.

**Is God harsh?** R. Yosi: לאו הוה אבא רחמן על בנוי כקודשא בריך הוא, "no father was ever as merciful to his children as the Holy One" (line 3684). "Not one word failed of all His *good* word": the bad He threatens, דהא מלה דביש לא בעי למעבד, "He does not want to do" (line 3684). When He raises the strap, אתאת אמא ואתקפת בדרועיה ימינא, "the mother comes and holds His right arm" (line 3685), and the strap stays in the air; both of them are of one counsel. At the calf the mother was not there, so Moses took her place: אחיד בדרועיה ימינא, "he held His right arm" (line 3737), then His left, then embraced His body so He could not move.

**What does it mean to love the people you learn with?** R. Abba: companions who do not love each other die before their time. In R. Shimon's days רחימו דנפשא ורוחא הוה בינייהו, "there was love of soul and spirit between them" (line 3688), דהא אורייתא רחימו ואחוה וקשוט אית בה, "for the Torah has love, brotherhood and truth in it" (line 3688).

**Can a good intention still go wrong?** Aaron at the calf meant only good: he tried to delay until Moses came, built an altar to God, and called "a feast to the Lord." וכלא ברעותא שלים טב, דלא אתכוין לביש, "all of it with a whole good will, not intending evil" (line 3731). Yet many fell, and his sons later paid. **(ours)** Good will saves the one who has it; it does not undo the harm of a careless act.

**Why did Adam fall?** He stood in the upper wisdom and did not separate from the tree of life, כיון דאסגי תיאובתא למנדע, ולנחתא לתתא, "once his desire to know, and to go down below, grew great" (line 3739), he was drawn after it. At the sea Israel ועל ימא, הוו חמאן ולא דחלין, "saw and were not afraid" (line 3742); after the calf they could not look even at Moses' face.

**What about those who worship the sun and stars?** The stars and the light of dawn are real, and לא בעי קודשא בריך הוא לשיצאה עובדוי מעלמא, "the Holy One does not want to destroy His works from the world" (line 3644) because fools call them gods.

**Is the start hard?** כל שירותין קשין, וסופא דלהון רכין, "all beginnings are hard, and their end is soft" (line 3629). Rosh Hashanah is hard; Shemini Atzeret is soft and glad.

## 6. For the guide (ours)

1. **"No light except light that comes out of darkness" (lines 3574, 3633)** is a central Zohar text for anyone who feels their past disqualifies them. It is also the source of a key Tanya theme.
2. **The king's son sent out with his mother (lines 3659-3669)** gives the guide a way to say "you are not alone even there."
3. **"By his anger you know a person" (lines 3542-3543)** is a sharp, usable test.
4. **"The face below draws the face above" (lines 3581-3582)** and **"'He' is the one not called by a name" (line 3445)** are short and strong.
5. **"No father was as merciful" and the mother holding His arm (lines 3684-3685)** answer the fear of a harsh God.

Proposed for the map (details in `ZOHAR-PART-2.md`): technique ZT2-10 *Setting the face to light*. Added sources to ZS2-05, ZS2-16 and ZS2-17.

Set aside: the detailed anatomy of the Ancient One and the letter mechanics of Sifra DiTzniuta and Tetzaveh (lines 3418-3470, 3496-3525), the goat to Azazel and the "prosecutor's meal" (lines 3588-3599, 3626-3628), the dawn worship of the east, the sorcery of the mixed multitude (lines 3693-3722), and passages about the nations (lines 3647-3654). The Roman official who asks a hard question and later converts (lines 3647-3649) is kept only as a story of honest argument.
