## What this cluster shows

These thirteen subjects are the inner life of the service: love, awe, the yoke, cleaving, the rhythm of running and returning, fervor, bitul and its opposite, humility, self-sacrifice and truth. In this cluster the one idea shows as the person's answer to it. If there is nothing besides Him, then love is possible only 'from the side of the unity' (Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 5), and 'the first foundation of what arouses love and awe is faith in His unity' (Chinukh Katan 12). Awe is the same unity overwhelming the self: lower awe sees Him clothed in the worlds, higher awe sees the worlds and oneself nullified in His word (Tanya 42-43; Kedushat Levi, Eikev 19). The hidden love and self-sacrifice are the unity already in the soul: Ein Sof clothed in its Chochmah, which cannot bear to be separated from His oneness (Tanya 18-19, 25). Bitul, the separate self and humility are F08 at length: not being nullified is 'a denial of His true unity' (Tanya 22), and whoever is more 'before Him' is more nothing (Iggeret HaKodesh 2). Truth is the unity named: 'the truth of Havayah Elokim is truth is His unity' (Tanya 35). Two correctives run through the cluster. First, the feelings are not the unity: the lover remains a yesh (Tanya 35), felt arousal can be the yesh of nogah (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 9), and true devekut is unfelt (Peri HaAretz). Second, the running is for the return: ratzo without shov is death, and the purpose of the flight upward is the dwelling below (Tanya 50; Hayom Yom 27 Adar II; the Rebbe, 5729). The tie is stated for ten subjects. It is partly stated for run and return (the Besht's own reasons are protective), fervor (largely psychology, tied by its source and its test) and truth (as a moral trait the tie is indirect).

## S112 · Love of God · אהבת ה'

**Faces:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F01 The world said into being now · F09 The unity of opposites · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** אהבת ה' · ואהבת את ה' · אהבה · כלות הנפש

> Love of God is the unity of God as it is felt in the heart: the recognition that He is 'our very life', the life in everything one loves, turned into longing to be included in Him.

## Derivation

1. Love of God is a commandment that seems impossible: how can one love what is infinitely remote? Strashelye states the difficulty and the answer: love, awe and cleaving can be gauged only 'from the side of the unity', known through Chochmah and Binah (Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 5). (*stated*)
2. The Alter Rebbe states the order: the first foundation of what arouses love and awe is pure faith in His unity (Chinukh Katan 12). Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, the book of the unity, follows directly on this sentence; that it was placed there to ground the love is our reading of the book's order, not something the text says (F12). (*stated*)
3. The content of the contemplation is F01: He is 'our very life', and as a person loves his own life, so he will love God, once he sets his heart on this (Chinukh Katan 8). The Chernobyler draws the same conclusion from the other side: whatever good one loves is His vitality in it, so every love has fallen from the root love and must be lifted back (Me'or Einayim, Kedoshim 3). (*stated*)
4. Love and fear exclude each other in creatures; in Him they coexist, because the ayin joins them (Maggid 3:1, F09, G10). Tanya 41 makes a parallel rule for service: love alone does not fly, 'as a bird cannot fly with one wing'. Joining the Maggid's metaphysics to Tanya's rule of service is our reading. (*stated*)
5. Love's limit is also stated. The one who loves remains 'a thing in itself, one who fears and loves Him', and cannot be one with Him by love alone; the full unity comes through the mitzvot, which are His will (Tanya 35:4-6; F08, F10). (*stated*)
6. The Rashab puts love at the end of the whole intellectual enterprise: the inner Torah exists 'to know God and come to love and awe' (Kuntres Eitz HaChaim, scan p. 44). Tanya 44:9 adds that the hidden love is brought into the open for the sake of deed, Torah and mitzvot. The chain knowledge, love, deed is our summary of the two texts. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Chinukh Katan 7-12: two loves, the self-rising love of tzaddikim and the love any Jew can make by contemplating that He is 'our life'; faith in the unity is the foundation. Tanya 41-50 grade the loves (S113, S114, S119). Tanya 35 marks love's limit: the lover remains a yesh.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 7:6-14 reads 'to love God, for He is your life' as a reason ('love Him because He is your life'), and distinguishes love (an arousal at times) from devekut (the constant essential bond) (S117).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not quoted on love of God in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Eitz HaChaim, scan p. 44 (OCR): the inner Torah's whole point is to know God and come to love and awe. Kuntres HaAvodah develops love 'like water' and 'like fiery coals' (S119).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 (read, not quoted): every Jew has an essential love, but it is hidden and not enough, hence 'you shall love' (S113). Hayom Yom 28 Nissan (compiled by the Rebbe): the Alter Rebbe answers that love of God and love of Israel are engraved together in every soul, and adds that love of Israel is the greater, since one loves what the Beloved loves.
- **The Rebbe.** Not quoted on love of God as such in this pass; see S116 and S118 for his reading of love's bitul within kabbalat ol.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (3:1): love and fear one in Him, joined by ayin. The Chernobyler (Kedoshim 3): all love has come down from the root love; the good tasted is His vitality; lift the hands in holiness. Strashelye (Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 4-5): love needs knowledge of the unity, and felt love still contains 'the lover', the yesh. Pillar 13's claims for Krakow, Berditchev and R. Bunim (fear first, love as gift) are carried from the earlier work, not re-verified.

## Thin or forced?

No. The tie is stated: Chinukh Katan 12 makes faith in the unity the foundation of love, and Sha'arei Avodah says love is possible only 'from the side of the unity'. The texts also state love's limit: love leaves the lover a separate yesh (Tanya 35), which is why love leads to mitzvah and bitul rather than being the unity itself.

## S113 · The hidden love · אהבה מסותרת

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** אהבה מסותרת · אהבה טבעית · ירושה לנו · אהבה הטבעית

> The hidden love is the unity of God as it sits in every Jewish soul: Ein Sof clothed in the soul's Chochmah, which by nature wants to be nullified in its source and cannot bear to be separated from His oneness.

## Derivation

1. The soul is a part of God, drawn from His wisdom, which is one with Him (F06). In every Jew, 'whoever he is', Ein Sof is clothed in the Chochmah of the soul and gives life to all of it (Tanya 18:6-7). (*stated*)
2. Chochmah is 'koach mah', what is not yet grasped, and so it is the place where Ein Sof, whom no thought grasps, can be clothed; its nature is bitul, 'not a thing in itself' (Tanya 18:10, 19:7). (*stated*)
3. Therefore the soul by nature wants to leave the body and cleave to its source, like a flame rising from the wick, even though there it would cease to exist (Tanya 19:2-4). This natural, above-reason wish is the hidden love (Tanya 19:9). (*stated*)
4. It is called hidden because it is covered by the kelipah; sin comes only from a 'spirit of folly' that hides it (Tanya 19:9, 24:8). Its content is the unity itself: to cleave to faith in His oneness and not be separated from it (Tanya 24:8; F03 in the soul). (*stated*)
5. It is inherited in every Jew, and a greater form is the son's love, 'for we all have one Father' (Tanya 44:6). Tanya 32:2 uses the same phrase, 'one Father to them all', to root ahavat Yisrael (F06). (*stated*)
6. It is 'truth of truth in itself', even if waking it feels imaginary; the work is only to bring it out into deed (Tanya 44:9). The Mitteler Rebbe and the Rayatz name its source the essential bond (yechidah, Chochmah's essential bitul) that cannot be separated from the One (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 28:26-30; Sefer HaMaamarim 5692). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 18-19 (source in Chochmah), 24-25 (it covers even sin; 'near to you'), 41 and 44 (inherited, the son's love, 'truth of truth').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 28:24-31: the yechidah shines below in the refusal to be separated from the One; this comes 'not from effort' but from the soul's essential bond with its source, and in kiddush Hashem it shines revealed.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Kiddush Hashem: Chochmah is bitul 'when one recognizes the truth that there is nothing else' (S123).
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not quoted in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692 (Re'eh): the 'neshamah of the neshamah' is Chochmah, essential bitul, from which comes the power in every Jew 'that cannot and will not be separated from Godliness'. Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 (read, not quoted): it is hidden, so 'you shall love' is still needed.
- **The Rebbe.** Not quoted in this pass; Inyanah 11-14 on the yechidah (spine F06) is the same point in his terms.
- **Other schools.** The term is essentially the Alter Rebbe's. Strashelye's 'natural love from faith' (pillar 13, not re-verified) is its cognate. Outside Chabad no verified passage in this pass uses 'hidden love' in this sense.

## S114 · Great love and eternal love · אהבה רבה ואהבת עולם

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F04 Filling and surrounding · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' **Search terms:** אהבה רבה · אהבת עולם · כמים הפנים · אהבה בתענוגים

> Great love and eternal love are the unity of God felt in two directions: eternal love rises from below, from contemplating that all worlds are as nothing before Him; great love comes from above, from Atzilut 'where there is no separation'.

## Derivation

1. Tanya 43 names two loves. 'Eternal love' (ahavat olam) is born of understanding the greatness of Ein Sof who fills and surrounds all worlds, before whom all is as nothing (Tanya 43:7). This is love generated from the unity as known from below (F04, F02 lower to higher, F12). (*stated*)
2. Through that contemplation love strips off every garment of pleasure, physical or spiritual, and wants nothing but Him, the source of all delights, beside whom all delights are 'nullified in existence' (Tanya 43:7). Calling this the lower unity seen through to the higher is our mapping onto F02. (*stated*)
3. 'Great love' (ahavah rabbah), 'love in delights', rises of itself and comes from above as a gift to one complete in awe, because it is from Atzilut, 'where there is no cutting off or separation' (Tanya 43:6; F02 higher unity). It cannot be commanded (Chinukh Katan 11). (*stated*)
4. Iggeret HaKodesh 6 calls the loves given from above 'truth', because His seal is truth and creaturely truth is as nothing before Him (S124). That the gift-love is the higher unity itself entering the heart is our reading; the text says only that it comes from the Creator and is therefore called truth. (*stated*)
5. The bridge between them is 'as water reflects face to face': He hid His infinite light through three contractions for love of the lowly man, so contemplating this love awakens ours (Tanya 46, 49:5; F03: the concealment is itself love). (*stated*)
6. The Maggid uses the same two names for God's loves toward us: great love for tzaddikim, which brought Him to contract Himself and create, and 'love of the world' for the penitent (Maggid 41:3). Placing this beside Tanya is our reading: the Maggid keeps the names as His love for us, Tanya turns them to ours, and 'as water reflects face' (Tanya 49) is the bridge. We make no claim about which usage came first. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 9:6 (great love = love in delights, in Chochmah), 43:6-7 (the two defined), 44 (both include countless grades; one love includes all, inherited), 46 and 49 ('as water reflects face'); Chinukh Katan 7-11; Iggeret HaKodesh 6 (the gift loves are 'truth').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not quoted on the pair in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah, scan p. 23 (read, not quoted): love 'like water' is nearness and cleaving; love 'like fiery coals' is thirst (S119).
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (41:3): 'great love' and 'love of the world' are God's loves, for the righteous and for the penitent; the former caused the tzimtzum. Kedushat Levi (Kedoshim 6, read, not quoted) likewise speaks of the Father's 'great love and eternal love' for a beloved son, whose fear then is not to grieve Him. A real difference in referent: God's love (Maggid, Berditchev) against man's (Tanya).

## Thin or forced?

No for Tanya: both loves are defined by the unity (Tanya 43:6-7). The comparison with the Maggid's usage, where the same names describe God's love, is our reading.

## S115 · Awe · יראה

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F01 The world said into being now · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** יראת ה' · יראת שמים · יראה עילאה · יראה תתאה

> Awe is the unity of God as it overwhelms the self: lower awe sees Him hidden inside the worlds as their life; higher awe sees the worlds, and oneself, nullified in existence before Him.

## Derivation

1. One fears a king for his inner life, not his body; so one fears God by seeing heaven and earth as garments in which Or Ein Sof is clothed to give them life (Tanya 42:16-19, F01 and F03). Habit makes this constant, which is the meaning of 'emunah' as training (Tanya 42:19). (*stated*)
2. That awe is 'lower' and 'outer' because it comes from the worlds, His garments, in which He hides to create yesh from ayin (Tanya 43:2). Mapping it to the lower unity (F02) is our reading. (*stated*)
3. The higher awe, shame, comes from Chochmah, which sees each thing born from nothing by His word, so that heaven, earth, and 'let a person not exclude himself' his own body and soul, are nullified in existence (Tanya 43:3-4). The self's bitul (F08) is stated ('let a person not exclude himself'); calling it the higher unity felt as awe (F02) is our mapping. (*stated*)
4. Berditchev states the same two grades independently: lower awe (fear of sin, which is fear of separation from God) grows from contemplating that all exists by His vitality renewed every moment; higher awe is bitul in existence before the Essence, with no self left even to reckon its lowliness (Kedushat Levi, Eikev 19). (*stated*)
5. The Maggid: through awe a person reckons himself as ayin, and ayin is Chochmah, the source of life (Maggid 19:1). Awe is thus the door into bitul (S120). (*stated*)
6. The order: the lower awe comes first as the gate to Torah and mitzvot; without it there is neither wisdom nor the gift of great love (Tanya 43:5-6, 43:11). (*stated*)

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 41-43 (standing before the King who fills the earth; lower and higher awe; awe as the first wing); Iggeret HaKodesh 6 (awe from above, 'truth').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not quoted on awe in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not quoted in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Eitz HaChaim: the purpose of Chassidus is love and awe (S112).
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:145, read, not quoted): outer fear of punishment is His hand stretched out and should awaken the inner fear, which is called love. The Maggid (19:1): awe leads to ayin. Berditchev (Eikev 19): the two awes as fear of separation and as bitul, with continuous creation as the ground. Pillar 13 reports Krakow (fear of punishment as gate), Radomsk and Apta on measure; not re-verified.

## S116 · Accepting the yoke · קבלת עול

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F05 The Essence beyond both · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** קבלת עול · קבלת עול מלכות · עול מלכות שמים · כעבד

> Accepting the yoke is the unity of God as kingship taken on without feeling: the self standing as the King's servant because that is what it essentially is, a bond deeper than love.

## Derivation

1. Kabbalat ol is Israel's side of His kingship: He leaves the upper and lower beings and unites His kingship upon us, and we accept it in Shema and again in deed in the Amidah's bowing (Tanya 42:20; the lower unity, F02). Framing this by 'no king without a people' (F03) is our reading; Tanya 42 does not invoke it. (*stated*)
2. Accepting it is 'true without doubt' even when no awe is felt, because not rebelling against the King is the nature of every Jewish soul (Tanya 41:8, F06). Hence a person must be both servant and son (Tanya 41:9). (*stated*)
3. Strashelye ranks it: the bitul of love is higher as a state, but the bitul of the yoke has the higher root, because obeying without love shows a bond of the heart's point 'from the essence' and not from what it enjoys (Sha'arei Avodah IV 17:5; F05). After the love of Shema one must accept the yoke again 'from the side of His Essence, besides whom there is nothing' (IV 17:6). (*stated*)
4. The Tzemach Tzedek to a learned chassid: kabbalat ol changes one's very essence (Hayom Yom 3 Adar II). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe, building on the Maharash's maamar of 5629: accepting the yoke of Heaven is bitul of the person himself, 'his whole existence is that he is the King's servant'; accepting the yoke of mitzvot is bitul of his revealed powers; and the purpose of the first is the second, through which the Essence's intent, the dwelling below, is completed (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Bamidbar 5729; F07). (*stated*)
6. Homel gives the grades: the servant who with all his powers serves the King and not himself is bitul hayesh; truer bitul is how servants stand in the King's own knowledge, where their existence does not register at all (Chanah Ariel, Noach 3:2; F02). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 41 (servant and son; accepting the kingdom is true by the soul's nature), 42:20 ('set a king over yourself'); Iggeret HaKodesh 15:29 (Malchus: to accept His yoke 'as every servant to his master').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Hayom Yom 3 Adar II: 'kabbalat ol changes the essence'; through a simple servant's yoke even a scholar can reach the worth of a simple Jew's self-sacrifice.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5629 (Bshaah shehikdimu, read at line 2085): Shema before 'vehayah' so the yoke of Heaven precedes the yoke of mitzvot, as ratzo precedes shov; the Rebbe builds on this.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 (S123): the soul, embraced in the Essence, 'bears Your yoke' to self-sacrifice.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Bamidbar 5729 (lines 2254-2259): yoke of Heaven = ratzo and self-sacrifice, bitul of the self; yoke of mitzvot = shov; the dwelling below is completed only when bitul reaches the person's existence.
- **Other schools.** Strashelye (Sha'arei Avodah IV 17:5-6): the yoke's bitul is higher in root than love's. Homel (Chanah Ariel, Noach 3:2): the servant's bitul hayesh versus the King's-eye bitul. The Maggid (6:2, read at line 18) reads 'set a king over yourself' as supernal Malchus, 'that His fear be upon you'.

## S117 · Cleaving · דביקות

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F01 The world said into being now · F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** דביקות · דבקות · ובו תדבק · להדבק

> Cleaving is the unity of God lived as life itself: the soul, a part of the Life of all life, bound to Him so that nothing screens between them, and completed by His will in Torah and mitzvot.

## Derivation

1. The Besht defines devekut by F01 and F06: God is the Life of life, so one who cleaves to Him lives essentially, and without it only accidentally, as heat belongs to fire but only visits water (Keter Shem Tov 1:55:4). (*stated*)
2. Cleaving is possible because the hiding is His: once one knows He hides in the garments, even in stray thoughts, 'there is no screen dividing him from his God at the time of Torah and prayer' (Keter Shem Tov 1:55:9-10; F03). (*stated*)
3. The Mitteler Rebbe distinguishes: devekut is the soul's life and bond with the Essence of Or Ein Sof, 'constant and essential' from the root of the soul, while love is an arousal from time to time (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 7:11-14; F06, F05). (*stated*)
4. It reaches the eye: one who longs for God sees in everything God whose power gives it life; one who does not sees only the physical, even though he would say God gives life to all (Yosher Divrei Emet I 12:1-2; F01, F12). From this one reckons oneself as nothing, as before creation (ibid.). (*stated*)
5. Its limit and completion: the soul of even a perfect tzaddik is not wholly nullified into the light; the mitzvot, His inner will, are (Tanya 35:4). And since in Torah He gave us His will and wisdom, 'as if He gave us Himself', nothing prevents cleaving to His unity but the will (Tanya 47:3-4; F10). (*stated*)
6. Vitebsk is sharper: every felt awe, love or delight in devekut is, in his word, false (כוזב); these are the body's enticements, a way one must pass through, and true devekut is beyond feeling, through faith, which is bitul of existence (Peri HaAretz, Vayakhel-Pekudei 3; F08). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 4:13 (Torah as embracing the King), 35 (the limit of the soul's devekut), 46-47 (cleaving to His unity blocked only by the will), 49 ('uniting spirit with spirit').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 7:6-15: devekut = the soul's essential and constant bond ('with one knot I am bound to Him'), versus love's intermittent arousal.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not quoted in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not quoted in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 (S123): the soul 'embraced and cleaving' in the Essence.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:55): devekut = essential life; no screen once the hiding is known; keep a spark of it all day. Tzava'at HaRivash 111: full devekut in fervor cannot be constant; cleave constantly to His garments, the letters (S119). Zbarazh (Yosher Divrei Emet I 12): devekut is seeing His vitality in all and reckoning oneself as nothing. Vitebsk (Peri HaAretz): true devekut is unfelt, by faith, bitul of existence. Pillar 02's findings on Polonoye (the few directly, the many through the tzaddik) and on the word's decline in later schools are carried from the earlier work.

## S118 · Run and return · רצוא ושוב

**Faces:** F07 The home below · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** רצוא ושוב · רצוא · והחיות רצוא

> Run and return is the unity of God as a rhythm: the soul runs toward the higher unity, where it would dissolve, and returns to make a dwelling for that unity below.

## Derivation

1. The soul by nature runs to its source and would cease to exist there (Tanya 19, S113). The Besht: if it burned constantly it would be annulled from existence, 'as before the world of Tikkun', so God gave time for bodily needs (Keter Shem Tov 1:146:1). (*stated*)
2. The Besht gives a second reason: a constant delight becomes nature and is no delight; a person rises and falls so that there be delight, which is the main thing in the service (Keter Shem Tov 1:88:1). (*stated*)
3. The Alter Rebbe gives the reason from the unity: when the heart runs to expire into its source, one must say 'perforce you live', because the soul is in the body to draw life below and make 'a dwelling in the lower realms for His unity, revealed', so that the hidden unity become the revealed world (Tanya 50:6; F07). (*stated*)
4. The Maharash (5629): the holy chayos want to be included and nullified in Or Ein Sof, and 'perforce you live' draws the return; the divine vitality itself runs and returns (Toras Shmuel 5629). The Rashab via the Rayatz: running without returning is death (the sons of Aaron); 'the higher a thing, the lower it descends' (Hayom Yom 27 Adar II; G05). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe: the purpose of the running is the return. Accepting the yoke of Heaven is ratzo and self-sacrifice; accepting the yoke of mitzvot is shov; through the second the Essence's intent, the dwelling below, is completed (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Bamidbar 5729). (*stated*)
6. Mapped on the faces: ratzo is the higher unity (the self nothing before Him), shov is the lower unity lived as a dwelling. That mapping is ours, though Tanya 50 comes close to stating it ('that the hidden unity be in the revealed world'). (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 50:4-6 (the Levites' ratzo; shov for the dwelling below); Iggeret HaKodesh 1:5 (read at line 988: ratzo, then shov, awe of His greatness).

## S119 · Fervor · התלהבות

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · F01 The world said into being now **Search terms:** התלהבות · התפעלות · רשפי אש · ברשפי

> Fervor is the unity of God as fire in the soul: the flame that rises from contemplating that all is nothing before Him, true when it burns away the self and false when the self enjoys its burning.

## Derivation

1. The source: the love 'like fiery coals' is kindled by contemplating that all is as nothing before Ein Sof, so the soul flames up to leave the wick (Tanya 50:3; F12). The Rashab repeats it: true love in fiery coals comes from understanding His greatness, filling and surrounding, before whom all is nothing, and it burns up the animal soul's craving (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan pp. 26-27). But he adds at once that this is 'in high levels'; for everyone else the fiery love is kindled by contemplating how far one is from Godliness (scan p. 27). So the tie to 'all is nothing before Him' holds for the flame's source, not for how most people reach it. (*stated*)
2. The Besht's circle ties fervor to presence: 'the Shechinah surely stands opposite me', and from this comes great fervor (Tzava'at HaRivash 68; F01). Serving in fervor is complete devekut (Tzava'at HaRivash 111:2). (*stated*)
3. It cannot be constant; it touches and does not touch, like fire. What can be constant is cleaving to His garments, the letters of Torah, 'for they too are from the Holy One', even in ordinary talk (Tzava'at HaRivash 111:2-3; F01, F10). See S118. (*stated*)
4. The test is bitul. In holiness even felt arousal comes with complete bitul, without feeling oneself; the exaggerated outward arousal comes from the spreading yesh of nogah and can turn to bad (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 9:5-12; F08). Divine arousal is 'Godliness itself, not the arousal of a thing separate from Godliness' (20:10). (*stated*)
5. The Rashab's warning: after general contemplation one may be pleased with one's arousal and fervor, 'that is, the feeling of selfhood', and the traits remain unchanged (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan p. 45). (*stated*)
6. Piaseczno: a flame acquires no place and has no yeshut; fervor that flares at times is not sure to remain, so one also needs the coal, the self worked on in holiness (Derekh HaMelekh, Ha'azinu 7-10). This is the counter-voice from the side of the self (S121). (*stated*)

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 50 (love like fiery coals, from 'all is as nothing before Him'); Tanya 13 (a beinoni's love in prayer is 'true' at his level though it passes).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 9 and 20: holy arousal is without self-feeling; outward exaggeration is the yesh of nogah; divine arousal is 'Godliness itself'. The whole kuntres grades hitpa'alut through the five soul levels to the yechidah.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not quoted in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah, scan pp. 26-27 and 45 (OCR): fiery love from contemplating the unity burns up the animal soul; self-satisfied fervor is 'the feeling of yeshut'. Kuntres HaTefillah: learning without prayer's work becomes more yeshut (S121).
- **Rayatz.** Not quoted in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** Tzava'at HaRivash 68, 111; Keter Shem Tov 1:55:4 ('eish tamid': keep a spark of devekut). Piaseczno (Derekh HaMelekh, Ha'azinu 7-10): flame versus coal. Pillar 10 notes Ger's 'hischadshus' as a later form; not re-verified.

## S120 · Nullification · ביטול

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F05 The Essence beyond both · F07 The home below **Search terms:** ביטול היש · ביטול במציאות · בטל במציאות · ביטול

> Bitul is the unity of God from the person's side: since in truth everything is nothing before Him, holiness rests only on what stops standing as a thing in its own right.

## Derivation

1. If all is as nothing before Him, then not being nullified is 'a denial of His true unity'; holiness rests only on what is nullified to Him (Tanya 22:8; F02, F08). (*stated*)
2. Every mitzvah has this aim: 'the purpose and foundation of all the mitzvot is to turn yesh into ayin', as the parchment of tefillin becomes a vessel for 'One' (Likkutei Torah, Behar 2:18; F10). (*stated*)
3. There are grades. The bitul a person makes is 'not true bitul, since he is still a yesh who nullifies himself', yet it is the arousal from below that draws true ayin from above (Likkutei Torah, Behar 2:19). Homel: the servant's bitul hayesh; the courtier's forgetting of self before the King, which ends when he leaves; and essential bitul, which is how the servants stand in the King's own knowledge, where their existence does not register (Chanah Ariel, Noach 3:2; F02). (*stated*)
4. Its faculty is Chochmah, which knows 'there is nothing besides Him'; felt love still carries 'the lover' (Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 4). The Tzemach Tzedek: at Modim we concede the higher knowledge, that above is the true yesh and below is nothing, but only as concession, not sight (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 23). (*stated*)
5. The Maggid gives the direction: heaven and earth were yesh from ayin; tzaddikim make ayin from yesh, even through eating (Maggid 6:2). (*stated*)
6. The Rebbe gives the purpose: the higher bitul is the one that reaches the person's existence itself, and only through it is the dwelling below completed (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Bamidbar 5729; F07). The Rashab's formula is the same from the other side: the purpose of creating yesh is that it become nullified to ayin (spine F07e). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 19, 22, 35; Shaar HaYichud 3 (creatures nullified in existence like the ray in the sun); Likkutei Torah, Behar 2:18-19 (bitul hayesh versus true bitul). Via the Rayatz: 'remove the chametz of selfhood, bring in the matzah of bitul' (S121).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 9 (holy arousal without self-feeling, S119); Toras Chaim, Mishpatim (two lowlinesses, S122).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 23: two knowledges; Modim as concession of the higher.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5629 (S118): the chayos run to be nullified in Or Ein Sof.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Eitz HaChaim (spine F08e, F07e): contemplating the unity nullifies one 'at least for the hour'; the purpose of yesh is to become batel.
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus, scan p. 21 (S121).
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Bamidbar 5729: bitul of midbar (bitul of existence) and of 'yom hazeh' (existence that is itself batel); only the latter completes the dwelling.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (6:2, 19:1). Homel (Chanah Ariel, Noach 3:2): three grades. Strashelye (Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 4; IV 17:5). Zbarazh (Yosher Divrei Emet I 12:3) quotes the Maggid: tzaddikim return yesh to ayin. Pillar 07 reports Berditchev's 'white' and the Chozeh on humility; not re-verified.

## S121 · The separate self · ישות

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F06 The soul as part of the One · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** ישות · מציאות לעצמו · יש ודבר נפרד · ישותו

> The separate self is the unity of God denied from inside: the creature taking its apparent independence, which He grants and hides in, as its own, saying 'I, and there is none besides me'.

## Derivation

1. Creatures appear to be things in themselves only because His vitality hides in them (F03, SH 4). Kelipah takes the appearance for the truth: it 'raises itself like an eagle, saying I and there is none besides me', and so is called other gods (Tanya 22:7). (*stated*)
2. To be 'a yesh and a thing in itself' is the opposite of Chochmah, and therefore called death (Tanya 19:8). Gassut haruach is literally idolatry, because the root of idolatry is regarding oneself as separate (Tanya 22:8, spine F08a). (*stated*)
3. It survives even inside holiness. The Rashab: without the work of prayer, Chassidus learned becomes poison and makes the learner more of a yesh (Kuntres HaTefillah, scan p. 21). The Alter Rebbe to a learned newcomer: remove the chametz of selfhood (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus, scan p. 21). (*stated*)
4. Yet some yesh is needed. The Besht via the Degel: the 'yesh' in Yisrael keeps the worlds from returning to ayin, but a Jew should keep only as much yeshut as their existence needs (Degel, Vaetchanan 12). (*stated*)
5. Piaseczno goes furthest: only the Creator exists singly and the world has no yeshut; but a Jew, held by the King's 'body' and not only His garments, receives a spark of yeshut from above, which is why his will begins in himself and he can choose (Derekh HaMelekh, Ha'azinu 8; F06, F05). He must work that yeshut into holiness (Ha'azinu 10, 12). (*stated*)
6. R. Nosson: pride is the yesh not included in the ayin; the repair is to fold the yesh (after creation) into the ayin (before creation), as greatness and humility are one in Him (Likutei Halakhot, Kilayim 2:4; S122). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 6, 19, 22 (kelipah = yesh; 'I and none besides'); via the Rayatz, chametz = yeshut.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 9:5-10 (the vessel of nogah is yeshut that spreads beyond its light).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 23 (S120): the creature sees itself as yesh from below.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah, scan pp. 18-21 (OCR): coarsening deep matters with 'the yeshut of comprehension'; learning without prayer increases yeshut; pride is 'the extreme of yeshut'.
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus, scan p. 21 (OCR): transmits the Alter Rebbe's chametz and matzah.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** The Degel (Vaetchanan 12, from the Besht): yeshut rationed to what the world's existence needs. Piaseczno (Derekh HaMelekh, Ha'azinu 7-12): yeshut is a spark of the King's body, the root of choice; the flame without the coal has no yeshut (S119). R. Nosson (Likutei Halakhot, Kilayim 2:4): pride as yesh not in ayin.

## S122 · Humility · ענוה ושפלות

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F05 The Essence beyond both · F09 The unity of opposites **Search terms:** ענוה · שפלות · שפל רוח · ענותנותו

> Humility is the unity of God as nearness measures it: since all is nothing 'before Him', the nearer one comes, the more one is nothing, and that lowliness is where the Essence rests.

## Derivation

1. Everything is as nothing specifically 'before Him', so whoever is more 'before Him' is more nothing; hence with every kindness, which is nearness, one must become lower (Iggeret HaKodesh 2:2; F02). (*stated*)
2. The Besht: to praise, praise God; to disparage, disparage yourself, because the one who praises affirms the unity and the one who disparages others separates from it (Keter Shem Tov 1:41). Pride removes the yud from 'ani' and leaves 'an'; when you think yourself ayin you come near (1:42). (*stated*)
3. Zbarazh: holy height is endless longing, and 'the more one cleaves to God, the more one feels one's lowliness and poverty' (Yosher Divrei Emet I 31). (*stated*)
4. The Mitteler Rebbe distinguishes two lowlinesses: the crushed self of a pauper, still wholly there, and lowliness in the essence of the soul, which becomes a vessel for the Essence's exaltedness (Toras Chaim, Mishpatim; F05, G05). The bitul path (earlier work, not re-verified here) makes this the line between avodah and sadness. (*stated*)
5. R. Nosson: in Him greatness and humility are one, 'where His greatness is, there His humility'; creation is His robe of greatness (yesh), humility is ayin, and pride is the yesh not folded into ayin (Likutei Halakhot, Kilayim 2:4; F09). (*stated*)
6. It is not always self-crushing. Piaseczno: one already fixed in service, in the hour of greatness, need not belittle himself; his selfhood is nullified of itself before the holiness he feels. The one still working to keep from sin must belittle himself ('know whence you came') (Derekh HaMelekh, Vayetzei 2:8). Hayom Yom: Chassidus shows how small a person is and how high he can rise. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Iggeret HaKodesh 2 (Katonti: nearness = lowliness, the trait of Yaakov and Avraham, 'I am dust and ashes'); Tanya 22 (pride as idolatry).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim, Mishpatim ('Lo tihyeh meshakelah'): two lowlinesses; essential lowliness as vessel for the Essence.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah, scan p. 21 (S121): pride as 'the extreme of yeshut'.
- **Rayatz.** Not located by name in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Hayom Yom, which he compiled from the Rayatz: 19 Iyar (unattributed): Chassidus shows 'how small he is and how high he can rise'; 20 Iyar (read, not quoted): with belittling others and raising oneself all is lost.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:41-42). Zbarazh (Yosher Divrei Emet I 12, 27-31: height and lowliness both needed; false lowliness keeps one far). R. Nosson (Likutei Halakhot, Kilayim 2). Piaseczno (Derekh HaMelekh, Vayetzei 2:8). Pillar 07 reports the Chozeh and Yismach Moshe (humility received as gift); not re-verified.

## S123 · Self-sacrifice · מסירות נפש

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F05 The Essence beyond both · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** מסירות נפש · מסירת נפש · במסירות נפש · מסירת נפשו

> Self-sacrifice is the unity of God as the soul's refusal to be separated from it: the point of Ein Sof in every Jew that gives up life rather than deny the One, and so draws down what is above reason.

## Derivation

1. Even the unlearned and sinners give their lives rather than deny the one God, without reason or argument, because 'the one God shines in and gives life to the whole soul' through its Chochmah, which is above understanding (Tanya 18:11-14; F06). (*stated*)
2. The hidden love includes awe: not to be separated in any way from His oneness, even at the cost of life; and since the light in the soul is above time, a Jew will not bow to idols even for a moment and repent later (Tanya 25:2-5). (*stated*)
3. The Mitteler Rebbe: being asked to separate entirely from the One awakens the innermost power, the yechidah, which does not come from effort but from the soul's essential bond with its source (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 28:26-30; F05). Hayom Yom 17 Tishrei: trials reveal the yechidah, 'yechidah to unite You'. (*stated*)
4. The Tzemach Tzedek: giving the soul to God is 'itself the main thing, for which man was created'. Because it is above reason, it draws the Or Ein Sof that is above Chochmah, the encompassing light, to be revealed 'below, below' among Israel (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Kiddush Hashem; F04, F07). (*stated*)
5. Hayom Yom 1 Adar I reads the abbreviation in Torah Ohr, 'bimesirat nefesh bifnei atzmo', as 'in the inwardness of His Essence' (F05). The Rayatz: the soul embraced in the Essence bears the yoke to self-sacrifice, and this is stronger in simple folk, whose essence shines without the garments of intellect (Sefer HaMaamarim 5689). (*stated*)
6. Other schools tie it to other unities. R. Nachman: the essence of the yichud of the Holy One and His Shechinah comes through self-sacrifice (Likutei Moharan 260). R. Tzadok: Avraham's refusal to deny comes from knowing there is a Creator; Yitzchak's positive offering comes from 'I have set God before me always' (Resisei Layla 47:2). That these are different senses of 'unity' is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 18-19, 25 (self-sacrifice from Chochmah where Ein Sof is clothed; not to be separated from His oneness); Torah Ohr, Lo tihyeh meshakelah (via Hayom Yom 1 Adar I).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 28:24-31: the yechidah shines revealed in kiddush Hashem; if it shone always one would never sin.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Kiddush Hashem: self-sacrifice is the purpose of man and draws the surrounding light below; Kedushah recited daily is self-sacrifice 'at least in potential' (this last in the next section, the mitzvah of reciting Kedushah).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5629 via the Rebbe: the first paragraph of Shema is ratzo and self-sacrifice (S118).
- **Rashab.** Not quoted in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 and 5692: the power of self-sacrifice is from Chochmah, essential bitul; strongest in simple people.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Bamidbar 5729: naaseh is 'the bitul of self-sacrifice, the highest bitul', but it is the person's bitul, and exists for the sake of the mitzvot (S116).
- **Other schools.** R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 260): self-sacrifice effects the yichud above; souls returning to the Shechinah. R. Tzadok (Resisei Layla 47): two inherited kinds, Avraham's and Yitzchak's. Pillar 07 reports Radomsk (Torah endures in one who dies over it); not re-verified.

## S124 · Truth · אמת

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F05 The Essence beyond both · F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' **Search terms:** אמת · אמיתית · אמת לאמיתו · מדת האמת

> Truth is the unity of God as what is really so: He alone is, and every creaturely truth is as nothing before Him, so to be true is to concede, and in the end to see, that there is nothing besides Him.

## Derivation

1. The Alter Rebbe defines it: the truth of 'Havayah Elokim is truth' is His unity, that He alone is and there is literally nothing besides Him (Tanya 35:5). His note, from the Maggid: Ein Sof is 'the true One', and this is the level of Chochmah (35:7). (*stated*)
2. The Tzemach Tzedek makes it a commandment: the mitzvah of unity is 'to know the truth that He is one and there is nothing else' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Achdut Hashem). (*stated*)
3. From this, creaturely truth is relative: His seal is truth, 'the true truth', and all the truth in creatures is as nothing before Him; so only love and awe given from above are called truth (Iggeret HaKodesh 6:10; F02). Yaakov's trait of truth runs through all levels, and each level is as nothing before the one above it (Tanya 13:13-14). (*stated*)
4. The two knowledges are two truths. At Modim we concede that the truth is as it is before Him, that above is the true yesh and below is nothing, but 'only as concession, not in sight' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 23; F12, G02). (*stated*)
5. R. Tzadok: this world is a world of falsehood because the truth is 'You are He before the world was created', all complete unity; Yaakov's truth is cleaving by union, not by recognition (Resisei Layla 47:3). (*stated*)
6. Truth as a human virtue (not lying, examining every motion 'as it truly is', Hayom Yom 2 Adar I; Sefat Emet's truthful speech as testimony of a Godly power in man) is tied to the unity only through 'His seal is truth'. That link is stated by Sefat Emet for truth in general; for the ethics of honesty in particular it is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 13 (relative truth by level), 35 (truth = His unity), 44 (the hidden love 'truth of truth'); Iggeret HaKodesh 6 (His seal is truth; creaturely truth as nothing). Hayom Yom 2 Adar I: he set the chassidim to 'search for the truth' and examine every motion.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Achdut Hashem (to know the truth that He is one) and Tefillah 23 (Modim concedes the truth of the higher knowledge).
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Hayom Yom 27 Adar I (read, not quoted; 'my father said', i.e. the Rashab via the Rayatz): truth is the middle path, strict with oneself.
- **Rayatz.** Transmits the Hayom Yom sayings. Hayom Yom 20 Adar I (unattributed, read, not quoted): 'not that the service be in truth, but truth itself is a service'.
- **The Rebbe.** Compiled Hayom Yom; not quoted directly in this pass.
- **Other schools.** R. Tzadok (Resisei Layla 47:3): this world is falsehood, truth is complete unity. Sefat Emet (Vayishlach 28:4): His seal is truth, so truth in a person is a Godly power, reached through Torah and covenant. Pillar 02 notes that the Polish schools speak of emet where the founders spoke of devekut; not re-verified here.
