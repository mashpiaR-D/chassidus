*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Weekly talks (sichos) on Vayikra, Bamidbar and Devarim, as the Rebbe edited them. Each one works through a close problem in Rashi, the Rambam or halacha and ends with a lesson for inner service. Volume 39 adds long commentaries on his father's notes to Iggeres HaTeshuvah. Each volume has a section of letters, sorted by parsha. The letters are where the Rebbe answers individuals who are sad, despairing, bereaved, worried about money or health, or stuck in their return.

**How much of it is medicine.** some — most of the text is Rashi, Rambam, halachic or Kabbalistic analysis, and the concentrated medicine sits in the appended pastoral letters plus a few sichos (Metzora, Emor) and parts of the teshuvah commentary.

**Its model of the person.** A soul in a body with a good and an evil inclination, set up that way so there is choice. By nature the mind rules the heart. The wisdom-level of the soul can fall 'asleep', but its essence (yechida) never sleeps, so change is waking up, not creating something new. Moods and thoughts are judged by what they produce. Change comes through simple doing, not introspection, and self-nullification through action goes deeper than through understanding. A returnee starts from feeling above reason.

**Its philosophy.** G-d is the essence of good, and it is the nature of the good to do good. His Providence reaches every person and every detail, so every event holds good, even in this world. Nature and human effort also come from Him and carry His intent. The Torah is a 'Torah of life' for daily living. Every Jew's essence stays untouched, so return (teshuvah) is a going back to one's own source.

**What it adds.** The appended letters give concrete, operational tools. Test thoughts by what they breed. Divert attention rather than argue. Answer the urge to introspect with 'I am simple, not an investigator'. Expose despair by noticing it leaves the appetites intact. Treat teshuvah as return to self, not regret. There is also a halachic map of degrees of compulsion (Metzora), the claim that the essence never sleeps (Emor), and practical guidance on grief, prognosis and livelihood.

## I keep sinking into sadness and heaviness and can't shake it

A standing low mood, sometimes taken for seriousness about one's failings; serving from gloom; mood swings that sap daily practice.

**What is really going on.** The inclination's tactic to weaken him from acting, dressed up as fear of heaven. Sadness gets in the way of service, so it is not holiness.

**Program: Learn the Tanya chapters against sadness until fluent.** (1) Ask the mashpia (spiritual mentor) to study with you the Tanya chapters that expose sadness and show how to negate it. (2) Review them several times until you know their content well. (3) Alongside: have tefillin checked; keep the daily portions of Chumash, Tehillim and Tanya from now on. *When:* Repeated study over weeks until fluent; the portions every day. *For:* Someone with recurring spells of sadness who has a learning setting or a mentor. *Not for:* Clinical depression treated by study alone, without professional care. `LS3·Q03`

**Program: Divert attention from yourself (hesech hada'as).** (1) Stop monitoring your mood; treat the urge to examine your state as the inclination's lure. (2) Answer it: I am simple, not an investigator; I have no time; my task is to do His will. (3) For now, drop thoughts about yourself, self-accounting included. (4) Sink your thought into Torah, prayer and your job. (5) Do not argue with the thoughts intellectually; diversion is the effective tactic. *When:* Whenever the pull to self-scrutiny comes; framed as 'for now', a temporary phase. *For:* People caught in loops of self-examination and mood-checking. *Not for:* Someone who needs honest reckoning with a real wrong, or clinical OCD needing treatment. `LS3·Q04` · `LS3·Q05` · `LS3·Q06`

**Program: Test regret thoughts by their fruits.** (1) When regret thoughts come often, ask which side they come from. (2) Judge by what they produce: more energy and a steadier daily order mean a good source. (3) If they breed sadness, sloth, laziness or despair, they come from the evil inclination dressed as piety. (4) Turn the energy into good deeds, not despairing thoughts. (5) Keep the regret joined with joy that return is possible and nothing stands before it. *When:* Each time such thoughts arise, briefly. *For:* The guilt-prone and scrupulous; the recently bereaved who blame themselves. *Not for:* Someone avoiding responsibility for harm done to others. `LS3·Q07` · `LS3·Q08` · `LS3·Q09`

## I've given up; there's no point trying anymore

He announces to others and to himself that he is hopeless, so nothing more can be asked of him.

**What is really going on.** Despair is one of the inclination's clearest tactics for exempting a person from duty. The sign: despair does nothing to lessen his physical appetites.

**Program: Test regret thoughts by their fruits.** (1) When regret thoughts come often, ask which side they come from. (2) Judge by what they produce: more energy and a steadier daily order mean a good source. (3) If they breed sadness, sloth, laziness or despair, they come from the evil inclination dressed as piety. (4) Turn the energy into good deeds, not despairing thoughts. (5) Keep the regret joined with joy that return is possible and nothing stands before it. *When:* Each time such thoughts arise, briefly. *For:* The guilt-prone and scrupulous; the recently bereaved who blame themselves. *Not for:* Someone avoiding responsibility for harm done to others. `LS3·Q07` · `LS3·Q08` · `LS3·Q09`

**Reframe.** From *"I've given up; I'm hopeless."* to **Despair is a tactic to excuse you from what is asked of you. Look: it has not dimmed your physical desires.** Once despair is shown to be motivated rather than a perception of truth, it stops being an exemption. `LS3·Q01` · `LS3·Q02`

## My return isn't real; I still can't control myself

A newcomer to observance who still slips concludes his teshuvah is fake, or that returning out of fear is worthless.

**What is really going on.** The conclusion comes from the inclination. His direction is from left to right. The essence of return is leaving the sin, and even return out of fear is inwardly the soul going back to its source.

**Program: The two ways of lower return.** (1) Arouse compassion over the G-dliness in your own soul, which your actions have exiled. (2) Humble the self through a broken, contrite heart, so the acceptance is felt inwardly. (3) Remember the essence is leaving the sin; fasting is not the essence. *When:* In seasons of return; the text gives no fixed schedule. *For:* Someone whose commitment is only notional and who is emotionally stable. *Not for:* The depressed or self-punishing; the letters warn that regret breeding sadness is from the inclination. `LS3·Q31` · `LS3·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"My return is fake; I still can't control myself."* to **You are moving from left to right. Return means going back to your own source; its essence is leaving the sin.** Measuring by direction rather than a finished state removes the proof that it is fake. Return becomes coming home, not a verdict. `LS3·Q10` · `LS3·Q21` · `LS3·Q22` · `LS3·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"I came late and from far away; I'm second-class."* to **The returnee's attachment starts from feeling above reason, a total self-nullification the tzaddik's reasoned service lacks.** The distance itself becomes the source of a deeper bond. `LS3·Q30`

**Reframe.** From *"I only return out of fear of punishment; it's worthless."* to **Even the lowest return is inwardly the soul turning to G-d; the body only makes it look like fear.** His motive is re-read as the soul's own movement, so the return is not despised. `LS3·Q29`

## I'm trapped in a habit; I'm not free anymore

He wants out but feels forced from inside, and blames himself for having brought it on.

**What is really going on.** There are grades: an involuntary residue in everyone (a light matter), evil added by provoking oneself, and entrenchment in which he becomes compelled. Even the last has a remedy.

**Reframe.** From *"I'm trapped, and it's my fault, so there's no way out."* to **Some pull is an involuntary human residue and a light matter. Even someone deeply entrenched has a remedy.** Separating residue from cultivated evil eases blanket guilt; a sure remedy restores agency. `LS3·Q23` · `LS3·Q24` · `LS3·Q26`

## The more I grow, the more faults I find; am I getting worse?

After real progress he is troubled by subtle failings he never noticed before.

**What is really going on.** Rising in level makes finer faults count, just as a stain disqualifies a scholar's garment but not an unlearned man's.

**Reframe.** From *"I keep finding new faults; I'm getting worse."* to **Finer faults show up because you have risen.** New self-criticism becomes a sign of ascent, not decline. `LS3·Q25`

## My inner life feels dead; I'd have to become someone new

Spiritual numbness that feels permanent.

**What is really going on.** The wisdom-level of the soul only sleeps. The essence of the heart is always awake, so what is needed is waking up.

**Reframe.** From *"My soul is dead; I'd have to become new."* to **The core of the heart is always awake; what is needed is waking from sleep.** Waking up feels possible where becoming someone new does not. `LS3·Q27` · `LS3·Q28`

## I lost someone close and now I'm alone

Grief turning into the conclusion that life is now loneliness.

**What is really going on.** The conclusion does not follow. The soul lives on, freed of the body's limits, and the living have been handed an added role, with the strength for it given beforehand.

**Program: After a loss: search for the good and turn it into action.** (1) Acknowledge the sense of loss, then search for the good in the event. (2) Continue in practice the activities the departed was engaged in. (3) Know the strength for this added role was given beforehand. (4) Push away thoughts that confuse you or diminish your energy. (5) Increase activity among people, with more energy and wider scope; use your talents for many. *When:* Ongoing, as a way of arranging life after the loss. *For:* The bereaved who are sliding into isolation. *Not for:* The raw first days of grief; not a demand to skip mourning. `LS3·Q12` · `LS3·Q13` · `LS3·Q14`

**Reframe.** From *"With this loss, I'm alone."* to **No room for a conclusion of loneliness: the soul is freed of limits, your deeds reach it, and you were given strength for your added role.** Grief is turned into a mission among people, which keeps a link with the departed. `LS3·Q12` · `LS3·Q13` · `LS3·Q14`

## I can't stop worrying about money, health, the future

Anxiety or doubt over livelihood, illness or fertility prognoses; either passivity or feverish over-effort.

**What is really going on.** Losing sight of Providence over every detail. Effort in natural means is required but is only a vessel; the blessing comes from Above.

**Program: Trust with real effort, without heat.** (1) Take up livelihood (or treatment) as real engagement, not waiting for a burst of desire. (2) Do it without heat or a divided heart: in the end all depends on G-d's blessing. (3) Follow the doctors and your community rav in practical matters. (4) Know clearly that G-d is the Healer and oversees every detail; from that, peace of mind. (5) Do not rely on amulets. *When:* Daily life; regular set times for Torah alongside work. *For:* The anxious, the passive who 'wait for Providence', those facing a grim prognosis. *Not for:* Anyone who would take it as leave to ignore medical advice. `LS3·Q15` · `LS3·Q16` · `LS3·Q20`

**Reframe.** From *"Everything hangs on my effort, or on the doctors' odds."* to **Do the natural part fully but without heat; the outcome comes from Above.** Effort stays; the anxious heat goes, giving peace of mind. `LS3·Q15` · `LS3·Q16` · `LS3·Q20`

## The world looks dark to me

A habitual negative lens on people and events.

**What is really going on.** How one sees depends mostly on the seer, not the world. The mind can be made to rule, filled with trust in G-d as the essence of good.

**Program: Change the lens: let the mind rule.** (1) Notice that what you see depends mostly on you, the seer. (2) Look for the good examples as well as the bad in the people around you. (3) Fill the mind with trust in G-d as the essence of good, and let it rule the heart. (4) Treat events as tests; make your small world a place of joy and light. *When:* A standing discipline of perception. *For:* People who have grown used to a negative lens. *Not for:* Someone whose darkness is clinical; the text's 'lack of will' line would shame them. `LS3·Q17` · `LS3·Q18`

**Reframe.** From *"The world is dark."* to **How you see depends mostly on you, and you can let the mind rule.** Moving the problem from the world to the lens puts it within his control. `LS3·Q17` · `LS3·Q18`

## My commitment stays external; my heart doesn't feel it

Accepting obligations in principle while the heart stays untouched.

**What is really going on.** Accepting the yoke has two parts: feeling the King (compassion over the G-dliness in one's soul) and subduing the self (a broken heart), which lets the yoke sink inward.

**Program: The two ways of lower return.** (1) Arouse compassion over the G-dliness in your own soul, which your actions have exiled. (2) Humble the self through a broken, contrite heart, so the acceptance is felt inwardly. (3) Remember the essence is leaving the sin; fasting is not the essence. *When:* In seasons of return; the text gives no fixed schedule. *For:* Someone whose commitment is only notional and who is emotionally stable. *Not for:* The depressed or self-punishing; the letters warn that regret breeding sadness is from the inclination. `LS3·Q31` · `LS3·Q32`

## Cautions

- Calling sadness 'the inclination' can add shame for someone with clinical depression; the text itself defers to doctors, so refer to professional care. - 'If it isn't happening, it's lack of will, not ability' (Q18 context) can wound the depressed or traumatized; don't use it as a verdict. - The broken heart and self-subduing of return (G07) must not be pressed on the depressed or self-punishing. - Diversion helps rumination but is not treatment for OCD or scrupulosity; don't let it replace care. - Trust letters say to follow doctors; never use them to dismiss a prognosis or promise outcomes (fertility, illness). - The bereavement letter pushes activity; don't rush someone in the first raw days of grief. - Segulos (checking tefillin/mezuzos, tzedakah) are addressed to observant Jews; present them without magical promises (he rejects amulets).
