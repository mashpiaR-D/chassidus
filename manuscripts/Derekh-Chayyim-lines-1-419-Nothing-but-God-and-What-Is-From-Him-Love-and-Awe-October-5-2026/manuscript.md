Derekh Chayyim ("The Way of Life") is the Maharal's commentary on Pirkei Avot (Ethics of the Fathers). This range covers his introduction, the opening mishna, and the first two chapters. The book is about musar (the training of character). But the Maharal grounds every rule of character in one claim about G-d: nothing stands by itself, and all being is from Him.

Five teachings matter most for the guide:

- **Nothing in being but G-d and what is from Him.** "כי אין במציאות רק השם יתברך, שהוא מחויב המציאות, והנמצאים שנמצאו מאתו" (74): "there is nothing in being but G-d, who must exist, and the things that exist from Him." So the world must serve, because "if it existed for itself, there would be something besides G-d" (79).
- **Love and awe have one root** (88-91). Love comes from knowing His greatness. Awe comes from knowing "G-d is in heaven and you are on earth." Each keeps the other honest.
- **This world is split, so a person's work is to join.** "שיהיה האדם מחבר הכל, עד שהכל אחד" (139): "that a person join everything until all is one." G-d says: "I am One, and so the world that is Mine is one" (180).
- **Prayer is supplication.** A person must pray as one who needs, not as one who is owed (370). The pious who prayed this way reached "the stripping off of the physical" (397).
- **A person is made to move, not to rest.** What is complete rests. A person is always moving toward his completion, and that moving is itself his completion (301-303).

## 2.1 Nothing but G-d and what is from Him

The Maharal says it twice. First in the opening mishna: "כי אין במציאות רק השם יתברך, ואשר נמצאים מאתו" (38): "for there is nothing in being but G-d and what exists from Him." Then in the three pillars: "כי אין במציאות רק השם יתברך, שהוא מחויב המציאות, והנמצאים שנמצאו מאתו" (74): "there is nothing in being but G-d, who must exist, and the things that exist from Him."

From this he draws why the world rests on service. The world "אי אפשר שיהיה עומד בעצמו, רק על ידי השם יתברך אשר מאתו הכל" (77): "cannot stand by itself, only through G-d, from whom is all." If the world were a thing for itself, "כי אם נמצא לעצמו, הרי יש חס ושלום דבר זולת השם" (79): "if it existed for itself, there would be, G-d forbid, something besides G-d." Service is the cure: "כל אשר משמש לזולתו, אין עליו שם בפני עצמו" (79): "whatever serves another has no name of its own." The world that serves Him is not a second thing beside Him.

A related rule: "כי הדבר שאינו טוב בעצמו, אין ראוי שיהיה לו המציאות" (69): "what is not good in itself has no right to exist." Things are because of the good in them. The three pillars are how the world is good: good in itself (Torah), good to its Maker (service), and good to others (kindness).

## 2.2 Only He has no pair

"ואינו יחיד בעולם, כי אם השם יתברך, שהוא יחיד מיוחד בעולם" (39): "nothing is alone in the world except G-d, who is single and one of a kind." Everything made has a pair. And "כי הפעולה שהיא אחת, מעידה על הפועל שהוא אחד" (41): "the act that is one testifies that the Actor is One."

## 2.3 What is one has rest

"הדבר שהוא אחד אין כנגדו דבר מתנגד לו" (43): "what is one has nothing set against it." Unrest comes from being opposed. "אבל הדבר שהוא אחד, אין לו התנגדות, לכך יש בו המנוחה" (43): "but what is one has no opposition, so it has rest." This is why Shabbat, the day of rest, is tied to "You are One." The same rule gives life to the middle: the center of a circle "אין שייך בה קצה וסוף, לכך יש בה החיים" (28): "has no edge and no end, so life is in it."

## 2.4 This world is split; man joins it

"העולם הזה הוא עולם החלוק והפירוד" (135): "this world is the world of division and split." That is why quarrels are common. Hillel's disciple must "love peace and pursue it", and the Maharal gives the goal: "שיהיה האדם מחבר הכל, עד שהכל אחד" (139): "that a person join everything until all is one."

Why? "כי העולם הוא מן השם יתברך שהוא אחד" (179): "for the world is from G-d, who is One," so the world should be one. The midrash says man was made from the upper and the lower worlds so there would be peace in G-d's world. The Maharal reads G-d's words: "שאני אחד, וכך העולם שהוא שלי הוא אחד" (180): "for I am One, and so the world that is Mine is one." Man, who holds all the worlds in himself, "והוא מקשר ומאחד את העולם, עד שהוא מקושר לגמרי, והוא עולם אחד" (180): "binds and unites the world, until it is fully bound and is one world."

People quarrel "במה שכל אחד רוצה שיהיה הוא הכל" (171): "because each one wants to be all." The cure is peace, and "ולפיכך השלום משלים הכל" (174): "so peace completes everything." Peace (shalom) is from the word for whole (shalem): without the bond between parts, each part is lacking.

## 2.5 From Him and back to Him

"כי העולם הזה הוא בא מן השם יתברך, ואליו שב כאשר הושלם" (194): "this world comes from G-d, and returns to Him when it is complete." The world is tied to Him at its start and at its end.

## 2.6 He is utterly simple

A borrower who does not repay takes from others. The Maharal contrasts this with G-d: "כי הוא יתברך פשוט בתכלית הפשיטות" (345): "He is simple with the utmost simplicity," and "כי הפשוט עומד בעצמו, אינו מקבל מזולתו" (346): "the simple stands by itself and takes from no other." To be simple, for a person, is to stand on what is his and not live off others.

## 2.7 The eye that sees and the ear that hears

Against philosophers who said G-d knows only Himself, the Maharal says He sees and hears each person (223-226): "כי השם יתברך מקבל את מעשה בני אדם" (224): "for G-d receives the deeds of people." They are not lost: "שמעשה האדם יש להם ציור מושכל" (226): "a person's deeds have a form the mind can grasp," and that form lasts.

- **MH-S32 "Still becoming" (236, 301-303).** Inside: I am not done, and that is not a failure. I feel myself moving toward what I can be, and the moving is my wholeness now. There is a kind of safety in it: the Maharal says the evil inclination has no power "over what is still becoming" (236). How to enter: when you feel you have "arrived" or feel stuck, say: I was made to move, not to rest (301). Take the next small act. It differs from S47 Running, which is longing toward Him. This state is peace with being unfinished.
- **MH-S33 "One will" (249-250).** Inside: I want what He wants, not by force but because my will has been tied to His "until the person's will is G-d's will." How to enter: before a choice, say "His will, and so mine." It differs from S62 Only Him (where the self is not there). Here the self is there and wants the same.
- **MH-S34 "Prayer with nothing between" (370, 397).** Inside: I stand as one who needs everything, and in that need the body stops being a wall. How to enter: pray as supplication, with no claim that I deserve an answer (370). Say the words out loud (371). It differs from S55 Own words to Him, which is talking to Him in my own words. This is the stripping of the barrier that comes from full dependence.

## 4.1 Three kinds of wholeness (`mh_three_wholes`, new)

The Introduction sets three tractates side by side. A person "צריך האדם שיהיה שלם עם זולתו" (11): "must be whole with others" (the laws of damages), whole in himself (Avot), and whole with his Maker (Blessings). Of the last: one who blesses on everything, "וזהו הדביקות בו יתברך" (16): "this is the cleaving to Him." Practice at night: Was I whole with others today? Was I whole in myself? Did I bless Him on what He gave?

## 4.2 Love, then awe (`mh_love_then_awe`, new)

Serve for love, not for reward. The core of service is to love Him "ובזה יש לו דביקות בו יתברך מצד עצמו" (88): "and through this he cleaves to Him for His own sake." Love comes "כאשר יכיר גדולתו ורוממותו" (88): "when he knows His greatness and His height." But "מדרך מי שאוהב את אחד לבו קשורה בו, ודבר זה מבטל היראה" (90): "one who loves another has his heart tied to him, and this cancels awe." So add awe: "השם יתברך בשמים ואתה על הארץ" (90): "G-d is in heaven and you are on earth." In the end "כי עיקר האהבה והיראה יש להם שורש אחד" (91): "love and awe have one root."

Practice: think one thing about His greatness until the heart warms. Then say: He is in heaven and I am on earth. Hold both. Love brings the doing; awe guards from sin (109).

## 4.3 Love G-d, love His creatures (`mh_love_his_creatures`, new)

"מי שהוא אוהב השם יתברך, אוהב את הבריות שהם ברואיו יתברך" (112): "one who loves G-d loves the creatures, for they are His creatures." And honor your friend because he is "חבירך שהוא בדמיון של מי שאמר והיה העולם" (382): "your friend, who is in the likeness of the One who spoke and the world came to be." Practice: when someone annoys you, say: he is His creature and in His likeness. Bring near, do not push away.

## 4.4 Join until one (`mh_join_until_one`, new)

When there is a quarrel, the peacemaker goes to the other person himself (140). Aim at the goal in 139: join the parts until they are one. Remember why people quarrel: each wants to be all (171). Practice: before you speak in a fight, ask: am I trying to be all? Then find the word that makes the two sides one whole (174).

## 4.5 Silence so the mind can work (`mh_silence_for_mind`, new)

"כאשר ישתוק האדם, אז השכל פועל פעולתו" (162): "when a person is silent, then the mind does its work." Speech is tied to the body; the mind is quiet. Practice: before learning or deciding, keep a few minutes of silence. Not emptiness: let the mind do its work on one clear thought.

## 4.6 Look up (`mh_look_up`, new)

"ברא השם יתברך את האדם בקומה זקופה, כדי שיסתכל כלפי מעלה" (228): "G-d made man standing upright, so that he would look upward," and know that He sits in heaven, sees and hears. Practice: stand straight. Lift your eyes. Say: an eye sees, an ear hears, and all my deeds are written (223-226).

## 4.7 Supplication (`mh_supplication`, new)

Prayer is "כי העבודה כאשר האדם צריך אל השם יתברך, ונתלה בו יתברך לגמרי" (370): "service is when a person needs G-d and hangs on Him fully." If one prays as if G-d ought to do what he asks, he is not hanging on Him fully. "ועיקר התפלה הוא הדבור" (371): "and the heart of prayer is the speaking." The pious "שהיו מתבודדין בתפלה, עד שהיו מגיעים להתפשטות הגשמיות" (397): "were alone with G-d in prayer until they reached the stripping off of the physical." Supplication does this: "שבזה מסלק הגשמית אשר הוא המפריד בין השם יתברך ובין האדם" (397): "by this he removes the physical, which is what separates G-d and man." Practice: pray with the words out loud, as one who needs, asking nothing as owed. This is MH-S34.

## 4.8 Return every day (`mh_return_today`, new)

"Return one day before your death." No one knows that day, so the Maharal says: "שיהיה בתשובה כל ימיו, שימות בתוך התשובה" (357): "that he be in return all his days, that he die in the midst of return." Practice: each night, one line of return. Not a long list; a turning.

## 4.9 Toil, not finishing (`mh_toil_not_finish`, new)

"לא נתנה התורה רק שיהיה האדם עמל בה" (410): "the Torah was given only so that a person toil in it," not so that he finish it. "בעמל התורה כל עמל שלימות בפני עצמו" (415): "in the toil of Torah each toil is a completion of its own." And man is made to move: "וזהו שלימתו בעצמו, שהוא מתנועע אל הפעל, ומוציא שלימתו תמיד אל הפעל" (303): "this is his very completion: he moves toward act and always brings his completion out into act." Practice: when a task feels too large, do one piece and count it as whole. This is MH-S32.

## 4.10 Fix the trait (`mh_fix_the_trait`, new)

Cleave to a good trait "עד שתהא המדה קבועה בנפשו" (313): "until the trait is fixed in his soul," until it becomes nature. Practice: pick one trait (a good eye, a good heart). Do it on purpose every day for a set time.

## 5. The big human questions

**Is the world real, or is only G-d real?** Both, in order. There is nothing in being but G-d and what is from Him (38, 74). The world is real as "from Him", not as a thing for itself (79). It is not a rival.

**Why can't I rest?** Because rest belongs to what is one and complete (43, 301). A person is made to move toward his completion. That is not a curse. It is how he is whole now (303). And while he is still becoming, the evil inclination cannot hold him (236).

**Why do people fight?** Because this world is split (135), and because each wants to be all (171). Peace is not a compromise; it completes everyone (174).

**How do I love G-d and still fear Him?** Love comes from knowing His greatness (88). Awe comes from knowing He is in heaven and I am on earth (90). They have one root (91).

**How should I pray?** As one who needs, not as one owed (370), out loud (371). Then the body stops being a wall (397).

**Must I finish?** No. Toil, and each piece of toil is whole (410, 415).

**Why should I worry less?** "It is a bad trait to worry about trouble" (115). Worry is not the same as care.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **Nothing but G-d and what is from Him** (38, 74, 77-79). This is close to Tanya, Gate of Unity ch. 1-3: the world has no standing of its own and is made anew from Him at every moment. The Maharal says the world "cannot stand by itself" (77) and that service keeps it from being "something besides G-d" (79). He does not say, with the Gate of Unity (ch. 3), that from G-d's side the world is as nothing. His frame stays "from Him" and "for Him".
- **Tzimtzum.** Not in this range. The Maharal explains how the One makes a split world by the rule that the world is the "world of division" (135) and that man's work is to join it (139, 180). This is his answer to what tzimtzum answers in Chabad: how a many can come from the One.
- **The Rambam's attributes.** He uses the Rambam's words "מחויב המציאות" ("must exist", 74) and "simple with the utmost simplicity" (345). He does not say we may speak of Him only by negation. He says positive things freely: He sees, He hears, He receives deeds (224). This is a step away from the Rambam's negative theology.
- **The Ramak's formula** ("He is all, and all is not He"). Not stated. The nearest is 74 and 194: all is from Him and returns to Him. The Maharal does not say all is He.
- **Love and awe.** Tanya ch. 41-43 teaches lower awe first, then love, then higher awe. The Maharal teaches love as the core (88) and awe as its guard (90), with one root (91). Both agree that neither alone is whole (Tanya ch. 40, citing the Zohar: "love and awe are the two wings").
- **Supplication and the stripping of the physical** (397). The Tanya teaches bittul (self nullification) and that the "animal soul" is the barrier. Here the barrier is the physical, and full dependence in prayer removes it. The guide can use this as a Maharal path to bittul in prayer.
- **Joining until all is one** (139, 180) is close to the Chabad teaching of making a dwelling for Him below: the lower world is where oneness must be made plain.
