Shevuot is the pillar of Torah. The Shelah gives the laws of the festival night and a long code of how to learn; then a great essay on Psalm 19 ("the Torah of G-d is whole"), in six steps: the Giver, the Torah, those who learn it, those who do it, Moshe the go-between, and "these and these are words of the living G-d". Taanit is the pillar of service (*avodah*): the laws of 9 Av and fasts, then sermons on the destruction, on the death of the righteous, and on the secret of the offerings. The richest unity page in this range, and one of the richest in the book, is line 4477.

## 1.1 No existence but His

The false oath is forbidden, the Shelah says, because it denies the truth of "the whole earth is full of His glory". Then he says what that verse means:

> אלא הפירוש שאין שום דבר נמצא כי אם מציאותו, כי הוא ית' מהווה את כולם ומחיה את כולם, והוא מציאות הנמצאים, ולא יש שום מציאות זולת מציאותו (line 4477)

"It means that nothing exists except His existence, for He brings all into being and gives all life, and He is the existence of what exists, and there is no existence apart from His." At once he adds the ladder:

> רק שזה המציאות דבק בו יותר, וממציאות זה משתלשל מציאות אחר (line 4477)

"Only that this existence cleaves to Him more, and from this existence another chains down." Things differ by how close they cling, not by having a being of their own. A vain oath is a lie, and "anything in vain is not true existence, and He is the true Existent" (כי כל חנם אינו מציאות אמתי, והוא ית' נמצא האמתי, line 4479). One who pulls away "removes his own existence from His existence" (כי מסלק את מציאות עצמו ממציאותו ית', line 4480); this is what "hiding the face" means.

Two pages later he says it again about Moshe:

> כי הוא יתברך מהווה את כולם ומחיה את כולם, ובהסתלק השגחתו רגע אז הכל היה כלא היה (line 4594)

"He brings all into being and gives life to all, and if His watching were removed for a moment, all would be as if it had never been." With line 3938 (the Pesach sermon) this is now the Shelah's settled thesis.

## 1.2 Nothing between Him and us but sin

> ואין הבדל בינו ית' לבינינו, כי אנחנו תכלית המציאות והכל ממציאותו, והעונות הם מבדילים (line 4481)

"There is no separation between Him and us, for we are the goal of existence and all is from His existence, and sins are what separate." Distance is not a fact of being; it is made by what we do, and it can be unmade.

## 1.3 His attributes are inside Him

Quoting Avodat HaKodesh on "you shall have no other gods before Me (*al panai*)": His being, His eternity, His knowing and His power are His "face", "that is, they are inside Him and not outside Him" (כלומר פנימיים בו ואינם חוץ ממנו, line 4536). Every other power is "other" to them, "for they depend on Him and have no existence or standing except from Him" (כי הם תלוים בו ואין להם מציאות וקיום כי אם ממנו, line 4536).

## 1.4 Good and bad from one place

G-d appeared at the sea as a warrior and at Sinai as a merciful elder; so He said "I", lest they think there are two powers (line 4473). "All that is done in the world, good and bad, is from Me; from Me all is united, there is no separation" (וכל מה שנעשה בעולם הן טוב הן רע ממני ומאתי הכל מיוחד לא יש הפרד, line 4474). "Judgment at its root is mercy" (והדין בשרשו רחמים, line 4557). Even the other side, "the side of impurity, has holiness at its root" (line 4508).

> ואין כוונתי שהרע היא בהסתרת פנים בלבד ולא בעצם, כפי ההבנה בדברי המתפלספים אשר דברו בזה. רק הרע הוא בכוונה מכוונת, אך שאין כוונת הרע רק בשביל הטוב (line 4480)

"I do not mean that evil is only a hiding of the Face and not real, as the philosophers are understood. Evil is intended on purpose, but its intent is only for the sake of good." The Rambam (Guide 3:10-12) treats evil as absence. The Shelah says evil is willed, and willed for good.

## 1.5 The finer, the more one

Why were the Ten Commandments said in one word? Writing is slower than speech and speech slower than thought; "the finer and more spiritual a thing is, the more it unites and joins together" (ביותר שהדבר דק ורוחני, ביותר מתייחד ומצטרף, line 4472). His speech is spiritual without end, so in it all is one.

## 1.6 The Torah is His oneness, and man carries it down

> הקב"ה הוא אחד והתורה היא אחדותו (line 4554)

"The Holy One is One and the Torah is His oneness." He wished through the Torah "to unite all from above to below and from below to above, to reveal the unity; and this must be through man, for his soul is from heaven above and his body from the earth below" (line 4554). This is why the Torah went to humans and not to angels (lines 4555-4559). The same essay opens with the line already found in Shaar HaOtiyot: "the Torah is the imprint of G-dliness, and the world is the imprint of the Torah" (line 4401).

## 1.7 The Giver still gives

We bless "who gives the Torah", in the present. The voice at Sinai "did not stop", and "all that a student renews was all within the power of that voice" (line 4516); "we only bring the hidden thing to light through our soul, which is from the source of that thing" (line 4518). Even the letters seen at Sinai "are living G-dly powers" (line 4501).

## 3.1 The study intention (proposed: `study_intent`)

Before opening a book: "I wish to learn so that the learning will bring me to action, to upright traits, and to knowing the Torah" (הנני רוצה ללמוד כדי שיביאני התלמוד לידי מעשה ולידי מדות ישרות ולידי ידיעת התורה, line 4344). This, he says, is what "Torah for its own sake" means. Inquiry into His ways is learned "in order to know His Name and His greatness... and then his heart will flame to fear Him and to love Him" (line 4343). Learn from anyone: "accept the truth from whoever said it, great or small" (line 4347). *Tie:* `arrive`, `choose_line`. *Ours:* one sentence said aloud before any study.

## 3.2 Learn with the whole body (proposed: `learn_aloud`)

"If it is set in all 248 limbs of a person, it is kept in the heart" (line 4319). Among fifteen ways of study (line 4339): aloud, "moving all the organs of speech and all the powers of the body", in a tune, calmly, with a friend, one text at a time. On reading Scripture: it is a great mitzvah "even if he does not understand what he reads, as long as he knows that he is reading" (line 4503); do not rush the repeated passages, "for on every single letter hang myriads of myriads of spiritual worlds" (line 4507); "happy is he who cleaves with the breath of his mouth and the sight of his eyes to the letters of the Torah" (line 4507). *Tie:* `lips`, `niggun`, `letters`.

## 3.3 Stand at Sinai today (proposed: `stand_at_sinai`)

The Shavuot practice (line 4635): "Let him think of the standing at Mount Sinai, that we were all there and received the Torah." Think that He spoke face to face; think of the oath; "and let him be filled with trembling and make himself a pure heart; and let him make a firm resolve as if on this very day he received the Torah, and let his heart flame to stamp in love for G-d, to serve Him with joy and a good heart." And every day: "let it be in your eyes as if the Torah was given today" (line 4638). *Tie:* `picturing`, `before_whom`, `heart_after`, `act`. *Ours:* a short daily form: picture the mountain, hear "I", resolve one thing.

## 3.4 The threefold cord

"This threefold cord of deed, speech and thought must be in every good act" (line 4638). The complete deed is "with upright intention... made of speech and thought" (line 4372). *Tie:* `act`, `lips`, `hold`.

## 3.5 Offer yourself (proposed: `offer_self`)

The offering "draws the lower will near and unites it with the upper will" (line 4923). Offerings are called *avodah* more than any mitzvah because "the main thing is the service of himself" (line 4922). Now a person offers himself "in deed, in speech and in thought" (line 4696): in speech, by saying the offering passages with understanding (line 4700); in thought, by a broken heart. The model prayer (line 4939): "You are just in all that comes upon me", glad even in suffering, "yet not holding back from mercy", asking to be cleansed "with great mercy, and not through suffering"; "and He accepts his broken heart in place of the sacrifices." Also: "now a person's table atones for him" (line 4695), a table open to the poor. *Tie:* `own_words`, `cheshbon`. *Ours:* no fasting is taken from this range; the offering is the heart, the words and the open table.

## 3.6 The bedtime release (proposed: `bedtime_release`)

"At the time of lying down, it is fitting for a person to lie down at peace with his fellow", like the pious man who each night said "I release and forgive everyone who pained me" (line 4806). *Tie:* S86. *Ours:* said nightly, before the nightly check.

## 3.7 Guard against anger

On a day of self-denial, and every day: "above all let him guard against anger, the greatest flaw in the world, for anger is a strange god"; business "in honesty and calm, and let him hurry back to his cleaving" (line 4671). *Tie:* S15.

## 3.8 Feel the poor man's hunger

"Then he feels the pain of the poor man who always lacks bread... and he has mercy on him" (line 4680). *Ours:* without fasting: notice any lack you feel, turn it into a gift that day.

## 3.9 Each person a sanctuary

"Every person is bound to make a sanctuary, and this holds always" (line 4687). Prayer stands in place of the offering: with intent and no other thought, standing, in a fixed place (line 4694). *Tie:* `arrive`, S82.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Why do I exist?** "The purpose of creation was to serve Him" (line 4946); even Adam sent from Eden was sent "to work the earth", that is, to bring others to service. Service is "a need above" (line 4440).
- **Why is there evil?** It is willed, but only for good (line 4480). "What looks like tearing down the building is tearing down in order to build" (line 4747): the husk comes before the fruit, trouble cleans, trouble wakes return. "No evil comes down from above; it is only for good" (line 4632).
- **Why grieve, if grief blocks the mind?** He asks it himself (line 4750) and answers: grief that searches the deeds, that He counts as the deed ("the breaking of the heart in place of the deed", line 4751), and that clears the husk. Not grief for its own sake.
- **Why do I keep failing?** "Because he is lacking and not like an angel, it is impossible for him to be without sin" (line 4854); so he must keep doing good to fill the lack. This is why the Torah went to humans.
- **How do I face death?** "Let him always set it in his heart, seeing a generation going and a generation coming" (line 4864). The righteous are like stars below the horizon: "the one who looks thinks they are lost, but in truth they will rise again" (line 4899). The body shares the end (line 4438).
- **How do I treat people?** "Either way, you are bound to honor every person" (line 4591). The Second Temple fell for causeless hatred, "the opposite of love your fellow as yourself" (line 4731).
- **Can I trust what I learn?** Only with help: "it is Your salvation, for one needs help from Heaven" (line 4575).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **"There is none else."** Line 4477 (nothing exists but His existence; He is the existence of what exists) and line 4594 (if His watching stopped, all would be as nothing) are the thesis of Shaar HaYichud ch. 1-3 and Tanya ch. 20-21. The added clause "only that this existence cleaves to Him more" is the Chabad ladder of worlds (*hishtalshelut*): one being, many rungs of closeness.
- **Tzimtzum.** "Hiding the face" here is a person pulling his own existence out of His (line 4480), not G-d leaving. That is close to Chabad's reading that the hiding is only from our side. But the Shelah adds that evil is real and willed, not only hidden; Chabad also says the other side is given life on purpose, for the sake of choice (Tanya ch. 6, 22, 36). They agree.
- **The Rambam.** Three open disagreements in this range: the world to come is body and soul, "not like the Rambam" (line 4438); Israel heard the words at Sinai, not only a created sound, against Guide 2:33 (lines 4530-4533, 4540-4541); and evil is not mere absence (line 4480). The Shelah also rejects the study of philosophy as a path (lines 4331-4338). Chabad keeps the Rambam's negative attributes for the Essence and agrees with the Kabbalists on the rest, and the Alter Rebbe also warns against studying philosophy (Tanya ch. 8).
- **The Ramak.** Attributes "inside Him and not outside Him" (line 4536) and "no existence but His" (line 4477) sit with "He is all that exists". "Sins are what separate" (line 4481) and "the finer, the more one" (line 4472) keep the second half: the lower things are not Him as they appear; they are His as they cling.
- **The purpose.** "To unite all from above to below and from below to above... through man" (line 4554) is the Tanya's dwelling below (ch. 36) and its teaching that the body is needed for the highest unity.
- **Not carried.** The ban on teaching Torah to non-Jews (lines 4382-4386); converts' souls placed lower (line 4561); the austerity of 9 Av (no music, no full laughter, lines 4656-4660); all fasting regimens (lines 4663, 4907-4920, 4950-4958); the halacha of fasts, 9 Av and the order of offerings (lines 4641-4723); number-play on the kingdoms (lines 4771-4779, 4822-4841); the Shavuot night Wednesday rules (line 4284). Against the particularist lines, this range itself holds a universal one: "even a non-Jew who busies himself with Torah is like a High Priest" (line 4798).

1. **"Nothing exists except His existence... only that this existence cleaves to Him more"** (line 4477), paired with line 3938: the Shelah's core unity teaching.
2. **"There is no separation between Him and us... sins are what separate"** (line 4481).
3. **The study intention** before any learning (line 4344).
4. **Stand at Sinai today** (lines 4635, 4638).
5. **Worry before, joy during** (line 4618).
6. **The broken heart as the offering**, asking to be cleansed by mercy and not by suffering (line 4939).
7. **The bedtime release** (line 4806).
