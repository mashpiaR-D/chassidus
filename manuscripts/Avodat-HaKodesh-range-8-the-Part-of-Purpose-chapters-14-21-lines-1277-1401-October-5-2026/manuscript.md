## The shape of the range

Most of this range is a sharp attack on philosophy as a path to G-d. For the guide the attack matters less than what it protects: the claim that G-d's will and inner Torah are known by receiving, not by reasoning, and that a person reaches G-d by doing His will and being answered.

1. **Moses, the fullness of creation** (1277-1291). Creation was "good" only when there was someone to receive the Torah.
2. **Reason cannot reach His will** (1292-1305). Israel's wisdom is that they call and He answers.
3. **Greek wisdom forbidden** (1306-1358). A long quote of R. Avraham Bibago (author of the philosophical *Derekh Emunah*, not ibn Gabbai's book of the same name), then a line-by-line rebuttal.
4. **The chain of the received wisdom** (1359-1366). From Sinai to the Zohar to Elijah's revelations in Provence. The Zohar was hidden and revealed "to give rest to the Shechinah in exile".
5. **The two women of Proverbs** (1367-1385). Outer wisdom as the "strange woman", inner wisdom as the "woman of valor".
6. **Abraham did not find G-d by philosophy** (1386-1401). He learned from Shem, and from the Sefer Yetzirah, and G-d taught him through his own inner parts.

## The unity of G-d

**The world is a feast, and its point is to bless the King.** The Midrash compares creation to a king's banquet: whoever eats and blesses the King may eat and enjoy; whoever eats and does not bless is punished (line 1279). Ibn Gabbai:

> כן תכלית הבריאה הוא סגול מצות ומעשים טובים שהוא הרצון והחפץ בבריאה (Avodat HaKodesh 3:14:4; line 1280)
> So the purpose of creation is gathering mitzvot and good deeds, which is the will and desire in creation.

That is why "it was good" is said of the newborn Moses ("she saw him, that he was good"), and not of Adam: only when the receiver of the Torah came was creation's good complete (lines 1279-1280). And "the good is not proven whole until it rises wholly on high" (line 1281).

**Israel's true wisdom: they call and He answers.** Ibn Gabbai quotes R. Yosef ibn Shem Tov (himself a philosopher) against the Rambam. "A wise and understanding people" (Deut. 4:6) is explained by the next verse, "who has G-d so near, as the Lord our G-d whenever we call Him":

> כי כאשר יראו העמים כי אנחנו קוראים אל יי' אלהינו בעשיית אלה החוקים והוא יענה אותנו ויתן את בקשתנו (Avodat HaKodesh 3:15:7; line 1298)
> For when the nations see that we call to the Lord our G-d in doing these statutes, and He answers us and grants our request.

Aristotle studied how rain forms; Honi drew a circle and rain fell. Galen studied leprosy and had it on his forehead; Elisha said "wash seven times and be clean" (line 1298). Ibn Gabbai sums up:

> כי אין חכמה ואין תבונה כי אם בעסק התורה ובקיום מצותיה, ובעליה קוראים אל יי' והוא יענם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:15:8; line 1299)
> For there is no wisdom and no understanding except in the study of Torah and keeping its mitzvot; its people call to the Lord and He answers them.

*Ours.* This is the same point the Kuzari makes (the G-d of Abraham is known by contact, not proof), from a harder angle. The guide takes the positive side: **the proof is the answer**. A relationship where one calls and is answered is its own knowing.

**Abraham: partner in creation.** The Midrash's "castle on fire" is usually read as Abraham reasoning from the world to its Maker. Ibn Gabbai reads it differently. The people of his time did not deny a First Cause; they denied that He *runs* the castle, and gave its care to the stars (line 1398). Abraham showed them the castle "burning and shining with the light of the upper leading and watching". And there is a deeper meaning: the King was "walking in dark alleys" because His Name was not known, and Abraham lit the way (line 1399), for "the need of the Godhead, its unity and its light, depends on the service of the complete ones". Then G-d said:

> אני לא היה שמי ניכר לבריותי והכרת אותי בבריותי, מעלה אני עליך כאלו אתה שותף עמי בברייתו של עולם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:21:16; line 1401)
> My Name was not known to My creatures, and you made Me known among My creatures; I count it for you as if you were My partner in the creation of the world.

*Ours.* Chassidus loves this: making G-d known in the world *is* completing creation (Tanya ch. 36; the Rebbe's teaching that every Jew is a partner in creation through making the world a dwelling). The guide can offer **partner in creation** as a state: when I make Him known in one place, one conversation, I am finishing the world with Him.

**"He took the place of Kindness."** From the Bahir: the attribute of Kindness said, "All the days Abraham was in the world I did not need to do my work, for Abraham stood in my place" (line 1396). He fed every passerby, and spoke to their hearts until they returned. *Ours.* A person can stand in for a divine attribute. The guide can hold kindness done fully as the attribute acting through the person (ties to Tanya ch. 23, "the chariot").

## Received wisdom: the soul of the Torah

**The inner wisdom is the soul, the Torah the body.** The true wisdom "is like the soul, and the true Torah like its body; nothing in one opposes the other, for both were hewn from one source, the upper Wisdom" (line 1339). It "helps its owners toward fear of Heaven and love of Him and accepting the yoke of His kingdom ... and straightens a bent nature, and fills what is missing in one's traits" (line 1339). One must first be a full Torah scholar, "and then he enters to stroll in the orchard of the wisdom" (line 1343): the body first, then the soul enters it in order.

**The Zohar gives rest to the Shechinah.** From the Tikkunim: the Zohar was written "to find rest for the Shechinah in exile, and freedom for her and her children" (line 1363). Ibn Gabbai:

> כי אין נחת רוח לה כעסק בנשמת התורה שהיא החכמה האמיתית המקובלת (Avodat HaKodesh 3:18:5; line 1363)
> For she has no rest like the study of the soul of the Torah, which is the true received wisdom.

The Zohar would be hidden, and revealed in the last generation, and its study would bring the redemption (line 1364). *Ours.* This is exactly the Chabad claim about spreading the inner Torah (the Baal Shem Tov's letter: "when your wellsprings spread outward", and the Rebbe's campaigns for Chassidus study). Ibn Gabbai writes this in 1531, before the Ari.

**Learn from a teacher.** "Make yourself a teacher and be rid of doubt" (line 1376). "One who learns from others, his learning stands, the closed is opened, and he is rid of doubts; one who learns by himself, his learning does not stand, his heart knocks at him" (line 1377). *Ours.* The guide itself stands on this: it draws on received teaching, and asks the user to find a living teacher (mashpia).

**Generating from received roots.** Even the angels do not invent; they receive and then "speak", unfolding what they were given (lines 1354-1356). So the scholar of true wisdom "brings out true offspring from the roots of wisdom ... and there is nothing new or made up from his own mind, only the uncovering of what was in those roots" (line 1356). "In Your light we see light": "what we understand from that light is light" (line 1357).

## Wisdom from folly

A surprising Zohar that ibn Gabbai quotes: "There is an advantage to wisdom from folly, as light from darkness" (Eccl. 2:13). "Were there no folly in the world, wisdom would not be known ... Rav Yeisa the elder, when the students learned secrets of wisdom from him, would set before them a chapter of foolish things, so that wisdom would gain from it" (line 1384). *Ours.* The guide can use this gently: the confusion one passes through is part of how clarity is known. It ties to Chabad's teaching of light from darkness (yitron haor min hachoshech, Tanya ch. 27, 36).

## The big human questions

**What is the world for?** A feast where the guests bless the King (line 1280).

**Can I reason my way to G-d?** Not to His will. But I can call, and be answered (line 1299).

**Who taught Abraham?** Shem, the Sefer Yetzirah, and G-d through his own inner parts, "his two kidneys became like two teachers" (line 1393).

**Does my small part matter?** Abraham made G-d known to his guests, and G-d called him a partner in creation (line 1401).
