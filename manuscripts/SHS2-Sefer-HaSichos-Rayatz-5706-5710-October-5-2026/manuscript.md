*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Transcripts of the Rebbe Rayatz's farbrengen talks, yechidus remarks and a few written reshimos from his American years. Most of it is Yiddish. The talks are mainly stories of earlier Rebbes and chassidim, memories of Lubavitch, and calls to strengthen Tomchei Temimim, chassidic minyanim and prayer. The aim is to wake up American Jews, and descendants of chassidim who have drifted, through memory, stories and the "essential light" of Chassidus. Scattered through it, and gathered in one 1948 reshimah, is an explicit model of Chassidus as medicine for the soul.

**How much of it is medicine.** some: most of the range is history, stories, nigunim and institution-building, but one 1948 reshimah lays out a doctor-and-remedy model of the soul, and short remarks address worry, dark temperament, anger, stagnation and dead prayer.

**Its model of the person.** A person has an animal soul that works on the mind and a yetzer hara that works on the heart. Both exploit his inborn temperament, black bile or white bile. He has ten soul-powers, mind and heart, which must all take part in service. Under everything lies an essential self: the light of Chassidus, inherited from the self-sacrifice of earlier chassidim, hidden like fire in flint. Change needs diagnosis by an expert guide and remedies actually taken. Understanding must be carried into feeling, prayer and deed ("become another person tomorrow"), with the help of farbrengen and memory.

**Its philosophy.** What is essential stays itself everywhere. Spread-out light is put out by water, but essential light is not. The light of Chassidus is essential light, revealed through real self-sacrifice. It is alive in itself and so gives life, so it can heal every sickness of the soul, as long as the Rebbe's words are kept as he said them. Each soul came down into the world on a mission.

**What it adds.** An explicit physician-of-the-soul model: diagnose before prescribing; general remedies (for heat toward desire, coldness to holiness) versus expert-prescribed ones; knowing the remedy without taking it lets illness take root; fantasy and self-image as the core sickness of the spirit. Also: essence as fire in flint for people who have drifted in America; a dark temperament as a tactic of the yetzer hara; the rule of delaying anger an hour and a minute; worry turned into mission.

## I know what's wrong with me, I've heard the advice, I even cry about it, and nothing changes

He has seen the 'doctor', knows his illness, weeps bitterly over his state and listens eagerly to remedies, yet never does them. He feels that knowing and caring is the same as healing.

**What is really going on.** The mistake is thinking that visiting the physician and knowing the remedy is enough. A remedy works only when taken in its order and at its time. Left untaken, the illness takes root and grows old.

**Program: Treat the soul like a patient: diagnosis, prescription, and actually taking it.** (1) Admit you are ill; many sick people think they are healthy. (2) Go to an expert physician (a true mashpia) to define the illness exactly; remedies rarely work until it is clear. (3) For general states, take the ready-made remedies in mussar and Chassidus books: one for heat toward worldly desire, one for coldness to Torah and mitzvos. (4) For specific illness, receive the expert's personal prescription, which takes time to prepare. (5) Take the remedies in their order and at their time; having them on the table heals nothing. (6) Learn and repeat the Rebbe's words as he said them; in that form they carry power to heal. *When:* Ongoing. General remedies whenever the symptom is felt; specific ones on the schedule the physician sets. *For:* Someone who knows he is struggling, seeks advice, even weeps over his state, but does not act on it. *Not for:* Not a substitute for medical or psychiatric care. The 'imagination' diagnosis should not be pinned by a layperson on someone who disagrees with him. `SHS2·Q01` · `SHS2·Q02` · `SHS2·Q03` · `SHS2·Q04` · `SHS2·Q05` · `SHS2·Q08`

**Reframe.** From *"I know my illness and I've been to the doctor, so I'm handling it."* to **Knowing the remedy, even weeping over the illness, heals nothing; untaken medicine lets the illness take root.** A remedy works only when taken in order and on time. Seeing that knowledge has been posing as treatment moves him from listening to doing. `SHS2·Q03` · `SHS2·Q04`

## I can't stop worrying about money and my family

Constant worry and anxiety about livelihood and household crowd out joy and purpose.

**What is really going on.** He has forgotten why he came here: each person has a mission in this world. Livelihood is set from Above, so worry adds nothing. Joy, not anxiety, opens the way to help.

**Program: From money-worry to mission.** (1) Remember why you came here: each person has a mission in this world. (2) Know that G-d feeds and sustains all creatures; worry over livelihood and family is not needed. (3) Still do the work of earning, but make an accounting grounded in faith: one cannot earn a penny more than is decreed. (4) Pray that what you earn goes to its proper place: yourself, your family, and above all enabling others to learn. (5) Be joyful rather than anxious, and then G-d will help in all you need. *When:* Whenever worry arises; as a standing outlook. *For:* Someone whose working life is eaten up by chronic anxiety about money and family. *Not for:* Not a reason to skip practical steps in a real financial crisis; the text insists one must work. `SHS2·Q16` · `SHS2·Q17` · `SHS2·Q18`

**Reframe.** From *"If I don't worry about making a living, who will?"* to **G-d sustains every creature. Your task is the mission you came for. Worry about doing good for another Jew instead.** Earnings are set from Above, so worry adds nothing. Joy invites help, and pointing the worry at others redirects its energy into purpose. `SHS2·Q16` · `SHS2·Q17` · `SHS2·Q18`

## I see everything in black; I only notice what I lack

The future looks dark, and even the present shows only what is missing, never what he has gained.

**What is really going on.** The yetzer hara uses the dark temperament, and with the sunny-tempered it paints a rosy future instead. Either way the aim is to push him off his true path. People feel only their material lacks and never think about what they do have.

**Program: Night accounting, morning prayer: bitterness, then joy.** (1) At the bedtime Shema, make an accounting of the day. (2) Feel honestly what is not good as it stands now. (3) Turn that feeling into a decision: 'become different; let tomorrow be different from today.' (4) Do not take satisfaction from having felt bad; that is worthless. (5) In prayer be humbled, and let that humility itself lead to action. (6) Move to joy: bitterness belongs to the past, joy to the service taken on for the future, but the joy must not come from the bitterness. *When:* Nightly before sleep, then the next morning's prayer. Bitterness has its set time; joy belongs to prayer. *For:* Someone functioning but stuck: days pass without change, and prayer is flat. *Not for:* Not for someone depressed or self-punishing. The bitter accounting can deepen despair unless the move to joy is certain. `SHS2·Q21` · `SHS2·Q22` · `SHS2·Q23` · `SHS2·Q24`

**Reframe.** From *"Everything is black; I only see what I lack."* to **The black coloring is not the truth about your life. It is the yetzer hara using your temperament to push you off your path.** Once he names the dark lens as a tactic working on his temperament, he stops treating it as reality and can count what he actually has. `SHS2·Q19` · `SHS2·Q20`

## I'm just a sad person by nature; even joy comes out heavy

Some people say even the joyful Simchas Torah verses with gloom and sullenness, just as others say the confession with liveliness.

**What is really going on.** The real point is that true avodah is missing. Matter overpowers form, so even spiritual things turn heavy and material.

**Program: Learning Chassidus until it is felt in the heart.** (1) Set fixed times to learn Chassidus, alone and in public. (2) First learn maamarim that explain contemplation of G-d's unity. (3) Then learn the teachings on the service of the heart, refining character, love of fellow Jews and fear of Heaven. (4) Work with effort of the soul, not only effort of the mind, until you feel the divine life in what you learn. (5) Carry the understanding down into prayer and deed; understanding that does not reach prayer is not yet understanding. *When:* Fixed daily or weekly times; the effort is lifelong. *For:* Someone who learns or understands but whose feeling and prayer stay dead. *Not for:* Not for someone who would turn it into more heady intellectualism with no practice. `SHS2·Q12` · `SHS2·Q13` · `SHS2·Q14` · `SHS2·Q25` · `SHS2·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm just a sad person by nature."* to **The heaviness shows that true, whole service is missing. Matter is ruling form. When mind and heart, all ten powers, take part, the service becomes whole and alive.** It moves the cause from fixed nature to missing engagement, which can change, and so opens a path out of the heaviness. `SHS2·Q25` · `SHS2·Q26`

## I've drifted so far from where I came from; there's nothing left in me

Swept away by the rushing current of life, or taken into another crowd, far from his upbringing.

**What is really going on.** The essential light is not put out by any waters. It lies hidden like fire in flint lying in mud, and it comes out when something strikes it, such as a memory of his upbringing.

**Program: Farbrengen and memory to wake the hidden fire.** (1) Set gatherings on Shabbos Mevarchim and festivals. (2) There, each person wakes the other in the ways of Chassidus. (3) Recall and retell memories of a chassidic home and upbringing, its stories and nigunim. (4) Let a memory of your upbringing be the 'strike' that brings out the fire hidden in the flint. *When:* Monthly (Shabbos Mevarchim) and on every festival. *For:* People who have drifted from a traditional or chassidic background. *Not for:* Someone whose upbringing memories are painful or abusive. `SHS2·Q10` · `SHS2·Q11` · `SHS2·Q15`

**Reframe.** From *"I've drifted too far; whatever was once in me is gone."* to **The essential light is like fire in flint that has lain for years in mud and water. It is dirty outside, but the fire is intact and comes out when struck.** The essential, unlike the spread-out, stays itself wherever it is. Water puts out a flame but not the fire in flint. The self is never lost, only hidden, and a memory can strike it out. `SHS2·Q09` · `SHS2·Q10` · `SHS2·Q11`

## I get furious at people who do wrong, and I judge them

Seeing wrongdoing, he burns with anger and wants to pierce the other.

**What is really going on.** Anger that is not delayed turns out to be foolishness. Judging starts from the other's lack instead of his good, and puts the blame outward instead of on oneself.

**Program: Handling anger and judgment of others.** (1) When you see a wrong and anger flares, put the anger off for an hour and a minute; then you will see it was foolish. (2) If you see a fault in someone, be angry not at him but at yourself. (3) With others, start from their good, and what is missing will fill in; with yourself, start from what is missing. (4) Let the other's spiritual lack pain you inside, but show him a shining face and draw him close. *When:* In the moment of anger; as a standing stance toward others. *For:* Someone quick to anger or harsh in judging others. *Not for:* Not for someone who already turns every failing inward with self-hatred; 'start from your own lack' can feed that. `SHS2·Q28` · `SHS2·Q29` · `SHS2·Q30` · `SHS2·Q31`

**Reframe.** From *"My anger at people who do wrong is justified."* to **Put it off an hour and a minute and it shows itself as foolishness. Turn it on yourself, and meet the other with his good.** The delay lets the heat pass so judgment can see clearly. Turning to oneself changes blame into self-work, and that is what affects others. `SHS2·Q28` · `SHS2·Q29` · `SHS2·Q30`

## Who am I to do anything?

Asked to do good for his community, he answers with humility and excuses.

**What is really going on.** This humility is out of place. He never asks 'who am I' when counting his day's profits, only when spiritual work is asked of him.

**Reframe.** From *"Who am I to do anything?"* to **You never ask 'who am I' when counting your day's profits. Ask it about material things, not as an excuse to avoid spiritual work.** It exposes the humility as selective, a cover for avoidance, and so frees him to act. `SHS2·Q27`

## Cautions

- The nightly bitter accounting that 'makes the heart weep' can deepen depression; the text requires joy afterward, joy not born of the bitterness. - 'Don't worry about livelihood' must not become advice to ignore a real financial crisis; the text itself says one must work. - 'Chassidus heals every illness' and 'sickness of the spirit' are spiritual categories, not a substitute for medical or psychiatric care. - Telling someone that 'you think you're healthy, that's your sickness' can be coercive; the text puts diagnosis in an expert physician's hands. - Blaming a sad temperament on 'lacking true avodah' can shame someone with clinical depression. - The essence-in-flint image is framed for descendants of chassidim and Jews; adapt it with care for others. - 'Be angry at yourself, start from your own lack' can feed self-hatred in someone already self-critical.
