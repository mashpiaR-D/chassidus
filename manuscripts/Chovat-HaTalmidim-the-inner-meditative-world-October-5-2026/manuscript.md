---
R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira's first book, written for yeshiva boys and their teachers in Warsaw (printed 1932). It is a book about education, not a meditation manual. But its whole argument is that a boy cannot be educated from the outside. The Rebbe's view is that a hidden soul has to be woken, uncovered and joined to God, and most of the book is concrete instruction on how to do that. The last third (the "Three Essays for senior students") is the most interior writing in the file.

Line numbers refer to the source file. The Hebrew is quoted as it stands in the file, with abbreviations left in.

**What this file does not contain.** The brief lists hashkatah (quieting), the group of "bnei machshavah tovah", and a worked method of melody. None of these appears in Chovat HaTalmidim. They belong to his later works (Hachsharat HaAvreichim, Mevo HaShe'arim, B'nei Machshavah Tovah). Song (shirah) is treated at length here, but as a state the soul breaks into, not as a tune used to bring a state on (see 2.10 and 3.8). Picturing and imagination are here in force.

## 1. What the range is: structure

**Introduction, "A talk with teachers and fathers" (lines 1-48).** "Chanoch la-na'ar al pi darko" means uncovering and growing the potential already hidden in the child. It does not mean command and habit. The Rebbe diagnoses his generation: the child's ego and feelings mature early and are unripe. So feeling has to be won for holiness from childhood, and dry intellect is not enough. This is where he states the founding principle: start with low, bodily arousal and let it wake the soul (lines 36-39, from the Pardes). He also tells teachers to tell parables vividly, because the child sees them in imagination (line 48).

**Ch. 1, "A call to the student" (49-61).** You are a hidden great soul. The spark of the prophets is in you and you need only dig for it. The Shekhinah mourns a boy who wastes himself.

**Ch. 2, "The student's first adjustment" (62-68).** The first morning practice. Think over your responsibility, feel concern but not sadness, then say the blessing over the Torah from the depth of the heart. In prayer, at least remember that you stand before your Father.

**Ch. 3, "The main illnesses of the soul and their cures" (69-72).** You are your own chief educator. People who feel no lack are the sickest. Demand greatness of yourself.

**Ch. 4, "Laziness and slackness" (73-90).** He separates the lazy boy from the slack boy (mitrapeh), whose soul is half asleep in everything he does. This chapter holds the long parable of the Hasidic shoemaker who prays in the forest with his face burning, and who then sells the resurrection flower for a loaf of bread.

**Ch. 5, "Particular counsels for the slack" (91-98).** Time each task in advance, keep a written daily schedule, and rebuke yourself aloud (in Yiddish) when you fall short. Take on a yoke.

**Ch. 6, "The illness of self-importance (yeshut)" (99-107).** Arrogance toward teachers. It includes an exercise of picturing the study hall full of angels, with God's voice clothed in the teacher's voice.

**Ch. 7, "False lowliness" (108-119).** Shame before mockers, and "ape-knowledge" that only imitates the world. The cure is "da'at atzmit", a mind of one's own, which is really the Jewish self uncovered from inside. It includes a model prayer for a boy forced out of yeshiva.

**Ch. 8, "The need for ascents of the soul and of the traits" (120-126).** Cleaning the room is not enough. The king must be invited in. Every Jew has to rise above himself at least at times. "Atah hor'eita la-da'at" means seeing and feeling, not only knowing. Free choice is only possible from a moment of elevation, from which you look down on your own pettiness.

**Ch. 9, "Counsels for rising and repairing oneself" (127-142).** This is the practical core. Wake the soul before learning. Repeat an affirmation of faith several times a day. Pour out the heart in prayer, starting with the petitions that hurt. Look up at the sky and picture the other side. Stress the word "Atah". Use devices of the tzaddikim, such as imagining yourself in the grave. Make resolutions in the moment of elevation and recall them in anger. Use bodily tricks: say a piece of Torah when anger rises, write an unsent letter, sleep across the bed.

**Ch. 10, "Turning bad traits into luminaries" (143-152).** The traits are higher sefirot drawn down and soiled by use. Traits are woken only by an object, unlike intellect and strength, which wake by themselves. So train them early on holy objects. You must feel love and awe openly, not leave them asleep in your nature.

**Ch. 11, "Constant work, constant seeking, and an elevated outlook" (153-165).** Do not limp between two openings. Keep what you took on in the high hour. Constant seeking depends on "hashkafah", how you see yourself: a prince fallen from wealth still grieves, while a prince who now sees himself as a pauper stops seeking. Keep repeating faith until it becomes "sight". Includes a model outpouring (siach) on exile.

**Ch. 12, "On Hasidism and binding to Torah through it" (166-179).** The earlier chapters are only "drops" to revive a fainting man. Hasidism cannot be explained by intellect any more than prophecy can. Torah learned with an uncovered soul is union with God: "this will is not yours". There is a test of true and false pleasure in learning. Change your place or room for study.

**Ch. 13, "Not letting oneself go sour" (180-194).** Do not postpone. What is trained in youth becomes the body's own nature (the eyes turn away before the mind knows). Each age of life has its own capacity: the young catch fire, the old taste sweetness.

**"Instructions and Admonitions" (195-252).** These are short maxims summing up the chapters. They cover the evening and morning routine, a test of true and imagined arousal (237), the difference between wanting and resolving (234-242), and how to handle scattered thoughts: straining the body, picturing yourself pushing through a crowd toward God, and seeing your soul chased by beasts (250-252).

**"Three Essays for senior students and young married men" (253-362).** A Passover letter to his circle (263). Then: - **Essay 1, "How to study Hasidic books" (264-282).** Every Jew is one of the "children of the prophets". The funnel image: the wide mouth of the soul (chayah, yechidah) stays above the body and the narrow end enters it. When you study the higher worlds you are studying your own share of them. Test your study by whether the body itself is raised. - **Essay 2, "Torah, prayer and song to God" (283-329).** Human intellect cannot see anything spiritual; it only estimates. What knowing there is comes through a "non-egoic feeling" when nefesh and ruach are woken. It is easier to wake them through body, deed and imagination than through intellect (dancing at Hakafot, praying aloud). He explains song, then the Pesach night experience, then the Jew as the vessel through whose form God is revealed (the Pardes on right and left). Prayer is the smallest degree of the spirit of song and needs effort. - **Essay 3, "On the holy Sabbath" (330-362).** Knesset Yisrael, the Shekhinah as Matronita, revealed in Jews. Singing Eshet Chayil at the window on Friday night. Inner unification (yichuda tata'ah) as the gathering of the whole self to one purpose. Friday's change of the whole world. Seudah shelishit as the prince weeping in his father's lap.

## 2. The method: every concrete instruction for inner practice

In order of the book. Where he gives a time, posture, word, picture or sequence, it is noted.

**2.1 Begin low, with the body and the senses, and let it climb.** From the Pardes: colours seen or pictured stir the spirit, "nefesh wakes ruach, ruach wakes neshamah". > מהגונים הנראים בעינים או המצטירים בשכל, בגופני, יתפעל הרוחני (line 36) > ובדברים נמוכים יתחיל והתרגשויות גופניות יסגל להתעורר בהן לקדשה (line 37)

He adds a limit: the lamp was lit with an outside flame, but the priest stayed until the flame rose by itself. > לא עזב הכהן את המנורה עד שתהא שלהבת עולה גם מאליה (line 39)

**2.2 Tell the story so it is seen.** Tell parables in order, as if they happened, because the listener sees it in imagination and is moved more by the story than by its moral. > רואה הוא בדמיונו את כל המעשה כאלו נעשה באמת (line 48)

**2.3 The morning sequence (first practice).** Get up and think over the gist of what you have been told. Let yourself feel concern, not sadness: concern is the feeling of a man who knows a treasure is buried and he has to dig. Out of that concern say the blessing over the Torah, "Ve-ha'arev na", as a real plea. During the day remember the goal without worrying, "like a traveller who reminds himself where he is going". > התגבר בבוקר לקום ממטתך וחשוב את תמצית דברינו (line 64) > אבל רק דאגה ולא עצבות (line 64) > ומתוך דאגתך זו תתחיל ברכת התורה ומעומק לבך תתפלל (line 65)

**2.4 Minimum for prayer.** Even if you cannot follow the meaning of every word: > תזכור בכל עת תפלתך שעומד אתה לפני אביך (line 67)

**2.5 Picture the room (against arrogance).** Remind yourself that the room is full of angels and that God's voice is clothed in your teacher's voice, until fear and joy shake the body (line 102).

**2.6 Pour out the heart in any form** (for a boy forced to leave yeshiva). He gives a model prayer, but says any way will do, "in speech or in the thought of the heart". > בין בדבור ובין בהרהור הלב (line 117)

**2.7 Wake the soul before study, then let it run through the text.** While learning you must work at the plain meaning. So wake and uncover the soul beforehand, and the uncovered soul will "run through the garment" of Torah to the King. > עורר את נפשך טרם שתבא ללמוד, גלה אותה וקשרה בד׳ (line 128)

**2.8 Affirmation of faith, several times a day, turned over and over.** The soul is not woken like a sleeping body, all at once. It wakes only through steady work. The words he gives: "I believe in God truly and with a whole heart... You surround me and fill my body and spirit; I am Your servant and all of me is given to You." > הקיצת נפש הקדושה לא כהקיצת גוף הישן היא (line 129) > ואתה מקיף אותי וממלא את גופי ורוחי (line 129) > הפוך והפוך במחשבה זו דכולה בה (line 129)

In the Admonitions this becomes "think and speak of it to yourself and to your friends a great deal". Repetition does more than help memory. It widens the thought until "his room and everything around him are filled with this thought, and he is placed wholly inside it". > הוי מרבה לחשוב ולדבר לעצמך ולחבריך מאמונתך (line 229) > עד שגם חדרו אף כל סביבו מתמלאים מן מחשבה זו (line 228)

**2.9 Enter prayer through what hurts.** The soul is the king's daughter. God sends worries so that she will cry out and speak to Him. So begin with the petitions you actually feel: understanding, return to Torah, livelihood for your parents. Once the soul has learned to rise there, bring it to "Atah kadosh". > מתחלה עורר עצמך בעניני התפלה שכואבים לך (line 131)

**2.10 A free time each day for talk and song with God** (outside the fixed prayers): > גם בכל היום תשתדל להמציא לך איזה זמן פנוי, שבו תשיח עם ד׳ בתפלה ובשירה (line 131)

**2.11 Eyes to the sky and picture the other side.** If it is hard to picture yourself before God in a physical world, look up and think: on the far side of this sky is a completely different world, with angels, the souls of the patriarchs and the Throne. "Whether I see You or not, I strain my eyes and look at You and bless." > הבט נא השמימה והתבונן, אמץ את מחשבתך וחשוב (line 132)

He supports this with Heikhalot (via the Tur, 128): God's delight when Israel's eyes are raised to His during Kedushah (line 133). **Posture:** eyes raised, looking.

**2.12 Stress the word "Atah".** When you say "You" in a blessing, even without looking up: > תדייק את ה-״אתה״-, ותצייר בדעתך שלד׳ אשר לנגדך אתה אומר (line 134)

**2.13 Invent your own means, and take the ones that come unbidden.** "Anyone who works with himself will from time to time find ways and means." Some awakenings come on their own. He gives the Yamim Noraim example of feeling too lowly to sing, then seeing yourself as the dirty barefoot child running along with the wedding band. > ויש גם פעמים שתבואנה לך מחשבות והתעוררויות מעצמן (line 134)

**2.14 The tzaddikim's devices: imagine yourself in the grave.** From Beit Aharon: before prayer they pictured themselves lying in suffering in the grave, when someone comes and says, get up and pray. His footnote adds that this was only for the beginning. R. Aharon used it for one year. > שהם שוכבים ביסורים בקבר ובא אחד ואמר לו, קום עמוד והתפלל (line 135)

**2.15 Resolve in the high hour, in words from the heart, repeated.** At the moment of elevation, look down at your pettiness and say, several times, in Yiddish, "I want to be a Jew; Master of the world, I take on myself to be Your true servant." The reason: "this is the law of the soul, it yields to words that come out strongly from the heart." > כי זאת חקת הנפש, נכנעת היא לפני דבורים היוצאים בחזקה מן הלב (line 136)

**Order:** do not do accounting during the fire of prayer. Do it right afterwards (even during the cantor's repetition), while the spark is still lit. > טוב שלא ירבה בחשבונות, לא יטפל ולא ילכלך את ידיו בחסרונותיו (line 137) > כל עוד שלא כבה בדוק א״ע לתקנך (line 137)

Then, when the trait flares (anger), your main work is "to picture to yourself in the strongest way and the clearest thought" that hour of elevation and the resolution you made then (line 137). Admonition 38 repeats it: "while your spirit is still warm and the visions of holiness still fly before your eyes" (line 232).

**2.16 Bodily and practical counsels.** When anger stirs, say a piece of Torah aloud, preferably one that needs some thought, because it lets the madness pass. > תאמר בפיך איזה דבר תורה (line 139)

For hidden hatred, write the man an unsent letter, hide it, and read it aloud with full feeling on several days until it can no longer be read and you go to make peace. Do this rarely. > כתוב אליו מכתב, לא תשלחהו רק תטמנהו בצלחתך (line 141)

To get up early, sleep across the bed with your feet on a chair (not more than twice a week, and not if it harms your health) (line 142).

**2.17 Keep repeating the high outlook until faith becomes "sight".** "In the measure he repeats it, he trains his soul, with its seeing, to come out and be seen in him" (line 159). The model siach on exile follows (line 165).

**2.18 In learning: begin the hard way, rest when empty, change place.** Treat every topic as hard before you start, but not so hard that you despair (line 176). When you read a Hasidic author, picture an angel putting his head out of heaven and speaking these words to you (line 177). If you are tired, stop: > טוב יותר שתפסיק מלמודך ותנוח רבע שעה (line 178)

A change of body brings a change of mind. Go to another place, or at least a fixed room or corner, because the bodily act cuts the threads that tied your thoughts to the world. > תשתדל בכל יכולתך ללכת אל מקום אחר (line 179)

**2.19 Training the eyes** (not by wrestling thoughts). Do not fight with "look / don't look". Just remember you are a Jew and not ownerless, or think of something holy as you walk. Trained from youth, the eyes turn away before the mind knows. > גש הלאה מן מחשבות אלו (line 188)

**2.20 Evening and morning routine (Admonitions).** At night, say Shema with intention and stay inside that thought until you fall asleep (water by the bed). Then in the morning you will get up quickly and gladly. > קרא את שמע בכונה, והוי מעמיק במחשבה זו עד אשר תישן (line 197)

In the morning: from inside the "concern", say "Master of the world, I give all of myself to Your service", then the blessing over the Torah (line 198). Weigh every word of Adon Olam (line 199). Before Shema, **stop for a few moments** and think "I accept the yoke of the kingdom of heaven and nullify myself to His greatness", then say Shema, ready to give your life (line 200). > קדם ק״ש הפסק איזה רגעים וחשוב (line 200)

On the way home from yeshiva, feel longing for it (line 204).

**2.21 Talk with friends.** Students should talk among themselves about God's greatness and His service, even though neither has anything new to tell the other (line 211). > ואף שאין להם להגיד חדשות זה לזה (line 211)

**2.22 Listen to your own soul.** Even a simple Jew, if he listened to his soul's sighs, songs, climbs and falls, could write a thick book from one day's events. "Listen to her, and you will wake by yourself." > אם יאזין לקולי נשמתו (line 226)

**2.23 Before prayer: stop and strengthen.** Thoughts are hooked one to another and flow like water. A good thought that arises without effort is swept away. So: > הפסק קודם כל תפלה ועבודה וחשוב (line 245)

The words: "I gird my loins and with all my strength strengthen my love, my fear and my thought in God, so that the flood will not sweep it away" (line 245).

**2.24 Train mastery of thought outside prayer.** In the middle of a pleasant daydream, push it away by force and think of something else, for no reason except practice (line 248). But do not only push away bad thoughts. Strengthen a good one instead, because > א״א לדחות את החושך בלתי אם באור (line 249)

**2.25 Push through the crowd (imagination plus the body).** When you cannot concentrate, picture yourself forcing your way through a huge crowd to the place where God is. At the same time, actually strain your body, limbs and sinews, and contract your face. Think "through them all to God". But keep the mind on "to God", not on the straining. > צייר בדמיונך שרוצה אתה להדחק דרך המון עם רב אל המקום אשר שם האלהים (line 250) > ובלבד שלא תשמש את מוחך להתאמצות והתכווצות גופך (line 250)

**2.26 See your soul pursued.** If that fails too, see the part of your soul above running from destroying angels and wild beasts toward the gates of Eden, crying "Please, God, save us." Heaven shakes, the beasts stop, she escapes. Then let the part of the soul inside you cry out silently in the same way against the crowd of thoughts (lines 251-252). > הוי רואה בשעה זו את חלק נפשך במרום כשהיא רצה (line 251)

**2.27 How to read words of arousal: as your own.** For the Pesach, Simchat Torah and Shabbat passages, do not read them as someone else's words. Picture that they come out of you and let your soul roll in them, "for not words but events of the soul are recorded in them". Read a little at a time. > רק לצייר בעצמו שממנו יוצאים הדברים ונפשו מתגוללת בהם (line 259) > נכון שלא לקרות הרבה בפעם אחת (line 258)

**2.28 Understand the higher worlds through yourself.** When you meet something in the Zohar with no explanation, picture it in yourself, and try to understand it through your own will, the parts of your soul, your traits and your four worlds. > תצייר את הדבר בעצמך (line 281)

**2.29 Start from nefesh and ruach, not from intellect.** For most people it is easier to wake the soul through what comes from nefesh and ruach (imagination, "thought" in the sense of picturing, desire, senses, the body) than through reasoning (line 291). His examples: - **Dancing.** A man with a scattered soul dances at the Hakafot with all his strength, holding one simple thought ("the Torah we always shed our blood for; I will rejoice and dance in my God"), and the dust falls off him like a shaken garment (line 293). - **Praying aloud.** When the heart is shut, pray in a loud voice, "like one who wants to roll the stone by force off the well of his heart". Mean the words as they are: at "Hodu" you stand facing the whole world and shout at it, "are you asleep?". > כמי שרוצה לגול בחוזק את האבן ממקור לבו ונפשו (line 294)

But the deed does nothing without a strong thought, and the thought needs a matching deed: > א"א לעשי' שתפעל בלא מחשבה (line 295)

He cites the Alter Rebbe's letter in the Siddur: in prayer, hold only the gist, a broad strong thought of God filling the earth, together with the mouth. Leave the detail for study time (line 295).

**2.30 The meditation before prayer (Essay 2).** Strain your mind and picture that the whole world is divine light and you stand inside His divinity. Then add: the purpose of creation is revealed in my heart and mind, and my form is the form of the revelation above. All of that hangs on this hour of prayer. Strain the whole body too, speak the words aloud with a cry of the heart. **Sequence:** force yourself at the start, then the soul prays on its own. > איך כל העולם ומלואו הכל אור אלהות הוא וכבודו מלא עולם ואני בתוך אלהותו ית' עומד (line 325) > בראשית תפילתו יכריח א"ע ואח"כ תתעורר נפשו ומעצמה תתפלל (line 325)

**2.31 Guard the morning after prayer.** Make it holy: learn, say Psalms with intention, say nothing unnecessary. > תרגיש עצמך בו כאיש המכין עצמו לדבר עם המלך (line 329)

**2.32 Friday night at the window.** After Kiddush, when the mind is still not settled, go to the window with your eyes raised and let the soul sing Eshet Chayil to God about Knesset Yisrael. **Posture:** standing at the window, eyes up. > הולך אתה אל החלון ועיניך נשואות למרום (line 343)

Also: go to the mikveh on Friday, like souls passing the river of fire before Eden, and say Shir HaShirim as your own longing ("draw me, and we will run after You") (line 353).

**2.33 Checking over the long run.** You cannot tell after every session whether it worked, so check every half year, at least once a year (line 282).

## 3. The inner experience: what it is like inside

**3.1 The soul going out.** In good hours, "he feels how his soul goes out and draws near to holiness... burns, fears, rejoices, longs and melts." > מרגיש בשעות הטובות איך נפשו יוצאת ומתקרבת לקדשה (line 33)

**3.2 Face burning, as if seeing with eyes of flesh.** The shoemaker in the forest: > פניו בוערים, משבח, מודה ומתפלל ומדבר לד׳ כאלו גם בעיני הבשר שלו ראה (line 84)

**3.3 Father and son looking into each other's eyes.** At Kedushah: our eyes in His eyes, His in ours, the father cannot hold back and embraces and kisses. > הלב מתמוגג, והנפש מתפרצת, מתלהבת וקוראה, אל אבי, אל קדושי, אני עולה, אני עפה (line 133)

**3.4 The barefoot child in the choir.** First a fear of being unworthy to sing with the angels, then suddenly "as if a breath of life was blown into you". You see a great choir of angels above and Israel below, and you run to join them "like a barefoot, dirty child". The heart melts and weeps more than at Kol Nidrei. > ואתה כילד יחף ומלוכלך בכל כחך רצת ואליהם נתחברת (line 134)

**3.5 Seeing oneself from the moment of elevation.** In a moment of rising "he sees and feels himself a Jew close to God". From there his childish ways look like a nest of snakes, and he trembles and shakes himself free. > וברגע של עלי׳ וטהרה זו רואה ומרגיש א״ע לישראל קרוב לד׳ (line 126)

**3.6 A will stronger than you.** Trained from youth, you can reach a good will "stronger than you", as if a strong wind of God that splits mountains had struck you and carried you to the task. > כאלו פתאום רוח ד׳ חזק מפרק הרים ומשבר סלעים פגע בך (line 185)

**3.7 Seeing, then not understanding it afterwards.** The Hasid in his hour of rising sees, and it is plain and certain to him. After he falls, he does not understand himself or what he saw. > ואח״כ אם נופל גם א״ע ואת ראיתו הקדומה אינו מבין (line 169)

The same in Essay 2: he feels "the events of his soul" but his intellect does not know what he felt (line 302). Our "sight" is only "feeling, fire, longing and delight", which cannot be described (line 299). > והוא את מאורעות נפשו מרגיש אבל בשכלו לא ידע מה הרגיש (line 302) > רק הרגשה והתלהבות תשוקה ועונג (line 299)

**3.8 Seasons of the inner life.** Some periods of life (or of a year) are for catching fire, some for tasting. In some, nothing is felt without fire. In others, sweetness is felt "even from every simple thing, even from every letter". > מרגיש ערבות ומתיקות אף מכל דבר פשוט (line 192)

**3.9 The non-egoic feeling.** When nefesh and ruach wake and "the ego and the human side are nullified to that degree", one can feel "a little, with a feeling that is not egoic, not human". It glimpses the radiance of a world or sefirah "a little here, a little there". > אז אפשר לו להרגיש מעט בהרגשה בלתי אנכית אנושית (line 290)

**3.10 Shaken clean.** After dancing: the dust falls off like a garment shaken clean, "and his inner self and thoughts are like the very sky in its purity". > כעצם השמים בטהרו (line 293)

**3.11 Pain of longing; nullification and sweetness from above.** When more light rests on him: he burns with love and fear, is ashamed before God's eyes "which look at him and pierce his whole body and soul". At times the longing is so great that he feels physical heartache. At times a spark of supernal beauty appears, and "in his mind he does not know what is with him". > עד שמרגיש בפועל כאב הלב מגודל תשוקתו לד' (line 296) > התבטלות ועונג, ערבות ושמחה הבאה אליו ממרום ומושכתו למרום (line 296)

Song is what this overflow is forced into, like a child who kisses his father and cannot stop saying "my dear father, my sweet father" (line 296).

**3.12 Song brings the lamp here.** The one who tells God's praises reports a light far away. The one who sings them "brings the lamp from there to here" (line 297). Because our sight is small, we put our spark into the prophets' songs, like a short-sighted man who is told "that white is the king's palace" and suddenly sees it (line 299). > דומה, למי שמביא את הנר מהתם להכא (line 297)

**3.13 Pesach night: the whole room bows.** "Even the table, the candles and the walls, all of us together become one mass, and all of us bow, weep, rejoice and praise." The limbs leave the body and the senses their senses. > אף השלחן הנרות והכתלים כלנו יחד נעשים חטיבה אחת (line 305)

Earlier (line 263): "I have cast off the whole world and even myself, and I have only to stand in the great camp of angels", so that he can barely keep his body from leaping up to the sky.

**3.14 The difference in colour between states.** On Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur every feeling has the form of fear. On Pesach, majesty, and even fear is inside love. > ובפסח גם היראה באהבה היא (line 306)

**3.15 Not delight, not joy: nullification of self.** Friday night, seeing Knesset Yisrael: "all is nullified, there is no world and no matter". In that moment "you see yourself a little as one of the children of God". > לא עונג ולא שמחה אתה מרגיש בשעה זו רק בטול העצמיות (line 344) > וגם אתה עצמך בשעה זו מעט לאחד מבני אלהים אתה רואה (line 344)

**3.16 Friday afternoon.** "As if by force they take all the weekday with all its dust and throw it out of his mind and heart." The man, and the world around him, are changed (line 353). > כאילו בחזקה לוקחים את כל חול החול (line 353)

**3.17 Seudah shelishit: the prince in his father's lap.** After the height of Shabbat the heart breaks, but this is not falling. It is "a son snuggling up to his father". He weeps that he has forgotten who he truly is. > את מהות עצמי האמיתי, מהות בן מלך שוכח אני (line 359)

**3.18 Doubt as part of the inner life.** At times in the year: perhaps my elevations were only rescues from sinking, the way Balaam also had visions (line 304). Pesach answers this: "he already knows inside that he is a Jew" (line 305). > שגם עליותי רק מיני הצלה הן (line 304)

## 4. The self and God

The owner's thesis is that people seek themselves, and in finding the true self they find Him. This book supports it strongly, with one sharp qualification about which "self" is meant.

**4.1 The self is hidden, and the hidden thing is divine.** Education means uncovering what is already there: > רוח ד' נשמת שדי טמונה וגנוזה בו (line 5) > ועליך רק לחפור אחריו ולגלותו בקרבך (line 56)

**4.2 The Jew does not know himself.** The body has thickened "until he does not recognise himself and does not know of the treasure of heaven within him". The main work is to wake the soul, and then "he will return to his own essence", and then serve God like the angels. > עד שאינו מכיר א״ע ואינו יודע מאוצר השמים אשר בקרבו (line 167) > ואז אף הוא יהפוך את עורו ישוב אל עצמותו (line 167)

Also: "you do not know yourself and look only at the jug" (line 239). > שאת עצמך אינך מכיר ורק בקנקן אתה מסתכל (line 239)

**4.3 Knowing God happens by returning to the heart.** On "ve-hasheivota el levavecha": look at the trembling in your heart and your longing to pray like the Kozhnitzer Maggid. Where does it come from, "if not from the Lord of Hosts before whom your soul trembles"? The evidence for God is found inside. > כשתשיב אל לבך אז תדע כי ד׳ הוא האלקים (line 123) > רק הראת לדעת, שנראה ונרגיש אותו ית׳ הוא העיקר (line 123)

**4.4 The self is God's palace.** > גופך ונשמתך, הארמון של האין סוף הוא (line 125)

**4.5 Your own mind of your own, which is really the Jewish self.** Against the false lowliness of living by other people's opinion, he wants to wake "a mind from yourself". And what is that self? > הלא עצמותך ישראל הוא, ואת הרוח הישראלית שבך תגלה מקרבך (line 114)

**4.6 The true "I" is a spark of God's "I".** The strongest statement in the file (Admonitions 27-28). What both the arrogant boy and the self-effacing one lack is "the Jewish I-ness": > האנכי ניצוץ אנכי ד׳ אלהיך (line 221) > הוי מקיץ את האנכיות הישראלית שבך (line 222)

So finding the true "I" (anokhi) means finding the spark of "Anokhi Hashem Elokecha" from Sinai.

**4.7 In learning, your will turns out to be His.** When you wake your will for Torah, "this will is not yours". God, above all worlds, "unites with you and dwells within you". > הרצון הזה לא שלך הוא (line 175) > מתיחד בך ושוכן בקרבך (line 175)

**4.8 The higher worlds are yours.** Chayah and yechidah stay above the body, "yet they are his". When you study the higher worlds you understand your own portion of them (lines 276, 279). > ואף שאין מבינם ורק למעלה ממנו שרויים, מ"מ שלו הם (line 276)

**4.9 Each person's form reveals God in its own shape.** God's light is revealed "according to the likeness and form of Israel". Each tzaddik's understanding has its own face, as the Gemara says: > כשם שאין פרצופיהן שוים כך אין דעתן שוות (line 320)

Every Jew who works shows "some form of its own" (line 320). So the individual self is not erased. It is the particular vessel through which God appears.

**4.10 God calls to God.** From Kedushat Levi: when a Jew wakes the divinity within him and cries out with it, "God calls to God" (line 327). > אז ד' קורא לד' היינו בחלק האלהות שבו קורא לד' (line 327)

**4.11 Forgetting the self is forgetting God.** The two losses come together at seudah shelishit: "I forget my own true essence, the essence of a king's son, and do not even recognise the holiness of Your glory" (line 359).

**The qualification (keep it).** There are two selves in this book, and they point opposite ways. One is the true self (atzmut, the Jewish anokhi, the soul). The other is the bodily ego, "the I and the bodily anokhi", which is exactly what blocks knowledge of the spiritual: > היינו האני והאנכי הגופני (line 290)

Real feeling only comes when that ego "is nullified" (line 290). At the height of Friday night what he feels is "nullification of selfhood" (line 344). And the book opens by blaming the ego ("yeshut") of children who mature too early (lines 19-25). So for him, finding the true self requires the nullification of the self you thought you were, and at the peak the self is barely there. It is not "find yourself, then find God". It is "the self you find turns out to be His spark, and it is found by letting go of the ego". In 3:15 he says both things together: "nullification of selfhood... you see yourself a little as one of the children of God."

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

**5.1 Unripe excitement.** The core danger of his generation: children "get excited and moved with an excitement that is not cooked" (line 21), and they are moved by "the imagined beauty of the world's follies" (line 30). His answer is not to suppress feeling but to capture it for holiness early. > ולהתרגש ולהתפעל בהתרגשות לא מבשלה (line 21)

**5.2 Intellect alone does not hold.** "A bond in intellect alone is not a lasting bond" (line 31). Knowing the right thing does not stop the pull of the feelings (line 30).

**5.3 "I've fixed it in one go" is a lie.** > ודומה לך שבפעם אחת כבר תקנתי׳, דע לך שמרמה אתה עצמך (line 121)

**5.4 Awakenings are drops, not food.** The means he gives only revive a fainting man. Living on them without daily work on the traits and the body ruins everything (line 168). > כן דברינו רק טיפות לעורר את הנפש הקדושה (line 168)

He says the same of the tzaddikim's devices: only for the beginning (line 135, footnote).

**5.5 Limping between two openings.** Soaring in one moment and falling into mockery the next (line 155). Those who force themselves for a moment and fall back spend their whole life on the forcing (line 185).

**5.6 Self-deceiving motives.** Someone who imagines he learns for its own sake may be fooling himself. The sign is that he drops it when the profit ends (line 173). The reverse also holds: self-interest on the surface can hide a real inner bond. > יש איש שמדמה בדעתו שלומד לשמה ומרמה הוא א״ע (line 173)

**5.7 Test of pleasure.** Real "Eden pleasure" is greater after a hard topic than after an easy one. If you enjoy the easy one more, your pleasure comes from rest and slackness. > סימן ה־א שאין תענוגו אמיתי תענוג ג״ע (line 175)

**5.8 The main test: have the traits changed?** (Admonition 37). To know whether your arousal is real, "a revealing of the soul and its drawing a little closer to supernal purity, or only a deceptive excitement", look at your traits. If they have mended a little, it was real. If nothing has changed, your arousal did nothing, "and it may even be something bad deceiving you". > או רק התרגשות רמוי׳ היא הוי מסתכל במדותיך (line 231)

**5.9 Imagined ascent (the swamp).** A man sinking in a swamp is pleased that one foot has come up and does not notice the other has sunk. "All day he works and rises, and all day he stays where he is." Lift the root, the soul itself (lines 224-225). > כל היום יעבוד ויעלה, וכל היום ילך ועל עמדו ישאר (line 224)

**5.10 Feeling passes; resolution has to carry you.** Will and thought wake each other and then both go quiet. What remains is the decision: "this is what I decided, so I must" (lines 234-236). > כך החלטתי לעשות מקודם וכן מוכרח אני לעשות (line 235)

**5.11 Wisdom greater than deeds.** Studying the higher worlds as outside information is "wisdom exceeding deeds". The man whose body has been refined but who does not know how to think will either fall, or his thoughts will twist, "his mind will swell and he will think he is already in the world of Atzilut" (line 280). And when you understand a verse about the Throne, do not think you understand the prophet (line 276). The test is whether your body itself is raised (line 282). > תזוח דעתו עליו ויחשוב כי כבר הוא בעולם האצילות (line 280)

He says this is not "imagination alone": the taste of matzah when learning the laws of Pesach is the added light moving the body (line 280). > לא דמיון בלבד הוא זה (line 280)

**5.12 Intellect cannot see the spiritual.** It only estimates, as with electricity (lines 287-288). Those who try to reach spiritual things by reasoning end in heresy. > ששכל האדם בתוך אדם לא משיג שום דבר רוחני ולא יראהו, רק משער אותם (line 288)

**5.13 Excitement without a vessel is nerves.** Someone who does not work at Torah and mitzvot and has made no "chariot" of himself is weak. "Even if he catches fire at times, he cannot be sure it is not just empty imagination and irritation of the nerves", foam on water. Right after such false excitement he can be stirred by something low. > לא יהי' בטוח שאין זה רק דמיון שוא וגרוי העצבים בלבד (line 324) > כקצף על פני המים (line 324)

**5.14 A twitch of one part is not wholeness.** For the person with no single purpose, an occasional good fire is "only a twitch of one part of his soul", like the limbs of a dead animal (line 350). > רק מין פרכוס של חלק אחד שבנפשו היא (line 350)

**5.15 "Use" versus "work".** Doing mitzvot without effort (shimush) reveals nothing. Only work that costs strength (avodah) reveals the soul (line 308). An old idea grows stale unless one keeps rising (line 265).

**5.16 Sadness is not concern.** Sadness belongs to a man who has lost everything and despairs. Concern belongs to a man who knows where the treasure is (line 64). Constant concern exhausts itself, so remember the goal without worrying (line 66).

**5.17 Forcing.** Unlike some later teachers, he does not warn against forcing. He demands it. "A man who imagines he can do holy service without toil and self-coercion... stay away from the camp of the Shekhinah" (line 241). Prayer begins by force (line 325). What he warns against is forcing without the soul (the slack, half-asleep body going through motions, lines 80, 308) and forcing in fits (line 185). And he sets a limit on force inside the mind: strain the body, but do not use the mind for the straining (line 250). Do not wrestle with bad thoughts; bring light (line 249). Do not twist yourself around "should I look / not look" (line 188).

## 6. Comparison with Chabad hisbonenus

Measured against `/home/user/Research/Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md` (hisbonenus: think one truth through slowly until it reaches the heart; five stages of preparation, contemplation, deepening, arousal and drawing down; "the mind rules the heart").

**Where they agree.** - *Duration and repetition of one truth.* His affirmation ("You surround me and fill my body and spirit", line 129) is a single truth turned over many times a day, and repeating it widens it until the room is full of it (lines 228-229). The Chabad sources would recognise this. - *The object is the same.* God fills the world, and "I stand inside His divinity" (line 325). This is memale kol almin, one of hisbonenus's classic objects. - *Feeling must be felt, not left latent.* Love and fear that are only in one's nature and not felt are not enough (line 147). Chabad makes the same distinction between natural and revealed love. - *The same test.* His measure of real arousal is whether the traits have changed (line 231). The hisbonenus summary credits this as the Rebbe Rashab's distinctive test. The Piaseczner has it too, independently. - *Drawing down.* Right after the fire, apply it to the specific bad trait (lines 137, 232), and keep the rest of the day in line with prayer (line 329). This matches "contemplation that stops at feeling has not finished." - *He cites the Alter Rebbe with approval.* Twice from the letter in the Siddur (lines 295, 326): in prayer hold only the gist, a broad and strong thought of God filling the earth, spoken with a full voice that wakes the heart.

**Where he differs (do not smooth these).** - *Order of entry: body and soul first, intellect later.* Hisbonenus goes through Binah and Da'at down to the heart. He says the opposite for most people of his time: "it is easier to wake oneself by starting with nefesh and ruach than by starting from the neshamah through reasoning" (line 291). While a man is reasoning about God's greatness and the order of the worlds, "it is hard for him to be moved even to open love and fear". Only afterwards, when he extends his understanding, can it come, and even then it is not easy for a simple man unless he adds deeds of the soul such as prayer (line 291). His reason is that intellect is more under the control of the ego than the traits are (line 292). This is a direct disagreement with "the mind rules the heart" as a starting point, though not with it as a goal (line 296 requires full immersion of intellect in Torah). > יותר יקל לאיש לעורר את עצמו כשיתחיל לעורר את הנפש והרוח (line 291) - *Imagination as a tool, not a distraction.* His "thought" that wakes the heart is "a kind of imagination", picturing forms (line 291). He instructs picturing all the time: the far side of the sky (132), the room full of angels (102), the grave (135), the crowd (250), the chased soul (251), the text as coming from your own mouth (259), a higher idea pictured within yourself (281). The hisbonenus material works through concepts and analogies, and imagination sits there nearer the risk side. > מין דמיון היא המחשבה של האדם שאנו מדברים עתה ממנה (line 291) - *The body as a direct lever.* The Pardes principle that colour and sense wake ruach and neshamah (lines 36, 295). Dancing (293), a loud voice (294), straining the limbs and face (250), changing place (179). Chabad also has "with a voice that wakes the intention of the heart", and he quotes it, but he makes the body a starting point and not only an accompaniment. - *Arousal is the aim, and excitement is not suspect in itself.* His whole book is built for hitlahavut and hitpa'alut. There is no Chabad-style wariness of "self-referential arousal" as such. His counterfeits are different ones: unripe feeling, nerves without a vessel, a twitch of one part, and no change in the traits. Close to the Mitteler Rebbe's and R. Aharon's defence of being moved, not to the reserve of those who put bittul first. But at the Friday-night peak what he names is exactly "not delight and not joy, only nullification of selfhood" (line 344), so the two meet at the top. - *No quieting stage.* Hisbonenus's diagnostic layer says "unsettled: settle first". This book has no stage of quieting before contemplation. It prescribes the opposite: stop for a moment, then gird and strengthen (line 245), strain through the noise (250), and if nothing works, cry out (251-252). Hashkatah appears only in his later works, not here. - *Song.* In hisbonenus, song is not a method. Here song is a degree of the holy spirit, and prayer is a smaller degree of song (lines 322, 325). Singing the praises of the earlier tzaddikim with our own spark "brings the lamp here" (297). Singing is set in the daily practice itself (131). He does not, in this book, use a melody to bring on a state. - *Foreign thoughts.* The Chabad layer: "return without commentary", and the matter is disputed. He has an active programme: practise breaking off pleasant thoughts outside prayer (248), strengthen a good thought rather than push away the bad one (249), then picturing plus bodily force (250-252). His picture of the soul chased by beasts turns the distraction itself into the material of the cry. - *Affect as a sign of truth.* He is surer of feeling than of reason: a child's urge to kiss his father "is more certain than all the reasoning in the world" (line 32). Chabad, through Da'at, keeps feeling answerable to understanding.

## 7. For the guide

1. Before you try to think about God, notice what already hurts or longs in you, and start there. The Rebbe says to begin prayer with the requests you actually feel (line 131).
2. Pick one sentence, for example "You surround me and fill my body and my spirit", and say it slowly, several times, today and again tomorrow. Let it widen until it fills the room you are sitting in (lines 129, 228).
3. Look up, if you can see the sky, and picture the other side of it. Whether you see anything or not, say "You" to the One you are facing, and mean the word "You" (lines 132, 134).
4. If your thoughts keep scattering, do not fight them one by one. Picture yourself pushing through a crowd toward God, and let your body strain a little with it, but keep your mind on "toward God", not on the effort (lines 249-250).
5. When something in you lifts, even a little, use that moment. Say one sentence from the heart about what you will change, aloud if you can, several times (lines 136-137).
6. Afterwards, do not judge the moment by how strong it felt. Judge it over weeks by whether one trait has actually softened. If nothing changes, the feeling was not yet the real thing (line 231).
7. What you are looking for is not new. It is you, the self you have forgotten, and the Rebbe calls its "I" a spark of God's own "I am" (lines 221, 359).
8. Expect it to go quiet again, and expect not to understand afterwards what you saw. That is normal. Keep what you decided in the good hour, and act on the decision when the feeling is gone (lines 169, 235).
