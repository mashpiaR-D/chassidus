## S039 · Holiness · קדושה

**Face:** F08 Bitul. **Also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both · F04 Filling and surrounding **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5)

**The glimpse.** Holiness is not something you own; it is His, resting on whatever stops standing apart, even inside an ordinary act.

**Hold.** 'The Holy One rests only on what is nullified before Him' (Tanya, ch. 6).

**The move.** Open it: He is holy, separate; He gives life to the worlds and is not clothed in them or changed by them. So holiness in a person is never his own property: it is His holiness resting on him, and it rests only where a person is set aside before Him, as every Jew is at least in his readiness to give his life; nothing can be received from holiness except by setting yourself aside. You, here, now: because He gives life to everything while holy and separate, the Sefat Emet says a person can be holy in every act, inside nature, by setting himself aside to the inner point in it, this meal, this piece of work.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'He rests on what is set aside for Him'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Lightness: nothing to own or defend, and the act carrying what is not yours. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Before one ordinary act today, a meal or a piece of work, say with your lips 'l'shem Shamayim', for the sake of Heaven, and do it that way, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'set aside, and He rests'.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 6:5 (line 124): וצד הקדושה אינו אלא השראה והמשכה מקדושתו של הקדושברוךהוא, ואין הקדושברוךהוא שורה אלא על דבר שבטל אצלו יתברך - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of circumcision 5 (line 11): שממדת הקדושה א"א לקבל כ"א ע"י ביטול - Sefat Emet, Leviticus, Kedoshim 1:3 (line 4179): כי הש"י מחי' הכל אף כי הוא קדוש ונבדל מכל. וז"ש כי קדוש אני ה' ועי"ז יכולין להבין כי בכח האדם להתקדש בכל מעשיו אף שהם תוך הטבע

## S040 · Kelipah and the other side · קליפה / סטרא אחרא

**Face:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation. **Also touches:** F08 Bitul · F04 Filling and surrounding **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6)

**The glimpse.** Even what stands against Him lives only from His word, hidden; the 'other side' is not a second power but His own light pushed out of sight, there so that it can be overcome.

**Hold.** 'The contractions and the hiding were so great that even impure things and kelipot could come into being, and receive their life and existence from the word of God and the spirit of His mouth' (Tanya, ch. 22).

**The move.** Open it: everything lives from the word of God, the kelipot too; the contractions were so great that things could stand as if apart from Him, and there is no second source. What puts something on 'the other side' is not what it is made of but whether it is for Him, so idle talk and idle thought, not for His sake, are there too; its only voice is 'I, and none else'. You, here, now: the idle stretch in your day, the scrolling or the empty chatter, lives from His word, hidden, and can be turned to Him. The Rebbe says the whole intent of the other side is that a person overcome it, and the Besht adds that sparks of the Shechinah fell even there.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'even this lives from His word'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The other side losing its size: not a rival power, only His word pushed out of sight. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips 'ein od milvado'. Then turn one idle stretch today to Him: a chapter of Tehillim, a line of Torah or a kind message, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'even this lives from His word'.

**Disagreement kept.** How the kelipot receive life is put differently: Tanya 22, from the back of the will, in exile; the Rashab, from the surrounding light that flows without regard to vessels; Breslov, from the utterly hidden first utterance, which must not be pondered.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 22:2 (line 295): וכל כך גברו ועצמו הצמצומים והסתר פנים העליונים, עד שיוכלו להתהוות ולהבראות גם דברים טמאים וקליפות וסטרא אחרא, ולקבל חיותם וקיומם מדבר ה׳ ורוח פיו יתברך - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 6:4 (line 123): וכן כל הדבורים וכל המחשבות אשר לא לה׳ המה ולרצונו ולעבודתו, שזהו פירוש לשון ״סטרא אחרא״, פירוש – צד אחר, שאינו צד הקדושה - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד, יו"ד שבט, ה'תשי"ג*:5 (line 1271): ובפרט הקליפה וסט"א שכל הכוונה בהם הוא שהאדם יתגבר עליהן לאכפיא להון

## S041 · Kelipas nogah and the permitted · קליפת נוגה / דברי הרשות

**Face:** F09 The unity of opposites. **Also touches:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F08 Bitul **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)

**The glimpse.** The permitted world is undecided: the same food, the same pleasure, can rise into holiness or sink away, depending on whom it is for.

**Hold.** 'The life of the meat and the wine is refined, and rises to God like a burnt offering and a sacrifice' (Tanya, ch. 7).

**The move.** Open it: the permitted world, food and drink and every ordinary act done for the body, lives from a middle realm, nogah, which holds some good and some bad. Eaten to widen the mind for Him, or for the delight of Shabbos, the meat and wine rise like an offering; eaten for appetite alone, their life sinks for a while and can rise again with teshuvah, which is why it is called 'permitted', not bound. The Maharash calls nogah the turning sword, able to go either way; only a picture, but it says what tips it: whether it is for Him or for oneself. You, here, now: the plate in front of you is undecided until you decide whom it is for.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'it rises like an offering'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Nothing permitted is merely neutral, and nothing is yet lost. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over your food with your lips, slowly, and eat for strength to serve Him; on Shabbos, eat one delicacy as the mitzvah it is. During the day, return to: 'like an offering'.

**Disagreement kept.** The Mitteler Rebbe says nogah is always in bitul and only the three impure kelipot are a separate 'something'; Likkutei Torah says even nogah's good is a 'something', and can be refined until it is wholly set aside, with the Mitteler Rebbe's own verse. The entry does not resolve it.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 7:4 (line 135): נתברר חיות הבשר והיין שהיה נשפע מקליפת נוגה, ועולה לה׳ כעולה וכקרבן - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 7:2 (line 133): והיא בחינה ממוצעת בין שלש קליפות הטמאות לגמרי ובין בחינת ומדרגת הקדושה - Toras Shmuel, תרל"ב ח"ב / מאמרים / וידעת היום כו' / תכט:2 (line 5112): ירצה הרע תעשה אותו הטוב והוא המתקת קליפת נוגה להחזירה אל הקדושה. כי ק"נ נק' להט החרב המתהפכת

## S042 · The three impure husks and the forbidden · שלש קליפות הטמאות

**Face:** F08 Bitul. **Also touches:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F11 Darkness turned to light **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4, 5, 6)

**The glimpse.** What stands wholly apart, saying 'my strength', cannot be lifted, only let go of; each 'no' to the forbidden serves His unity, and teshuvah from love reaches even what fell lowest.

**Hold.** 'Whatever fell lowest, its root is highest' (the Tzemach Tzedek, Derekh Mitzvotekha).

**The move.** Open it: below the permitted realm are three kelipot with no good in them, and what is forbidden draws from them; 'forbidden', asur, means bound, so its life cannot be lifted by using it, even to serve God with its strength. What makes them impure is complete separateness, the voice of 'my strength and the might of my hand'; they are not lifted but annulled, and each 'no' to a prohibition keeps life from being drawn into them. You, here, now: the word of gossip you leave unsaid today is that 'no'. And teshuvah from great love turns even willful sins into merits, because what fell lowest has its root highest.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'what fell lowest, its root is highest'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The dignity of a plain 'no', and the width of a teshuvah that reaches even what fell lowest. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips 'ein od milvado'. Then leave one forbidden word unsaid today, gossip or a jibe, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'the root is highest'.

**Disagreement kept.** Which is higher is kept: the Tzemach Tzedek and R. Tzadok rank the penitent's transforming above; the Rebbe (5730) holds that as a general rule the light drawn down by holding back is higher. The entry does not reconcile them.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of confession of sin 3 (line 85): כי הזדונות הם ממה שנפל מבחי' אלה המלכים למטה מטה ונתהוו מזה קליפות הטמאות … כי כל מה שנפל למטה ביותר שרשו גבוה ביותר - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 7:9 (line 140): מה שאין כן במאכלות אסורות וביאות אסורות, שהן משלש קליפות הטמאות לגמרי – הם אסורים וקשורים בידי החיצונים לעולם … או, עד שיעשה תשובה גדולה כל כך שזדונות נעשו לו כזכיות ממש - The Gate of Unity 53:24 (line 2669): והגם שיש מהם שהן בבחינת יש נפרד גמור לומר כחי ועוצם ידי כו'

## S043 · The sparks · ניצוצות

**Face:** F01 The world said into being now. **Also touches:** F07 The home below · F11 Darkness turned to light **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 4, 5)

**The glimpse.** In every thing there is a spark of His word keeping it alive, and the ones on your path are waiting for you to gather them back into oneness.

**Hold.** 'There is nothing in this world without a holy spark, drawn from the speech of the Holy One, that gives that thing life' (Me'or Einayim, Matot).

**The move.** Open it: in every thing a spark of His word keeps it alive, and in food, Chernobyl says, it is the good taste: 'taste and see that Havayah is good'. The sparks fell and were scattered, and the worlds were made so that Israel would raise them; what a person eats and uses is his own share to repair. You, here, now: providence leads a person on his journeys to the places where his sparks are waiting, this street, this errand, this meal. Raising them is gathering the scattered from separation back into oneness; the Rayatz's picture is the sheaves in Joseph's dream, only a picture, since what is gathered is the letters of His word.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'nothing without a holy spark'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being expected: the place you are sent to today has something of His waiting there for you. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over the first thing you eat today with your lips, then the verse 'taste and see that Havayah is good'. Do one mitzvah on today's errand, a coin or a kind word, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'waiting here'.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Me'or Einayim, Matot 1 (line 522): שאין לך דבר מזה העולם שאין בו ניצוץ הקדוש שנאצל מדבורו של הקב״ה המחיה אותו הדבר - Hayom Yom, חשון / א חשון, שר"ח:2 (line 543): וע"פ גזירת ההשגחה העליונה האדם הולך למסעיו במקום אשר הניצוצות הצריכים להתברר על ידו מחכים לגאולתם - Sefer HaMaamarim (Rayatz), תרפ"ט / עוז והדר לבושה / 188:1 (line 1507): שמתאספים האותיות והניצוצות שנתפזרו ושיהיו עולים ונכללים מהפירוד אל היחוד

## S044 · Refinement · בירורים

**Face:** F11 Darkness turned to light. **Also touches:** F08 Bitul · F12 Knowing the unity **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4, 5)

**The glimpse.** Standing before 'there is nothing besides Him', what is mixed in a person sorts itself: the good rises and is included in Him, and what was hiding in it shows itself and can be set aside.

**Hold.** 'The good mixed in it is separated from the bad, prevails, rises and is included in holiness' (Tanya, ch. 7).

**The move.** Open it: in this world even the good comes mixed with dross, so everything needs sorting, and refining is the good separated out, rising and included in holiness. Likkutei Torah's way is to contemplate in prayer that there is truly nothing besides Him; in that light a person sees where he is still a 'something', and what was hiding in the good shows itself and can be set aside. You, here, now: in exile the chief sorting is halacha, permitted from forbidden and pure from impure, and only people below, in bodies, can do it. The Rayatz says it goes slowly, step by step, from below, because a flood of light would wipe out the bad and lose sparks with it.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'the good rises and is included'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Slow, steady work rather than a verdict: the good rising a little at a time. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips 'ein od milvado'. Then learn one halacha today, one line of what is permitted and what is not, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'a little at a time'.

**Disagreement kept.** Who completes the refinement is kept open: the Rayatz says it must be gradual and from below; the Tzemach Tzedek, from below and from above; Radzin, that a person refines as far as his hand reaches and God refines the rest, so that no spark is lost.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 7:3 (line 134): ופעמים שהיא נכללת ועולה בבחינת ומדרגת הקדושה, דהיינו, כשהטוב המעורב בה נתברר מהרע וגובר ועולה ונכלל בקדושה - Likkutei Torah, Bamidbar 5:5 (line 1173): איך שאפס זולתו ממש אז יבוש ויכלם ממעשיו שהוא מבחוץ עדיין בבחי׳ יש ודבר - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 26:24 (line 1404): לברר המותר והטהור מהאסור והטמא – עלידי עיון ופלפול הלכה בחכמה בינה ודעת

## S045 · Foreign thoughts · מחשבות זרות

**Face:** F09 The unity of opposites. **Also touches:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F08 Bitul **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 4, 5)

**The glimpse.** Even the stray thought in prayer is made of letters that are His, fallen; it comes because prayer is the hour of sorting, and turning back gladly is part of the unity, not a failure of it.

**Hold.** 'Precisely because the time of prayer is the time of refinement, to separate the bad from the good and push it away' (Likkutei Torah, Bamidbar).

**The move.** Open it, at a time that is not prayer: every thought is made of letters, and for the founders a stray thought in prayer is holy letters fallen into a bad arrangement. It comes precisely then because prayer is the hour of sorting, when the bad clings hardest to the good it lives from, and its coming does not make the prayer worthless. You, here, now: for most of us the Alter Rebbe says not to try to lift such a thought, only to turn the mind away and pray with more joy. And now, outside prayer, the Rebbe says to consider that a spark of the Shechinah came down into those thoughts to rouse a stronger intention.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'the hour of sorting'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Steadiness: the wandering is expected at that hour, and turning back gladly is itself the work. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** At your next prayer, say the Shema with your lips, and when a stray thought comes, do not argue with it; turn back to the words a little more gladly. During the day, return to: 'the hour of sorting'.

**Thin.** For the ordinary person the Tanya's practice during prayer is to push the thought away, not to contemplate it; so this meditation is for another time, as the Rebbe says, and it never takes the thought's content as its object.

**Disagreement kept.** Raise or push away divides the sources: the Besht and the Maggid raise the thought to its root; Berditchev turns it by an a fortiori; Tanya 28 keeps raising for tzaddikim and has everyone else push it off; Breslov says to pray in order and not look at it; the Rebbe pushes it off during prayer and contemplates it at another time.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Likkutei Torah, Bamidbar 5:7 (line 1175): והענין דדוקא מצד שבשעת התפלה הוא שעת הבירור להפריד הרע מהטוב ולדחותו - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 28:2 (line 373): וגם, אל יהי שוטה לעסוק בהעלאת המדות של המחשבה זרה, כנודע, כי לא נאמרו דברים ההם אלא לצדיקים - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד. מוצש"ק פ' בא, יו"ד שבט ה'תש"ל:29 (line 1371): דירידת השכינה והתלבשות ניצוץ מהארתה בהדיבורים ובהמחשבות המבלבלים הוא בכדי שעי"ז יתעורר בכוונה יתירה

## S046 · Subduing and transforming · אתכפיא ואתהפכא

**Face:** F11 Darkness turned to light. **Also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both · F07 The home below **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5)

**The glimpse.** Holding the pull back is already His delight; turning its darkness into light is the Essence showing itself, because only what has no opposite can turn an opposite around.

**Hold.** 'When the other side is subdued and darkness is turned to light, so that the light of the Infinite shines in the place of the darkness' (Tanya, ch. 36).

**The move.** Open it: the purpose of all the worlds is the delight above when the other side is held down and the dark is turned to light, in the very place of the dark. There are two ways: holding it back, as the beinoni does, and turning it, as the strength from food, once spent on Torah and prayer, is turned from bad to good. Any light, however high, can only fight the dark, because the dark stands opposite it; only His Essence, which has nothing opposite it, can turn the dark itself to good. You, here, now: the one pull you know well, impatience or a craving, is where the home below is made, by holding back and by turning.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'darkness turned to light'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The dignity of the struggle, and the sweetness of light that came out of darkness. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips 'is'hapcha chashucha l'nehora', darkness turned to light. Then give one small 'no' to the pull today, and put the same energy at once into a mitzvah, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'darkness to light'.

**Disagreement kept.** Which is higher is kept: R. Tzadok and the Tzemach Tzedek put transforming, the penitent's way, above; the Rebbe (5730) holds that as a general rule the light drawn down by holding back is higher, while transforming has the advantage of the new. The entry does not reconcile them.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 36:5 (line 488): להיות נחת רוח לפניו יתברך, כד אתכפיא סטרא אחרא ואתהפך חשוכא לנהורא – שיאיר אור ה׳ איןסוף ברוךהוא במקום החשך והסטרא אחרא של כל עולם הזה כולו - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 37:9 (line 503): ועתה היא מתהפכת מרע לטוב ונכללת בקדושה, על ידי כח נפש החיונית הגדל ממנה - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 20:2 (line 111): ורק עצמותו ית' שהוא פשוט בתכלית הפשיטות ומושלל מכל הציורים וגם אין שייך שום מנגד אליו, ובמילא – ביכלתו לשנותו ולהפכו לטוב

## S047 · Descent for the sake of ascent · ירידה צורך עליה

**Face:** F11 Darkness turned to light. **Also touches:** F07 The home below · F06 The soul as part of the One **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 4)

**The glimpse.** The soul came down from near Him into a body and a pulling world, not as a fall but to lift that world; and what comes back up reaches higher than where it began.

**Hold.** 'This descent is for the sake of an ascent: to raise to God the whole animal soul and all its garments' (Tanya, ch. 31).

**The move.** Open it: the soul is a part of the One who fills and surrounds all worlds, and yet it is dressed in a body with its animal life; the Tanya says that descent is for the sake of lifting that life to Him. The Maharash's picture is water: before its descent the soul is like the sea, and after it, it can become living water, which is higher; a picture only, for he means that before it is a tzaddik and after it one who returns, not from sin but as 'the spirit returns to God'. What fell lowest has its root highest, so the return reaches higher than the start. You, here, now: the hungry, busy, pulled-at day in a body is that descent, and each thing done in it for Him is the way up.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'descent for the sake of ascent'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The day's weight as the road up, not the proof of a fall; a homesickness that is really love. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say Modeh Ani with your lips tomorrow on waking, the soul given back to the body. Then eat one meal today with a blessing, for strength to serve Him, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'down, for the sake of up'.

**Disagreement kept.** To what 'descent for ascent' applies is kept: in the Chabad passages read, to the soul's descent into the body and the world's into exile, while a personal fall is handled as teshuvah from love; Breslov applies it also to a person's own fall, where asking 'where is the place of His glory?' is itself the ascent.

## S048 · Good and evil and the source of evil · טוב ורע

**Face:** F09 The unity of opposites. **Also touches:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F05 The Essence beyond both **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 5)

**The glimpse.** Good and evil are two opposites, and still they are held in one: the Shechinah includes them both, because the evil is a throne for the good.

**Hold.** 'Evil is a throne for the good' (the Baal Shem Tov, Keter Shem Tov).

**The move.** Open it: good and bad mixed together, in the world and in the animal soul, are the tree of knowledge of good and evil. The Besht asks how the Shechinah, the true unity, can hold two opposites in one subject, and answers that the evil is a throne for the good. A throne is not the king, only what he sits on; and it is only a picture, since He has no seat. You, here, now: the two pulls in you, toward the good and away from it, are not two gods, and only the Essence, which has no opposite, can turn the bad itself to good.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'a throne for the good'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Steadiness before the bad: it is not a second power, only a throne under the good. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips 'Hashem echad', God is One. Then, when a bad pull comes today, do the good it pulls against, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'a throne for the good'.

**Thin.** Each source ties evil to the unity by a different mechanism, and reading them as one account is ours; so this meditation holds the Besht's picture alone and leaves the others standing beside it.

**Disagreement kept.** The source of evil is given differently: the Mitteler Rebbe, an opposing force that also comes from the essence, by way of contraction; the Alter Rebbe, the back of the will; the Besht, a throne for the good within the Shechinah's unity. The entry does not merge them.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Keter Shem Tov 1:17:1 (line 24): ואיך כוללת ב' הפכים בנושא אחד טו"ר שהם ב' הפכים והיא היחוד. אבל באמת א"ש כי הרע הוא כסא להטוב - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 1:17 (line 62): כי בישראל נפש זו דקליפה, היא מקליפת נוגה, שיש בה גם כן טוב, והיא מסוד ״עץ הדעת טוב ורע״ - The Gate of Unity 54:6 (line 2678): כי כל דבר מה שבא בבחינת התפשטות מן העצם, ודאי יש כח המנגד אליו שבא גם כן מן העצם

## S049 · The sin of the tree of knowledge · חטא עץ הדעת

**Face:** F08 Bitul. **Also touches:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F11 Darkness turned to light **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4, 5)

**The glimpse.** Before the sin there was no running tally of 'my gain, my loss', only Godliness seen; the sin planted the habit of seeing oneself, and serving Him is mending it.

**Hold.** 'Before the sin they were naked': they felt no lack or gain of their own and saw only the revelation of Godliness (Torah Ohr, Mishpatim).

**The move.** Open it: Torah Ohr says the sin planted in human nature the point of 'seeing oneself', one's own lack and one's own gain, the root of pride; before it, they saw only Godliness. Since then the covering is thicker, and serving God is mending it so that the dark turns to light; the sorting of permitted and forbidden in halacha is the sorting of that very tree. You, here, now: the running tally in your head, what you gained today, what you lost, how you came across, is that seeing of oneself. And the Rebbe says the world's descent through the sin is for an ascent higher than before it.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'they saw only Godliness'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Lightness: the tally set down, nothing to gain or lose for yourself for a moment. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips 'Shivisi Hashem l'negdi tamid', I have set God before me always. Then do one mitzvah today without checking what it gains or costs you, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'Him, not the tally'.

**Disagreement kept.** Whether the sin was a real fall is kept: in Chabad it planted self-awareness and scattered sparks, to be refined; Izhbitz says that in the future it will be clear Adam ate only the good, and the sin was 'only according to his understanding, like garlic peels, and no more'.

**Anchors** (the entry's own passages, verified there): - Torah Ohr, Mishpatim 7:20 (line 2086): והוא עיקר נקודת קליפת גסות הרוח שנעשה בטבע האדם בחטאו מעץ הדעת טוב ורע דהיינו שרואה א"ע … משא"כ קודם החטא היו ערומים - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 26:15 (line 1395): דגוף דבר האסור ודבר המותר – הוא מאילנא דטוב ורע, שהוא קליפת נוגה - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד. מוצש"ק פ' בא, יו"ד שבט ה'תש"ל:3 (line 1345): שהירידה שנעשתה בו ע"י חטא עה"ד והענינים הבלתי רצויים שלאח"ז הוא צורך עלי'

## S050 · Impurity and purity · טומאה וטהרה

**Face:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation. **Also touches:** F08 Bitul · F10 Torah and mitzvah **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4, 6)

**The glimpse.** Impurity is where His life is in exile and 'my strength' rules; purity is drawing back in the setting-aside of self, and then what is impure scatters of itself.

**Hold.** 'Through the living water, which is the drawing in of bitul, all the workers of iniquity scatter of themselves' (Likkutei Torah, Chukat).

**The move.** Open it: impurity is where the face of His will does not rest and the life there is in exile, where nature covers Him so thickly that a person can say 'my strength'. Likkutei Torah reads the red heifer this way: it is burned down to its root, and living water is added, the source of wisdom and of setting oneself aside; that is a reading, not a ruling of law. Then the forces of impurity scatter of themselves. You, here, now: what purifies is drawing that living water in, and in the end, 'I will remove the spirit of impurity from the earth'.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'living water, and they scatter of themselves'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Something fresh running in from its source. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Wash your hands for bread today and say the blessing with your lips, 'al netilas yadayim'; R. Nosson ties every purification to the water of the river that went out of Eden. During the day, return to: 'living water'.

**Thin.** The Chassidic readings state the tie, but the halachic works do not, and mikveh as such is tied only by the Maharash and R. Nosson; this meditation stays with living water and bitul and makes no halachic claim.

## S051 · Idolatry and 'other gods' · עבודה זרה

**Face:** F08 Bitul. **Also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 6)

**The glimpse.** Idolatry is not only bowing to a statue; it is treating anything, a star, a channel or oneself, as a thing on its own beside Him.

**Hold.** The stars are 'an axe in the hewer's hand' (the Tzemach Tzedek, after the Rambam, on the first idolaters).

**The move.** Open it: the first idolaters did not deny God; they honored the stars as channels of blessing and wanted the blessing without setting themselves aside. Their mistake, the Tzemach Tzedek says, is that the stars are an axe in the hewer's hand, and in truth there is nothing besides Him, all of it set aside like the ray in the sun; pictures only, but they show where the error lies. So the root of idolatry, the Tanya says, is counting something as a thing on its own, apart from Him, which is why arrogance is like idolatry, and Izhbitz says anger is too, because the angry man imagines he is master in his own domain. You, here, now: the salary, the doctor, the clever plan, your own cleverness, each is an axe in the Hewer's hand.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly: 'an axe in the hewer's hand'. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Lightness: nothing you lean on has to hold you up by itself, and neither do you. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips 'ein od milvado'. Then, when 'I did this' rises after a success today, thank Him and give a coin to charity, in your measure. During the day, return to: 'an axe in the hewer's hand'.
