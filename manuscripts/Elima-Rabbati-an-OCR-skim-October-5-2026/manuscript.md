Elima Rabbati is the Ramak's second great system after the Pardes. It is built as four "springs" (maayanot), each with "palm trees" (tamarim) of chapters: Ein Kol HaAretz (His existence, unity and providence), Ein HaBedolach (the Crown and emanation), Ein Ro'i, and Ein Shemesh (the chain of the sefirot). The headings can be read in the OCR, for example "the first palm: on His existence and oneness, that He is not a body" (התמר הא' במציאותו ואחדותו שאינו נוף וכיוצא, line 55), and "the fourth palm, on the line of measure explained in the Zohar" (תמר רביעי בענין קו המרת המתבאר בזוהר, line 267).

**This is OCR, and it is poor.** Almost all of the body text is set in Rashi script, and the OCR read it as square letters with systematic errors: aleph comes out as the digit 6, heh as samekh, tzadi as lamed or nun, and so on. A line like "and He is the whole of all that exists" appears as "וסן6 כלל כל סנמ6". Only the front matter and the opening prayer are in square type and readable. So this report is a skim, not a reading. Every Hebrew quote below is copied as the OCR has it, errors and all, and each one was checked with `find_he.py verify` against the OCR file and came back FOUND near the line given (12 phrases, 12 found). Where we give the meaning, it is our reading of the OCR, marked as such.

We searched the whole file, letters only and with the common OCR letter swaps, for the famous line "He is all that exists, and not all that exists is He". It was not found. The OCR is too broken to say whether it is there or not.

## 2. The unity of God in this book

**He and they are one** (our reading of OCR). At line 978 the Ramak writes about how Ein Sof knows all things. The OCR reads "וסן6 כלל כל סנמ6" (line 978), which decodes as "and He is the whole of all that exists" (והוא כלל כל הנמצאים). It goes on: "וכבסי" זו הס עלמותו" (line 978), decoded "and in this aspect they are His essence" (ובבחינה זו הם עצמותו). And then "טפינו מליסו' סוץ ממנו 556 סוס וסס 6'" (line 978), decoded "for they are not an existence outside Him, but He and they are one" (שאינו מציאות חוץ ממנו אלא הוא והם אחד). He knows all things by knowing Himself.

This is the Elima's version of the unity the Chabad masters quote. It says the first half of "He is all that exists": nothing has being outside Him. The same book (as quoted by later writers) adds the second half, that the things as they are, in their limits, are not He. We could not find that half in the OCR.

**The Master is one with them, fully.** In the opening prayer: "the single Master, with no second, who is united with them in complete union" (שארון יחיר ואין שני מתייחר עמהם יחור גמור, line 51; OCR has ר for ד throughout this line).

## 3. States of consciousness

- **Serving for His good, not mine** (new, RK-S36; our reading of OCR). Ein Kol HaAretz, Tamar 1, chapter 3, "the pillar of service" (ועמור העבודה, line 59). As far as the OCR can be read, the Ramak says that true service is to direct prayer and Torah to the sefirot so they are mended and can receive His light, and that the servant should not seek any benefit for himself from it, "only that all his salvation and desire is to do good" to another (רק טכל ושעו וחפנו לסעיב, line 59; decoded רק שכל ישעו וחפצו להטיב). The state: I pray and learn with no hope of gain for myself, only that it be good for Him and the world. Links S60 (the essential bond) and S61 (will and delight above reason).

## 4. Meditation and practice

**RK-T48. The Ramak's prayer before learning** (new). At the door of this book the Ramak put a prayer "fit for anyone who comes to enter the palace of the Holy of Holies". Its moves, as the text has them: - Wonder at how far He is beyond grasp: "who can reach to grasp one of a thousand thousand thousands and myriads of myriads" (מי יגוע להשיג אחר מאלף אלפי אלפי' וריבי רבבות, line 51). - Say that He is one with all He made (line 51, above). - Ask for open eyes: "so, my God, uncover my eyes and I will see wonders from Your Torah" (לכן ת' אלקי גל עיני ואביטה נפלאות מתודתיך, line 51). - Confess plainly, with the whole body and soul: "all of us, like sheep without a shepherd, have strayed and spoiled what we cannot mend unless by the great flow that comes from above" (כלנו כצאז בלי רועה תעינו וקלקלנו את אשר לא נוכל לתקן אם לא בשפע הרב הבא מלמעל', line 52). - Ask Him to mend it: "You who know my crookedness, straighten what I have spoiled" (היורע מעוותי תקן את אשר שחתתו, line 52). - Ask to learn in order to teach and to do.

The practice: before deep study or contemplation, say a short prayer with these five moves in your own words: wonder, oneness, open my eyes, I have spoiled things, mend them. Links arrive, before_whom, and lips.

## 5. The big human questions

**Can I mend what I broke?** Not alone. The prayer says we "cannot mend unless by the great flow from above" (line 52), and so it asks for it. The answer to guilt is not despair but asking.

**Why serve?** Not for reward. Service is for the good of the One served (line 59, our reading of OCR).

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **"He and they are one."** Line 978 (decoded) is the Ramak's statement that the world is not an existence outside God. The Alter Rebbe's Gate of Unity and the Tanya chapter 20 build on this: "there is nothing besides Him" (Tanya line 308, checked in the Sefer Gerushin report). The famous formula "He is all that exists, and not all that exists is He" is quoted in later literature in the Ramak's name. We could not locate it in this OCR and do not quote it as found.
- **Service without self.** Line 59 (decoded) matches the Chabad ideal of serving "to give pleasure to Him" and not for reward, and the Tanya's picture of the soul serving as a son for the Father's sake.
- **Tzimtzum.** Tamar 4 of Ein Kol HaAretz is "on the line of measure" (kav ha-middah) from the Zohar (line 267), the topic the Arizal later ties to tzimtzum. Its body text is unreadable in this OCR.
