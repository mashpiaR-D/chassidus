## 0. In one paragraph

Abulafia (1240 to after 1291) taught a way to reach prophecy by working with the Hebrew letters. You sit alone, clean, at night, in white, with tallis and tefillin. You write letters and shuffle them fast, then you sing them with the vowels, one letter to one long breath, the head drawing each vowel's shape in the air. The shuffled letters mean nothing, and that is the point: they give the mind nothing to hold, so it lets go of the pictures it usually lives in. The heart grows warm, fear and trembling come, and then, if you are worthy, an "influx" (shefa) comes down from the Active Intellect: new understanding, joy, sometimes a sense of being anointed with oil, sometimes a vision or a voice. His theory: the soul is "tied in knots" to the body and its images; the letters sit halfway between the body and the mind, so they can untie the knots; once untied, the soul's powers run back to their one source. The method came down, mostly without his name, through Cordovero (who copied his breathing instructions word for word into the Pardes), Vital (Sha'arei Kedusha), and books like Reshit Chokhmah, into the air the Baal Shem Tov breathed. Chabad hisbonenus shares his goal (loosen the grip of the "something" until the soul meets its source) but goes the other way: it does not break meaning apart, it builds one meaning so large and holds it so long that the self gives way.

## 1. Who he was, and why he matters

**The man.** Avraham ben Shmuel Abulafia was born in Zaragoza, Spain, in 1240, and died some time after 1291. He signed his *Or HaSekhel* "Avraham the Sefardi, son of Shmuel Abulafia" (Or HaSekhel 10:27). **Attested.** In a letter to his friend R. Yehudah Salmon (*VeZot LiYehudah*) he lists what he studied: Bible and grammar, Talmud with two teachers, logic, natural science, some medicine, Saadya, the *Chovot HaLevavot*, Ibn Ezra, and above all the *Moreh Nevuchim* of the Rambam, "a true guide, in its wonderful depth". He says none of this brought him to prophecy; what did was the Names: "when I came to the Names and untied the knots of the seal, the Lord of all was revealed to me" (VeZot LiYehudah, line 6). **Attested.** The same letter answers the Rashba (R. Shlomo ben Aderet), who had written against him (line 5). **Attested.**

**His story, as scholars tell it.** He travelled to the Land of Israel as a young man, studied the *Moreh* in Italy, began having visions in Barcelona around 1271, went to Rome in 1280 to speak to the Pope (who died before they met), and spent his last known years in Sicily writing handbooks for students. He saw himself as a prophet and, at times, as a messianic figure. The Rashba's opposition helped push his books out of the mainstream; they were copied in manuscript and mostly not printed until modern times. **Reported** (Scholem, *Major Trends*, Lecture 4; Idel, *The Mystical Experience in Abraham Abulafia*). Not checked here beyond the Rashba line above.

**Why he matters for meditation.** Most Kabbalah tells you *what* the letters and Names are. Abulafia is the one who wrote down *how to sit with them*: the room, the clothes, the time, the breath, the head, the order, the warnings. He knew he was breaking a silence: "now in this time the hidden has already been revealed, because forgetting has reached its very end" (Or HaSekhel 8:31, line 222). **Attested.** Kaplan built much of *Meditation and Kabbalah* on him for exactly this reason (the owner's thread 02).

## 3. The method, step by step, as the texts give it

Here is a sitting as Abulafia lays it out, mostly from *Chayei HaOlam HaBa* and *Or HaSekhel* 8:59-60. Each step carries its mark.

## Before: who may do it

1. **Clean character first.** The book opens with a warning: nobody should rush into the holy until he has cleaned his mind of "the vanities of the time" and **"cleansed himself of pride and anger"**, bears insults without answering, has mercy even on his enemies, and hates falsehood (Chayei, opening, line 8). **Attested.**
2. **A strong enough mind, and a quiet one.** "If you feel that your mind is weak in wisdom or in Kabbalah, or that your thoughts are strong in the vanities of the time, **do not say the Name**" (Or HaSekhel 8:60, line 251). **Attested.**
3. **A living teacher.** "Take care not to seek to bring your mind from potential to act until you receive the way from a wise man, complete in Kabbalah, who has already brought his own mind to act" (Gan Na'ul, line 158). **Attested.**

## The setting

4. **Alone, unheard, in a closed room.** "Choose a special place where no one will hear your voice. Be alone, set apart, secluded with no one else; sit in a room or an attic, and tell your secret to no one" (Chayei, line 907). *Or HaSekhel*: "adorn yourself and seclude yourself in a special place, so that no one else hears your voice" (8:59, line 250). **Attested.** 5. **Night is best.** "If you can do it by day, in the house, make it a little dark; but the good and right way is to do it at night" (Chayei, line 908). With **many candles** if it is night, "until your eyes are well lit" (lines 912-913). **Attested.** 6. **Clean body, white clothes, tallis and tefillin.** "Wrap yourself in tallis and tefillin … so you will be in awe and trembling before the Shechinah which is with you then. Clean yourself and your clothes; if you can, let them all be white" (Chayei, lines 910-912). *Or HaSekhel*: "seated, wrapped in white garments, washed and pure, or new, over all your clothes, or your tallis, your head crowned with tefillin" (line 251). **Attested.** 7. **Facing east.** "Turn your face toward the east, not down and not up … for from there light goes out to the world" (Or HaSekhel 8:60, line 251). **Attested.** (Kaplan thread 03 marked eastward as unverified. It is now verified in the Hebrew.) 8. **Empty the mind, and "die" for the hour.** "Purify your heart and your soul of all thoughts of this world, and think that at that hour your soul separates from your body, and you die from this world and live in the World to Come" (Or HaSekhel 8:59, line 250). "Your mind must remove from itself the yoke of all foreign thoughts" (same line). **Attested.**

## Stage one: the hand (writing)

9. **Pen, ink and tablet.** "Then take in your hand ink, pen and tablet. This will be your witness that you come to serve the Lord your God in joy and good heart" (Chayei, lines 913-914). In *Gan Na'ul* the writing hand is itself the first of three powers: "engraving by the touch of the fingers on the pen, the pen in ink, on the tablet … he makes new creatures from them, builds his world and destroys it" (line 38). **Attested.** 10. **Permute fast.** "Begin to combine letters, few or many, turn them over and roll them quickly, **until your heart grows warm** from their turning; and set your heart on their movement and on what they give birth to" (Chayei, line 915). The simplest model is Sefer Yetzirah's three mothers, all six orders: "אמש, אשמ, משא, מאש, שאמ, שמא" (Gan Na'ul, line 65). **Attested.** 11. **Letter-by-letter, "head, middle, end".** In *Sefer HaCheshek* each letter is sounded with the three positions "head, middle, end", "head of the head, in dread and trembling, joined with the joy of the mind" (I:1:19, line 38). **Attested.** How exactly the student chooses which letters to write on a given night is not fixed by the texts. **Reconstructed** if anyone says otherwise.

## Stage two: the mouth (sounding, breath, head)

12. **Aleph with each letter of the Name, forward and back.** The first "turning of the Name" is to sound aleph with each letter of the four-letter Name, "face and back": אי / יא, אה / הא, או / וא, אה / הא, each in five rows (Or HaSekhel 8:5-8:29, lines 196-220). **Attested.** Abulafia deliberately left out the full Name "so the secret would not be revealed to the people" (8:30). **Attested.** 13. **One letter, one breath, sung.** "Do not lengthen it beyond one breath, and make no break in that breath at all until you finish saying it; lengthen that one breath as long as you can, and sing on the aleph" (Or HaSekhel 8:60, line 251). In part 6 he explains why: after the letter's sound leaves the mouth, "the vowel joined with the aleph stays, rolling in a long melody, in steady motion" (6:12, line 152). *Chayei*: "the breath you breathe on each letter is a breath drawn out after the vowel in it, in its melody" (line 964). **Attested.** 14. **Breath counts between letters.** Between the aleph and its letter, "only one breath, short or long"; between a letter of the Name and the aleph in the reversed forms, up to two breaths without sound; at the end of each row, up to five breaths, no more; between one page (letter-block) and the next, up to twenty-five; and never a word or a stray thought in between. If you slip, go back to the head of the row (Or HaSekhel 8:60, line 251; the same text in Pardes 21:1:5, line 1635). **Attested.** 15. **The head draws the vowel.** Here is the table as the Hebrew gives it (Or HaSekhel 8:60, line 251, confirmed by Cordovero's copy, Pardes line 1635):

| Vowel | Where the point sits | What the head does | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cholam (o) | above the letter | "begin from the middle of the east … raise your head with the breath, little by little, until you finish, your head up"; then bow to the ground once | **Attested** |
| Chirik (i) | below | "sing the chirik, drawn downward, **drawing the upper power to cleave it to you**" | **Attested** |
| Shuruk (u) | middle | "not up and not down, but a middle drawing, toward the middle face" (forward) | **Attested** |
| Tzere (e) | below, two points | "draw your head from the left to the right" | **Attested** |
| Kamatz (a) | below, line and point | "from the right to the left" | **Attested** |

*Chayei* adds the frame: "move your heart and your head: your heart in picturing it, because it is inside; your head itself, because it is outside. **Nod your head in the shape of the vowel-point of the letter you are saying**" (lines 976-978); "at the start, do not tilt your head right or left, up or down at all … as if speaking face to face with a man as tall as you" (line 981); close the eyes, open the mouth, clear the throat, and let the head's movement end with the breath (lines 982-988). **Attested.** This settles the question Kaplan's threads 02-03 left open: the lateral directions (tzere left-to-right, kamatz right-to-left, shuruk forward) are in the Hebrew, not reconstructed. The only thing still **reconstructed** is how the modern teachers time the inhale. 16. **Music is the model.** "Know that combining is like the ears' hearing: the ear hears, and the sounds combine according to the form of the tune" (Gan Na'ul, line 64). Letters are strung like the strings of a harp. **Attested** (see §4.4).

## Stage three: the heart (thought alone)

17. **Put down the pen.** Everything that follows happens "after you throw the ink and quill from between your fingers, or after they fall by themselves from the greatness of your thoughts and the greatness of your joy" (Chayei, line 928). *Sefer HaCheshek* names the three levels outright: letters "outer, in the books"; "middle, in the mouth's speech"; and "third, in the mind, inner" (III:38-40, lines 137-139). **Attested.** Idel calls this the written → spoken → mental sequence. **Reported** (his wording), but the three levels themselves are attested. 18. **"Skipping and jumping."** The mind is meant to leap from combination to combination and meaning to meaning. Abulafia describes his own writing as "rolling many matters, skipped and jumped from one to another" (Gan Na'ul, line 80), and in *Sefer HaCheshek*: "I skip to something else near its matter yet far in the extreme … for that is how the life of wisdom is" (II:III:3, line 228). *Chayei* shows letters "skipping" to new meanings "at a run" (lines 572-575). **Attested** as a practice and a style. The fixed technical name *chochmat ha-dilug ve-ha-kefitzah* ("the science of skipping and jumping") is Scholem's summary of it. **Reported** as a formal term. 19. **Leave every first thought.** The student must link one word to another, drop the second, find a third, by halves, heads, ends, numbers and exchanges, "**until he has to leave all his first thoughts and renew others**, new ones, one after the other" (Gan Na'ul, line 81). **Attested.**

## What arises

20. **Warmth, then new understanding.** "When you feel your heart has grown very, very warm from the combinations, and you understand from them new things you never received from a human tradition nor knew by your own reasoning, **you are ready to receive the influx**" (Chayei, lines 916-918). **Attested.** 21. **Fear and trembling.** "The first thing that comes to him from the combining, in his seclusion: **fear and trembling will come over him, the hair of his head will stand up, his limbs will shake**; then, if he is worthy, the spirit of the living God will pass over him" (Chayei, line 255). The blood moves: until "the holy Name sealed in his blood" stirs, and "his blood has not fled from before it in the attribute of fear together with the attribute of joy", nothing prophetic has happened (Gan Na'ul, line 81). **Attested.** 22. **Fear and joy together.** Each letter is said "in awe, in reverence and in fear, until the joining of the soul's joy in its grasp, which is great" (Or HaSekhel 8:60, line 251). **Attested.** This fear-with-joy is not fright. It is the "double awe" (awe of love and awe of majesty) of 8:59. 23. **Anointing, and the body weakening.** "It will seem to him as if his whole body, from head to foot, were anointed with the oil of anointing" (Chayei, line 257). "The stronger the influx grows in you, the weaker your outer and inner limbs become; your whole body begins to shudder very strongly, **until you think that you will surely die at that moment**, for your soul will separate from your body out of joy" (lines 931-933). **Attested.** 24. **Picturing the court; hearing.** At the height he is told to picture God's angels around him "as if they were people standing or sitting", and himself "like a messenger the king wants to send", waiting to hear (Chayei, lines 919-922). *Or HaSekhel* (in Cordovero's copy): "if you see a form before you, bow at once before it; if you hear a voice, small or great, answer at once, 'Speak, my Lord, for Your servant hears', and do not speak at all" (Pardes 21:1:5, line 1635). **Attested.** 25. **The knots come untied.** The inner powers, "when they are untied from their knots, run to their first source, which is One without any twoness" (VeZot LiYehudah, line 8); "when you untie its knots you will find with it the unique unity" (line 7). **Attested.** (The phrase English readers know, "to unseal the soul, to untie the knots which bind it", is Scholem's rendering; the Hebrew of the idea, verified, is these lines of the letter to R. Yehudah.)

## Coming back, and the stop-rules

26. **The exit.** "Hide your face … and 'do not come near, take your shoes off your feet' … **Then go back to the body's matters, get up, eat a little, drink a little, smell a good smell, and put your spirit back in its sheath until another time. And let your heart be glad with its portion**" (Chayei, lines 934-938). **Attested.** 27. **If fear is too much: bow and stop.** "If you fear a great fear that you cannot bear, even in the middle of the saying, bow at once" (Pardes 21:1:5, line 1635, copying Or HaSekhel 8:60; the local Or HaSekhel copy is garbled at this point). **Attested.** 28. **If nothing comes: leave it for the week.** "If you neither see nor hear, leave the thing for that whole week" (same line). **Attested.** 29. **If the heart runs off: come back.** "If your blood flees from your heart … return to the place: that is why it says *ratzo va-shov*, running and returning" (Gan Na'ul, line 139). "If your heart runs, return to the place, and beware of the flame of the turning sword, lest your heart go out" (line 159). **Attested.** 30. **The danger inside the method.** "In exchanging letters (*temurah*) is the place of danger", because the same exchanges can turn blessing into curse and wisdom into folly if "the eyes are blind" (Gan Na'ul, line 49). Whoever sets out to kill the bad imagination "sees before him demons without number … all frightening him and trying to kill him" (line 125); the powers "test and try his strength", and "he must stand firm in their war" (line 82). **Attested.**

## The goal

31. **Prophecy, through union with the Active Intellect.** *Or HaSekhel* promises its keepers "the grasp of the Active Intellect, which also brings one to receive speech from its mouth" (introduction 4). The influx "comes from the Active Intellect, which tells a person **the truth of his own essence** through the combining of letters", until he cleaves to it in this life (Sefer HaCheshek I:1:21, line 40). His name for the same power, in his own tradition: "Shechinah, also *ruach hakodesh*, or the spirit of the living God" (Or HaSekhel 10:7, line 268). **Attested.** 32. **The method is the road, not the arrival.** "This way is not prophecy, but it brings one to it" (VeZot LiYehudah, line 15). **Attested.** And it is only "if you are worthy" (Gan Na'ul, line 133).

## 4. His theory: how meditation works

Abulafia is unusual because he tells you *why* each piece works. Here is his theory in six parts, in his own terms, then in ours.

## 4.1 The knots

The soul's powers are spread out and tied down inside the body. Each is held by what Abulafia calls "knots" and "seals". Untie them, and they run home to the One (VeZot LiYehudah, line 8). His own breakthrough was exactly this: "untying the knots of the seal" (line 6). *Or HaSekhel* gives a whole part (part 9, "the secret of binding and loosing") to tying and untying; its payoff is compared to Joseph let out of prison to rule, "able to bind his binders and to loosen himself" (10:4). **Attested.**

What are the knots? Abulafia's letter only says they are the soul's powers "divided in the bodies". Scholars read the "seals" as the forms stamped on the soul by the senses and the imagination: the pictures of things we carry and live inside. **Reported** (Scholem, Idel). The passages below on imagination make this reading very likely. **Ours** in plain words: *a knot is a place where the mind is stuck to a picture.*

## 4.2 Why letters: halfway between the body and the mind

A letter is a thing you can see and say, but it is also the stuff thought is made of. Abulafia says three things about letters that together explain the method:

1. **Letters are the matter of thought; meanings are its forms.** "The letters are like matter and the meanings like forms; so the meanings roll over the letters" (Or HaSekhel 7:21, line 191). **Attested.**
2. **A letter can hold only one thought at a time**, as wax can hold only one seal's picture at a time (Sefer HaCheshek I:1:29, line 48). **Attested.** So if you roll the letters fast, each new order *pushes the old meaning out*. You cannot keep a picture while the ground under it keeps moving. That is why the student must "leave all his first thoughts and renew others" (Gan Na'ul, line 81).
3. **We already think in letters, without knowing it.** Even picturing which bread to buy, a person "pictures and does not know that he is picturing in letters" (Cheshek I:1:35, line 54). **Attested.** So working the letters is working the mind's own raw material, from underneath.

**Why meaning-free combinations?** Abulafia notices that a blessing or curse in a language the hearer does not share "does not stir" him to any picture in the soul (Cheshek II:III:7, line 232). **Attested.** Put that beside point 2 and you have the mechanism: *meaning is what hooks the soul into its old pictures and feelings. A letter-string with no meaning gives the habit nothing to grab.* Cordovero admits the Names look like "words without meaning" and warns against scorning them for it (Pardes 21:1:4, line 1634). **Attested.** The strong claim that Abulafia's permutation "deconstructs" ordinary language so a higher sense can appear is Idel's (*Language, Torah and Hermeneutics*). **Reported.** The mechanism sentence above in italics is **ours**, built from the attested pieces.

## 4.3 Imagination against intellect (the Rambam's psychology)

Abulafia is a student of the *Moreh*. For the Rambam, prophecy is an overflow from God through the Active Intellect, first to the mind and then to the imagination. **Reported** (Guide II:36-37, cited from the owner's thread 05; not re-read here). Abulafia uses the same map and sharpens the danger:

- **The imagination invents.** It "never grasps any real existence"; sometimes it serves the senses, sometimes the mind, "and sometimes it makes everything up from its own heart", thinking what it grasps is the only truth (Or HaSekhel 10:17, line 278). **Attested.**
- **It cannot reach what is not its kind.** When imagination chases a grasp beyond its nature, it is "like one who chases a horse he cannot catch", takes huge strides, falls and breaks his legs, and loses even what he had (10:16, line 277). **Attested.** (A plain warning against forcing visions.)
- **Killing the bad imagination is hardest of all**, harder than beating the senses; and **"the power of habit is almost harder than all of them"** (Gan Na'ul, line 124). **Attested.**
- **The right order of the flow**: the Divine Intellect pours into the prophet's speaking power (his mind), "and the speaking power pours into the imagining power", and from there outward to the senses and to speech and writing (Or HaSekhel 10:20, line 281). **Attested.**

So the goal is not to destroy imagination. It is to clear it of its own inventions and habits so it can carry what comes from above. Vital says the same, using the same map (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:5:8-11, §5 below).

## 4.4 Music is the model

"Combining is like the ears' hearing: the ear hears, and the sounds combine according to the form of the tune." On the harp, the strings struck by the right and left hand bring sweetness to the ear, and "from them the sound passes to the heart … and joy is renewed through the pleasure of the changing melodies" (Gan Na'ul, line 64). You cannot make that pleasure except by combining, one string after another. Letters are played the same way. **Attested.** Two things follow. First, *changing* is what moves the heart; a stuck note does not. Second, the letters are chanted "in many tunes" (Chayei, line 251), and each vowel has its own "tune" (Or HaSekhel 8:60). This joins Abulafia to the biblical prophets' music (1 Sam. 10:5; 2 Kings 3:15; the Rambam, Yesodei HaTorah 7), which the owner's thread 05 gathered. **Reported** for the Rambam here.

## 4.5 Why breath and body

- **Breath is life, and aleph is the breath-letter.** No letter can be said without the aleph's breath, and "if a person's breath stops for a short time he dies at once"; so the aleph points to God, "the life of all living" (Or HaSekhel 6:11-6:12, lines 151-152). "Every breath (*neshimah*) praises" is read as *neshamah* (6:14). **Attested.** Tying each letter to one full breath ties the mind to the most basic, unbroken rhythm of being alive. No room is left for a stray thought "in speech or in thought" between letters (8:60).
- **The body is the last matter, not the enemy.** In *Sefer HaCheshek* there is a ladder of matter and form: thought is the form of the letter; the letter is matter for desire; desire is matter for picturing; picturing is matter for the human mind; the human mind is matter for the Active Intellect; and the soul carries all of it "by means of the body" (I:1:33, line 52). **Attested.** The head's movements and the breath are the body's part of the same ascent: the body *draws* the vowel so the heart can *picture* it (Chayei, line 977).
- **Desire is the engine.** Each round of combining, if the heart wakes, adds "desire on your desire", doubling, until the tenth desire (Cheshek II:III:8, line 233). **Attested.** Abulafia calls his book "Desire" for a reason.

## 4.6 What the influx is

The influx (*shefa*) is the overflow of the Active Intellect into a mind that has been cleared to receive it. Abulafia's comparison: a king's wealth flows down through the ranks to the last servant (Or HaSekhel 10:20). Its signs are a warm heart, new understanding "you never received from tradition nor knew by reasoning", joy, fear, trembling, a body that feels anointed or too weak to hold it (§3). Its content is knowledge, above all "the truth of his own essence" (Cheshek I:1:21). In his theological language the Active Intellect is what the tradition calls the Shechinah and *ruach hakodesh* (Or HaSekhel 10:7). Its highest form is union: "binding the lower part and cleaving to the upper, and the upper comes down and kisses what rises to meet it, like a bridegroom kissing his bride" (9:6, line 259). **Attested.**

## 4.7 The theory in our words (**ours**)

A person lives inside pictures held in place by meaning and habit. Abulafia goes under the pictures to their raw material, the letters, and keeps that material moving so no picture can settle. Breath and body keep the attention whole and unbroken. When the old pictures can no longer hold, the powers of the soul, "untied", run back toward their source, and something from above can write in the cleared space. What comes is felt first as heat and fear, then as joy and new knowing.

| **→ Chabad** | The Alter Rebbe uses the Sefer Yetzirah letter-machine itself, but as a *truth to contemplate*: a stone lives through "combinations and exchanges of letters, rolling through the 231 gates, face and back", until the name *even* comes down from the Ten Utterances (Tanya, Sha'ar HaYichud 1, line 788). He shares the term *ratzo va-shov* (line 1517). Chabad does not teach permuting or chanting Names. | Shared material: **documented**. | **→ The Piaseczner** | He cites Vital by name: "in *Sha'arei Kedusha* of R. Chaim Vital, especially part three", on how the prophet had to strip his soul, "remove his thoughts entirely", and picture himself rising to his root (Mevo HaShe'arim 1:44). He wants to "reveal the spark of the seeing of the *bnei ha-nevi'im* within us", by "strong thought, not rejected imaginings" (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 6:17). | **Documented** (Vital → Piaseczner). Abulafia behind Vital: **reported**. |

**The honest summary.** Abulafia's *method* came into Safed by name (Cordovero) and by quotation (Vital, reported); his *psychology* (strip the senses, quiet the imagination, cleave to the intellect) came down through Acco, Vital and Reshit Chokhmah into books the first chassidim read. His *technique of permuting and chanting Names* did not become Chassidic practice. What Chassidus kept is the inner move (stripping, *ayin*, equanimity, the letters as living) and dropped the machinery.

## 6.1 One target, two directions

Both Abulafia and Chabad hisbonenus work on the same thing: **the mind's grip on its pictures.** Abulafia calls it knots and the bad imagination and habit. Chabad calls it the *yesh*, the sense of being a something on one's own, and the "empty imaginings" that thinking without binding produces (ONE-PROCESS §1, Tanya 3).

|  | Abulafia | Chabad hisbonenus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Material** | letters with the meaning taken out | one teaching, with its meaning filled out in breadth, length and depth |
| **Move** | keep the material moving so no picture can settle; "leave all first thoughts" | hold one picture still and long, "stand on it a long time" (Gate of Unity 1:3), until it is as real as a thing seen |
| **What gives way** | the habit of meaning itself (the knot) | the self's claim to exist on its own (the *yesh*) |
| **What fills the space** | an influx from above: heat, fear, joy, new knowing, sometimes a vision or voice | bitul, and a feeling the sources say should not feel itself; at the top, the heart's simple will |
| **Role of imagination** | cleared, then used as a screen for what comes down | used as a servant (the *mashal*, the picture), then let go "where it breaks" (manual stage 5) |
| **Body and breath** | central: one breath per letter, head draws the vowel | kept small; no breath method (manual's guard) |
| **Tone** | fear and joy together; overwhelming | awe and joy; "the deeper, the less he feels it" |

So Abulafia **deconstructs** meaning until it stops holding, and Chabad **constructs** understanding until it holds so completely that the self it held is gone. Both arrive at "the mind stops". Chabad's own map already says so: at step 9, "no grasp, not even grasp by negation" reaches the Essence. Abulafia reaches the same wall from the other side, by running out of meanings instead of climbing through them.

## 6.2 What the comparison shows about the mechanism

1. **The engine is one-pointedness plus time.** Abulafia fills every breath with a letter and forbids any gap "in speech or in thought". Chabad fills the hour with one line. Both remove the room a stray picture needs. This supports the manual's "one line, and stay".
2. **Habit is the real opponent.** Abulafia puts habit above even the bad imagination (Gan Na'ul, line 124). That fits the Chabad and Piaseczner stress on repetition over years, and on judging by the fruit later (manual part 5).
3. **Joy is a precondition, not a reward.** Abulafia: you come "in joy and good heart"; the pen falls "from much joy". The Rambam and Vital say prophecy rests only in joy. Chabad says the same. This is a place where all our sources agree.
4. **The imagination is neither enemy nor guide.** Abulafia's horse (Or HaSekhel 10:16) and the Piaseczner's "strong thought, not rejected imaginings" are the same rule: never chase a vision. Use the picture as a tool and let it go.
5. **Emptying is a door, not a room.** Abulafia's emptying is followed at once by the letters. The Piaseczner's quiet is followed at once by the line. The manual already has this ("This is not the goal"). Abulafia is strong support for keeping the quiet short: his emptying is *active and filled* (letters on every breath), never a blank.
6. **The exit matters as much as the entry.** Abulafia gives an exit protocol: hide your face, go back to the body, eat a little, drink a little, smell something good, sheathe the spirit, be glad with your portion. That is the earliest Jewish written instruction I know of for *coming down safely*. It fits our steps 12-14 and the manual's "do something plain with your hands".

## 6.3 What it means for the 14-step ladder

| Step (ONE-PROCESS) | What Abulafia adds | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1-2** Where you stand; the true self | His entry test is character: no pride, no anger, bear insult, mercy on enemies (Chayei line 8). And his promise at the top is "the truth of your own essence" (Cheshek I:1:21). That is the owner's thesis in a 13th-century mouth: the influx tells you who you are. | Attested pieces; the tie is ours |
| **3** Being said, right now | The Tanya's own letter-machine (the stone lives by letters rolling through 231 gates, line 788) is the Chabad-safe form of Abulafia's material. **Proposal:** a variant sitting for step 3: take the Hebrew name of one thing in front of you (אבן, a stone; מים, water), look at its letters, and contemplate, with the Tanya, that these letters are being said now and are its life. Meaning kept, no permuting, no Names. | Proposal, ours |
| **6** Binding until the heart answers | Abulafia's "until your heart grows warm" is the same test in a different key: the practice goes on until the heart answers. Note for the card: warmth is a sign, not the goal. | Ours |
| **9** Where the mind stops | The meeting point of the two roads. Abulafia's "leave all first thoughts" and the Rashab's "grasp by negation" both end in not-knowing. Add Abulafia's warning here: "if your heart runs, return to the place" (*ratzo va-shov*), the same pair the Tanya uses. | Attested pieces; the pairing is ours |
| **10** The heart's simple will | **A real difference, keep it.** Abulafia's top is *felt*, even overwhelming (you think you will die of joy). Chabad's top is *not felt* ("the deeper, the less he feels it"). The ladder should not borrow Abulafia's peak phenomena as signs of step 10. The ladder itself does not need a new step. Abulafia's method is a **different road to the same wall**, and it is better kept beside the ladder as a comparison than inside it.

## 6.4 What it means for the manual

**Do not bring in** (and say why, in the manual's voice): - **Saying or chanting Divine Names, permuting Names, or "yichudim".** Abulafia himself hid the full Name; Cordovero refused "use" of the Names; Vital forbids practical Kabbalah and warns of the Other Side. Halachically, pronouncing the Name is forbidden. The manual stays with the Shema and plain words. - **The breath-and-head drill.** The manual's guard is "no breath-watching". Abulafia's breath is tied to letters, not watched, but it is the part most likely to produce strong body states (trembling, weakness) in someone alone. Keep it out. - **Seeking visions or voices; picturing angels around you.** Vital's test and the Piaseczner's "not rejected imaginings" both say no. So does Abulafia's own horse.

**Worth bringing in** (all attested, all mild): 1. **The exit ritual.** At the end of a sitting that went deep: stand up, eat or drink a little, smell something pleasant, do something plain, and let the heart be glad with its portion (Chayei lines 937-938). It sits well with manual stage 10 and part 5. 2. **"If nothing comes, leave it for the week"** (Pardes 21:1:5). A source against straining and against daily forcing, next to the Piaseczner's warning that a device used every day stops working. 3. **"If your heart runs, come back to the place"** (Gan Na'ul 139, 159). A traditional wording for manual stage 6 ("come back when the mind drifts"). 4. **Music before the sitting.** Gan Na'ul 64 plus the prophets' harp: a niggun is a legitimate way to open (the manual's niggun use has this behind it). 5. **The companion rule** from the Baal Shem Tov: "a person alone is in danger" in seclusion (Tzava'at HaRivash 63:1). For deeper or longer sittings, the manual can advise telling someone, or sitting in the same house as another person. 6. **Character as the entry ticket.** Abulafia's opening list (no pride or anger, bear insult) matches the manual's after-test ("a little less full of myself"). It can be quoted in Part I as the oldest form of the same rule.

## 6.5 Safety

Abulafia and Vital are frank that this road is dangerous. Their warnings, in one list: - The method can turn blessing to curse: "in exchanging letters is the place of danger" (Gan Na'ul 49). - Fears and "demons without number" come against the one who fights the imagination (125); stand firm, pray (82). - Do not start without a teacher who has already arrived (158); do not start if your mind is weak or full of worldly things (Or HaSekhel 8:60). - If fear is more than you can bear, bow and stop at once; if nothing comes, leave it for a week (Pardes 21:1:5). - If the blood flees the heart, come back (Gan Na'ul 139). - Test every spirit: if what comes is false, mixed, trivial or against the Torah, push it away (Sha'arei Kedusha 3:8:10); do not adjure or force (3:7:11); practical Kabbalah is forbidden (3:6:21). - Not alone (Tzava'at HaRivash 63:1).

**What this means for the owner's rules.** - **No drink.** Abulafia's method uses no substance at all; its "intoxication" is breath, letters and desire. Nothing in these texts supports drink. The rule stands. - **Stop for unreality.** Several of Abulafia's *goal* signs look, from outside, like the states the manual treats as stop-signs: feeling the soul separate from the body, thinking you are about to die, seeing forms, hearing voices, the limbs going weak. In Abulafia they come inside a guarded frame (teacher, character, Torah, test). In our setting, with no teacher at the side, **they are stop-signs, not milestones.** The manual's rule ("if a sitting leaves you frightened or feeling things are unreal, stop, go back to sitting 1 or 13, do something plain with your hands") is the right response, and Abulafia's own exit (eat, drink, smell, be glad) is its traditional form. - **The "special mission" line.** Abulafia's own life is the warning the manual's danger line describes: visions that grew into a messianic mission, a journey to the Pope, and a ban from the Rashba (reported history). This is said with respect; he was a great master. But the manual's rule that "sleepless certainty about a special mission" stops the sitting and goes to the crisis protocol is exactly the lesson of his story.

## 7. What is still unverified

- **Part 4 of Sha'arei Kedusha.** Not in the local copy. Kaplan's and Idel's report that it quotes Abulafia nearly word for word, and that it carries Acco's equanimity story, is **unverified**.
- **Acco's use of Abulafia.** Reshit Chokhmah shows Acco's teaching; that Acco drew it from Abulafia is **reported** (Idel, Gottlieb). Acco's *Otzar HaChaim* and *Me'irat Einayim* were not read.
- **The path into Chassidus.** That Abulafian/Accoan ideas reached the Baal Shem Tov through Vital's Part 4, Reshit Chokhmah and manuscripts is **reported** (Idel). No text shows the Baal Shem Tov or the Maggid reading Abulafia.
- **Works not read:** *Imrei Shefer, Otzar Eden Ganuz, Sheva Netivot HaTorah, Sefer HaOt, Chayei HaNefesh*, the disciple's *Sha'arei Tzedek*, and the Jellinek anthologies (1853) beyond VeZot LiYehudah. Anything said about them is **reported**.
- **Brit Menuchah.** The local OCR is unreadable.
- **The web copy of Chayei HaOlam HaBa** (emol.org) is a transcription with modern glosses in brackets. I quoted only unbracketed words, but the transcription itself was not collated with a manuscript or with Gross's edition.
- **Or HaSekhel 8:60** is garbled in the local copy at the point of the visions and the week's pause; I relied on Cordovero's copy for those lines.
- **Biography** (dates, travels, the Pope, the Rashba's ban) is the standard scholarly account, **reported**, except for the lines of VeZot LiYehudah quoted above.
- **The Rambam's prophecy chapters** (Guide II:36-37; Yesodei HaTorah 7) are cited from the owner's thread 05, not re-read here.
- **The inhale/exhale timing** that modern teachers give is still **reconstructed**. The Hebrew says: one breath per letter, drawn out after the vowel, the head moving with the breath and stopping when it stops. It does not say when to inhale.
- **"Dilug ve-kefitzah"** as a fixed technical term is Scholem's; the practice of "skipping and jumping" is attested, the formal name is not.
