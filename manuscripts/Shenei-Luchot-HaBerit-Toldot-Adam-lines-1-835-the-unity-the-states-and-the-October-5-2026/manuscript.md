Toldot Adam ("The Generations of Man") is the long opening of the Shelah. R. Yeshayahu Horowitz wrote it as the door to the whole book, an ethical will to his children made as he left Prague for the Land of Israel (line 23). He says why he opens with it: to show "how awesome man is", so that a reader who sees that his hand "rules over everything and makes an impression" above will burn to cleave to G-d (line 37). The whole introduction is one long reading of a single Zohar passage: three things are bound to each other, the Holy One, the Torah and Israel, and each is "hidden and revealed" (line 52). He builds ten "houses" on it (lines 57-58) and closes with a "Great Gate" on service as a need on high.

Much of it is quotation. He copies long pages from Cordovero's Pardes, the Avodat HaKodesh of R. Meir ibn Gabbai, Recanati, Gikatilla's Sha'arei Orah, Ginat Egoz, the Ma'arechet HaElokut with its commentators, Galante, and the Maggid of R. Yosef Karo. Below, "the Shelah" means his own words; a borrowed page is named by its source.

## 2.1 "There is nothing else but His G-dliness"

The strongest unity line in the range comes, almost by the way, inside the question of knowledge and choice (line 607). The Shelah reads Deuteronomy 4:39, "there is none else", not as "there is no other god" (that is already known from the Shema) but as a claim about everything:

> שאין עוד בעולם שום ענין רק אלהותו (line 607; Toldot Adam, Beit HaBechirah 7)

"There is nothing else in the world but His G-dliness." He spells it out:

> שאין דבר בעולם יהיה מה שיהיה לו איזה חיות וכח ותנועה וגם הדומה, שלא ישתלשל ממנו יתברך (line 607)

"Nothing in the world, whatever it is, has any life, force or movement that does not come down from Him." And the person who thinks otherwise:

> ומי שאינו מאמין כן, ומפריד ונותן לאיזה דבר כח לעצמו, ח"ו עושה שתי רשויות (line 607)

"Whoever gives anything power of its own makes two domains." This is the Shelah's own voice, not a quotation, and it is very close to the Tanya's reading of "ein od" (Sha'ar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 6). The difference is in the ground: for the Shelah the world is "nothing else" because every force in it is drawn down from Him (hishtalshelus, chain of descent), so to give it force of its own is idolatry. He does not yet say, as the Tanya does, that the world is "nothing" before Him. He says it is "not other".

## 2.2 The revealed is the hidden

The method of the whole introduction is one sentence (line 62), and he repeats it for Torah (line 315) and for deeds (line 317):

> הנגלה הוא הנסתר, דהיינו גילוי ההסתר והתפשטותו הוא הנגלה (line 62)

"The revealed is the hidden: the revealing and spreading of the hidden is the revealed." People think the hidden is a separate thing; "it is not, it is the same thing" (line 315). The small deed is the great thing, "like the grain of wheat from which the ear grows" (line 317). This is the Shelah's version of Chassidus's "the Essence is in the lowest": nothing below is outside what is above; it is the same thing thickened.

## 2.3 One light, thickened

From the Pardes (lines 107-111): sunlight strikes the moon, the moon lights the air, the air enters a room:

> הרי האור נשתנה ונתעבה מצד השתלשלותם והכל אור אחד (line 108)

"The light changed and thickened as it came down, and it is all one light." Atzilut is "like a flame bound to a coal, not a thing outside it" (line 108). Each world is soul to the one below, "only the light was hidden and thickened relative to those who receive it" (line 109). And no change happens in Him: "no change or newness on His side, only on the side of the creatures" (line 90). The flame and the coal return again and again (lines 268, 524, 735): it is the Shelah's master image of unity.

## 2.4 Hanging on Him

From Ginat Egoz (line 92): Havayah means necessary existence. "All beings hang on Him, and all would cease if He ceased." From R. Sheftel's Shefa Tal (line 708): if the flow from Ein Sof stopped "even one moment, all the physical worlds would cease and be lost in an instant". The manna teaches the same (line 490): "the life and existence are not from the bread but from G-d's will that clings to it". The Shelah draws the practical line: G-d "gives the Torah at every moment; the spring does not stop" (line 544), and the blessings are in the present tense, "who forms the lights", "who gives the Torah", "who chooses" (lines 522-533). This is F01 (the world said into being now) in the Shelah's own terms.

## 2.5 Creation by will, and oneness after it

The philosophers ask how many came from one. The Shelah's answer is will: creation is "not by necessity but as a free gift", and that removes the question (line 375). Then Job 23:13, "He is One":

> הוא באחדותו הפשוט אף אחר שהמציא הנמצאים (line 376)

"He is in His simple oneness even after bringing beings into existence." And the many are not cut off from Him: "all existence is like a jointed candelabrum, each part tied to the next" (line 376). The unity is kept by will and by the chain.

## 2.6 Where the unity is not said (kept honest)

The Shelah does not locate G-d's unity in the Essence beyond the sefirot the way Chabad does. He follows Cordovero: the Name Havayah is the essence of the sefirot, not of Ein Sof (lines 64-66), and a person has no leave to contemplate above "his own world and building" (line 103). His own solution to the problem of whether sin touches G-d is to split: the upper three sefirot are untouched, the "building" below them is touched (line 726). And he copies without objection the Maggid's picture of "the world of complete union" (the ten sefirot, "all complete union with no side of separation", line 735) above "the world of separation" (line 736). So in this range the lower worlds are "one light thickened" and "not other", but they are also, in the Maggid's words, "not His essence". The Tanya would not accept that last step; see section 6.

## 4.1 Contemplation

- **The image of G-d in yourself** (lines 151, 178, 183-188). "Set before your eyes what a person is inside and what his form hints at; then who would not be stirred to make himself holy" (line 178). The Name is drawn in the body: head like yud, trunk like vav, the ten fingers like the two hehs, two eyes and the nose like two yuds and a vav. *Guard:* "not that the essence of a hand is like a hand" (line 263); the body is a sign, not a likeness. *Tie:* close to technique `mashal` (the picture and where it breaks) and to Chabad's "from my flesh I see G-d". *Proposed technique:* "Reading the Name in the body".
- **The flame on the coal** (lines 526-529, from the Zohar). "Whoever wants to know the wisdom of the holy unity should gaze at the flame rising from a coal." The white light above never changes and never burns; the blue-black light below clings to the wick and consumes it; both are one flame. The Shelah: when Israel unite the Name, it is "one flame", and without Israel, the wick, the Presence would not cling above (line 529). *Tie:* the Tanya uses the candle for bitul (ch. 19); here it is a gazing practice for unity. *Proposed technique:* "Gazing at the flame".
- **One light thickened** (lines 107-111). Sun, moon, air, room: a simple picture to hold that the world is the same light in a thicker form. *Tie:* `walk_down`, `mashal`.
- **The limit of contemplation** (lines 103, 757). "A person has leave to contemplate only in his own world and building." Do not ask what is before, behind, above. *Tie:* technique `edge` (stopping at the edge of understanding). This is a guard the Shelah shares with Chabad, drawn from Chagigah 11b.

## 4.2 The deed with the heart

- **"Like a new decree"** (line 346). A mitzvah must be "not a commandment of men learned by rote, but like a new royal decree", done with haste and care.
- **Serving with both inclinations** (lines 346-349). Before a positive mitzvah think: "from my matter and my desire I would not do this; I do it only because G-d commanded me." Before a prohibition say, with R. Shimon ben Gamliel, "I would like it, but my Father in heaven decreed against it." So "in both he serves G-d with the evil inclination and the good" (line 347). A prohibition kept this way is a "day"; kept passively it is only a "night" (line 349). *Proposed technique:* "I would, but He said". *Tie:* S30 (holding back), S83 (accepting the yoke).
- **Limb by limb** (lines 215, 263-264, 293, 842-843). Purify one limb through its own mitzvah and it becomes a throne for its root above; "limb holds limb". The fathers became the chariot this way. *Proposed technique:* "Limb by limb". *Tie:* S81, S82.
- **Touch the tefillin** (line 456, from Avodat HaKodesh). "A person must touch his tefillin every hour", so that he takes to heart "that he is a throne of glory and a dwelling for the Presence". A physical reminder carried through the day. *Tie:* `carry`.
- **Intend the need on high** (lines 627, 632, 748). Rebbi at his death raised ten fingers: he "did not ask or intend anything for himself; all his intent was for the need on high". *Tie:* close to the Chabad "for the sake of the dwelling below"; new technique "For His need, not mine".

## 4.3 Character work

- **Conquer, do not kill** (line 451). The Shelah's own reading of "who is mighty? one who conquers his inclination": it says conquers, not kills. Take the bad traits and turn them: "envy into the envy of scholars, desire into the hidden treasure, hatred into hatred of wickedness." Then the evil "is not only cancelled but turned to good". *Proposed technique:* "Turning the trait". *Tie:* S31 (turned over), F11.
- **Humility as the root** (lines 781-787). "The root of all traits is submission, and this is the heart of return." Envy, lust and honor were Adam's sin; the one who is "very humble, his soul like dust, does not envy, does not lust, and flees honor" (line 782). Humility is the one trait with no middle path (Rambam): "be very, very lowly". A scholar keeps a sliver only for the sake of rebuke. *Tie:* S59 (true lowliness).
- **Search for the hidden fault** (lines 5, 455, 798). The early chassidim "would search their deeds, confess their sins and put on the fear of G-d" before studying secrets (line 5). Reuven "opened" return because he searched a deed that looked like a mitzvah: "perhaps some sin lies hidden, not seen" (line 798). *Tie:* `cheshbon` (the evening look-back), with a sharper edge: search the good deeds too.
- **The teaching tied to a verse** (line 24). He joins each moral lesson to Torah words "in length, breadth and depth, so that it enters the heart more". *Tie:* techniques `breadth`, `length`, `depth`.

## 4.4 The minimum for everyone

The sukkah may have four walls, three, or two and a bit (lines 812-813). The Shelah reads this as the four walls of a Jewish life: Torah, prayer, charity, return. Someone who cannot learn and cannot give can still hold two walls, return and prayer, and give "a little" charity: "this every Jew can fulfil" (line 812). This is a humane floor for the guide: no one is outside the practice.

## 5. The big human questions

- **Why we exist.** "The purpose of creation was His free gift, in His great goodness, to do good to another, that they recognize His G-dliness" (line 281). Man is made "to know that Hashem is G-d, and to be like G-d" (line 39), and "so that he too will testify to the unity of the One Name" (line 154). Man is the purpose of creation because he includes all of it (lines 151, 409).
- **Suffering.** The Shelah rejects the view (he names Ralbag) that bad things come by accident from matter: "the good things are meant for their own sake, the bad for the sake of something else" (line 44); "the good aim at love, the bad at awe" (line 45). "Judgment is for the sake of mercy", the way a father disciplines a son (line 282). He distinguishes suffering of love from suffering that ends the wicked (line 283). And G-d is inside the pain: "in all their trouble He was troubled" (lines 686, 699), "the salvation of Israel is the salvation of the Holy One" (line 671).
- **Is it better not to have been born?** (lines 441-460). He takes the Talmud's debate head on. Before the sin, all agree it is good to be born. After it, the answer turns on repair: "by the power of the darkness the light will be greater" (line 449). The pressed grapes: the dregs come out and the wine is clearer than before (line 450).
- **Reward.** Reward is not a wage but the deed's own root: "reward and punishment are not agreed upon from outside but belong to the thing itself" (line 42); "there is no mitzvah, even a light one, without a great root above, and that root is itself the reward" (line 288). Sin's punishment is also natural: Adam "cut himself off from the source of life" (line 437).
- **The soul.** Breathed from G-d: "one who breathes into another gives of his own breath" (line 156, Ramban). Its levels are one light seen by receivers of different size (line 271).
- **Death and eternity.** Death is not natural; it follows from cutting off (lines 428-437). Eternity is in body and soul, "from grasp to grasp" without end (lines 434-435, 469-519).
- **Being held.** G-d is "your shadow" (lines 633-634). The Presence pursues the righteous one to give to him, because "more than the calf wants to suck, the cow wants to nurse" (line 719): the Giver wants to give more than we want to take.
- **Joy and love.** The end of the book is a circle dance: "the Presence in the middle and they around" and each points with his finger, "this is our G-d" (line 819). Thanks will outlast all prayer (line 505).

## 6. With Chabad: agreements and disagreements (kept)

- **"Ein od".** Agreement in words, difference in ground. The Shelah: nothing has force of its own; to think so is "two domains" (line 607). The Tanya (Sha'ar HaYichud VehaEmunah 6): the world is truly nothing before Him, like the light of the sun in the sun. The Shelah's version is the one the Ramak holds ("not other"); Chabad goes further ("nothing"). Both use the flame and the coal.
- **Tzimtzum.** The Shelah quotes the Pardes that limiting the unlimited is itself "the power of the greatness of our Creator" (line 704). This is close to the Chabad teaching that G-d's power includes the power of limit. But he never reads the tzimtzum as only concealment from our side (Tanya, Gate of Unity); for him the "building" really is affected by deeds (line 726).
- **Is G-d touched by our deeds?** The Shelah records a real argument among the kabbalists (lines 672-731): Ma'arechet says the lack is only in the children; Chayat says sin truly blemishes the Presence; Shefa Tal says it is only "nachat ruach" that His will is done (line 720). His own answer divides the sefirot: the upper three are untouched, the building below is touched (line 726). Chabad keeps both sides by speaking of the Essence, who is beyond being touched, and who nevertheless "desired a dwelling below". The Shelah has the dwelling too: "He desired that the Presence be among the lower ones" (line 778), "the main place of the Presence was among the lower ones" (line 643).
- **Rambam's attributes.** The Shelah reverses the Rambam's "borrowed names": "the limbs of the chariot carry their names in Him essentially; the names of our limbs are borrowed" (line 263). The Rambam says the words are ours and applied to G-d; the Shelah says they are His and applied to us. He keeps the Rambam's guard ("not that the essence of a hand is like a hand"). Chassidus later takes the Shelah's side (the upper root is the "real" hand) while keeping the negative theology for the Essence.
- **Rambam on the world to come.** The Shelah sides with Ramban and the kabbalists: eternity in body and soul (lines 469-519).
- **Particularism.** In this range the Shelah follows the Zohar in saying the nations are not called "adam" and that their souls come from impurity (lines 177, 193). This is kept, not smoothed. The guide (D28) does not carry it; it takes the Shelah's claim that "adam" means the inner form, not the body (line 177), and applies it to every person.

## 7. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **The Shelah's "ein od" as a daily test**: "does anything here have power of its own?" (line 607). Simpler to carry than the Tanya's "it is nothing", and a real first step toward it.
2. **The body as a throne**, limb by limb, with the tefillin touch as a reminder (lines 215, 456). This gives the guide a bodily practice of unity that Chabad has only in passing.
3. **Conquer, do not kill**: turning each trait to its holy object (line 451). Clear, humane and usable.
4. **"He is your shadow"** for the being-held work (line 633): the response of G-d follows the face you turn to Him.
5. **Reward is the deed itself** (line 288): this frees service from bargaining, and is the ground for "for His need, not mine".
6. **The four walls, two for anyone** (line 812): no one is outside.

## 8. Thin places

- About half the range is borrowed text. The Shelah's own voice is strongest in lines 62, 260-269, 281-288, 313-321, 346-349, 451-460, 596-626 and 683-726. - Very little here is a sitting practice. The practice is in deeds, limbs, traits and prayer, not in quiet contemplation. - The kabbalistic arithmetic (gematria, letter shapes) takes many lines (e.g. 126-145, 751-835); it was read, but little of it bears on the guide.
