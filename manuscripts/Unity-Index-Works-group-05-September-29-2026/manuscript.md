## Summary

Group-05 has twenty-two works: thirteen from the Chabad Rebbeim and the Strashelye circle, nine from other schools. The Chabad maamar collections carry the one idea as their stated center, each from its own side. Torah Ohr and Likkutei Torah take it through the year of Torah readings. Torah Ohr gives the spine's own 'yachid, not echad' in the Alter Rebbe's words (Megillat Esther, 2378). Likkutei Torah has the most material on the two unities and on 'know today'. The Mitteler Rebbe's maamarim (OCR) have the highest density of 'Essence' in the group; they find sovev 'in every single detail' and say that before the Essence the worlds are not even 'as nothing'. The Tzemach Tzedek's maamarim and Toras Shmuel put creation in a 'mere radiance' and the dwelling in the Essence. That is the Maharash's side of the spine's kept dispute, already present in his father. Toras Shmuel also has the strongest non-literal reading of tzimtzum ('the world only seems separate'). The Rashab's Kuntres Umaayan applies the idea to the animal soul, to livelihood and to nature ('the Godliness in nature is itself the Godliness above nature'). His Eitz HaChaim makes it a curriculum and keeps the warning about learning without bitul. The Rebbe's Likkutei Sichos vols. 30-39 prove the idea from pshat and halacha and state its history: 'ein od' from the Besht, the unity 'from the world's side' from the Alter Rebbe. Thin, and said plainly: Kuntres Acharon (halacha), most of the annotated Siddur, the Tzemach Tzedek Sefer HaSichos (mostly stories), and Poked Akarim. Sefer HaMiddot is also thin; its center is the tzaddik and faith. Avodas HaLevi is unreadable Rashi-script OCR, and its counts are noise. Outside Chabad the idea is stated with force in R. Tzadok's Resisei Layla, which has the highest rate of 'ein od milvado' in the group and makes it the purpose of free choice. It is also stated in the Radziner's Sha'ar HaEmunah (God's side and man's side, held by faith), in Mevo HaShearim ('no yesh and no tzimtzum, only Godliness'; a Jew as radiance of the Essence), in Tzava'at HaRivash (a discipline of seeing providence and vitality everywhere, with spatial tzimtzum wording) and in Yosher Divrei Emet (devekut as recognizing 'we are nothing'). Bat Ayin and Irin Kadishin carry it partially, through fear and achdut and through the tzaddik. Bat Ayin also reads 'know today... there is none else' as a moral scale, and that reading is kept.

## Signature (reading, resting on stated passages)

In these volumes the one idea is proven from the plain sense. A sicha typically starts from an exact word in Rashi, a Mishnah or a halacha, solves the question on its own terms, and only then shows the inner point it carries. The inner point is usually the unity as 'no other existence besides Him' (1147) or the dwelling below made by Torah and mitzvot (46), and the sicha closes in an instruction. That is the Rebbe's grammar G07 ('add life, not a fifth interpretation') and G11 (begin from the effect, end in action), carried out volume after volume. The collection adds two things of its own, both stated. The first is a history of the idea: the Besht revealed 'ein od milvado', felt as simple faith above the self, and the Alter Rebbe revealed the unity 'as it is from the world's side', so that the world does not contradict it but reveals its depth (35574-35575). The second is where the idea must be tested: not in withdrawal but in the ledger. The chassid wrote 'ein od milvado' as the sum of his timber accounts after writing every figure exactly (15944). That the whole ten-volume set, including its many purely exegetical or halachic sichos, is one expression of this idea is our reading; the Rebbe states the tie often, but not in every sicha.

## Structure

Each volume runs through the Torah portions and festivals. Every sicha has numbered sections: a difficulty in the text, a resolution in pshat or halacha, an inner explanation (often marked 'ובפנימיות' or 'ע"פ חסידות'), and a directive (הוראה, 301 lines). Holiday sichos, general letters (e.g. the Rosh Hashanah letters in vol. 39, 38431) and the indexes follow. The index layer (41% of lines) is itself a map: 'אין עוד' and 'דירה בתחתונים' are subject headings in the index of almost every volume (e.g. 17489, 22420, 27573, 38742), so the Rebbe's editors treated them as running themes.

## Disagreements kept

- Where the unity is brought down: these sichos put it in business and in deed (15944, 20500), the Rebbe's side of the spine's 'prayer versus action' question; 15943 still grants that the recognition is most easily reached in prayer and study. - Knowledge versus faith: 35574 gives the Besht's side as faith above comprehension and the Alter Rebbe's as the unity understood from the world, and keeps both as stages rather than choosing one.

## Signature (stated in its parts; the whole-book claim is reading)

The Tzemach Tzedek's maamarim take the unity from the side of its purpose. Creation, he says in so many words, came 'not by toil': it is from a mere radiance, a breath, the letter hei. The dwelling below, however, is 'the revelation of the Essence of the light of Ein Sof below', and it comes through toil in Torah, because Torah is Chochmah, Chochmah is bitul, and the light of Ein Sof rests only on what is batel (674). The same maamarim give the reason in the other direction: the bare act of a mitzvah has an advantage even over Torah study, since the main intent is the drawing of Ein Sof below, a dwelling below (216). His method is to explain the Alter Rebbe. Whole maamarim are commentaries on Tanya chapters, and the explanation proceeds by the soul analogy (G06): God fills the worlds as the soul fills the limbs, hidden, but the dwelling is like the soul's seat in the brain, revealed (189). The spine names the Tzemach Tzedek's definition of Chassidus as 'chayus'. In this book the working vocabulary is rather dwelling, Essence and Torah and mitzvot; that his 'vitality' definition is the same point is our reading.

## Structure

Maamarim in calendar order for 5614 and 5615, then supplements. Each long maamar opens with a verse or a Zohar passage, raises difficulties, builds the Kabbalistic frame (sefirot, memale/sovev, Havayah/Elokim) and ends in avodah, usually Torah, mitzvot and the dwelling below. The Tanya expositions show the book's role: it is the Alter Rebbe's system unfolded one chapter at a time.

## Signature (stated in the passages; the whole-corpus reading rests on the Mitteler Rebbe's own statement of Shaar HaYichud's aim (spine §1))

The Mitteler Rebbe said his Shaar HaYichud is about 'the unity of Godliness in every single detail' of the chain of worlds. These maamarim carry out that program at great length. The angle is the Essence, and how it stands to every detail. Even the surrounding light, which seems to give nothing to the worlds, is present 'in every single detail' of the chain, down to the four elements and the mineral, plant and animal. It is hidden and present equally, as a general thought is in each word (31188). At the other end he sharpens the higher unity. Toward the very Essence, 'the single King alone', one cannot even say that the worlds are 'as nothing before Him' by lowliness of rank; 'they do not exist at all', for there is none besides Him (17258). It ends that one must 'hear that there is none besides Him, in truth, as before creation so now' (39249-39250). What he adds is density and detail: sefirah mechanics carried out so that each detail can be joined to the whole (G04). The dwelling below (F07) is comparatively rare here. ## Signature (stated)

Tzaddikim, who strip off physicality and see all as ayin as before creation, return yesh to ayin, a greater wonder than creation (26). R. Meshullam insists that this is knowledge, not assent. The one who does not think of God constantly 'believes it, yet it is not truly pictured before him' (24). He also has an angle on the lower unity: creatures are many from their outside and one from their inside, and they become one when all their desire is for devekut; that is Shabbat, 'united in the secret of One' (84-85).

## Structure

First kuntres: letters on humility, devekut and truth, with the Maggid's teachings (1-59); second kuntres (from 60): prayer, thought, the mikveh, Shabbat and the holy days; Derekh Emet (101-110).

## Passages

> **Yosher Divrei Emet, First Kuntres 12:2** (line 25 · stated · F08, F12)
> ונמצא אם נזכה לזה הדביקות נחשוב על עצמינו ג"כ כן כי אנחנו אין בלתי השי"ת שמחיה אותנו הוא המצוי וזולתו אין עוד
> R. Meshullam Feivush: the fruit of constant devekut is to regard ourselves as nothing, since God who gives us life is the one who exists and 'there is none besides Him'; when we recognize that we are as nothing 'as before creation', God has true delight (12:1-2). Ein od milvado as the goal of devekut, with knowledge defined as seeing it oneself, not mere belief.

> **Yosher Divrei Emet, First Kuntres 12:3** (line 26 · stated · F08)
> וצדיקים במעשיהם שמפשיטים את עצמן מגשמיות וחושבים בהשי"ת תמיד ורואין ומבינים ומדמים באמת שהוא כאלו היה אין כקודם הבריאה נמצא מחזירים מיש לאין
> The Maggid (as reported by his disciple): creation descended from nothing to something; tzaddikim who strip themselves of physicality and see that it is as if all were ayin 'as before creation' return yesh to ayin, a greater wonder than creation itself.

> **Yosher Divrei Emet, Second Kuntres 47:1** (line 84 · stated · F02, F07)
> והברואים הם רבים מצד חיצונותם אבל מצד פנימותם הם אחד ואימתי הם אחד כשהם דבקים וחשקים למחשבה אחת לדביקות השי"ת
> Shabbat as 'the secret of One': creatures are many from their outside and one from their inside, and they become one when their whole desire is for devekut; this devekut 'unites and joins God with the creatures'. Unity here is an achieved state of the creatures' longing, a non-Chabad angle on the lower unity.

## Disagreements kept

- Knowledge or faith: 24 asks for seeing 'in one's own mind', which is closer to the Chabad demand for comprehension than to simple faith, though the means is devekut rather than hisbonenus.
