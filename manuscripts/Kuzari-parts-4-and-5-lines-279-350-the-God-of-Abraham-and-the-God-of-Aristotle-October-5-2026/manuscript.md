## The shape of the range

Part 4 goes back to the Names and reaches the book's most famous line: the difference between "Elokim" and the four-letter Name is the difference between the G-d of Aristotle and the G-d of Abraham. Then it shows how the prophet sees, how the other faiths stand, what Sefer Yetzirah teaches, and what science the sages had. Part 5 gives the king what he asks for, a short course in the philosophers' physics and psychology, and then takes it apart. It ends with free will and providence, and with the Chaver leaving for Jerusalem.

1. **Elokim and the Name** (279-281). A title of rule against a proper name. The Name is known by prophecy, not proof. The inner eye.
2. **Fear and love come from what is seen** (283-285). Thought alone cannot hold a vision. The times and places where the light is seen.
3. **Abraham and Aristotle** (291-297). Taste and seeing against proof. Abraham leaves his astrology.
4. **The seed in the ground** (301). Exile as a seed that turns the soil into itself.
5. **Sefer Yetzirah** (303-318). The One in many things; counting, speech and writing are one in Him; "run and return".
6. **The philosophers' soul, and its limits** (323-336). The four elements, the soul's powers, the active intellect, and the questions they cannot answer.
7. **Choice, providence and the road** (340-350). Four kinds of causes. The veil of bright light. "His service is true freedom."

## The unity of G-d

**Elokim is a title; the Name is His name.** "Elokim" means a ruler, of all or of a part; it is plural because idol-worshippers called the sum of all powers "gods" (line 279). The four-letter Name answers the question "which power should I serve, the sun, the moon, the fire, the angels?" with a proper name, as one says "Reuven". It is "a known name, pointed to by traits, not by place" (line 279).

**The Name is known by seeing, not by proof.** The king asks: how can one name what cannot be pointed at, except by proof from His works? The Chaver:

> אבל רומזים אליו ברמיזה הנבואית ובראיה הרוחנית, כי הראיות מתעים (Kuzari 4:3; line 281)
> But one points to Him by the prophetic pointing and by spiritual sight, for proofs lead astray.

Proofs led the dualists to two gods, the eternalists to an eternal sphere, the sun and fire worshippers to their gods. The finest proofs, the philosophers', led them to a G-d who "does not help us or harm us, does not know our prayer and offerings", "and none of them has a known name to point to Him" (line 281). Adam knew the Name only because He spoke to him.

**Ehyeh: "I will be with them."** When Moses asks His name, the answer "Ehyeh asher Ehyeh" means: why seek what they cannot grasp? Tell them "I will be" there when they seek Me:

> אל יבקשו ראיה גדולה מהמצאי עמהם, ויקבלוני כן (Kuzari 4:3; line 281)
> Let them seek no greater proof than My being with them, and let them receive Me so.

*Ours.* This is the Kuzari's whole method in one line of Torah. The proof of G-d is His presence, met. It is the root of the guide's first move: start from where He is already present (technique 1, S65).

**Many traits, one Essence, as many sparks and one sun.** "The traits are many and the Essence is one, because the dwelling that receives differs, as the sparks differ and the sun is one" (line 281). The Chaver adds an honest limit: the likeness only works if the sun itself could not be seen, only its sparks.

**The king you know through all his clothes.** The senses grasp only outer traits. You see a king in battle, at court, at home, young and old, sick and well, and you say "he is the king" by the judgment of the mind, because he spoke to you and commanded you, and that part of him is "an essence without bounds that cannot be pointed at" (line 281). When he dies you see the same shape and know he is not the king. So the prophets saw forms, and "the forms are true in relation to what imagination and sense seek, and not true in relation to the Essence that the mind seeks" (line 281).

**The inner eye.** As G-d set a fit between the outer sense and bodily things, He set a fit between an inner sense and what is not bodily:

> ושם למי שבחר מברואיו עין נסתרת רואה דברים בעיניהם, לא יתחלפו (Kuzari 4:3; line 281)
> And He gave to those of His creatures He chose a hidden eye that sees things as they are, without change.

> ומי שנבראה לו העין ההיא הוא הפקח באמת, ויראה כל בני אדם כעורים (Kuzari 4:3; line 281)
> One who was made with that eye is the truly seeing one, and he sees all people as blind.

Perhaps that eye is "the imagining power while the mind makes use of it". The proof that these sights are true is that all prophets agree on them, as we all agree that honey is sweet (line 281).

**The sun of the Names: plain light and piercing light.** The book's clearest picture of how one G-d relates to the world in two ways (line 293). One sun shines on everything. On clear air and water its light is "piercing" (נוקב); on polished stones "bright"; on wood and earth "seen"; on all things together, "light" with no special name. Plain light is "Elokim". Piercing light is the Name, the light that passes through the pure souls of the prophets "as the sun's light passes through onyx and pearls". Then:

> וענין אלהים ישג בהקשה, כי השכל מורה שיש לעולם מושל ומסדר ... אבל ענין יי לא ישג בהקשה, אך בראיה ההיא הנבואית (Kuzari 4:15; line 293)
> What "Elokim" means is grasped by reasoning, for the mind shows that the world has a ruler and orderer ... but what the Name means is not grasped by reasoning, only by that prophetic seeing.

*Ours.* Chassidus reads the same two Names with the same weight but a different picture: Elokim is the power that hides and limits, the Name is the light that brings into being, and "the Name and Elokim are one" (F03, F09; Gate of Unity ch. 4-6). For the Kuzari, Elokim is the G-d reached by the mind through nature, and the Name the G-d met in revelation. The Tanya takes up the Kuzari's contrast but turns it inward: Elokim is nature seen as G-d's garment, and the Name is what the garment hides. Both agree the Name is the nearer one.

**"This is the G-d of Abraham; that is the G-d of Aristotle."** The king sums it up, and this is the line of the book:

> כבר התבאר לי ההפרש בין אלהים ויי והבינותי מה בין אלהי אברהם ואלהי אריסטו, ושיי תכספנה לו הנפשות בטעם וראיה, ואלהים יטה אליו הקשה (Kuzari 4:16; line 294)
> The difference between "Elokim" and the Name is now clear to me, and I understand what is between the G-d of Abraham and the G-d of Aristotle. For the Name, souls long by taste and by seeing; toward Elokim, reasoning leans.

> והטעם ההוא יביא מי שיגיע אליו שימסר נפשו על אהבתו ושימות עליה, וההקשה הזאת היא רואה כי רוממותו חובה בעוד שלא תזיק ולא יגיע בעבורה צער (Kuzari 4:16; line 294)
> That taste brings whoever reaches it to give his soul for His love and die for it. This reasoning sees that exalting Him is a duty only as long as it does no harm and brings no pain.

And the Chaver adds that Abraham bore Ur of the Chaldees, exile, circumcision, sending away Ishmael and the binding of Isaac "because he saw of the divine matter what he saw, taste, not reasoning" (טעם לא הקשה, line 295). The sages read "He took him outside" as "go out of your astrology": leave all the wisdom of reasoning and cling to the service of the One you have reached by taste, "as it says: taste and see that the Lord is good" (line 295).

**What the taste does to a person.** When the Name is seen, "a person almost parts from his kind and clings to the angelic kind, and another spirit enters him" (ותכנס בו רוח אחרת, line 293). Then the old doubts leave, he mocks the proofs he used to lean on, and:

> ואז ישוב האדם עובד, חושק בנעבדו, ומוסר נפשו להרגה על אהבתו, לגדל מה שהוא מוצא מערבות הדביקה בו והנזק והצער בהתרחק ממנו (Kuzari 4:15; line 293)
> And then a person becomes a servant, in love with the One he serves, ready to give his life for His love, for the greatness of the sweetness he finds in clinging to Him, and the harm and pain in being far from Him.

*Ours.* This passage is the reason the owner asked for the Kuzari. It says there are two ways to hold the same One. By proof, which is true and cold and gives way under pain. By taste, which is sweet and makes a person ready to die. An experiential guide is on the side of taste. But note the Kuzari's taste is not a private feeling; it is "seeing" (ראיה) given through prophecy, the chain of those who saw, and the deeds they were told. See KUZARI.md on how Chassidus joins the two (the Tanya's "da'as" makes the proven thing felt).

**The Name as the heart of the world.** "The Name" belongs to the heart among men, Adam and his chosen line; that is why after creation the Torah says "the Lord Elokim": "a full Name on a full world", and "the world was only complete in Adam, who was the heart of all that came before" (line 293).

**Sefer Yetzirah: the One in all the many.** The book is Abraham's, and it "showed His G-dliness and oneness in things that differ, many from one side, but united and leaning on one another from another side, and their agreement comes from the One who orders them" (line 303). Counting, speech and writing (ספר, ספור, ספר) are three in a person and one in G-d:

> הנה שב הספר והספור והספר בחק האל דבר אחד, ובחק האדם שלשה (Kuzari 4:25; line 303)
> So counting, telling and writing are one thing in G-d, and three in man.

For man, the measure, the word and the letter only point to the thing; "but G-d's measure and His word is the thing itself, and it is His writing" (line 303), as if a weaver thought a pattern and the silk took it. *Ours.* This is F01 in the Rishonim: His speech is not about the world, it is the world. Chassidus will build its whole doctrine of the letters of speech that bring things into being (Gate of Unity ch. 1, 12) on the same Sefer Yetzirah, and the Kuzari is one of the bridges.

The "faithful witnesses" of His oneness are "world, soul and year": the same order runs through space, the person and time, "the wisdom in them all is one, and the providence is one" (line 303). And the center that holds the six sides: "the holy palace set in the middle carries them all ... He is the place of the world, and His world is not His place", which the Kuzari reads as "the divine matter that joins opposites" (line 316). *Ours.* This is F09 (the unity of opposites) and F04 (He surrounds and is not held) stated by the Kuzari through Sefer Yetzirah.

**His being is His essence.** On the Name: "G-d's existence is His essence", for He has no genus and no difference, so no definition, "and He must be He" (line 316). The Rambam's point, in the Kuzari's words.

**Renewed every day.** The king says the plain faith is enough: G-d made all things at once and "sustains them moment by moment by the divine power, as we say: who renews in His goodness every day always the work of creation" (line 317). The Chaver: "Well said, king of the Khazars! This is the truth" (line 318). *Ours.* The Chassidic reading of that blessing (F01, S65) is already the Kuzari's.

**The veil of bright light.** When reasoning reaches the First Essence and its traits, it stops and "sees before them a curtain of bright light that defeats the sight", withheld from us "because of the shortness of our sight and mind, not because it hides or is lacking, for it is bright and revealed" to those with prophetic seeing (line 343). So:

> אם כן נבחן בפעליו יתברך ונעמד מספר עצמו, כי אלו היינו משיגים אמתתו היה זה חסרון בו (Kuzari 5:21; line 343)
> So let us study His works and stop short of telling of His Essence; for if we grasped His truth, that would be a lack in Him.

*Ours.* This is S35 (the edge of knowing) and technique 14 (stopping at the edge of understanding), in a picture: not darkness at the limit, but too much light.

## States of consciousness

**The one who has seen, and longs to see again.** Fear and love, says the Chaver, enter the soul from what is present to the senses, not from what is told (line 283). The prophet sees in one moment the greatness of the form made for him, the outstretched arm, the fire, the voice warning and telling, the angels bowing, the hand stretched to those who return:

> ויכנס המורא והאהבה תקועים בנפשו כל ימי חייו, והולך כל ימי חייו חושק, הומה ומבקש שיתראה אליו פעם שניה או שלישית (Kuzari 4:5; line 283)
> And awe and love enter, fixed in his soul all the days of his life, and all his days he goes on longing, yearning, asking that He show Himself to him a second or a third time.

A proposed state: **after the seeing: longing that stays**. It is close to S13 (the unnamed longing) and S19 (after the high), but it is the healthy form: the vision did not leave a crash, it left a fixed love and fear and a lifelong wish. The guide can use it to tell a real opening from a counterfeit: the real one leaves awe and love fixed, and a longing that serves.

**We are the weak-eyed; there are times and places of seeing.** The king says a vision cannot be told in a book. The Chaver:

> אבל אנחנו כבעלי העינים התרוטות אשר לא יוכלו לראות האור ההוא, וצריך שנסמך על הפקחים אשר קדמונו (Kuzari 4:7; line 285)
> But we are like people with weak eyes who cannot see that light, and we must lean on the seeing ones who came before us.

> כן הפקח באור האלהי יש לו עתים ומקומות, בהם רואה האור ההוא, והעתים הם עתות התפלות, כל שכן בימי התשובה, והמקומות הם מקומות הנבואה (Kuzari 4:7; line 285)
> So the one who sees by the divine light has times and places when he sees that light. The times are the times of prayer, and more so in the days of return; and the places are the places of prophecy.

*Ours.* Two states in one line. **Leaning on the seers**: humble, trusting those who saw (S83, accepting the yoke, joined with S84). And **the open hour**: prayer, and above all the Days of Awe, are times when the light is nearer. The guide already keeps a calendar of such times; the Kuzari gives it a reason.

**The balanced man who longs upward.** The fullest human state in part 5: a person whose natures are even, with "opposite traits in his power like two pans of a true scale in the hand of the weigher":

> והאדם ההוא בלי ספק לבו ריק מן התאוות המפלגות, וכוסף אל מדרגה למעלה ממדרגתו, והיא המדרגה האלהית (Kuzari 5:10; line 332)
> That person no doubt has a heart empty of scattering desires, and longs for a level above his own, which is the divine level.

He thinks what to do to master his nature and traits, gives neither anger nor desire its will, "and he takes counsel and asks his G-d to guide him on the straight way". To such a one the divine spirit flows: prophetic if he is fit for prophecy, or a spirit of learning if lower, "and he will be pious, not a prophet; for there is no stinginess before Him" (שאין כילות לפניו, line 332). This is the same ruler of part 3, seen from the side of his longing. It fits S56 (the self quieted) with S13.

**Shabbat: the world of rest.** Psalm 104 is read as the six days of creation. On the seventh He rested because the natural works that need time were done and reached, in man, "the level of the angels who need no natural powers". The mind shapes heaven and earth in one moment, "and that is the world of the angels and the world of rest; when the soul clings to it, it rests, and so it is said of Shabbat that it is a taste of the world to come" (line 332). S76.

**Freedom as service.** At the end, the king says: you once chose freedom; now you want more duties, going to the Land where more commandments bind you. The Chaver:

> אבל אני מבקש החרות מעבדות הרבים אשר אני מבקש רצונם ואינני משיגו ... ועבודתו הוא החרות האמתי, וההשפלה לו הוא הכבוד על האמת (Kuzari 5:25; line 347)
> But I seek freedom from serving the many, whose favor I seek and do not win ... His service is true freedom, and lowering oneself to Him is true honor.

A proposed state: **free in His service**. The person who has stopped living for the approval of people. Close to S59 (true lowliness) and S83, with the Kuzari's clear felt note of relief: one service, whose favor is won "with a little effort", in place of many masters who are never pleased.

**The one born with faith.** "Only the few have faith by nature, and all the other opinions fall away from them, and the place of their error falls from their souls at once" (line 324). Most people must pass through many opinions. Later (line 338) the natural believer is compared to one with an ear for poetry: he tastes the meter and never slips, though he cannot teach it; "such people, natural to the Torah and to nearness to G-d, kindle sparks in their souls from the words of the pious, and they become lights in their hearts". *Ours.* A state and a way: **the spark caught from the pious**. The guide's reading of the masters is exactly this.

## Meditation and practice

**Seeing needs the senses; do not trust the one who says he thinks it all.** "Do not believe the clever one who says his thought is put together in order until it reaches all the matters of G-dliness by his mind alone, without leaning on the sensed, on the sight of a likeness, of words or writing or forms" (line 283). You cannot even hold all your prayer in thought without reading it, or count to a hundred without saying the numbers. *Ours.* This is a strong source for why the guide uses pictures, words said aloud, and scenes (techniques 9, 10, 18). The Kuzari says the mind alone cannot carry a great thing; it needs a sensed form.

**Sefer Yetzirah's instruction: stop the mouth, stop the heart, run and return.** The Chaver quotes it as the book's secret of the ten:

> בלם פיך מדבר, בלם לבך מלהרהר, ואם רץ לבך שוב למקום, שלכך נאמר: רצוא ושוב (Kuzari 4:40; line 318)
> Stop your mouth from speaking, stop your heart from thinking; and if your heart runs, return to the Place, for so it says: "running and returning".

> ודע וחשב יצור, שהיוצר אחד ואין בלעדיו; ולפני אחד מה אתה סופר (Kuzari 4:40; line 318)
> Know and think and picture that the Maker is One and there is none besides Him; and before One, what do you count?

*Ours.* This is C5 (running and returning) and technique 14 at their source, quoted by the Kuzari. And "before One, what do you count?" is a meditation in itself: count the many things and come to the One before whom counting ends. It is a door to S62 (only Him).

**Abraham's path: think, then leave the thinking when He speaks.** Sefer Yetzirah may be Abraham's reasoning before G-d spoke to him; "after He spoke with him he left all his reasonings and turned to seek G-d's will" (line 318). Plato told of a prophet who said to a philosopher in a vision: "you will not reach Me by these ways, but by those I set as messengers between Me and My creatures" (line 318). *Ours.* The guide's order: contemplation is a road, not the goal; when the meeting comes, the thinking bows.

**Do what the means require, then hand over the rest.** On choice: the lazy man's claim ("G-d already knows what will be") is no claim; prepare weapons for your enemy and food for your hunger, for your safety or loss comes through means (line 342). Then the line on trust:

> וכאשר ישתדל האדם בסבות האמצעיות, אחר שימסר אל האלהים במה שנעלם ממנו בלב שלם, ימצא טוב ולא יחטיא (Kuzari 5:20; line 342)
> When a person makes an effort in the means, after he has handed over to G-d with a whole heart what is hidden from him, he will find good and not miss.

But to walk into pure danger trusting in G-d is "testing the Lord" (line 342). *Ours.* This is the Chovos HaLevavos and the Chabad balance of trust (S84): effort in the means, heart given to Him. The guide should keep the guard: trust is not a license for danger.

**Rebuke kindles a spark.** Even when rebuke does not turn a sinner back, "it kindles a spark in his soul, and he sees that the deed is bad, and that is part of return and its beginning" (line 342). And one with Torah who sins keeps "traces" of an angelic way, "and remains in the fire of longing for it" (ונשאר באש הכסף אליו, line 342). He would not trade places with one who has no Torah, as a sick man in pain would not choose to be a horse with no pain.

**Intention and deed, both.** The king: "the Merciful One wants the heart". The Chaver:

> ואם היה המעשה בלעדי כונה או כונה בלתי מעשה תאבד התוחלת, אלא במה שאי אפשר המצאת הכונה (Kuzari 5:27; line 349)
> If there is a deed without intention, or intention without a deed, the hope is lost, except where the intention cannot be had.

*Ours.* The book began with a dream: "your intention is pleasing, your deed is not" (line 1). It ends by joining them. The whole Kuzari is one sentence: the heart wants a deed, and the deed wants a heart.

## The big human questions

**Why are the Jews scattered? Is the light gone?** The light "has not set except in the eyes of one who does not see us with an open eye" (line 299). Exile is a seed in the ground: it looks rotted into earth and water, but "it is what turns the earth and water into its own nature", until it yields fruit like the first; so every faith that came after the Torah of Moses "will turn to it in the truth of its meaning" and all will be one tree (line 301). The Divine light "rests only on the humble souls" (line 300).

**Free will and providence.** Four kinds of acts: divine (from the First Cause alone), natural, chance, and chosen. All go back to G-d, but the chosen ones "by a chain", not by first intention (line 342). If every event were made directly by Him "at every moment", there would be no wonder in miracles and no difference between the servant and the rebel (line 342). His knowledge of what will be is not its cause, as knowing the past did not cause it.

*Ours.* Here the Kuzari differs from the later Chassidic "He alone does all, at every moment" (F01, S63). The Kuzari keeps a real middle world of means and chance so that choice and reward stay real; Chassidus keeps choice real too, but sees the means themselves as garments of His constant speech. On this point the Tanya's Gate of Unity goes further than the Kuzari; the Kuzari's line 215 (He holds each limb each moment) comes closest.

**Suffering and the righteous who suffer.** Some causes can be found by study (testing, paying off sins, reward in this world or the next, the fathers' sins or merits). "What is not revealed is handed to G-d's knowledge and justice, and a person admits his ignorance of the revealed causes, all the more the hidden ones" (line 343).

**Where is G-d near?** The king says nearness to G-d is reached "in every place with a pure heart and strong longing" (line 344). The Chaver agrees in part: the seen Presence is gone, but "the hidden, spiritual Presence is with every Israelite and every follower of the true faith, pure in deeds, pure of heart, whose soul is clear to the G-d of Israel" (line 345). Yet a place known to be G-d's awakens longing and purifies the soul; and Jerusalem will be built "when Israel longs for it with the utmost longing, until they cherish its stones and its dust" (line 349).

**The philosophers.** They are excused: without prophecy they built the proven sciences as far as they go (line 336). Socrates is quoted twice: "your divine wisdom I do not deny, but I say I do not know it; I am wise in human wisdom" (lines 291, 336). But their account of the soul cannot explain sleep, drunkenness, a blow to the head, or why the souls of Aristotle and Plato are not one (line 336). And their goal, knowledge as happiness, lets a man steal or kill "as long as he is a philosopher" (line 291).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 302 (request), 320-322 (the calendar, anatomy and the laws of treifos as signs of the sages' science), 324-331 and 334 (the philosophers' physics of form and matter and their theory of the soul's powers, which the Chaver gives only to refute), and 340 (the kalam proofs of creation, eternity and incorporeality) were read in full. They matter for the book's argument that Israel held real science and that reason alone does not settle first things. Only the passages quoted above carry teachings on the unity, states or practice.
