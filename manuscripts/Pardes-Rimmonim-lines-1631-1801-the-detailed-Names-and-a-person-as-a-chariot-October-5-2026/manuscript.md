# Pardes Rimmonim, lines 1631-1801: the detailed Names, and a person as a chariot

*Range:* lines 1631-1801: Gates 21 (The Particular Names) and 22 (The Cognomens)

---
Two gates. Gate 21 is the densest in the book: the long "particular Names" of God (the 72-triplet Name from Exodus 14, the 42-letter Name from Genesis 1, the 22-letter Name from the priestly blessing, the 12 diagonal boundaries, the 54 and 72 permutations). Most of it is tables. But it opens with a full quotation of an Abulafian meditation on the Name (from "Sefer HaNikud"), and it is full of guards on what may and may not be done with Names. Gate 22 asks why the Torah speaks of God's hand, eye and ear, and gives one of the Ramak's most useful teachings for the guide: a person becomes a "chariot", a seat, for a divine attribute by what he does with his body and his life.

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**Why He showed Himself at all.** "Before the emanation, the greatness of the Necessary Being was not known to any other, for there was nothing besides Him" (כי קודם האצילות לא הי' גדולת מחוייב המציאות נודע לזולתו לפי שאין בלתו, line 1792). The worlds exist so that His greatness can be known by someone. And the light had to be stepped down, "from trace to engraving, engraving to hewing, hewing to making" (line 1792), because the first light was too fine for any creature to draw from.

**The simple does not change.** Why does the one light branch into so many? "The nearer a thing is to the simple Source, the more it must be free of all change, for the simple does not change" (כי כל עוד שיתקרב הדבר אל המקור הפשוט יחוייב היותו משולל ומופשט מהשנויים, line 1667). So He works change through branches, and branches of branches. All the multitude of names and powers exists so that the One can stay One.

**His exchange is One.** R. Nechunya's acrostic for the Name of Keter: "One, head of His oneness, head of His union, His exchange is One". The Ramak: even when the unions above are broken, "His true name is One, for He never changes, never lacks and never adds" (כי לעולם לא ישתנה לא יחסר ולא יעדיף, line 1647).

**No secret Name behind the Torah's Names.** Some held that the "real" Name of God was a hidden one and that the Torah's Names were given to the masses. "God save us from this view... the main Names are those that came in the mouth of the Torah" (כי עיקר השמות הם אותם אשר באו בפי התורה, line 1649).

**Not a body, not an organ.** Gate 22: "hand", "eye", "ear" are not organs, above or in the sefirot. In our own body, "the eye" is really the power of seeing and "the hand" the power of taking; the Sages speak of a minor who "has no hand" to receive a divorce (line 1772). So "He who plants the ear, shall He not hear?" means the power of hearing is from Him. Father and Mother among the sefirot is "all a parable from the bodily to the spiritual" (והכל משל מן הגשמי אל הרוחני, line 1773), and "permission was given to use parables, as much as a person needs to understand" (כי ניתן רשות להמשיל בהם משלים, line 1775). The body is a set of signs, "like writing 'Reuven son of Yaakov', which is not Reuven, only a reminder of him" (line 1776, from Sefer HaOrah).

**The shining face hides judgment.** The Zohar's parable of a perfect king: his face always shines. The fool sees "the king's face shining and smiling and is not careful"; the wise man says, "I see that in that very shining, judgment sits and is hidden" (אנא חמי דבההוא נהירו דינא יתיב ואתכסייא, line 1662). "I the Lord have not changed: I did not move to another place; everything is included in Me" (line 1662). Mercy and judgment are not two moods; both are in the one face.

## 3. States of consciousness

**The king's smile (RK-S16, proposed).** From line 1662. Inside: I see His face shining at me, and I am glad; and I know that in the same shining sits justice, so I am glad and careful at once. Not fear that shrinks, not ease that forgets. How to enter: when things go well, stay in the gladness and add one line, "and He is whole: justice is here too". This is S46 (Higher awe) held inside S53 (Glad): the map has both, not the two in one face.

**Being a throne (from Gate 22).** "The soul of the righteous who does the mitzvot becomes a chariot and a throne for the Shechinah, and the Shechinah is clothed and hidden within that soul" (נמצא נשמת הצדיק שהיא הפועלת המצות נעשה מרכבה וכסא אל השכינה, line 1787). Before Abraham, the Shechinah "was clothed in a bird's nest, until he made her a palace in his holy soul" (line 1787). Inside: this deed is not only mine; something rests in it. This is RK-S12 (Sought by the Presence) from the other side, and S82 (The home below). No new id.

**The eye of the mind.** "The eye sees only what is before it... but the mind sees even what is not before the eye" (line 1771). R. Elazar saw hidden things not by learning but by "the seeing of solitude, by which a person grasps things to come and things past" (זו היא ראיית ההתבודדות, line 1771). And "as water shows face to face, so the heart of one person to another" (line 1771): hearts read each other. The Ramak states these as facts, not as practices.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**One limb, one mitzvah (RK-T15, proposed).** Sefer HaOrah, quoted and accepted: "if a person merits to purify one of his limbs, that limb becomes like a throne for the upper thing called by that name" (ואלו יזכה האדם לטהר אבר מאבריו יהיה אותו האבר כדמיון כסא לאותו דבר העליון, line 1776). "One who guards the sight of his eyes... that eye becomes like a throne" (line 1776). The Ramak adds two conditions (line 1782-1783): first the whole body must be clean of sin, "for the Shechinah does not rest on a blemished soul" (כי אין השכינה שורה על נשמה פגומה, line 1782); then "let him be exact in one mitzvah and hold it more than the rest" (ואח"כ ידקדק באיזו מצוה ויאחז בה ביותר, line 1783). What it is for: to make one part of life a place for Him, and to let it grow from there. How: choose one limb (the eyes, the mouth, the hands) and one mitzvah, and keep them with special care for a season. Guard: not a substitute for the rest; he says so plainly. Ties to `act`, `cheshbon`.

**Become the chariot of the trait you practice (RK-T16, proposed).** The key sentence of Gate 22: "Although a person's soul is drawn from one attribute, he can become a chariot for another attribute by his deeds, for every soul holds all ten; and when a person strives in kindness he becomes a chariot for kindness" (אפשר שיעשה מרכבה למדה אחרת ע"י מעשיו... וכאשר ישתדל אדם בחסד יהיה מרכבה למדת החסד, line 1795). "By people's deeds a person becomes a chariot for one of the sefirot" (line 1799). And the Bahir gives Abraham's kindness in three rungs: he "set his bread and water before all who came" (סדר לחמו ומימיו לכל באי עולם, line 1790); he found merit even for the guilty, as the attribute of Kindness itself says, "even if they are guilty, I acquit them" (ואפי' נתחייבו אני מזכה אותם, line 1789); and he brought hearts back to their Father. Kindness says of him: "all the days Abraham was in the land I did not need to do my work, for Abraham stood in my place" (line 1789). What it is for: nature is not fate; a trait is taken on by doing. How: pick the trait you lack; do its three rungs, from the outward (feeding, helping) to the inward (seeing merit, helping someone come back). Ties to `act`, `carry`; closest to CATALOGUE A3 (imitatio Dei); new is the claim that practice changes which attribute you carry.

**Holy work at the bench (RK-T17, proposed).** Enoch was a shoemaker, "and with every hole he pierced with the awl he blessed for the sake of the Above" (ובכל נקיבה ונקיבה שהיה נוקב במרצע היה מברך לצורך גבוה, line 1799); the Ramak reads it as uniting the Shechinah (the "shoe") with Tiferet through his work. What it is for: the plain repeated motion of a day's work as a place of union. How: attach one short blessing or intention to one repeated motion in your work. Guard: the work must still be done well. Ties to `carry`, `act`. (The later Chassidic retelling of Enoch the cobbler grows from this line.)

**From blue to the Throne.** The Bahir quoting R. Meir: "tekhelet is like the sea, the sea like the sky, and the sky like the Throne of Glory" (מפני שתכלת דומה לים וים דומה לרקיע ורקיע דומה לכסא הכבוד, line 1668). A looking-ladder: from the blue thread to the sea to the heavens to the Throne. It fits RK-T09 (Let the senses move the soul) and `picturing`. No new id; the guide can use it as an example of RK-T09.

**Every hour a thought of awe.** Ma'arechet Elokut: each hour has 1080 parts, one for each combination of the Name, "so that all day you should think of the love of Heaven; therefore idle talk is forbidden" (שכל היום תחשוב באהבת שמים, line 1639). Ties to `carry`, S65 (Given right now).

**Before sleep.** The 42-letter Name (Ana BeKoach) "is good to say before sleep" and near death, so that the soul rises and is guarded (line 1729). The guide can keep the plain form: a set prayer at bedtime, said slowly.

**Abulafian meditation on the Name: recorded, not for the guide.** The Ramak copies at length the method of "Sefer HaNikud": solitude, white clothes, facing east, one breath per letter, head moved with each vowel, "purify your heart and soul from all thoughts of this world, and think that at this hour your soul is parting from your body" ( line 1634), "your mind must remove from itself all other minds" (צריכה דעתך להסיר מעליה כל הדעות הזרות, line 1634); if a form appears, bow; if a voice, answer "Speak, my Lord, for Your servant hears" (line 1635); if great fear, bow at once. It carries its own guard: "if you feel your mind is weak in wisdom or kabbalah, or your thoughts are strong in the vanities of the time, do not pronounce" (line 1635). The Ramak values it "for knowing the greatness of our Creator", but also rules: the Names were known "not to use them, God forbid, but to know the power of their Maker" (לא להשתמש בהם ח"ו אלא לדעת יכולת קונם, line 1634), "and for this reason we will not inquire into the uses of the Names" (line 1634); and he names Yosef della Reina as the warning. Ours: the guide should record this as history of Jewish meditation and not teach it. It involves pronouncing the Name, it courts visions and terror, and the Ramak himself fences it. The practices in the guide that come closest (`quieting`, RK-T01) are enough. The same applies to the "vowel instructions" for the 42-letter Name (line 1713) and the 22-letter Name's "virtues" (line 1734), which are practical kabbalah.

**We have no kavanah now.** A humbling line: "the Sages of Israel agreed that we have no kavanah at present" (שהסכימו חכמי ישראל שאין לנו כעת כוונה, line 1779), because one would need to know which aspect of which sefirah each word touches. The Ramak's answer is not to stop praying but to learn; the guide's answer (ours) is that the heart's turning (`before_whom`) does not wait for the map.

## 5. The big human questions

**Who am I? Is my nature my fate?** Each soul is drawn from one attribute (line 1783), so there is a real "type". But "every soul holds all ten" and deeds can make a person the chariot of another attribute (line 1795). Isaac's nature was Malchut, and he took on Gevurah by the binding (line 1796). Jacob wanted Malchut and was given the middle (line 1797). Our root is given; our seat is chosen.

**Help for one who wants to be pure.** "One who comes to be purified is helped" (line 1790), and the Ramak explains: "they open doors for him beyond what is due" (אז פותחין לו פתחים יותר מן הראוי, line 1799), as God dug a tunnel for Menashe under the Throne. For one who comes to defile, doors are merely left open.

**Judgment and trust.** The king's smile (RK-S16). And in exile, "the Father comes down in the Tzaddik to guard the Shechinah in exile" (דאבא נחית בצדיק לנטרא לשכינתא בגלותא, line 1751). No exile is unguarded.

**Suffering and poverty.** David was poor, the Zohar says, because all his work was adorning the Bride, not yet the union that brings plenty (line 1786). Some lives are preparation whose fruit comes later.

**Joy in the world's filling.** "When there is fitness of deeds in this world, the moon is full" (כשיש כשרון מעשים בעוה"ז אז היא לבנה במילואה, line 1701). What we do is what fills her.

**Knowing the Judge.** Isaac "made known to all who came into the world that the world has a Judge and a God" ( line 1783) and rescued the oppressed. Justice is a way of serving, not only a thing to fear.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **"The fathers are the chariot."** Both use it. The Tanya: "the limbs of a person doing a mitzvah become a chariot for the Supernal Will, like the hand that gives charity to the poor" (היד המחלקת צדקה לעניים... הם נעשו מרכבה ממש לרצון העליון, Tanya line 329). The Ramak (with Sefer HaOrah, line 1776): a purified limb becomes a throne for its matching upper power (eye for Eye, hand for Hand). The Tanya's chariot is bitul to one Will; the Ramak's is a correspondence, organ to attribute. The guide should keep both: the Tanya's is simpler to live, the Ramak's lets a person feel which part of him is being made holy.
- **Becoming the chariot of a trait by deeds.** Line 1795 is not in the Tanya in this form. The Tanya speaks of the beinoni who never changes his nature but rules it. The Ramak says practice can change which attribute a person carries. These fit together (ours): the Tanya is about the animal soul's pull; the Ramak about the godly soul's shape.
- **Names.** Chabad inherits the Ramak's firm line (line 1649: only the Torah's Names) and his refusal of practical kabbalah. It does not teach Abulafian pronunciation; neither should the guide.
- **Bodily language.** The Ramak (and Sefer HaOrah) reads "eye" as power, not organ; the Gate of Unity does the same with "speech" and "breath" of God. Full agreement, and both warn that the parable is permitted "as much as a person needs to understand" (line 1775).
- **Enoch the shoemaker.** The Ramak's reading (line 1799) is the source behind the Chassidic story of holy work at a trade. Chassidus widens it into avodah b'gashmiyut, service in physical things.
