## 1. The book as one meditative journey

This volume is not a maamar or a book of chapters. It is an encyclopedia entry, a very long one, built from hundreds of sources in Chassidus and the Rebbe's talks. Yet read in order it walks a person down a real road: from the word 'one' said without thinking, to the Essence present as Himself in the plainest things, with the world still standing. The editors name the road on the first page: two truths that seem to fight, 'there is nothing besides Him' and 'the world is really created', which in the end 'strengthen one another' (line 21).

The road starts low and wide. The entry Achdus (lines 27-1049) looks at oneness in the world before it looks at God. It teaches the mind that 'one' has levels, echad (many made one) and yachid (one with no parts), and that every unity we know falls short (lines 33-35, 87-100). Then it finds oneness everywhere: in the binding force that holds a piece of wood together until fire takes it apart (lines 237-239), in the ox that gores three times, in the etrog that grows from every kind of weather, in the plain willow whose lack of quality lets simplicity show (line 339), in flax that grows one stalk alone. It warns that reaching for the undivided One while ignoring the details made Cain careless (line 371). Then it turns to people: we are made incomplete so we will need each other (line 506); the cause of unity is bitul, and a proud man cannot bear another (line 585); the root of unity is the yechidah (line 701); unity among people is a vessel for the Shechinah (line 738). And it guards the road: unity does not erase the lines God drew; fire and water cook only with a pot between them (line 927). Korach saw the future truly and lived it too early (line 1026).

Then the entries on the Unity of God (lines 1050-3637) climb. First the plain meaning, one God with no partner, brought right into the day: livelihood is a garment (line 1117), anger is a small idolatry because 'at the time of his anger the faith left him' (line 1313), pride is idolatry, and even a holy deed at the wrong time is 'foreign service' (line 1444). Then the Chassidic meaning, no existence besides Him, in four rungs: the world is not a 'true' existence, like a spring that stops once in seven years (line 1553); it is not an existence for itself, because the word is said inside it every moment and is its very essence (line 1767); it counts for nothing before the Ein Sof (line 1807); and before the Essence it was never made (line 1822). At the top the book turns around: the highest oneness is not the world erased but the world's own existence united with Him, which only the Essence, who 'bears opposites', can do (lines 1205, 1884). Nature shows it (a thrown stone 'opens our eyes', line 2081); purpose shows it (the Rebbeim 'saw not the ceiling but the word', line 2179).

The last and longest entry (lines 2243-3637), on the higher and lower unity, is where the book becomes a manual of the inner life. It tells the reader where he stands. Most souls live in the lower unity: the world is real, and one yields oneself to God in it by effort and acknowledgment (lines 2378, 3199). The higher yielding comes 'of itself' and is given from above (lines 2353, 2499). Even self-sacrifice that is one's own is still a 'something' (line 2407). Yet everyone must taste the higher, 'even imaginatively' (line 3208), or the lower service is not true; and those who fly high must keep 'their eyes below' (line 3293), because the lower unity refines the world, lasts forever, and completes the home below (lines 2580, 3326). At the end the two are joined, like measuring the Ark and finding it both two and a half cubits and no space at all (line 3408), and on Shabbos the two loaves lie side by side, 'exactly equal' (line 3572).

So the book goes from 'God is one, and the world is over there' to 'the world, as it is, is one with Him, and I can hold both measurements at once'. The contemplation it asks for is mostly thinking through, with many worked pictures (wood and ash, a thrown stone, a mirror, sunlight inside the sun, a teacher shrinking an idea for a student), each marked where it breaks. It asks too for acknowledgment (hoda'ah): believing and admitting what one does not see, which it says anyone can do 'as long as the soul is within me' (line 3488). It places the unity in prayer (the Shema's two Names with a pause between, 'Baruch Shem' said quietly, the Amidah's 'still, small voice'), in blessings over food, and in plain conduct: anger, money, charity, peace at home. It asks for bitul more than feeling, and it is suspicious of feeling that one produces oneself. It has no song and little of the heart's cry. Because it is a reference work, some sections are mostly definitions; this reading takes each one for the single step it gives the mind, and says so when a section does not reach the edge.

## Publisher's foreword (lines 15-25)

**The movement.** It sets two truths side by side before any teaching starts, and asks the reader not to drop either one. The mind that likes a single clean answer is told, gently, that it will have to carry two.

> שתי נקודות אלה לא רק שאינן סותרות זו את זו, אלא מחזקות אחת את רעותה
> *These two points not only do not contradict each other, but strengthen one another.*

**Contemplating it.** Take the two points wide first. One: God is not only the only ruler; He is the only reality, and treating anything else as real on its own has a touch of idolatry in it (line 19). Two: the world is not a dream. 'In the beginning God created' is written in a Torah of truth, and the Rambam rules that He brings every existing thing into being (line 20). Then step by step. If only the first were true, the world would be a mirage and nothing we do in it would count. If only the second were true, the world would stand on its own and God would be one more thing in it. Then to the root. The editors say the book will show how each point makes the other stronger (line 21). That is the whole volume in one sentence: the more real the world is, the more it shows Him; the more He is all, the more the world matters. Bring it to your table. The cup in front of you is really there, and you will really bless it. And the cup is nothing apart from Him. Try to hold both for a moment, without letting one swallow the other. The picture here is two points on one line. It breaks, as the book will show, because these are not two ends pulling apart. They meet in One who carries both.

**From inside.** A kind of steadiness. The relief of not having to choose between a spiritual life and a real one. The world gets heavier and lighter at the same time.

**At the edge.** Not yet. It names the paradox the whole volume will walk into, and promises it can be held.

**Into the day.** Before you eat today, look at the food for a moment and say: this is real, and it is His.

## Achdus (Unity), introductory chapters, §§1-2: what 'one' means, and how the many become one (lines 27-59)

**The movement.** It slows the mind down on a single word, 'one', until it opens into levels. The reader finds that 'one' is not a number but a ladder.

> ענין העשוי מהתאחדות פרטים - נקרא ,אחד", וענין שמופשט מפרטים - ,יחיד"
> *A thing made of particulars that have become one is called 'echad'; a thing abstracted from particulars is 'yachid'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: 'one' can mean three things (lines 27-28). Nothing else like it outside. No parts inside. Or many separate things joined together. Length: a day is 'one day' though it has evening and morning; a man is 'one man' though he has 248 limbs (line 33). That is echad. But the truer 'one' has no parts at all. That is yachid (lines 34-35). Depth: even yachid has levels. The power to move is one power, though it will become many different motions; it is one only as a source. Higher still is what is not even a source: the soul's yechidah, which is not the root of its four lower levels (lines 37-45). And what is 'above source' at one level is only 'source' at the next (line 45). The ways many things become one: joined for a purpose, like a group formed to do His will; helping each other, like limbs; becoming one body; standing still before a king; or feeling something above all division shine in them (lines 51-55). Dust is the picture: grains of sand that the moisture binds into one clod (line 59). Bring it home. Look at your hand: five fingers, one hand. Then at the will that moves it: one will, before any finger. Then ask what is above even your will. The dust picture breaks: the moisture is something added to the grains. The unity the book is after is not glue from outside.

**From inside.** A gentle climbing. The ordinary word 'one' starts to feel deep, like a staircase going up out of sight.

**At the edge.** It points there. The rung 'above source' has no top here; each 'above' becomes a 'source' for something higher (line 45).

**Into the day.** Say the word 'Echad' once today slowly, and let it mean more than a number.

## Achdus, introductory chapters, §§3-6: only God is truly one; perfect unity comes only from Godliness (lines 60-105)

**The movement.** It empties the mind of the hope that anything in the world could be fully one by itself. Then it shows the one way full unity can happen.

> כאשר נרגש בכל דבר שמציאותו האמיתית היא אלקות
> *When each thing feels that its true existence is Godliness.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: true oneness is only in God; Torah and Israel share it because they are one with Him (line 60). Creation, by its very definition, is 'many': many creatures, and each one made of many parts (line 60). Length: yet creation still shows His oneness, the way a vessel shows the power of the craftsman who made it (line 61). People show it more than other creatures (line 64). Depth: every unity we know falls short (lines 87-100). Ministers stand as one before a king only while they forget themselves; the moment they leave, they are separate again. Grains of dust are one only because of the moisture. Even the limbs of one body meet only through the part of each that faces the other; the head and the foot, in themselves, never touch. Then the turn: full unity comes only from Godliness (line 101). When each thing feels that its real existence is Godliness, and that its own particular job is to show that there is nothing besides Him, then things are one from inside, and each keeps its own shape (line 102). Bring it home. Think of the people in your house. You help each other, you love each other, and still each one is closed in his own world. The book does not ask you to erase the differences. It asks what would happen if each of you felt that your own particular life was a way of showing Him. The king picture breaks here: before the king the ministers lose their shapes. In the unity from Godliness, the shapes stay and are themselves the unity.

**From inside.** First a small grief: nothing is really one. Then a softening toward the people and things around you, as if each were a different letter of the same word.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: a unity that is not made but found, because each thing's own being is Godliness.

**Into the day.** Pick one person you live or work with and say to yourself: his way of being, different from mine, is also His.

## Achdus, introductory chapters, §7 and the four groups of entries: the world made a 'private domain', with its boundaries kept (lines 106-123)

**The movement.** It guards the reader against a false unity that melts everything together. Unity, it says, keeps the lines God drew.

> שהדברים שבעולם כמו שהם עכשיו (שלכל דבר יש הגדר שלו) יהיו באחדות
> *That the things in the world, as they are now, each with its own boundary, should be in unity.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: in the world, what shows is separation; the unity hides. So the world is a 'public domain', a place of many. Through Israel, 'one nation', and Torah, 'one Torah', it becomes a 'private domain' of the One (line 106). Length: this does not erase the definition of each thing (line 107). Erasing the walls between things is not peace; it is strife. Korach's 'the whole congregation is holy' sounded like perfect unity and was the worst kind of quarrel (line 108). Depth: there are two stages. Now, even after things are joined, the division stays, because the unity is not yet simple. In the time to come, a simple unity will shine, and it will shine inside the division itself (lines 110-113). So the work here uses the soul as it is in the body, with its separate powers, and the mitzvos in their 613 details. Yet each separate act should be soaked in the point of oneness above all division (lines 115-120). Bring it home. In your family, each person has a place and a role. The book says you do not make peace by pretending you are all the same. You make it by letting the one thing you all serve shine through your different jobs.

**From inside.** Respect for difference that does not feel like distance. A quieter kind of love, that does not need everyone to be alike.

**At the edge.** It names what is beyond for now: a simple unity that belongs to the future and will shine within the differences (line 113).

**Into the day.** Today, do your own part in a shared task well, without taking over someone else's part.

## Unity in creation (Achdus b'Briah): introduction and §§1-2 (lines 124-187)

**The movement.** It turns the eye to the many things of the world and asks it to find, in their very greatness, a trace of the One. It moves the mind from counting to weighing.

> בדוגמת עשיית כלי ע"י אומן, שבהכלי ניכר
> *Like a vessel made by a craftsman, where the vessel shows the craftsman's power.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: 'How many are Your works' (line 136). There are endless creatures, no two exactly alike (line 162), and each is made of many parts: four elements, matter and form, the thing and what brings it into being (lines 163-174). Length: the vessel picture. You can tell something about a craftsman from what he made; the book even tells of a Chassidic master who could tell from a vessel that its maker was blind in one eye (line 140). So the unity of the Maker shows in what He made (line 137). Depth: two verses. 'How many are Your works' speaks of quantity, the endless creatures of this world. 'How great are Your works' speaks of quality, the higher worlds and the host of heaven (line 176). Quantity is division. Quality is unity, because the higher a thing is, the more it is above parts (lines 177-187). And the verse adds the Name: 'How many are Your works, Havayah' (line 139). In the many, the Name that is above the root of creation shows. Bring it home. Look at a crowded street, or a shelf full of jars. Then look for the 'greatness' in one thing there: what is fine, whole, worth something in it. The book says that point of worth is where the One shows. The craftsman picture breaks: a craftsman's power stays in his hands and leaves the vessel. Here the Maker is not outside what He made.

**From inside.** Wonder, and a slower gaze. Things stop being a heap and start to have faces.

**At the edge.** It does not reach it here. It prepares the eye.

**Into the day.** Choose one ordinary object today and look for one quality in it that points above it.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Unity in creation §3: unity inside the plurality (the binding force, nature, chazakah) (lines 188-274)

**The movement.** It shows the mind that under every 'many' there is a hidden 'one' that holds it. Then it names the moment when contemplation flips the whole picture.

> ומזה באים אח"כ ל,אלקים בגימטריא הטבע"
> *And from this one comes afterward to 'Elokim has the numerical value of hateva, nature'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: all people carry the seal of Adam, yet none is like another (lines 201, 207). The world is like one body, each creature a limb (line 210). Each creature has a point that unites its parts, because its life is divine and divine life means oneness (line 219). Length: burn a piece of wood. The fire, air and water go up in smoke; only ash is left, and almost all its 'thing-ness' is gone. Separate the elements of the ash too, and nothing is left (lines 237-238). So a thing's real existence is the binding force that holds its elements together, rooted in the word that creates it (lines 239-240). Nature does the same: all the events in a thing flow from one nature (line 250). Even halachah knows it. An ox that gores three times becomes a known gorer; the law looks past three different places and days to the one point they share (lines 267-270). Depth: 'nature' and 'Elokim' have the same number. Before contemplation, nature is what shows and Godliness hides inside it. After contemplation it turns over: Godliness shows and nature hides (line 259). Bring it home. The chair you sit on seems solid and simple. Think of it as many parts held by one invisible grip, and the grip as a word being spoken. Then read the same sentence backwards: not 'nature, which is God', but 'God, who looks like nature'. The wood picture breaks: fire takes the elements apart in time. The binding word never stops, and that is why the chair is still a chair.

**From inside.** The familiar goes a little transparent. A quiet sense that everything is being held, and a readiness to see the holding more than the held.

**At the edge.** It touches it: the turning from 'nature' to 'God' is the moment the mind's usual order is reversed (line 259).

**Into the day.** When you pick up something solid today, think: what holds this together is being said now.

## Unity in creation §4: unities revealed near the end of days (lines 275-295)

**The movement.** It lets the reader see his own times as part of the story. The growing sense of oneness in trade and science is read as a sign of what is coming.

> התחיל להיות נרגש גם בעולם ענין האחדות
> *The matter of unity began to be felt in the world too.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the world is made by hiding its Maker, so the unity that reflects Him does not show on its own. As Israel's work adds light to the world, the world becomes able to show that unity (line 276). Length: so near the end of days, 'Havayah will be One and His Name One', and unity starts to be felt (line 277). Trade: once each merchant worked alone; now people see that success comes from joining with others, each giving and receiving like limbs of one body (lines 284-285). Science: once each natural force seemed separate and matter seemed made of many elements. As science grows, the number of elements shrinks, and we see that all things rest on a union of 'quantity and quality', until it will be seen that a thing's true being is the divine power making it (lines 286-294). The way of learning itself now looks for the general rule behind the details (line 292). Depth: a footnote marks a line. Divine wisdom shows the bitul, the self-yielding, in each thing; the wisdom of nature, left to itself, shows its 'somethingness' (line 293). Yet the new way is to look for unity even there (line 302). Bring it home. When you look for one reason behind many events in your week, notice that pull. The book calls it a small echo of the One.

**From inside.** Hope, and a sense of being in the right place in time. The news of the world looks less scattered.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It reads the times; the edge is elsewhere.

**Into the day.** Join with someone today in something you would usually do alone.

## Unity in creation §5: the four species (lines 296-339)

**The movement.** It takes a mitzvah the hands already know, the lulav and etrog, and fills it with oneness. Then it surprises the reader by giving the crown to the plainest of the four.

> ודוקא הערבה, מכיון שאין בה מעלה
> *And particularly the willow, since it has no special quality.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the four species were chosen because each has a nature of unity, and the mitzvah is to draw unity down (line 305). Length: the etrog 'dwells in its tree from year to year'; it takes in all the changes of weather and grows from them, turning them all into one fruit (line 306). The palm's leaves are bound to the spine as one (line 307). The myrtle's three leaves grow from one stem (line 308). The willows grow 'in brotherhood' (line 318). The sages say of each: 'this is the Holy One' (line 320). The unity in them comes from His simple oneness shining there, before we even pick them up (line 319). Depth: the willow has neither taste nor smell. The other three have a quality, and the quality hides the simplicity shining in them. The willow has nothing, so the simplicity shows (line 339). Bring it home. Think of the person you pass over, the one with no obvious gift. Or of your own plain days. The book says the plainness itself can be the window. The picture breaks where plants end: a willow does not choose its plainness. A person can choose not to hide behind his qualities.

**From inside.** Tenderness toward the ordinary, including the ordinary in yourself. A sense that nothing has to be special to be close to Him.

**At the edge.** It points to it: 'simplicity' shining where there is no quality (line 339) is a hint of what is above every quality.

**Into the day.** On a plain day, do one plain thing without needing it to be impressive.

## Unity in creation §6: flax (lines 340-371)

**The movement.** It raises the mind to a oneness with no parts at all, and then brings it right back down. A unity that ignores the details, it warns, can make a person careless.

> וצריך לקשר ולהמשיך בחינה זו גם בפרטים
> *And one must bind and draw this level down into the particulars as well.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: wheat grows as many grains from one seed. Flax grows as one stalk from each seed, 'alone' (lines 340-341). So flax is called 'bad', from 'bedad', alone (line 341), and the High Priest serves on Yom Kippur in linen, because that day is the level of yachid (line 354). Length: the four species are 'echad', many things made one. Flax is 'yachid', one from the start, with no room for another (lines 346-352). Depth: yet echad has its own advantage. Yachid means nothing else exists, but the world then stands apart from His oneness. Echad means even the world's existence is united with Him (lines 356-361). And the warning: Cain's offering was flax. He reached for the undivided One, and so he did not bother to choose the best, since choosing 'best, ordinary, poor' is division (lines 362-370). So the high oneness 'is not enough'; it has to be drawn into the details (line 371). Bring it home. It is easy to feel close to God in a big, general way and then be sloppy with the small things: the blessing said fast, the promise half-kept. The book says the details are where the oneness has to arrive. The flax picture breaks: a plant does not choose between general and particular. We do, every day.

**From inside.** A lift, and then a firm hand on the shoulder. Awe at the 'alone', and care for the small.

**At the edge.** It touches yachid, the One with no room for anything else (line 352), and then refuses to stay there.

**Into the day.** Choose one small detail of a mitzvah today and do it with care, as if the One were in it.

## Unity in creation §7: plurality itself flows from unity (lines 372-390)

**The movement.** It removes the last quarrel between one and many. The reader sees the many as what the One looks like when it spreads without limit.

> שענינה הפנימי (של ההתחלקות גופא) הוא אחדות
> *Its inner content, of the division itself, is unity.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: creatures are countless, without end (line 373). Division is a limit, so how can it be endless? Because it flows from the simple One (lines 373, 377). Length: fire and water, as they are, can never meet. But each contains a little of the other, and through that division they can blend (line 380). Division that leads to blending shows that its inside is unity. Depth: if every creature were the same, one might think the Maker acts by necessity, like a thing that can only do one thing. That no two are alike shows He acts by will, and so the very differences prove He is One (lines 382-390). Bring it home. Look at the faces around you tomorrow, each different. Do not read the difference as distance. Read it as a sign of a free and single will behind them. The picture of fire and water breaks: they blend because each holds a piece of the other. The One does not hold pieces of anything.

**From inside.** Relief. The many no longer feel like a threat to the One, or to your own inner peace.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It reconciles, but stays with what can be understood.

**Into the day.** Notice one difference between you and someone close to you today, and thank Him for it.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Unity in creation §8: the plurality belongs to 'Havayah is One' (lines 391-423)

**The movement.** It takes the Name that seems to make the many, Elokim, and shows it as a channel for the One. The mind watches the One pour itself into many vessels without breaking.

> ואדרבה האחדות דהוי' הוא העושה לבחי' הריבוי דשם אלקים
> *On the contrary, the unity of Havayah is what makes the plurality of the Name Elokim.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the many creatures come through the Name Elokim, a plural word, many powers (line 392). But their very existence comes from Havayah; Elokim only gives each its shape and limit (line 393). Length: the book's picture is a full barrel and pipes (lines 405, 410). To pour water into small cups, you fit narrow pipes. The pipes do not make the water small; the barrel's own water comes through them divided. Depth: so the many is not separate from the One; the One makes the many (line 411). And even after dividing, the many stands in the true unity of Havayah, because the division is endless, and endlessness is the mark of its source (lines 412-423). When the source is the deep, endless springs flow from it (line 422). Bring it home. You receive your life in small cups: this hour, this task, this bit of strength. Think of each cup as filled from one barrel. The barrel picture breaks: a barrel can run dry, and its water is not the barrel. The One is not emptied by pouring, and the water is Him.

**From inside.** A sense of being fed. Small portions stop feeling small.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: the division is endless because the source is truly without end (line 425).

**Into the day.** At one meal today, say a blessing over a small portion as if it came from a full barrel.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Unity in creation §9: unity in holiness (place, year, soul) (lines 424-446)

**The movement.** It shows three ladders of holiness, each with a top rung that is above measure. The reader learns that the highest is found inside the whole, not instead of it.

> מקום ארון אינו מן המדה
> *The place of the Ark is not part of the measure.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: creation has place, time and soul, and in each there is holiness (line 430). In each, three kinds of unity appear: the levels complete each other, all of them together make one composite whole, and at the top a simple oneness shines (lines 429-430). Place: ten degrees of holiness, one above another; the Land includes them all; and in the Holy of Holies the place of the Ark 'is not part of the measure', a place above place, which can only come from the Essence (lines 431, 437). Time: weekdays, festivals, Shabbos, Yom Kippur. Weekday work adds to Shabbos; the year includes all its days; and Yom Kippur is 'one in the year', a oneness apart (lines 438, 442). Soul: Kohanim, Levites and Israelites; the whole people includes all ranks; and the High Priest is 'one', where the simple oneness shines (line 443). Bring it home. Your week has ordinary days and one Shabbos. Your house has ordinary rooms and one corner where you pray. Your people has many kinds of Jews. The top rung needs all the others to be what it is. The Ark picture breaks: the Ark stood in one place. The 'above measure' it points to is everywhere, and only showed there.

**From inside.** Gratitude for the ordinary levels, and awe at the one point that goes beyond them.

**At the edge.** It touches it: a place 'not in the measure' comes only from the Essence (line 437).

**Into the day.** Give one ordinary weekday task today to your coming Shabbos.

## Unity in mankind (Achdus b'Min HaEnoshi): introduction (lines 447-499)

**The movement.** It turns from the world to people, and to the reader himself. It says that being human means being made for oneness.

> פירוש ,אדם" הוא אדמה לעליון
> *The meaning of 'adam' is 'I will be like the One above'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: man has 613 limbs and powers, all different, all one person. And humanity as a whole is like one man (lines 452-455). Length: 'adam' is read as 'I will resemble the One above' (line 465). The ten sefiros above are called 'man' because they blend into each other; so the lower man is made for blending too. Depth: 'adam' also has the number of 'mah', 'what', the word of self-yielding: 'we are what?' (line 476). Because a person can yield himself, people can be more one than other creatures. And two roots: the 'Supernal Man', and the simplicity above even the form of man (lines 488-493). Human unity comes from both and draws both down. The chapter also sets limits: the fullest human unity is tied to Israel's service; the unity of the wicked is real but not like that of those who fear God; and true unity is bound to Torah and mitzvos (lines 497-499). Bring it home. Your hands, eyes and moods are many; you are still one you. Think of the people you know as limbs of that kind of one.

**From inside.** Dignity. The sense that the wish to belong is not weakness but a likeness to the One.

**At the edge.** It points toward it: a simplicity 'above the form of man' (line 488).

**Into the day.** Sit with someone today and let yourself need them a little.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Unity in mankind §1: unity in their very being (lines 500-515)

**The movement.** It shows the reader that he is incomplete on purpose. The lack is how he is bound to others.

> ואין לשום אחד שלימות בלי חבירו
> *And no one has completeness without his fellow.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: every human being in every generation bears the seal of Adam, and no two are alike (line 501). The seal is the shared point that unites them all (line 505). Length: each person is like one limb of the great body of mankind. In some quality each one is the 'head' that others must receive from (line 506). Depth: God even built it into the land. One country grows spices, another grain, so every land must give and receive (line 514). The earth itself has an 'eye' and a 'navel' and a 'mouth', like a body (line 515). Bring it home. Name one thing you cannot do that someone near you does well. Then name one thing you give them. The gap is not a flaw in the design. It is the design. The body picture breaks: a limb has no choice about serving the body. People can refuse, and the next section shows what that costs.

**From inside.** Humility without shame. Warmth toward the people you depend on.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there.

**Into the day.** Ask someone for help today with something you could have muddled through alone.

## Unity in mankind §2: the will to unite (lines 516-569)

**The movement.** It finds a hidden self-yielding inside ordinary cooperation. Then it shows Sodom as the ruin that comes from refusing it.

> שכל אחד מניח ומבטל את עצמו לגבי זולתו
> *That each one sets himself aside and yields himself to the other.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: a person wants by nature to join with others, and the wish comes from bitul, self-yielding (line 525). Length: even joining for profit, in business, is a small yielding, because I admit I need you (lines 530-538). To understand an idea truly I must listen to someone else's reasoning, especially when he disagrees (lines 539, 547). When many people with different minds agree on one thing, each has set himself aside (line 548). And friends who help each other are one (line 549). Depth: the wish goes so deep that it touches a person's existence. Sodom said, 'what is mine is mine and what is yours is yours'. Each wanted to be alone, neither giving nor taking. That is the way of Tohu, broken worlds, and so it was overturned (lines 566-569). Bring it home. The next time you have to hear an opinion you dislike, notice the small death in listening. The book calls it a movement of unity. The business picture breaks: in trade, each still wants his own gain. The yielding hides under the self-interest; it does not replace it.

**From inside.** A little less armor. Listening starts to feel like a gift you give, not a loss.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It stays with what people already do.

**Into the day.** In one conversation today, let the other person finish before you answer.

## Unity in mankind §3: the tendency to see the one point (lines 570-583)

**The movement.** It shows the mind its own habit of looking for one reason behind many things. It names that habit as a trace of the One in the intellect.

> שיש ענין אחד שהוא הטעם והסיבה לכל דברים אלה
> *That there is one thing that is the reason and cause for all these things.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: when several things can be explained one by one, or by one reason for all, the human mind leans to the one reason (line 571). Logic does not force this; it is laid into us (line 576). Length: the reason is that one of man's defining marks is unity (line 577). This leaning is the basis of natural science: if something happens again and again, we assume it is its nature, not a new cause each time (line 582). Depth: the leaning is strongest in a Jew, who by nature is bound to His oneness and cannot bear to be cut off from Godliness (lines 578-579). Bring it home. When your day feels like a hundred unrelated problems, try asking what one thing runs through them. The question itself turns you toward the One. The science picture breaks: a law of nature is still a thing in the world. The one point the soul is after is not a rule; it is Him.

**From inside.** Order in the mind. A gathering of scattered thoughts into one line.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there.

**Into the day.** At the end of today, find the one thread that ran through what happened.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Unity in mankind §4: its cause is bitul (lines 584-680)

**The movement.** It tells the reader plainly why he cannot get along with someone: there is too much of him. Then it shows two kinds of room: a wide mind, and a self that is simply not there to be hurt.

> דמי שהוא בישות וגאוה אינו יכול לסבול את זולתו
> *One who is full of himself and proud cannot bear his fellow.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the cause of unity is bitul. A proud person cannot bear another, let alone join him; when he yields, he makes room, and then he can be one with him (line 585). Holiness is unity and the 'other side' is separation, because the other side is selfhood (line 586). Length: two great kings or ministers cannot unite, because each one's very existence shrinks the other's; two poor men, two refugees, even opposites, love each other easily (lines 607-614). Even people who truly love each other will sometimes snap (line 615). Then two cures. A wide mind, mochin d'gadlus: in a wide place two opposites can live, so a broad mind can hold a view against it without anger. Hillel was not provoked (lines 624-626). And higher, the bitul of 'mah': here the one who hurt me is 'not opposed to me at all', because there is no self left to be opposed (line 636). Depth: in the soul, a wide mind makes the traits 'soft as a reed', bendable; kindness and severity make room for each other. But when the Ein Sof light above the traits shines, they cancel in their very being and become one, a 'complete place' where He rests (lines 655-676). Bring it home. Think of the person who irritates you most. First picture a wider room in your mind, where he fits. Then go further: picture that what he hits in you is not really there. The kings picture breaks: kings are big in fact. The self that makes room is small only in its own eyes, and that smallness is its greatness.

**From inside.** The heat of irritation cools. First patience, then something quieter than patience: a place in you that the insult does not reach.

**At the edge.** It touches it: in the bitul of 'mah' opposites are 'not opposites at all' (line 636), and the Ein Sof dwells there (line 676).

**Into the day.** When someone provokes you today, say nothing for one moment and remember: there is less of me than I think.

## Unity in mankind §5: its root is simplicity (lines 681-701)

**The movement.** It sends the reader down under his thoughts and feelings to the point where all Jews are simply one. It locates true unity below agreement.

> הוא דוקא ע"י גילוי בחינת היחידה
> *It comes only through the revealing of the level of yechidah.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: unity from a wide mind only makes room; true unity, 'as one man', comes from the simplicity above the form of man (line 682). Length: above, that simplicity is Godliness entirely above worlds, the Name Havayah and especially the Name 'mah' (lines 683-684). In the soul, it is the soul's essence above its separate powers (line 692). Depth: why? Because even at the highest power, wisdom, 'their minds are not alike'. So true unity needs something above mind. And fully true unity, one man in fact, needs the yechidah, the single point of the soul (lines 693, 701). Bring it home. You and a fellow Jew may disagree about almost everything. Under the arguments there is a point in each of you that is not an opinion at all. Try to picture meeting him there. There is no mashal here, and none would hold. The book says outright that the mind cannot get there by agreeing.

**From inside.** A quiet under the noise. A kinship that does not wait for agreement.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: the yechidah, the soul's single point, is where true unity lives (line 701).

**Into the day.** Think of someone you argue with, and wish him well from the place below the argument.

## Unity in mankind §§6-7: unity draws down the Supernal Man and the simple light (lines 702-736)

**The movement.** It shows that loving another person is not only good manners but an act that pulls blessing from above. The reader's small kindnesses become large.

> וכשהוא אוהב את רעהו הוא אוהב דמות אלקים
> *And when he loves his fellow, he loves the image of God.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: human unity comes from above, and it also draws down from above (line 702). Length: one reason: man is made in the image of God. When you despise your fellow you despise that image; when you love him you love it, and the heart of the Man above turns toward the man below (line 703). 'As water shows face to face, so is the heart of man to man' (line 713). Another reason: each soul has a root above; when souls unite below, their roots unite above (line 704). And measure for measure: unity below draws the unity above (line 711). Depth: it draws even the simple light above all order, because that light is itself oneness, and the Holy One 'rests only in a whole place' (lines 718, 730). Bring it home. The next time you smile at a stranger, think that you are greeting the image of God in him, and that above, a face is turning toward you. The water picture breaks: a reflection in water only returns what you showed. Here what returns is more than you gave.

**From inside.** Kindness gets weight. It feels less like a favor and more like prayer.

**At the edge.** It points there: the light that comes is 'above the chain of worlds' (line 718).

**Into the day.** Do one act of love for someone today with the thought: this is His image.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Unity in mankind §8: its worth, a vessel for Godliness (lines 737-756)

**The movement.** It turns togetherness into a place for God to live. The reader sees that a quarrel pushes the Presence out of the room.

> אחדות היא כלי להשראת אלקות
> *Unity is a vessel for the resting of Godliness.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: because He is One, the vessel for Him is oneness (line 738). When Israel are 'many', each saying 'mine is mine', the One does not rest in them; when they are one, He does. The Zohar calls it 'to be one in One' (line 738). Length: this is mainly about the Name Havayah, of whom it says 'Havayah is One' (line 739). Midian, from 'strife', is the shell that fights the whole Name, not just one letter of it (lines 740, 745). Depth: Scripture says 'Havayah echad', not 'yachid', on purpose. The news of the Shema is that the seven heavens, the earth and the four directions, many and opposed, are nullified into One (line 749). So people who do not think alike and still join are the vessel for 'Havayah is One' (line 750). Bring it home. Picture your table on a good night, when everyone is at peace. Then on a night of fighting. The book says the first is a vessel and the second is a crack. The vessel picture breaks: a cup holds water that is outside it. Here the unity itself is a likeness of what fills it.

**From inside.** A wish to mend. Peace starts to feel holy, not just pleasant.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: 'one in One', the lower unity holding the higher (line 738).

**Into the day.** Make peace in one small quarrel today, even if you were right.

## Unity in mankind §9: the power of unity, and the unity of the wicked (lines 757-839)

**The movement.** It shows the frightening strength of unity even among the wicked, and then its limit. The reader learns both to respect unity and to ask what it serves.

> כי הטבע דבחינת אחדות העליונה היא לשכון במקום האחדות
> *The nature of the higher unity is to dwell in the place of unity.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: unity and peace draw all blessing; division pushes it away, even among people who keep the Torah (lines 751, 760). The generation of Saul and David, learned and God-fearing, had informers and fell in battle; Ahab's generation served idols, but had no informers, and won (lines 761, 769). Length: the builders of the Tower were one people with one language, and their unity 'forced' blessing on them until their speech had to be confused (line 770). The higher unity's nature is to dwell where there is unity, even very far down (line 818). Depth: but there are limits. The wicked draw only from the outside of His oneness, and only for a time (lines 800-806). And if there is no yielding to God at all, unity does nothing; Ahab's men trusted only in God in battle, while the Tower's builders, wholly cut off, only thought they would succeed (lines 825-839). Bring it home. You have seen groups held together by a shared enemy. The book says their bond is real and strong. Then it asks what your own circle is bound by.

**From inside.** Sober respect. A wish that the bonds you are part of be turned toward Him.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It stays with the workings of blessing.

**Into the day.** Ask yourself what holds one group you belong to together, and add one thing to it that is for Him.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Unity in mankind §10: among the nations, only outward; 'as one man with one heart' (lines 840-902)

**The movement.** It uses two small word orders in Rashi to show two kinds of togetherness. The reader finds that at Sinai the Jews were one before they felt anything.

> האיר אצלם נקודת היהדות, בחינת היחידה
> *The point of Jewishness, the level of yechidah, shone in them.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the nations too have unity, for choice's sake and through the outer side of 'mah', mostly in matters of settling the world (lines 844-869). Deep unity 'as one man' comes from Godliness entirely above worlds and from the yechidah (lines 877-888). Length: of Egypt chasing Israel, Rashi says 'with one heart, as one man'. Of Israel at Sinai he says 'as one man, with one heart' (line 890). Depth: 'one heart, as one man' means separate people made one by a shared feeling, here hatred (lines 895-896). 'As one man, one heart' means they were one in their very being, and so they also had one heart for receiving the Torah (lines 897-902). At Sinai the point of Jewishness, the yechidah, shone (line 898). Bring it home. Think of a crowd moved by a single feeling at a game or a protest. Then think of a family at a funeral who are one without needing to feel the same thing. The second is the Sinai kind.

**From inside.** A deeper belonging than feeling. The sense that you are one with your people even on days you feel nothing.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: oneness from the yechidah, the soul's single point (line 898).

**Into the day.** Do something today for a Jew you do not know, simply because you are one.

## Unity in mankind §11: true unity is bound to Torah and mitzvos (lines 903-915)

**The movement.** It gives practical advice for any partnership. It shows the mind why unions break, and how to make one last.

> ,אגודה אחת" אפשרית רק כאשר מטרת האגודה היא ,לעשות רצונך"
> *'One band' is possible only when the aim of the band is 'to do Your will'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the closer things are to Godliness, the more one they can be (line 903). 'Let them all form one band to do Your will' (line 904). Length: a union for any other aim joins only the powers that aim needs, and even there each member wants it for his own reasons (lines 905-909). So when interests clash, it falls apart, as seen many times (lines 910-911). Depth: the advice is to set a mitzvah at the base of any union, even one for ordinary things; then it will last (line 912). Bring it home. Think of a friendship or business you care about. What would it mean to put one mitzvah at its center: giving together, learning together, helping someone together?

**From inside.** Clarity about what lasts. A calm about partnerships that used to feel fragile.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It is practical.

**Into the day.** Add one shared good deed to a relationship you want to keep.

## Unity through keeping the boundaries (Achdus al yedei Shmiras haGevulos): introduction (lines 916-940)

**The movement.** It hands the reader a cooking pot. Things that would destroy each other can help each other, if there is a wall between them.

> הוא דוקא כשהחיבור שלהם הוא ע"י מחיצה (הקדירה) המפסקת ביניהם
> *It is precisely when their joining is through a partition, the pot, that divides between them.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: unity does not come to cancel the distinctions God set between one thing and another (line 919). Breaking the walls between things confuses the order of creation, and is strife, not peace; Korach's 'all are holy' is the proof (lines 919-925). Length: the two go together. Keeping the boundaries is what lets separate things unite (line 926). The picture: fire and water can cook a meal only when the pot stands between them. Without the pot, the joining helps nothing; one simply wipes out the other (line 927). Depth: the rule holds for the unity that belongs to the world, even for the joining that comes from the simple light. Only the unity above any connection to the world is above boundaries, and that will be drawn down in the future (line 935). Bring it home. In a marriage, in a friendship, between parents and children, there are lines: roles, privacy, respect. Look at one of those lines as a pot that lets two fires cook together. The pot picture breaks: a pot is dead metal. The boundary God sets is itself part of His will, and so it is part of the unity.

**From inside.** Relief from the pressure to merge. Closeness that can breathe.

**At the edge.** It names it and closes the door for now: a unity above all boundaries belongs to the future (line 935).

**Into the day.** Keep one boundary today that you usually let slip, for the sake of peace.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Boundaries §1: even for a mitzvah (no more than a fifth) (lines 941-947)

**The movement.** It surprises a generous reader. Even giving can confuse the lines, if you give what was meant for you.

> גבולות חלק הקב"ה בעולמו" הוא גם בנוגע נכסיו וממונו של האדם
> *'Boundaries the Holy One set in His world' apply also to a person's property and money.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: a person should not dedicate all his property, and one who spends freely on mitzvos should not spend more than a fifth (line 942). Length: why, if it is a mitzvah? Because God divided a person's money too. One part is a deposit held for the poor; one part is given for his own needs (line 944). Giving away your own part mixes what He separated. Depth: that is why the Rambam puts this law just before the laws of kilayim, forbidden mixtures: both are about not confusing the order of creation (line 945). Bring it home. Look at your own week. Some of your time and strength is a deposit for others; some is for your own life and family. Honoring the line is also service.

**From inside.** A calm kind of generosity, without guilt and without showing off.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there.

**Into the day.** Set aside a fixed part of what you earn this week for charity, and keep the rest for your needs without apology.

## Boundaries §2: joining in holiness does not cancel existence (lines 948-972)

**The movement.** It shows opposites living together without either being lost. The reader sees love and awe of God held in one heart.

> מיכאל שר של מים וגבריאל שר של אש ואינם מכבים זה את זה
> *Michael, the minister of water, and Gavriel, the minister of fire, do not extinguish each other.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: joining two different things is a problem when each is a 'something' for itself; then one must lose its shape and cannot do its job (lines 949-950). In holiness, which is bitul to God, joining opposites is 'the most wonderful quality' (line 949). Length: why? Because in holiness each feels that its real content is the Holy One's mission; both have the same content, so each helps the other (lines 951-957). 'He makes peace in His heights': Michael is water and Gavriel is fire, and they do not put each other out; each angel is half fire and half snow (line 959). Depth: in a person, love and fear are opposites. In worldly matters they cannot live together. In the service of God they can, because what is felt in them is not the feeling itself but that both are His service (lines 967, 972). Bring it home. You may fear God and love Him in the same prayer, or be firm and kind with a child in the same hour. Let both be for Him, and they stop fighting. The angels picture breaks: angels do not choose their mission. We choose, each time, whether our opposites serve us or Him.

**From inside.** Inner peace between feelings that used to pull against each other.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It stays with opposites that keep their shape.

**Into the day.** Before one hard conversation today, say to yourself: my firmness and my softness both serve Him.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Boundaries §3: the boundaries remain even when existence is set aside (lines 973-1001)

**The movement.** It shows a deeper joining, where the parts forget themselves before the King. Even there, it says, the lines are not erased; they are only not felt.

> וכל מה שנרגש הוא רק מציאות המלך
> *And all that is felt is only the existence of the King.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: opposites in holiness join in two ways. Each can feel it is on His mission, which is true unity but not complete, because one mission is kindness and the other severity (lines 982-984). Length: or a light above both can shine in them, the revealing of the Essence, simple in every way. Then they become like one thing (lines 985-986). The picture: ministers standing before the king. At that moment nothing is felt but the king, so they stand as one, with no division at all (lines 987-991). Depth: even so, the boundary is still there. They are one only because the light shines in them; in themselves they are still two opposites, now simply not felt (lines 996-1001). Bring it home. At a moment of real prayer, your worries and your plans go quiet. They have not gone away. They are standing before the King.

**From inside.** Stillness before Someone. A self that is present but not in the way.

**At the edge.** It touches it: the revealing of the Essence, 'simple in every way', makes opposites one (line 985).

**Into the day.** In one blessing today, stand still for a second as before a King.

## Boundaries §4: kilayim in the priestly garments (lines 1002-1025)

**The movement.** It uses a precise halachah to teach a precise inner truth. Even in the holiest service, a mixture is permitted, not undone.

> כשמכין את עצמו לעמוד לפני המלך
> *When he prepares himself to stand before the King.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: wool is kindness and linen severity; mixing them is forbidden (line 1009). In the priestly garments the Rambam permits it, only during the service, and only in the belt (lines 1003-1006). Length: during the service, the priests stood in bitul before the King, and the great revealing of Godliness let the opposites join (line 1012). The belt, unlike the other garments, stands for the general readiness of the whole person to stand before the King: 'prepare to meet your God, Israel' (lines 1013, 1018). Depth: yet the Rambam calls it a 'permission', not an absence of the prohibition. The wool and linen are still two opposites; they are one only because the simple light shines (lines 1020-1025). Bring it home. Before you pray, there is a moment of putting on your 'belt', of gathering yourself to stand before Him. In that moment many things in you can come together that do not usually fit.

**From inside.** Readiness. The feeling of tightening a belt before an important meeting.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: the simple light of the Essence lets opposites be one, for the time it shines (line 1009).

**Into the day.** Before one prayer today, pause and gather yourself, as if tying a belt.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Boundaries §5: Korach's error (lines 1026-1049)

**The movement.** It gives Korach credit, and then shows where he went wrong. The reader is warned against seeing the future truly and living it too early.

> קרח שפיקח הי' מה ראה לשטות זה
> *Korach, who was clever, what did he see to do this foolishness?*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: unity has two levels: as it relates to the world, and as it is above the world (line 1027). Length: the world was made with ten sayings, divided and recombined, so things in it are many. The unity that comes into it is the many made one, like limbs in one body whose wholeness needs all its different limbs (lines 1028-1035). Above, there is a simple oneness with no division, not even a joined division (line 1036). Korach wanted all Israel equal, with that simple oneness (line 1042). Depth: he was clever, because a clever man sees what is hidden inside a thing that will be revealed later (lines 1044, 1047). He was foolish, because that simple oneness will be drawn down only in the future; now, in the time 'to do them', unity must keep the boundaries (line 1043). Bring it home. You may sense a truth about how things should be. The book asks you to hold it and also to live in the day you are in.

**From inside.** Patience with the gap between vision and now. A clever mind learning to wait.

**At the edge.** It names it: a simple unity above the world, true and still hidden (line 1036).

**Into the day.** Take one ideal you hold and do the next small step today, not the whole thing.

## Introductory chapters to Achdus Hashem, §§1-3: one God, no partner, providence, livelihood (lines 1050-1117)

**The movement.** It takes the Shema's first meaning, one God, and brings it down to the question of where your paycheck comes from. It moves faith from the heavens into the wallet.

> שהשפע שהוא משפיע תהי' ע"י לבושי הטבע
> *That the flow He sends should come through the garments of nature.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: 'one' means there is no other god; that He is not made of parts; and, as Chassidus reveals, that He is the only existent (lines 1050-1071). Length: even a 'god' He created and gave power to would deny His oneness. Thinking a created power works alone is idolatry; thinking it helps God is 'shituf', partnership (lines 1069-1070, 1092). The same holds for the stars and for the laws of nature. Holding that the world runs by itself, with God stepping in now and then, is idolatry, not just partnership. Believing His oneness means He watches over every creature and every detail (lines 1100-1108). Depth: livelihood. 'God will bless you in all that you do'. The blessing is His; your work is only its garment (line 1109). Thinking your work is the source makes it your 'god' (lines 1115-1116). The true belief: He gives, and chose to give through the garments of nature (line 1117). 'We will no longer call the work of our hands our god' (line 1114). Bring it home. Think of your job, your business, your plans for money. Picture them as a coat that His blessing wears to reach you. The garment picture breaks: a coat can be taken off. The blessing always comes dressed, and yet the dress adds nothing.

**From inside.** Less anxiety about money. Diligence without the fear that it all depends on you.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It sets the plain meaning in order before the deeper one.

**Into the day.** Before you start work today, say: the blessing is His; this is the garment.

## Introductory chapters to Achdus Hashem, §§4-7: the Chassidic reading, revealed in stages; above and below; the 'I' made nothing (lines 1118-1151)

**The movement.** It moves from 'no one else rules' to 'nothing else exists'. Then it asks the reader what that does to the word 'I'.

> ויעשה מה;אני" ,אין"
> *And he makes the 'I' into 'nothing'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: as revealed in the inner Torah, 'Havayah is One' means not only that no one else has power, but that there is no existence at all besides His (line 1118). 'There is none else' means not even anything secondary (lines 1125-1126). Length: 'I, God, have not changed': creating the worlds changed nothing in His oneness; He is alone now as before creation (lines 1128-1133). This reading was revealed late, and earlier generations lacked nothing without it. Now that it is revealed, one who knows it and rejects it has 'a subtle of subtle' flaw, and everyone must learn it (lines 1134-1139). Deeper ways keep being revealed in every generation (line 1127). Two ways it is said: from below, all is Godliness; from above, Godliness is all. As in the two versions of the Mishnah: 'I was created to serve my Maker', and 'I was created only to serve my Maker' (lines 1141-1142). Evil too: from below it is only an absence; from above its spark rises into holiness (line 1146). Depth: the result in service is to make the 'I' into 'nothing' (line 1150). The Tzemach Tzedek adds, in Yiddish, 'there is no I' (line 1153). Bring it home. Say the word 'I' slowly, the way you say it all day: I want, I need, I did. Then say 'I was created only to serve', and feel the 'only'.

**From inside.** A lightness in the chest, as the heavy 'I' becomes a little hollow. Not emptiness, but space.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: 'there is no existence at all besides His' (line 1118), taken into the self.

**Into the day.** Today, when you catch yourself saying 'I', add silently: for You.

## Introductory chapters to Achdus Hashem, §§8-10: created and emanated; not an illusion; the levels; Va'ed, Echad, Yachid (lines 1152-1203)

**The movement.** It lays out a map of how 'nothing besides Him' can be true while the world is real. At the top of the map, the highest world and the lowest are the same.

> באופן שאצילות ועשי' הם בשוה ממש
> *In such a way that Atzilus and Asiyah are exactly equal.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: created things do not contradict His oneness because they are nullified before Him; emanated things (the sefiros) do not, because they are united with Him. 'Heaven forbid to say He and creation are one' (lines 1154-1157). So the Tanya uses two pictures: light inside the sun's body, and light one with its luminary (lines 1158-1164). Length: one might say the world is only a dream. But then the mitzvos too would be a dream; so the world is real (line 1167). It is not a 'true' existence, since it can stop (line 1170). It is not an existence for itself, since He makes it every moment (lines 1172-1173). It has no worth before Him, 'all before Him is as nothing' (line 1180). Depth: the truest 'no relation' is before the Ein Sof that came before the contraction, where Atzilus, the highest world, and Asiyah, ours, are exactly equal (line 1188). And creation is itself a lowering: He 'lowered Himself' to be great enough to create; before Him as He is, there is no existence at all (lines 1194-1195). Va'ed, Echad, Yachid are the three rungs (lines 1197-1202). Bring it home. Think of the highest moment you have had in prayer and your most ordinary chore. Before Him, the book says, they are exactly equal. Not because the prayer is small, but because both are nothing before Him and both are His. The sun pictures break: the sun's light comes from a body. He has no 'body' that light leaves.

**From inside.** A leveling that is also a lifting. The chore gets holier, and the high moment loses its pride.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: before the Ein Sof before the contraction, high and low are equal (line 1188); before the Essence, nothing exists (line 1195).

**Into the day.** Do one plain chore today with the same care you would give to prayer.

## Introductory chapters to Achdus Hashem, §11 and the list of entries (lines 1204-1213)

**The movement.** It turns the whole map around. The highest unity, it says, is not the world disappearing but the world, as it is, being one with Him.

> בזה שגם המציאות דהעולם מיוחד עמו ית'
> *In that the world's existence too is united with Him.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: because His oneness has no limits, its truest expression is not denying the world, but the world's existence being united with Him (line 1205). Length: a unity that works by denying others is limited, since existence is the opposite of oneness. And it gets its oneness from the state of the other, so the other is mixed into it (line 1205). A unity in which the world's existence is itself united comes only from His unlimited oneness (line 1205). Depth: another reason: the world was made for Torah. Every other explanation still leaves the world felt, very faintly. Here the world's true existence is 'not world, but Torah, Godliness' (lines 1207-1208). And even nature shows the unity, like a vessel that shows its craftsman (line 1211). Bring it home. You do not have to leave your life to be one with Him. Your very life, your body, your street, can be where the oneness is.

**From inside.** Homecoming. The world stops being an obstacle.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: a unity 'without limit' in which even existence is united (line 1205).

**Into the day.** Take one ordinary part of your life and say: this, as it is, can be one with Him.

## Achdus Hashem (1), idolatry and partnership: introduction (lines 1214-1254)

**The movement.** It unpacks the Shema's command into three layers. It makes the reader see that even a helper beside God is too much.

> מי הי' שותף עמי בברייתו של עולם
> *Who was a partner with Me in the creation of the world?*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the Shema's oneness holds three things: there is no other god; He has no partner; and, as Chassidus reveals, there is no existence besides Him (lines 1229-1241). Length: when God said 'you shall have no other gods', Israel answered 'God is One' (line 1238). The Shema also rules out partnership, which 'no other gods' does not (line 1239). Depth: why were the angels not made on the first day? So no one would say Michael stretched out the south of the sky and Gavriel the north. 'Who was a partner with Me?' (line 1267). Even a helper, who is not a god, would be a partner. Only Israel was commanded against partnership, and only from Sinai, because only then was this level of His oneness revealed in them (line 1249). Bring it home. When things go well, notice the quick thought: 'I helped God a little here'. The book is not against your effort. It is against thinking He needed it.

**From inside.** A clean humility. Credit given back.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It sharpens the plain belief.

**Into the day.** When something succeeds today, say: You did this alone.

## Achdus Hashem (1) §1: idolatry and partnership in daily life (nature, livelihood, harm, anger, honor, pride) (lines 1255-1358)

**The movement.** It walks the reader through a day and finds small idolatries in it: in money, in anger, in pride. It is the most practical passage in the volume.

> שבעת כעסו נסתלקה ממנו האמונה
> *At the time of his anger, the faith left him.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: thinking the stars or the laws of nature run the world, even if God sometimes overrules them, is idolatry, not just partnership (lines 1277-1285). The same with livelihood: work is only the garment of His blessing (line 1294). Pouring your mind into schemes, thinking cleverness brings the money, is like 'bowing' your head to the stars (line 1296). Length: harm too. If someone hurts you, it was decreed above; he is only the messenger, since his free choice is about himself, not about you (line 1312). So 'whoever is angry is as if he served idols': at the moment of anger, the faith that 'this came from God' left him (line 1313). The intermediaries have no choice at all: 'an axe in the hand of the woodcutter' (line 1331), like the earth that must grow what 'let the earth sprout' tells it (line 1332). So they deserve no honor; the sign is that yes and no are the same to you (line 1340). Work is not even a 'vessel', only an empty space, like Elisha's empty jars for the miracle oil (lines 1342, 1352). Depth: idolatry at root is giving importance to anything besides Godliness (line 1349). So pride equals idolatry (line 1351), and anger, pride's branch, is idolatry too (line 1358). Bring it home. Remember the last time you were angry at someone. Watch, slowly, the moment the thought 'he did this to me' pushed out 'this came from Him'. The axe picture breaks: an axe is lifeless. The person who hurt you has a choice, and will answer for it; but not to you, and not about you.

**From inside.** Anger loosens its grip. Money worries go quieter. A strange freedom from other people's power over you.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It brings the unity into the hardest ordinary moments.

**Into the day.** The next time you feel anger rising today, say before anything else: this, too, is from Him.

## Achdus Hashem (1) §1, continued: Israel and the nations; seeing and hearing (lines 1359-1421)

**The movement.** It explains why a Jew's faith can feel like sight rather than argument. The One is not proved to the soul; it shines in it.

> תוצאה מזה שהשם אחד מאיר בנפשם
> *A result of the One Name shining in their soul.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the nations were commanded against idolatry but not against partnership (line 1359). Their root is in a level that makes room for something beside Him; Israel's root is in the surrounding light, sovev, which makes no room for anything that is not Godliness (lines 1360, 1372). Length: Israel's root is the inner will, where nothing else has room, so they know the intermediaries are only an axe (line 1380). Depth: a Jew's faith is a result of the One Name shining in his soul, not of his reasoning (line 1381). The nations' faith rests on their mind (line 1382). That is the difference between hearing and seeing: what you hear, you hold as your mind pictures it; what you see, you hold as it shows itself (lines 1390-1391). At Sinai, 'you were shown that there is none besides Him' (line 1397). For Israel the Name Elokim does not really hide Havayah; it brings Havayah's light all the way down (line 1405). So nature is only an outer garment over what is above nature (line 1406). Bring it home. On days when you cannot prove anything, notice that the faith is still there. The book says that is not a weakness. It is sight.

**From inside.** Confidence that does not need proof. A sense of standing inside something rather than arguing for it.

**At the edge.** It touches it: faith as the One Name shining in the soul, beyond reasoning (line 1381).

**Into the day.** Say the Shema once today without trying to prove anything, only looking.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Achdus Hashem (1) §2: Chassidus deepens the plain meaning (lines 1422-1452)

**The movement.** It takes the plain meaning of idolatry and cuts it finer and finer, until even a holy task done at the wrong time is called 'foreign service'. The reader's own spiritual ambitions come under the light.

> משמעות הלשון ,עבודה זרה" הוא - עבודה שהיא זרה
> *The meaning of 'avodah zarah' is service that is foreign.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the plain faith rules out not only the heavenly hosts but the laws of nature as rulers (line 1423). And not only lowly intermediaries: the higher the level, even in Atzilus and above, the more one must deny that it deserves honor (line 1427). Length: Yisro said 'now I know that God is greater than all gods'. He had known every idol in the world, and only after knowing every level could he see how great God is. At first he saw the revealed levels as things in themselves; then he knew the contraction hides nothing, and all is one with Him as before (lines 1428-1435). Some say God made another 'god' to run the world, to spare Him lowering Himself. They think they honor Him; they deny Him (lines 1437-1443). Depth: 'avodah zarah' literally means foreign service: even a holy service, if it is not the one required of you now, is foreign to you (line 1444). Aiming at what is really wanted of you now cannot come through reasoning; it comes only through acknowledgment and accepting the yoke. That, says the book, is why 'whoever denies idolatry is called a Jew', a 'Yehudi', one who acknowledges (line 1452). Bring it home. Maybe today you would rather learn than help at home, or pray long when someone is waiting for you. The book asks: what is wanted of me now?

**From inside.** Honesty about your own motives. A readiness to set aside even a good thing for the right thing.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It sharpens.

**Into the day.** Once today, ask before a good deed: is this what He wants of me now?

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Achdus Hashem (1) §3: no existence besides Him; the result in service (lines 1453-1467)

**The movement.** It brings the whole teaching to the person and his things. He feels himself not an 'I' but a 'nothing', and the world around him as existing only for His glory.

> מרגיש האדם אשר הוא (האדם) לא ,אני" אלא ,אין"
> *The person feels that he is not an 'I' but a 'nothing'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: as revealed in the inner Torah, there is no existence besides His, as 'there is none else besides Him' plainly says (line 1453). This belongs to the mitzvah of His oneness, and now that it is revealed, everyone must learn it (lines 1455-1460). Length: the result is negating the 'I' (line 1461). Depth: not only does the person feel he is 'nothing', but his relation to things outside him changes: in themselves they take up no room; their whole point is His glory (line 1466). There are many levels of this, as many as the levels of the unity itself (line 1475). Bring it home. Look at the things you own: your phone, your coat, your books. For a moment see them not as 'mine' but as places where His glory can show.

**From inside.** Things lose their grip. The self goes quieter. What is left feels like service.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: no existence at all besides Him, lived as 'not I but nothing' (line 1466).

**Into the day.** Use one thing you own today for someone else's good.

## Achdus Hashem (2): 'there is none like Him' (lines 1468-1481)

**The movement.** It asks why anyone would need to be told there is none like Him. The answers carry the mind from comparison to paradox.

> שהקב"ה הוא ,נמנע הנמנעות"
> *That the Holy One is 'the impossibility of impossibilities'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the Midrash says 'God is One' means 'there is none like Him'. That seems obvious (line 1471). Three readings follow. Length: one, He is beyond all comparison; high and low are equal before Him (line 1469). Two, He is 'the impossibility of impossibilities': nothing is impossible for Him, so He can join two opposites, limit and no limit, place and above place, time and above time (lines 1470, 1476). Depth: three, He is One even from the worlds' side: after they were made they are nullified and united as before (line 1477). This is itself a joining of opposites (line 1478). And perhaps 'none like Him' means there is no oneness like His, in which even existence is united with Him (line 1480). Bring it home. Think of a pair of things in your life that cannot both be true: you are small, and you matter. The book says that holding both is His signature.

**From inside.** Wonder, and a little dizziness. The mind bends and does not break.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: He joins opposites, 'the impossibility of impossibilities' (line 1470).

**Into the day.** Hold one pair of opposites about yourself today, without choosing between them.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Achdus Hashem (3), 'no existence besides Him': introduction and §1, the world is not an illusion (lines 1482-1551)

**The movement.** It protects the reader from the easy mistake that 'nothing besides Him' means the world is fake. It makes the world's solidity itself a sign of His power.

> עצם מציאותו של הנברא מתהווה ממדת גדולתו של הקב"ה
> *The creature's very existence comes from the Holy One's attribute of greatness.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: four proofs that the world is not a dream (lines 1519-1530). 'In the beginning God created'. The mitzvos, whose point is to act on the world. The dwelling below, which would be only a dream if the world were. And sorcery: the one who really does magic is liable, the one who only fools the eye is not. Length: the lowest world, full of what is 'against God', is not only real but strong, because only the 'impossibility of impossibilities' could make something that exists by Him and opposes Him at once (line 1529). Depth: each creature has two sides. Its bare existence comes from His greatness, the power to create from nothing. Its feeling of being a thing on its own comes from His strength, the contraction and the hiding (line 1537). And even that feeling of self is 'according to the truth' (line 1514), because the vessels shape how the light acts. Only before the light itself does it hide nothing (lines 1544-1546). Bring it home. The table is hard. The book does not ask you to pretend it is soft. It asks you to see its hardness as a work of His might. The magic picture breaks: magic borrows from outside powers. The world's reality is lent by Him and is, in that way, truly its own.

**From inside.** Firm ground. The relief of not having to deny what you see in order to believe.

**At the edge.** It points there: before the light itself, the concealment hides nothing (line 1546).

**Into the day.** Touch something solid today and thank Him for making it real.

## Achdus Hashem (3) §2: not a true existence (lines 1552-1597)

**The movement.** It gives the reader a precise word for the world: not false, but 'not true', the way a spring that stops once in seven years is called a 'lying' spring. Then it climbs to the Rambam's highest knowledge.

> מים הפוסקין פעם אחת לשבע שנגים שנקראים בשם ,מים המכזבין"
> *Waters that stop once in seven years are called 'lying waters' (the scan reads 'shnagim' for 'shanim').*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the Rambam says 'there is none else besides Him' means 'there is no true existent besides Him' (line 1552). 'True' means what never stops; whatever can stop is called a 'lie' even while it lasts (line 1553). The picture: a spring that stops once in seven years is called 'lying waters' and is unfit for the red heifer's water, even on the days it flows (line 1553). Length: the world can stop in three ways: if the creating power left it; if its root were revealed in it; if the Ein Sof as it is were revealed (line 1560). A rooster below has its root in the angel Gavriel, and higher in Atzilus; if it felt its root, it would vanish (lines 1569, 1575). Depth: the Rambam writes, 'if it entered the mind that He does not exist'. Read with the Rebbe: after knowing He is the 'First Existent', one must rise to a higher knowing, that as He is in Himself He is not 'existent' at all, beyond the category of existence, and from there nothing else can exist (lines 1590-1597). Like a great sage who is still his whole self while talking to a small child (line 1603). Bring it home. Think of something in your life that feels permanent: your house, your health, your plans. Hold it gently, as a flowing spring that is real and not 'true'.

**From inside.** A loosened grip, without fear. Gratitude for what flows today.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: He as He is is beyond 'existing', and from there nothing else could be (line 1597).

**Into the day.** Thank Him today for one thing you usually treat as permanent.

## Achdus Hashem (3) §3: not an existence for itself (continuous creation, its reasons, the Baal Shem Tov, ayin and efes) (lines 1598-1795)

**The movement.** It is the longest walk through creation-every-moment in the book. It ends by moving the word from outside the creature to its very core.

> אלא גם מפני שמהותו של הנברא גופא הוא הדבר ה
> *But also because the creature's own essence is the word of God.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: 'Forever, God, Your word stands in the heavens': the saying 'let there be a firmament' stands in the sky; if it left for a moment, the sky would be as if it never was (lines 1599, 1604). So creatures are not existences for themselves (line 1605). Length: reasons. A silversmith only changes the shape of silver that was there, so the vessel stands without him; creation from nothing is wholly new, so it needs its maker always (lines 1663-1669). A thrown stone rises only while the force holds; the rising is the force itself (lines 1670-1672). A face in a mirror is not like a portrait: step away and nothing remains (lines 1692-1709). What comes from will, not from other powers, is nothing once the will withdraws (lines 1729-1733). And reasons of love: He makes it anew each moment to show His endless kindness, so our devotion can be whole and constant (lines 1626-1627); so each creature can add to His glory at every moment and in every detail (lines 1629-1637). Say 'borei pri hagafen' and you reveal not only the word that made the vine in six days but the word making it now (lines 1638-1643). And so that even the mind would understand He is One (line 1644). Depth: the Baal Shem Tov's news: the word is not only near the creature, keeping it; it is enclothed inside it, its very essence (lines 1759-1767). Then the creature's nullity has two levels: 'ayin', like sunlight in space that depends on the sun, 'there is none besides You'; and 'efes', like light inside the sun, 'there is nothing but You' (lines 1776-1795). Bring it home. Hold a piece of bread before the blessing. It is being said right now. And the saying is not around it but inside it; it is what the bread is. The stone and mirror pictures break, and the book says so: the thrower's power leaves his hand, but the word never leaves the Speaker (line 1673).

**From inside.** Everything feels spoken. A tenderness, as if the world were a voice you could listen to.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: 'efes', light inside the sun, where there is nothing but Him (line 1795).

**Into the day.** Say one blessing today slowly, thinking: the word is making this now.

## Achdus Hashem (3) §4: of no account (k'lo chashiv) (lines 1796-1815)

**The movement.** It moves from 'depends on Him' to 'counts for nothing before Him'. It uses the picture of a person raised so high that others no longer bother him.

> הזולת אינו מבלבל אותו, כי במדריגה זו, הזולת אינו נמצא שם
> *The other does not disturb him, because at that level the other is not there.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the lower unity says creatures are not existences for themselves. The higher says nothing has any worth or standing before Him: 'all before Him is as nothing' (lines 1796-1797). Length: these are two levels in God. To the Godliness that became the source of creatures, creatures take up room. To the Ein Sof above being a source, they, and the source itself, count for nothing (line 1806). Depth: the picture: a person raised to a level that has nothing to do with others is not disturbed by them, because at that level they are not there (line 1807). And since the Ein Sof is in every place, creatures count for nothing even in their own place (line 1813). And they themselves come from the surrounding light above being a source, so their nothingness is also from their own side (line 1815). Bring it home. Remember a moment you were so caught up in something great, a birth, a wedding, a deep prayer, that small annoyances did not reach you. The book says that is a picture of how the world stands before Him. The picture breaks: the raised person still knows the others exist. Before Him, even their existence has no place.

**From inside.** A spaciousness where little things do not sting.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: before the Ein Sof above being a source, everything is as nothing (line 1806).

**Into the day.** Let one small annoyance pass today by remembering something great.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Achdus Hashem (3) §5: not existent at all (the Rambam's first two laws; Rambam and Rashi) (lines 1816-1865)

**The movement.** It takes the mind to the top of the ladder, where the world was never created. Then it shows that even this does not cancel the mitzvos, and makes peace between the Rambam and Rashi.

> שהשפיל את עצמו כביכול להיות ,גדול" לברוא עולמות
> *That He lowered Himself, as it were, to be 'great' enough to create worlds.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the world is real only relative to the Ein Sof as He contracted Himself to create. Before the Essence as He is in Himself, there is no existence at all (line 1822). Length: 'where you find His greatness, the making of the world, there you find His humility' (line 1830). All creation is a lowering: He lowered Himself to be 'great', to create. The level above the contraction is in force now too, only hidden (line 1832). A great sage is still fully himself when he stoops to talk with a child (line 1836). This does not cancel the mitzvos. The mitzvos are cancelled only by the idea that there is only the Essence without the contraction. In truth He did contract Himself, and from that side the world is real (lines 1841-1842). And the contraction too was His will, so even from above, the world's reality is willed (line 1848). Depth: the Rambam's first law: 'there is a First Existent', He who lowered Himself to be existent and first. The second: 'if it entered the mind that He is not existent', the higher knowing that He is beyond existence, and from there nothing else could exist (lines 1850-1858). So the Rambam, writing law for a real world, says 'no true existent'; Rashi, explaining what was seen at Sinai when the Essence was revealed, says 'they saw that He is alone'. 'One said one thing and one said another, and they do not disagree' (lines 1863-1865). Bring it home. You live in the Rambam's world of laws and deeds. And in one moment of prayer you may glimpse Rashi's Sinai. Both are true, and you do not have to choose.

**From inside.** Awe at His humility. A feeling of being talked to by Someone much greater who chose to stoop.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: before the Essence, no existence at all (line 1822).

**Into the day.** Do one act today of stooping to someone younger or weaker, and think of His humility in creating.

## Achdus Hashem (3) §6: true unity, the world's existence united with Him (lines 1866-1893)

**The movement.** It crowns the entry with the highest unity: not the world erased, but the world as it is, one with Him. It even finds it in the word 'today'.

> הוא מפני שהעצמות הוא כל יכול ונושא הפכים
> *Because the Essence is all-capable and bears opposites.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: since His oneness has no limits, its truest expression is the world's own existence united with Him (line 1866). Length: a unity that depends on nothing else existing is limited, and in the subtlest way it can change (line 1870). It also gets its oneness from the state of the other, so the other is mixed into it (line 1878). The verses: 'You were shown ... there is none besides Him' rules out everything; 'know today ... there is none else' allows heaven and earth and says they are one with Him. Only the second says 'today', because 'today' means no change, always (lines 1871-1877). Depth: the first kind of unity comes from the revealed lights; the second from the Essence, which can do anything and bears opposites (lines 1879-1884). The Essence is not changed by anything, so it can make a real 'something' and stay one (line 1885). And it is fully true only when the creature's bitul comes from the creature's own side (line 1893). Bring it home. 'Know today'. Not in a vision, but on this ordinary day, with your house standing and your work waiting. The unity is here, with all of it in place.

**From inside.** Wholeness. A peace that includes everything instead of leaving things out.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: the Essence, all-capable, bearing opposites (line 1884).

**Into the day.** Say 'know today' to yourself once this afternoon, looking at your ordinary surroundings.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Achdus Hashem (4), the unity shows in nature: introduction (lines 1894-1915)

**The movement.** It names the trap in nature's regularity. The sun that rises every day makes it easy to forget who raises it.

> יש מקום לטעות ח"ו שהעולם מתנהג מעצמו
> *There is room to err, heaven forbid, that the world runs by itself.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the world has two modes, nature and miracle. Nature comes from the Name Elokim, which has the number of 'hateva', nature; miracles come from Havayah, who was, is and will be as one (line 1894). Length: Elokim hides the light that makes the world, so in nature Godliness is hidden. And because nature is always the same, the sun rising in the east and setting in the west every day, there is room to think the world runs by itself (lines 1895, 1900). In nature, He leads each creature by its own character, so creatures take up room; in miracles He leads by His will, and creatures count for nothing (line 1901). Depth: and yet the unity shows even in nature, and not only as 'bitul of the something' but as bitul in existence (line 1908). A footnote even gives nature's bitul an edge over miracle's: in a miracle He ignores the world's rules; in nature the rules themselves are His will (line 1912). Bring it home. Watch tomorrow's sunrise, or just the light coming into your window. The very sameness that lulls you is a daily act.

**From inside.** Morning wonder. The routine gets a pulse.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It opens a door the next sections walk through.

**Into the day.** Look at the morning light once tomorrow and say: You are doing this again.

## Achdus Hashem (4) §1: the world can hold miracles (lines 1916-1965)

**The movement.** It shows that the world was built from the start to be able to bend. The capacity for miracle is part of what a thing is.

> בחינת הפשיטות דהאור היא ענינו\* וגדרו של העולם
> *The simplicity of the light is the world's own content and definition.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: when a miracle happens, the world does not vanish. Only the thing in the miracle changes, and only in one detail: at the Red Sea the water stood like a wall but stayed water (line 1916). This shows that inside the world is hidden a light above its limits (line 1922). Length: a thing's matter comes from the divine word, 'let there be light'; its form, shape and nature come from the divine will, through wisdom (lines 1930-1931). So a thing's nature is already a yielding to His will, like a vessel ready for the miracle (line 1940). Depth: God made a condition with the sea, and with everything made in the six days, each one separately, that it would reverse its nature when needed (lines 1955, 1959). Why, if He could change it anyway? Because for the world to be His home, the Godliness above the worlds must be its own content. By building the miracle into each thing at creation, the simplicity of the light becomes the world's own definition (line 1959). Bring it home. Think of a part of your life that seems fixed: a habit, a weakness, a situation. It was made with a condition that it could bend. The Red Sea picture breaks: the sea split once. The condition was made with everything, all the time.

**From inside.** Hope. Nothing in you or around you is as hard as it looks.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: the Godliness above the worlds as the world's own definition (line 1959).

**Into the day.** Try once today to act against a small habit, remembering it was made to bend.

## Achdus Hashem (4) §2: nature's wisdom is divine wisdom (lines 1966-1991)

**The movement.** It lets the reader stand beside the physicians and astronomers of the text at the end of their knowledge. The weariness of the wise becomes a sign.

> שנלאו חכמי הרופאים לעמוד עד סוף החכמה
> *The wise physicians grew weary of reaching the end of the wisdom.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: 'You made them all with wisdom'. Physicians tire out trying to reach the end of the wisdom in the body's limbs; so with animals, plants, the earth, and the heavenly spheres (line 1966). Length: why can't they reach its end? Because it is divine wisdom (line 1966). Yet the very fact that they tire shows it is the kind of thing a mind can work at. Of creation from nothing you cannot say 'they tired'; it is not in the mind's category at all (line 1967). Depth: wisdom stands in the middle. It is bitul, 'what', the absence of self, and so it can hold the will. And it is the first of all existence, so it brings the will into the word, the form into the matter (line 1976). That is why nature's wisdom is both within reason and beyond it (lines 1977-1985). Even the fire's rising and water's falling are not demanded by fire and water; they are His will (line 1993). Bring it home. Look at your own hand and try to understand it fully: the bones, the nerves, the way it knows to close. At the point where your understanding tires, stop, and let the tiredness become praise.

**From inside.** Humble curiosity. Knowledge that ends in awe instead of pride.

**At the edge.** It brings the mind to its edge: the point where the wise grow weary (line 1966), and above it, creation from nothing, which is not in reason's category at all (line 1967).

**Into the day.** Learn one small fact about the body or nature today, and end with a blessing.

## Achdus Hashem (4) §3: natural things that go against the laws of nature (lines 1992-2082)

**The movement.** It collects small wonders the eye usually passes over: a rooster, a square, a thrown stone. Each one 'opens the eyes' to the power under nature.

> וזה מפקח עינינו ומגלה לנו שאמיתית היותו של האבן הוא ה,אין"
> *And this opens our eyes and shows us that the true being of the stone is the 'nothing'.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: some things join what cannot join. Spirit and body; fire and water; the four elements in every object (lines 1995-2013). The rooster that wakes a man at midnight comes from the angel Gavriel waking souls, and above that, from higher sources; the spiritual comes down into a rooster's crow, and only 'He who does wonders' can bind them (lines 2001-2002). Length: even angels are compounds, so they are not an existence at all: 'there is none apart from You' (line 2024). The heavens have not weakened in almost six thousand years; 'the heavens tell' of a power without limit (lines 2033-2035). Many spheres, each moving its own way, keep one order of day and night, summer and winter (line 2045). Summer's heat cannot come from the sun's tiny change of distance, a mustard seed beside a lamp; 'summer and winter, You made them', He sends it through the heavens (lines 2052-2060). The side of a square of fifty square cubits can only be approximated, 'not from our lack of knowledge, but from the nature of the number' (lines 2064-2071). Depth: throw a stone up. Its nature is weight and rest, and both are gone as it rises, yet the stone is still there (lines 2074-2075). 'This opens our eyes': its true being is the 'nothing' that makes it; its nature is only a garment (line 2081). If the eye were allowed to see the life inside things, their matter would not be seen at all (line 2082). Bring it home. Toss a pebble in the air today and watch it at the top of its rise, for the moment when it has neither weight nor rest. That is what it always is, underneath. The stone picture breaks: the stone falls back to its nature. The creature never falls back to being without the word.

**From inside.** Eyes opening. The ordinary goes thin, and something steady shows through.

**At the edge.** It touches it: if the eye could see the life within, matter would not be seen (line 2082).

**Into the day.** Pick up a stone or any small thing, and let it remind you: its nature is a garment.

## Achdus Hashem (4) §4: the unity shows even in nature's concealment (lines 2083-2095)

**The movement.** It turns to the darkest hiding, exile, and finds it full of light. The reader's own hard times are reread.

> שענינו הפנימי דההעלם גופא הוא שיהי' גילוי אלקות
> *The inner content of the concealment itself is that there be a revealing of Godliness.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: nature not only limits the light; it hides even the limited light, until one could think the world runs itself (line 2083). Length: in exile, the power of self-sacrifice is more open than in the time of the Temple (line 2091). How can hiding bring out revealing? They are opposites. Depth: a thing's true reality is the purpose it was made for. The purpose of hiding is a greater revealing, so the inner reality of the hiding is the revealing of Godliness (line 2092). That hiding brings out self-sacrifice shows this even on the outside (line 2093). Bring it home. Think of the hardest stretch of your life, when God seemed far. Was there something in you then, a stubborn faithfulness, that did not show in easy times?

**From inside.** Meaning in the dark. Not that pain is good, but that it is not empty.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: the hiding's inner reality is revealing (line 2093).

**Into the day.** Name one thing that grew in you during a hard time, and thank Him for it.

## Achdus Hashem (5), from the side of the intent: introduction and §1, three degrees of nullity (lines 2096-2176)

**The movement.** It climbs three steps: the world made each moment, the world made on a condition, the world made for a purpose. At the top, the world's solidity turns out to be 'an accident'.

> אינו אמיתית מהותו אלא מקרה אשר קרה לו
> *Is not its true essence, but an accident that happened to it.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: three reasons the world is one with God: it is made each moment; it was made on condition that Israel accept the Torah; it was made for the sake of Israel and the Torah (lines 2102-2105). Length: made each moment gives only 'bitul of the something', because the power that creates is a limited power; even the heavens' endlessness comes from a power that surrounds them, not from inside (lines 2116-2119). The condition goes further: if it had failed, the world would be undone backward, as if it never was (lines 2122-2124). 'The sea returned to its strength', 'le'eisano': to its condition (line 2151). Yet even a condition leaves the deed as the main thing (line 2148). Depth: a person's plan does nothing by itself; above, thought itself creates. So the world's true reality is the intent: Israel and Torah (line 2158). That the world looks like a thing is 'not its true essence but an accident that happened to it'; its true being is as it is in the Creator's thought (line 2166). The sages say of the seven species, 'this whole verse was said for measures'. Wheat and barley exist so that halachic measures can be counted by them: the olive, the barley-grain, the fig. The measures are not added to the fruit; they are what the fruit really is (lines 2170-2176). Bring it home. Look at your life's 'things', your house, your work, your body, and ask what they are for. The answer, says the book, is more real than the things.

**From inside.** A sense of purpose that feels firmer than the ground. The world is less heavy and more meaningful.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: the world's true reality is as it is in His thought (line 2166).

**Into the day.** Before you eat bread or fruit today, think: this was made for a mitzvah.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Achdus Hashem (5) §2: which level (speech, mitzvos, Torah) (lines 2177-2217)

**The movement.** It tells of the Rebbeim who looked at a ceiling and saw only the word making it. Then it places the fullest 'nothing besides Him' in Torah itself.

> שאין רואים את התקרה (דעם באלקן) ורק את הדבר הוי' שמהווה את התקרה
> *They do not see the ceiling (the beams), only the word of God that brings the ceiling into being.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: at the level of divine speech, the world as world is not there at all; its whole existence is the word making it. So it is told that the Rebbeim said they did not see the ceiling, only the word creating it (lines 2178-2179). Length: that is tied to the world being for Torah, for keeping the mitzvos. So what they saw in the physical world was the chance to do mitzvos; in the physical body, 'You chose us from all nations' (lines 2180-2189). Depth: the will for mitzvos is above the will for worlds. But the mitzvos are instructions for living in the world, so at that level, very faintly, the person and the world still have standing. Torah is His own will and wisdom, which relates to no world; there the truth of 'nothing besides Him' is complete (lines 2196-2200). 'Halichos olam lo': read not 'ways of the world' but 'halachos', laws (line 2211). Bring it home. Look up at your ceiling tonight. Try not to see beams and plaster but the word holding them. Then open a page of Torah and feel that here nothing stands outside Him.

**From inside.** The room seems to speak. Learning feels like standing inside His will.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: in Torah there is no existence besides Godliness (line 2196).

**Into the day.** Look at the ceiling before sleep and say: I see the word.

## Achdus Hashem (5) §3: why a condition was needed as well (lines 2218-2223)

**The movement.** It answers a small puzzle: if the world is made for Torah, why add a condition? The answer reaches down to the world as creatures feel it.

> ובכדי שגם בבחינה זו דהבריאה תהי' ענינם של הנבראים ישראל ותורה
> *So that on this level of creation too, the content of the creatures would be Israel and Torah.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the intent is higher than the condition, so why the condition (line 2218)? Length: the world is made by His thought, and above, thought does make things. But creation as the creatures feel it comes from His 'action', and what comes from action is not one with the one who acts (line 2219). Depth: so He set a condition on creation, on creation as creatures know it, so that there too their content would be Israel and Torah (lines 2220-2223). Bring it home. Your big purpose is set above you. But you live in the world of doing. Tie today's doing to that purpose with one small condition: 'this is for Torah'.

**From inside.** A bridge between high meaning and daily tasks.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It links the high to the low.

**Into the day.** Attach one ordinary task today to a mitzvah, in words: I am doing this so I can...

## Achdus Hashem (5) §4: how it shows in halachah (lines 2224-2242)

**The movement.** It proves the teaching from the law books. A thing's purpose outweighs its body in several rulings.

> אמיתית מציאותה של ,שריפת תרומה" הוא זה שמצוה לשרוף תרומה טמאה
> *The true reality of 'burning terumah' is that it is a mitzvah to burn impure terumah.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: because each thing's true reality is the purpose it was made for, when a mitzvah is done with it, its outer reality falls away and it is only the mitzvah (line 2226). Length: one may not burn impure terumah on a festival, though one could cook with its fire. Tosafos: 'the need of the Most High cancels the need of the ordinary' (lines 2228-2231). Why? Because the burning's true reality is the mitzvah; revealing the true reality cancels the outer one (line 2233). Depth: even a person's own intent counts this way. Carrying less than a measure of food in a vessel on Shabbos is not liable, even for the vessel, which alone would be enough; the vessel is secondary to the food it holds (line 2240). Bring it home. Your car, your kitchen, your clothes are vessels. When you use them for a mitzvah, the book says, that is what they become.

**From inside.** A cleaner way of seeing possessions: as vessels, lighter in themselves.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there.

**Into the day.** Use one object today only for a mitzvah, and let it be only that.

## Achdus Hashem (6), the higher and the lower unity: introduction (lines 2243-2366)

**The movement.** It lays out the whole field of the lower and higher unity before walking it. It tells the reader that one comes by thinking and the other comes of itself.

> הביטול דיחודא עילאה בא ממילא, והביטול דיחודא תתאה בא ע"י התבוננות
> *The nullity of the higher unity comes of itself; the nullity of the lower unity comes through contemplation.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: Chassidus says He is the only existent. The difference between the lower and higher unity is either how much this is felt, or a difference in the unity itself (lines 2272-2273). Even creation each moment, itself a teaching of Chassidus, is only the lower unity (line 2276). Length: the Shema's 'Echad' is the higher unity; 'Baruch Shem ... va'ed' is the lower, since va'ed has the letters of echad exchanged (line 2285). They come from two knowledges: the lower, 'below is something, above is nothing'; the higher, 'above is something, below is nothing' (line 2294). Four explanations of the difference (lines 2297-2323). The lower yielding stays on the surface, or comes from the creature's own grasp, or is like sunlight outside the sun; the higher is its opposite. Sunlight inside the sun cannot even be called 'something' (line 2314). Or: both are included in the source, but before the source the creatures still take up room; before what is above the source they take none (line 2323). And 'source' and 'above source' are relative (line 2340). Depth: in contemplation, one way: the higher bitul comes of itself, the lower through contemplation (line 2353). Another: both come through contemplation, but in the lower, even after thinking, one must set oneself aside by choice (line 2354). In the lower service a person does mitzvos to bring his soul near; in the higher, to cleave to Ein Sof and draw Him into the world (line 2359). Bring it home. There are prayers you work at, thought by thought, until you bow a little. And there may be a moment when the bowing just happens. The book says both are real, and they are different.

**From inside.** Orientation. The reader sees the map and where he stands on it.

**At the edge.** It names it: a bitul that comes 'of itself' (line 2353), not made by effort.

**Into the day.** Before prayer today, spend one minute thinking of how He makes the world now, and then bow your head by choice.

## Higher and lower unity §1: outward or inward (lines 2367-2389)

**The movement.** It shows the lower unity as a fight and the higher as an embrace. The reader recognizes his own days of struggle, and learns that they are a real, if lower, oneness.

> הוא מאמין ומודה%5 שכן הוא האמת
> *He believes and acknowledges that this is the truth.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the lower yielding is on the outside; inside, the person is still a 'something'. The higher goes through his whole being (line 2368). Length: two hands. The higher is the right hand that hugs a friend so he cannot move away. The lower is the left hand, strength: because he sees the world as separate, he must overpower it (line 2373). So he closes his eyes to evil and does more good than his habits want (line 2377). The lower is acknowledgment: the truth that all is as nothing does not shine for him, but he believes and acknowledges that it is true. The higher is when it shines openly (line 2378). Or: in the lower he understands that he is in his source like sunlight in the sun; in the higher he feels it (line 2379). Depth: in the lower service, physical acts stay physical, but he wants only the Godliness in them. In the higher, like the Patriarchs, even the physical acts are Godliness (line 2388). The lower subdues the animal soul; the higher transforms it (line 2389). Bring it home. On a day you must push yourself to pray or be kind, you are in the left hand. That is still being held. And on the day it comes easily, remember the right hand.

**From inside.** Self-respect in struggle. The effort itself becomes a form of closeness.

**At the edge.** It points there: the right hand's embrace, where the truth shines openly (line 2378).

**Into the day.** Do one good thing today that goes against your habit, and call it: the left hand.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Higher and lower unity §2: from the creature's side or from Godliness (lines 2390-2421)

**The movement.** It tells the reader something humbling: even his self-sacrifice, if it is his, is still 'something'. Then it shows Rabbi Akiva, whose soul went out in 'One' because a light from above caught it.

> ע"י שנמשך עליו הארה מבחינת אחד האמת
> *Through a radiance from the level of the True One being drawn onto him.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: yielding that comes through effort is 'bitul of the something'; inside he stays as he was (line 2396). 'There is one who is nullified', but he is still one who is (line 2396). Length: another meaning: the yielding itself is a 'something'. Even when it fills his whole being, if it comes from his understanding, his feeling, even his self-sacrifice, it is still his (line 2407). True bitul comes from Godliness's side (line 2408). Depth: Rabbi Akiva's soul went out in 'Echad'. His self-sacrifice, from his side, was only the lower unity. A light from the True One was drawn onto him, and his self-sacrifice was taken into 'One' (line 2416). That is 'to be one in One' (line 2417). Bring it home. Think of the most selfless thing you ever did. Could you feel yourself doing it? The book is not criticizing. It is saying there is a giving beyond that, which you cannot make, only receive.

**From inside.** Humility about your best moments, and openness to a gift.

## Higher and lower unity §3: two divisions within the lower unity (lines 2422-2447)

**The movement.** It shows how one word, 'echad', becomes 'va'ed' by changing letters, and what is lost in the change. The reader learns two different ways he feels separate.

> האור של האות (הקודם) יורד ממדריגתו ומתלבש במהות (אות) אחר
> *The light of the first letter comes down from its level and dresses in another essence, another letter.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the lower unity has two kinds of 'something-ness'. Creatures take up room, or they feel themselves separate (line 2423). Length: echad becomes va'ed when its letters are exchanged; the light of the first letter comes down and dresses in another letter that hides it (line 2424). In echad, the creating light is open; in va'ed it is dressed in a hiding garment (line 2425). The change does two things: it makes the light a source for creatures, so they take up room; and it hides even the source, so they feel separate (lines 2429-2430). The Name Elokim does the same two things: it contracts Havayah's light into a source, and it hides even the contracted light (lines 2431-2440). Depth: 'God of knowledges': both knowledges are in God. Below, 'something' means a thing on its own; above, it means only that it has worth and takes up room (lines 2441-2446). Bring it home. Notice two kinds of distance you feel from God. One: 'I matter too'. Two: 'I am on my own'. The first is less far than the second.

**From inside.** Clarity about one's own distance, without despair.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It maps the lower rungs.

**Into the day.** When you feel alone today, say: the source is only hidden.

## Higher and lower unity §4: the source makes room for the self (lines 2448-2459)

**The movement.** It explains why the world can doubt God, with the picture of a teacher who simplifies for a student. The student's doubts are real, and they are a gift of the simplification.

> מצד עצם השכל, כמו שהוא אצל הרב, יתורצו כל הקושיות
> *From the side of the intellect itself, as it is with the teacher, all the questions are answered.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: creatures feel separate because they do not feel the power making them. But that too is because the source makes room for it (line 2448). Length: a teacher gives a student an idea shrunk to the student's size. Then a question may come that the student cannot answer. With the teacher, in the full idea, every question is answered. The question has power only because the idea was shrunk (line 2454). Depth: so with creation: the creatures can seem to be separate things because the light was contracted to fit them, and the contracted light gives room for that (line 2455). Above being a source, even that room is not possible (line 2456). Bring it home. Your hardest question about God may be a sign of how much He shrank Himself to fit you. With Him, the question is already answered. The teacher picture breaks: a teacher can explain later. Here the answer may wait until the light is no longer contracted.

**From inside.** Patience with your own questions. They feel less like enemies.

**At the edge.** It points there: in the full light, no question stands (line 2454).

**Into the day.** Write down one question you have about God, and next to it: 'with the Teacher, answered'.

## Higher and lower unity §5: three levels (angels, souls, lofty souls) (lines 2460-2529)

**The movement.** It tells the reader where his soul stands among the levels. Angels cannot go where a soul can, and the highest yielding is given, not taken.

> בטילה באוא"ס ממש כנר בפני האבוקה
> *Nullified in the Ein Sof truly, like a candle before a torch.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: three levels (lines 2461-2467): being outside the source but knowing all depends on it; being contained in the source, yet still taking up room before it; and counting for nothing before the Ein Sof above being a source. Length: angels can have the first. They cannot be contained in their source, because then they would stop being creatures. A soul can, because a soul is Godliness in essence (lines 2489-2491). The third, 'as nothing', a soul cannot reach from itself, because it goes against its very nature; it is given from above (lines 2499-2502). It is given to souls, because souls are related to it, and so it can reach even most souls (lines 2507-2508). Depth: every divine soul, even a low one, is nullified in the Ein Sof 'like a candle before a torch'. What blocks it is being wrapped deep in the animal soul, so that it is not moved by fine, spiritual things (line 2528). Bring it home. Think of lighting a small candle in a room where a great fire burns. It is still a flame, but it vanishes in the light. Your soul is like that before Him. What you feel is only how thickly it is wrapped. The candle picture breaks: a candle and a torch are the same kind of fire. The soul is 'part of God above', and yet the Ein Sof is beyond all comparison.

**From inside.** A sense of your soul's dignity, and of how much is still wrapped around it.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: 'as nothing', the highest yielding, given from above (line 2499).

**Into the day.** Light a candle (Shabbos or any candle) and think: a candle before a torch.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Higher and lower unity §6: 'Va'ed', the lower unity, is eternity (lines 2530-2580)

**The movement.** It turns the ladder upside down. The lower word, 'va'ed', turns out to touch what is above 'echad', and the darkness of this world is the reason.

> כי יתרון האור הוא מן החושך דוקא
> *For the advantage of light comes precisely from darkness.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: va'ed is lower than echad, yet 'va'ed' means forever, and belongs to Atik, the 'Ancient', which is yachid, above echad (lines 2530-2537). So the lower unity reaches above the higher. Length: why is the lower one eternal? Excitement from contemplation changes, big one day, small the next; the lowliness of bitul does not change (line 2545). Even the lower yielding is yielding, and so it does not stop (line 2547). The tribes told Jacob, 'as in your heart there is only One, so in ours'. They were all the souls to the end of time, with only the lower unity; and yet their 'One' reached higher than Jacob's (lines 2548, 2556). On Yom Kippur this root is revealed, so 'Baruch Shem' is said aloud (lines 2565-2573). Depth: 'forever and ever', 'le'olam va'ed', means this physical world, where not even the lower unity shows (lines 2575-2579). Refining the darkness here reaches Atik, the source of true eternity, 'for the advantage of light comes from the darkness' (line 2580). Bring it home. Your ordinary, steady faithfulness, the prayer said on a flat day, the kindness done with no feeling, may reach higher than your high moments. The flatness is not a failure. It is the ground where the forever grows. The light and dark picture breaks: in the world, darkness is only a lack. Here the dark place is where the highest light is found.

**From inside.** Quiet worth in plain days. The sense that steadiness is a form of height.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: va'ed touches yachid, above echad (line 2537).

## Higher and lower unity §7: the lower unity is in all creatures, as acknowledgment (lines 2581-2599)

## Higher and lower unity §8: the higher and lower knowledge (lines 2600-2692)

**The movement.** It changes what the reader counts as 'real'. It walks from the child who believes only what he can touch to the one who counts only Godliness as real, and then gives a practical way to hold that in the middle of doing.

> בשעת מעשה הוא מודה שהעולם הוא אין וכלא חשיב
> *At the moment of action he acknowledges that the world is nothing and of no account.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the lower knowledge: creation is 'from nothing to something'; the world is something, and the power making it is called 'nothing', because it is not grasped and is not a thing (line 2609). The higher: Godliness is the true something, and all creation is as nothing before Him (line 2609). Both are in God (line 2612). Length: two different 'nothings'. The nothing that makes created things; and the light that is nothing before its own source. Before the second, even the first is as nothing (lines 2632-2638). Depth: the knowledges differ over what counts as existing (line 2651). A small child counts only what he can touch; an idea is not real to him. A thinker, after much work with ideas, counts a reasoning as real, and a block of wood as hardly anything. So after much work and toil in knowing God, a person can come to count only Godliness as 'real' (lines 2653-2658). The Rambam: even one who knows everything exists from the truth of His existence may think He is 'not an existent', because He has no definition; no, He is the true existent, and anything defined cannot truly exist (lines 2666-2671). For most people this arrives as acknowledgment: he sees the world as real and keeps the mitzvos in it as real, and at the moment of action he acknowledges that it is nothing (line 2679). This joining is possible only from the Ein Sof, who bears opposites (line 2676). Bring it home. Remember how real your toys were as a child, and how a new idea later felt realer than furniture. Picture one more step, where He is realer than both. The child picture breaks: a child grows up by himself. This change comes only with much work, and at its peak is given.

**From inside.** A slow re-ranking of what is real. Solid things feel lighter; He feels closer.

**At the edge.** It brings the mind to it: 'only Godliness counts as existing' (line 2658), held as acknowledgment.

**Into the day.** In the middle of one task today, stop for a second and acknowledge: this is nothing before You, and I will do it well.

## Higher and lower unity §9: both knowledges are true (lines 2693-2790)

**The movement.** It lets both knowledges be true, and explains a Midrash in which Truth and Kindness argued about creating the world. Kindness won, and Truth was not wrong.

> חסד אמר יברא שכולו מלא חסדים
> *Kindness said, let it be created, for it is all full of kindness.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: even the creatures' knowledge that the world is a real 'something' is true (line 2696). The last words of creation, 'bara Elokim la'asos', end in the letters of 'emes', truth (lines 2697, 2702). Length: the Midrash: 'Truth said, let it not be created, for it is all full of lies; Kindness said, let it be created, for it is all full of kindness' (line 2723). Truth stands at the level above the contraction, where the world is as nothing, so its seeming 'something' is a lie. Kindness stands at the contracted light, where creatures really do take up room (line 2724). The contraction is real, so both are true (lines 2718-2722). Both come from the Ein Sof, who is 'above without end' and 'below without limit'. They are true only when this root shows in them; that is why they must be joined (lines 2731-2739). So too nature and miracle, one in their inside (lines 2741-2751). Depth: still, the main truth is the higher, because it is so everywhere and by itself (line 2768). Truth is what no one opposes; even severity does not oppose mercy (line 2769). And through the world's 'something', a truth higher than the higher knowledge is revealed, for truth must be known (lines 2776-2779). Even where one might think He is not there, He is (line 2787). Bring it home. On a day when the world feels very real and God feels far, remember: Kindness wanted this world. Its solidity is kindness, not a lie to be escaped.

**From inside.** Peace with the world. The heaviness of things feels like a gift of kindness.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: the Ein Sof, above without end and below without limit, as one (line 2732).

**Into the day.** When the world feels too real today, say: Kindness said, let it be created.

## Higher and lower unity §10: the 'something' of the lower knowledge does not contradict His unity (lines 2791-2821)

**The movement.** It shows how the light that fills creatures stays untouched by them. The reader meets 'none is holy like God'.

> *There are many holy ones, and none is holy like God.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the lower knowledge is true and still does not contradict His oneness (line 2792). Length: creatures matter only relative to the light enclothed in the worlds; before the Essence all is as nothing, and the source itself is only an outer radiance (line 2801). Depth: 'none is holy like God': the Zohar says there are many holy ones, and none is holy like Havayah. The light inside creatures gives them life from within and yet stays holy and apart, not grasped by them, not changed by them (lines 2802-2810). And the creatures' worth itself comes from the perfection of the Ein Sof, who includes both 'as nothing' and 'worth something' (line 2811). Each knowledge shows its inside only when joined to the other (lines 2820-2821). Bring it home. You can live fully in the world, and the light that gives you life is not dragged down with you. It is inside you and holy at the same time.

**From inside.** Trust. The light will not be spoiled by your ordinary life.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: holy and apart while enclothed (line 2806).

**Into the day.** In a very ordinary moment today, whisper: none is holy like You, and You are here.

## Higher and lower unity §11: surrounding and filling (sovev and memale) (lines 2822-2881)

**The movement.** It distinguishes the light that fills things from the light that surrounds them. The reader is warned not to picture God and the world as a soul in a body.

> העולם אינו גם מציאות טפילה, יחודא עילאה
> *The world is not even a secondary existence: the higher unity.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: 'You give life to them all': read it 'You bring them into being' too (line 2829). Life comes from the filling light, memale; being comes from the surrounding light, sovev (line 2830). Length: before memale, the world is real but secondary, like a body to its soul: the lower unity. Before sovev, the world is not even a secondary existence: the higher unity (line 2831). 'There is none else' in full, not even something secondary, is only before sovev (line 2832). The nations, who know only memale, think God and the world are like soul and body (line 2839). A soul fills the body and lives differently in each limb, so the body takes up room (lines 2855-2857). The surrounding light is the same in all worlds and does not dress in them (line 2856). Depth: why is being from sovev? Because to enclothe in something, the thing must already exist. So the root of being must come from what does not enclothe (line 2880). Memale then enclothes to bring it out in fact, as if the thing already stood on its own (line 2881). Bring it home. Feel your own aliveness: breath, warmth, thought. That is the filling light, as it were. Now think of what gave you being before you had any life to feel. That is around you, not inside. The soul and body picture breaks, and the book says so outright: God is not to the world as a soul to a body (line 2840).

**From inside.** Being held from two sides: from within and from around.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: before the surrounding light, the world is not even secondary (line 2831).

**Into the day.** Put your hand on your heart and say: You fill me; then open your hands and say: You surround me.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Higher and lower unity §12: Havayah and Elokim (lines 2882-2972)

**The movement.** It shows that even the hard 'something' made through the hiding Name is, in truth, nullified. The reader is told his solidity is only how things feel from below.

> גם הנבראים שנעשו מציאות יש, הם בטלים במציאות
> *Even the creatures that became something are nullified in existence.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: in the Name Havayah is the higher unity; in Elokim, the lower (line 2882). The Shema's 'Havayah is One': before Havayah, the seven heavens, the earth and the four directions are wholly nullified (line 2908). Length: Elokim means 'strong and mighty, able over upper and lower', a God fitted to the worlds (line 2934). Havayah means 'was, is, will be as one', above the worlds (line 2935). And the world as it comes from Havayah has past, present and future as one, because its content is only that Havayah makes it (lines 2941-2942). Nature, 'teva', from 'tova', sunk: like something sunk in water, covered so it cannot be seen (line 2961). Miracles join the lower and higher, 'one in One' (line 2959). Depth: yet even the world made through Elokim has its body and being from Havayah; Elokim is only the channel (line 2966). Every act needs its maker to be revealed, so being comes from Havayah; but from pure revealing no 'something' could exist, so it came through Elokim, the hiding (line 2967). So in truth, even the creatures that became 'something' are nullified in existence (line 2968). Elokim hides only toward them; toward Havayah it hides nothing (lines 2969-2974). Bring it home. Look at your hands. From your side they are very much 'something'. From His side, the book says, they are already one with Him. You are not asked to feel it. You are told it is so.

**From inside.** Being known from the other side. A calm that does not depend on feeling.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: even what became 'something' is truly nullified (line 2968).

**Into the day.** Wash your hands today and say: from Your side, these are already Yours.

## Higher and lower unity §13: the lower and the higher Havayah (lines 2973-3002)

**The movement.** It reads the two Names of Havayah in the Shema. The first gathers everything up; the second is One; the word between them is a pause.

> ביטול היש של כל העולמות לגבי האין דשם הוי'

'Shema' means gathering, as in 'Saul gathered': the gathering of all worlds' 'something' up into the 'nothing' of Havayah, a rising from below (line 2996). The second Havayah is 'One', the higher (line 2997). 'Elokeinu' between them is a pause, because the source of the worlds is beyond comparison with the Essence (line 2998). And on one reading, the upper Havayah is yachid, above even the higher unity (line 2999). Bring it home. Say the first line of the Shema slowly tomorrow. On 'Havayah' gather everything you carry. On 'Elokeinu' pause. On the second 'Havayah Echad', let it all be One. The fish picture breaks: a fish lives in water that is not the fish. Before Him there is nothing else to live in.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: the upper Havayah, yachid, above even the higher unity (line 2999).

## Higher and lower unity §14: in the sefiros and the worlds (lines 3003-3086)

## Higher and lower unity §16: seeing and hearing (lines 3128-3197)

**The movement.** It gives two opposite answers to which unity is sight and which is hearing, and keeps both. Then it says that a little light from far off still drives away much darkness.

> כי העיקר הוא להיות אוזן שומעת בענינים הנעלים
> *For the main thing is to have an ear that hears lofty things.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: one view: the higher unity is seeing, the lower is hearing. What you hear, even if you understand it well, leaves room in the soul for doubt; what you see leaves no room at all (line 3137). Length: the more common view is the reverse. The higher unity is only heard, because we cannot picture the sefiros of Atzilus or their nullity (line 3170). The lower is seen: the spheres bowing westward are visible to the eye (line 3170). Hearing grasps finer things; seeing moves a person more (lines 3152-3154). So the Shema, the higher, begins with 'hear'; 'Baruch', the lower, begins with 'blessed', a revealing (lines 3155-3160). Depth: yet the sage is greater than the prophet: knowing that the high exists outweighs seeing the essence of the low (line 3181). The evil impulse rules only over what the eyes see, but in holiness even from hearing comes great arousal, 'a little light drives away much darkness' (line 3187). 'The main thing is to have an ear that hears lofty things' (line 3188). And at Sinai they saw what is heard (line 3190); the pilgrim came to see with both eyes, the lower and the higher (line 3191). Bring it home. Some truths about God you will never see. You can still hear them, and let them move you. That hearing is not second best.

**From inside.** Trust in listening. Being moved by what you cannot picture.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: at Sinai, 'seeing what is heard' (line 3190).

**Into the day.** Listen to one teaching today about something you cannot picture, and let it stay with you.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Higher and lower unity §17: the higher unity is only for the chosen few (lines 3198-3207)

For lofty souls, Elokim does not hide (line 3200). Depth: even when a person contemplates that Havayah's light truly shines in creatures, and only hiding keeps it from being felt, since he does not feel it, his yielding stays the lower one (line 3207). Bring it home. You do not need to reach what the great ones reach. Your job is the lower unity, done truly: the world is real, and you set yourself aside for Him in it.

## Higher and lower unity §18: everyone must still reach a little for the higher (lines 3208-3264)

**The movement.** It says the lower service is not true without a taste of the higher, even an imagined one. And it tells of a king's daughter who smelled the pot.

And 'quietly' may itself be the higher yielding mixed into the lower (line 3264). Bring it home. Once a week, or once a day, even for a minute, picture yourself as sunlight inside the sun. You may feel you are only imagining. The book says: imagine.

**From inside.** A taste, even faint, that changes how the rest tastes.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: a taste of the higher, 'even imaginatively' (line 3208).

## Higher and lower unity §20: why the lower unity is needed (lines 3289-3340)

**The movement.** It tells those who fly high to keep their eyes down. The lower unity refines the world, lasts, and completes the home below.

> שבתפלה צריך להיות לבו למעלה ועיניו למטה

Length: the lower has what the higher lacks (lines 3303-3326). It refines the shells, raising the sparks; the higher only overpowers them for a while. It lasts; it is 'va'ed', forever. What is seen moves a person more than what is heard. It is new, a something that yields, and so it draws a new light. It is the person's own work, and a person's work reaches higher than what is given from above. And the purpose of creation, a home below of 'something' and its yielding, is completed in it (line 3326). Depth: one who contemplates only the higher in prayer may fall after prayer, because the high yielding has nothing to do with the 'something' he meets afterward (line 3328). So 'eyes below': everything he sees, heaven and earth, is the outer garment of the King (line 3338). The 'nefilim' were lofty souls drawn straight from above, not through Malchus, and they fell, because a yielding that does not touch the self cannot hold the self (lines 3334-3337). Bring it home. After a lifted prayer, look down at your shoes, your street, your to-do list. That is where the prayer has to land.

**From inside.** Groundedness. Height that does not leave you dizzy.

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It brings the height down to land.

**Into the day.** After your prayer today, look at one ordinary thing and say: this is the King's garment.

## Higher and lower unity §22: joining the two (lines 3360-3409)

**The movement.** It gives the reader the image of the Ark: measured, it is two and a half cubits; measured from the walls, it takes no space. The joining is to hold both measurements at once.

> כשתופס שני הענינים דמדידת הארון ומדידת עשר אמות לכל רוח בבת אחת
> *When he grasps both measurements, of the Ark and of ten cubits on each side, at once.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: there is a third level: the joining of the two (line 3360). In the joining, the lower stays lower, yet the higher yielding shines in it (line 3366). The chief novelty is when a person's awareness stays the lower one and his yielding becomes the higher (lines 3372-3374). Length: the joining draws a light above both: the Name Kel, 'God of knowledges'; the Ein Sof's 'above' and 'below' as one; and finally the capacity of the Essence alone (lines 3379-3384). It goes first from below, then from above. In the Shema he contemplates the higher unity, but grasps it only with the mind's eye, so it is lower; then he contemplates how his own eyesight, which sees the world as real, is the opposite of the truth, and from that comes self-giving, and his lower yielding rises into the higher (line 3387). Then Torah draws the higher down into the lower (line 3388). Depth: the Ark. Measure the Ark and you find two and a half cubits, exactly as the Torah says, and you see it; yet you know that from the walls there are ten cubits on each side. That is the lower. Measure from the walls and see ten cubits each side, knowing the Ark has size: the higher. Hold both at once, equally real to you: the joining (lines 3401-3408). Bring it home. Hold something in your hand, a cup. Feel its size, its weight. At the same moment know that before Him it takes no room at all. Do not let one erase the other.

**From inside.** A strange, steady double vision. Nothing is lost; everything is doubled.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: the Essence's capacity joins what the mind cannot join (line 3384).

**Into the day.** Hold one object today and see it both ways at once, for as long as you can.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Higher and lower unity §23 (first part): image and likeness; blessing and acknowledgment (lines 3410-3514)

**The movement.** It gives the reader a doorway anyone can walk through: acknowledgment. As long as the soul is in you, you can say 'I acknowledge'.

> כל זמן שהנשמה בקרבי מודה אני
> *As long as the soul is within me, I acknowledge (Modeh ani).*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: man was made 'in our image, after our likeness' (line 3414). An image is a thing's own form; a likeness only resembles it (line 3416). The image is the higher unity, the likeness the lower (line 3425). Only man was made with both (line 3449). Even the Shema's twenty-five letters, 'koh', 'thus', show that before what is higher, even the higher unity is only a likeness (line 3440). Length: blessing and acknowledgment. Blessing is revealing, like seeing. Acknowledgment is when he does not see, but believes and acknowledges that it is true (lines 3467-3469). They come in two stages. From above: first the truth shines, then it comes down as acknowledgment. From below: first he acknowledges, and then he may come to see (lines 3484-3485). Depth: any Jew, whoever he is, even the least, can acknowledge that the truth is as He knows it (line 3485). 'As long as the soul is within me, I acknowledge'; nothing special is needed (line 3488). 'Modim', we acknowledge, has the number of a hundred, the hundred blessings: through acknowledging, the higher knowledge is drawn into open revealing (lines 3486, 3492). In grace after meals, the blessing for the food, Moshe's, is the higher, and the blessing for the land, Joshua's, begins 'we thank', the lower; between them love is the bridge (lines 3504-3514). Bring it home. On a morning when you feel nothing, say 'Modeh ani'. You are not claiming to see. You are acknowledging. The book says that is where the road to seeing begins.

**From inside.** An open door. No test to pass before you may begin.

**At the edge.** It points there: from acknowledgment to open blessing, 'a hundred blessings' (line 3486).

**Into the day.** Say 'Modeh ani' tomorrow morning slowly, as an acknowledgment, not a feeling.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Higher and lower unity §23 (second part): sea and land; two eyes; Jacob and Israel; the two challos; bones and flesh; seeing and gazing; above and below; revealed and inner Torah; witnesses (lines 3515-3637)

**The movement.** It runs through pairs the reader already lives with, and ends at the Shabbos table. There, the two loaves lie side by side, because before the revealed Essence neither is higher.

> אין יתרון ביחו"ע על יחו"ת, ושניהם שוים ממש
> *The higher unity has no advantage over the lower, and they are exactly equal.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: sea and land: fish die outside the sea; land creatures live apart from the earth that fed them (lines 3516-3520). 'You have captured my heart with one of your eyes': the two eyes are the two unities (line 3529). Jacob is the heel with a yud in it, struggling; Israel is the one who prevailed, given from above after the weekdays' work, like Shabbos after the six days (lines 3540-3545). 'He saw no iniquity in Jacob and no toil in Israel' (lines 3547-3562). Length: bones and flesh: self-giving 'in One' and self-giving 'with all your soul' (lines 3583-3584). Seeing and gazing: from below, the sun and stars look near; looking down into a pit, a short depth seems far (line 3592). Above and below: Torah comes from above, prayer rises from below; 'God, open my lips' joins them (lines 3611-3620). The revealed Torah alone, without the inner Torah's love and awe, makes one a 'something' (line 3626). Depth: the two challos of Shabbos. By the revealed Torah they are placed one on top of the other, since in the order of worlds the higher gives to the lower. By the inner Torah they lie side by side, held as one, because on Shabbos the Essence is revealed, and before it the higher unity has no advantage over the lower; they are exactly equal (lines 3563-3573). Bring it home. This Shabbos, hold the two challos side by side in your hands. One is the day your faith shone; one is the day you only acknowledged. Before Him, both are whole.

**From inside.** Wholeness without ranking. Rest.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: before the revealed Essence, higher and lower are exactly equal (line 3572).

**Into the day.** At the Shabbos table, hold the two loaves side by side for a moment before the blessing.

## Supplements to the notes (Miluim) for all the entries (lines 3638-4086)

**The movement.** These are added notes, not a new teaching. Among them is one plain sentence that carries the whole book: He is alone even in the place where the creatures are.

> דזה שהוא לבדו ואין זולתו הוא גם בה,מקום" של הנבראים
> *That He is alone and there is none besides Him, also in the 'place' of the creatures.*

**Contemplating it.** Breadth: the notes add fine points to every entry: why the burning of wood shows the binding force (lines 3654-3661), why the new sciences are 'leftovers from the King's feast' of the inner Torah (lines 3670-3674), how a merchant who counts his schemes as the main source of income, needing God 'also', falls into partnership (line 3768). Length: they keep the line between the world being real and being nothing. The world is not a dream, not even the kind of dream that means 'real but counts for nothing' (lines 3831-3836). Depth: one note says the unity is true in the creatures' own place, even if creatures did not need constant renewal (line 3905). And one quotes the Tzemach Tzedek: the Essence can be called only 'yachid', alone, not 'echad' (line 3912). Another says that if heaven and earth are fixed in one's heart as the King's garments, the spheres' bowing is seen with the eye, not only reasoned (line 4052). Bring it home. Right where you are sitting, in your own place, He is alone. Not somewhere else, in a higher world. Here.

**From inside.** Nearness. The unity is not far away.

**At the edge.** It reaches it: 'the essence of the Ein Sof can only be called yachid' (line 3912).

**Into the day.** Wherever you are when you read this, say quietly: here too, You alone.

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations

1. **Higher and lower unity §22: joining the two** (line 3408): *When he grasps both measurements, of the Ark and of ten cubits on each side, at once.* The book's goal in one picture: hold the world's full reality and its nothingness before Him at the same moment, neither erasing the other. *To hold it:* Hold a cup. Feel its size and weight, and at once know it takes no room before Him. Stay as long as both remain true to you.

2. **Achdus Hashem (3) §3: not an existence for itself (continuous creation, its reasons, the Baal Shem Tov, ayin and efes)** (line 1767): *But also because the creature's own essence is the word of God.* The Baal Shem Tov's step: the word that creates is not beside the creature but its very essence. From there, 'none besides You' becomes 'nothing but You'. *To hold it:* Before a blessing over bread, think: the word is making this now, and the word is what it is.

3. **Higher and lower unity §8: the higher and lower knowledge** (line 2679): *At the moment of action he acknowledges that the world is nothing and of no account.* It changes what the mind counts as real, from the child's touchable to the thinker's idea to Godliness alone, and gives a way to hold it while acting: acknowledge. *To hold it:* In the middle of a task, stop for one second and say: before You this is nothing, and I will do it well.

4. **Higher and lower unity §23 (second part): sea and land; two eyes; Jacob and Israel; the two challos; bones and flesh; seeing and gazing; above and below; revealed and inner Torah; witnesses** (line 3572): *The higher unity has no advantage over the lower, and they are exactly equal.* At the Shabbos table the ranking stops. Before the revealed Essence, the higher and lower unity are exactly equal. *To hold it:* Hold the two challos side by side before the blessing, one for the day you saw and one for the day you only acknowledged.

5. **Achdus Hashem (1) §1: idolatry and partnership in daily life (nature, livelihood, harm, anger, honor, pride)** (line 1313): *At the time of his anger, the faith left him.* The unity comes down to the hardest moment of an ordinary day. Anger is a lapse of faith that this, too, came from Him. *To hold it:* When anger rises, say before anything else: this, too, is from Him.

6. **Unity in mankind §4: its cause is bitul** (line 585): *One who is full of himself and proud cannot bear his fellow.* The cause of every unity between people is bitul. At its height, the insult is 'not opposed to me at all', because there is no self there to hit. *To hold it:* When provoked, be silent for one moment and remember there is less of you than you think.

7. **Higher and lower unity §18: everyone must still reach a little for the higher** (line 3208): *To come a tiny bit at least (even imaginatively).* An honest, reachable instruction: taste the higher unity a little, even in imagination, or the lower service is not true. *To hold it:* For one minute a day, picture yourself as sunlight inside the sun; then go about the day.

8. **Higher and lower unity §6: 'Va'ed', the lower unity, is eternity** (line 2580): *For the advantage of light comes precisely from darkness.* The lower rung reaches above the higher. Steady yielding in the dark of this world reaches the Eternal. *To hold it:* Keep one plain daily practice on purpose, because it is plain.

9. **Achdus Hashem (4) §3: natural things that go against the laws of nature** (line 2081): *And this opens our eyes and shows us that the true being of the stone is the 'nothing'.* A thrown stone, at the top of its rise, has neither weight nor rest and is still there. Its nature is a garment; its being is the 'nothing' that makes it. *To hold it:* Toss a pebble and watch the moment at the top.

10. **Higher and lower unity §9: both knowledges are true** (line 2723): *Kindness said, let it be created, for it is all full of kindness.* Truth said 'do not create'; Kindness said 'create', and both are right. The world's solidity is kindness, not a lie to escape. *To hold it:* When the world feels too heavy, say: Kindness said, let it be created.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

*Compared with the Chabad method as mapped in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`, and with the other books in this index. The differences are kept, not smoothed.*

**A map of distinctions more than a guided sitting.** The hisbonenus ontology (`Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`) describes a process: preparation, contemplation, deepening, arousal, drawing down, with duration as the most repeated instruction. This volume barely describes the process. It supplies the objects of contemplation and cuts them finely: four levels of 'no existence' (lines 1552-1865), four explanations of the higher and lower unity (lines 2297-2323), and pairs of 'source' and 'above source' that shift as one climbs (line 2340). Its one method sentence is about the two unities: the higher yielding comes 'of itself', the lower through contemplation (line 2353), or both through contemplation but the lower needing a further act of will (line 2354). A person uses this book to know what to hold, not how long to sit.

**Acknowledgment is a full way of holding the truth.** The ontology marks 'grasp without heart' as a state to be worked through, and the Gate of Unity (Mitteler Rebbe) asks to know, not only believe. This volume gives acknowledgment (hoda'ah) its own dignity. The lower unity is 'believing and acknowledging that it is the truth' without seeing it (lines 2378, 3469); the higher knowledge comes to most people only as acknowledgment 'at the moment of action' (line 2679); anyone, 'even the least', can acknowledge, 'as long as the soul is within me' (lines 3485, 3488), and acknowledgment can open into seeing, 'a hundred blessings' (line 3486). It even asks for a taste of the higher unity 'even imaginatively' (line 3208), close to the Tanya's permission to contemplate 'in imaginings'.

**Felt yielding is still 'something'.** The ontology keeps a disagreement open: R. Aharon of Strashelye defends felt arousal, the Mitteler Rebbe warns it coarsens without a prior decay of self, and the Rashab describes an arousal so absorbed that it is not felt as one's own. This volume leans hard toward the last two without closing the question. Yielding that comes from a person's grasp, feeling or even self-sacrifice is 'bitul of the something', because the yielding itself is a something (line 2407); the true one is from Godliness's side, as Rabbi Akiva's self-giving was taken into 'One' by a light from above (line 2416). The noise and excitement of the Shema belong to a something being set aside; the Amidah is 'a still, small voice' (lines 3092, 3099). Yet it also says the lower unity, because it is seen, moves a person more (line 3314). Both stand.

**The lower is not only a step on the way up.** The manual's ladder climbs to rung three and comes back down (L12-L14). The SPINE says the lower unity exists for the sake of the higher. This volume says that and its opposite. The lower unity reaches above the higher ('va'ed' belongs to Atik, which is yachid, line 2537); it refines what the higher only overpowers (line 3304); it is the person's own work, which reaches higher than a gift (line 3325); the purpose of creation is completed in it (line 3326); and one who contemplates only the higher may fall after prayer (line 3328). The return is not a concession. It is where the highest is reached.

**Hearing can outrank seeing.** The Gate of Unity asks for a flash seen 'as if by the eye', and the Tanya's chapter 33 asks to picture the unity. This volume records two views. In one, the higher unity is seeing and leaves no room for doubt (line 3137). In the more common one, the higher unity can only be heard, because the sefiros cannot be pictured (line 3170), and yet 'a sage is greater than a prophet' (line 3181) and 'the main thing is to have an ear that hears lofty things' (line 3188). It does not choose; neither does this reading.

**Other people are an object of contemplation.** The ontology's objects are the lights, the order of worlds, the unity, God's greatness and one's own soul. This volume makes human unity a road to God: people are one because man is 'like the One above' and because 'adam' is 'mah', yielding (lines 465, 476); unity among people is 'a vessel for Godliness' (line 738); loving a fellow is loving God's image and draws the Man above toward the man below (line 703). The Tanya's chapter 32 grounds love of Israel in one Father; this book adds the mechanics and the limits (the unity of the wicked draws blessing, but only outward and for a time, lines 800-806).

**A guard against melting everything together.** The Gate of Unity joins every detail to the simple unity. This volume insists that the joining keeps the boundaries. In holiness opposites join while each keeps its reality (lines 949-957); even when the simple light makes them one, the boundary remains and the mixture is only 'permitted' (lines 996-1001, 1020-1025); breaking walls between things is strife, and Korach saw the future truly and applied it too soon (lines 1026-1049). Contemplation of the unity is not permission to dissolve roles, laws or differences.

**Continuous creation is only the first rung.** The Tanya's Gate of Unity builds the unity on the word said into each thing every moment. This volume honors that, and deepens it with the Baal Shem Tov's 'the word is its essence' (line 1767), but places it plainly: within Chassidus, it is the lower unity (line 2276); it gives only 'bitul of the something' because the creating power is a limited power (line 2116); and above it stand the intent (Israel and Torah, line 2165) and the Essence, before whom nothing was made (line 1822). The manual already warns 'never teach one rung as the whole'; this book is the source of that warning.

**A modern compilation, and its limits here.** Unlike the other books in this index, this is a modern reference work (published 5779, 2019), under copyright, and available only as uncorrected OCR. This reading therefore quotes only short phrases, as the scan has them (for example 'shnagim' at line 1553 and 'mishtasvim' at line 2597), and puts everything else in its own words. The entries cite and explain other books rather than speaking in one master's voice, so the 'movement' of each unit is sometimes the editors' arrangement of many sources.
