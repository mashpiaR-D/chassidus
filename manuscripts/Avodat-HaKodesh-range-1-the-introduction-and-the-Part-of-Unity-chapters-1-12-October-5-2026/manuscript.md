## The shape of the range

Avodat HaKodesh ("the holy service") has four parts: Unity (what G-d is and how the sefirot are one with Him), Service (what the service of man does above), Purpose (why man and the world exist), and the Secrets of the Torah. The introduction states the whole book in one idea, and this report gives it the most room because the rest of the work unfolds it: **the soul came down as a stranger on earth to complete the will above; the service of man is a "need on high" (tzorech gavoah); by it the Name is made one.**

1. **The introduction** (1-13). The soul as a stranger in a far land. David asks for nothing for himself. The deed completes the thought. The mitzvot are a need on high. Moses finds G-d tying crowns.
2. **The beginning of the Part of Unity** (14-47). Before the world, He and His Name alone. Emanation is not change: a flame lit from a coal. Keter is His Name, not Ein Sof itself.
3. **The ten are His very G-dliness** (48-81). "He is them, and they are He." So we may pray through them, and must unify all of them. The offering is the drawing near of the powers.
4. **Why there had to be emanation** (82-121). Against the philosophers' chain of intellects: He alone made the world, from Himself. Ein Sof is complete, so He works "in limit without limit". We know the hidden One by His traits as we know the soul by the body.
5. **One light, many receivers** (122-139). "Know today and return it to your heart." The flame of two colors. The sun that melts wax and hardens clay. The Names differ only on the side of those who receive.

## The unity of G-d

**The ten are not beside Him; they are He.** The book's first great claim is that the sefirot are not created things, nor intellects, nor angels. They are "the very G-dliness":

> אצילות עשר ספירות שבארנו הוא עצם האלהות, וזהו ענין האצילות כי אין הנאצל נפרד מן המאציל, ואינו חוצה לו (Avodat HaKodesh 1:4:1; line 53)
> The emanation of the ten sefirot that we explained is the very essence of G-dliness. This is what emanation means: what is emanated is not separate from the One who emanates it, and not outside Him.

> אבל ההתפשטות הזה הוא בעצם השרש הנעלם בלתי נפרד ממנו עד שנאמר שהוא הם, והם הוא והכל אחד (Avodat HaKodesh 1:4:1; line 53)
> This spreading out is in the essence of the hidden Root, not separate from it, so that we say: He is they, and they are He, and all is one.

He brings the Zohar's words for it: the ten names "are His name, and He is them" (ואינון ושמיה והוא אינון, line 57), and the Zohar on "the Name of the Lord I call": "He alone is all, He is one without division ... all rise and are bound and are one in One, and all is He, and He is one" (וכלא הוא והוא חד, line 64).

**Emanation changes nothing in Him: a flame from a coal.** How can the One give forth ten without change? The flame is in the coal before it is seen; when it comes out, nothing new is in the flame, only in the eyes of the one who sees; and the coal loses nothing (line 23). The emanated "was not separated from the Emanator at all, but remained in the oneness and being it had" (line 26). It is "like lighting a candle from a candle" (כמדליק נר מנר, line 26). The Zohar's line closes it: "from it they came out, and in it they dwell" (מניה נפקו ... ביה יתבין, line 32).

**Before the world, He and His Name alone.** The book opens its Part of Unity with the saying of Pirkei deRabbi Eliezer: "before the world was created there was the Holy One and His Name alone" (line 20). The Name is Keter, "ancient as He is ancient, for the Name is not separate from the essence" (לפי שאין השם נבדל מן העצם, line 20). But Keter is not Ein Sof: Ein Sof has no name at all, no hint in Scripture, and cannot be counted (lines 37-46, 109).

**He made the world from nothing but Himself.** Against R. Yosef Albo, who read the sefirot as the philosophers' separate intellects, ibn Gabbai insists that no middle being made the world. "He alone created the world" (line 83). Then the core line:

> והכוונה הנעלמת בזה שלא ברא הקב"ה את העולם בשום דבר זולת עצמו ואמתו (Avodat HaKodesh 1:7:13; line 91)
> The hidden intent in this is that the Holy One did not create the world with anything other than Himself and His truth.

And His oneness does not change when the many come into being. On "He is One, and who can turn Him":

> שלא נאמר כי אחר שברא העולם יצא מן ההיות ההוא השוה ברבוי הנמצאים, אבל היה תמיד באחדות ההוא (Avodat HaKodesh 1:7:10; line 88)
> So that we should not say that after He created the world He left that even being because of the many creatures; rather He was always in that same oneness.

Why make many at all? To testify to the One. The many things are bound to each other until all rise to one simple Cause, "and so G-d brought the many out of His simple oneness to testify to His oneness" (להעיד באחדותו, line 89). The world is one great Man, and the closer things are to the Root, the more one they are (line 90).

*Ours.* This is very close to the Gate of Unity (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7): "I, the Lord, have not changed", the world adds nothing and changes nothing in Him. Ibn Gabbai reaches it through the sefirot. The Alter Rebbe reaches it through the letters of speech and the ray in the sun. F04, F05.

**Ein Sof is whole, so it acts "in limit without limit".** He brings R. Azriel of Gerona's answer to "why must there be sefirot if there is only Ein Sof?":

> אי"ן סוף הוא שלימות מבלי חסרון, ואם תאמר שיש לו כח בלי גבול, ואין לו כח בגבול אתה מחסר שלמותו (Avodat HaKodesh 1:8:2; line 93)
> Ein Sof is completeness without lack. If you say He has power without limit and has no power in limit, you make His completeness lacking.

So He is "in limit without limit" (בגבול מבלי גבול), and the first limit from Him is the sefirot (line 93). Of Ein Sof itself "one cannot say will, or desire, or intention, or thought, or speech, or deed, though there is nothing outside Him" (אע"פ שאין חוץ ממנו, line 95).

*Ours.* This is the source of the Chassidic "just as He has power of the infinite, so He has power of the finite" (Avodas HaKodesh is cited for it in Chabad; see the Rebbe Rashab's Samach Vav and the Tzemach Tzedek). Chassidus takes it further: the finite is not only a lesser power but the sign of the Essence, which alone can make something that feels itself (F05). The seed is here.

**Many names, one essence; the change is in the receiver.** "The change of names and the count of sefirot do not make plurality in G-dliness at all" (line 126). Before the world there was nothing to act upon, so He was called by nothing; after it, He is named by His acts as they reach different receivers (line 127). Then the pictures: the sun hardens clay and melts wax, blackens the washer's face and whitens the cloth, "and these changes are not from it" (line 128). Sunlight through ten colored glasses seems to change, "but the wise will know that the change is not from the sun's light" (line 129). Fire cooks, burns, blackens, whitens, melts and hardens, all by one heat (line 130).

> אך מצדו ית' אין רק טוב לא דבר רע (Avodat HaKodesh 1:12:2; line 127)
> But from His side there is only good, not a bad thing.

**"Know today and return it to your heart."** The chapter ends with the verse that the Tanya and the Gate of Unity will make the center of their teaching (F12). Ibn Gabbai reads "today" (היום) as the sun: from the sun, which does opposite things by one power, you learn that "the Lord is G-d", many acts, one Essence (line 128). His son R. Chaim adds two witnesses to oneness, the sun and the heart: look at the sun, and then look into your own heart, where two inclinations come from One (line 139). And the Zohar he quotes reads "your heart" (לבבך, with two beits) as the good and evil inclinations "included one in the other, and it is one; then you will find that the Lord is G-d" (line 123).

*Ours.* This is the same verse and the same move as Tanya ch. 20 and Gate of Unity ch. 6: take the unity out of the sky and into the heart. Ibn Gabbai uses the sun as the teaching picture; the Alter Rebbe uses the sun too, but the ray in the sun that is nothing outside it (S58). The two pictures fit: ibn Gabbai's sun shows why many acts do not mean many gods; the Alter Rebbe's ray shows why the world is not a thing apart.

## The need on high: what the introduction teaches (the core of the book)

**The soul is a stranger here, sent to finish a work.** The introduction opens with the soul before birth, cut "from the radiance of His glory", learning Torah above, then sent down into "this low and dark body". It longs to return like a bird that wandered from its nest (line 1). The advice:

> זאת העצה היעוצה לראות את עצמו כגר בארץ נכריה ולחגור בעוז הצדק מתניו לשוב אל אדוניו אשר שלחו לעשות מעשהו ולעבוד עבודתו (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 1; line 1)
> This is the counsel given: to see himself as a stranger in a foreign land, and to gird his loins with strength of righteousness to return to his Master, who sent him to do His work and serve His service.

And the Shechinah (the Presence) is also a wandering bird, "as if a stranger in another land, longing to return to her place; but a prisoner cannot free himself from prison, for He gave the key into the hand of Israel, and that is teshuvah" (line 1). The soul has power while it dwells below "to awaken the higher things".

**David asked nothing for himself.** "I am a stranger in the land; do not hide Your mitzvot from me" (Ps. 119:19). "Deal kindly with Your servant, that I may live and keep Your word" means: the only reward I ask for my service is to keep Your word, "as the reward of a mitzvah is a mitzvah" (line 1).

> ולא יפנה אל זולת זה ולא ישאל ולא ינסה על צרכי עצמך כלל כי אם כל כוונתו בצרכי גבוה כי כן דרך העובר השלם (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 1; line 1)
> He will turn to nothing but this, and will not ask or try for his own needs at all; all his intention is for the needs on high, for this is the way of the whole servant.

"For he knew that to this end the whole ones turn, for all ends end in it, for it is the completion of the higher will" (כי זה השלמת הרצון העליון, line 1).

**The world was made as the place of service; the deed completes the thought.** This is the heart of the book's answer to "why is there a world":

> ואמנם לא תתכן העבודה ולא תושלם כוונתה כי אם בארץ הלזו והייתי גר בתוכה בי לזה נברא זה העולם להיותו מקו' העבודה (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 3; line 3)
> The service cannot be done, and its intent cannot be completed, except in this land where I am a stranger; for this world was created for this, to be the place of service.

> להשלים הרצון העליון אשר א"א להשלימו כי אם בו שהוא עולם המעשה ... וזה סוד והוצאת המחשבה לידי מעשה וידוע כי המעשה הוא סוף המחשב' ותשלומה (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 3; line 3)
> To complete the higher will, which cannot be completed except in it, for it is the world of deed ... and this is the secret of bringing thought into deed; it is known that the deed is the end of the thought and its completion.

> וכשהעובד השלם עובד על זה הדרך הנה הוא מייחד ראש המחשב' עם סופה (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 3; line 3)
> And when the whole servant serves in this way, he unites the head of the thought with its end.

In the higher thought, good and evil were mixed; the good was the main thing and evil the stranger. In the world of deed it is reversed: evil is the settled citizen and good the stranger (line 3). So the righteous man feels a stranger here, and the wicked man feels at home. "By service with the fitting intention the great Name is unified in its glory, and that is the true love, which is the unity" (line 3).

**We do not know how; we know that it does.** "Though our grasp is too short to find a reason and to know how this unity comes from this Torah and these mitzvot, we know faithfully that they are the great cause of it" (line 3). And Kohelet says the same: "a man cannot find out the work that G-d has done", that is, he will not know "by what cause, through the heart's intention in service, the great Name is unified" (line 4).

**Below awakens above.** "G-d gave to the sons of man" the power of awakening and unity from below "to awaken its counterpart above ... for the answer of the upper ones depends on the awakening of the lower ones" (כי עניית העליוני' תלוי בהתעוררות התחתוני', line 4).

**The mitzvot are a need on high, not only a need of man.** This is the book's name for its own teaching:

> והוא שישתדל לצורך גבוה שהוא הייחוד הנזכר בלבד לא לצרכו (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 5; line 5)
> That he should work for the need on high, which is that unity, alone, not for his own need.

"And this is the fruit of the Torah and its mitzvot, and the intent of creation" (וכוונת הבריאה, line 5). The unity above is the "eating and drinking" of Kohelet: the higher light shining, the lamps lit, the upper faces glad (line 5). The proofs: "all that Israel treasure of mitzvot and good deeds, they treasure for their Father in heaven"; "the Holy One longs for the prayer of the righteous"; "the Holy One longs for the priestly blessing"; and Rashi on "they shall put My Name on the children of Israel": "He did not make it a need of Israel, but a need of the Omnipresent" (ולא עשאה צורך ישראל אלא צורך המקום, line 5). The kabbalists say "the Presence below is a need on high":

> ודומה לזה הוא שאמרו חכמי האמת שכינה בתחתונים צורך גבוה (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 6; line 6)
> Like this is what the sages of truth said: the Presence among the lower ones is a need on high.

When Israel do His will and learn Torah and do mitzvot for their own sake, "they unify His great Name and bring His powers near to one another and bind them in a strong bond, one unity, up to Ein Sof", and the fruit is that the Presence dwells among them below (line 6). "By this the worlds are weighed as one and united as one, to testify to one Master" (line 6). When Israel sin, they "separate the Chief of the world from the Bride" and the Presence withdraws. "When Israel do the will of the Omnipresent they add strength to the Power above ... and when they do not, as it were they weaken the great Power above" (line 6).

**Moses finds G-d tying crowns: "you should have helped Me".** The Talmud tells that Moses went up and found the Holy One tying crowns on the letters, and did not greet Him. "Is there no peace in your town?" "Does a servant greet his master?" "You should have helped Me." Ibn Gabbai reads it as the secret itself: no mind could guess "that the repair above is handed over to the lower ones, and that the services and prayers are a need on high, had the Holy One not revealed this wondrous secret to the master of the prophets, until he wondered at it" (line 6). "You should have helped Me" means: "what the Lord asks of you is only to help Him in His work" (line 6). The crowns are the bond of the letters, the powers of the Name, and the Torah is woven of letters, so "the unity that is a need on high depends on the study and keeping of the Torah" (line 6).

**The whole world was made for this.** "Know the G-d of your father and serve Him" means to join Tiferet (the beauty, the Holy One) to the last hei of the Name (the Shechinah), "for this is the goal of service and the fruit of the whole Torah" (line 7):

> וזה כל האדם וכל העול' כלו לא נברא אלא לצוות לזה להשלימו (Avodat HaKodesh, Introduction 7; line 7)
> And this is the whole of man, and the whole world was only created to accompany this, to complete it.

"The man who does this makes the Presence dwell on earth and unites all the worlds, and the glory of the Omnipresent grows because of him, and he gladdens the Omnipresent" (line 7). The last word of the introduction is the Song of Songs: "I am my Beloved's and His desire is for me": the Bride's longing to rise to Him "depends on the awakening of the lower ones", and His longing is for children who keep His mitzvot (line 13).

*Ours.* This is the teaching the owner asked about, and it is the book's center. Three things in it matter for the guide.

1. **Service matters to G-d.** Most religious thought says G-d needs nothing and the mitzvot are for us. Ibn Gabbai says both are true, and the second truth is deeper: the mitzvot are a "need on high". G-d wanted to need us. That is the "desire for a dwelling below" of the Midrash, which Chassidus makes its center (F07, S82). The Alter Rebbe in Tanya ch. 36 says the purpose of creation is that "the Holy One desired to have a dwelling in the lower worlds", and the Tzemach Tzedek and the Rebbe Rashab cite Avodat HaKodesh by name for "avodah tzorech gavoah". Ibn Gabbai is one of the main bridges from the Zohar to the Tanya on this.
2. **The deed is the end of the thought.** "The deed is the end of the thought and its completion"; the whole servant "unites the head of the thought with its end". This is the Chassidic "the end of the deed was first in thought" (סוף מעשה במחשבה תחלה) and the teaching that the lowest is the aim (F07, F10, S81).
3. **The servant asks nothing for himself.** David's prayer asks for nothing but to keep G-d's word. This is the Chassidic "not for himself" in prayer (לא לעצמו) and the Tanya's service "to give pleasure above" (לעשות נחת רוח). Ibn Gabbai even quotes "to do nachat ruach to his Maker" (line 7).

Where does Chassidus part from him? See IBN-GABBAI.md. In short: ibn Gabbai's "need on high" is a need of the sefirot, a unity of Tiferet and Malchut that can be made or broken. Chassidus holds it, and then roots it in the Essence above the sefirot, whose desire "has no reason" (Samach Vav). In ibn Gabbai the service repairs; in the Rebbe Rashab it reveals the Essence.

## States of consciousness

**A stranger in a foreign land.** From inside: this world is real and good, but not home. The person lives here on purpose, sent, with a work to finish, and his heart looks toward where he came from (line 1). It is not despair of the world; the soul is here because the work can only be done here (line 3). A proposed state: **the sent stranger** (JSON in IBN-GABBAI.md). It sits near S13 (the unnamed longing) and gives that longing a meaning: the pull home is the soul's memory of its task.

**Asking nothing for oneself.** The whole servant's prayer turns from his needs to the need on high (line 1). From inside: a quiet turning of the question from "what do I need?" to "what does He want done?" A proposed state: **serving the need on high**. It is close to S56 (the self quieted) and S60 (the essential bond), and it is the core of what Chassidus calls prayer "for the Shechinah" rather than for oneself.

**The joy above.** "One who spends his days in this", in knowing G-d and His unity, "gladdens the higher heart" (משמח את הלב העליון, line 3), and the wisdom and knowledge the soul had above bring it joy and sweetness when it learns them below (line 3). The joy of study is the soul's remembering.

**"Look at the flame."** The Zohar passage ibn Gabbai quotes is a door to a state: "whoever wants to know the wisdom of the holy unity, let him look at the flame that rises from a coal or a burning lamp" (line 124). There are two lights in the flame: a white light above that never changes, and a blue or black light below that holds to the wick and turns red at times. The blue joins above to the white and below to the wick. *Ours.* A state and a technique at once (see below): the steady white above the changing colors. It fits S58 (a ray in the sun) and S63 (the world held).

## Meditation and practice

**The kavanah of Shema: unify all ten, and the ten with their Root.** The Shema is "the true unity that we were commanded, the root of faith, and the true service" (line 73). The four letters of the Name hold all ten sefirot; the yud with its thorn points to the Root, and "just as the yud is not outside its thorn, nor the thorn outside it", so the Root and the emanated are one (line 73). The Zohar: "I will exalt Him above all ... the one who knows how to unify the holy Name, for that is the service of the Holy One" (line 73). The ayin of "Shema" and the dalet of "Echad" are large: together they spell "witness" (עד), for He is the witness whether the one who reads unifies His Name (line 122).

**Do not pray to one trait, and do not pray to the Root without the traits.** This is the book's key guard for prayer, and it has two sides:

> המכוין וקורא למדה מן המדות אם לא ביחוד (כלים) [כלם] קצוץ נטיעות והריסה, ועם יחוד כלם צריך לכוין אל הכח העליון ליחד הענף בשרשו והנאצל במאציל (Avodat HaKodesh 1:5:6; line 71)
> One who directs and calls to one of the traits, if not in the unity of all of them, is a cutter of shoots and a destroyer; and with the unity of all of them he must direct to the higher Power, to unite the branch with its root and the emanated with the Emanator.

> וגם המכוין אל השרש לבדו בזולת מדותיו מכחיש פמליא של מעלה וקוצץ נטיעות (Avodat HaKodesh 1:5:6; line 71)
> And also one who directs to the Root alone, without His traits, denies the household above and cuts the shoots.

*Ours.* A clear rule for any unity meditation: do not stop at one face (only His kindness, only His judgment), and do not leap over the faces to an empty Beyond. Hold the many as one, and the one as present in the many. This is exactly the Chassidic balance of the higher and lower unity (F02, S62 with S63).

**Why the prayer may go through the traits at all.** Only because they are G-d Himself. "If the emanation were separate from the Emanator and not He Himself, how would it be allowed to pray and direct to what is not G-d? One who asks or prays to any power or angel is an idol worshipper and a cutter of shoots" (line 67). The Yerushalmi: "if trouble comes, do not call to Michael or Gabriel, but cry to Him and He will answer" (line 81).

**The offering as the drawing near of the powers.** "Korban" (offering) is from "karov", near: "the holy crowns that all draw near together and bind one to another until they all become one, in a complete unity, to repair the holy Name" (line 75). When all is joined, "then the higher soul of all awakens and enters them all and shines on all ... and all rises to Ein Sof and is bound in one bond" (line 77). The incense "binds the knot of faith" (line 78). *Ours.* After the Temple, this is prayer. The guide can use it as a picture of what a sitting does: many scattered powers of the soul drawn near to each other and then to Him.

**The flame meditation.** From the Zohar (line 124), as ibn Gabbai gives it: look at a burning candle. See the white light above, which does not change. See the blue or dark light below it, which holds to the wick, changes color, and joins the white above. The changing is from the wick, not from the light (line 125). *Ours.* Proposed technique: **the flame of two lights** (JSON). It teaches without words that the One does not change when His acts change, and that what is below (the wick, the body, the deed) is what lets the light hold. It pairs with technique 9 (the picture, and where it breaks) and the Tanya's candle of the soul.

**Know by looking at the sun, then at your heart.** "Know today": the sun does many opposite things by one power; "and return it to your heart": in you there are two inclinations and one heart (lines 128, 139). *Ours.* Proposed technique: **two witnesses**. First look outward at one cause with many effects; then inward, at the two pulls in you, and see that one heart holds both. It is a gentle bridge from F12 (knowing the unity) to S34 (two voices).

**Trust comes from knowing His oneness.** On "that your trust be in the Lord, I have made it known to you today": "from knowing Him and His unity, it follows that your trust will be in Him" (line 133). And "even if he sees acts that differ or are opposite coming from Him", he should not think this makes change in Him. *Ours.* A real teaching for suffering: the seeming harsh act and the seeming kind act come from one good Source; the difference is in the receiving. It is S84 (trust) grounded in unity.

**Hear it from the wise; say it with mouth and heart together.** The wisdom of G-dliness "is above the mind of man" and must be heard "from its knowers" (line 133). When one comes to unify the Name, "what he says with his lips and the intention of the heart" should be joined "like a flame bound to a coal" (line 133).

## The big human questions

**Why do we exist?** To finish the higher will in the world of deed: to join the head of the thought to its end (line 3). "The whole world was only created to accompany this" (line 7).

**Why do we have to die, and why do we want children?** Man was meant to live forever so that his service and unity would never stop. When death came, G-d put into nature the longing for a son, a student, or a book, so that "the thread of service and unity" would not be cut (line 9). Ibn Gabbai writes his book partly for that reason (lines 8-12).

**What happens to the soul-seed a sinner wasted?** It is not lost: "the good and whole man who completed the intent ... merits the imprint in the soul of the sinner who did not bring it to light", as the sages say, "he takes his portion and his fellow's portion in the Garden of Eden" (line 4).

**Is there evil from Him?** "From His side there is only good" (line 127). "No evil comes down from heaven" (line 127). The sun heals the righteous and burns the wicked with one light (line 128).

**Love and joy.** "The true love is the unity" (line 3). Love G-d "with all your heart, with both your inclinations": the service of the whole one is "to include the evil in the good until it all turns to good" (line 4). This is F11 (darkness turned to light) in a pre-Lurianic voice.

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 8-12 (the author's reasons for writing, sons and students and books), 28-32 (the counting of Chochmah, Binah and Da'at), 34-47 (whether Keter is Ein Sof, R. Hai Gaon on the three hidden lights) and 83-87 (the dates of the creation of angels) were read in full. They support the doctrine of unity above but add no state or practice.
