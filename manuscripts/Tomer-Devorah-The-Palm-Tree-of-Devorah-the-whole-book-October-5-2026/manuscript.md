---
Tomer Devorah is the Ramak's small book of how to live. The Pardes maps God's ten sefirot. Tomer Devorah turns each one into a way of treating people, starting with the thirteen attributes of mercy (Micah 7:18-20). Its first sentence is the whole program: "a person should make himself like his Maker" (האדם ראוי שיתדמה לקונו, line 1). A person who looks like the image in body but not in deeds is "a handsome form with ugly deeds" (צורה נאה ומעשים כעורים, line 1), "for the essence of the upper image and likeness is His *actions*" (שהרי עיקר הצלם והדמות העליון הן פעלותיו, line 1).

Chapter 1 covers the thirteen attributes of Keter, each first as God's way and then as ours. Chapter 2 covers the limbs of Keter (forehead, ears, eyes, nose, face, mouth) and humility, with a "poultice" for pride. Chapters 3 to 9 cover Chochmah, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach-Hod-Yesod and Malchut. Chapter 10 is a daily schedule: which level to cleave to at night, at dawn, at prayer, at learning, at the meal and at evening.

This is the source of the map's CATALOGUE A3 (Imitatio Dei). This report reads it for what the guide can use beyond A3.

Every Hebrew line below was checked with `find_he.py verify` and came back FOUND near the line given (82 phrases checked, 82 found; 2 Tanya lines, 2 found). The text is vocalized. Quotations are printed without vowel points and with the text's own spelling (it writes פעלותיו, כאלו, אפלו, etc.).

## 2. The unity of God in this book

Tomer Devorah does not argue about the unity. It assumes it and makes it felt. Two passages carry it.

**He is feeding the hand that sins against Him.** The first attribute, "Who is a God like You", shows the Holy One as "an insulted King" (הקדוש ברוך הוא מלך נעלב, line 2). "There is no moment in which a person is not fed and kept in being by a power from above that flows onto him" (ועוד אין רגע שלא יהיה האדם נזון ומתקים מכח עליון השופע עליו, line 2). So when a person sins, he sins with God's power flowing into his limbs at that very moment. "He uses that power at that very moment in sin and transgression and angering, and the Holy One bears it" (והוא מוציא אותו כח באותו רגע בחטא ועון ומכעיס והקדוש ברוך הוא סובל, line 2). He could have said, "sin with your own, not with Mine" (תחטא בשלך לא בשלי, line 2), and He does not. This is the unity as constant giving: nothing I do, even against Him, happens apart from His flow.

**He is the root of all souls.** In the fear of Malchut, the third thought is that He is "the root of all souls, and all are rooted in His sefirot" (lines 106-107), so a sinner soils the King's own palace. Souls "are included together, in this one is a part of that one" (מפני שהנשמות כלולות יחד יש בזה חלק זה ובזה חלק זה, line 10). Your neighbor "is truly you" (שהרי הוא הוא ממש, line 10). The unity of God shows up as the unity of people.

**Humility is Ayin.** The core of humility is "that he find in himself no worth at all, but think that he is the Ayin" (שלא ימצא בעצמו ערך כלל, אלא יחשב שהוא האין, line 47), as Keter "sees itself as nothing before its Emanator" (line 47). The Pardes named Keter "Ayin". Here the person takes on the same stance.

## 3. States of consciousness

**Held while I fall (RK-S26, proposed).** From the insulted King (line 2). Inside: I catch myself in the middle of doing wrong and see that the strength I am using is His, given to me right now. He has not pulled it back. I am held even here. It does not excuse me. It melts me. How to enter: in a moment of wrongdoing, or right after, stop and say: "You are giving me this very breath and this very hand." Then let the shame and the softness come together. Near S65 (Given right now) and S63 (The world held). What is new is seeing it in the act of sin, where it cuts deepest. The same line leads to the shame of line 8: "from here a person should be ashamed to sin again, for the King Himself washes the filth of his garments" (ומכאן יתביש האדם לשוב לחטא שהרי המלך בעצמו רוחץ לכלוך בגדיו, line 8).

**Their pain reaches Him (named).** "In all their trouble He is troubled": "their pain reaches the Wonder above" (שצערם מגיע לפלא העליון, line 9). And so for us: one should feel bad over another's harm "as if he himself were in that pain" (וירע לו ממנו כאלו הוא ממש היה שרוי באותו צער, line 10). Near S54 (Compassion for the soul). The person in pain is not alone. It is felt at the top.

**The returner loved more (named).** When God is reconciled, it is not like a human who takes a friend back a little. The returner is closer than before (lines 15-16), and so should we be with one who wronged us and asks our love: "he is to me like a returner" (הרי הוא לי כבעלי תשובה, line 17). Near S48 (Returning).

**Glad of insult (named, with a guard).** The third drug of the humility poultice: the best suffering is to be shamed, for it costs no health or bread, "and so when the insults come, he will rejoice in them" (ובזה כשיבאו העלבונות עליו ישמח בהם, line 51). This is a real state in the tradition. For the guide it needs a guard. It is for one whose pride is the problem, never for one already crushed (S11).

**Fear that is not of punishment (named).** "If he fears suffering, death, or Gehinnom, this is the fear of the outer forces" (הרי זו יראת החצונים, line 101). True fear has four thoughts: His greatness ("a man fears a lion... why not the great King?", ולמה לא יירא מן המלך הגדול, line 103); His constant watching (והאדם תמיד לפני הבורא ועינו פקוחה על כל דרכיו, line 105); that He is the root of all souls; and that sin separates "the King's desire from the Queen" (להפריד חשק המלך מן המלכה, line 109). Near S45 and S46 (Lower and higher awe), with the Ramak's sharp line between fear of punishment and fear of Him.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**The thirteen for people (RK-T30, proposed).** The Ramak's own method: "these thirteen attributes should not leave the eyes of the mind, and the verse should not leave his mouth", so that when a situation calls for one of them "he will remember and say: this thing depends on such-and-such attribute; I do not want to move from it" (הרי דבר זה תלוי במדה פלונית איני רוצה לזוז ממנה, line 31). The promise: "as a person behaves below, so he merits to open for himself that upper attribute" (כך יזכה לפתח לו מדה עליונה מלמעלה ממש, line 31). What it is for: carrying mercy into real conflicts. How: learn Micah 7:18-20 by heart. When someone wrongs you, ask which phrase fits (bearing, not holding anger forever, remembering the good) and say it inside as a choice. Guard: this is for your own conduct, not a demand you make on others. Extends A3.

**Enough for me in this one good (RK-T31, proposed).** "For He desires kindness": God spares even the guilty when they do kindness to each other. So when someone wrongs you, if there is in him one good side, "let that side be enough to remove his anger, and his heart will be reconciled, and he will say: enough for me in this good that he has" (די לי בטובה זו שיש לו, line 14). The Talmud's line about wives is the model: "it is enough that they raise our children". What it is for: letting go of anger toward a real person. How: name the person, name the hurt honestly, then find one good thing he does for anyone, and say "enough for me in this". Guard: this is not denial of harm and not staying in danger. It softens the heart. It does not remove the need for safety or justice.

**Remember them as children (RK-T32, proposed).** When you find no merit at all: "let him say, there was a time before they sinned... and remember the good they did in their childhood" (line 30). "And by this there will be no person who is not worthy of being done good to, prayed for, and pitied" (ובזה לא ימצא אדם שאינו ראוי להטיבו ולהתפלל על שלומו ולרחם עליו, line 30). With it goes "at the end of the day they are children of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob" (line 28). For the guide it is universal (D28): every person was once a child, and every person is someone's child. How: picture the hard person at five years old, and pray one line for his good. Near S54.

**The humility poultice and the drink (RK-T33, proposed).** The Ramak: "I found a remedy... a poultice made of three drugs" (והוא תחבשת הנעשה משלשה סמים, line 48). First, flee honor. Second, see your own lowliness honestly (what you do not know, your weakness, your body's needs). Third, think of your sins and welcome shame as the gentlest correction (line 51). Then "a very good drink": honor every creature as the Maker's craft, since "if he despises them, he touches the honor of their Maker" (ואלו יבזה אותם חס ושלום נוגע בכבוד יוצרם, line 53). And love all people, "even the wicked he will love in his heart" (ואפלו הרשעים יאהב אתם בלבו, line 54), saying "would that they were righteous, returning" (מי יתן ויהיו אלו צדיקים שבים בתשובה, line 54), and "why should I hate one whom the Holy One loves?" (ולמה אשנא מי שהקדוש ברוך הוא אוהב, line 54). What it is for: pride, contempt, harshness. How: once a day, one small act of fleeing honor and one honest look at your limits. When you see a poor or despised person, say "if he were dressed like a rich man, I would be glad of his company". Guard: the poultice is for pride. If it breeds sadness or self-hatred, stop and use the drink alone (honoring others and loving them). The Ramak says: "accustom himself to these traits little by little" (להרגיל עצמו באלו המדות מעט מעט, line 46).

**Bind the hard to kindness (RK-T34, proposed).** Love of God is the gate to Chesed. It means taking both good and suffering as love, "faithful are the wounds of a lover" (נאמנים פצעי אוהב, line 72). The model is Nachum Ish Gam Zu, who said "this too is for good" (גם זו לטובה, line 72), binding the hard thing to Chesed: "he set his mind on the good side in that attribute and hid its judgments" (והיה שם דעתו אל צד הטוב במדה ההיא ומסתיר דיניה, line 72). What it is for: meeting hardship without letting it cut you off from love. How: when something hard happens, say "this too is for good" and look for the thread of kindness in it, without pretending it is not hard. Guard: said about your own life, never to someone else in pain. Near S84 (Trust) and RK-S18 (Salt in the bitter).

**The day of the sefirot (RK-T35, proposed).** Chapter 10, from the Zohar: "to know which sefirah rules and bind oneself to it" (line 120). - At bedtime, sleep is "like death" (השנה היא כעין מיתה, line 121). Accept the yoke of Heaven in bed "with intent of the heart" (ויקבל על מלכות שמים שלמה בכונת הלב, line 121). - At midnight, rise, wash your hands, and learn. - At dawn, enter the house of prayer saying "and I, through Your great kindness, will come" (ואני ברב חסדך אבוא, line 122): kindness (Abraham), bowing, "bending his stature before judgment" (לכפף קומתו נגד מדת הדין, line 125) (Isaac), and awe (Jacob). Do this "in thought, speech and action" (מחשבה דבור ומעשה, line 127). - Morning prayer binds you to Chesed. Daytime learning binds you to Tiferet. - The meal is kindness to your animal soul: "the righteous man knows the soul of his beast" (יודע צדיק נפש בהמתו, line 129). - Mincha binds you to Gevurah. In the evening, Malchut: accept the yoke again. - "Always cleaving to the light that rules" (ולעולם דבק באור השולט, line 131).

What it is for: letting the day's hours carry you through the whole tree. How: pick two anchors to start, bedtime and entering prayer, and add one more each month. Guard: midnight rising only if health allows. Near RK-T19 (After midnight).

**Think return every day.** "One who thinks of return all his days causes Binah to shine in all his days" (ומי שמהרהר תשובה כל ימיו גורם שתאיר הבינה בכל ימיו, line 69). "He must think of it every day and do some return" (צריך שבכל יום ויום יהרהר בה ויעשה תשובה בצד מה, line 71). This fits "cheshbon". The Ramak's reason why it is never hopeless is Cain's: "do not think that because you are from the side of evil you have no repair; that is a lie" (שאין לך תקנה, זה שקר, line 70), "for every upper bitterness has a sweet root" (שכל מר עליון שרשו מתוק, line 70).

**Eyes open to the poor, face shining.** "When he sees the trouble of a poor man he will not shut his eyes at all" (וכשיראה בצרת עני לא יעצים עיניו כלל, line 39); "receive every person with a pleasant face" (ויקבל כל אדם בסבר פנים יפות, line 41); "not hold back from speaking good about everyone" (שלא יחשה מלדבר טובה על הכל, line 42). These are the Keter limbs as daily habits. They fit "act".

**Two faces.** Chochmah has two faces, and so should a person: "his seclusion with his Maker, to add to his wisdom and repair it" (הוא התבודדותו בקונו כדי להוסיף בחכמתו ולתקנה, line 60), and teaching others, each according to what he can bear (line 62). This is one of the Ramak's clearest words for hitbodedut, and it balances inward time with giving.

## 5. The big human questions

**How should I treat someone who hurt me?** Bear it (line 5). Do not hold anger forever. "It is a mitzvah to draw him near in love, perhaps it will help" (אלא מצוה לקרב אותו באהבה אולי יועיל בדרך זו, line 12). Press down the bad and forget it, and keep the good "arranged before him always" (ותהיה הטובה סדורה תמיד לפניו, line 19). "Even if he is a wicked man crushed by suffering, do not hate him" (אפלו שיהיה רשע מדכא ביסורין אל ישנאהו, line 22), "and do not say, his sin caused it" (ואל יאמר עונו גרם לו, line 22).

**Do I have to fix what others broke?** Yes. "Let him not say: shall I repair what so-and-so sinned?", because God Himself, "not through a messenger" (אין המחילה על ידי שליח, line 6), washes our filth (line 7).

**What about animals and plants?** Rebbi said to a calf fleeing slaughter "go, for this you were made" (זיל לכך נוצרת, line 67) and suffered. When he spared the weasels ("His mercies are on all His works") the suffering left. So "do not uproot a plant except for need" (ולא יעקר הצומח אלא לצרך, line 67), and kill only for need, with a checked knife. The only license is to raise them, "to harm in order to benefit" (לחוב על מנת לזכות, line 68).

**What is the right way to be poor or rich?** Always stand before God "as a poor man asking and pleading" (וישים עצמו תמיד כעני ויעמיד עצמו לפני קונו כדל שואל ומתחנן, line 99). Even a rich man should think "he has nothing but the bread he eats" (שאין לו כל דבר אלא הלחם אשר אכל, line 99). Family and wealth go with him only to the grave.

**Why is anger so bad?** Because it wakes the harsh powers above: "from here see how despicable anger is" (ומכאן יראה כמה מגנה הכעס, line 85). Even anger for Heaven's sake blocks the light of Keter (line 45).

**Should a scholar look down on the simple?** No. "Whoever lowers himself in words of Torah causes Tiferet to come down" (כל המשפיל עצמו בדברי תורה גורם אל התפארת שתרד, line 87). "His mind should be mixed with people" (יהיה דעתו מערבת עם הבריות, line 90). "Always admit the truth" (ולעולם יודה על האמת, line 92). "Learn from everyone" (מכל מלמדי השכלתי, line 95).

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Souls included in each other, neighbor as self.** Tomer Devorah line 10 (souls include each other, "he is truly you") is Tanya chapter 32's ground for loving one's fellow: "therefore all Israel are called truly brothers, from the root of their souls in the one God" (ולכן נקראו כל ישראל אחים ממש, מצד שורש נפשם בה׳ אחד, Tanya line 466). The Tanya adds that only those who make the soul primary can love truly. The Ramak adds the mechanism: each holds a part of the other, and so each is guarantor for the other.
- **Loving the wicked.** Tomer Devorah line 54 ("even the wicked he will love in his heart... would that they return") and Tanya chapter 32: "even those far from God's Torah... one must draw them with thick ropes of love" (שאף הרחוקים מתורת ה׳ ועבודתו, ולכן נקראים בשם בריות בעלמא – צריך למשכן בחבלי עבותות אהבה, Tanya line 469). Full agreement. The Tanya (as the Ramak does elsewhere) limits hatred to one's close friend who would not return after rebuke. Tomer Devorah's tone is warmer still.
- **The insulted King and "ein od".** Line 2 ("no moment in which a person is not fed from above") is the moral face of the Gate of Unity's constant creation. The Ramak draws from it patience and shame. The Tanya draws from it bittul: if every moment is given, the created is nothing apart from the Giver. Same fact, two fruits.
- **Humility as Ayin.** Line 47 ("think that he is the Ayin, as Keter sees itself as nothing before its Emanator") is the source of the Chassidic ideal of bittul, "I am nothing". The Maggid and the Alter Rebbe build a path on it. The Ramak's version is more ethical and includes the "poultice" of welcoming insult. Chabad stresses bittul as joy (S50), which is a guard against the poultice turning into self-hatred.
- **The day of the sefirot.** Chapter 10's daily cycle (Chesed at dawn, Tiferet in learning, Gevurah at mincha, Malchut at night) has no direct Chabad parallel. Chabad organizes the day by avodah (hisbonenus before prayer, Torah after, "the weekday: sorting"). It is a Pardes-style structure that the guide can offer as an option.
- **Tzimtzum.** Not addressed. Tomer Devorah is conduct, not cosmology.
