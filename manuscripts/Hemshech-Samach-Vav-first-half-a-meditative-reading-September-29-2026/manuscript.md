## 1. The book as one meditative journey

Hemshech Samach Vav is not a book you read once. It is a year of Shabbat and festival talks, given by the Rebbe Rashab from Rosh Hashanah 5666 (1905) onward, that keep circling one question until the reader is changed by it. This half of it walks through forty-five maamarim, from the first Rosh Hashanah to the Shemini Atzeret a year later. It asks for a particular kind of contemplation: slow, exact thinking about how God's light comes down into the worlds, pushed so far that the mind finds its own edge, and then a turn away from thinking toward something plainer and deeper: the will, the yoke, the deed, the whisper.

It opens by taking away every reason for why the world exists, until only a desire with no reason is left (line 208), and that desire turns out to be the reason for your own life. The first autumn teaches the mind to climb and to admit it never arrives. The higher it goes, the farther the Essence is (line 368). The only vessel for the Infinite is one who is "nothing" (line 546). The deepest thing in a person, like the deepest thing in God, is a will that has no reason (line 596), and a Jew is bound to Him "in one knot" (line 708).

The winter turns inward. Re'uta deliba, the heart's plain will, grasps what no thought grasps (line 897). In every Jew there is a will that nothing wears away (line 996). Hard questions become a road to truth (line 1242). There is a joy of the soul itself that has nothing to do with understanding (line 1354), and a narrow place that becomes a launching place (line 1489). The good feeling that comes from nowhere is only a preface to real work in mind and heart (line 1674). And a line runs through the whole season: it is easier to fly up to God than to come back down and keep His Torah here (line 2016).

The spring empties the mind of its best words. Even "Ein Sof" names His light and not Him: "in truth You are truly alone" (line 2299). His hiding is not weakness but His chief power (line 2503), and at its root the hiding itself will shine (line 2947). By Shavuot the book is teaching what to do when nothing opens: like the child whose father hides, run after Him along His paths without seeing His face (line 3255).

The summer is the book's great surprise. It sets three ways of serving side by side: the son who wants what his Father wants, the trusted servant who serves out of love, and the plain servant who carries a yoke against his nature and feels nothing. Step by step it shows that the plain servant, who "is not a being of his own" (line 3999), reaches highest. What he makes by his own toil is new, and a new light comes down from the Essence through it (line 4043). The low earth can make new things when the high heavens cannot, because "their end is wedged in their beginning" (line 4172). The practical mitzvah came down so low because it comes from the very Essence (line 4250).

The last maamarim, for the High Holidays and Sukkot of 5667, gather all this into bitul, self-giving. The refining done from below makes the lower thing itself God's home (line 4290). The returning light is stronger than the light that comes down (line 4351). The sukkah is the nothingness of Keter, which should be settled, not passing (line 4472). And this half ends in the middle of Shemini Atzeret with an honest test. Self-giving that comes from contemplation fades when the contemplation stops (line 4486); the true kind is silent, like a man who can only whisper before a king.

So the road runs from the mind's edge to the heart's plain will, from the heights down into the deed, and from loud feeling to quiet self-giving. It asks for thinking through, picturing, and prayer. Most of all it asks for the yoke taken on without waiting to feel anything, and it says that this is not the bottom of the ladder but where the ladder's top was always hidden.

## Maamar 1: Yom Tov shel Rosh Hashanah (Rosh Hashanah 5666): why there is a world at all (lines 75-246)

**The movement.** It takes away, one by one, the good reasons a mind gives for why the world exists, until only a desire is left that has no reason. On the book's first page the mind is brought to its edge, and the edge turns out to be the reason for your own life.

> ואין אנו יודעים טעם שכלי מפני מה נשתוקק
> *And we do not know a reason of the mind for why He desired it.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The maamar begins with the shofar, a plain cry. Then it asks the largest question there is: why are there worlds at all, and why did a soul come down into a body (line 119)? Every world, high and low, and your own life, stand inside that question. Then step by step. Try the first answer: He made the worlds so that His powers would be complete. It fails, because above, a power never lacks its act; the moment He willed, it was (line 169). Try the second: so that He would be known. That was already done in the high worlds, where Godliness is felt openly (line 195). Neither explains a low world made so that not even the existence of Godliness is felt. Then to the root. What is left is a desire. "A desire has no question," the Alter Rebbe said, in Yiddish (line 213). And because the desire comes from the Essence, which has no top and no bottom, its main showing can be below, and only below (line 213). Bring it home. Look at the room you are in: the dishes, the unanswered messages, the chair. This is the "lower world", the place made so that God is not felt. It is not a leftover of creation. It is the thing that was wanted. The picture is a home: a man lives in his house with his whole self (line 129). It holds, and then it breaks. A man wants a house for reasons, for shelter and rest. Here there is no reason, and the house is wanted because it is the lowest place.

**From inside.** A quiet comes from being wanted without a reason. There is nothing to earn and nothing to prove. The maamar opens with two ways the heart moves: a silent longing that comes from feeling close, and a cry that comes from feeling far; and deeper than the cry of the voice, a cry of the heart that makes no sound (line 92). Both belong here. A person can stop explaining why he is here and simply be where he was wanted.

**At the edge.** The mind is told outright that it has no reason to hold (line 208). Past it is the Essence's own desire, above reason and knowledge. The text places its revelation not above but below, through a mitzvah done by a person: that Name is called "your God" "because it is made through you" (line 237).

**Into the day.** Before one ordinary mitzvah today, a blessing or a coin for charity, say inside: "This is what the world was wanted for."

## Maamar 2: Zeh HaYom (Rosh Hashanah 5666): the Eden no eye has seen, and rising by letting go (lines 247-346)

**The movement.** It shows the mind that every light has a head and an end, and so every light comes in grades. Then it teaches the only way up between grades that have no common measure: to let go of the first one completely. It empties the mind of its last good understanding so that a higher one can come.

> אבל העצם הוא בכ"מ בשוה, ואין בו ראש וסוף, ולא התחלקות מדריג' כלל
> *But the essence is in every place equally. It has no head and no end, and no division into levels at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A candle's light is strong near the flame and weak across the room. So with a torch, so with the sun, and so with every light above: strong near its source, thin far away (line 290). Even Gan Eden has endless levels, so that the righteous have no rest there, going "from strength to strength" (line 290). Then step by step. How does one rise between levels that have no common measure? Only by losing the first level entirely, until it is as if it never was (line 292). Rabbi Zeira fasted a hundred fasts to forget the Babylonian Talmud so that he could take in the Jerusalem Talmud (line 302). A seed must rot in the earth before anything new sprouts (line 302). Then to the root. Eden itself, the King's delight in Himself, no eye has seen, not Moshe and not Adam (line 253). What souls receive now is carried by a "river", understanding, and the river both carries the light and hides it. A child can read every word aloud and miss the idea inside the words (line 266). Bring it home. In prayer it means bitterness first: an honest reckoning of how far you are. "The deeper the bitterness over the great distance, the more light and Godly joy" afterward (line 310). In learning it means working until you almost despair of understanding, and then the depth comes (line 312). The picture of light holds for everything but the Essence, and there it breaks. The Essence is not strongest anywhere. It is not nearer in heaven than at your own table.

**From inside.** There is a stunned moment the text names from the Talmud: "he was dumbstruck for an hour" (line 302). The old understanding is gone and the new one has not come. It does not feel like progress. It is the vessel. Then comes width: "From the narrow place I called... He answered me with expanse" (line 310).

**At the edge.** It points to Eden itself, the Essence's own delight, which will be revealed only in the future (line 253), and to the Essence that is "in every place equally". The mind reaches its edge in the blank moment between levels. The text says that blankness, the loss of what one had, is what lets the higher level come.

**Into the day.** When you are stuck on something you are learning, stop defending what you already understood. Say: "Maybe I have to lose this to get the next thing."

## Maamar 3: Shuvah Yisrael (Shabbat Shuvah 5666): "up to", and never arriving (lines 347-454)

**The movement.** It sets the mind climbing and then tells it the truth about the climb: the higher it goes, the farther the Essence is. It turns the frustration of never arriving into a kind of knowing, and then turns the mind back down to the deed and to the soul's reason for being in a body.

> כל מה שעולה יותר, יותר יפלא ממנו
> *The higher he rises, the more wondrous and beyond him it is.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "Return, Israel, up to (ad) Havayah your God." The word "ad" means "forever" and it also means "up to, and not including" (line 354). Both are true of the Essence. Praise of Him never ends, and it also never reaches Him (line 358). Then step by step. Between levels that share a measure, you climb: a small idea leads to a larger one, the surface of an idea to its depth (line 360). Between levels with no common measure, the first level only gets in the way; you must lose it (line 368). And before the Essence, every height is "up to, and not including". Then to the root. The Rashab says that only one who studies the high levels truly knows that the Essence is beyond knowing. One who does not study them holds it only as belief, "which is not knowledge" (line 368). The not-knowing he means is earned. Then to the deed. The end of the deed "rose first in thought": before the first stirring of the will to make worlds, there was the mitzvah done with the body (line 375). He "consulted with the souls of the righteous" in bodies, doing mitzvot. The soul is rooted in the Essence, "and so it went down into a physical body" (line 452). Bring it home. Think of the best moment of understanding you ever had about God. Hold it. Then let it be small. Whatever is higher than it is not its next step. And your plain deed today was in the thought before any of it.

**From inside.** First, a humbling that is not sad. You stop needing to arrive. Then a strange steadiness: what you cannot reach by rising, you already hold in the plain act, because the act was first. Teshuvah here is "seek My face" from the depth of the heart (line 450), a turning that goes past every level to where no level applies.

**At the edge.** It reaches the edge on purpose: "up to, and not including" (line 358). Beyond is the Essence, which even the Patriarchs and Moshe did not reach in all their rising, "no eye has seen" (line 373). The text says it will be revealed in the future to souls in bodies, and that it is drawn now through the physical mitzvah (line 374).

**Into the day.** After you understand something beautiful about God today, do one small physical mitzvah and say: "This came before the thought."

## Maamar 4: Lulav va'Aravah (Sukkot 5666): the essence that does not divide, and the pearl bored hollow (lines 455-572)

**The movement.** It shows the soul's own essence as something that cannot be split, so that when it wakes it lights every power at once. Then it hollows the person out, like a pearl bored for a string, so there is room for what is above him.

> שאין אוא"ס שורה ומתגלה אלא במי שבטל אליו במציאות לגמרי בבחי' אין ממש
> *For the Infinite Light rests and shows itself only in one who is wholly nullified before Him in his very existence, as truly nothing.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. An essence does not divide or spread out (line 468). When a father rejoices at his only son's wedding, the deepest part of him, the yechidah, comes out, and it lights every power in him at once, from his thinking to his speech to his hands (line 469). Then step by step. Real teshuvah is like that. When the distance truly touches a person, "the essence of his soul is moved from its place" (line 482). He feels only bitterness. He does not feel the pull toward God that is inside it; that pull is "the cry of the heart", a voice that is not heard (line 482). When it is real, even the first assumptions his mind stood on change (line 487). Then to the root. A new idea is brightest in its first flash. As it settles into understanding, the light hides and the pleasure fades (line 495). All understanding is "far from the essence"; even Moshe was told "you will see My back" (line 495). But the delight in a mitzvah is "exceedingly broad": the deed is a vessel for Godliness now, and its light will show in the future (line 507). The picture is a string of pearls (line 546). Torah and mitzvot are the thread that runs "from end to end", even into the world of separation, and binds it to His unity. But a pearl has to be bored hollow before the thread can pass: "my heart is hollow within me." Here the picture breaks. A pearl does not agree to be bored. A person has to. Bring it home. The maamar puts the thread through ordinary things: business done in good faith with no trace of theft, a meal blessed before and after and eaten to have strength to learn and pray (line 557). And it includes the "poor servant": the person who feels nothing at all and still takes on the yoke by force (line 536).

**From inside.** A hollow that is not despair. Room opens where a full self used to be. The despair the maamar describes is honest: "how could I be close, when by all my ways I have no connection to it at all" (line 482). And the force of the soul's pull is strongest exactly there, unfelt.

**At the edge.** It reaches the soul's essence and says what is above it: when the Essence of the Infinite Light shines, "it shines in all of them equally" (line 505), with no higher and lower. No grasp reaches it, not even the seeing of Chochmah (line 495). The text gives bitul, being nothing, as the only way it rests in a person.

**Into the day.** In one dealing with money today, be exact in honesty where no one would ever notice.

## Maamar 5: BaYom HaShemini Atzeret (Shemini Atzeret 5666): the will that has no reason (lines 573-664)

**The movement.** It turns the mind from the reasons for what it wants to the wanting underneath the reasons. It shows the mind a will it cannot explain in itself, and then says that the mitzvah comes from a will like that in God.

> ואמיתית בחי' פנימיות הרצון הוא שאין טעם לרצון כללא
> *And the true inwardness of the will is that there is no reason for the will at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A merchant travels, plans, argues prices. That is the outside of his will. The inside is the profit, and it never shows (line 584). A man builds a house because he needs one. That is a reason. But the pleasure he has in his own home, with his whole self, has no reason at all (line 584). Then step by step. The proof: if reason argues against the house, the outward will weakens. The inner will does not weaken at all, "because it is not from a reason but from the essence" (line 584). So it cannot be explained, and so it has no limit. Whatever a reason holds, the reason also bounds. Then to the root. The will that made the worlds has a reason: "the nature of the good is to do good" (line 587). The will in the mitzvot has none, not even a hidden one, not even the reason of "it tastes good to me" (line 596). That is why "the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah" (line 596). The mitzvah does "the will of the Place": it brings the will into place and time themselves (line 614). Bring it home. Think of the thing you love in a way you could never justify: your child, your home, your people. Any reason you give for it is smaller than it. Now hold that the tefillin, the Shabbat candles, the blessing over bread come from a will like that in Him. The Rashab also teaches what a will is. It is only "the soul's inclining" toward something (line 651). When the soul withdraws, nothing of the will is left, unlike an idea, which stays in a book. So Keter, the will above, is "the Essence inclining" and has no existence of its own (line 654). The picture breaks at the end: a man's inner will still wants a house. His wants no thing for His own sake.

**From inside.** A kind of rest from needing reasons. The mitzvah stops being a thing you have to be persuaded to do and becomes a place where you touch what cannot be persuaded. The maamar is honest that most service now is war: prayer where the Godly soul works on the animal soul "until it too understands the good of Godliness" (line 660).

**At the edge.** The mind is taken to the level of "no reason, not even a hidden reason" (line 596), above even the inner Torah. The text says this will be revealed only in the future, by Moshiach, and that it is drawn now through doing the mitzvah in deed (line 610).

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah today without giving yourself any reason for it. Just: "He wants it."

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Maamar 6: VaYechulu (Shabbat Bereishit 5666): time, the breath of the light, and one knot (lines 665-750)

**The movement.** It shows the mind that time itself is made of interruption, of light going out and coming back. Then it points above the rhythm to a will that never left its source, and to a person bound there by "one knot".

> בחד קטירא אתקטרנא נשמתי בי' אחידא בי' להיטא
> *"With one knot my soul is bound to Him; it holds on in Him; it is aflame in Him" (the Zohar's words of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai).*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The life that keeps every creature going runs and returns: it comes down and at once rises back to its source, and a new life comes (line 682). The picture is the breath of life in a person, drawn from the soul every moment and returning. Then step by step. "Time is interruption" (line 695). You say something lasted an hour only because it stopped. What left is past; what comes is future (line 695). The light running back to its source is the pull of love; its coming down and settling into a vessel is the return. Both happen because the light has gone far from its source. Then to the root. The will, the first stirring toward making worlds, never went far. It only "reaches and does not reach" (line 700). It does not run, because it never left. So it is above time. Rabbi Shimon's flame was like that: not a heat he worked up by understanding, but a flame from the source, "like a flame that rises by itself" (line 711). He knew the inwardness in the plainest laws, and as he studied he drew the Essence into all the worlds (line 711). Then down into the mitzvah. The will of the mitzvot comes like water that flows down from a high place and does not change on the way (line 731). So it brings the Essence "below as above, without contraction" (line 739), "so that even in place and time there will be no limit of place and time" (line 740). One who says "vayechulu" on Friday night becomes God's partner in creation (line 742). Bring it home. Your day is cut into pieces: calls, meals, the clock. That cutting is time. When you light candles or say kiddush, something enters those pieces that is not cut.

**From inside.** First, the relief of seeing that the ups and downs of feeling are the nature of light, not a failure. Then a steadier thing under them: a bond that does not run and return, because it never left. Rabbi Shimon's words describe it from inside: bound, holding, aflame, and the flame is not his own.

**At the edge.** It reaches past the rhythm of running and returning to the will that "reaches and does not reach", and past that to the Essence drawn by the mitzvah, where there is not even a root of time. The text says prayer in the future will be like that too (line 718).

**Into the day.** Say "vayechulu" this Friday night slowly, as a partner's signature.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Maamar 7: Eleh Toldos Noach (Shabbat Noach 5666): the measure, and the One above the measure (lines 751-864)

**The movement.** It lets the mind see how much measuring stands between it and God: channels, screens, a whole world built of "above" and "below". Then it shows that the exact measures of a mitzvah are a different kind of measure. They do not shrink the light; they only say where the unmeasured comes.

> אבל מה שנמשך בזה הוא בחי' אור העצמי כמו שהוא בלי מדה וגבול
> *But what is drawn down through it is the essential light as it is, with no measure and no limit.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The light that makes the worlds comes through a narrow line, the kav, so that there will be "above" and "below" (line 755). Without it, the worlds would be wholly nullified. And the Rashab adds that they truly are, even now, because before the Infinite Light the contraction hides nothing (line 762). Between the worlds hang screens, like letters written out of order, or a thick cloth that lets sunlight through only in pinpricks, so that what comes through is a different light (line 773). Then step by step. There are three things: what is measured, the measuring rod, and the one who measures (line 788). The cubit cannot measure any way but its own. The man holding it can put it down and measure another way. The scribe planned the letters in his mind, and the plan binds the pen. It does not bind the scribe. Then to the mitzvah. Tefillin must have four sections in four houses; a sukkah must be at least seven handbreadths (line 802). These are measures too. But no reason stands behind them; the deed is not the cause of the measure. "If the will had been to chop wood, we would have been bound to chop wood" (line 822). So the measure only says where and when the essential light comes. What comes is "the essential light as it is, with no measure and no limit" (line 825). Then to the root. The Name in the mitzvot is "the Name of the essence". Its first letter, the yud, is a contraction of a new kind: not a veil over the light, but the Essence, which "the heavens and the heavens of heavens cannot contain", contracting itself to be in the Holy of Holies "as it truly is" (line 827). Bring it home. The shape of your tefillin box, the size of the challah, the time for candle-lighting: small, exact, almost fussy. Hold that the exactness is not a fence around a little light. It is the address of the unmeasured. The picture of the ruler breaks here: a ruler shows a size. This measure shows no size at all, only a place.

**From inside.** A warmth for the small details of Jewish life that were starting to feel like red tape. The maamar says what it feels like to do a mitzvah this way: "he has great vitality and delight" simply in that the will is done, and the thing becomes "very precious" to him (line 849). That is "pleasure before Me, that I spoke and My will was done."

**At the edge.** It points above the measured world to "the essential one above the measuring line, from whom the measuring line comes, and in whose power it is to change it entirely" (line 788). The text says this is reached not by understanding the reasons of the mitzvot but by doing them under the yoke, with love "with all your might" that comes "from the essence of the soul drawn in essence to the Essence" (line 859).

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah today exactly by its measure, and let the exactness be your prayer.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Maamar 8: Vayomer... Lech Lecha (Shabbat Lech Lecha 5666): what no thought grasps, the heart's will grasps (lines 865-979)

**The movement.** It walks the mind up the whole ladder of contemplation in prayer and then says honestly that even its top is the outside of the heart. Then it turns to the bond a child has with a parent, which needs no thought, and says that is what grasps the Essence.

> אבל ברעו"ד נתפס בחי' העצמות
> *But by re'uta deliba, the will of the heart, the Essence is grasped.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There are two kinds of knowing: saying what a thing is, and saying what it is not (line 868). What it is belongs to the light clothed in the worlds. What it is not belongs to the light that surrounds them. Of the Essence, not even "not" says anything: "no grasp, not even the grasp of negation, the highest there is" (line 876). Then step by step, the way of prayer. In Pesukei deZimra you think of how great He is in the worlds: a strong man is known by the size of the stones he wrestles with, and all of it came from one saying (line 884). In the Shema's blessings you think of Him as holy and set apart. In the Shema itself, "all before Him is as nothing"; in "Baruch Shem", that everything is real and its life is Godly (line 897). All of it, the Rashab says, is still the outer heart, a love born of understanding. Then to the root. The inner heart is a bond that comes from the yechidah, the soul's deepest point. A friend loves a friend for reasons: he is good to me, he is fine. A father and son are bound because they are one essence, with no reason at all (line 906). "When the son cries 'Abba', the father is moved in his whole being," in a way no stranger's cry can move him. Bring it home. Think of a parent or a child of yours, and of what happens when that one voice calls your name. You did not think it through. It reached you where thinking does not go. The maamar says the bond of Israel to God is like that: always there, sometimes hidden, woken by remembering "You are our Father" or by being far away (line 906). The picture breaks at one place. A human father can be a simple man, below his son in every way, and the son still wants him (line 957). With God the Father there is no such gap to excuse. What stays true is that the bond is not built by understanding and is not undone by the lack of it.

**From inside.** Relief, and something like homecoming. The pressure to feel something grand in prayer lifts. Under the understanding, there is a pull the text calls "like a nature of the Godly soul to cling to its source" (line 906). After it comes a plain readiness: "we will do and we will hear" (line 917), doing without asking for anything for oneself.

**At the edge.** This maamar goes to the edge on purpose and names it: thought grasps nothing of the Essence (line 876). Beyond thought is re'uta deliba, the yechidah's bond. Past even that love, the Rashab puts the yoke: accepting the King is "more bitul" than the love "with all your might", and that is the servant's advantage over the son (line 917).

**Into the day.** Once today, instead of thinking about God, say inside the words "You are my Father", and do the next thing He wants.

## Maamar 9: Ki Yedativ (Shabbat Vayera 5666): the will that does not change, and the delight hidden inside it (lines 980-1086)

**The movement.** It weighs will against delight and lets the mind find, under both, a will in every Jew that nothing wears away. It takes a person who has given up on himself and shows him the thing in him that was never lost.

> וכמו הרצון העצמי שיש בכאו"א מישראל, לא ישונה כלל
> *Like the essential will that is in every single Jew: it does not change at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Delight seems to rule the will: we want what we enjoy (line 944). But the will also rules delight. A man who takes on hard fasts by choice finds them a pleasure; a man who has turned against something cannot enjoy it, even when it is fine (line 997). And nothing stands against the will (line 1007). Then step by step. The essential will of a Jew does not change even if he has sunk into every pleasure and gone far from God (line 996). The lightest person, at a happy moment, draws near to God-fearing people and says that he, too, wants closeness, "and it is not a lie" (line 996). "Many waters cannot put out the love" (line 996). Even "the lost in the land of Ashur" will come at the great shofar. Then to the root. Martyrdom does not come from the love of delight. Rabbi Akiva had that love at its height and still said "when will it come to my hand" (line 1005). It comes from the stubbornness of "a stiff-necked people": "a simple, essential will in every Jew, that cannot be separated from Godliness, because they are one essence" (line 1005). And hidden inside that will is a delight he does not feel: "for the unity of God it is worth it" (line 1026). Bring it home. Think of the person you have written off, perhaps yourself. The maamar says a father still wants a son who has gone astray; he takes no pleasure in his ways, but he wants him, his very self (line 1040). "Whatever evil is found in Israel is something added onto the essence, but the essence is good" (line 1046). The picture of the father holds, and it breaks upward. A human father's will can tire. This will, in the soul and in God toward the soul, is essence, and an essence does not wear out.

**From inside.** A hope that does not depend on how you have been doing. The maamar ends on hope itself: to wait for something beyond all measure, not knowing how it can come and believing it will, is already a bitul, like one who waits at a friend's table (line 1075).

**At the edge.** It reaches the will that grasps the essence of what it wants and not its outside: prayer "to Him, and not to His attributes" (line 1048), and "whom have I in heaven", wanting nothing, not even Gan Eden, but Him alone (line 1056). Past that the text puts the delight that is hidden in the mitzvah's will, which will be revealed only in the future (line 1073).

**Into the day.** Say to yourself once today, about yourself or someone you have given up on: "The evil is added on. The essence is good."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Maamar 10: Ve'Avraham Zaken (Shabbat Chayei Sarah 5666): the flint, and the toil that strikes it (lines 1087-1156)

**The movement.** It shows the mind two kinds of hiddenness in itself: a fire that already exists and only waits to be blown on, and a fire that does not exist at all until it is struck. It turns effort from a burden into the way to reach what no gift can reach.

> אבל העלם העצמי שאינו בבחי' מציאות כלל, והוא בבחי' העלם גם לעצמו
> *But the essential hiddenness is not an existing thing at all; it is hidden even from itself.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Real study is waiting over a thing: not being satisfied with the first look, learning it again and again until it makes less sense, then more, until the depth comes (line 1101). "The deeper he goes, the more wondrous it becomes to him." A man who digs deep enough finds a living spring, not stored water (line 1101). Then step by step. Wisdom that comes easily, as a gift, is bounded by the size of the mind it comes from; when that source is spent, it stops (line 1111). But people with small minds who toil reach higher than the gifted, because toil reaches the hidden root of wisdom itself (line 1117). Then to the picture. A flame in a glowing coal already exists; it is hidden only from others, and a breath brings it out. But when the coal dies, the flame is gone. The flint, "kremen" in Yiddish, has no fire in it at all, not even warmth. Struck with iron, it gives sparks without end (line 1119). Its fire is "hidden even from itself" (line 1125). Bring it home. The maamar knows the moment inside study when "it gets light": you do not yet know the answer, but you already feel its rightness coming (line 1133). Think of a problem you have been working at for days. The dark part is not wasted. It is the striking. The picture breaks at God's side. A flint is a stone, and its fire comes from outside it. The Rashab uses it to say that toil in Torah reaches "wisdom as it is in the very Essence"; the toil "returns to its origin" (line 1151). And he carries it into life: the last exile is full of obstacles, even to plain mitzvot, and standing firm against them is the striking (line 1155).

**From inside.** A new respect for your own slowness. What felt like being less gifted turns out to be a way in. And a quiet stubbornness: the obstacles are not signs that you are on the wrong road. Avraham was blessed "with everything", with no particular blessing named, because through his trials he reached a place with no particulars (line 1155).

**At the edge.** It goes past the mind's power to understand, to "the essential power of the mind as it is in the essence of the soul", which is not even a source of ideas (line 1148), like the deep that springs come from without being their source. The text says toil reaches there, and through it the Essence will be revealed in the future (line 1155).

**Into the day.** Stay with one hard line of Torah today for five minutes longer than you want to.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Maamar 11: Vayiten Lecha (Shabbat Toldos 5666): the question that darkens, and the light that comes through it (lines 1157-1282)

**The movement.** It starts from a person who knows that God has not changed and wonders why he does not melt at it. Then it gives him a road for a heart that is too heavy for that: honest struggle with hard questions. The darkness of the question becomes the way to a truth that plain light does not reach.

> כ"א ע"י הקושיות דוקא עי"ז מגיע לאור אמיתית הדבר
> *Only through the questions does he reach the light of the thing's truth.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "I, God, have not changed, and you, children of Jacob, have not come to an end" (line 1191). The prophet wonders. If you truly thought about how all the worlds are "a mere glow that does not touch Him at all" (line 1192), you would want nothing but Him, not Gan Eden, not the World to Come, and your soul would melt. Why doesn't it? Then step by step. Because you are "Jacob": the heart is crooked and weak. Sins, and even plain heaviness of body, stand between the soul and "your God" inside it (line 1200). So Torah is called "strength". It gives peace above, where the soul is bound to God in real bitul, and peace below, where the body and animal soul stop blocking it (line 1200). Then to the root. Before the golden calf, Torah came like the first tablets, whole and needing no toil. After it came the second tablets, with laws, midrash and all the fine detail, and toil (line 1179). Even the rabbis' fences come from great love, like a father who is stricter with the son he loves (line 1216). The Babylonian Talmud is "He set me in darkness": question upon question, until the matter is hidden altogether (line 1242). Then, by deep work, the true answer comes and every question falls away. The pictures are wine poured through a strainer again and again until it runs clear, and silver in the refiner's fire, where the flame churns it up and down until the dross leaves (line 1242). They hold. They break where the Rashab says the darkness does more than clean: through it one reaches "Torah as it is in His very Essence", which "only God understands" (line 1245). Bring it home. Think of a question about God or about your life that you could not answer and did not want to face. The maamar does not ask you to hide from it. It says each question comes from the other side, and that working it through is how it is refined (line 1247). A farmer who clears his field of every stone is the one who eats bread without end (line 1266).

**From inside.** The shame of having questions eases. A kind of courage comes, the courage to stay in the dark part of learning without running. And afterward a clearness that the text describes simply: the matter becomes "very pure and bright" (line 1242).

**At the edge.** It reaches the "essential hiddenness" of Torah, above even the hidden root of wisdom: Torah as the King's delight in Himself, which "no man knows its worth" (line 1245). The Name Elokim, the Name of hiding, is here "the essential hiddenness itself" (line 1245). The text says the main showing of this is in the future (line 1264).

**Into the day.** Write down one real question you have about God or Torah, and bring it to someone who can learn it with you.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Maamar 12: Vayetze (Shabbat Vayetze 5666): the simple song (lines 1283-1401)

**The movement.** It takes the mind through the joys of understanding, each higher than the last, and then past them to a joy that has nothing to do with understanding. It turns contemplation toward song, and song toward a delight too deep to be felt.

> שמחת הנפש בעצמה ומהותה, שלא לפ"ע ההשגה וראי' דחכמה כלל וכלל
> *The joy of the soul in itself and its very being, not measured by understanding or by the mind's seeing at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There is joy in understanding something about God. It is real, and it is exactly as big as the understanding (line 1343). There is a higher joy when, after all the details and explanations, the heart of the idea shines by itself, "very clear and very pure". Then the heart sings by itself, with no effort, like the song of the Levites (line 1343). Then step by step. When the thinking is only in the clothes of the idea, a person must work to rouse his heart. When he feels the Godly thing itself that he is thinking about, the heart is moved by itself (line 1343). Higher still is the wordless song of Shabbat, the mind's seeing, "song to God" that comes without toil (line 1352). All of these are "double song": joy compounded with a mind. Then to the root. The "simple song" is a joy of the soul in itself, not measured by any understanding (line 1354). It was the Song at the Sea, where each one pointed with a finger and said "this is my God", because the Essence was revealed to the yechidah (line 1354). It shows in David leaping and whirling, and in dancing with no song at all at the water-drawing celebration, which "shows the moving of the whole essence" (line 1382). Bring it home. Remember a moment of pure gladness at a wedding, a birth, a homecoming, when you did not know why your feet were moving. Nothing was being understood. Something in you simply was glad to be. The maamar says the soul has a gladness like that toward God, below the level of reasons. The picture of song breaks at the top. Even simple song is felt, and so it can overflow: the people at Sinai could not bear it and their souls flew out; Rabbi Akiva wept at the secrets of Torah (line 1382). Above that the text places a delight that is never felt at all (line 1391).

**From inside.** Permission to be glad without earning it by understanding. And a better way to judge a prayer: not only "what did I grasp" but "did something in me sing". The maamar says the song comes "by itself" when the feeling is in the Godliness and not in one's own grasp of it (line 1343).

**At the edge.** It reaches past the mind's seeing, which is only "you will see My back" (line 1352), to the yechidah's own gladness, and past that to "the essential delight hidden in the very Essence", which never comes into felt pleasure at all (line 1391). The text says a person rises there through the work of refining the world, as Jacob went out from "the depth of the oath" to Charan (line 1393).

**Into the day.** Sing one line of a niggun today with no words, and let it be glad for no reason.

## Maamar 13: Vayishlach Yaakov (Shabbat Vayishlach 5666): the go-between that does not divide, and the leap from the narrow place (lines 1402-1509)

**The movement.** It teaches the mind why everything it receives comes through a go-between and so reaches it only as a glow, and then names the one go-between that does not divide. Then it turns to the person who feels farthest away and shows that his narrow place is a launching point.

> זהו הכלל בכל דבר הבא מן המיצר והדוחק ביותר, הוא שבא לכלל התפשטות כח גדול וחזק שאינו בערכו כלל וכלל
> *This is the rule in everything that comes from the narrowest, most pressed place: it comes out as a great, strong force, out of all proportion to what it was.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. In the old study houses a great teacher spoke and a translator passed it to the people. The people received only what the translator could hold; the teacher's own mind stayed hidden from them, and even from the translator (line 1422). Every go-between in the worlds is like that: what passes through is a glow. But Torah is a different go-between. "In Torah the go-between does not divide," and souls are joined to the Essence itself (line 1438). Then step by step. There is a delight in God that will be felt in the future, through the reasons of Torah and mitzvot, like the Shabbat meal. And there is a delight never felt at all, like Yom Kippur, when a person eats nothing and afflicts even the soul, so that every felt pleasure falls away and he reaches what is not felt (line 1459). That second level, the maamar says, is reached not by Torah and mitzvot but by teshuvah from the depth of the heart (line 1461). Then to the root. Why is the penitent's pain stronger than the tzaddik's joy? Because of the rule of the narrow place. Water squeezed through narrow veins in the earth bursts up through rock. A stone held back in a sling flies farther; an arrow flies as far as the bow is drawn. A shofar blown into its narrow end sounds wide (line 1492). The penitent's bitterness touches "the very point of his Jewishness" (line 1492). Then the picture of the leap. A man who measures his strength cannot jump a wide river. One who throws himself with abandon, as in battle, clears it (line 1492). So teshuvah is not a climb step by step; it jumps "from utter distance to utter closeness". "From the narrow place I called... He answered me with the expanse of God": the true expanse is the Essence as it is, because anything drawn down is already less than expanse (line 1492). Bring it home. Think of the tightest place in your life right now, the thing that presses. The maamar does not ask you to like it. It says pressure is how force is made. And it adds an order: Jacob first refined the good in Laban's house, then went to face Esau (line 1507). Build the everyday good first; then the hard thing can be turned.

**From inside.** Hope for the one who feels he has no ladder. The pain of distance stops being only pain and becomes a spring under pressure. There is also a note of freedom: the jump comes from throwing oneself, not from calculating one's strength.

**At the edge.** It reaches past the felt delight of Torah to "the essential delight hidden in the Essence itself", which the text says only teshuvah from the depth of depths reaches (line 1492). The one who comes back reaches where the completely righteous cannot stand (line 1473).

**Into the day.** Take the thing that presses you most today and say one honest sentence to God about it from the narrow place.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Maamar 14: Ner Chanukah (Shabbat Vayeshev and Chanukah 5666): the flame, the air, and why the lamp of mitzvah comes first (lines 1510-1618)

**The movement.** It lets the mind watch a candle and see its own soul in it: a flame that wants only to go up, held on the wick by the air. It takes the longing to leave the world and turns it into a longing to stay and give light.

> ובחי' הוי', שזהו בחי עצמות אוא"ס, הוא עצמו ית' הוא אלקינו
> *And Havayah, which is the Essence of the Infinite Light: He Himself is our God.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "The soul of man is the lamp of God." Fire by its nature only rises and leaves. Wind rises too, but spreads sideways and also blows down (line 1531). The mind in a person is like fire: it wants to be alone, apart, cleaving upward (line 1549). The heart's feelings are like wind: they are always toward someone else, and so they both rise and come down. The soul at its core is breath: "He breathed into his nostrils" (line 1519). Then step by step. A candle flame goes up and down all the time. Without air it goes out; in stale air it is smothered; the air is what lets it rest on the wick and rise gently instead of leaping off (line 1579). So too the soul: its fire of longing is woken by a Godly wind, a stirring, and the same wind brings it down to rest in mitzvot (line 1606). Then to the root. To wake the soul's fire, think of its source, as in the Shema. Havayah is past, present and future as one; nothing changes in Him; all before Him is as nothing. "And Havayah, the Essence itself, is our God": our strength and our life, the source of every Jewish soul (line 1581). Then the heart flames up to be included in Him. Then down. A longing that only runs up is Tohu, the world that broke. The world was made "not for emptiness but to be lived in" (line 1595). Rabbi Akiva "went in in peace and came out in peace"; Ben Azzai went in and did not come back. The goal is the return. So the lamp of mitzvah comes before the light of Torah. On Chanukah the Maccabees first fought with their lives, and only then lit the lamps; the mezuzah is on the right, and only then the Chanukah light on the left (line 1614). Bring it home. Light a candle and watch the flame dip and rise. It is not failing; it is breathing. Your times of longing and your times of plain duty are one flame, and the air between them is from God.

**From inside.** Calm about your own ups and downs. A longing that used to make ordinary life feel like exile finds a place to rest: on the wick, in the deed. And the maamar is honest about the mind: it needs silence to receive anything new, "Rav was silent", "he was dumbstruck for an hour" (line 1540).

**At the edge.** It reaches the Essence named as "our God", our own life (line 1581), and above that, the essential will whose truth is the return, not the run (line 1595). It does not ask the mind to go past the edge here; it asks it to come back.

**Into the day.** Before a mitzvah today, stop for a moment and think: "The air that holds this flame is His." Then do it.

## Maamar 15: Vayigash Elav Yehuda (Shabbat Vayigash 5666): the sudden morning stirring, and what to do with it (lines 1619-1717)

**The movement.** It takes the sudden good feeling that sometimes comes from nowhere and tells the truth about it: it is a gift from above, it will pass, and it is only a beginning. It turns a mind that waits for inspiration into a mind that uses inspiration to do its own work.

> ה"ז רק כמו הקדמה אל העבודה האמיתית שייגע האדם את עצמו בעבודה במוח ולב
> *It is only like a preface to the true service, in which a person makes himself toil in mind and heart.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Where does the soul's pull upward come from? Not from its nature alone. A thing is drawn to its source only when it feels its source, and a soul in a body does not feel it (line 1623). So the pull comes from above: the life that runs through all creation itself runs and returns (line 1642), and a light from the Essence wakes the soul, like Aharon lighting the lamps "toward the face of the menorah" (line 1632). Then step by step. "Every morning, suddenly, there is a stirring in every soul" to serve God, to cleave to Him, and not to go after emptiness (line 1645). When it comes with no preparation at all, "that is a sure sign that it is from above" (line 1656). "For You are my lamp, O God" (line 1665). Then the honest part. Such a stirring passes quickly; "a little while, and it is as if it never was" (line 1674). It is not yet service. But while it lasts, the heart is open, and a person can think something through that on other days will not settle in his mind. That is the time to work, to contemplate until "the Godly thing shines openly in his soul" (line 1674). The picture is a child's first day of school. The father walks with him and fills his pockets with sweets, so that later he will go by himself (line 1686). And a new house is dedicated with a feast once, so that it will bless everything done in it afterward. The picture breaks at Aharon's lamps: that love from above is itself the service, not a preface to it (line 1697). Bring it home. Think of the last time you woke up and, for no reason, wanted to be good, to pray, to be close. What did you do with it? The maamar also speaks to the days when the mind will not settle at all: then arouse compassion on your own soul, and the compassion above opens the way (line 1674).

**From inside.** A gentler relationship with your moods. The good moments stop being proof that you are holy and the dull ones stop being proof that you are not. There is gratitude for the gift, and a quiet readiness to use it.

**At the edge.** It points to where the stirring comes from: "the face of the menorah", the inwardness and Essence of the Infinite Light (line 1659). The true will of the Essence, it says, is in the return, the coming back down to Torah and mitzvot after the run (line 1667).

**Into the day.** The next time a good feeling comes from nowhere, stop and spend five minutes learning or praying right then.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Maamar 16: Yehuda Ata Yoducha Achecha (Shabbat Vayechi 5666): the needle's eye and the open hall (lines 1718-1837)

**The movement.** It shows the mind how thin the light is that reaches the world, and how the surrounding light is inside everything without being felt. Then it gives the order of the way: do everything you can, and then what is above you is given as a gift.

> פתחו לי כחודה של מחט ואני אפתח לך כפתחו של אולם
> *Open for Me an opening like the eye of a needle, and I will open for you an opening like the entrance of the Hall.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The light that fills the worlds is an outer, narrowed glow (line 1729). In the lowest creatures it thins out until a creature does not even feel that its life is Godly, and so it can turn against Godliness. The surrounding light is not only "above". It is inside every creature, its main life, "I fill heaven and earth", but it is not felt, so it raises nothing to its own level (line 1737). Then step by step. A mitzvah draws that surrounding light onto a person, like tefillin that sit on top of the head: it surrounds, it helps, it does not yet enter (line 1740). On Shabbat the surrounding light is felt within; in Torah it truly becomes inner (line 1766). For that, the six days of refining must come first (line 1751). Then to the root. The great love that is above reason is not in a person's power to wake. It comes from above as a gift (line 1774). But a gift is given only to one who has pleased the giver. In the Temple every gate had doors that could open and close, except the gate of the Hall, which had none (line 1782). "Open for Me like the eye of a needle, and I will open for you like the entrance of the Hall." Then the morning. The maamar gives a contemplation any person can do. At night you sleep and your life withdraws; in the morning you rise "a new creature". So every creature is renewed each day, and in truth every moment (line 1832). When we see something new we are moved; so let the heart see that everything is new. Bring it home. The needle's eye is small: ten honest minutes of thinking in the morning, one real effort to understand. Then comes the Amidah, where Judah bows: "acknowledgment", a self-nullification that comes from the essence of the soul (line 1836). You do the small opening. The wide one is not yours to make.

**From inside.** Freedom from having to produce the highest things yourself. Effort becomes simple and bounded: the needle's eye. And a quiet expectancy, like a person who has set the table and waits for the guest.

**At the edge.** It reaches "the love above reason and knowledge" whose root is above the whole chain of worlds, which no human service reaches, and which comes as a gift only when a person's own work is complete (line 1774). The text calls the higher knowledge there "recognition and feeling, not intellectual knowing", where there is no room at all for the other side (line 1810).

**Into the day.** Tomorrow morning, before you look at anything else, think for one minute: "I woke up a new creature."

## Maamar 17: Vayelech Ish MiBeis Levi (Shabbat Shemot 5666): the sun's ray, and a nothing that holds everything (lines 1838-1968)

**The movement.** It takes the Tanya's picture of the ray inside the sun and pushes it until it breaks, so that the mind sees creatures as even less than a ray, and their whole reality as God's own "nothing" holding them. Then it builds prayer as an offering, fire by fire, and ends in the soul's silent incense.

> שבאמת כל מציאות הנבראים הוא האין האלקי המהווה אותם ומחי' אותם
> *In truth, the whole reality of creatures is the Godly "nothing" that brings them into being and gives them life.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A craftsman makes a vessel from wood or metal. Once it is made it stands on its own, and it even completes him with a use he does not have (line 1857). The sun's ray is different. It needs the sun every moment; at night it is gone. And inside the sun the ray is there more strongly, only it counts for nothing there (line 1857). Then step by step. Creatures are like the ray: they are new, made from utter nothing, and so they need their source every instant (line 1863). Their being "something" is "only in their own feeling". And everything they are, their life and even their perfections, is included in Him beforehand, so He lacks nothing (line 1863). That is why "He who planted the ear, shall He not hear?" (line 1881). Then the picture breaks, and the break is the teaching. The ray is of the sun's own kind; a creature is not of the Creator's kind at all (line 1871). So creatures are nullified more than the ray, not less. And the two ways they are nullified are opposites: needing Him always is closeness; adding nothing to Him is distance (line 1879). Seeing this, the angels cry "holy" (line 1894). Then into prayer as an offering. The fire from below is Pesukei deZimra: think of how much He makes and renews each day, as you yourself woke new this morning. The fire from above for the animal soul comes from thinking of the angels' love. The fire of God comes in the Shema, where the animal soul burns away (line 1915). And here is a mercy: the Second Temple had no fire from heaven, and the offerings were still valid. "Even if no flame from above falls on him, as long as he toils, it is a mitzvah to bring fire from the common man" (line 1902). Bring it home. The table you eat at "atones like the altar" when you eat to have strength to serve (line 1917). Honest business refines the world too. And on Shabbat the soul itself rises like incense, a scent that the soul enjoys and not the body, "a voice that is not heard" (line 1931).

**From inside.** Lightness about your own existence: you are held, not holding. And courage for the days when nothing burns: bring ordinary fire, your own small effort, and it counts. On the good days there may be the quiet of incense, a closeness with no noise in it.

**At the edge.** It takes the mind to the point where creatures have "no comparison" to their source (line 1884), past every parable. Beyond that it names the soul's bond with the Essence, "to be absorbed into the body of the King", which comes as a gift from above, "a service of gift", but only after one's own toil (line 1938).

**Into the day.** At your next meal, say the blessing slowly and eat the first bite "to have strength to serve".

## Maamar 18: Ki Ka'asher HaShamayim HaChadashim (Shabbat Va'era 5666): the return is harder, and higher (lines 1969-2034)

**The movement.** It turns around the usual picture of spiritual height. The rush upward, even to the point of the soul's expiring, is the easy part; coming back down to live rightly in the world is the harder and higher part. It sends a mind that longs to escape back to its table and its work.

> דבאמת בנקל הרבה יותר להיות בבחינת כלות הנפש מלהיות למטה בקיום התורה ומצות
> *For in truth it is far easier to be in a state of the soul's expiring than to be below, keeping the Torah and mitzvot.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Service has two movements: running toward God, and coming back to do His will below. The return is even more a person's own work than the run, because during the day, away from prayer, the stirring from above is hardly felt (line 1976). Yet the return is higher in its root, because it is the purpose. Then step by step. A man far from the king who hears of his greatness is fired up. A man brought before the king, who sees his glory, is not excited at all; he falls to the ground like a stone (line 1985). A minister, whose place is the mind, cleaves to the king with delight. A servant, whose place is the heart, falls back in awe (line 1990). Seeing, not hearing, makes a person turn back down to make vessels for Godliness. Then to the root. Light runs back to its source, because it is only a glow. The Essence has nowhere to run and does not settle into vessels, so it comes through the practical mitzvot, "which are outside the category of a vessel" (line 2005). "The return is truly planted and rooted in the Essence": "He did not create it for emptiness, but to be lived in" (line 2008). Then the unity. The higher unity alone says the world has no existence, as before it was made. Higher still is the union of both: "even as the world exists, it is in utter bitul and union" (line 2019). A person does not escape the physical; by his service he turns physical things into Godliness, as the righteous eat on Shabbat. Bring it home. The maamar notices something in a real servant of God: "he cannot find rest for his soul" unless he does some Godly work in speech or deed, a word of Torah, a coin to charity (line 2010). Not to quench a thirst, but because he is "nothing in essence". Think of the pull to stay in the lovely moment after prayer, and then close the siddur and go do the next thing.

**From inside.** Less guilt about not living in rapture, and more respect for plain faithful days. There is a settled kind of rest, the rest of one who is where he was sent. The text's image is Rabbi Akiva, who "went in in peace and came out in peace" (line 2015).

**At the edge.** It reaches the Essence's own will, which is for the return, with "no reason and no cause at all" (line 2008), and the union of both unities, which the Rashab ranks above the higher unity alone (line 2019). In the future, run and return will come "from the Essence of the Infinite Light", and today's plain service will be raised into it (line 2031).

**Into the day.** When prayer ends today, go straight to one small mitzvah before anything else.

## Maamar 19: HaChodesh HaZeh Lachem (Shabbat Bo 5666): the constancy of nature, and two kinds of soul (lines 2035-2192)

**The movement.** It turns the most ordinary thing, that the sun rises every day the same way, into a window onto the infinite, and then shows a second window in the miracle. Along the way it lets a simple person see that his broken heart and plain faith are a real road, not a lesser one.

> לכן הנהגת הטבע היא תמידית בלי שום שינוי והפסק, שניכר בזה כח הא"ס
> *So the order of nature is constant, with no change and no break, and in this the power of the Infinite is seen.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. We can know God's greatness two ways (line 2036). One is nature: sun, moon and stars turning from east to west, never stopping, never changing their task, and every kind of creature lasting through its generations. Anything with limits should wear out and change; that they do not shows "the power of the Infinite". The other is the miracle, the Exodus and the Sea, where nature itself gives way. Then step by step. Both come from God, by two kinds of drawing (line 2172). One is measured, the "line", that fills the worlds from inside; nature comes from it, and because the line is "like its source", nature never stops (line 2191). The other is unmeasured, the surrounding light; miracles come from it. So Tishrei is the head of the year, for creation, and Nissan is the head of the months, for redemption. Then to the souls. Some souls are like "fish in the sea" even on dry land: nullified and one with God, so that the body hardly hides from them (line 2079). Moshe, Yosef, Rabbi Shimon were like this, and their loftiness was not their own but "the Godliness in them" (line 2104). Other souls serve through the body: their bitul is a broken heart, "for I am poor and needy", with no loftiness in it at all (line 2104). Bring it home. The maamar says: "we see in many people" a bitul of self that is simply there, so that their coarse heart breaks easily, and "they serve God with their bodies, truly, keeping the mitzvot in deed, in faith and simplicity of heart, without much understanding or contemplation" (line 2104). If that is you, it is not a failure to become someone else. It is your road. Then look up. Tomorrow the sun will rise at the time printed in the calendar. The picture of constancy breaks at one point: a machine also repeats. The difference is that nothing limited should be able to keep this up, and it does.

**From inside.** A new kind of wonder, about what never changes rather than what surprises. And a quiet dignity for the plain servant. The maamar does not rank him low; it gives him his own place, like David, whose heart was "always broken and humble" (line 2104).

**At the edge.** It points above the measured line to "Ein Sof itself", where "the category of these sefirot does not apply at all" (line 2183), and before which even wisdom "comes from nothing". The miracle shows that level more than nature does, and the text ties it to the redemption.

**Into the day.** Look at the sunrise or sunset once this week and say: "This has not stopped once."

## Maamar 20: Vayolech Hashem Es HaYam (Shabbat Beshalach 5666): what "Ein Sof" names, and "You are alone" (lines 2193-2311)

**The movement.** It takes the most familiar name for God in Chassidus, "Ein Sof", the Infinite, and shows that even it names His light and not Him. The mind is emptied of its best word, and left before One who is alone even now.

> ובאמת אתה הוא לבדך ממש
> *And in truth, You are truly alone.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "I, God, have not changed." Creating vast worlds changes nothing in Him (line 2242). A soul in a body is caught in the body: it cannot give life to another body at the same time. But the worlds are made by His "speech", and speech is the soul's outermost glow; a man loses nothing of himself by talking a great deal (line 2242). "I fill heaven and earth", truly, and is not held by them (line 2251). Then step by step. What does "Ein Sof" mean? First, without end: truly said only of what has no beginning. Second, without any limit at all, so that nothing can be said of it: "the negation of limit and the negation of limitlessness, the negation of the positive and the negation of the negative" (line 2253). Only the Essence fits that. Then to the root. So the source of all the worlds, the surrounding light that makes them, is called "Ein Sof" only because it is like its source. Before the Essence it is "nothing, truly counted as nothing" (line 2292). He is above even being "surrounding" to the worlds, "because He is not in the category of worlds at all". "You are Havayah alone": alone now, after the heavens were made, exactly as before (line 2299). Bring it home. Think of the words you use for God when you pray: Infinite, Creator, King, Merciful. Each one is true and each one faces the world. Let them rest for a moment, and stand before Him with no word. The picture of a man and his speech breaks here: a man's speech still comes from him and returns to him. Here the Essence is not even a source. One more thing to hold. In the middle of this hard argument the Rashab writes a small note saying another reading seems possible, and ends: "This needs study. May God enlighten my eyes" (line 2235). The greatest mind in the room says he does not know.

**From inside.** A spaciousness, and a kind of quiet that comes when the mind stops reaching for a better word. There is also the humility of the Rashab's own note: it is permitted to not know. The maamar ends at the Sea, where the east wind, "the Ancient One of the world", split the waters and revealed the hidden Essence to those who walked through (line 2310).

**At the edge.** This is the book's clearest step past the edge of the mind. No name reaches Him; even "Ein Sof" is His light (line 2302). Beyond is the Essence, "alone, truly", whom the text says was revealed at the splitting of the Sea and through the Torah given after it (line 2310).

**Into the day.** Once today, begin a prayer with "You" and pause before any other word.

## Maamar 21: Vaydaber... Zos Chukas HaTorah (Shabbat Parah 5666): flow and light, and loving Him as your life (lines 2312-2401)

**The movement.** It teaches the mind to tell two ways of giving apart: the effortful passing-on of a teacher, and the effortless shining of a light. Then it turns that into love: loving God the way a person loves his own life.

> שע"י האהבה מאיר לו באמת האלקות בגילוי בנפשו ובחלקו בהעולם
> *Through the love, Godliness truly shines for him openly, in his soul and in his portion of the world.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There are two ways something passes from giver to receiver (line 2326). One is "flow": the giver's own substance goes out. A man kicking a stone upward puts some of his leg's force into it; while he kicks, he cannot walk (line 2334). A teacher must shape his words for this student, and while he teaches one idea he cannot think another (line 2345). The other is "light": the sun lights a room with no effort, a candle brought in fills the house by itself, iron grows hot in the fire without the fire trying (line 2362). Then step by step. The soul gives life to the body like light, not like flow: "everything alive moves" by itself, and the soul loses nothing by it (line 2367). From that life we know that the soul exists, but not what it is. So too above: from the light we know that He is, not what He is (line 2367). Then to the love. "To love God, for He is your life." As a person loves his own life, he wants that life to spread and show. So when he thinks how Godliness gives life to the worlds and "the main thing is the Godliness", he loves God and wants Him to shine in the world and in himself (line 2360). And through that love, Godliness truly shines in his soul and in his part of the world. Bring it home. Think of how much you want to be alive, to breathe, to wake tomorrow. That wanting is not an idea; it is you. The maamar asks you to feel that God is that life, and to want Him the same way. The Yiddish in the text is blunt about what cannot be forced: "you cannot put a head on someone" (line 2337). Love like this is not argued into a person; it is recognized. The picture of light holds and then breaks. The sun has no choice but to shine. Above, the light shines by will (line 2384). From that level the world is not a solid "something" at all, only an engraving, like letters cut into stone, and the Torah draws that down (line 2399).

**From inside.** A warmth toward God that is close to the warmth toward one's own life, without drama. Effort changes too: there is the effort of the teacher, and there is the effortless shining of a presence. Both are real.

**At the edge.** It reaches past the light that shows only that He is, to the level where creation is only "engraving", not yet a separate something (line 2399). The text does not go further here; it stays with love.

**Into the day.** When you feel glad to be alive today, even for a second, say: "He is my life."

## Maamar 22: Ki Imcha Mekor Chaim (Shabbat Vayakhel-Pekudei 5666): "He and His Name are one" (lines 2402-2490)

**The movement.** It takes the image of God as a sun and shows where it fails: the sun must shine, and He need not. Then it lets the mind rest in the moment before any light, where there is "only He".

> כי אז הכל בבחי' הוא לבד, שלא יש עוד דבר כלל, גם לא בחי' אור
> *For then all is "He" alone; there is nothing else at all, not even light.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A lit candle cannot choose not to shine; the sun shines "against its will", because light is its nature (line 2416). God is not like that. "He speaks to the sun and it does not rise." So the light that is in Him before it shines is not light at all; it is only "ability", that He is able to shine (line 2416). Then step by step. A gem is a better picture than the sun: the sun is all light, but a gem is a stone that also sparkles (line 2420). "The main thing of Godliness is not that worlds come from it and receive their life from it." He is apart even from being the source of the source of worlds. "You are holy" is Him; "Your Name is holy" is the light that relates to the worlds (line 2428). Then to the root. There are two sayings: "You are One and Your Name is One", and "He and His Name are one" (line 2438). In the first, the light is one and He is one, two ones. In the second, the light is absorbed in Him, and "all is He alone; there is nothing else at all, not even light". The Zohar's lamp spreads in every direction, "and when you look, you find only one lamp" (line 2479). Bring it home. Think of your own name. When no one calls it, where is it? It is not anywhere apart from you. There is only you, and the fact that you could answer if someone called (line 2479). So His Name before the worlds: only He, and that He can shine. The picture of a name breaks too. A man's name is given to him by others. Here there is no other at all. The maamar ends by saying that this level will be revealed in the future, and that toil in Torah now brings it (line 2487).

**From inside.** A deep stillness. Even the light, even the thought of light, can be set down, and nothing is lost. What remains is not emptiness but Him.

**At the edge.** It goes to the edge and a step past: "not even light" (line 2438), only the Essence and its ability. The text says this is "with You is the source of life", which will be revealed in the future through the Torah, while "in Your light we see light" is the light Adam saw from one end of the world to the other (line 2487).

**Into the day.** Say "Baruch Shem" today slowly, and at "His Name" think: "His Name is only He."

## Maamar 23: Adam Ki Yakriv (Shabbat Vayikra 5666): the power to hide is the great power (lines 2491-2574)

**The movement.** It turns around the feeling that God's hiddenness is weakness or absence. Holding back, hiding, limiting, the mind is told, is the chief power, and every limit in the world is the Infinite's own power, held in its own hand.

> ואדרבה, זהו עיקר בחי' הכח והגבורה להעלים ולהסתיר
> *On the contrary, this is the main power and might: to conceal and to hide.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. In the Essence all is ability: He is able to shine, and able not to shine (line 2505). The power to limit a light does not come from the light; a light cannot hold itself back. It comes from where the light comes from (line 2495). So "above without end and below without limit" does not mean high and low; it means showing and hiding. Then step by step. Showing is like kindness, water running down from a high place. Holding back, drawing the light back into its source, is might, gevurah, and in its root it is higher than kindness (line 2503). "The power of limit is the power of the Infinite itself" (line 2514). Then the picture. A man's hand could write a great deal. Instead it writes one aleph. The power that restrains the hand never reaches the paper; it stays in the hand, and the hand is not limited by it (line 2533). A limit a person sets on himself is not a real limit, only a chosen one. So the vessels of the highest world limit the light, and the limit is "within Godliness, not outside it" (line 2528). Bring it home. Think of a time you held back: a sharp word not said, a desire not followed, a gift given quietly so no one would know. That holding back was not less than acting. It was more of you. The maamar says God's hiddenness is like that: a strength, held in His hand. Then the offering. "When a man (adam) brings an offering": "adam" is one who cleaves, and only one who cleaves can raise an animal to its root (line 2564). The picture of the hand breaks at one point: a man's hand tires and has an edge. His "knowing" of each creature surrounds it "from its head to its bottom, inside and inside of inside", and is still unlimited (line 2546).

**From inside.** A different feeling toward the times when God seems far. Not "He is not here" but "He is holding back, and holding back is His strength." And a new respect for your own restraint.

**At the edge.** It takes the mind into the Essence, where there is only "ability" to shine or not, and where limit and limitlessness both come from one power (line 2505). The text calls this the root of the gevurot above the chasadim; it does not go further here.

**Into the day.** Hold back one thing today on purpose, a word or a want, and say inside: "This too is strength."

## Maamar 24: Adam Ki Yihyeh Be'or Besaro (Shabbat Tazria-Metzora 5666): the one thought, and the hidden love (lines 2575-2671)

**The movement.** It admits, in plain words, that the mind does not understand how something comes from nothing. Then it shows the mind something it can understand: that in every Jew, even the plainest, there is a hidden love that will give its life without a single reason.

> בחי' אהבה המוסתרת שיש בכל אחד מישראל בניצוץ נשמתו ליכלל ולידבק בה' אחד
> *The hidden love that is in every single Jew, in the spark of his soul, to be included in and cleave to the One God.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A man who wants to build a house first sees it all at once in his mind: roof, walls, windows, doors, "all together in one thought" (line 2621). Then he speaks it part by part, and then he hires builders. So all the worlds, every generation, every event of six thousand years, were seen "in one glance" in the first thought (line 2629). Then the break. The man's thought makes nothing; the builders make the house. "Not so above: by the thought itself they came into being" (line 2631). And the Rashab says honestly: how something comes from nothing "is not understood by us, for among creatures there is no example of it at all" (line 2603). Let the mind stop there for a moment. It does not know how you are here. Then to the root. That first thought reaches all the way down; its "feet" end in this lowest world, and they have to shine somewhere (line 2651). They shine in the hidden love in every Jew, "even in the simplest", to be one with God. It is not felt most of the time. It "guards them from above". So a Jew can give his life for God's Name "with no understanding at all, only by faith" (line 2651). Bring it home. "Although he does not see, his mazal sees" (line 2651): the root of your soul sees what your mind does not, and cannot move away from it. Think of the plainest Jew you know, the one who could not explain one line of Chassidus. The maamar says the same seeing is in him. Then to the lesson of the plague of skin. "Adam", the highest kind of person, who has fixed everything, may still find a mark on the outermost edge of his skin (line 2670). The last part to be refined is the farthest out. The cure comes through Aharon, who draws down a love from above.

**From inside.** Relief at not having to understand everything. And a quiet solidarity with every Jew, however far or simple, because the same hidden love sits in all of them. It can be chosen and brought out: "each one has the choice to bring it out of hiding" (line 2653).

**At the edge.** It reaches two edges. One is the mind's own: creation from nothing is not understood (line 2603). The other is the soul's: a love above understanding, from a root that "sees" though the person does not. The text says it is chosen and revealed most of all when a Jew is asked to give his life (line 2653).

**Into the day.** Next time you meet a Jew who seems far from everything, think: "His mazal sees."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Maamar 25: Vaydaber... Kedoshim Tihyu (Shabbat Acharei-Kedoshim 5666): the four letters of a teacher's love, and "My holiness is above yours" (lines 2672-2741)

**The movement.** It shows the mind the Name of God as a great teacher shrinking himself, step by step, for a small student. Then it gives the call to holiness together with its limit: be like Him, and know He is beyond you.

> כי קדוש אני, קדושתי למעלה מקדושתכם
> *"For I am holy": My holiness is above your holiness.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A great teacher with a small student cannot pour out his whole mind; the student would take in nothing. So he first holds everything back until only a point is left, the yud (line 2701). Then he spreads the point out in his own mind, the details this student can hold, the first heh. Then he draws it down in a short, straight line, the vav, like a Mishnah that holds much in few words. Then it spreads in the student's own mind, the last heh, where the student finally grasps it (line 2701). Then step by step. So God's Name, Havayah, is the way the Infinite came down to make a world that has no comparison to Him (line 2709). The last letter is "the power of the Maker within the made itself" (line 2710). That is why every creature, even the lowest, can praise His Name: its being comes from Havayah (line 2725). And a seed must rot before it sprouts; there is no new something without a nothing in between (line 2683). Then to the root. "Be holy, for I am holy." How can God's holiness be a reason for ours? Because the higher world is "like the One above": its letters are the same letters as the ones above it, as the bet taken out of the word "baruch" is still that bet (line 2681). A person, through the mitzvot and above all through what he does not do, can rise into that likeness (line 2736). Bring it home. Think of a time someone you respected made himself small to teach you, spoke slowly, used an example from your world. That lowering was love. The maamar asks you to see the letters of God's Name as that lowering, and to answer it by lowering yourself: by fences, by the "no". Then the limit, and here the picture of the teacher breaks. The Midrash hears a question: "Can you be like Me?" And answers: "My holiness is above yours" (line 2737). The student can grow toward the teacher; here the likeness is real and the distance stays.

**From inside.** Gratitude for being taught at all, for the smallness God took on so that you could grasp something. And a humility that is not sad: you can be holy, truly, and He is still above it.

**At the edge.** It points above the Name to the will before the contraction, where "the Infinite Light as it is before the contraction is not in the category of worlds at all" (line 2727). The text calls that "My holiness is above yours". The mind is left at the edge, with a holiness that can be imitated and a holiness that cannot.

**Into the day.** Keep one "fence" today, one small thing you do not do for the sake of holiness, and let it be your answer to His lowering.

## Maamar 26: Usfartem Lachem (Shabbat Emor 5666): hidden seeing, fish of the sea, and counting the days (lines 2742-2851)

**The movement.** It shows the mind a kind of knowing that comes before any detail, like seeing beauty before knowing why. Then it names two ways of seeing the unity, and brings them into the counting of the Omer, one day at a time.

> כח החכמה הוא שרואה ביטול כל דבר והתכללותו בבחי' אין, וכח הבינה הוא איך שהאין הוא בכל דבר
> *The power of wisdom sees each thing's nullification and its inclusion in the "nothing"; the power of understanding sees how the "nothing" is in each thing.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A man sees something beautiful and does not yet know what makes it beautiful; the fine lines that make the beauty are all there, and he sees only the whole (line 2761). The soul's hidden powers are like that: whole, simple, before any definition. At Sinai they "saw what is heard and heard what is seen" (line 2761). Then step by step. In the soul's hidden depths, wisdom is not an idea at all but a feeling of the Divine: it sees everything included in the Godly "nothing". Understanding feels how that "nothing" is inside each thing (line 2756). These are the two unities of the Shema: from above down and from below up. Then to the souls. In the hidden world everything is "like fish of the sea, which cannot be separated" (line 2774). Some souls carry that down. The world does not hide from them at all, and Yosef could sit over his accounts, busy with great affairs, and stay "a chariot" for God in the very act (line 2810). Bring it home. Most of us are not Yosef. So the Omer is given to us: forty-nine days, each drawing down a light to refine one trait of the animal soul, one day at a time (line 2823). Today is one of them, or like one of them. Pick one trait, kindness or strictness or pride, and let this day's light touch only that. The picture of beauty breaks, as the Rashab says: in a physical thing the details are really there and you only miss them. In the hidden powers there is no detail at all, only a simple wholeness that the mind cannot hold (line 2761).

**From inside.** Two gentle kinds of seeing. One looks at the things on your desk and sees them resting in God. The other looks at the same things and sees God resting in them. Neither needs you to leave your desk.

**At the edge.** It reaches the hidden sefirot, powers "with no definition at all", which "we cannot understand, for all our grasp is of what is defined" (line 2757). Above them the text puts the Infinite, where "the title of sefirah does not apply at all" (line 2785), and names Rabbi Shimon, who "searched the hidden treasures" (line 2834).

**Into the day.** Tonight or on the next night of the Omer, count, and name one trait you will work on tomorrow.

## Maamar 27: Ki Tavo'u el HaAretz (Shabbat Behar-Bechukotai 5666): the sun and its sheath, and the hiding that will shine (lines 2852-2972)

**The movement.** It lets the mind look at God's hiddenness from both sides at once, and then gives three answers to it, each deeper than the last: the hiding is only toward us; the hiding is our work; and the hiding itself, at its root, will shine. It turns the dark of the world from an obstacle into a place of meeting.

> שההעלם בעצמו יאיר
> *That the hiding itself will shine.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There are two knowledges, "real opposites" (line 2860). From the Giver's side, creation goes "from something to nothing": from the true Something, His Essence, to a glow that counts for nothing. From our side it goes "from nothing to something": we feel solid, and the power that makes us is hidden and is no "thing" at all (line 2868). "There are only three levels: Something, nothing, something" (line 2874). Then step by step. The sun is too strong to look at, and its light reaches the earth through a sheath, the way the Name Elokim sheathes the Name Havayah (line 2925). Without the sheath there would be no world. But "essence does not hide essence" (line 2891): the power that hides is itself the Essence, and the hiding is real only toward us. Then the work. Jacob "serves Elokim": he repairs the hiding. A person in a body wants physical things for themselves. He refuses, thing by thing, and works to feel the Godly life in them; the physical without that life is like a body without a soul, which soon rots (line 2938). Then the world stops hiding. "Through the world he sees Godliness more palpably", because "Havayah and Elokim are all one" (line 2938); the world itself becomes a parable that teaches its meaning. Then to the root. Even the servant who knows God through the hiding does not know Him as He is. The deepest thing is that "the root of the something and of all hiddenness is the essential hiddenness of the Infinite", higher than any revealing, and in the future "the hiding itself will shine" (line 2947), like "the cloud and the darkness, and it lit up the night". Bring it home. Think of the part of your life where God is hardest to find: a loss, a long wait, a grey stretch of work. The maamar does not say it is secretly bright. It says it is the sheath, that working in it is the service, and that its own root is higher than the light you miss. In the Temple the Ark took up no space at all; the Baal Shem Tov was not limited by distance or time (line 2956). Place and time stayed. They stopped hiding.

**From inside.** A strange steadiness in the dark places, not forced cheer. The work of Jacob becomes possible: to go on, thing by thing, seeing more through the world, not in spite of it. And under that, a patience with the dark, because the text says it will itself give light.

**At the edge.** This is one of the book's deepest edges. It goes past revealed Havayah to the Essence's own hiddenness, "a higher light beyond comparison" (line 2947). The text says it will shine in the future through the servant's present work, and that great souls taste it now, "you will see your world in your lifetime".

**Into the day.** In the hardest hour of your week, say once: "This is the sheath. He is here." Then do the next right thing.

## Maamar 28: Vaydaber... BeMidbar Sinai (Shabbat Bamidbar 5666): the face engraved on the Father's heart (lines 2973-3099)

**The movement.** It turns the mind from how God made the world to how God holds Israel in His thought, before anything else, like a father who carries his only son's face in his heart. Then it gives the way that face shines down: thirst in prayer, then light in Torah.

> הנה דמות דיוקנו חקוק על לבו ומוח זכרונו תמיד
> *His image is engraved on his heart and in his memory always.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "The thought of Israel came before everything" (line 2991). The world was made by God's speech. Seven things came before the world, from a higher place, His thought: Torah, teshuvah, Gan Eden and the rest. And before all of them, in the most ancient thought, were the souls of Israel. Then step by step. A man with an only son whom he loves and delights in carries the boy's face engraved on his heart and his memory always (line 3012). So Israel "rose in His thought". But a man can picture his son only after he is born and seen. Above, the thought comes first. And "My thoughts are not your thoughts": a man can think about a house all day and night and nothing is built; above, the moment it rose in thought, it was (line 3023). Then to the root. Every Jew, even "the emptiest", has a natural longing to be included in the One, and the power to give his life with no reason, only faith (line 3000). That is the light of the root shining below. The root of the soul is higher even than the Torah (line 3010). Yet the Torah is what makes it shine: the soul was made as a being of its own, like a son who is of his father's essence and still himself, and the Torah is one with God (line 3080). Then the order of service. First prayer, the desert: "my soul thirsts for You", a love like thirst (line 3096). Then Torah, the Tent of Meeting, where the light comes down. "A man's wisdom makes his face shine": Rabbi Eliezer's face lit up when he found a new teaching (line 3049). On Shabbat a person's face is not like his weekday face (line 3094). Bring it home. Think of a face you carry in your heart, someone you love, whom you can picture with your eyes closed. Now reverse it. Yours is carried like that. The picture breaks where the maamar says: the father sees his son and then remembers him; here the remembering made the son.

**From inside.** A sense of being known before you did anything. Not observed, held. And an easy thirst in prayer, followed by a quiet shining in learning, the kind of light that shows in a face.

**At the edge.** It points to the root of the soul above the yechidah, "a spark of Godliness" rooted in the inwardness of the first thought, higher even than the Torah (line 3010). The text says this is revealed in the soul only through Torah, after the thirst of prayer.

**Into the day.** Before you learn today, pray for one minute with the words "my soul thirsts for You", and then open the book.

## Maamar 29: VeAsita Chag Shavuot (first night of Shavuot 5666): three hearts, two contemplations, and the point that never forgets (lines 3100-3251)

**The movement.** It maps the heart in three layers and shows when the heart is moved by itself. Then it looks at forgetting, God's and ours, and finds under it a point in every Jew that nothing reaches, awake even when everything else sleeps.

> מהארת בחי' סוכ"ע, שמאיר בכל נפש מישראל גם בזה"ג בבחיי נקודת הלב שלמעלה מהדעת
> *From the glow of the surrounding light, which shines in every Jewish soul even in exile, as the point of the heart that is above knowing.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Torah below is a parable for what is above. A parable hides its meaning, yet only through it is a deep meaning grasped (line 3104). Solomon knew three thousand parables, each a picture of the level above it. Torah is like snow: water at the start, water at the end, and in between frozen so it can be held (line 3112). Then the heart. The outer heart thinks about the light that makes and fills the worlds, and has to work to be moved (line 3146). The inner heart thinks about the Infinite as it is in itself. The hidden heart does not think at all; it sees. "When the feeling is in the Godliness itself" and not only in one's grasp of it, "it shines by itself in the heart as well" (line 3153). Before any of this, the coarseness has to be cut away, by bitterness over one's distance, a broken heart (line 3155). Then two contemplations, side by side (line 3173). In one, physical things have no existence at all; they are now exactly as they were before they were made. In the other, they exist, "but their existence is Godliness". The Rashab says the second is not lower but higher: through it a person, even busy with physical things, "is not separate from Godliness", like eating on Shabbat. Then forgetting. "God has forsaken me, my Lord has forgotten me." Forgetting is not in God; it is in the layers that hide Him, and our forgetting below causes it above (line 3205). But no forgetting reaches the surrounding light. "I sleep, but my heart is awake": in every Jew, even in exile, there is a point of the heart above knowing that never goes out (line 3223). "Though she goes here and there, My image remains in you forever." Bring it home. Think of the weeks when you forgot God entirely, busy and dulled. Under all of it, the maamar says, a point was awake. Prayer here is "walking after Him" like a son who follows his father even when the father is busy and shows him no face, as Israel followed Him "in the desert, in a land not sown" (line 3166).

**From inside.** A deep reassurance: something in you never forgot. And, in the good moments, a heart that moves by itself because what you are thinking about is God and not your thought about God. Shavuot asks for generosity to match, a hand that gives more than it has to (line 3239).

**At the edge.** It reaches the surrounding light, "the Guardian of Israel who neither slumbers nor sleeps" (line 3221), and the point of the heart that is its glow in us, above knowing. The text calls it "Forever, O God, Your word stands in the heavens", rooted in a crown that never comes down (line 3223).

**Into the day.** When you notice you have forgotten God for hours, say without guilt: "My heart was awake." Then turn to Him.

## Maamar 30: HaShamayim Mesaprim (second night of Shavuot 5666): the father who hides, and the child who runs after him (lines 3252-3332)

**The movement.** It tells the Baal Shem Tov's parable of the father who hides to test his son, and puts the reader inside it. The mind is taught what to do in the dry seasons of prayer: not to weep and forget, but to run after the Father's footsteps without seeing His face.

> כ"א תיכף בהסתלקות האב ממנו, ירוץ אחריו לבקשו בדרכי נתיבות אביו
> *Rather, as soon as the father withdraws from him, he runs after him to seek him along his father's paths.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A father wants to test the son he loves: is he wise, is his love faithful? So he hides (line 3255). A foolish child thinks the distance is real. He weeps bitterly that he will never see his father again, gives up, and after a while forgets and stops crying. So his love was not whole either. Then step by step. A wise child understands that the distance is not real, only a test, and that by searching he will double his father's love. So he does not cry. At once he runs after his father along his father's paths. He does not see him, but he knows the roads his father walks, and runs to see him at least from behind (line 3261). When the father sees how hard the child is trying, running in the dark, his love doubles; he turns his face to him and kisses him with a double kiss, and the love that was hidden in the hiding "is revealed from darkness to light". Then into prayer. There are times when nothing opens: the mind will not go deep, the heart will not move, even ordinary understanding is heavy (line 3263). The wise child still thinks it through: all the worlds are only a glow, "all before Him is as nothing", so the soul should want nothing but Him. He feels it only on the outside of his will. He does it anyway. Then all day he holds to Torah and mitzvot, "only because he knows that the truth is" that they are His wisdom and will, though he feels nothing (line 3278). Bring it home. Think of your last dry month. Did you weep and then forget? Or did you keep walking the paths you know He walks: the blessings, the learning, the kindnesses? The maamar says that is how Egypt led to Sinai. They walked into a desert, "a land not sown", after Him, from far away, and He answered with "I am" (line 3274). The picture breaks where it must. A human father hides and could also fail to come back. The maamar says the hiding is not real, and that the last and longest exile, "I will surely hide My face", will end in "they will all know Me", a double kiss (line 3278).

**From inside.** Stubborn tenderness. The dryness stops being proof that you have lost Him and becomes the shape of a game you understand. There is also a certain lightness in the running: you do not need to see His face to know which way He went.

**At the edge.** It reaches past all revealed light to "the essence of the light", the far surrounding light that has no relation to the worlds at all (line 3316), which Shavuot brings down like a wedding canopy. And it names the revelation at Sinai: "I", whom none knows but Himself, came into "your God" (line 3274).

**Into the day.** On a day when prayer feels empty, keep one fixed time of learning anyway and tell yourself: "I am walking His paths."

## Maamar 31: Naso (Shabbat Naso 5666): the "do" that clothes you and the "do not" that reaches the Self (lines 3333-3406)

**The movement.** It sets the 248 things we do beside the 365 things we hold back from, and asks which reaches higher. The mind is walked from garments to house, from white hair to black hair, until it sees that holding back touches the very Essence, and that this is why the "do nots" still stand in the darkest exile.

> כי הלאו מגיע יותר בפנימיות ועצמות הנפש
> *For the "no" reaches further into the inwardness and essence of the soul.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A garment fits your size. A house surrounds you from far off (line 3346). The mitzvot we do are like garments: they make vessels, and the light they bring down is the near surrounding light. The mitzvot we hold back from make no vessel at all, "do not", and so they draw from the Essence of the light that is above any flow (line 3346). Then step by step. Think of a truly noble person. Small pleasures do not move him; he is too high for them. But show him something he hates, and he stands up inside and says: I will keep far from this (line 3378). The "no" wakes him where the "yes" could not. So too above: the things against His will touch "the loftiness of the Essence, what is not in the category of flowing down at all" (line 3378). Then the picture of hair. White hair, pure as wool, is the kindness that can come into the open. Black hair, curled, is the "no", the hidden light that does not shine now (line 3383). What we know of it now we know only by negation: it is not this, not that. Bring it home. You may feel that holding back from something is dry and small, a mere "not doing". The maamar says it reaches deeper than you think. The moment you turn away from what He does not want, the essence of your soul is standing up. And this is why, in exile, when most of the "do" mitzvot cannot be kept, the "do nots" all still apply, and they keep a hidden love alive "even in the time of darkness" (line 3398). Like Neilah at the end of Yom Kippur, it wakes even the farthest. The picture breaks here. A human's hatred is a feeling that comes and goes. The "no" above is not an emotion at all. And the maamar does not let the "no" stand alone: Torah, it ends, is what draws this far light inward (line 3401).

**From inside.** A quiet firmness. There is a place in you that does not need to feel anything in order to say "not this". Standing there is its own kind of closeness, plain and dark and sure.

**At the edge.** It points to the Essence of light before the tzimtzum, "what is not in the category of flowing at all", which will be revealed only in the future (line 3383). Now it is known only as black fire, by what it is not.

**Into the day.** Today, when you hold back from one thing you should not do, say to yourself: "This 'no' touches Him."

## Maamar 32: UReisem Oso (Shabbat Shelach 5666): the white and blue threads, and the reflection of a reflection (lines 3407-3491)

**The movement.** It asks why the "do not" mitzvot guard the "do" mitzvot, and answers with the yechidah, the single will of a Jew that cannot be split from God. The mind is taken from the danger of the mind's loose thoughts, to the one will that no darkness can hold, to Torah as a roofed house.

> שאינו יכול להיות נפרד מאלקות בשום אופן
> *that he cannot be separated from Godliness in any way at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The sages say demons have a reflection, but not a reflection of a reflection (line 3441). The first reflection is the near surrounding light: the other side has one too. The reflection of a reflection is the yechidah, the single point of the soul that receives from the Single One, the Essence itself. That the other side does not have. Then step by step. The other side also has strong will; it is even harder, like cedar, while holiness bends like a reed (line 3448). But its strength could be otherwise. A person there wants to give his life for his will; he is not forced by it. A Jew's inner will is a different kind: it cannot be otherwise. So when the time comes he gives his life without choosing to, though he wants to live, because nothing stands against that bond. And that will is one, not many. The wills of the world fight and weaken each other; the soul's single will has no parts. Then the danger. Thought is always running, because it comes from the soul itself, and it never stops (line 3432). An empty mind fills with idle and bad thoughts, and these draw a heavier darkness down, doubled. That is why "a person sanctifies himself a little and they sanctify him a lot" (line 3423). The same law works both ways. And sometimes a stray thought falls on you from outside, even when you did not want it (line 3439). Bring it home. Think of a time a bad thought would not leave. The maamar does not say to fight it with feeling. It says: fill the house. Torah is a roofed house; the courtyard lets in rain and dirt, but a house covered on top keeps the rubbish out (line 3474). When the mind is dressed in learning, head to foot, nothing can feed from it. And "a sin does not put out Torah" (line 3474). The picture breaks where a house is only walls. Torah here is not only protection; it draws from the Essence, and at the end the white threads and the blue thread of tzitzis meet: the blue, which is not worn now, is the hidden light that will be seen (line 3488).

**From inside.** Relief. Under all the many wants there is one want that cannot be moved. You do not have to make it; you only have to stop covering it. And with it comes a clear sense that the mind should not be left empty.

**At the edge.** It reaches "the essential bond of Jewish souls with the Essence of Ein Sof" (line 3441), a place with no opposite at all, since against the Essence there is no other side (line 3453).

**Into the day.** When your mind is free today, on a walk or in line, give it one line of Torah to hold instead of leaving it open.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Maamar 33: Vayikach Korach (no date header; added in the glosses to the series): the will that moves the hand, and the all-blue tallis (lines 3492-3581)

**The movement.** It starts from Korach's trick questions, a tallis all of blue and a house full of books, and turns to your own body. The mind is shown how your will moves your hand without being caught in it, and from there how His will fills every world the same, and why even the highest light must still come down through the plain threads of a mitzvah.

> ותופס בהלבוש להניעו ולעשות בו כחפצו, והלבוש אין לו שום תפיסא בו
> *He takes hold of the garment to move it and do with it as he wishes, and the garment has no hold on him at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Your mind lives in your brain; the state of the brain shapes the state of the thinking. Your feelings live in your heart. That is light in a vessel: the vessel holds the light, and the light is shared out, more in the head, less in the foot. But your will is different. When you want to stretch out your hand, the hand moves at once. Yet the hand gets no more life from the will than it had before (line 3544). Then step by step. The will holds the hand; the hand does not hold the will. It is like a person in a garment: he moves it as he wishes, and the garment has no grip on him (line 3544). And the will is the same everywhere. The same will that moves your mind to think is the one that moves your foot to walk (line 3547). It does not come in pieces. Then lift it up. So too with Him. "He holds all the worlds and none can hold Him." None of the worlds can grasp Him, yet His will reaches the lowest, the stars in the sky and the grass in the field, and "all is according to His will" (line 3560). The same will runs the highest worlds and this one, with no difference at all. Bring it home. Look at your hand as you turn a page. Something you cannot see is moving it, and your hand does not feel it as a thing. That is how near He is to every part of your life, not as a feeling you can hold, but as the will that moves it. Now Korach: he said a tallis all of blue, the far high light, needs no fringes; a house full of books needs no mezuzah (line 3579). Enough with the high light; skip the plain threads. Here the picture turns. Korach was wrong. The Essence of the far surrounding light does not come inward by itself. It comes only through the plain mitzvah, through Aharon's hair, through fringes on a corner (line 3579). The high light needs the low thread to reach you.

**From inside.** A calm sense of being carried. You do not have to feel His will to be moved by it. And a small humility: the grand light does not excuse you from the small string.

**At the edge.** It points to the true Keter, the surrounding light that is "separate in value" from all the worlds and does not shine in them part by part (line 3552), the tallis that is all blue.

**Into the day.** When you do one small mitzvah today, touch it with your hand and think: "This is where the far light comes in."

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Maamar 34: Az Yashir (Shabbat Chukas 5666): the well that is dug from below (lines 3582-3695)

**The movement.** It follows the long chain of narrowing by which endless light reaches a limited world, like a teacher shrinking his thought for a student. Then it turns: a home for Him below needs more than light coming down. It needs a soul that digs through its own earth until its simple will, "to You I lift my soul", comes up like water from a well.

> והוא בוקע חשך הגוף ונה"ב להיות בבחי' רצון פשוט לה' לבד
> *And he breaks through the darkness of the body and the animal soul, to be a simple will for God alone.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. How does the Endless reach a world with limits? A teacher cannot pour his whole mind into a student. First he shrinks it to what the student can hold; then he says it in words; and even then the student grasps only the edge (line 3622). There are many narrowings between what the teacher knows and what the student hears. So too the Infinite comes to us through narrowing after narrowing (line 3638). Then the turn. But when bride and groom stand face to face, the student gets the teacher's own understanding, poured "like from vessel to vessel", with no change at all (line 3675). That is what He wants: a home below where His Essence is here just as it is above (line 3680). Torah brings it down. But Torah is wisdom, and the Essence cannot be held by wisdom. So what holds it? A simple will: "to You, God, I lift my soul" (line 3680). Then the well. Sea water is salty; when it breaks through the earth it comes up sweet. The soul above had no such will. Only down here, clothed in a body that pulls toward other wants, when it breaks through that darkness to want God alone, does it become "a well of living water" (line 3680). Bring it home. The earth over your well is real: the wants that cover your one want. The maamar says a well must be dug. That digging is honest reckoning, a bitterness at how far you are (line 3680). And before sleep we say "into Your hand I entrust my spirit"; think whom you are giving your soul to, and what you did with it today. A soul that is sorry, that cries out, becomes a spring from below (line 3693). The picture breaks where a well is only a hole in the ground. Here the water that rises calls down an answer: "sing to it", the drawing from above (line 3695). Your digging and His answering are one song.

**From inside.** A clean ache. Not sadness that sinks, but the ache of someone who has found the water under the dirt and wants it to run. With it, a quiet thirst that is itself sweet.

**At the edge.** It points to the Essence of Ein Sof shining below just as above, with no change (line 3680), which no wisdom can hold and only a simple will can catch.

**Into the day.** Tonight before sleep, when you say "into Your hand I entrust my spirit", stop for a moment and think whose hand it is.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Maamar 35: Ki Merosh Tzurim (Shabbat Balak 5666): the dancers who move apart, and the rock under the world (lines 3696-3788)

**The movement.** It climbs the four ways the moon can receive from the sun, from the tiny point to face to face and beyond. Then it stops at the lowest point, the soul crying from far away, and shows that this very distance is how the greatest closeness is made. It ends on the Patriarchs, the rock on which He could finally build His world.

> כי כך היא המדה בכ"מ, שמסיבת תכלית הריחוק יבוא תכלית שלימות הקירוב
> *For this is the rule everywhere: out of the utmost distance comes the utmost fullness of closeness.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The moon (Malchus) can receive from the sun in four ways, each higher than the last: from the very end of the chain, from the middle, face to face, and at last both drinking from the same source, "equal in height" (line 3726). But before face to face, she must first become a tiny point, as small as can be (line 3728). Then step by step. What is that point in you? It is prayer where your mind grasps almost nothing of God. You only know, in a general way, that you are far, and it hurts. You are not moved by Godliness, only by the distance. "From afar God appeared to me" (line 3728). It is the child who sees his father only from behind, and walks in his father's paths (line 3731). Then the rule. Think of dancers facing each other. They step back, apart, and then come together, closer than if they had never moved away (line 3733). That is what the distance was for. The walking behind, in the desert, "your going after Me in the wilderness", is what makes "I remember the kindness of your youth" (line 3733). Bring it home. The maamar is honest: even your deepest thought in prayer, how all is as nothing before Him, is only seeing Him from behind; the thing itself is far from you (line 3735). Do not be discouraged by that. The strength of the walking is what makes Him turn His face, first with a double kiss, then with the light of the King's face inside the soul (line 3740). The picture breaks where dancers choose their steps and know the music. You do not know when the turn will come. And the rule is not a trick for feeling close: the goal is His home below, and it was built on the Patriarchs, a rock under the waters of chaos (line 3772), and on their children, a people that dwells alone.

**From inside.** A hopeful patience. The far place stops feeling like failure and starts feeling like the first half of a dance. With it, a quiet pride in being one of the stones He builds on.

**At the edge.** It points to the moon and sun both receiving from the very essence of the mind, above any link to feelings (line 3726), and to "My own Name", the Essence where the souls are rooted (line 3772).

**Into the day.** When you feel far today, say one sentence to Him anyway, and tell yourself: "This is the step back in the dance."

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Maamar 36: Titen Emes LeYaakov (no date header in these lines; between Balak and Matos-Masei 5666): Yaakov who toils and Yisrael who sings (lines 3789-3878)

**The movement.** It shows that everything we can contemplate, all the worlds and all their delights, is only the glow of His kingship, "behind" Him. That very thought is meant to make the soul thirsty for Him Himself. Then it sets two ways side by side: Yaakov, who labors through the hiding, and Yisrael, who sings without labor, and asks that Yaakov be given the truth of truth.

> שכל רצונותיו בטלים לגבי צמאונו למים ואין לו רצון אחר כלל
> *All his wants fall away before his thirst for water, and he has no other want at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A king sits; sitting is "the back" (line 3809). All the worlds, up to the highest, all the Garden of Eden and all its rising delights, are only the glow of His kingship, a light of a light, not touching Him (line 3819). He is "single, alone, as before the world was made"; His living in the worlds is only as King. Then step by step. In prayer we linger on this: blessed be the Name of His glorious kingdom, only a glow. Why? So that the soul will say "whom do I have in heaven" and become thirsty (line 3829). Not hungry, thirsty. A thirsty person wants water so badly that all his other wants fall away. That is the goal: to see that all you can grasp is only a glow, and so to want Him. Then Yaakov. The one who serves this way labors hard (line 3839). The light does not shine openly. The animal soul throws in stray thoughts right when you try to go deep; "the time of prayer is the time of battle" (line 3847). Yisrael is different: the Name shines openly, and his prayer is like Shabbat prayer, the soul delighting in God "like a spark to the flame", pouring out in song without effort (line 3849). Bring it home. Most of us are Yaakov. Our prayer is work, and we see Him from behind. Do not despise that. Notice what song does to you: a melody can lift you past what you understand, and "all masters of song go out in song" (line 3857). And ask, as the verse asks, "give truth to Yaakov": let the one who toils be given the truth that is above all the worlds (line 3877). The picture of thirst breaks here. Thirst for water is filled by water. This thirst is for what no glow can give, and the maamar says so openly: we do not contemplate the Essence itself; we contemplate the glow, and from knowing it is only a glow, we thirst (line 3831).

**From inside.** A sharp, clean longing. The good things of the world, even the holy ones, stop being enough, not because they are bad but because they are only light. And with the longing, a relief that the labor itself is counted.

**At the edge.** It points to "the truth of truth", the Name Havaya above all the worlds (line 3877), and to Yisrael's song, where the soul goes out of the body's walls to cling to its real will (line 3857).

**Into the day.** When you say "Baruch Shem Kevod Malchuso" today, think: "All this is only His glow; I want Him."

## Maamar 37: UMikneh Rav (Shabbat Matos-Masei 5666): the son, the faithful servant, and the plain servant (lines 3879-3947)

**The movement.** It lays out three ways to serve: the son, for whom God's will is his own will; the trusted servant who works from love of his master's greatness; and the plain servant who carries a yoke against his nature. Then it shows that the plain servant, the one who goes one step past his habit, is the one called "a servant of God", and that his work wins a land the others could not.

> שאינו דומה השונה פרקו מאה פעמים למאה פעמים ואחד
> *One who reviews his chapter a hundred times is not like one who reviews it a hundred and one times.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A son loves his father by nature. What his father wants, he wants; the work is no burden, he does it with joy, "more than for himself" (line 3888). Some souls are like this with God. Their minds and hearts match His will, and they do not feel the yoke at all. Then the servant. A plain servant does not want the work. It is heavy; he is woken at night; yet he takes it on, until carrying it becomes like nature to him, as an ox gets used to the yoke. Why? Because he has given himself away. "This is how it must be" (line 3894). He is not a thing on his own; all he earns belongs to his master (line 3896). There is also a higher servant who works from love and joy, because he knows how great his master is, and is trusted with the whole house (line 3898). Then the surprise. Who is called "a servant of God"? Not the one who learns a hundred times because he loves learning. The one who learns the hundred-and-first time, past his habit, against his grain (line 3929). The stingy man who gives more than his nature allows. The angry man who holds his tongue; the one who hears himself insulted and does not answer (line 3935). Bring it home. Find your hundredth time: the thing you already do well because you like it. That is good, but it is your nature. Now find the hundred-and-first: one step past where you would stop. People see only the deed; "God sees the heart" (line 3938), whether a yoke was taken on there. The picture breaks where a human servant is sad and owns nothing. David asks, "gladden the soul of Your servant" (line 3927): the joy is God's to give, not the servant's to make. And the servant's land is not less: Reuven and Gad's side of the Jordan becomes an inheritance through the war with the animal soul (line 3944).

**From inside.** A sober dignity. There is no glow here, only the plain strength of one more step. With it comes a kind of freedom: the work is no longer about how you feel.

**At the edge.** It points to the son's soul, drawn "from the inwardness of the Essence of Ein Sof" (line 3888), and to the running of the land of Canaan, "whom do I have in heaven" (line 3940), which even the plain servant is promised if he fights.

**Into the day.** Pick one thing you do every day, and today do it one time more, or one minute longer, than you would have.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Maamar 38: Eileh HaDevarim (no date header in these lines; summer 5666): why the plain servant never changes (lines 3948-4020)

**The movement.** It weighs the trusted servant, who serves with joy from knowing how great his Master is, against the plain servant who serves only under the yoke. The mind is led to a hard, freeing truth: the service that has nothing of "me" in it is the one that never wavers. It ends with the two kinds of rebuke, the hint for the heart that is already awake and the open word for the one that needs it.

> שהעבד אינו מציאות לעצמו, ואינו מהות בפ"ע כלל מן האדון
> *The servant is not a being of his own, and not a separate thing from his master at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The trusted servant is great. He knows his Master's greatness and gives himself over completely, not for anything he gets. Like Eliezer with Avraham, Yehoshua with Moshe (line 3962). His work adds blessing to the house beyond what it had (line 3976). Even his giving up of self is not a squeezing of the soul; he is simply glad (line 4010). Then the turn. Yet the maamar says the plain servant has an advantage. Love has two sides: the one who loves and the one who is loved. The Master does not change, but the servant does. Sometimes the love is hidden; sometimes the sense of the Master's greatness is dim (line 3998). Even Moshe, the faithful one, missed once at the rock (line 3999). Then step by step. The plain servant serves only because he must. There is nothing of himself in it to go up or down. So his work is always the same: chopping wood and drawing water are the same to him as setting a fine table, because he does it all for his Master (line 3995). "The servant is not a being of his own" (line 3999). Bring it home. Your good days in prayer, when love is awake, are real. But notice how often they change. The part of your service that does not depend on your mood, the plain showing up, is the part that holds. Do not despise it as lower. It is the steadiest thing you have, and it is called "real work" (line 4002). Here the picture of a human servant breaks. A slave's lowness is sad. This servant's lowness is his bond: he has no self standing between him and his Master. And the rebuke in Devarim first comes as a hint, because "my heart is awake" (line 4019): the simple will in every Jew needs only a hint to wake.

**From inside.** A steady, low peace. You stop checking your feelings before you begin. The work goes on whether the heart is warm or not, and there is quiet strength in that.

**At the edge.** It points to the servant who knows "the loftiness and wonder of Ein Sof" and lays himself down completely (line 4007), and to the simple will hidden in every Jew, which wakes at a whisper (line 4019).

**Into the day.** Do one plain task today, the kind that seems beneath you, for His sake, and do it the same as you would a fine one.

## Maamar 39: Acharei Hashem Elokeichem Telechu (no date header in these lines; summer 5666): bread earned by your own hands (lines 4021-4088)

**The movement.** It asks why "walk after God", the lowest rung, is listed first, and answers through the plain servant. His Master's pleasure is his pleasure; what he does, his Master is said to do. And what he makes from nothing by his own sweat is sweeter to the Master than any gift, because it is new. So the lowest walking reaches "cleave to Him", essence to essence.

> כי גם במעט פרנסה מיגיעה ועמל נפשו, ישלים נפשו הרבה יותר מכל הון שמתעשר מהתאמצות אחרים זולתו
> *For even a small living earned by his own toil satisfies his soul far more than any wealth he gains from the efforts of others.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Even the plain servant, who has no love for his master, sometimes works extra hard to make a thing beautiful. Why? Because he has given himself away so completely that his master's pleasure has become his own (line 4027). He is like one of his master's limbs. Then step by step. When an ox plows a field, we do not say the ox grew the crop; we say the owner did, through his ox. So the plain servant's work is called by his Master's name: "the Master Himself did it, only through His servants" (line 4035). The trusted servant, who figures out how to do things with his own wisdom, is still somebody. The plain servant is not. Then the heart of it. A person who earns a little by his own hands is happier with it than with a fortune handed to him by friends (line 4043). Why? Because it is new; he made it from nothing. So too above. The plain servant, heavy and far, with no light to help him, turns his own nature around by his own effort. That is a real renewal, from one thing to another (line 4070). And it draws down "a new light, renewed from its root" (line 4084). Bring it home. Think of your work, your business, your cooking, your field. Honest dealing, food eaten for His sake, the long day done faithfully: this is the gathering of sparks, the plainest service (line 4080). You may feel it is far from holiness. The maamar says it is the very work of the plain servant, and it reaches higher than revealing light: "those who sow in tears will reap in joy" (line 4084). The picture breaks where an ox is only an ox. This servant chose his yoke with his own strength; even a king rules only when he is accepted (line 4063). And his lowness is the door to the highest: "and to Him you shall cleave", essence to essence (line 4086).

**From inside.** A quiet satisfaction in plain effort. The labor stops feeling like a distraction from God and starts to feel like the very place He wanted to be served. With it, a lowly pride: this piece was made by hand.

**At the edge.** It points to "cleaving of the essence of the soul to the Essence of Ein Sof, essence in essence" (line 4086), reached not through light but through giving up all of oneself.

**Into the day.** At work today, do one honest thing that costs you, and say quietly: "This is His bread, made by my hands."

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Maamar 40: VeHayah Ki Savo (Shabbat Ki Savo 5666): why the earth can do what heaven cannot (lines 4089-4180)

**The movement.** It starts from Adam's curse, "by the sweat of your brow you will eat bread", and finds the blessing hidden in it. The mind is taken from manna to bread of the earth, from blessing to prayer, and down to the rotting seed. It ends at the rule that the end is wedged into the beginning: the lowest earth holds the power to make something new, which the heavens do not have.

> כ"א לאחר שנרקב ונתבטל לגמרי לכח הצומח שבארץ, ואז תוציא צמחה
> *Only after it has rotted and given itself up completely to the growing power in the earth, then the earth brings out its growth.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. There are two kinds of livelihood. One comes down from above, set on Rosh Hashanah and shared out day by day, like manna, bread from heaven, with no waste in it and no toil (line 4105). The other comes up from the earth, and it takes plowing and sowing, sweat and worry. That second kind is judged "every moment", and it renews every moment (line 4110). It is a curse, and it is the bigger blessing. Then step by step. Blessing only draws down what is already in the root. Prayer asks for something new, a new will that was not there (line 4125). And the whole chain of worlds is only light that was first hidden and then shown; nothing really new. A truly new light comes only from the Essence itself, and it comes through the plain servant's toil (line 4134). Then the earth. Look up: the stars never add one to their number; the heavens have not changed since the six days (line 4153). Look down: the earth brings up more every year, grain from a seed and trees from nothing. But a seed kept whole grows nothing. Only after it rots and gives itself up to the earth does it sprout (line 4163). Why can the low earth do what the high heavens cannot? Because "their end is wedged in their beginning" (line 4172). The lowest holds the power of the very first. Bring it home. Think of the days you worked hard and saw nothing, when you felt low and even hopeless in the field. The maamar says that very giving-up of self is the rotting of the seed; out of it comes growth beyond measure. "Those who sow in tears will reap in song" (line 4108). Your mitzvot are seeds; you, a Jewish soul, are the "land of desire" (line 4175). The picture breaks where a seed rots whether it wants to or not. Here the giving-up is chosen: plain work, honest dealing, trust in God and not in cleverness, charity past your habit (line 4120). And each year on Rosh Hashanah the old life leaves and a new light comes (line 4178).

**From inside.** A sturdy hope that does not need to see results. The low, heavy days take on meaning: something is rotting so that something can grow. Under it, a quiet trust.

**At the edge.** It points to "a new light, renewed from its root", the Essence of Ein Sof shining not by hiding and showing (line 4134), and to the power to make something from nothing that lives only in the Essence and in the lowest end.

**Into the day.** On a day your work feels fruitless, give a little charity from what you earned, and think: "I am sowing."

## Maamar 41: Ki HaMitzvah HaZos (Shabbat Nitzavim-Vayelech 5666): the finished building and the first thought (lines 4181-4251)

**The movement.** It uses a person building a house: the plan comes first, then mind, heart, words, hands, and at last the finished building, which was what he wanted from the start. So too above: the lowest point, the deed, is wedged into the very Beginning. The mind is led to see why the practical mitzvah is "not in heaven" and "not across the sea" but here, in your hand, because it comes from the Essence itself.

> ומשו"ז גופא ירדו למטה מטה בבחי' עשיה, לפי שהן בבחי' העצמות ממש
> *And for this very reason they came down lowest of all, into doing: because they are of the very Essence.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. A man decides to build a house. First a hidden, firm will. Then reasons, then feelings, then thinking it over, then telling the builders, then the actual building. At each step the will gets smaller and more outward; the deed is the most outward of all (line 4190). Yet when the building is finished, he delights in it as he did at the very start, because that is what he wanted all along (line 4201). Without the deed, all the wanting is nothing. Then step by step. "Their end is wedged in their beginning." What is the Beginning? Not a first moment in time. It is He Himself, from whom everything is, not as a source tied to what comes from it, but as the Essence, wholly apart (line 4209). Even "light" cannot be called "the Beginning" (line 4224). And that Beginning is lodged more in the end than in the high spreading head (line 4232). Then why. The high levels are all about showing light. The lowest, Malchus, is a hidden power, rooted in the Essence's own hiddenness; so only it can make something from nothing (line 4234). That is why the earth grows new things and the heavens do not. Bring it home. "This mitzvah is not wondrous for you, and not far." It is not in the heavens of high wisdom, not across the sea of the deepest hidden (line 4250). It is the deed: the coin in the charity box, the tefillin on your arm. You may think the deed is the least spiritual part. It came down so low precisely because it comes from the Essence. The last in doing is the first in thought. The picture breaks where a builder needs the house and is changed by it. He needs nothing. His "delight" in the deed is not lack being filled; it is His own Essence reaching all the way down, "near to you, in your mouth and your heart, to do it".

**From inside.** A grounded closeness. The plain act stops feeling like a small outer thing and feels like the place where He is most fully present. With it, an easy confidence: it is near, you can do it.

**At the edge.** It points to "the Beginning", the Essence that is before all and "separate entirely" from what is found from Him (line 4209), above even the light that is called Ein Sof.

**Into the day.** When you do a mitzvah with your hands today, hold still for a moment and think: "The end was the first thought."

## Maamar 42: Tiku BaChodesh Shofar (a Rosh Hashanah maamar; no date header in these lines): the hidden staircase (lines 4252-4325)

**The movement.** It sets two ways of refining side by side: light pouring down so that the lower thing melts away by itself, and the slow work from below, where you dress your understanding in words your animal soul can grasp, until it wants God too. Only the second makes the lower thing itself a home for Him. Then it pictures that rising as a hidden spiral staircase, and ends at the shofar, the moon hidden on Rosh Hashanah.

> והעלי' בה הוא שלא בהדרגה, שאינו מרגיש שעולה, ופתאום הוא למעלה
> *The climb on it is not by visible steps; he does not feel that he is rising, and suddenly he is at the top.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Some refining is done from above: a great soul shines, and the body and the world around it simply fall into line, like Moshe's body (line 4256). There is no labor in it. Other refining is done from below, by going into the thing itself. That is labor, and it is the lower thing rising by its own strength (line 4258). Then step by step. How do you refine your animal soul? Not by soaring past it. You bring your Godly understanding down into terms it can follow: in every thing there is a Godly power that makes it and keeps it alive; "from my flesh I see God" (line 4260). You work until even your natural mind agrees that the Godliness is the main thing, and the food only its outer shell. Then you eat what you need, for His sake, and the spark in it gives you strength to learn and pray. Then the staircase. Some stairs are outside and you see each step, like a ladder. Others are hidden, a spiral stair around a pillar: you do not see the top, you do not feel you are climbing, and suddenly you are there (line 4281). When the animal soul, which wanted only the things of this world, comes to love God, it jumps from the lowest to very high, not step by step. Even when it happens slowly, each bit is such a jump. Bring it home. You may feel your inner work is going nowhere: the same explanations, the same small choices at meals and at work. The maamar says you cannot see this staircase from inside. And this, only this, fulfills why He made the world: that the lower thing itself become His vessel (line 4290), as it will at the resurrection, when bodies will live on the light of the Presence (line 4295). The picture breaks here. A staircase leads to a floor that was already there. This climb makes something new, "from nothing", because the Beginning is wedged in the end (line 4314). On Rosh Hashanah the moon is hidden, rising into the Essence's own hiddenness, and the shofar is the soul giving itself over (line 4322).

**From inside.** Trust in hidden progress. You stop measuring your growth by what you can feel. And a warm patience with your own animal soul, which you are teaching, not fighting only.

**At the edge.** It points to the moon rising into "the hidden Essence of Ein Sof" on Rosh Hashanah (line 4322), and to the time when the lower world will know "that You made it" (line 4305).

**Into the day.** At one meal today, before eating, think one plain sentence your body can follow: "God's word in this food keeps me alive to serve Him."

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Maamar 43: Kadosh Atah (5667, per the editor's cross-reference): the crown of a good name and the light that bounces back (lines 4326-4400)

**The movement.** It reads the four crowns, Torah, priesthood, kingship, and the crown of a good name that rises over them all, as light going down and light coming back up. The mind is shown, with the sun striking a cloud and a ball thrown at a wall, why the returning light is stronger. Then it names what it reaches: "My mitzvot", the mitzvot as they are in His very Self, drawn by the plain servant whose work is as if the Master Himself had done it.

> וכמו הזורק בכח, הרי עיקר הכח יתאסף בסוף הנחתו
> *Like one who throws with force: the main force gathers at the end of its landing.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Light going down is strongest at the top and weakens as it goes, like a will that is strongest at the start and thinnest in the final deed (line 4343). But there is also returning light, where the lowest becomes the crown. Its spread is smaller, but its force is greater. Then step by step. Throw a ball hard at a wall. The force gathers at the end, where it hits, so hard that it bounces back (line 4351). The sun's heat is strongest where it strikes the ground, which is why the air near the earth is warmer. When sunlight strikes a cloud, a rainbow is born, a returning light, stronger than the plain rays. Some jewels shine outward; others flash back into your eye so sharply you must look away. Then the crowns. The crowns of Torah, priesthood and kingship are the surrounding lights of the order of worlds. The crown of a good name is Malchus after she has gone down into the lowest world, gathered the sparks, and risen with them (line 4341). "The stone the builders despised has become the cornerstone" (line 4369): David, the fourth leg, the lowest, made a name. Bring it home. The plain work of refining, done under the yoke, with no feeling of light, is the ball hitting the wall. It is where the force gathers. The trusted servant adds blessing to what already exists. The plain servant draws "My mitzvot", the mitzvot as they are in God's own Self, and his work counts as if the Master Himself had done it (line 4387). "Gladden the soul of Your servant": the Master's own joy becomes his. The picture breaks here. A ball bounces back because of the wall. Here the rising back is the whole purpose: the Essence planned it from the start. And what it reaches is not a light at all, but "the light included in His very Self", His Name that is one with Him (line 4392).

**From inside.** A strange strength in being low. The hard, lightless work stops feeling like the bottom of the scale and starts feeling like the place of impact, where the force is greatest. And a quiet joy that is not your own.

**At the edge.** It points to "the light included in His very Essence, which is not a light at all", "He and His Name are one" (line 4392), and to the holiness that is holy in itself, not only set apart from others (line 4399).

**Into the day.** Before doing one hard duty today, think: "This is the ball hitting the wall; this is where the force is."

## Maamar 44: BaSukkos Teishvu (second night of Sukkot 5667): the wise man's softness and the crown's nothingness (lines 4401-4473)

**The movement.** It sets two kinds of "nothing" side by side. The first is the humility of wisdom, which softens the feelings so that opposites can live together, as in a grown-up compared to a child. The second is the nothingness of Keter, where the self is simply not there because the light is so strong. The sukkah is that second nothing, and "you shall dwell" means it must become settled and lasting, not a passing mood.

> שיהי' בחי' ביטול זה בבחי' קביעות בנפש, ולא בדרך עראי לבד
> *That this self-nullification be fixed and lasting in the soul, and not only passing.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Feelings are born from thought. If you think deeply about how good something is, you love it more; stop thinking about it and the love goes cold, even if it stands right in front of you (line 4439). Even fear of a real danger lives on thought; stop thinking about it and you stop being afraid. Then step by step. Watch a small child: whatever he wants, he wants with all his force, and he cannot stand anything in his way. A grown-up wants things too, even more strongly, yet he can hold back and make room for the opposite (line 4442). That is the mind softening the feelings. A truly wise person is humble, because he knows he has not reached the truth of anything, sees his own faults, and sees the good in others (line 4444). Only such a mind can hold two opposites at once. Then the turn. But that humility is weakness of light: the mind feels something higher and gives way. The mind itself is still a thing (line 4463). Keter's nothingness is different. It comes from strength of light, not weakness. The Infinite shines there so fully that there is no self to feel: "the head that does not know and is not known" (line 4470). Bring it home. Your humble moments are good; they let you make room for others and hold hard truths together. But notice how they come from thinking, and fade when the thinking stops. The sukkah asks for more: to sit in that nothingness, the one Rosh Hashanah's acceptance of the King and Yom Kippur's return from the depths have drawn down. And to sit in it, "dwell", as you would in your home, not only visit (line 4472). The picture breaks where a sukkah is a flimsy hut you leave after a week. Here the temporary dwelling is where the self-nullification is meant to become fixed. It is lasting only when it moves from surrounding you to being inside you.

**From inside.** A spacious quiet. The need to win, to be right, loosens. And under that, a deeper stillness where there is simply less of you in the way.

**At the edge.** It points to the "Ayin" of Keter, which "does not know itself", nullified in its very being because Ein Sof shines there openly (line 4463), a nothingness born of strength and not of weakness (line 4470).

**Into the day.** Sit in the sukkah today, or in one quiet place, for a few minutes with no phone, and let yourself be small in His house.

## Maamar 45: BaYom HaShemini Atzeres (Shemini Atzeret 5667), first part only (my portion ends at line 4518) (lines 4474-4518)

**The movement.** It corrects the last maamar: the inner depth of wisdom is also a true nothingness, like Moshe, "what are we". The mind is shown two kinds of self-giving: the one that thinking produces, which is loud and fades when the thinking stops, and the one that is simply there, quiet as a whisper before a king. It begins to climb toward the bare point above even that, where this portion ends.

> ולכן כשנפסק ההתבוננות, אינו בבחי' ביטול אז, וחוזר לישותו
> *So when the contemplation stops, he is no longer nullified, and he goes back to his selfhood.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. Moshe said "what are we". He was heavy of speech, because speech is spreading yourself out as a thing, and he was too truly nothing for that (line 4477). Picture a man standing very close to a king. He cannot say a word and a half. If the king asks him something he stands like a stone, and whispers, not knowing what he says, only because the king's strength holds him up. Then step by step. That is the Amidah. "God, open my lips": the words come by themselves, not from my own preparing (line 4486). So we pray it in a whisper, like Chana, whose lips moved and her voice was not heard. There is a difference between "crushed" and "made to feel crushed": one who is crushed in himself, and one who works at humbling himself by thinking deeply. The second is real, but it is on the outside. Then the honest test. Self-giving that comes from contemplation fades. When the contemplation stops, he is not nullified, and goes back to himself (line 4486). The Shema is "flames of fire", noise and stirring, because it comes from understanding. The Amidah is "a still small voice". Wood makes noise while it burns; once it is fully burned it is silent (line 4493). Noise comes from somebody still being there. Bring it home. Think of a strong moment in prayer, and then of an hour later. Did you go back to yourself? The maamar is not scolding. It is telling you what to look for: not a louder feeling, but the quiet kind that stays when the thinking stops, that shows in plain life: using your mind only for His greatness, not looking at what is bad, not saying what should not be said, "sanctify yourself in what is permitted" (line 4495). The picture breaks where a man before a king is afraid. This silence is not fear; it is having no self to speak with. And even this, the maamar says, is only the "point" of wisdom, still a letter yud with a little surface. Above it is the bare point with no shape at all, the thorn of the yud (line 4503). My portion stops as it begins to climb there.

**From inside.** A humbling clarity. You see how much of your "bitul" was a feeling you made, and you are not crushed by seeing it. You begin to want the quiet kind, and to notice the moments it is already there.

**At the edge.** It points to the Ayin of Keter, beyond even wisdom's inner nothingness: not a yud but the thorn of the yud, which has no form at all (line 4503), a place with no color, only room for color (line 4512).

**Into the day.** Say the first words of the Amidah today, "God, open my lips", slowly, as a real request that He speak through you.

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations

1. **Maamar 1: Yom Tov shel Rosh Hashanah, line 208:** ואין אנו יודעים טעם שכלי מפני מה נשתוקק. *The book's first page: the world exists because He desired it, and no reason can be given. Every reason the mind offers is taken away until only a desire is left, and that desire is why you are here.* Before one ordinary mitzvah today, say: "This is what the world was wanted for."

2. **Maamar 20: Vayolech Hashem Es HaYam, line 2299:** ובאמת אתה הוא לבדך ממש. *Even "Ein Sof" names only His light. Under every name, He is alone, even now, even while the world stands.* Begin one prayer today with "You", and stop before the next word.

3. **Maamar 8: Vayomer... Lech Lecha, line 897:** אבל ברעו"ד נתפס בחי' העצמות. *Every rung of contemplation in prayer is still the outside of the heart. The plain will of the heart, like a child's bond to a parent, grasps the Essence that no thought can.* Once today, instead of thinking about God, say "You are my Father", and do the next thing He wants.

4. **Maamar 38: Eileh HaDevarim, line 3999:** שהעבד אינו מציאות לעצמו, ואינו מהות בפ"ע כלל מן האדון. *The plain servant is not a being of his own, so nothing in his service goes up and down with his mood. The service with no "me" in it is the steadiest one there is.* Do one plain task today, the kind that seems beneath you, for His sake.

5. **Maamar 27: Ki Tavo'u el HaAretz, line 2947:** שההעלם בעצמו יאיר. *His hiding is only toward us; it is our work; and at its root the hiding itself will shine. The darkest hour is where the deepest light is kept.* In the hardest hour of your week, say once: "This is the sheath. He is here."

6. **Maamar 30: HaShamayim Mesaprim, line 3255:** כ"א תיכף בהסתלקות האב ממנו, ירוץ אחריו לבקשו בדרכי נתיבות אביו. *The father hides to test his child. The wise child does not weep and forget; he runs after him along his paths, seeing him only from behind.* On an empty day, keep one fixed time of learning anyway: "I am walking His paths."

7. **Maamar 35: Ki Merosh Tzurim, line 3733:** כי כך היא המדה בכ"מ, שמסיבת תכלית הריחוק יבוא תכלית שלימות הקירוב. *Like dancers who step back to come closer, the soul's utmost distance is how the utmost closeness is made. Even the deepest contemplation sees Him only from behind.* When you feel far, say one sentence to Him anyway: "This is the step back in the dance."

8. **Maamar 41: Ki HaMitzvah HaZos, line 4250:** ומשו"ז גופא ירדו למטה מטה בבחי' עשיה, לפי שהן בבחי' העצמות ממש. *The deed is last in doing and first in thought. The practical mitzvah came down so low because it comes from the Essence itself, and so it is near, in your hand.* Touch the next mitzvah you do and think: "The end was the first thought."

9. **Maamar 9: Ki Yedativ, line 996:** וכמו הרצון העצמי שיש בכאו"א מישראל, לא ישונה כלל. *In every Jew there is a will that nothing changes and nothing wears away. The evil is added on; the essence is good.* About yourself or someone you have given up on, say once: "The essence is good."

10. **Maamar 45: BaYom HaShemini Atzeres, line 4486:** ולכן כשנפסק ההתבוננות, אינו בבחי' ביטול אז, וחוזר לישותו. *Self-giving that contemplation produces fades when the contemplation stops. The true kind is silent, a whisper before the King, and it stays.* Say "God, open my lips" slowly today, as a real request.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

*Compared with the Chabad method as mapped in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`, and with the other books in this index. The differences are kept, not smoothed.*

**Contemplation admits its own limit.** The hisbonenus ontology runs contemplation through five stages to arousal and drawing down, and treats the choice of object as a practical tool. Samach Vav keeps doing long, exact contemplation, and keeps telling the reader where it stops. The higher the mind climbs, the farther the Essence is (line 368). The whole ladder of contemplation in prayer is still the outside of the heart; only the heart's plain will grasps the Essence (line 897). All thinking about how "all is as nothing before Him" is seeing Him only from behind (line 3735). We do not contemplate the Essence; we contemplate the glow, and from knowing it is only a glow, we thirst (line 3831). The Gate of Unity climbs the worlds rung by rung toward the top; this book climbs in order to be told, honestly, that the top is not reached that way.

**The good feeling is only a preface.** The ontology's diagnostic layer treats "moved from the grasp" as a state to stay in. Samach Vav is cooler about feeling. The sudden stirring that comes from above is a gift that will pass and is "only like a preface" to the real work of toiling in mind and heart (line 1674). Bitul made by thinking is on the outside: when the contemplation stops, the person "goes back to his selfhood" (line 4486). Noise in prayer, the Shema's "flames of fire", is a sign that a self is still there; the Amidah's whisper is the self-giving that stays (line 4493). This is close to the Rashab's own test in the ontology (has the dominant trait changed?), but here the test is simpler and harder: does it last when the thinking stops?

**The plain servant above the son.** The ontology records the central disagreement: R. Aharon of Strashelye defends felt arousal, the Mitteler Rebbe names Godly arousal as the aim but warns against arousal without prior self-effacement, and the Rashab describes arousal so absorbed that it is not felt as one's own. Samach Vav's summer maamarim add something none of these say. The son serves by nature, the trusted servant from love, and the plain servant under a yoke, with no light, no joy, always "contracted" (lines 3894, 4010). And the plain servant is ranked highest: his service does not change (line 3999), it is counted as the Master's own work (line 4035), and what he makes by his own toil is new and draws a new light from the Essence (lines 4043, 4084). The ontology's diagnostic for "cold: correct, empty, habitual" is to change the object. This book would say: carry the yoke one step past habit, the hundred-and-first time (line 3929). The two are kept side by side here, not reconciled. The same book also praises the trusted servant's joy (line 4010) and Yisrael's effortless song (line 3849).

**Distance as the road, with a guard.** The ontology treats bitterness and sadness as an obstacle: keep the resolution if it sharpens, stop if it flattens. Samach Vav makes distance itself the way. The narrow place becomes the launching place (line 1489). The well is dug by honest reckoning and bitterness at how far one is (line 3680). The dancers step apart to come closer (line 3733). But the father-and-child parable carries its own guard: the foolish child who weeps bitterly and then forgets has failed; the wise one does not cry, but runs (line 3255). This reading follows that guard and never asks a reader to stay in sadness.

**Down, not up.** The ontology's diagnostic for "yearning with no return" is to bring it down into a concrete act. Samach Vav goes further and ranks the descent above the ascent. It is easier to expire in love than to keep Torah below (line 2016). The candle's flame, which wants only to go up, is held on the wick so that it can give light (the Ner Chanukah maamar). Refining from below, by going into the lower thing, outranks light from above, because only it makes the lower thing itself a vessel (line 4290). Imrei Binah agrees that "the return is the main thing" (its line 283) and that the earth holds the power of the beginning (its line 179); Samach Vav builds a whole summer on that rule, and adds that the hard, lightless toil of the plain servant is where it happens.

**The "no" and the bare deed as content for contemplation.** None of the ontology's objects is a prohibition. Samach Vav says the "do not" mitzvot reach higher than the "do" mitzvot, touching the Essence where no light flows (lines 3346, 3378), and that the plain deed of a mitzvah came down so low because it is rooted in the Essence (line 4250). The Rashab's Kuntres HaTefillah (as read in this index) warns against reaching for the highest in contemplation; this book agrees and gives the reason: what the mind cannot reach, the "no" and the deed already touch.

**Song and silence.** The ontology has no place for song. The Mitteler Rebbe's Kuntres HaHitpa'alut treats the niggun as a sign of the soul's movement. Samach Vav describes song as what comes out by itself when the soul is moved from its essence (line 3857), "all masters of song go out in song", and says the voice only wakes the inner powers, not the essential stirring. It also sets silence above both: the whisper before the King (line 4486). This reading turns song into song and never into drink.

**An uncorrected scan, and a half.** The ontology notes that the Rashab's most practical texts survive here only as uncorrected OCR. So does this one. The quotes are copied as the scan has them, with its misreadings; their English is a plain rendering of what the scan evidently means. This reading covers only the first half of the file (lines 1-4518), and stops in the middle of the Shemini Atzeret maamar of 5667; the later maamarim, with their own turns, are not weighed here.
