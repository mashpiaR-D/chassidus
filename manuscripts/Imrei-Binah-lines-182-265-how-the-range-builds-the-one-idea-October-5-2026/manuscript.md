## The shape of the range

This part of the book is a commentary on the Shema, and it has one question at its centre. Why does the lower unity (Baruch Shem, God's kingship in time and space) have an advantage over the higher unity of the first verse (line 183)? The Gate of Unity already set the two unities side by side. This range argues that the lower one reaches higher.

- The yoke of heaven, bare acceptance without understanding, reaches the Essence more than understood bitul does (lines 187-191). - The chain of worlds is mapped from "Asiyah of Asiyah" (a thrown stone) up to the "window", to show that God is not divided at any rung (lines 195-230). - Time is shown to be a creature, and the Essence, which is beyond time, will shine inside time (lines 231-245). - The whole thought of creation began from its purpose: souls in bodies doing mitzvot, a dwelling below (lines 244-247).

The last third reads the Shema paragraphs themselves: self-sacrifice, love, Torah study, fear, joy and tefillin (lines 246-263).

F05 leads, as it does in the Gate of Unity. But the practical faces (F10, F07, F08, F06) are much stronger here than in the Gate's first range, because the Shema is a commandment and the book keeps coming back to the mitzvah. The long middle stretch on doing, speaking and thinking (lines 192-215) is mostly *structural*.

## §23 end to §24 (182-186): the lower unity has more

Section 24 states its thesis directly: > מעלה יתירה שיש ביח"ת דבשכמל"ו על יח"ע דפסוק ראשון (line 183)

It gives three proofs. The first is creation. The earth brings new things out of nothing without limit, and knowing Him from His works in this way "reaches His very essence": > יש מאין לאין שיעור שזהו דוק' מגיע בעצם מהותו (line 183)

The other two are the written and oral Torah, and souls. A soul in a body is "like sowing in the very dust of the earth" (line 182). So the unlimited love "with all your might" belongs only to a soul in a body (line 183, F06 + F07). The soul goes down in order to go up. Dust holds the first, creating power more strongly than any higher place: > ובעפר יש כח הראשון ממש ביתר שאת (line 184)

A seed grows only after it rots completely, "as nothing" (line 185, F08). In the same way the soul, swallowed in the body, grows bitter and weeps. That weeping reaches deeper than the pleasure it had at its source: > ובכי' זו מגעת לעומק הענג האלקי יותר ממה שהי' עונג אלקי בתחלה (line 185) > שיש יתרון מעלה בחומר מבצורה (line 185)

This is F11 in its strongest form in the range: the distance itself yields the deeper closeness. It ends with a claim about history: > וזהו שבשכמל"ו בזמן הגלו' יותר נעל' מבזמן בהמ"ק (line 186)

## §25-26 (187-194): the yoke reaches the Essence; contemplation of creation first

If Baruch Shem is higher, why is it only "acceptance of the yoke"? Because the end is wedged in the beginning. The higher fear, real nullification, hangs on the lower one: > דחילו עילאה בטול במציאות ממש תלוי בדחילו תתאה שהוא רק קבלת עול (line 187)

The spheres' constant turning is their bowing (line 188). They give out influence "with particular providence" only while they bow toward the Shechinah (line 188.2, F01). The sphere's soul is nullified only by the contracted divine power it perceives. But its matter bows with its whole being, and that reaches beyond the contraction: > הרי הוא בטול כל מהותו ממש שמגיע לעצמו' אלקו' שלמעלה מצמצום האלקי שמהוה אותו מאין (line 188)

A coronation parable follows. The king is pleased by his people's eager wish, but more by their giving up of their own will (line 189). Mitzvot done under the yoke "arouse pleasure before His Essence, that He spoke and His will was done", and from this the World to Come is made (line 189.3, F10). That is the advantage of the lower unity (line 190).

Section 26 then answers the question of line 187. "You were shown, to know" works through Malchut's making of a separate world, and from this in particular one knows the Essence: > שמזה דוקא יכירו וידעו בבחי' מהות ועצמו' אא"ס ממש (line 191)

The practical rule is the strongest F12 line in the range. Start with simple contemplation of how heaven and earth come from nothing. Doing so "is as if one contemplates the very Essence before emanation, with no change at all": > ה"ז כמו שמתבונן ממש בעצמות אא"ס שלפני האצילות בלי שינוי כלל (line 191)

Without this foundation, all one's work on Atzilut "will fix nothing in his soul" (line 192). Providence, "the eyes of God roam", is placed in the wisdom of Malchut of Asiyah (line 192.3). A tzaddik can change the letter combinations of Malchut of Asiyah and so change nature (line 194.2, F01 + F08).

## §27-32 (195-215): the rungs down to "Asiyah of Asiyah"

Baruch Shem is laid over the worlds word by word (line 195.2). "Le'olam va'ed", the lowest world, is the name of forever, and "va'ed" in exchanged letters is "echad" (line 195.3): > שכל חפץ עצמותו ממש בה (line 195)

Lines 196-214 are the map. Each world holds thought, speech and deed. The lowest rung, "Asiyah of Asiyah", has no mind in it, like a thrown stone. The book then tests its own parable. The stone's body is not made by the throw, but the spheres' bodies are made from nothing by the divine power: > אבל הגלגלי' וכה"ג גופן נעשה ונתהווה יש מאין מכח האלקי הזה (line 199)

A letter, once spoken, stands. Yet God still renews creation every day, because His word is not outside Him: > משום שאין זה דבר שחוץ ממנו כדבור האדם (line 200)

The outside forces draw only from "hair and nails". They receive nothing from even the last divine power, the one to which the spheres bow "without separation ever" (line 201.2). Then comes a long study of the absent-minded doer, of work done by habit, and of dreams (lines 202-204). Dreams are the book's own parable for exile, which Joseph sets right (line 203.2).

The whole of Asiyah is made "in wisdom". So the whole divine mind that belongs to the work is present in each detail: > אך ודאי שמלובש כל עצם השכל האלקי העליון ששייך לכל אשר עשה גם בכל פרט ופרט (line 207)

Torah is where this becomes concrete (F10). He "looked into the Torah and created the world" (line 207.3). One who repeats a halacha keeps all of creation in being (line 208): > משחרב בהמ"ק אין להקב"ה בעולמו רק ד' אמות של הלכה דוקא (line 208)

The Ten Commandments are the root of the Ten Utterances (line 210.2). Through the form and matter of Torah speech, the form and matter of creation are drawn down (line 211.3). The account of speech and thought (lines 209-215) ends with the whole order renewed every moment "with actual particular providence" (line 215.2, F01).

## §33-36 (216-230): screen, hole, window

There are three ways a giver's flow reaches a receiver. Through a screen, the light is cut off from its source. Through a hole, a narrowed point of the giver's own essence gets through. Through a window, it passes "as from vessel to vessel". The hole is still the essence, because an essence cannot be divided: > שכל עצם בלתי מתחלק דהיינו שגם בחלק הקטן האחרון שבו ימצא בו הכל (line 217)

The soul is therefore an essential part, and can grow to equal its root: > כה"ג ממש יובן בנר ה' נשמת אדם שנק' ניצוץ אלקי ה"ז חלק עצמי עכ"פ (line 217) > אבל הנשמות שרשם ממוח האב בחי' חלק עצמי (line 218)

Angels are like a pool cut off from its spring (line 218). The future is the window: "to be one in the One" (line 220.2, 221.1). From a small hole "a great space can be made, until it holds all the light of the Essence" (line 220). The master craftsman's whole mind is in his work, and so the divine wisdom is the same in Beriah and in Asiyah (line 221.2). This is why "you were shown, to know" works through creation: > אין הפרש בזה בין עול' היות' עליון שלפני עולם האצי' ובין עולם השפל הזה (line 222)

Line 222.4 limits this to Jewish souls, because of the screen. Section 35 then explains hiding (F03). The "distance" between levels is what lets the receivers exist: > שאם הי' נמשך הקו מעילה לעילה בקירוב הרי הכל הי' בבחי' א"ס ולא הי' מציא' י"ס כלל (line 224)

Wherever there is "nothing", Ein Sof is revealed more closely (line 225.1). But grasping is itself a "something", and it hides: > כי השגה זו עצמה שהוא בחי' יש מסתרת אור עצמותו שהוא בחי' אין האמתי (line 225)

The screen between Atzilut and Beriah is one source that yields two opposites: > שממקור א' יוצא ב' הפכים גלוי והסתר (line 228)

The reshimu, the trace left after the contraction, is the power of limit within the unlimited: > ורשימו זו היא בחי' כח הגבול שבאא"ס הבלתי מוגבל כי כל יכול הוא וגם כח הגבול ימצא מאתו (line 229)

A miracle inside nature, and the Ark that took up no space, are "two opposites in one place" (line 229.3, F09).

## §37-40 (231-245): time is a creature, and the purpose comes before the thought

Why will Baruch Shem be above the higher unity in the future? Malchut is time, and Havayah is only the source of time. The Essence, wholly beyond time, will shine into time: > משום דעצמות אא"ס שלמעלה מן הזמן לגמרי יאיר בבחי' הזמן דנוק' (line 231)

Time itself is made from nothing, so "why did He not create earlier?" is an empty question (line 232.1, F01 + F05): > דהזמן עצמו הוא מחודש ונברא מאין ליש בבחי' גבול (line 232)

The first thought sees "to the end of all generations in one glance" (line 233.1). The Essence is higher even than being a whole to its parts (line 233.2). The middle level is Baruch Shem, the Essence in bounded time, which joins them (line 234). Time comes from the soul's "running and returning", like the heart's two beats (line 235.2). Its root is the "touching and not touching" in the Emanator (line 236). The beginning and end of a thought are one: > ובאמ' הכל א' משו' דנעוץ תחלתן בסופן וסופן בתחלתן (line 236)

The six directions of the world are nullified back into the beginning. That is the meaning of the large dalet of "echad" (lines 238-239).

Then comes the key step (lines 244-245). Above the beginning of thought there is a third level. There the "end of the deed" rises, as a builder first pictures the finished house he will enjoy. God "consulted with the souls of the tzaddikim" about whether to will creation at all: > שזהו סוף מעש' שנתאוו' הקב"ה להיו' לו דירה בתחתוני' דוק' (line 245)

So at that end, in a bounded world, the Essence itself will shine. The sign is a limit that does not limit: > הרי הגבול עצמו נעשה בלתי בע"ג שזהו א"א להיות כ"א מצד העצמות ממש (line 245) > למהוי א' באחד למעלה ולמטה שוה ממש (line 245)

## §41-42 (246-257): the Shema, self-sacrifice, and Torah as a shared palace

"Israel arose in thought" means that Israel arose before the first thought (line 246). So Havayah is "our God", and only Israel can make the unification (line 247.2). Every Jew has the power to give up his life in "echad", from his root: > ולכך יש לכאו"א מישראל כח למס"נ באח' כשיאריך בא' מצד שרשו בעצמו' ממש (line 247)

"Enthrone Him in heaven, earth and the four directions" is read upward. The directions come into bitul before the Essence: > אלא להיפך שיכוין בבחי' הבטול שמים וארץ וד' רוחות (line 248)

There are two self-sacrifices. One comes from the soul's root and needs no command. The other belongs to the soul in the body, and "you shall love" is said to it (line 248.2). The second has the advantage: > כי לא נמלך רק בנשמתן של צדיקים שבגופי' ולא בנשמות מצד עצמן (line 249)

Rabbi Akiva's longing to give his life in deed is "the Essence in time below, as above, equally" (line 249.3).

Section 42 asks how Israel can be God's "brothers and friends". Israel and Torah have one root, chosen in the Essence's will before creation (line 251.1). His fixed dwelling is wisdom: > לפי שאין עצמות אא"ס שורה אלא בחכמה לפי ששעשועי' העצמיי' שלו בקביעות תמידי אינו אלא בחכמ' שבתורה דוקא (line 252)

Torah is a "palace", not a garment. It is "the four cubits of halacha", His own place: > שאין העולם מקומו העצמי אבל ד"א של הלכה הוא מקומו העצמי (line 255)

When a person studies, God studies opposite him, inside the same palace: > כך הקב"ה מוקף באותו היכל עצמו שהאדם מוקף בה (line 256)

It is precisely because "none is holy like God" that He comes down to delight in Torah (line 256.2). Then "the three knots become truly one knot" (line 256.3). The souls came down for this: > שלזה ירדו נ"י למטה לקבל התורה כדי שיהי' לו דירה למטה בד"א של הלכה על ידם דוקא (line 257)

## §43-45 (258-265): love and fear joined by Peace; joy; tefillin; all "something from nothing"

Love comes from distance and fear from nearness (line 258). Even the highest worlds are "desert", and thirst grows there (line 259.1). Contemplating that "below and above are equal" brings awe (line 259.2). The two opposites are joined only by the Essence: > דקמי' סובב וממלא שוין ע"כ גם בחי' הרחוק וקרוב שוין (line 260) > שזה א"א כ"א מצד בחי' העצמו' שלמעלה מהכל ע"כ נק' שלום (line 260)

"God is Peace." Above love and fear is the joy of the mitzvot. There, joy and trembling are in one place, which only the revealed Essence can bring about (line 261.2). "Anochi" is reached through the mitzvot (line 261.3). Torah speech makes the Essence revealed "below just as above" (line 262.1). Tefillin are the witness to all these inclusions (line 263): > לפי שהתפילין עיקר העדות לכל בחי' התכללו' דיחו"ע ויחו"ת (line 263)

The range closes (line 265) on the rule under all of it. The whole chain is "something from nothing", Atzilut included. Malchut of Asiyah is "complete Godliness", and the only difference between the rungs is in the worlds they make: > אבל מצד המקור הכל א' שבא מעצמו' א"ס בדלוג המהות (line 265)

## What this range adds that the Tanya does not say (or says differently)

- **The lower unity above the higher.** Tanya SH 1 and 7 pair the two unities, with the Shema as "the higher". This range argues that Baruch Shem has an extra advantage now (lines 183, 190), is higher in exile than in Temple times (186), and will be above the higher unity in the future (231, 249). The reason is that the Essence's purpose lies in the bounded and physical.
- **The purpose comes before the thought.** The Tanya (ch. 36) gives the dwelling below as the purpose of creation. This range places it before the first will: "the end of the deed" is what He "consulted" about, above both the beginning and the end of thought (lines 244-247). F07 becomes the cause of the will, not only its goal.
- **The yoke reaches further than understanding.** Bare acceptance of the yoke, the "matter" bowing, reaches the Essence beyond the contraction. Understood bitul reaches only the contracted power (188-190). Matter has an advantage over form (185).
- **Time as a creature, and eternal time.** Tanya SH 7 puts time and space under Malchut. This range builds a full account: world, year and soul come into being together (235); time comes from running and returning (235-243); "why not earlier?" is an empty question (232); and in the future the Essence makes bounded time never end (231, 243, 245).
- **Screen, hole, window.** The Etz Chaim grades become a theory of how much of the giver's essence reaches the receiver, with "an essence cannot be divided" (217) as the reason the soul is a real part of God (217-218).
- **Torah as a shared palace.** God and the one who studies sit in one palace of the same letters, as "brothers and friends" (254-257). The Tanya (ch. 5) has Torah knowledge as a union with Him. It does not have this picture of a surrounding shared by both.
- **God called Peace.** Opposites (love and fear, far and near, surrounding and filling) are joined only by the Essence, and so His name is Peace (260).

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- **The psychology of doing, speaking and thinking (lines 192-215).** Most of this is *structural*: the craftsman, the absent-minded doer, work by habit, idle words, stray thoughts. It is the map the argument needs. Only where the text itself turns to the meaning (207, 208, 210.2, 211.2-3, 215.2) is a tie *stated*. Lines 212, 214 and 227 carry no face.
- **Two readings.** Line 203.2 (dreams as the parable of exile) and line 204.1 (a teacher contracting for a child as the model of the tzimtzum) are ours. The text names exile in the first. The second only points ("clarified in wisdom").
- **Kabbalistic tallies.** The 42 and 72 words of the Shema passages and the exchange of lights and vessels (264-265.1) are *structural*. The author himself says the two ways of counting are "in truth one".
- **F11 is rare (6 rows)** and appears only as weeping, exile and desert (185, 186, 203.2, 258, 259.1). The range says nothing about the evil inclination turned to good, apart from "with both your inclinations" (261.1, tagged F09).
- **Line 222.4** limits recognizing the Essence through creation to Jewish souls, because of the screen. It is recorded as stated, not softened.
- **OCR.** Line 199 has האחקי for האלקי, and line 262 has התכללוץ. Quotes avoid or keep these errors exactly as the file has them.

## Disagreements with the Tanya kept

- **Which unity is higher.** In the Tanya the lower unity is how things look from below. Here it has "an extra advantage" (183), and in the future it is above the higher unity (231). The Mitteler Rebbe does not deny the Shema's rank. It is the root of the soul's self-sacrifice (247-248). But he puts the higher point at the bottom, where the Essence's purpose is fulfilled. The Tanya's language does not go that far.
- **Knowing the Essence through creation.** Tanya SH 7 leaves "no change in Him" to faith, and treats the knowledge of creation as knowledge of His works. Here the question "how can one recognize His essence from His works?" (187.2) is answered yes. Contemplating the power that forms the heavens "is as if one contemplates the very Essence before emanation" (191). The divine wisdom in the lowest world is no different from that in the highest (222). A limit is set only for non-Jewish souls (222.4) and by grasping itself being a "something" that hides (225.2).
- **The craftsman analogy.** Tanya SH 2 rejects the craftsman (the vessel stands without the smith). This range uses the thrown stone and the craftsman at length (197-222). It agrees with the Tanya in substance, since the parable fails because the spheres' very bodies are made from nothing (199), and His word is not outside Him (200). But it goes further than the Tanya in the other direction: a tzaddik can recognize the maker's essence in any tool (222). The Tanya does not say that.
- **Love or fear first.** The Tanya (ch. 41) makes fear the beginning. Here the lower fear, acceptance of the yoke, is the root of the higher fear (187, 189), which agrees. But in the Shema love is "the main service", and fear is added as secondary to it, "the left included in the right" (261.1, 262.2). Both are in the text. The index keeps them apart rather than making them agree.
