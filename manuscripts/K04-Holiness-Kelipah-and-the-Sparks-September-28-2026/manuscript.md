## What this cluster shows

The moral cosmos of Chassidus (holiness, the husks, the permitted middle, the sparks) looks at first like a dualism: two sides at war. Read against the spine, every one of these subjects is defined by its relation to the one idea, and the sources say so in so many words. Holiness is what is nullified to Him (Tanya 6:5). Kelipah is what is not nullified and stands as a separate thing, living from the back of His will (Tanya 6:7, 22). Idolatry is regarding anything as a thing in its own right (Tanya 22:8). The three impure kelipot are a 'wholly separate yesh' (Shaar HaYichud 53:24). So the 'other side' is not a second power. It is the unity concealed (F03), measured by bitul (F08), and it exists to be subdued and transformed (F11), which only the Essence can do (Inyanah 20). The sparks are the fallen letters of His word (F01). Refinement is the unity at work: in Likkutei Torah (Bamidbar 5:5), contemplating 'there is truly nothing besides Him' is what exposes the evil hidden in the good.

What the cluster adds to the spine is its disagreements, kept below: nogah as bitul or as yesh, raising foreign thoughts or pushing them away, subduing against transforming, how kelipot receive life, the source of evil, and whether Adam's sin was real. The tie is thinnest where the subject is halachic (the laws of impurity and mikveh) and where several accounts of evil's source are treated as one.

## S039 · Holiness · קדושה

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F05 The Essence beyond both · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** קדושה · קדושתו · קדושת · בטל אליו

> Holiness is the unity of God as it rests on whatever is nullified to Him: His own separateness from the worlds He enlivens, drawn onto what stops standing apart.

## Derivation

1. God is 'holy and separate' from the worlds. He brings them into being from nothing but is not enclothed in them, so creation changes nothing in Him. Unlike the soul, He is not affected by what He enlivens (Likkutei Torah, Emor 1:5-7). (*stated*)
2. So holiness in a creature cannot be its own property. It is a resting of His holiness, and He rests only on what is nullified to Him: actually, as the angels are, or potentially, as every Jew is, through readiness to give his life (Tanya 6:5). (*stated*)
3. The Tzemach Tzedek states the rule: nothing can be received from holiness except through bitul. The Rashab: holiness lives from the inner will, kelipah from the outer will that only surrounds it (Derekh Mitzvotekha, circumcision 5; Kuntres Umaayan 2:2:8). (*stated*)
4. The two senses of 'holy' (God's separateness and the creature's bitul) are one: whatever is nullified is 'separated' with Him from its own selfhood. This joining of the two senses is our reading. (**reading**)
5. Because He enlivens everything while holy and separate, a person can be holy inside nature, in every act, by nullifying himself to the inner point in it (Sefat Emet, Kedoshim 5631). (*stated*)
6. Holiness is therefore a task done in the world: it must be brought into permitted things (Hayom Yom 27 Nissan, citing Tanya 7). That this is the home below (G09) is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 6:5 and 22:8: holiness rests only on what is nullified to Him. Likkutei Torah Emor 1:7 and Kedoshim 1:4-6: God is 'holy and separate', and praise draws His holiness down ('You are holy, enthroned on the praises of Israel').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** not located in this pass as a separate theme.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, circumcision 5: holiness is received only through bitul, which is why the generation of Enosh turned to the stars.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel, Kedoshim 5629: God kept one crown of holiness and gave Israel two. He explains this through the soul's descent for ascent (see S047).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Umaayan 2:2:8: holiness and good live from the inner will; kelipah and the forbidden from the outer will.
- **Rayatz.** Via Hayom Yom 27 Nissan: holiness must be brought into permitted things. The line is unattributed within Hayom Yom.
- **The Rebbe.** Compiled Hayom Yom; no separate maamar on holiness was read in this pass.
- **Other schools.** Ger (Sefat Emet, Kedoshim 5631): God enlivens all while holy and separate, so holiness is possible within nature, and a holy point is hidden wherever concealment is greater. R. Tzadok (Yisrael Kedoshim; pillar 20) treats holiness by kinds (place, time, soul; earned and inherited) and as separation within the permitted ('kedoshim tihyu, perushim tihyu', seen in search, not quoted). That typology is not tied to the unity in what was read, so its link is our reading. Pillar 20's R. Tzadok and Beit Yaakov citations were not re-verified.

## S040 · Kelipah and the other side · קליפה / סטרא אחרא

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** קליפות · קליפה · סטרא אחרא · הסטרא אחרא

> Kelipah is the unity of God as concealed: His own word, contracted until something can stand as if apart from Him and say 'I, and none besides'.

## Derivation

1. Everything lives from the word of God, kelipot included. The contractions were so great that impure things could come into being from His speech (Tanya 22:2). There is no second source. (*stated*)
2. What makes something kelipah is a relation, not a substance. What is not nullified and stands as a separate thing receives life only from the 'back' of the will, through countless contractions, and that life is in exile in it (Tanya 6:7; 22:3-6). (*stated*)
3. Hence 'the other side': anything not for Him, His will and His service, including neutral thought and speech (Tanya 6:4). (*stated*)
4. It is defined by the unity it denies. It raises itself 'like an eagle' and says 'I, and none else' (Tanya 22:7; 36:4). The Rashab adds that kelipot draw from the surrounding light (sovev), which flows without regard to vessels (F04). The Rayatz adds that human arrogance causes the outer forces above to raise themselves and draw from a very high place. (*stated*)
5. Even there the Shechinah is present: in foreign thoughts, evils and kelipot there are sparks of the Shechinah that fell in the breaking (Besht, Keter Shem Tov 1:62:3). (*stated*)
6. It exists for its own overcoming: life is thrown to it only 'to reward the tzaddikim who subdue the other side' (Tanya 22:5). The Rebbe: 'the kelipah and the other side, the whole intent in them is that a person overcome them' (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 10 Shevat 5713:5). The Mitteler Rebbe: 'the husk precedes the fruit', and the opposing force comes by the tzimtzum that precedes every light (Shaar HaYichud 54:3, 54:10-11). (*stated*)
7. That in the Mitteler Rebbe the husk is there for the sake of the fruit (as against merely coming first) is our reading of 54:10-11; he says only that it precedes, as darkness precedes light. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 6 and 22: the other side is whatever is not nullified. Its life comes from the back of the will and is in exile in it. It is called 'other gods'.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 53:21-54:12: the degrees of kelipah (nogah in constant bitul, the three impure as a 'wholly separate yesh'). Kelipah is 'the waste in everything', from the mixture after the breaking. It precedes the fruit and comes by the tzimtzum that precedes every light.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, circumcision 3-6: the orlah as kelipat nogah; in holiness Havayah and Elokim are one, while in kelipah Elokim becomes complete concealment (read in context, not quoted).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5632 (Vyadata): nogah, the 'turning sword', can go to the three impure kelipot or be refined (see S041).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Umaayan 9:3:6-8: kelipot 'raise themselves like the eagle' to receive from the surrounding light, which does not need vessels. This, he says, explains the worldly success of the nations.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692 (441-443): the kelipah has a fixed allotment plus what human lowering adds. Arrogance toward people is arrogance toward God and makes the outer forces raise themselves to draw from a high place.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 10 Shevat 5713: kelipah and the other side are rooted in the outer will, and the whole intent in them is that man overcome them (iskafya).
- **Other schools.** Besht: sparks of the Shechinah are in the kelipot ('Surely God is in this place'). Breslov (Likutei Moharan II 12): His revealed glory has a border and does not spread into sins or into places of idolatry. They live only from the utterly hidden first utterance, and 'this cannot be understood and one must not ponder it'. This is a real difference: Chabad explains how they draw life (the back of the will, the surrounding light), and Breslov forbids the inquiry. Yet the Rashab's account and Breslov's point the same way: both place the source above the ordered, revealed flow (the Rashab: above seder hishtalshelus, without reckoning; Breslov: the hidden utterance that includes all utterances). That structural parallel is our reading; the difference over whether to inquire stands. Pillar 20's Chernovitz ('the husk steals a letter') was not re-verified.

## S041 · Kelipas nogah and the permitted · קליפת נוגה / דברי הרשות

**Faces:** F09 The unity of opposites · F11 Darkness turned to light · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** קליפת נוגה · נוגה · דברי הרשות · דבר הרשות

> Kelipat nogah is the unity of God as the undecided middle: the permitted world, whose life can be included in holiness or in the impure depending on whether it is used for Him.

## Derivation

1. The kelipot have two grades. Nogah lies between the three impure kelipot and holiness, and it contains some good (Tanya 7:1-2; 1:17). (*stated*)
2. Everything permitted draws its life from it: the animal soul, kosher animals and food, and every neutral act done for the body rather than for Heaven (Tanya 7:1). (*stated*)
3. Used for God (meat and wine to widen the mind, delight on Shabbat), its life is refined and rises 'like an offering'. Used for appetite, it sinks for the time being into the impure, and it can rise again with teshuvah, which is why it is called 'permitted', that is, not bound (Tanya 7:4-6; IH 26:16-17). (*stated*)
4. What makes even its good 'kelipah' is yesh, being a thing for oneself ('so that they call me Rabbi'), where holiness is bitul. Yet nogah can be refined until it is wholly nullified in the light of Ein Sof (Likkutei Torah, Chukat 3:6-7). (*stated*)
5. The Maharash, with the Ramaz: nogah is 'the turning sword', able to go either way. 'Turning darkness to light' means sweetening nogah and returning it to holiness (Toras Shmuel 5632). (*stated*)
6. Its purpose: when the life-force of all Israel becomes a chariot, the general life of this world, now nogah, will rise into holiness, and all will see 'that Havayah is God, there is none besides Him' (Tanya 37:10-12). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 1, 7 and 37; Iggeret HaKodesh 26 (on Shabbat, nogah itself ascends); Likkutei Torah Chukat 3 (even its good is yesh).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 53:26: nogah, even of Asiyah, 'is always in bitul', as the spheres and the seventy princes bow. Only the three impure kelipot are a separate yesh.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, circumcision 3: the root of the orlah is kelipat nogah, explained through the stars' role in the flow (read in context).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5632 (Vyadata): nogah is the turning sword. Transforming darkness means sweetening it into holiness.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Umaayan 14:1:13: the animal soul is rooted in tohu and in kelipat nogah, and from it come both bad traits and the natural traits of mercy and kindness.
- **Rayatz.** not located in this pass beyond Hayom Yom 27 Nissan ('permitted things done for one's pleasure are wholly evil, as in Tanya 7').
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 11 Shevat 5728: the offering takes an animal whose life is from nogah and makes it an offering to Havayah. Done with the animal in one's own heart, this is iskafya and is'hapcha, and it reaches the Essence.
- **Other schools.** Dinov (Agra DeKala, Toldot 14) adopts the scheme and cites Tanya by name. Nogah is the Zohar's 'scale', tilted by intent, and the forbidden may never be used for God. Ropshitz's 'nogah as the diaphragm' (pillar 20) was not re-verified.

> אבל בבחינת נגה, גם בבחינת נגה דעשיה שרובו רע ומיעוטו טוב, המה בבחינת ביטול תמיד
> — The Mitteler Rebbe, differently: nogah, even of Asiyah, is always in bitul (the spheres bow). Only the three impure kelipot are 'a completely separate yesh'.

> **Toras Shmuel, תרל"ב ח"ב / מאמרים / וידעת היום כו' / תכט:2** (Maharash, Toras Shmuel; line 5112)
> ירצה הרע תעשה אותו הטוב והוא המתקת קליפת נוגה להחזירה אל הקדושה. כי ק"נ נק' להט החרב המתהפכת
> — The Maharash, citing the Ramaz: 'turn darkness to light' means sweetening nogah and returning it to holiness; nogah is 'the turning sword', able to go either way.

> **Agra DeKala, Toldot 14** (R. Tzvi Elimelech of Dinov, Agra DeKala; line 1369)
> והקליפה ההוא נקרא בזוהר הק' תיקל"א משקל אל כל אשר יחפוץ יטנו
> — Dinov (citing Tanya by name): nogah is the Zohar's 'scale', tilted by the intent of the one who uses the permitted thing.

> **Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד. מוצש"ק פ' בשלח, י"א שבט (מאמר ב), ה'תשכ"ח:3** (Lubavitcher Rebbe, Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat; line 1300)
> כי עבודת הקרבנות היא שלוקחים בהמה (שחיותה הוא מקליפת נוגה) ועושים אותה קרבן להוי'
> — The Rebbe (5728): the offering takes an animal whose life is from nogah and makes it an offering to Havayah; so the service of the animal soul is iskafya and is'hapcha.

## Thin or forced?

None in Chabad. Kept tension: the Mitteler Rebbe says nogah is always in bitul, while Likkutei Torah says even nogah's good is yesh. Likkutei Torah itself narrows the gap: the next line (Chukat 3:7) says nogah can be refined until it is wholly nullified in the light of Ein Sof, 'as the host of heaven bow to You', the very verse the Mitteler Rebbe uses for nogah's constant bitul. Our reading is that they speak of different layers (the heavenly ministers, already bowing, against nogah in the human soul, still yesh until refined), but neither text says so.

## S042 · The three impure husks and the forbidden · שלש קליפות הטמאות

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F11 Darkness turned to light **Search terms:** שלש קליפות הטמאות · ג' קליפות · קליפות הטמאות · איסור

> The three impure kelipot are the unity of God as denied outright: what stands as a wholly separate yesh ('my strength'), which can only be annulled, or turned by teshuvah from great love.

## Derivation

1. Below nogah are three impure kelipot, 'wholly evil with no good at all'. From them flow the forbidden foods and the acts of all 365 prohibitions and their rabbinic branches. Tanya also lists the souls of the nations and of impure animals among what flows from them (Tanya 6:11-12; 1:18). (*stated*)
2. 'Forbidden' (asur) means bound. The kelipah rests on the thing and it cannot rise, even if one prays with the strength it gave, unless it was eaten to save a life (Tanya 7:9; IH 26:16-18; Dinov). (*stated*)
3. Their existence too is from above. Every prohibition has a root of life in the kelipot, because nothing could exist without a flow from above (Kuntres Acharon 5:2). (*stated*)
4. What makes them impure is complete separateness: they are a 'wholly separate yesh' that says 'my strength and the might of my hand' (Shaar HaYichud 53:24). A transgressor, while he sins, is further from His unity than the kelipah itself, which does not defy its Master (Tanya 24). (*stated*)
5. They have no ascent, only annulment: 'I will remove the spirit of impurity from the earth'. The 365 prohibitions keep the life-force from drawing on them (Tanya 37:11-13). (*stated*)
6. Yet teshuvah from great love turns willful sins into merits. Because these sparks fell lowest, their root is highest, so the penitent stands where the wholly righteous cannot (Tanya 7:9; Derekh Mitzvotekha, Viduy 3). (*stated*)
7. That this turning is possible only from the Essence, which has no opposite (Inyanah 20), is our reading of how S042 meets S046. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 6-8, 24 and 37; IH 26; Kuntres Acharon 5 (even prohibitions never actually committed have their root in the chambers of the kelipot).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 53:24: the three impure kelipot are 'a wholly separate yesh' ('my river is mine'). Even they are subdued at times, when God visits them.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Viduy 3: willful sins are what fell lowest and became the impure kelipot, which Torah does not refine by mitzvot. So repentance that lifts them exceeds the mitzvot of the righteous. His gloss in Kuntres Acharon 5:3 puts errors in nogah (seen, not quoted).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5632: nogah can be drawn into the three kelipot or refined (S041).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Umaayan 2:2:8: forbidden things receive life from the kelipot 'and so they are death and evil'. Kuntres Umaayan 3-4 restates Tanya 24.
- **Rayatz.** not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** not located in this pass under this term. See Inyanah 20 in S046.
- **Other schools.** Dinov (Agra DeKala, Toldot 14): the forbidden is bound in the three impure kelipot and may not be enjoyed even to serve God, since those kelipot need annulment. This follows Tanya, which he cites.

> — Forbidden things are 'bound' by the outer forces until the end of days, or until teshuvah from great love turns the sins into merits.

## Thin or forced?

Not thin: the tie is stated as separateness from His unity (Tanya 24; Shaar HaYichud 53). Recorded without extension: Tanya 1:18 and 6:12 derive the souls of the nations from these kelipot.

## S043 · The sparks · ניצוצות

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F07 The home below · F11 Darkness turned to light **Search terms:** ניצוצות · ניצוצי קדושה · רפ"ח · העלאת ניצוצות

> The sparks are the unity of God as the fallen letters of His word, scattered in every thing and waiting to be gathered from separation back into oneness.

## Derivation

1. Everything exists by the divine word in it (F01). Chernobyl: nothing in this world lacks a holy spark from His speech that keeps it alive. It is the good taste in food, 'taste and see that Havayah is good' (Me'or Einayim, Matot 1). (*stated*)
2. The sparks are letters broken in the shattering of tohu and scattered again by Adam's sin. They fell into the kelipot, which hide them (IH 26:22; the Rebbe, 10 Shevat 5730, after the Maggid). (*stated*)
3. The worlds were created so that Israel refine the sparks. What a person eats and uses are his own sparks, his share to repair (Keter Shem Tov 1:160-161). Every Jew must raise the sparks that fell to his portion (IH 26:27). (*stated*)
4. Providence brings a person to the place where his sparks wait (Hayom Yom 1 Cheshvan, the Rashab via the Rayatz). This is G12: every journey is an instruction. (*stated*)
5. Raising them is 'from separation to unity'. The scattered letters are gathered like sheaves, so that the yesh down to the lowest world becomes a vessel and a dwelling (Rayatz, Sefer HaMaamarim 5689, 'Oz VeHadar' 187-188). (*stated*)
6. The spark in each thing is where Ein Sof rests in the lowest (G05). Identifying the two is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** IH 26:22-28: every word of halacha is a spark of the Shechinah, and each soul must raise the sparks of its portion. Tanya 37 raises the life of food through Torah and mitzvot.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 45:4 and 46:7 speak of what follows 'the refinements of the 288 sparks' (seen in search, not quoted). Toras Chaim, Vayechi (line 1804) raises idle words 'from the world of separation to the world of unity' (read, not quoted).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Viduy 3: the lowest-fallen have the highest root. (Hayom Yom 15 Teves records a textual correction to Torah Ohr on raising sparks, 'even the animals will rise'. It was seen, and its author was not checked.)
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel uses the term often (108 plain hits); no passage was read in context in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Via Hayom Yom 1 Cheshvan: a person travels where his sparks await redemption, and the tzaddikim see where their refinements wait.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 ('Oz VeHadar'): the soul descends to refine the 288 sparks, which are letters, and to raise them into unity. 'Mah Tovu' 204: sparks that fell lower have a higher root.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 10 Shevat 5730: the sparks are broken letters, refined by being brought, in a new combination, into Torah (after the Maggid on 'my signs'). 13 Tishrei 5741: see S047.
- **Other schools.** Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:160-161): the parable of the king's lost jewel; food is one's own sparks. Maggid (90:1): sparks were even in Esau, from whom came converts. Chernobyl ties the spark to the enlivening word, the closest non-Chabad statement of F01 here. Breslov (Likutei Moharan 26): raising sparks that enter as foreign thoughts requires self-sacrifice (read, not quoted). Difference of emphasis only: the Besht's circle makes eating itself the raising (Me'or Einayim calls it an offering); Tanya 37 stresses the food's strength clothed in Torah, prayer and mitzvot. Tanya also has eating for Heaven's sake refine the food so that it rises 'like an offering' (Tanya 7:4; IH 26:16), so this is not a clean opposition. Radzin and Karlin (pillar 05) were not re-verified.

## S044 · Refinement · בירורים

**Faces:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F12 Knowing the unity **Search terms:** בירורים · בירור · לברר · עבודת הבירורים

> Refinement is the unity of God sorting the mixture: the world of good-mixed-with-evil clarified until what belongs to Him is included in Him and the rest falls away.

## Derivation

1. Nogah is good mixed with evil. Refinement is when the good in it 'is separated from the evil, prevails, rises and is included in holiness' (Tanya 7:3). (*stated*)
2. In the soul, refinement happens in prayer. Contemplating that 'there is truly nothing besides Him' shows a person that he is still a yesh, so evil that seemed good is exposed and can be pushed away (Likkutei Torah, Bamidbar 5:5-6). Here the unity is itself the refining agent. (*stated*)
3. The same happens above. Malchut of Atzilut descends into the lower worlds to refine nogah, and the 288 sparks rise (Likkutei Torah, Bamidbar 5:6-7; IH 26:23). (*stated*)
4. In exile the chief refinement is the study of halacha: sorting permitted from forbidden and pure from impure (IH 26:24). Only those below, clothed in bodies, can do it (IH 26:26). (*stated*)
5. It is a gradual work. The Tzemach Tzedek: this is a world of falsehood where even the good is mixed with dross, so refinement is needed from below and from above (Hayom Yom 11 Elul). The Rayatz: it must go step by step from below, because refinement by a flood of light annuls evil but loses sparks with it (Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, Pekudei). (*stated*)
6. Refinement of the self: a vessel used with selfhood must be purged, because of the Holy One and the arrogant 'I and he cannot dwell together' (Hayom Yom 27 Tammuz, a story of the Alter Rebbe). (*stated*)
7. Its purpose: 'to refine and purify the world is the purpose of the descent into this world' (Inyanah 26:8), so that the yesh becomes a vessel and a dwelling (Rayatz 188:1). When refinement is complete, Torah and mitzvot will serve higher unifications instead (IH 26:30). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 7; IH 26; Likkutei Torah Bamidbar 5 (refinement in prayer through 'ein od'); Hayom Yom 27 Tammuz (a story of him: purging selfhood).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim uses the term heavily (207 plain hits). One line was read: raising idle words 'from the world of separation to the world of unity' (Vayechi, line 1804). No passage is quoted here.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Hayom Yom 11 Elul: refinement from below and from above, because this is a world of falsehood. Compare the Rayatz's insistence on refinement from below.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel uses the term often (163 plain hits). Not read in context in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah and Kuntres HaAvodah use 'birur' often (20 and 18 hits). Not read in context in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692 (Pekudei): refinement must be gradual and from below. 5689 ('Oz VeHadar'): the refinements gather the letters from separation to unity.
- **The Rebbe.** Inyanah 26:8: refining the world is the purpose of the descent. Inyanah 27:3: the last generations complete the refinements.
- **Other schools.** Pillar 05 (not re-verified): Karlin reads the thirty-nine labors as refinements, so Shabbat has none; Radzin's Sod Yesharim refines the receiver. Tiferet Yosef (Motzei Yom Kippur 1, now quoted): a person refines himself 'as far as his hand reaches' and God refines the rest, because He wants not one spark lost. The Rayatz shares the concern that no spark be lost but draws the opposite conclusion: refinement must go gradually from below, since refinement by revelation from above loses sparks. The Tzemach Tzedek (Hayom Yom 11 Elul) asks for both, from below and from above. So the difference is who completes the refinement, and the Chabad sources themselves differ in emphasis.

> — Radzin: a person can refine himself only as far as his hand reaches; God refines the rest, because He wants not one spark lost. The same passage grounds the work in 'even the inanimate is full of light'. Read in context and verified in the check pass (previously cited from pillar 05 unverified).

## S045 · Foreign thoughts · מחשבות זרות

**Faces:** F09 The unity of opposites · F11 Darkness turned to light · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** מחשבות זרות · מחשבה זרה · מחשבות רעות · הרהורים

> Foreign thoughts are the unity of God as His fallen letters surfacing in the mind at prayer, to be raised by the tzaddik or pushed away by everyone else.

## Derivation

1. Every thought is made of letters. A foreign thought is holy letters, 'limbs of the Shechinah', fallen in the breaking into a bad combination, like good foods spoiled by mixing (Maggid 12:13). They are the 288 sparks one must refine each day (Besht, Keter Shem Tov 1:79). (*stated*)
2. So the founders' remedy is to trace the thought to the attribute it fell from (lust from love, pride from glory) and bind it back to God (Maggid; Besht). Berditchev adds the a fortiori: if this is loved or feared, how much more the Master of all worlds. (*stated*)
3. Why they come at prayer: prayer is the hour of refinement, and the evil clings hardest then to the good it lives from (Likkutei Torah, Bamidbar 5:7; Tanya 28:4). Their coming does not prove the prayer worthless. (*stated*)
4. The Alter Rebbe withdraws the raising from the ordinary person. It was said only of tzaddikim, whose foreign thoughts are other people's. One whose thoughts come from the evil in his own heart is bound below and cannot raise them, so he should turn his mind away and pray with more joy (Tanya 28:1-8). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe joins the two. During prayer, push the thoughts away (iskafya). At another time, contemplate that a spark of the Shechinah descended into them in order to rouse greater intention (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 10 Shevat 5730, 19-20). Tzava'at HaRivash: a disturber's speech is itself the word of Havayah enlivening him at that moment (Zos Chanukah 5738). (*stated*)
6. Breslov: every foreign thought is a kelipah. One defeats it by praying in order without looking at it (Likutei Moharan II 122), though raising sparks that enter as foreign thoughts is also mentioned, with self-sacrifice (26). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 28; Likkutei Torah Bamidbar 5:7. Do not raise them unless you are a tzaddik; push them off; do not argue with them.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim uses the term (5 hits); not read in context.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel uses the term (14 hits); not read in context.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah and Kuntres Umaayan use the term (2 hits each); not read in context.
- **Rayatz.** His Sefer HaMaamarim uses the term (5 hits); not read in context.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat 5730 (sections 19-21): push the thoughts off in prayer (iskafya), and contemplate the Shechinah's spark in them at another time (is'hapcha). 5738, citing Tzava'at HaRivash, on a disturber's speech.
- **Other schools.** Besht and Maggid: raise the thought to its root. Berditchev: the a fortiori. Breslov: a kelipah to ignore and cut down by praying in order. Pillar 05 adds R. Tzadok (tzitzit and tefillin give the power to push) and R. Nosson (the foreign thought as a small heresy of 'nature'), not re-verified. The fault line is real: raise (founders) against push (Tanya, Breslov).

The Rebbe restores the contemplation for another time.

## S046 · Subduing and transforming · אתכפיא ואתהפכא

**Faces:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F05 The Essence beyond both · F07 The home below **Search terms:** אתכפיא · אתהפכא · מהפכין חשוכא לנהורא · לאהפכא

> Subduing and transforming are the unity of God overcoming its own opposite: first holding the other side down, then turning its darkness itself into light, which only the Essence can do.

## Derivation

1. The purpose of all worlds is the delight when the other side is subdued and darkness is turned to light, so that the light of Ein Sof shines in the very place of darkness, more than in the upper worlds (Tanya 36:5). (*stated*)
2. There are two modes. Subduing holds evil back (the beinoni). Transforming turns it: the life drawn from food, once clothed in Torah and prayer, 'is turned from evil to good' (Tanya 37:9). The tzaddik transforms the animal soul itself (Tanya 53:7, seen). (*stated*)
3. Only the Essence can transform. Every revelation, however high, is defined as light, so evil stands opposed to it and it can only fight evil. The Essence has no opposite and so can turn evil to good (Inyanah 20:1-2). (*stated*)
4. The Maharash reads it as the unity itself. 'Know today... that Havayah is Elokim': as evening and morning are 'one day', the two hearts, the good and the evil inclination, can become one when the other side is subdued (Toras Shmuel 5632). (*stated*)
5. The dwelling below is made by human work, by iskafya and is'hapcha, which is why Israel are called 'hosts', soldiers who make the enemy's land the king's (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 10 Shevat 5730). (*stated*)
6. The Besht: through evil one delights more in serving God, as evil becomes a throne and the left is subdued in the right (Keter Shem Tov 1:18). (*stated*)
7. That this is G10 (opposites joined in Him) is our reading; Keter Shem Tov 1:18 speaks of the left subdued in the right and of the delight in service, not of the Essence. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 33, 35-37 and 53; IH 26:26 (only those below, in bodies, 'weaken' the other side).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim uses the term (14 hits); not read in context.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Viduy 3: repentance that lifts what fell lowest exceeds the mitzvot of the righteous. Calling this 'the transforming side' is our label.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5632 (Vyadata): darkness first so that light comes from it (tohu before tikkun). The two hearts become one.
- **Rashab.** not located in this pass under this term.
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus (spine F11): matter becomes form and the physical a vessel for Godliness. Not re-read here.
- **The Rebbe.** Inyanah 20: only the Essence transforms. Basi LeGani 5730 (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat): the dwelling is made by iskafya and is'hapcha. In the same maamar (section 4) each has its own advantage: is'hapcha has novelty, iskafya has bitul. Section 5 then states generally that the drawing-down through iskafya is higher than that through is'hapcha (it is 'the Essence of Or Ein Sof above relation to worlds', called 'withdrawn'). Section 20 applies this to foreign thoughts.
- **Other schools.** Besht: evil as a throne for the good. R. Tzadok (Likkutei Maamarim 13): two services, subduing (Joseph, the ox under the yoke) and transforming (the penitent, where the sin itself becomes merit, a place the wholly righteous cannot stand).

The two sides partly measure different things (R. Tzadok: the standing of the penitent; the Rebbe: the light each draws down), but that this softens the disagreement is our reading. We do not reconcile them.

## S047 · Descent for the sake of ascent · ירידה צורך עליה

**Faces:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F07 The home below · F06 The soul as part of the One **Search terms:** ירידה צורך עלי' · ירידה לצורך עלי' · יתרון האור · מן החשך

> Descent for the sake of ascent is the unity of God as the soul's and the world's fall into concealment, undertaken so that the return reaches higher than the starting point.

## Derivation

1. The soul is a part of the light of the One who 'fills and surrounds all worlds, and all is as nothing before Him', yet it is clothed in 'the serpent's skin'. This descent is for an ascent: to raise the animal soul from nogah (Tanya 31:17). (*stated*)
2. All the contractions exist for the physical body, for subduing the other side, and for 'the advantage of light from darkness' (Tanya 49:4). The Besht reads Avram's going down to Egypt the same way (Keter Shem Tov 1:18). (*stated*)
3. Why the return exceeds the start: the Maharash says a soul before descent is a tzaddik and after it a baal teshuvah. Teshuvah here is not from sin but 'the spirit returning to God'. Living water surpasses the sea (Toras Shmuel, Kedoshim 5629). (*stated*)
4. What the soul refines fell lowest because its root is highest, like the top stone of a wall that falls furthest (the Rayatz, 5689; the Rebbe, 13 Tishrei 5741; Derekh Mitzvotekha, Viduy 3). (*stated*)
5. The world's descent through the sin and through exile is likewise for an ascent (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 10 Shevat 5730; see S049). (*stated*)
6. Outside Chabad the principle is applied to a person's own fall. Breslov: one who has fallen to the lowest places asks 'where is the place of His glory?' and by that question rises to the hidden glory, 'descent is the purpose of ascent' (Likutei Moharan II 12). Ger says only that where concealment is greater a holy point is hidden (Sefat Emet, Kedoshim 5631); it does not use the phrase, so its place here is our reading. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 31, 36 and 49.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Imrei Binah uses the phrase (3 hits); not read in context.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Viduy 3: what fell lowest has the highest root.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel, Kedoshim 5629: tzaddik before, baal teshuvah after. Sea, mikveh and living water.
- **Rashab.** not located in this pass in the works searched.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5689 ('Mah Tovu' 204): trials hide the highest spark; the lower the fall, the higher the root.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, 13 Tishrei 5741 (on the Maharash's hemshech): the soul refines sparks of tohu and rises above its prior state. 10 Shevat 5730: the world's descent through sin and exile.
- **Other schools.** Besht: the advantage of light from darkness. Breslov: the question 'where?' is itself the ascent. Ger: the hidden holy point. Difference: in the passages read, Chabad uses the phrase for the soul's descent into the body and the world's into exile; Breslov uses it for a person's actual fall into sin and doubt. Chabad's nearest counterpart for a personal fall is teshuvah from great love, which turns willful sins into merits (Tanya 7:9; S042), but it is not framed there as a descent for ascent. No source read here licenses seeking a descent.

## S048 · Good and evil and the source of evil · טוב ורע

**Faces:** F09 The unity of opposites · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** טוב ורע · תערובת טוב ורע · שורש הרע · מקור הרע

> Good and evil are the unity of God as a single will with a face and a back: good receives from its inner side, and evil stands opposed and lives only from its outer side, so that it can be overcome.

## Derivation

1. Good and evil mixed in the world are 'the tree of knowledge of good and evil', kelipat nogah, from which the animal soul comes (Tanya 1:17). (*stated*)
2. The source of evil, according to the Mitteler Rebbe: whatever spreads forth from the essence has an opposing force that also comes from the essence (pleasure and its opposite, pain: ענג against נגע). The opposition comes by the contraction and concealment that precede every light (Shaar HaYichud 54:4-12). (*stated*)
3. The Alter Rebbe: evil receives from the 'back' of the will, as one tosses something over the shoulder to an enemy, only to punish the wicked and reward those who subdue it (Tanya 22:3-5). It is defined by what it denies: 'I, and there is none besides me' (Tanya 36:4). (*stated*)
4. The Rayatz: the animal soul exists from gevurah and tzimtzum. Since contraction precedes revelation, the evil inclination comes first, and the greater the soul, the greater its inclination (Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, Pekudei). (*stated*)
5. In Him they are joined. The Shechinah, the true unity, holds good and evil 'in one subject' because evil is a throne for the good (Besht, Keter Shem Tov 1:17). Only the Essence, which has no opposite, can turn evil to good (Inyanah 20). (*stated*)
6. That these three accounts of evil's source (from the essence through tzimtzum, from the back of the will, a throne within the Shechinah's unity) are one account is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 1, 22 and 36; IH 26 (the tree of good and evil is the permitted and the forbidden).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 54: the opposing force comes from the essence, by tzimtzum.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Hayom Yom 11 Elul: this is a world of falsehood, so even the good is mixed with dross (see S044).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5632: darkness was first so that light should come from it (tohu before tikkun).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Umaayan 2:2:8: good from the inner will, evil and death from the outer.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692 (Pekudei): the evil inclination comes first, from tzimtzum.
- **The Rebbe.** Inyanah 20:2: evil opposes every revelation but not the Essence.
- **Other schools.** Besht: evil is a throne for the good. Berditchev (Kedushat Levi, Tetzaveh 5, citing the Ari): God is only good and does not attach His name to 'the evil' ('go to Pharaoh', not 'I send you'). His 'evil' is harm and punishment, not kelipah, so he answers a different question; that his distancing of evil from the Name is a counter-emphasis to the Besht is our reading, kept as thin. Izhbitz (S049) goes furthest, holding that the sin was only apparent.

## S049 · The sin of the tree of knowledge · חטא עץ הדעת

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F11 Darkness turned to light **Search terms:** חטא עץ הדעת · חטא אדם הראשון · עץ הדעת · קודם החטא

> The sin of the tree of knowledge is the unity of God first obscured in man: when a person began to see himself, good and evil mixed and the sparks fell, to be refined.

## Derivation

1. Torah Ohr: the sin planted in human nature 'the point of the kelipah of arrogance': seeing oneself, one's own lack or gain. Before the sin they felt no lack or gain of their own and perceived only the revelation of Godliness (Torah Ohr, Mishpatim 7:20). That is F08 in reverse. (*stated*)
2. After the sin the covering thickened into nogah, 'garments of skin'. Serving God means repairing that concealment of Elokim, so that darkness turns to light (Torah Ohr, Bereshit 5:9-10). (*stated*)
3. The sparks fell again. Malchut descends into nogah to refine the sparks that fell through Adam's sin and the 288 of the breaking (IH 26:22). The Rayatz: the sin reawakened the death of the kings of tohu. (*stated*)
4. The tree of good and evil is the permitted and forbidden thing itself, which is nogah. So the halacha of permitted and forbidden is the refining of the tree (IH 26:15, 24). (*stated*)
5. The world's descent through the sin is for an ascent: the Shechinah drawn down again by human work exceeds what was there before the sin (the Rebbe, Basi LeGani 5730). (*stated*)
6. Izhbitz holds that in the future it will be clear Adam ate only the good, and that the sin was 'only according to his understanding, like garlic peels, and no more' (Mei HaShiloach, Bereshit 8; whose understanding is left unsaid). Reading this as 'all is His will' and so as the unity is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Torah Ohr (Bereshit, Mishpatim); IH 25:21 (the exile of the Shechinah from the sin onward, read) and IH 26.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim uses the term (9 hits; one line on Torah descending 'in the sackcloth of nogah' to refine the tree was seen). Not quoted.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel uses the term (16 hits), including line 2890, where 'all of Torah is the refinement of the tree of knowledge' was seen. Not read in context.
- **Rashab.** not located in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692 (442): the sin reawakened the death of the kings and the 288 sparks fell. Removing chametz, the yetzer, is the remedy.
- **The Rebbe.** Basi LeGani 5730 and 5728: the Shechinah left through the sin, beginning with the tree of knowledge. Its return through iskafya and is'hapcha exceeds its first dwelling.
- **Other schools.** Izhbitz: the sin was, in the end, only a husk. R. Nosson (Likutei Halakhot, washing the hands 3): the flaw was eating food in which the light of the Will does not shine, and impurity entered through it (S050). Chernobyl: souls' sparks fell into food through Adam's sin (Me'or Einayim, Matot 1). Real difference: for Chabad the sin is a real fall to be refined; for Izhbitz it is finally reduced to 'garlic peels and no more', a husk that existed only according to (someone's) understanding. Mei HaShiloach minimizes it rather than denying it outright.

## S050 · Impurity and purity · טומאה וטהרה

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual **Search terms:** טומאה וטהרה · טומאה · טהרה · מקוה

> Impurity and purity are the unity of God as hidden and as restored: impurity is where His life is in exile and the separate yesh prevails, and purity is where bitul is drawn back in.

## Derivation

1. The other side is 'the place of death and impurity' because the inner will does not rest on it and its borrowed life is in exile (Tanya 22:6). (*stated*)
2. Impurity was decreed on the lands outside Israel because there the divine flow is wholly clothed in Asiyah. That garment of nature lets one say 'my strength' and is idolatry, a separate yesh (Likkutei Torah, Masei 3:1-3). (*stated*)
3. Even permitted pleasures and idle talk leave an impurity of nogah that must be purged; forbidden speech needs more (Tanya 8:4-6). (*stated*)
4. Purification is the drawing in of bitul. The red heifer is burned to its root, and living water, the source of Chochmah and of bitul, is added, so that the forces of impurity scatter of themselves (Likkutei Torah, Chukat 3:8-10). (*stated*)
5. The Maharash reads sea, mikveh and spring as the soul's descent: after its descent it can become 'living water', which purifies even the severest impurities (Toras Shmuel, Kedoshim 5629). The tie to the unity here goes through bitul beyond reason, and that link is our reading. (**reading**)
6. In the future, when the world no longer conceals, 'I will remove the spirit of impurity from the earth' (Likkutei Torah, Masei 3:3; Tanya 37:11-14). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 8, 22 and 37; Likkutei Torah Masei 3 and Chukat 3.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 26:24 uses 'from impure to pure' only of reasoning. Not located in this pass as a theme.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha treats the laws of the metzora and the heifer (seen in search, not read in context).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel, Kedoshim 5629: sea, mikveh and living water.
- **Rashab.** Toras Shalom uses 'tumah' often (26 hits); not read in context. Kuntres Umaayan 2:2:8: the forbidden is 'death and evil'.
- **Rayatz.** not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** R. Nosson (Likutei Halakhot, washing the hands 3:3): impurity came from the tree of knowledge, and every purification is by mikveh water, 'the river from Eden', the light of the Will above knowledge where the other side has no hold. Breslov and Chabad agree in structure (our reading): purity comes from a level above the knowing that was damaged (R. Nosson: the Will above daat; Likkutei Torah: living water as the source of Chochmah and bitul).

## S051 · Idolatry and 'other gods' · עבודה זרה

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** עבודה זרה · אלהים אחרים · עבודת אלילים · כפירה

> Idolatry is the unity of God denied from within creation: treating any channel, star or self as a thing in its own right beside Him.

## Derivation

1. The root of idolatry is not denying God outright, since idolaters call Him 'God of gods'. It is regarding something as a separate thing, set apart from His holiness. So arrogance equals idolatry (Tanya 22:8). (*stated*)
2. The other side is literally 'other gods' because the life in it is in exile, it is not nullified, and it says 'I, and none else' (Tanya 22:7). Whoever transgresses the will is, while he sins, further from His unity than the kelipah called 'idolatry' (Tanya 24:10-11). (*stated*)
3. Historical idolatry: the generation of Enosh honored the stars as channels of blessing. They wanted the flow without bitul. Their error was that the stars are 'an axe in the hewer's hand', and in truth there is nothing besides Him and all is nullified like the ray in the sun (Derekh Mitzvotekha, circumcision 5, after the Rambam). (*stated*)
4. The Rashab: bowing to the constellations as sources in their own right is denial of providence and of His giving of life. The error is possible only because the light is so hidden in the garments of nature (Kuntres Umaayan 23-24). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe: shituf ('God of gods') is forbidden to Israel in particular because Jewish souls are rooted in the Essence and in sovev, where there is no room for any existence besides Him (Basi LeGani 5713). That this is F02's higher unity made into law is our reading. (*stated*)
6. The person's side: anger is like idolatry because the angry man imagines himself master in his own domain (Izhbitz, Beit Yaakov, Noach 19). Yet even houses of idolatry must receive life from Him (Breslov, Likutei Moharan II 12). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 22 and 24; Likkutei Torah Masei 3 (the garment of Asiyah as idolatry, 'yesh and a separate thing').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 53:24 (the separate yesh of the three impure kelipot, the ministers who 'know their Master and intend to rebel').
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, circumcision 5 (the origin of star-worship and the Rambam's fifth principle).
- **Maharash.** not located in this pass (3 plain hits in Toras Shmuel, not read).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres Umaayan 23-25: idolatry as denial of providence, and bowing to the channel.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692 (443): arrogance toward people shows arrogance toward God (S040). No passage on idolatry proper was read.
- **The Rebbe.** Basi LeGani 5713 (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat): shituf, and Israel's root in the Essence. 5722 (Haazinu), seen: denial of providence over the earth.
- **Other schools.** Izhbitz (Beit Yaakov): anger as idolatry, the self as master. Breslov: houses of idolatry live from the hidden utterance. Chernobyl and Tiferet Yosef mention 'ein od' against other gods (seen in search, not quoted). The inward reading (idolatry as selfhood) is shared by Tanya and Izhbitz.

> לכן מאמינים שאין עוד, דלא רק שאין שום אלקות מבלעדו, כ"א שאין שום מציאות מבלעדו
> — The Rebbe (Basi LeGani 5713): Israel alone is warned against shituf because its souls are rooted in the Essence and sovev, where there is no room for any existence besides Him.

> **Beit Yaakov on Torah, Noach 19:2** (R. Yaakov Leiner of Izhbitz, Beit Yaakov on Torah; line 188)
> כל הכועס כאלו עובד ע"ז, לפי שנוטל לעצמו כח זר שנדמה לו שהוא כבעה"ב ברשות עצמו
> — Izhbitz (Beit Yaakov): anger is like idolatry because the angry man takes a foreign power to himself, imagining he is master in his own domain.

> **Likutei Moharan, Part II 12:1:9** (R. Nachman of Breslov, Likutei Moharan; line 6226)
> ואפלו מקומות המטנפים או בתי עבודהזרה צריכין גם כן לקבל חיות ממנו יתברך
> — Breslov: even houses of idolatry must receive life from Him, though from the hidden utterance.

## Thin or forced?

None. This is the most directly stated subject in the cluster: Tanya 22 defines idolatry as denial of 'His true unity'.

## Disagreements kept

| question | positions | subjects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is nogah in bitul or in yesh? | Mitteler Rebbe (Shaar HaYichud 53:26): nogah, even of Asiyah, is always in bitul; only the three impure kelipot are a separate yesh. Likkutei Torah (Chukat 3:6): even the good half of nogah is yesh and so is called kelipah, though it can be refined into full bitul 'as the host of heaven bow to You' (Chukat 3:7), the Mitteler Rebbe's own verse. | S041 |
| Raise the foreign thought or push it away? | Besht and Maggid: raise it to its root. Berditchev: turn it by an a fortiori. Tanya 28: only tzaddikim raise; everyone else pushes it off. Breslov: ignore it and pray in order. The Rebbe: push it off during prayer, contemplate it at another time. | S045 |
| Is subduing or transforming higher? | R. Tzadok and the Tzemach Tzedek: transforming (the penitent) is higher. The Rebbe (5730, section 5, applied in section 20): as a general rule the drawing-down through iskafya is higher than through is'hapcha, though is'hapcha has the advantage of novelty (section 4). The two sides partly measure different things (standing against drawing-down); that is our reading. | S046, S042 |
| How do kelipot receive life? | Tanya 22: from the back of the will, in exile. Rashab: they raise themselves to the surrounding light, which needs no vessels. Breslov: from the utterly hidden first utterance, and one must not ponder it. The Rashab and Breslov both place the source above the ordered flow (our reading); they differ on whether to inquire. | S040, S051 |
| What is the source of evil? | Mitteler Rebbe: an opposing force that also comes from the essence, by tzimtzum. Alter Rebbe: the back of the will. Besht: a throne for the good within the Shechinah's unity. Berditchev: God does not attach His name to evil, but he means harm and punishment, so his place in this question is our reading and thin. | S048 |
| Was the sin of the tree real? | Chabad: a real fall that planted self-awareness and scattered sparks, to be refined, and a descent for an ascent. Izhbitz: in the future it will be clear that Adam ate only the good, and the sin was 'only according to his understanding, like garlic peels, and no more': minimized rather than flatly denied. | S049 |
| Who completes the refinement? | The Rayatz: it must be gradual and from below, since refinement by revelation from above loses sparks. The Tzemach Tzedek (Hayom Yom 11 Elul): both from below and from above. Tiferet Yosef (Motzei Yom Kippur 1, verified): man refines as far as his hand reaches and God refines the rest, so that no spark is lost. The Rayatz and Tiferet Yosef share the aim of losing no spark and draw opposite conclusions. | S044 |
| To what does 'descent for ascent' apply? | Chabad (in the passages read): the soul's descent into the body and the world's into exile; a personal fall is handled as teshuvah from love (Tanya 7:9), not under this phrase. Breslov: also a person's actual fall, where the question 'where is His glory?' is itself the ascent. | S047 |
