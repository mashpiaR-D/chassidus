## The shape of the range

Bamidbar counts Israel; Nasso holds the sotah, the nazir and the priestly blessing. The Zohar builds a daily life out of them. The strongest material here is about the morning and the night: depositing the soul at night, the order of the morning prayer, the falling on the face after the Amidah as a real giving-over of the self, and the priestly blessing as the hour when the Hidden is revealed and "whisper and silence" fill the worlds. The Raya Mehemna adds its fullest account of teshuvah as the return of the letters of the Name, with fear, love and Torah as the three letters that rest on a person's head.

Blocks: - 2045-2064: the counting, blessing with a good eye. - 2065-2121: joy only in the land; the camps and the bed between north and south; the soul deposited at night; the order of the day's prayer and nefilat apayim. - 2122-2180: the soul examined at night; the Raya Mehemna on teshuvah and the image over the head. - 2181-2225: the sotah; the Raya Mehemna with Elijah (the Zohar names itself, 2211). - 2226-2255: the first ten in the synagogue; death and its seven judgments; the nazir. - 2256-2320: fear, humility and kindness; the priestly blessing; Abraham who counted judgment as mercy.

## The unity of G-d

**One mitzvah and the ten sefirot.** The Raya Mehemna gives the most practical line of the range on the unity: "when a person does one commandment, and no more, and does it in fear and love of the Holy One, because of it the ten sefirot rest on him. And whoever keeps one commandment properly, it is as if he kept all 248 positive commandments, for there is no commandment that does not include all 248" (2180). The whole is present in the part.

**The priestly blessing: the Hidden in the revealed.** "That hour is when the hidden, the Ancient of Ancients, is revealed in the Small Face, and peace is found in all" (2293). "When the priest below rises and spreads his hands, all the holy crowns above wake and are prepared to be blessed" (2274), and:

> בההוא זמנא, לחישותא ושתיקותא הוי בכל עלמין. למלך דבעי לאזדווגא במטרוניתא, ובעי למעאל לה בלחישו (Zohar, Nasso 15:10; line 2275)
> At that time there is whispering and silence in all the worlds, like a king who wants to join the Matrona and wishes to come to her in a whisper.

The three verses of the blessing begin with three yuds: "to show the complete faith, and to bless from the Ancient One whoever needs it" (2294). The Name is "revealed and hidden": revealed in YHVH, hidden in other letters, "and even the revealed one is hidden in other letters, because of the Hidden of hiddens within" (2282).

**The Name is not complete while she is in exile.** "He swore He would not enter the upper Jerusalem until Israel enter the lower Jerusalem ... as long as the Shechinah is here in exile, the Name above is not complete ... as it were, the holy Name remains lacking" (2309).

**Teshuvah as the Name made whole (Raya Mehemna).** "Whoever returns in teshuvah, it is as if he returned the heh to the vav ... and YHVH is completed by it. This is teshuvah: tashuv heh, the heh returns to the vav" (2149). When a person sins "he causes the heh to be distanced from the vav ... and so the Temple was destroyed" (2150). "All depends on teshuvah, which is the completion of His Name" (2150).

## States of consciousness

**Fear, love and Torah as the three letters on the head.** The most detailed inner anatomy of the range (Raya Mehemna):

> י' יראה, ודא איהי על רישיה דבר נש, ומנה ייעול דחילו ללבא דבר נש ... ה' אהבה על רישיה דבר נש, ומניה עאל רחימו דקודשא בריך הוא, על רמ"ח אברין דיליה ... ו' איהי על רישיה דבר נש, ומניה ייעול על פומא דבר נש מלולין לאולפא באורייתא (Nasso 4:16; line 2158)
> The yud is fear, and it is on a person's head, and from it fear enters his heart ... The heh is love, on a person's head, and from it love of the Holy One enters his 248 limbs ... The vav is on a person's head, and from it words enter his mouth to learn Torah.

The image over the head is life itself: "it is the breath that goes in and out of a person's mouth without toil or effort" (2152); if it departs, "life departs from him" (2153). *Ours:* this fits the map's love-and-awe states (S41-S46) and adds a picture the guide can use: fear, love and learning are not moods one produces but letters resting over one, that enter the heart, the limbs and the mouth.

**Hidden and revealed service.** "The hidden things: fear and love, which are in the brain and the heart ... If a person fears the Holy One or loves Him, no one else knows it, for it is a thing revealed only between him and his Maker. But one who toils in Torah and goes in the positive commandments, this is revealed to all" (2171-2172). *Ours:* a guard against the performance of feeling. The inward is private by nature.

**The levels of return.** There is a wicked man who regrets and afterward does neither good nor bad: "the Holy One forgives him, but he does not merit the higher teshuvah". There is one who, after atonement, "goes in the way of mitzvah and toils with all his strength in fear and love": he reaches the lower teshuvah, the heh. And there is one who "toils in Torah in fear and love of the Holy One, not to receive reward": he reaches the vav, "and he causes the vav to return to the heh" (2168-2169). This is S77 and S78 with a middle step.

**Abraham counted judgment as mercy.** "Koh" (thus) is the tenth crown, from which judgments wake. Abraham was told "thus (koh) shall your seed be", and:

> ואברהם אף על גב דידע דדינין מתערין מנה מהאי צדק. הוא חשבה צדקה, דדינין לא מתערין מנה, בגין דהוא רחמי (Nasso 20:2; line 2312)
> And Abraham, though he knew that judgments wake from this tzedek, counted it as tzedakah, from which judgments do not wake, for it is mercy.

"And he counted it to him as righteousness": Abraham counted it to the Holy One (2311). *Ours:* this is a whole inner move the guide should name. It is not denial: Abraham "knew" it was judgment. It is the choice to hold the hard place as kindness. It is close to S68 (the hiding seen as His) and to the Chabad teaching of "this too is for the good" (gam zu l'tovah). *Proposed state: counting judgment as kindness.*

**Seeing the Shechinah's face.** "Whoever sees the face of the Shechinah must go and run after it: let us know, let us pursue to know the Lord" (2310). Greeting Rabbi Shimon's circle is seeing that face.

**Joy only when she is home.** "There is no joy except when Israel dwell in the holy land ... When Israel are not in the holy land, a person may not rejoice and show joy" (2067). "Serve the Lord in joy" is for the land; "serve the Lord in fear, and rejoice with trembling" is for exile (2069). The Shechinah in exile, lying in the dust, "is not called joy until the Holy One comes to her and raises her from the dust" (2070). *Ours:* this is a sharp difference from Chassidus, which makes joy a duty in every place and time. The Zohar's joy in exile is always "with trembling". The guide should keep both: joy is the goal; it is honest about what is missing.

**The last hour.** The Zohar's fullest death account in this volume (2231-2243): a person "thinks the world is his forever"; his only defender is his good deeds (2232); two scribes write all he did and said; his deeds go before him to the grave. An angel stands before him; he opens his eyes "and gives over himself and his soul"; his spirit goes through every limb "and takes leave of it, like a man taking leave of his friend" (2239-2240). And at the end: "when the spirit comes to go out, having taken leave of all the body, the Shechinah stands over him, and at once it flies from the body. Happy is the one who cleaves to her" (2242). *Ours:* the same teaching as part 2, line 958, from a second place. The guide can say with the Zohar's authority that a person does not die alone.

## Meditation and practice

**Deposit your soul at night.** "Into Your hand I entrust my spirit": at night the tree of death rules, "and a person must first deposit his soul in His hand, as a deposit" (2086). The tree of death must return every deposit at dawn, when the tree of life wakes (2088). *Ours:* with part 2's "nightly handing over", this makes a full bedtime practice: examine the day, confess, give the soul over as a deposit, sleep.

**The order of the morning (and why it is an order of deeds).** On waking, bless the Faithful One who returned the deposit (2111). In the synagogue: tefillin, tallit, the offerings, the "yoke of the kingdom" in the praises of David, then the seated prayer and the standing prayer (2112). "Though prayer hangs on speech and the words of the mouth, all hangs first on the root of the deed, and then on speech" (2113); the deed is the tallit and tefillin, "like the prayer". "If the deed is blemished, the word finds no place to rest" (2116). To those "in the south" who want to unify: "accept the yoke of the holy kingdom every day first, and by it you enter the holy chamber of the south, and go round the sides of the world until they are tied in one knot" (2104). The order of the unity follows the corners of the altar (2105-2109).

**Falling on the face: give yourself over.** The most searching practice in the range. After the Amidah, a person has tied the unity, confessed and prayed; now he must fall on his face and say "To You, Lord, I lift my soul":

> כדין בעי ליה לבר נש לאחזאה גרמיה, בתר דסיים צלותא דעמידה, כאלו אתפטר מן עלמא ... השתא בעי לאתכנשא לגבי ההוא אילנא דמותא, ולמנפל, ולימא לגביה אליך יי נפשי אשא (Bamidbar 8:8; line 2117)
> Then a person must show himself, after finishing the standing prayer, as if he had departed from the world ... Now he must gather himself to that tree of death, and fall, and say to Him: "To You, Lord, I lift my soul."

"At first I gave it to You as a deposit; now that I have tied the unity ... here, I give my soul to You truly" (2117). "Not as a deposit, as at night, but as one who has departed the world" (2118). "This needs the intention of the heart", and then "the Holy One has mercy on him and atones his sin" (2119). And its guard: "woe to one who comes to coax his Master with a distant heart, not with his will ... he says 'To You, Lord, I lift my soul' and his words are all with a distant heart" (2119); "he must cleave his soul and his will to his Master and not come with a false will" (2120). *Proposed technique: the daily giving-over (nefilat apayim).* It is the Zohar's form of mesirat nefesh in prayer, with its own guard against pretense.

**Bless with a good eye and a loving heart.** "Whoever tells his friend's praise, of his children or his wealth, must bless him ... and a blessing must be blessed with a good eye, not a bad eye. In all things the Holy One wants the love of the heart" (2055-2056). "How much more one who blesses the Holy One: he needs a good eye and a good heart and the love of the heart; therefore 'you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart'" (2056). A priest "whom the people do not love, or who does not love the people, should not spread his hands"; a priest who blessed without love "became a heap of bones" (2304). Rav Hamnuna Sava's blessing: "May the Holy One open His eyes on you" (2306).

**At the priestly blessing, give Him your dreams.** "One who is troubled by his dream should come when the priests spread their hands and say: Master of the world, I am Yours and my dreams are Yours", because at that hour mercy is in all the worlds and "judgment is turned to mercy" (2294). The people should stand "in fear and awe" (2293) and not gaze at the priests' hands, "so as not to be brazen toward the Shechinah" (2292).

**Be among the first ten.** The Shechinah comes to the synagogue first; "happy is the one who is among the first ten", for the body of the community is completed in them (2226-2227). If the ten do not come together, "the Holy One cries: why did I come and there was no man?" (2229).

**Bless with the whole body while you can.** "Bless the Lord, my soul": David, seeing the judgments of the dying, said to his soul "bless before you leave the world, now while you are with the body", and to his limbs, "hasten to bless the holy Name before the time comes when you cannot" (2244). *Ours:* a line for `lips` and for the guide's embodied practice.

**The night watches (Raya Mehemna).** One who cannot learn at night should "arise and sing in the night, at the beginning of the watches", and say "supplications and requests, in every kind of song in his throat", the voice rising from the understanding of the heart through the windpipe to the mouth (2132-2135).

## The big human questions

**Why we die.** Adam "saw wisdom above and below; when he sinned the faces were diminished and he saw only the things of his body" (2048). Death is the tree of death that holds the deposit (2085-2088).

**Examined each night.** "When people sleep and taste the taste of death, the soul rises and stands where it stands, and is examined on the deeds it did all day, and they are written" (2127). A holy word "rises and splits the heavens and stands there until night comes, and the soul rises and takes that word and brings it before the King" (2128).

**The Zohar on itself.** The Raya Mehemna names "this composition of yours, which is the Book of the Zohar", and says that because Israel "will taste of the tree of life, which is this book of the Zohar, they will go out of exile with it in mercy" (2211).

**Fear, humility, kindness.** "How great is fear before the Holy One, for within fear is humility, and within humility is chasidut (kindness beyond the line)" (2256). "Whoever has fear of Heaven merits all of them" (2257). *Ours:* a ladder of character for the guide's work: fear first, then humility, then kindness.

**Holiness from below.** "When a person comes to purify himself, they purify him; when a person wants to be sanctified, they sanctify him, and spread over him the upper holiness" (2230).

## For the guide (ours)

1. Add *the daily giving-over* (2110-2121) as a technique, with its guard against the distant heart.
2. Add fear, love and Torah as the three letters over the head (2156-2159) to the love-and-awe states, and the hidden/revealed rule (2171-2172) as a guard.
3. Propose the state *counting judgment as kindness* (2311-2312).
4. Add the priest's rule (2304: bless only with love) and the dream prayer at the priestly blessing (2294).
5. Mark the Zohar's joy-with-trembling in exile (2067-2070) as a real difference from Chassidus's unconditional joy.
