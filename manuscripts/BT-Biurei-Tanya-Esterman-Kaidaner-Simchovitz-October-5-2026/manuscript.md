*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Three commentaries on Tanya by chassidim who taught it. Esterman is the longest. It glosses phrase by phrase and often says what a line means "in avodah." Its introduction argues that Chassidus made inner work (the soul's service) the main thing, where earlier musar worked on the body's actions. Kaidaner gives long answers to hard questions in the Tanya, above all on sadness versus bitterness and on who can be a beinoni. Simchovitz's notes are almost all cross-references to maamarim and Talmud sources, with a biography of him as a mashpia (spiritual mentor). The practical psychology is concentrated in their treatment of Tanya ch.12-14 and 26-31.

**How much of it is medicine.** some. Most of the text is Kabbalistic cosmology and source-references, but the notes on Tanya ch.12-14 and 26-31 hold a real and precise psychology of sadness, desire, stray thoughts and self-deception.

**Its model of the person.** A person has two souls. The godly soul is essentially self-nullifying (bittul). The animal soul is essentially ego (yeshus). Its natural layer carries each person's temperament: "white bile" inclines toward love, black bile toward fear. The mind and its will can govern the soul's garments: thought, speech and deed. The heart's essence is not under the mind's control. Hating evil is a gift from above, not a choice. Change comes through habit becoming second nature and through a will above reason that can turn one wish into another. Real change shows when what you want changes, not in feelings during prayer.

**Its philosophy.** Holiness is life and kelipah is death. So any state can be read by its vitality: a lifeless state comes from ego and the other side, and a vital one, even bitter, comes from the godly soul. The beinoni who only restrains himself (iskafya) brings as much divine pleasure as the tzaddik, and even draws down the divine essence. Descent is for the sake of ascent.

**What it adds.** This is the Tanya as working chassidim taught it. It gives practical tests the Tanya only implies: tell sadness from bitterness by the presence of vitality; test for real change by whether your wants have changed; a love like honor shows only when denied. It splits responsibility sharply: the mind rules the garments, not the heart. It says contemplation is useless mid-craving, so the hidden love must be used. Its prayer advice on stray thoughts is concrete, down to 'think about it after prayer'. It unmasks holy anger as temperament.

## I'm crushed that I'm not the person I should be, and it drains all my life

Heavy and lifeless, eyes down; a grief over one's failings that feels like piety but leaves one unable to serve with joy.

**What is really going on.** It is ego (yeshus), not repentance. The animal soul wants to be complete and important. It grieves at not being a tzaddik the way a man grieves at not being rich. When it has that completeness it turns coarse; when it lacks it, it sinks into sadness.

**Program: Turning dead sadness into living bitterness, then joy.** (1) Diagnose by vitality: heavy, lifeless, 'like a stone' is sadness from kelipah; alive and moving, even in pain, is bitterness from the godly soul. (2) Do not accept sadness that falls on you by itself or at random times; push sadness over worldly matters outside. (3) Come to bitterness deliberately, by daas, with a settled mind, not by sinking into it. (4) Keep the heartache small: 'not very'. Recognise the tzaddik's level is infinitely beyond you, so there is nothing to envy. (5) Recall that the beinoni's struggle gives G-d nachas like the tzaddik's. (6) Let bitterness pass into joy of the soul in prayer, then into Torah study; joy after bitterness exceeds the tzaddik's joy. *When:* At set, chosen times and within the order of prayer: humbling first, then bitterness, then joy, then Torah. Never as a mood one drifts into. *For:* A person with inner vitality who is complacent, or whose remorse has become self-absorbed grief over not being 'someone'. *Not for:* Someone clinically depressed or lifeless. The text itself says sadness is the danger, and 'not every mind can bear' intense states. `BT·Q15` · `BT·Q16` · `BT·Q22` · `BT·Q23` · `BT·Q24` · `BT·Q20` · `BT·Q31`

## My prayer feels real, then an hour later it's as if it never happened

Real love and understanding during prayer, then afterwards appetite for worldly pleasures with no sense of contradiction.

**What is really going on.** There is no daas, the power that tells things apart, so two opposites sit together as in a dream. Feeling in prayer did not change the person's nature or what he wants.

**Program: Habit becoming nature, and renewing the will.** (1) Practise the right response even while it feels imaginary; in the end habit becomes second nature. (2) Notice that habit makes will routine; arouse a new will beyond habit (the '101st time'). (3) Judge progress by whether what you want has changed, not by feeling in prayer. *When:* Ongoing, as fixed times for the work; the text asks one to set times for it. *For:* One who keeps the practices by nature and routine, and one whose prayer-feeling does not carry into life. *Not for:* Someone who expects fast transformation; the text says hating evil is ultimately a gift. `BT·Q13` · `BT·Q09` · `BT·Q10`

**Reframe.** From *"I had an intense prayer, so I've changed."* to **Change means your wants have changed. Feeling that fades into craving after prayer is like a dream that joins opposites.** Moving the test from feeling to will stops the person relying on emotional highs and points him to habit and renewed will, which the work says actually reshape nature. `BT·Q04` · `BT·Q10`

## I can't make myself stop wanting what I know is wrong

No amount of contemplation makes the heart despise the thing; the person feels like a hypocrite for still wanting it.

**What is really going on.** The mind rules only the garments: thought, speech and deed. It has no power over the heart's essence. When the heart is thickened by permitted indulgence, no contemplation will turn it. Despising evil is a gift, not a choice.

**Program: The first moment, and the point that doesn't want to be cut off.** (1) Choose at the very start of the enticement: do not give it heart at all. (2) If desire has already taken hold, don't rely on contemplation; the mind is covered. (3) Recall your hidden love: 'I don't want to be separated from G-d', a point above reason that the folly cannot cover. (4) Govern thought, speech and deed even while the heart still wants; that is within your power. *When:* In the moment of temptation, as often as it comes. *For:* Anyone; the text says every person can be a beinoni at every time. *Not for:* Not a tool for forcing feelings to change; it governs action, not the heart. `BT·Q06` · `BT·Q07` · `BT·Q11`

## Stray thoughts and worries hijack my prayer

Lustful, angry or anxious thoughts intrude during prayer; fighting them only makes them grow.

**What is really going on.** The animal soul's aim is to confuse. Answering it confuses more. Sadness makes it worse, because a lifeless heart takes in every stray thought and cannot push it away.

**Program: Not arguing with stray thoughts in prayer.** (1) Do not pay the thought any attention; divert your mind from it entirely. (2) Do not answer it: its whole aim is to confuse, and answering confuses more. (3) If it is something truly urgent, tell yourself it is not needed now, or decide to think about it after prayer. (4) Do not try to 'elevate' the thought; that belongs to tzaddikim, whose thoughts come from others. (5) Know yourself: each person knows in his own soul which case he is in. *When:* During prayer, each time a thought intrudes. *For:* A beinoni whose prayer is invaded by desire, anger or worry. *Not for:* A person with obsessive intrusive thoughts should not treat them as a spiritual failure; that needs other care alongside this. `BT·Q25` · `BT·Q26`

## Nothing moves me; my heart feels like stone

The soul's light and understanding do not shine in the body; contemplation does not take hold in the mind or rule the heart.

**What is really going on.** The wood that won't catch fire needs to be beaten, not more fire. The body's coarseness must be humbled; adding more contemplation is not the remedy.

**Program: Beating the wood that won't catch fire.** (1) Do not respond to a dull heart by piling on more contemplation. (2) Humble the body's coarseness: lowliness and submission of self first, as in the opening of prayer. *When:* At the start of prayer, as 'weight of head' and submission. *For:* A stable person whose heart is merely coarse or complacent. *Not for:* Anyone depressed, self-loathing or traumatised; the text's 'beating' and suffering-as-crushing language can harm. `BT·Q27` · `BT·Q31`

## I don't know if I really believe anymore

Doubts about faith dominate, though the person would never actually deny.

**What is really going on.** Doubts are the other side's words, not the self. Proof: if forced to deny, he would give his life. So the counsel is not to believe the doubts.

**Reframe.** From *"My doubts mean I don't really believe."* to **The doubts are not you. If forced to deny, you would give your life; you believe.** Moving the doubt outside the self frees the person from identifying with it. Self-sacrifice is the proof of what the core holds. `BT·Q28`

## I tell myself my anger is righteous

Anger or cruelty feels like zeal for heaven; faults like love of honor go unseen by oneself.

**What is really going on.** Once work is inward, self-deception becomes possible. A cruel nature dresses itself as holy severity (gevurah). A love of something, such as honor, is invisible even to oneself until it is denied.

**Reframe.** From *"My anger at wrongdoers is holy zeal."* to **Check it: it may be a cruel nature dressed as holiness, like dyeing wool with fake blue and calling it techeles.** Inner work makes self-deception possible, and faults show only when crossed. Naming that disguise lets the person test the source of the emotion instead of trusting its label. `BT·Q05` · `BT·Q01` · `BT·Q08`

## Why is this so hard for me when others seem fine?

The person feels uniquely weak or strangely built, comparing his struggles and emotional responses to others'.

**What is really going on.** The inclination is not the same in everyone. Each has his own. Temperament (the mix of the natural soul) shapes whether contemplation yields love or fear.

**Reframe.** From *"Something is wrong with me; others don't struggle like this."* to **Each person's inclination is specific to him, and temperament shapes whether contemplation yields love or fear.** Particularising the struggle removes shameful comparison and defines the real battlefield: 'his inclination', where conquest counts. `BT·Q29` · `BT·Q30`

## My mind and feelings have gone dark and the desire is stronger than ever

All understanding and feeling are withdrawn, leaving only bare faith, while the pull of desire intensifies.

**What is really going on.** That is what a trial (nisayon) is. Mind and feelings are taken away, desire is added, and the person is left with simple acceptance of the yoke.

**Program: The first moment, and the point that doesn't want to be cut off.** (1) Choose at the very start of the enticement: do not give it heart at all. (2) If desire has already taken hold, don't rely on contemplation; the mind is covered. (3) Recall your hidden love: 'I don't want to be separated from G-d', a point above reason that the folly cannot cover. (4) Govern thought, speech and deed even while the heart still wants; that is within your power. *When:* In the moment of temptation, as often as it comes. *For:* Anyone; the text says every person can be a beinoni at every time. *Not for:* Not a tool for forcing feelings to change; it governs action, not the heart. `BT·Q06` · `BT·Q07` · `BT·Q11`

**Reframe.** From *"I've lost my spiritual life; the desire is stronger than ever; I'm falling."* to **This is what a test is: understanding and feeling are removed, desire added, and you are left with bare acceptance of the yoke.** Recognising the darkness as the form of a test, not as proof of decline, keeps the person from despair and points him to the one faculty still available. `BT·Q32`

## Cautions

- 'Beat the body', humbling oneself 'like dust' and 'breaking the heart' (ch.29, 26) can deepen self-loathing in someone depressed or traumatised. - Kaidaner links black-bile sadness to sins and ego; never tell a clinically depressed person their illness is sin, ego or kelipah. - Calling sadness 'avodah zarah' or the doormat of all evil can shame a depressed person; use the vitality test gently, not as a verdict. - Esterman reads suffering as sent from above to crush pride and joy in suffering as higher; do not press this on someone in pain or grief. - Contemplating being 'in darkness, far from G-d' is meant to intoxicate and confuse the mind; unsafe for anxious or fragile people, as the text itself says of some minds. - Waving off doubts as the 'other side' may shut down honest questioning; offer it as relief, not as a gag. - The stray-thoughts advice is not treatment for OCD-type intrusive thoughts; those need clinical care too.
