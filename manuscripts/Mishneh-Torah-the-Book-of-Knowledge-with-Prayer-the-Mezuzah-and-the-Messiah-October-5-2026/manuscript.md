## The shape of the range

The Mishneh Torah opens with the unity of G-d and not with a law about deeds. The first book, *Madda* ("Knowledge"), is the Rambam's whole inner path in law form:

1. **Yesodei HaTorah (Foundations), 10 chapters.** G-d exists, is one, has no body (ch. 1); love and fear, the angels, and His knowing (ch. 2); the heavens and the elements (ch. 3-4), which are "the account of creation"; sanctifying His Name (ch. 5); the holy Names (ch. 6); prophecy and Moses (ch. 7-10).
2. **Deot (Dispositions), 7 chapters.** The middle way, the healing of traits, a whole day given to G-d, a health regimen, the conduct of a scholar, love of others and speech.
3. **Talmud Torah (Torah Study), 7 chapters.** Who must learn, how to divide time, how to teach, honor for teachers.
4. **Avodah Zarah (Foreign Worship), 12 chapters.** It opens with the history of idolatry and of Abraham (ch. 1-2); the rest is law. Chapter 11 ends with a clear word against superstition.
5. **Teshuvah (Return), 10 chapters.** Confession, what return is, the scales, free will and G-d's knowing, the World to Come, and the last chapter on serving from love.

## 1. The unity of G-d

**The first law is to know that He is.** "The foundation of foundations and the pillar of the sciences is to know that there is a First Existent, and He brings every existent into being" (line 1, YT 1:1). All that exists "exists only from the truth of His existence".

> יסוד היסודות ועמוד החכמות לידע שיש שם מצוי ראשון (line 1)

**He alone is true existence.** If nothing else existed, "He would not be cancelled by their cancelling" (line 3). "He alone is the truth, and nothing else has truth like His truth"; this is what "there is none else besides Him" means: "there is no true existent besides Him like Him" (line 4).

> הוא לבדו האמת ואין לאחר אמת כאמתתו (line 4)

This is the Rambam's version of "ein od milvado". Note the words "like Him": for him the world exists, but its existence is borrowed and lesser. Chassidus reads the same verse to say nothing else exists at all from His side (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah 6). The Rebbe reads line 1's "only from the truth of His existence" as saying that every creature is held by Him at every moment (Likkutei Sichos vol. 30, Vayishlach 1, p. 151; `Likkutei Sichos (Brooklyn, New York, 1951-1992).txt` line 1095).

**One, unlike any other one.** "Not one like a kind that holds many ones, and not one like a body that divides into parts, but a oneness that has no other oneness like it in the world" (line 7). The Tanya's "a wondrous unity that has no unity like it" (Tanya ch. 5, line 112 of the Tanya file) echoes this line.

> אלא יחוד שאין יחוד אחר כמותו בעולם (line 7)

**No body, no change, no feeling.** No place, no time, no anger, joy or sadness (lines 11-12). All the Torah's bodily words are a *mashal*, a parable, "and the truth of the matter the human mind cannot understand" (line 9). Moses saw only "His back", knowing G-d as distinct from all else, not His face (line 10).

**The knower, the known and the knowing.** YT 2:10 (line 22) is the Rambam text Chassidus quotes most:

> נמצאת אתה אומר הוא היודע והוא הידוע והוא הדעה עצמה הכל אחד. ודבר זה אין כח בפה לאמרו ולא באזן לשמעו (line 22)

He does not know by a knowledge outside Him as we do. If His life and knowledge were other than Him, there would be many gods. Therefore "because He knows Himself He knows everything, for everything leans on Him for its being" (line 22). Even the highest angels "do not know the Creator as He knows Himself" (line 20). The Tanya quotes this halachah word for word (Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah 7, line 804 of the Tanya file) and builds the chapter on it; in the gloss to ch. 9 (line 832) it says this holds only for the light clothed in the vessels of Chochmah, Binah and Daat, not above tzimtzum. All this is "like a drop from the sea" of what needs saying; it is *maaseh merkavah*, "the account of the chariot" (line 23).

**The forms are seen with the eye of the heart.** "Forms that have no matter are not seen by the eye but are known by the eye of the heart, as we know the Master of all without the sight of the eye" (line 42).

**The soul that knows lasts.** The human form, the *tzelem*, is *daat*, the knowing mind (line 43). It is "from G-d, from heaven", and because it knows the Creator "it stands forever" (line 44).

**No go-between.** Idolatry began not with denying G-d but with honoring His servants, the stars (line 241): "all know that You alone are He; but their error is to think this vanity is Your will". The core of the law is not to serve anything "as a go-between between you and the Creator" (line 244).

> להיות סרסור ביניכם ובין הבורא (line 244)

**Abraham found the One alone.** As a small child "he began to wander in his mind... and wondered how this sphere could always turn with no one to turn it" (line 243). With no teacher, among idol worshippers, "his heart wandered and understood" until he found the truth; "at forty Abraham recognized his Creator". Then he went from town to town calling out, and taught each person "according to his mind" (line 243).

## 2. States of consciousness

- **Love and awe from the same sight (YT 2:2, line 14).** When a person looks at G-d's great works and sees His wisdom, "at once he loves and praises and glorifies, and longs with a great longing to know the great Name". When he thinks about the same things, "at once he draws back and is afraid, and knows he is a small, low, dark creature, standing with a light, small mind before the One perfect in knowledge". *Ties to:* S39 (The heart answers), S43 (Love like fire), S45 (Lower awe), S46 (Higher awe), S49 (Weeping and joy in one heart). The Rayatz builds whole maamarim on this halachah (`Sefer HaMaamarim (Riga and Otwock, 1929-1933).txt` lines 732-733). In YT 4:12 (line 47) the same contemplation "adds love, his soul thirsts, his flesh yearns", and awe comes when he finds himself "like a vessel full of shame, empty and lacking".

> מיד הוא אוהב ומשבח ומפאר ומתאוה תאוה גדולה לידע השם הגדול (line 14)

- **Love-sick (Teshuvah 10:3, line 454).** The fitting love is "very great, strong love, until his soul is bound in the love of G-d and he is always lost in it, like one sick with love for a woman, whose mind is never free of her... sitting, standing, eating and drinking". *Ties to:* S43, S44 (Love as delight), and the Guide's *chesek* (Guide III:51, line 1443 of guide.txt). The Rambam's strongest line on feeling.

- **Love follows knowing (Teshuvah 10:6, line 457).** "One loves G-d only by the knowledge with which he knows Him; according to the knowledge will the love be, if little then little, if much then much." *Ties to:* C3 (From mind to heart to hand), S16 (Knowing it, not feeling it), S25.

> ועל פי הדעה תהיה האהבה אם מעט מעט ואם הרבה הרבה (line 457)

- **Serving from love: the truth because it is the truth (line 453).** "One who serves from love... does the truth because it is truth." This is Abraham's level. Children and beginners are taught to serve for reward "until their knowledge grows and they serve from love" (line 452), and the secret is revealed to them "little by little" (line 456). *Ties to:* S60 (The essential bond), S61.
- **Glad all one's days (Deot 1:4, line 92; 2:7, line 102).** The middle way in mood is neither loud laughter nor grief, "but glad all his days, calmly, with a pleasant face"; "the little that is his portion, he is glad in it". *Ties to:* S53 (Glad).
- **Joy in the commandment (Lulav 8:15, zemanim line 1272).** "The joy a person has in doing the commandment and in the love of G-d who commanded them is a great service." David "leaping and dancing before G-d". *Ties to:* S53, S50 (Joy and bittul together).
- **Prophecy needs joy (YT 7:4, line 72).** Prophets "direct their minds and sit glad and good-hearted, and alone, for prophecy does not rest amid sadness or laziness but amid joy". *Ties to:* S53; guard against S04 (Low and heavy).
- **Becoming another person (YT 7:1, line 69).** When the spirit rests, "he becomes another man and understands that he is not as he was". Moses' mind "was bound to the Rock of the worlds" always (line 74). *Ties to:* S74, and R-S6 "Heart awake while the hands work" (Guide).
- **The returner's new self (Teshuvah 2:4, line 396; 7:6-7, lines 439-440).** He "changes his name, as if to say, I am another and not the man who did those deeds". "Yesterday he was hated before G-d... and today he is beloved, near and dear"; "yesterday separated from G-d... today cleaving to the Shechinah". Returners "hear and are glad" when mocked for their past (line 441). *Ties to:* S31 (Turned over), S77, S78.
- **Half and half (Teshuvah 3:4, line 407).** "A person should see himself all year as if half innocent and half guilty, and the whole world as half and half"; one deed tips himself and the whole world. The shofar says "wake up, sleepers, from your sleep". The Rebbe reads this halachah often (Likkutei Sichos vol. 34, Re'eh-Elul 1, p. 89, line 15789 of that file). *Ties to:* S81 (The deed in the hand).
- **The inner will (Gerushin 2:20, nashim line 583).** A Jew forced to do what he must has not been forced: "since he wants to be of Israel and wants to do all the commandments and keep far from sins, and it is his inclination that overpowered him", when the inclination is weakened he acts by his own will. The Rebbe lists and uses this halachah (Likkutei Sichos vol. 38, index of Rambam sources, line 34303). *Ties to:* S41 (The hidden love wakes). This is close to the Tanya's hidden love (Tanya ch. 18-19) in law form.

## 3. Meditation and practice

- **Contemplation of creation as the door to love (YT 2:2 and 4:12).** "I explain great principles of the Master of the worlds' work so that they will be an opening for the one who understands to love G-d" (line 14). Chapters 2-4 are written *as* that contemplation: angels, spheres, elements, forms. *Ties to:* techniques `breadth` and `depth`; C1. The guide can use the Rambam's order: look at a creature, see the wisdom, let love come; then compare yourself and let awe come.
- **First the bread and meat (YT 4:13, line 48).** Walk in the orchard only after being filled with the practical law, because law "settles a person's mind first". And the principles of these four chapters can be known "by small and great, man and woman". *Guard (ours):* ground before ascent.
- **The middle way and its cure (Deot 1-2).** The straight path is the middle trait (line 92). One who is at an extreme is cured by going to the other extreme for a long time (line 97), then returning to the middle. Pride and anger are exceptions: go to the far end (line 98). For anger, a teacher may *act* angry "while he is not angry" inside (line 98). "Those who live in anger have no life." Sick souls should go to the wise, "the healers of souls" (line 96). *Ties to:* technique `discern`, and the Eight Chapters (see `eight-chapters.md`).
- **Habit makes the trait (Deot 1:7, line 95).** "He does, repeats and does a third time the deeds that follow the middle traits, and returns to them always, until they are easy." *Ties to:* technique `chazarah`, `act`. The Rambam's key law of training.
- **Imitate His ways (Deot 1:6, line 94).** "As He is called gracious, you be gracious; as He is called merciful, you be merciful." *Ties to:* `act`.
- **Everything for His sake (Deot 3:2-3, lines 104-105).** "A person must direct his heart and all his deeds only to knowing G-d." Eat to be healthy, work to live, sleep to rest the mind, all so that the body can serve knowing G-d; "his sleep is service to G-d"; "in all your ways know Him". *Ties to:* technique `carry`, S63, S82. This is the Rambam's "avodah b'gashmiyus", worship through ordinary acts. Chassidus takes it further: the act itself, not only its purpose, can be holy (ours, to check against the Baal Shem Tov material).
- **Against self-torment (Deot 3:1, line 103).** No vows of abstinence beyond the Torah, no constant fasting: "do not be too righteous". *Guard.*
- **Silence (Deot 2:4-5).** Speak little; "a fence for wisdom is silence". *Ties to:* technique `quieting`.
- **Kavanah in prayer (Tefillah 4:15-16, lines 112-113; 5:4, line 120).** "Prayer without intention is not prayer." "What is intention? He clears his heart of all thoughts and sees himself as if standing before the Shechinah." Sit a little before prayer to direct the heart, pray slowly and pleadingly, and "not like one carrying a load who throws it down and walks off"; sit a little after. "The first pious ones waited an hour before prayer and an hour after, and prayed for an hour." Stand with eyes down "as if looking at the earth, and the heart turned upward as if standing in heaven" (line 120). Do not rise to pray out of laughter, talk or anger, "but from words of Torah" (line 115). *Ties to:* techniques `arrive`, `before_whom`, `quieting`, `heart_after`; S33, S80. The Chassidic custom of long preparation for prayer (Tanya ch. 41, "before Whom you stand") has its halachic floor here.

> שיפנה את לבו מכל המחשבות ויראה עצמו כאלו הוא עומד לפני השכינה (ahavah line 113)

- **One verse, one blessing.** The Shema is valid if the heart was directed in the first verse (ahavah line 14); prayer need not be repeated if the heart was directed in the first blessing (line 186). This is the legal root of the Guide's first step (R-T2, Guide III:51).
- **The mezuzah as a bell (Tefillin-Mezuzah 6:13, ahavah line 397).** "Whenever he goes in or out he meets the unity of the Name, remembers His love, wakes from his sleep and his errors in the vanities of time, and knows that nothing lasts forever except knowing the Rock of the world." *Ties to:* technique `carry`. *Proposed new technique* **R-T3 "The doorpost return"**: each time you pass a doorway, stop for one breath on the unity and on His love.

> וידע שאין דבר העומד לעולם ולעולמי עולמים אלא ידיעת צור העולם (ahavah line 397)

- **Confession in words (Teshuvah 1:1, 2:2-3).** Say it with the lips: "I sinned... I regret and am ashamed... and I will never return to this." Words alone without a firm heart are "like immersing with a creeping thing in his hand" (line 395). The inner test: "He who knows secrets will testify that he will never return to this sin" (line 394). *Ties to:* technique `cheshbon`, `lips`, S77.
- **Search out traits, not only deeds (Teshuvah 7:3, line 436).** One must return from anger, hatred, envy, mockery, chasing money, honor and food; these are harder than sins of action. *Ties to:* `cheshbon`.
- **Night learning (Talmud Torah 3:13, line 192).** "Most of a person's wisdom is learned at night"; "the song of Torah is only at night". *Ties to:* S79.
- **Forgive fully and wipe the heart (Deot 7:8, line 159; Teshuvah 2:10).** Do not bear a grudge: "wipe the thing from his heart", because to one who understands, all worldly matters are "vanity and nonsense" (line 158). *Ties to:* S86 (The other held).

## 4. The big human questions

- **Am I free?** "Permission is given to every person" (line 424). "Every person can be righteous like Moses our teacher or wicked like Jeroboam" (line 425). G-d wills that man be free, as He wills fire to rise (line 427). How this fits with His foreknowledge we cannot know, because His knowing is Himself (line 428); here Teshuvah 5:5 uses YT 2:10 directly.
- **Can I change?** Yes; "nothing stands in the way of return" (line 417). "One who comes to be purified is helped" (line 433). The returner stands higher than one who never sinned (line 437).
- **Why suffer or struggle?** Blessings and curses in the Torah are means: peace and plenty free us to learn and serve (line 450). The days of the Messiah are wanted not to rule, eat or drink, "but to be free for Torah and its wisdom" (shoftim line 809).
- **What lasts?** Only the knowing of G-d (ahavah line 397). The World to Come is souls without bodies who "know and grasp of the truth of G-d what they do not know in the dark, low body" (line 443), and it "exists and stands" now (line 449).
- **What is a good person?** Compassion, a pleasant face, judging everyone favorably (line 135), being "among the pursued and not the pursuers" (line 141), love of every Jew and of the convert (lines 144-145), gentle rebuke without shame (line 149).
- **When will it be good?** The Messiah will be a king who keeps the Torah and fights G-d's wars; no miracles are required of him (shoftim line 799). "The world goes on in its way" (line 806); the wolf with the lamb is a parable. The details "bring neither to fear nor to love", so do not calculate the end but "wait and believe in the general matter" (line 807). In that time "the whole world will be busy only with knowing G-d" (line 810):

> ולא יהיה עסק כל העולם אלא לדעת את ה' בלבד (shoftim line 810)

## 5. Where Chassidus reads this book, and where it departs

- **YT 2:10, knower and known.** Quoted and limited by the Tanya (see RAMBAM.md for the full account, with the Gate of Unity and the Tzemach Tzedek).
- **YT 1:1-4.** The Rebbe reads "only from the truth of His existence" as constant dependence (line 1095 of Likkutei Sichos). The Rashab (Hemshech Ayin Beis) and the Maharash (Toras Shmuel) also quote "the foundation of foundations". Where the Rambam says no other existence is "like His", Chabad says there is no other existence at all from His side.
- **YT 2:2.** Chassidus agrees that love and awe are born of contemplation, and makes it the method of hisbonenus. The Rayatz's maamar (lines 732-733) uses exactly this halachah as the path.
- **Deot 3:2-3 and Guide III:51.** Chassidus agrees that every act can be for G-d, and adds that the physical act itself becomes holy and draws G-d below (dirah betachtonim), which the Rambam does not say.
- **Teshuvah 10:6 (love by knowledge).** The Tanya agrees that love comes by knowledge (Tanya ch. 3, 42), but adds a love that is not in proportion to knowledge: the hidden love inherited from the Fathers, which even a simple Jew has (Tanya ch. 18-19). The Rambam's love is only as big as one's understanding.
- **Hilchot Talmud Torah.** The Alter Rebbe wrote his own Hilchot Talmud Torah; the Rambam's rule that learning brings to deeds (line 182) stands behind the Chabad stress on learning as the first service.
- **Shemittah 13:13 (see `mishneh-torah-index.md`).** The Rebbe uses "not only the tribe of Levi, but every person from all who come into the world" (Likkutei Sichos vol. 31, Tetzaveh 1, p. 163; line 5348).

## What this range adds

- The Rambam's inner path in legal form: know, love, fear, imitate, return. - A plain meditation for love and awe (YT 2:2), and two short practices for the day: the doorpost return (R-T3) and the hour before and after prayer. - The halachic basis for the Guide's attention ladder (first verse, first blessing). - The strongest statements of free will and of return in the tradition.

## Where the thread is thin

- YT 3-4 and parts of 2 are medieval astronomy and physics; their content is the wonder they are meant to cause, not the science. - YT 5-6 (martyrdom, erasing Names), Deot 4 (the health regimen, which is medieval medicine and not advice for today; the guide should not use it as health guidance, ours), Talmud Torah 4-7 (classroom rules, honor of teachers, bans) and Avodah Zarah 3-12 (the laws of idolatry, the condemned city, superstitions) were read in full and carry little inner content beyond the lines cited. - Teshuvah 3:6-14 (lists of those without a share) and 4 (the 24 hindrances) are sharp and legal; the guide should use their end: all hindrances yield to return (line 423).
