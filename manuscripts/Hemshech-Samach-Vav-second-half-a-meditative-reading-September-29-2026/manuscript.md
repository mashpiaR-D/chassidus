## 1. The book as one meditative journey

The first half of Hemshech Samach Vav asked why there are worlds at all, and answered: a desire with no reason, that the Essence of God be at home below. This second half asks the next question, the one a person lives with. If that is what He wants, what in me reaches Him? Not His light, which the Rashab has already shown can be known and felt and ranked. Him. The road this half walks goes from the bright to the plain, from what the mind grasps to what the self gives over, and from above to below.

It starts at the top of the mind. White adds nothing to linen, and the light adds nothing to God; the Tanya's own formula, "He alone is, and there is none besides Him", is placed as the bitul of wisdom, and above it stands a silence "like the stone" (lines 4519-4542). Then, maamar by maamar, the Rashab keeps lowering the place where the highest is found. Not in the son who runs his father's estate but in the son who goes far away and learns a craft (line 4601). Not in the clear Written Torah but in the sage's doubt at the moment he must rule, worked through back to the same truth (line 4718). Not in the scholar's pleasure but in study done in fear of missing the truth, and in the unlearned man who simply does what he is told (line 4887). Not in the human will that has reasons but in the animal's stubborn will, turned (line 4990). Not in the angels, who stand, but in the soul that comes down into a body of dust, because its root shines only below (line 5909), the way a lever is gripped at its lowest end.

Along the way the mind is brought, honestly, to its edge. The world is real, only not true (line 5175). From all His light we know nothing of Him, not even that He is a luminary; from the plain fact that things exist we know one thing only, that He is from Himself (line 5548). His Name has no meaning, because it is not a power (line 5708). His hiding is His own choice, and a hiding He chooses is no hiding (line 5684); in Himself He shines openly, and the worlds simply do not feel it (line 5948). The contemplation does not stop there. It turns, each time, to what reaches past the mind: the yoke, the deed, the question, the descent, thanks said before understanding anything (line 5262), doing before hearing (line 6282), the will in the mitzvah that is whole in any part of it (line 6189). And it ends where the series ends, on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur: in the shofar's cry, "a voice not heard" that moves the essence of the soul (line 6400), in a Name "not called light at all" (line 6417), and in Israel "bound together in one band" (line 6454).

So the journey goes from "I understand" to "I am given over", and from "He is above" to "He wants to be found here". The contemplation it asks for is thinking-through at full stretch: the Rashab's pictures are drawn from the person's own life and body (hair and skull, a name, a good man, a jumper, a lever, a craftsman resting), and each one is marked where it breaks. But the thinking is meant to arrive somewhere it cannot enter. Contemplation, the series says in its first half, sees Him only "from behind" (line 3735); what reaches the Essence is bitul, and bitul here has a plain shape: the servant who does not carry his work as his own, the halacha learned in order to do it, the mitzvah done when there is no taste, prayer begun in heaviness of head and mercy for one's own soul (line 5824). Its images are silence rather than song, the wordless cry rather than the melody, the seed that tastes of nothing rather than the fruit. It is a demanding book. It is also a merciful one: "the unlearned and simple people also reach" (line 4887), and the Rashab refuses, in the end, to rank those who learn above those who do (line 6354).

## Bayom HaShmini Atzeres 5667, second part: the white that adds nothing, and the silence above wisdom (lines 4519-4542)

**The movement.** It takes the most exact formula of the unity a person may already hold, the Tanya's "He alone is, and there is none besides Him", and shows a floor under it. It empties the mind even of the one who is doing the nullifying, and leaves it in silence.

**The line to hold** (line 4538, OCR as it stands):

> אבל אפס בלתך (הוא) היינו שגם עמך ית' לא יש שום מציאות כלל
> *But "nothing apart from You" means that even with You, blessed be He, there is no existence at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Start with the picture. Red or green is added to cloth. White is not. Linen is simply white, and washing adds no whiteness. It only takes the dirt and the black off (line 4519). So white is not "a something". It is the thing itself, shown. The light of God is like that. It is not a thing standing next to Him. It is the Essence revealed, and so it adds nothing to Him (line 4521). That is why one light can enter every vessel, even vessels that are opposites, the way the Name Havayah joins every other Name. Now climb. Wisdom (Chochmah) is white too, but it is still "some existence", which is why disagreement starts in wisdom (line 4527). The light that stays close to its source feels that He alone is, and that apart from Him nothing is. That is "there is none besides You", and the Rashab says the Tanya's words "He alone is" name exactly this level, the level of Chochmah (line 4538). Above it is "nothing apart from You": even with Him there is no one there to feel anything. Then the picture he gives for it: silence. Prayer that says "O Lord, open my lips" is still speech, even if it is not chosen. Silence is "like the stone", with no spreading out and no self at all. It is the bitul of the yechidah, the soul's deepest point, and "a fence for wisdom is silence" (line 4542). Bring it home. Think of the moment with someone you love when you run out of words and do not need them. Nothing is missing in that quiet. The picture breaks here: linen has dirt that came from outside. Before Him there is no outside at all.

**From inside.** Words thin out. The quiet that is left is not empty. It is full, and there is no one in it keeping score. A person may notice that even the thought "I am nullified" has gone quiet.

**At the edge.** Yes, and this is where the passage lives. It goes past the bitul that feels "He alone" to efes, nothing at all, the silence of the yechidah and of Keter, which is counted as the Emanator's own Essence (line 4542).

**Into the day.** Before "O Lord, open my lips" today, stand one moment in silence first.

## Bayom HaShmini Atzeres 5667, second part: the bare skull, the equal deed, and the yoke that takes it in (lines 4543-4587)

**The movement.** It stops the mind from grading its mitzvot. It turns attention from what a mitzvah means to the plain doing of it, and there all the deeds are found equal.

**The line to hold** (line 4567, OCR as it stands):

> דעצם עשיית המצות הוא למעי מבחי' התורה ולמעלה מכוונת המצות
> *The very doing of the mitzvot is above Torah, and above the intentions of the mitzvot.*

**Contemplating it.** Torah comes from wisdom, and wisdom divides. The Rashab pictures it as hairs on a head, each hair its own light (line 4551). Even the inner Torah and the intentions behind the mitzvot divide into many levels of meaning and delight (line 4567). The mitzvot are rooted higher, in the bare white of the skull where no hair grows, the "essential nothing" of Keter. So in the doing itself there are no differences at all: "do not sit weighing the mitzvot of the Torah" (line 4567). He gives two pictures. Food is divided, the best blood to the brain and the rest to the limbs. A garment is not: the same cloth can cover head or foot, and only the tailor's cut differs (line 4551). And a faithful servant who loves his work has favourite jobs, while a simple servant who works only because of the yoke finds every job the same (line 4570). Then the festival. Shemini Atzeres is when all that was drawn down in Tishrei is taken in, inwardly, and this needs the yoke, even in the joy, so that it does not burst out past its bounds (line 4580). On Simchat Torah we circle the bimah holding the scroll. Circling is a deed, and a deed is greater than reading (line 4580). It belongs to the second tablets, the Torah won by toil (line 4586). Bring it home. There is a mitzvah you love and one you only put up with. Pick up both with the same hand. The picture breaks where the Rashab breaks it: a garment is still cut to measure. The deed is not cut at all.

**From inside.** A quiet evenness. The small, unglamorous mitzvah weighs as much as the beautiful one. The need to feel something before doing it loosens.

**At the edge.** Yes. The doing reaches the essential nothing of Keter, where Atik is "separated from the whole chain" (line 4565), and the second tablets carry what comes from the Essence's own hiddenness (line 4586).

**Into the day.** Do the mitzvah you like least today with the same care as the one you like most.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Amar R' Akiva (Noach, Rosh Chodesh Marcheshvan 5667), first part: the son on his father's estate, and the son who learns a craft (lines 4588-4656)

**The movement.** It humbles the gifted mind and honours the toiling one. What a person understands easily is the Father's; what he wins by his own work, far from home, is the new thing that delights the Father most.

**The line to hold** (line 4612, OCR as it stands):

> וא"כ, כל מה שמשכיל ומתחכם בתורה ובידיעת אלקות, אין זה מכח עצמו, אלא בכח עליון שבו
> *So everything he understands and grows wise in, in Torah and in knowing God, is not by his own power, but by the higher power within him.*

**Contemplating it.** The parable is long and tender (lines 4593-4602). A father has a perfect son, wise and upright, who runs the whole estate and makes it prosper. The father is still not content. Everything the son does is with the father's goods. Even his bread is the father's. True completeness would be for the son to go far away, serve a craftsman as a servant "until the soul is wrung out", and then feed himself by the work of his own hands, even a little. That bread is sweeter to him than many pleasures that are not his own, which the Sages call "bread of shame". And the father is happier with it than with all he could give him (line 4601). The analogue: the souls of Atzilut are Godliness itself, and through Torah they become "like the One above" and light the Name even in the dark (lines 4604, 4610). Yet all of it is still the Father's. What comes to them from the Written Torah is "something from something" (line 4612). Only toil in the Oral Torah, working out how a mitzvah is done, makes something new, "like something from nothing" (line 4621). Yosef flourished only after he was sold as a servant (line 4631). He adds a second picture. The Written Torah is manna, bread from heaven with no chaff. The Oral Torah is bread from the earth: ploughing, sowing, the thirty-nine labours, sifting and baking, until it is food (line 4646). First it is called "the Torah of God", and after the toil it is called "his Torah" (lines 4648-4656). Bring it home. List what you have that came easily and what you won by work. The picture breaks where the text marks it: the new thing is only "like" creation from nothing, and the Rebbe's footnote stresses that even the student's discovery was given at Sinai (lines 4622-4629).

**From inside.** A softness about one's own talents, since they are not one's own. And a dignity in plain, hard work, with the sense that Someone is pleased by the small loaf.

**At the edge.** It points there: the toil of the Oral Torah reaches "God, except You", the essential hiddenness, and Eden that no eye has seen (lines 4614, 4621).

**Into the day.** Today, work one hard thing through yourself instead of taking the ready answer.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Amar R' Akiva, second part: three knots, face to face, and the mikveh (lines 4657-4689)

**The movement.** It replaces the picture of a long chain between a person and God with a picture of faces meeting. It lets a person feel that even the outside of Torah, its letters read aloud, touches the hidden inside.

**The line to hold** (line 4662, OCR as it stands):

> אבל בבחי' ממוצע דתורה הוא שמחבר ב' המהותים יחד, וממילא ההתחברות הוא בעצם מהותם ממש
> *But Torah, as a go-between, joins the two essences together, and so the joining is in their very essence.*

**Contemplating it.** The Zohar says three knots are tied: Israel, Torah and the Holy One, each hidden and revealed. The simple picture is a chain, where the head of each lower ring holds the tail of the ring above (line 4658). That is how everything else in the order of worlds works. The Rashab refuses it as the whole truth. At Sinai it was "face to face": the inner light of Ein Sof was received by the inner part of the souls. So the hidden of Torah and the hidden of the Holy One "became truly one, without a chain from head to end" (line 4660). That is why reading the letters aloud is "as if reading His Names" (line 4660). Every other go-between passes on only the outside of what is above it, like a translator who gives the sense but not the speaker. So it also separates (line 4662). Torah joins the essences themselves. That is why it is "not wondrous, and not far" from you (line 4668). Then the mikveh. The pit is understanding, the water is wisdom, and immersion (tevilah) has the letters of bitul. It has two parts: letting go of the former self, and being taken into what is higher. It works mainly when one is pained over where one stands (line 4675). Prayer purifies from below, Torah from above: "before whom you purify yourselves, and who purifies you" (line 4675). Bring it home. Read one verse aloud, slowly, the way you would go down into water. The picture has its limit: the Rebbe's footnote says Torah joins essences between God and the souls only; between God and the worlds it separates, until Israel's toil joins them (line 4664).

**From inside.** Nearness without steps. The sense of being received, the way water closes over a person who goes down into it.

**At the edge.** Yes: through the revealed letters one is bound to "the hidden of the Holy One" (line 4665), and the rising is into "the higher delight" (line 4686).

**Into the day.** Read one verse aloud today, slowly, as if saying His Names.

## Lech Lecha (8 Marcheshvan 5667), first part: the sage's doubt at the moment of ruling (lines 4690-4736)

**The movement.** It turns doubt from an enemy into a door. The question that shakes what one was sure of is itself a hiding, and working through it back to the same truth reaches deeper than the first certainty did.

**The line to hold** (line 4718, OCR as it stands):

> הנה ביגיעה והעמקה זאת ה"ה מגיע לבחי' עצמות א"ס ממש, היינו בחי' ההעלם העצמי דא"ס
> *In this toil and deepening he reaches the very Essence of Ein Sof, the essential hiddenness of Ein Sof.*

**Contemplating it.** The Written Torah is "clear wisdom", already sifted in God's own wisdom, the way a great sage simply sees how a thing is, without arguing it back and forth (line 4702). Everything that flows into the worlds, even the life of the other side, is measured by it. But "there is no sage like one who has been tested" (line 4711). A builder knows the plan and only learns it truly when he builds. So a sage who knows the law well, when he must rule on it in practice, suddenly has doubts. Contradictions appear. Not that Torah has doubts, he says, but now it is real: maybe it is not true (line 4716). He strains and deepens until every contradiction falls away and he comes back to the same truth he knew before. In Yiddish the Rashab says he listens very hard to whether it is really so (line 4716). "Every question is from the other side" (line 4718). The questions are a hiding, and they belong to the Oral Torah only. What comes out is the same law, and yet it comes now from the Essence, as new light, "creation from nothing" (lines 4720, 4725). That is why the laws branch without number (line 4727). Bring it home. Think of the moment before you sign, or before you tell a child "no", when the thing you were sure of wobbles. The picture breaks here: in the parable the doubt is the sage's weakness. In the Torah the hiding is put there on purpose, so that it can be walked through.

**From inside.** Doubt stops being frightening. The second "yes", reached through the question, sits deeper and quieter than the first.

**At the edge.** Yes: the toil reaches the essential hiddenness of Ein Sof (line 4718).

**Into the day.** When something you were sure of wobbles today, stay with the question until it settles, instead of pushing it away.

## Lech Lecha, second part: two ways of refining, and the three lands you leave (lines 4737-4782)

**The movement.** It turns the person toward his own animal side, not to crush it but to teach it. It ranks the slow work of changing one's nature above the quick relief of being flooded with light.

**The line to hold** (line 4753, OCR as it stands):

> נשלם ע"י הבירור מלמטה למעלה דוקא, מפני שעושה את הדברים התחתונים כלים לאלקות
> *[The purpose] is completed only by the refining from below upward, because it makes the lower things themselves vessels for Godliness.*

**Contemplating it.** The first way is light from above. "A little light pushes away much darkness". When a person contemplates something Godly and it shines in him even a little, he takes his mind off the world's vanities, and the coarseness falls away by itself (line 4738). The second way works from below. The animal soul itself is brought to understand the good of Godliness and to want it, and not to be drawn any more after cravings (lines 4749, 4751). Sometimes this needs bitterness and plain forcing; sometimes a contemplation fitted to what the animal soul can grasp. He pictures silver refined two ways. In one, the pure silver is taken out first and the dross left. In the other, the grossest dross is removed first, and then finer, and finer, until the whole lump is clean (line 4755). The second is higher, because the lower thing itself becomes a vessel, and that completes the purpose of the dwelling below (line 4753). Then Avraham's journey. "From your land" is the coarse desires that go beyond even the animal's nature. "From your birthplace" is habit, the bad neighbours one got used to. "From your father's house" is the animal soul's nature itself, and that is the main work, long and patient (line 4781). Every trial hides and holds back, and standing firm against it cancels the hiding (line 4769). Bring it home. Take one pull in your life and ask which land it is. The picture breaks with the silver: silver does not want to be pure. Here the animal soul itself comes to want it.

**From inside.** Patience with oneself. The animal side is not the enemy; it is the student. A person may feel less divided.

**At the edge.** Yes: this walk draws "His essential Name" and the hidden, essential delight (line 4781).

**Into the day.** Name one pull that is only habit and one that is your nature, and treat them differently today.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## VaHashem Amar HaMechaseh (Vayeira, 15 Marcheshvan 5667), first part: help from behind (lines 4783-4852)

**The movement.** It gives a person back the hands that were holding him all along, without taking away his own walking.

**The line to hold** (line 4786, OCR as it stands):

> ונדמה לתינוק שהולך מכח עצמו, ובאמת זהו בכח הגדול, אלא שהוא בהעלם
> *And it seems to the toddler that he walks by his own strength, but in truth it is by the grown-up's strength, only hidden.*

**Contemplating it.** A small child cannot walk alone. His father stands behind him, holding him, or gives him a little push. The walking is really the child's; the power is really the father's; and the child is sure he did it himself (line 4786). So is help from above. Every morning a glow comes to rouse a person to serve. "One who comes to purify himself is helped", from a place he does not know (line 4795). Sometimes the light pushes from behind; sometimes it holds from behind, so that he does not fall all day (line 4795). In exile He hides completely, and still "stands behind our wall, looking through the windows" (line 4814). Open help is another thing. The father bends down and lifts the child onto his shoulder. That, the Rashab says, is the Essence lowering itself, and so it is seen (line 4805). A dwarf on a giant's shoulders sees far, not by his own height (line 4805). Then the teacher. A good teacher teaches the short way, like the Mishnah, and the student finds the answers inside it and thinks he found them himself (lines 4816-4825). A deeper teacher, when the matter is too fine, gives the student the essence of the whole subject, and the student comes out of his confusion by his own power (lines 4829-4831). "Let one who repeats a teaching see its master standing before him" (line 4841). Bring it home. Remember one thing you are proud of doing alone. Who was behind you? The picture breaks here: a father lets go in the end. This holding never stops, and still the walking is truly yours (line 4852).

**From inside.** Gratitude that does not shrink the self. The loneliness of effort eases. Someone has had a hand on your back.

**At the edge.** It points there: open help is "the lowering of the Essence" (line 4805). Mostly it stays with the hidden help.

**Into the day.** At one success today, say quietly: You were behind me.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## HaMechaseh, second part: what is born below, study in fear, and the unlearned who reach (lines 4853-4899)

**The movement.** It takes the ladder of learning and lays it flat. The one who studies in fear of missing the truth, and the simple Jew who does what he is told, reach the same hidden place.

**The line to hold** (line 4887, OCR as it stands):

> דבאמת גם ע"ה ואנשים פשוטים מגיעים בעבודתם לבחי' העלם העצמי דא"ס
> *In truth the unlearned and simple people also reach, in their service, the essential hiddenness of Ein Sof.*

**Contemplating it.** The moon has no light of its own, yet once it receives the sun's it shines its own light (line 4854). A woman receives, and the birth, the new body, comes from her. The earth receives a seed and brings forth grain, "something from nothing always", though the Rashab adds that we have no true creation from nothing, only its likeness (line 4859). Speech is like that too. When you say a thought aloud, or write it, it widens and deepens in ways it never did in the head (line 4859). The Oral Torah is the same: it receives from the Written, and gives birth to the new by its own hidden power, which only toil wakes (line 4867). Then fear. One person studies for the taste of it, which the Rashab gives in Yiddish as the geshmak. Another studies afraid that maybe he will not hit the truth, with a tightening in the soul, "a strong not-good feeling" (line 4869). The second reaches above wisdom itself. That is the son who becomes a servant (lines 4871-4875). The hard question: if hidden Eden is for those who dig into Torah, what of the unlearned? And the Essence is drawn by the deed, not by thinking about it (line 4887). His answer: the simple servant who lays his whole self down and does the mitzvah only because of the fear of Heaven, reaches it. Study reaches it only when it becomes that too. Bring it home. Think of a simple, faithful person you know, who never studied much and never missed a day. The picture breaks with the moon and the earth: they give birth by nature. Here the birth comes only from toil and fear.

**From inside.** No one is shut out. A person who feels "I am not a scholar" can stand straight. And a scholar can taste the relief of not needing to enjoy it to reach Him.

**At the edge.** Yes: hidden Eden, "no eye has seen, God, except You" (line 4895).

**Into the day.** Honour one simple, faithful person today, in words or in a deed.

## VeHayah Or HaLevanah (Tazria-Metzora, 6 Iyar 5667), first part: hair, the skull, and the higher that bends lower (lines 4900-4974)

**The movement.** It reverses the mind's sense of height. What reaches lowest must come from highest, and the very strength to stoop is the sign of greatness.

**The line to hold** (line 4917, OCR as it stands):

> שכל הגבוה יותר יכול להשפיל א"ע למטה יותר
> *For the higher a thing is, the lower it can lower itself.*

**Contemplating it.** "Turn it and turn it", said Ben Bag Bag. The Talmud's picture is a man curling his hair, separating the strands so that they do not tangle. So one separates the laws, answering the contradictions until each law comes to its place in peace (line 4911). Hair takes its life from the brain but grows from the skull, which is above the brain (lines 4913, 4915). That is why the laws can come down so far, into physical doing: their root is higher than wisdom (line 4915). "The higher can lower itself lower" (line 4917). "He is enthroned on high", and so He looks equally on heaven and on earth (line 4926). His picture from life: a small scholar understands a law well but cannot explain it to a child. A great scholar can, because he can invent a new way to dress the idea for the child (lines 4949-4950). Only the wisest man could tell three thousand parables (lines 4955-4958). Bending down takes more power than standing tall. Then a limit. All ten sefirot are only "the secret of My Name". A person's name has nothing to do with his essence (line 4974). So even the source of wisdom is not Him. Bring it home. Remember explaining something to a child, and how much you had to know to make it simple. The picture breaks with the hair: hair grows by itself. The laws grow down only when someone toils over them.

**From inside.** Tenderness toward the low places, one's own and other people's. The high reaching down without looking down.

**At the edge.** It marks the limit: His Essence is "raised and exalted" beyond the ten sefirot, which are only His Name (line 4974).

**Into the day.** Explain one thing today to someone younger, in their words.

## VeHayah Or HaLevanah, second part: the will that never changes, and the animal that turns (lines 4975-5012)

**The movement.** It gives stubbornness a new meaning. The blind, reasonless will of the animal soul, once turned, reaches higher than the human will that follows reasons.

**The line to hold** (line 4990, OCR as it stands):

> פי' אלקיכם היינו בחי' האלקות שבנשמה
> *"Your God" means the Godliness within the soul.*

**Contemplating it.** The tablets were engraved right through; the mem and the samech stood by a miracle (line 5775). Letters engraved partway have a floor and can be filled with ink; letters cut right through cannot (line 5775). "The further from the Godly nothing, the more something" a thing becomes (line 5790). The souls are rooted in letters that are one with the light (line 5800). Before prayer, the Sages say, a person's soul is still "in his nostril", not spread through his body. Prayer joins the soul in the body with its root, in four steps, from thanks to the Amidah (line 5821). "Your sins separate between you and your God". "Your God" is the Godliness in the soul. Sin here means crookedness, following cravings; it makes a screen, and it makes the heart heavy like a liver, and the mind dull (line 5821). The remedy is a holy heaviness. "One stands to pray only with heaviness of head", which Rashi explains as humility: bitterness over being far, and mercy stirred for one's own soul. That mercy lifts: "I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to Me", to His very Essence (line 5824). So too charity before prayer (line 5824). Bring it home. Stand one minute before prayer and feel how far you are, without blame. The picture breaks here: an engraved letter cannot move. A soul can be lifted.

**From inside.** Mercy for oneself instead of self-contempt. The heaviness becomes the start of a lifting, and the head that was bowed is raised.

**At the edge.** Yes: the lifting is "to Me", to His very Essence (line 5824).

**Into the day.** Give a coin before prayer today, and feel one moment of mercy for your own soul.

## Vayigash Eilav Yehudah (9 Teves 5668), first part: why a soul comes down, and the lever gripped at its low end (lines 5832-5912)

**The movement.** It answers the question "what am I doing here?" at its root. The soul's deepest power shines only in the low place, and the low place is where the lifting is gripped.

**The line to hold** (line 5909, OCR as it stands):

> ונמצא, דדוקא בירידת הנשמה למטה נמשך בה כח שרשה ומקורה
> *So it is precisely in the soul's descent below that the power of its root and source is drawn into it.*

**Contemplating it.** The soul above is so high that it exists even before it was created: it is "Godliness itself, only the Godliness became a something" (line 5862). Above, it always stood in love and awe. Below, even the righteous feel love only at times, and "against your will you live" (lines 5871-5880). Why come down? Because only below does its root shine in it (lines 5881-5885). "He breathed into his nostrils": breathing from within, with force (line 5886). A jumper who must leap far has to bring out a hidden strength beyond his measure (line 5896). In great fright, a person lifts what several people cannot (line 5900). To put a part of God above into a body of dust is a leap with no proportion, and only the hidden power of the Essence can make it (line 5901). The human body was made first from dust, with no life, lower than the animals, whose bodies came out of the earth already alive. The human soul is higher than all (lines 5904-5907). "Behind and before You formed me" (line 5907). Why so far apart? To lift something with a lever, you must grip it at its lowest end (line 5909). The soul was put in the lowest body to lift all of creation, through bread eaten and clothes worn for His sake, and business done honestly (line 5909). Bring it home. Think of the lowest hour of your week. That is where the lever is held. The picture breaks here: a lever is lifeless and someone else pushes it. Here the one who lifts is inside.

**From inside.** Meaning in lowness. A strength that surprises a person in the very place he felt weakest.

**At the edge.** Yes: the root, in the power hidden in the Essence, shines only below (line 5909).

**Into the day.** At the lowest point of today, say: this is where the lever is held.

## Vayigash, second part: standing and walking (lines 5913-5928)

**The movement.** It moves a person from a love that stays inside its measure to a love that leaves every measure, and shows that this is what the descent was for.

**The line to hold** (line 5922, OCR as it stands):

> לצאת מגדר הכלי ולהכלל בעצמות אוא"ס
> *To leave the bounds of the vessel and be included in the Essence of the light of Ein Sof.*

**Contemplating it.** Angels "stand". Their love and awe are always the same, by the measure of what they grasp: "they do not add and do not take away from their service" (line 5914). Souls above stand too. They rise, but step by step, always in proportion; that is not called walking (line 5914). Below there are two loves. "With all your heart and all your soul" is love that comes from understanding. It is held in the heart, and when it grows it spills into the limbs, like a river over its banks after rain, so that a person runs to do a mitzvah (line 5920). But it stays measured, because reason never tells a person to leave the vessel (line 5920). "With all your might" (me'od) is different. It comes from sensing how far above the worlds He is. It is not ordered by reason; it comes by itself, a great love "to leave the bounds of the vessel and be included in the Essence" (line 5922). That is walking: a rise with no proportion. It comes only from the soul's root, and the root shines only below. So the descent is worth it, though the soul loses much of the love it had above (lines 5926-5928). Bring it home. Think of a love in your life that grew past its reasons. The picture breaks here: walking on earth is step by step. This walking is a leap.

**From inside.** Movement where there was sameness. The feeling that one's love of God is not stuck on a level.

**At the edge.** Yes: me'od, love without measure, to be included in the Essence (line 5922).

**Into the day.** At "with all your me'od" in the Shema today, pause on the word.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Hakavtzu VeShimu (Vayechi, 16 Teves 5668), first part: letters that do not grow, and the hiding only toward the worlds (lines 5929-5960)

**The movement.** It relocates God's hiddenness from Him to us. He shines openly in Himself; the worlds simply do not feel it.

**The line to hold** (line 5948, OCR as it stands):

> דבעצמו ה"ה מאיר בגילוי ממש, רק שבהעולמות אינו נרגש האור
> *In Himself He shines openly, only in the worlds the light is not felt.*

**Contemplating it.** How can Yaakov be called "El", God (line 5930)? The answer starts with walking. Angels come from letters of speech, and letters are "silent", like stone: they do not grow from small to large. One letter does not become another, so one angel does not do two errands (line 5937). A revealed thought is limited too: "this far and no further", unless a new flow comes from the mind's source. The raw power of mind has no shape at all, and from it the flow is without end (line 5940). The soul's walking comes from its root, which is like that. Then the plain sentence. The Essence is in every place. It is hidden, but "nothing hides the Essence"; it hides itself (line 5948). Only the unlimited can hide itself. A limited light can only be hidden by something else. So this hiding is only toward the worlds: in Himself He shines openly, and the worlds simply do not feel it (line 5948). Sometimes the soul's walking is woken by the hiding of the animal soul, "from the narrow place I called"; sometimes, in the righteous, by itself (lines 5950-5959). Bring it home. Think of a day He felt absent. The picture breaks here: when we hide, someone else cannot see us. When He hides, He is not hidden from Himself at all.

**From inside.** Fear eases. The absence is not His leaving. A person can wait in the dark without concluding that no one is there.

**At the edge.** Yes: nothing hides the Essence; the hiding is His own, and only toward the worlds (line 5948).

**Into the day.** When you cannot feel Him today, say: You are shining; I am the one who cannot see.

## Hakavtzu VeShimu, second part: pure and holy, the oil, and the hand at rest (lines 5961-6031)

**The movement.** It gives the mind a still picture of oneness that is not a sum: a hand at rest, holding all its work before it moves.

**The line to hold** (line 5987, OCR as it stands):

> אבל בחי' יחיד היינו יחוד בעצם, והיינו כח היד כפי שהוא בעצם חיות היד, קודם שמנענע לעשות מלאכה
> *But "only" (yachid) is oneness in essence: like the power of the hand as it is in the hand's own life, before it moves to do any work.*

**Contemplating it.** "There is none holy like God". The light is clothed in the vessels and one with them, and yet it does not mix with them. Light through green glass is not dyed green; a man thinking about a field is not the field; oil soaks into everything and stays apart (lines 5964-5971). "Kadosh" is the glow drawn down into the worlds. "Kodesh" is what the worlds cannot hold; if it were revealed, they would be nullified (lines 5973, 5980). "I, God, have not changed" (line 5980). Then oneness. "Echad", One, is what spread out and came back together, like the strength in a hand that moved to work and returned to rest (line 5987). "Yachid", Only, is oneness in essence: the power in the hand before it moved at all, when all its work is in it and none of it is counted. "You are One, but not in number" (lines 5987, 5995). The souls' true root is higher even than Torah, and the practical mitzvot now will reveal it (lines 6022, 6026). Yaakov's image is engraved under the Throne; through his descent and his work of gathering sparks ("gather and listen") he rose into the light itself, and so he is called El (lines 6028, 6031). Bring it home. Rest your hand flat on the table before you start work. All the day's work is in it, and none of it has begun. The picture breaks here: a hand's power still waits for the hand to move. His oneness waits for nothing.

**From inside.** Stillness that is not empty but full of power. The day's many tasks are gathered, before they begin, into one.

**At the edge.** Yes: yachid, "One but not in number" (line 5987), and the root of souls in the Essence's hiddenness (line 6022).

**Into the day.** Rest your hand on the table before you begin work today, and feel the work already in it.

## Kodesh Yisrael LaHashem (Shemos, 23 Teves 5668), first part: the good man, even when he is not giving (lines 6032-6123)

**The movement.** It turns a person toward the goodness under his moods, the kindness that is there before anything stirs it, and uses it as a window onto the light before creation.

**The line to hold** (line 6097, OCR as it stands):

> שנקרא איש הטוב גם בשעה שאינו עושה חסד בפועל
> *That he is called a good man even at a time when he is not actually doing a kindness.*

**Contemplating it.** The aim of the dwelling below is not only to bring back the light that filled everything before the contraction. That would be nothing new. It is to draw what is above it (line 6045). Every power has three levels. First the raw power rooted in the soul's essence. Everyone has every power there, even a cruel man and a fool (line 6083). Then the goodness in the heart before anything stirs it. Then the stirring to a particular kindness, and the planning of how it will suit the receiver, and the holding back needed to give it (lines 6098-6099). Why is a man called "a good man" even when he is not giving? Not for the raw goodness, which everyone has, and not for the deeds, which are only his acts, but for the goodness in his heart that is always ready to be stirred (line 6097). Even a stingy man gives freely at his only son's wedding, when the deep power is woken (line 6097). So above: the light as it is included in Him is "not light at all, but ability" (line 6102). Then the light spread out but still for itself; then the light that relates to worlds. The Rashab calls them Only, One and Primordial (lines 6110, 6119). Bring it home. Think of someone you know is good even when they are quiet. The picture breaks here: a man's goodness can be blocked by poor garments (line 6089). His cannot.

**From inside.** Trust in one's own goodness, under the tired days. And a gentler way of seeing other people, by what is ready in them, not only by what they did.

**At the edge.** Yes: the light included in the Essence is not light at all, only ability (line 6102).

**Into the day.** Do one kindness today that no one asked for, from the goodness in the heart, not from the request.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Kodesh Yisrael LaHashem, second part: the Shechinah, the Holy One, and the seed in the earth (lines 6124-6165)

**The movement.** It makes the plainest, most tasteless mitzvah into a seed, and the person into the earth that makes it grow.

**The line to hold** (line 6157, OCR as it stands):

> והנה, הזריעה היא בנש"י, דהזריעה צ"ל בארץ, ונש"י הן בחי' ארץ הראוי לזריעה
> *The sowing is in the souls of Israel: sowing must be in earth, and the souls of Israel are earth fit for sowing.*

**Contemplating it.** The line of light that comes into the worlds is called the Shechinah, because it dwells in them. The light above the line is called the Holy One, because it stands apart (lines 6125, 6131). True "kodesh" is His essential Name, included in Him (line 6149). "Light is sown for the righteous". God sowed the Torah and the mitzvot into physical things: tzitzit in wool, tefillin, laws about physical matters. It is like a garden seed, the kind no one eats, with no taste or smell, which grows into a tree with fruit that has taste and pleasure (line 6157). The souls are the earth for this seed, because of their bitul: they are not a thing on their own. "You will be a land of desire" (line 6157). Angels asked for the Torah and did not get it; it can grow only in souls in bodies. Through Torah the souls rise to the essence of the light. Through the practical mitzvot, "the King's commands", they rise to the true Essence itself (line 6157). And because Israel is holy in this way, "all who devour him will be held guilty" (line 6164). Bring it home. Think of one mitzvah that has no taste for you at all. The picture breaks here: earth does nothing to make a seed grow. Here the earth's bitul is its own work.

**From inside.** Patience with deeds that taste of nothing. A quiet hope that something is growing in the dark.

**At the edge.** Yes: the practical mitzvot raise the soul into the true Essence (line 6157).

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah today that has no taste to you, and think: seed.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## HaShamayim Kis'i (Vaera, Rosh Chodesh Shevat 5668), first part: the will with no reason, whole in its part (lines 6166-6249)

**The movement.** It makes the small deed whole. Because the will in a mitzvah has no reason, it cannot be cut into parts, and whatever piece of it a person holds, he holds all of it.

**The line to hold** (line 6189, OCR as it stands):

> שכשאתה תופס במקצת מן העצם אתה תופס בכולו
> *When you grasp part of the essence, you grasp all of it.*

**Contemplating it.** There is an outer will: wanting business for the profit, wanting a house because one needs somewhere to live (line 6181). It is for the sake of something else, and so it can change. It comes clothed in reasons, which cut it into many small wants. The will that made the worlds is like that: for the sake of the righteous who would keep the Torah, and so it can be withdrawn, "God regretted" (line 6186). The will in the mitzvot is inner: "all His Essence, as it were, is in this will" (line 6186). Reason is not its cause; by reason, tzitzit might be the other way around (line 6189). So it cannot be divided. Sometimes part of a mitzvah is nothing; sometimes part is all of it, as with partners in one mitzvah, or one busy with one mitzvah who is exempt from another. The Baal Shem Tov: "when you grasp part of the essence you grasp all of it" (line 6189). That is why it rests only in a physical thing, "which is not a vessel at all" (line 6189). Torah's delight is felt, in its reasons. "The mitzvot were not given for enjoyment"; their delight is not felt, and it is deeper (line 6234). Yet Torah too reaches that place when a law is clarified under the yoke (line 6241). And only Torah as it is below has this: it was given to souls in bodies, not to angels (line 6249). Bring it home. Hold the coin, the cup, the thread of today's mitzvah. The picture breaks here: grasping a corner of a cloth is not holding the cloth. The essence has no corners.

**From inside.** The small deed stops feeling small. A person may feel, for a moment, held whole in something he is holding.

**At the edge.** Yes: the essential will, and the unfelt, essential delight (lines 6186, 6232).

**Into the day.** Hold the object of one mitzvah today for a moment and say: all of it is here.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## HaShamayim Kis'i, second part: doing before hearing, the servant who reaches and the son who reveals (lines 6250-6355)

**The movement.** It honours the act done without wanting to. Forcing oneself to do is not a failure of love; it is the bitul that reaches deepest.

**The line to hold** (line 6282, OCR as it stands):

> ואע"פ שבעצם אין לו רצון וחשק בעסק התורה ומצות וכן בתפלה כו', ה"ה מבטל טבעו ורצונו, וכופה א"ע בכל פרט
> *And even though in himself he has no will or desire for Torah and mitzvot, and for prayer, he sets aside his nature and his will and compels himself in every detail.*

**Contemplating it.** Rabbi Tarfon says doing is greater; Rabbi Akiva says learning is greater. Both are right: the deed wakes the Essence, and learning is the vessel that reveals it. "Learning is greater because it brings to doing" (lines 6266, 6272). "We will do" before "we will hear". Hearing here means the heart's acceptance, when the thing is sweet to a person (line 6276). Doing means forcing oneself: though he has no wish for it, he sets aside his nature and does it anyway, in every detail (line 6282). That is the simple servant, "like an ox to the yoke", and it drew the crowns (lines 6282, 6289). The souls called sons reveal the light. The souls called servants reach the Essence (line 6318). So the returning penitent and the righteous one need each other: "Would that you were like a brother to me" (line 6318). Yehudah is truly above Yosef, but Yosef reveals what Yehudah reaches (line 6327). All of it rests on prayer: "Where is the house you would build Me? ... To this one I look: to the poor and broken of spirit" (line 6349). And then the Rashab corrects himself. He had suggested that learning under the yoke ranks above doing under the yoke; he will not say it, because "all Israel have a share in the World to Come" (line 6354). Bring it home. Remember a morning you did not want to pray, and prayed. The picture breaks here: a forced servant resents his master. This forcing is a giving-over.

**From inside.** The shame of "I did it without feeling" lifts. The forced act is seen as a gift, and it is enough.

**At the edge.** Yes: the servant's work reaches the essential hiddenness of Ein Sof (line 6318).

**Into the day.** Do one thing today before you feel like it, and say to yourself: na'aseh, we will do.

## The written conclusion: Malchiyos, Zichronos and Shofros (lines 6356-6405)

**The movement.** It gathers the whole series into Rosh Hashanah, and ends it not in understanding but in a cry with no words.

**The line to hold** (line 6400, OCR as it stands):

> צעקת הלב בבחי' קלא פנימאה דלא אשתמע", שזהו הזזת עצם הנפש ממש
> *The cry of the heart, an inner voice that is not heard, which is the moving of the very essence of the soul.*

**Contemplating it.** There are three levels: the light that fills worlds, the light that surrounds them, and the Essence above both (line 6357). Each Rosh Hashanah the life of the worlds, which is only His kingship, rises back into its source and must be drawn again (lines 6359, 6372). "I, God, have not changed": the kingship is only an outer glow, like a king whose name is called over the land (line 6372). Malchiyos asks for the inner kingship, seen below, as at the sea, where even a maidservant saw (lines 6372-6375). Zichronos: one remembers only what is far and easy to forget. The glow of kingship is that small before Him, and so it must be "remembered" (line 6381). Remembering is the union of the surrounding light in the filling light. Two opposites can be joined only from the Essence, "where neither takes up any place, and it includes both" (line 6390). That is the shofar. Its inner meaning is the heart's cry, "a voice not heard", which moves the essence of the soul (line 6400). A ram's horn: people "wise like man, following You like beasts", giving themselves over beyond reason (line 6400). And it is also the cry of the animal soul (line 6400). Shofros is said after the other two, because a union of two draws what is above both (lines 6402, 6405). It is the son who becomes a servant, and the great shofar of the future (line 6405). Bring it home. Remember a time a sound came out of you to Him with no words in it. The picture breaks here: our cries are heard. This one is "not heard", and it moves Him more.

**From inside.** The whole self moved at once, below words. After it, a quiet that feels like being known.

**At the edge.** Yes: the shofar reaches the essential hiddenness of Ein Sof (line 6400).

**Into the day.** Once today, let a sigh to Him go out with no words.

## The written conclusion: Yom Kippur, and the rest of the third Shabbat meal (lines 6406-6452)

**The movement.** It leads from felt delight to a delight with no object, and from light to what is not called light at all.

**The line to hold** (line 6435, OCR as it stands):

> ומתענג אח"כ בבחי' תענוג העצמי בעצם נפשו, לא רק מטוב ויופי הדבר, כ"א מעצם הענין בכללות
> *And afterward he delights with the essential delight in the essence of his soul, not only in the goodness and beauty of the thing, but in the matter itself as a whole.*

**Contemplating it.** Rosh Hashanah begins the drawing; Yom Kippur completes it, at Ne'ilah (lines 6406-6407). Yom Kippur is like the World to Come: no eating and drinking, a delight not felt (line 6409). Shabbat's delight is felt. Yom Kippur is "Shabbat of Shabbatot", a rest even from Shabbat, because it draws His essential Name, "which is not drawn at all, until it is not called light at all" (line 6417). Forgiveness comes from there, "with You" (line 6417). The Rashab's picture is a craftsman. While he works, all his powers are pressed into the work and there is no pleasure. When he finishes and rests, he looks at what he made and delights in it. Then the delight rises further, into a delight in himself that does not depend on the thing at all (lines 6428-6435). So above: "God saw all He had made, and it was very good" (line 6436). Friday night is the filling light; the Shabbat day meal, the surrounding light; the third meal, at the time of favour, is the Essence above both: "remember and keep were said in one utterance" (line 6439). The word oneg, delight, spells Eden, river and garden (line 6452). "He will revive us after two days; on the third He will raise us up, and we will live before Him": two days of Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur (lines 6419-6423). Bring it home. Think of Friday evening after a full week. The picture breaks here, and the series has said it: His making cost Him no toil (line 5524). The rest is His delight, not His recovery.

**From inside.** Rest that is not laziness, and a pleasure with no object. Wanting nothing, and lacking nothing.

**At the edge.** Yes: His essential Name, "not called light at all" (line 6417).

**Into the day.** At the third Shabbat meal, sit one minute wanting nothing.

## The written conclusion: one band, the verses of Torah, and the Rashab's last notes (lines 6453-6495)

**The movement.** It closes the series by turning the person outward, to the others. The inner light waits for Israel to be one.

**The line to hold** (line 6454, OCR as it stands):

> כולם אחודים וקשורים יחד באגודה אחת
> *All of them united and bound together in one band.*

**Contemplating it.** Why was the shofar not blown on a Rosh Hashanah that fell on Shabbat? Because Shabbat draws all three levels by itself (line 6452). But not equally: the shofar drew the inner light of the mitzvot only in the time of the Temple (line 6454). Then the priests at their service, the Levites on their platform and Israel at their posts were "all united and bound together in one band" (line 6454). In exile they are scattered, "the sons of Avraham in the exile of Edom", and the inner light cannot be drawn that way (line 6460). Yet the verses of Shofros are said even on Shabbat, because Torah brings the light down, and "all that will be drawn, even the highest of the future, was already hidden in the giving of the Torah" (lines 6461-6462). The blessing ends: "Sound the great shofar for our freedom, and raise a banner to gather our exiles" (line 6468). This is "the true intent rooted in the very Essence": a home for Him below (line 6468). The Rashab's own short notes for the conclusion say the same in a line: the essential hiddenness is reached by refining, "which is the main purpose of the descent" (line 6494). Bring it home. Picture your shul, your family table, the people you are cool to. The picture breaks here: a band is tied from outside. This one is tied from the root.

**From inside.** Belonging. Private depth opens into a longing for others, and the feeling that one's own prayer is not finished without them.

**At the edge.** Yes: the essential hiddenness, the home below (line 6468).

**Into the day.** Before your next prayer, make peace, even silently, with one person.

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations

1. **Bayom HaShmini Atzeres 5667, second part, line 4538:** אבל אפס בלתך (הוא) היינו שגם עמך ית' לא יש שום מציאות כלל. *Past "there is none besides You", the bitul that still feels Him, to "nothing apart from You": no one left even to feel it. Its sign is silence.* Stand silent a moment before "O Lord, open my lips".

2. **HaShamayim Kis'i (Vaera, Rosh Chodesh Shevat 5668), first part, line 6189:** שכשאתה תופס במקצת מן העצם אתה תופס בכולו. *The will in a mitzvah has no reason, so it has no parts: whatever piece of it you hold, you hold all of it, and it rests only in a physical thing.* Hold the object of one mitzvah and say: all of it is here.

3. **Hakavtzu VeShimu (Vayechi, 16 Teves 5668), first part, line 5948:** דבעצמו ה"ה מאיר בגילוי ממש, רק שבהעולמות אינו נרגש האור. *Nothing hides the Essence; it hides itself, and only toward the worlds. In Himself He shines openly.* When you cannot feel Him, say: You are shining; I am the one who cannot see.

4. **Panim BePanim, second part, line 5684:** והסתר רצוני אינו הסתר, שהרי ברצותו הוא מתפשט. *A hiding that nothing forces is His choice, and a chosen hiding is no hiding. Engraved, not written in ink.* When He seems hidden, say: a hiding You chose is not a hiding.

5. **Vayigash Eilav Yehudah (9 Teves 5668), first part, line 5909:** ונמצא, דדוקא בירידת הנשמה למטה נמשך בה כח שרשה ומקורה. *The soul's root shines only in its descent. The lever is gripped at its lowest end.* At the lowest point of the day, say: this is where the lever is held.

6. **Anochi, second part, line 5548:** הרי דמהתהוות יש מאין אנו יודעים שהעצמות מציאותו הוא מעצמותו. *From all His light we know nothing of Him; from the fact that anything exists we know only that He is from Himself.* Look at one ordinary thing and say: it is, and it did not make itself.

7. **Im Bechukosai (Behar-Bechukosai, 5667), first part, line 5251:** דענין עבודת עבד הוא שאינו מציאות לעצמו כלל, וכל מציאותו הוא מציאות האדון עצמו. *Above "all before Him is as nothing" is "there is none else", and its practice is the servant whose whole existence is his master's.* In one argument, look for the view that holds both.

8. **The written conclusion, line 6400:** צעקת הלב בבחי' קלא פנימאה דלא אשתמע", שזהו הזזת עצם הנפש ממש. *The shofar is the heart's cry, a voice not heard, which moves the soul's very essence and reaches His essential hiddenness.* Once today, let a sigh to Him go out with no words.

9. **Kol HaShoneh Halachos, second part, line 5175:** דבאמת מציאות העולם הוא מציאות, אלא שאינו מציאות אמיתי ממש. *The world is real existence, and not true existence. Both are held, and neither cancels the other.* Put your hand on the table: real, and from You.

10. **HaMechaseh, second part, line 4887:** דבאמת גם ע"ה ואנשים פשוטים מגיעים בעבודתם לבחי' העלם העצמי דא"ס. *The unlearned and simple reach the Essence by the yoke; learning reaches it only when it becomes that too.* Honour one simple, faithful person today.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

*Compared with the Chabad method as mapped in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`, and with the other books in this index. The differences are kept, not smoothed.*

**Contemplation is given a ceiling, on purpose.** The hisbonenus ontology (`Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`) runs from contemplation through deepening and arousal to drawing down, and treats "absolute unity" as one object among several. This half of Samach Vav contemplates at full stretch and then says, again and again, that what it reaches is only light. From the light "we do not know the Essence" (line 5534), not even that He is a luminary (line 5546); true simplicity "we do not know at all" (line 5716). What reaches the Essence is not a better contemplation but bitul of a particular kind: the yoke, the deed, the question worked through, the descent. The Gate of Unity asks a person to earn the whole through detail and then see it "as if by the eye's own sight". The Rashab asks him to earn it, and then to give it over.

**Pain ranked above pleasure.** The ontology treats bitterness and sadness as an obstacle to be managed: take the resolution if it sharpens, stop if it flattens. Here the ache is a way in. "Pain reaches much deeper into the soul than pleasure" (line 5044); study in fear of missing the truth, with a tightening in the soul, reaches above wisdom (line 4869); the mikveh works mainly for one pained over where he stands (line 4675); prayer begins with heaviness of head and bitterness over being far (line 5824); the scholars are "the weak ones" who break their bodies (line 5259). The Rashab's own frame keeps it from turning dark: the heaviness stirs mercy for one's own soul, and mercy lifts (line 5824), and "the day God binds up the break" heals the broken heart (line 5327). This reading keeps the manual's guard: not on a crushed day, and stop if it flattens you.

**Forcing is allowed, but only of one thing.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut puts arousal made on purpose at the lowest rung. The Rashab agrees that will and understanding cannot be forced, and adds what can: binding. "With binding he can force himself to bind himself to the matter, and then the will is revealed by itself" (line 5130). At the start of prayer the love is "made" and outward, "and it is necessarily so" (line 5024). "We will do" is forcing oneself though one has no wish at all, and it is ranked above "we will hear", the heart's acceptance (line 6282). This is close to the Tanya's consolation in ch. 16, and further from Hitpa'alut's suspicion of whatever is not natural. Both are kept.

**The unfelt over the felt, as in Hitpa'alut, but reached by the deed.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut's great rule is that the deeper the arousal of the essence, the less it is felt. The Rashab says the same of delight: Torah's delight is felt, the mitzvot's is not, and the unfelt one is deeper (line 6234); Yom Kippur's delight is above Shabbat's felt one (line 6417). The difference is the road there. Hitpa'alut finds it in the soul that sings without hearing itself. Samach Vav finds it in the mitzvah that was "not given for enjoyment" (line 6234), done under the yoke.

**Silence rather than song.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut puts the niggun at its centre as a sign of real arousal. Here the highest bitul is silence, "like the stone" (line 4542), a fence for wisdom; the king is David who "made himself still" (line 5257); and the last image of the series is the shofar's "voice that is not heard" (line 6400). There is no melody in this half of the book.

**The object is the person's own life, used as a mirror and then broken.** The Gate of Unity takes the chain of worlds as its object; Hitpa'alut takes the person's own states. This half of Samach Vav keeps the worlds in view but thinks them through the person: his hair and skull, his name, his changing mind, the good in his heart, a jumper, a strong man in a fright, a lever, a craftsman resting after work. "From my flesh I see God" (line 5116). Each picture is marked where it fails, often by the Rashab himself: the sun is known by its light and He is not (line 5546); the soul's layers are still not its essence (line 5131).

**The Tanya's summit becomes a middle rung.** The Tanya's gloss to ch. 35 names "He alone is, and there is none besides Him" as the level of wisdom; the Rashab agrees and puts "nothing apart from You" above it (line 4538). Shaar HaYichud's contemplation, "all before Him is as nothing", is "true" but not "true to its truth"; it still leaves something that counts as nothing, while "there is none else" leaves nothing at all (line 5187). A person who has lived with the Tanya's contemplation is not told it is wrong. He is told there is a floor beneath it, and that he reaches that floor by service, not by a sharper thought.

**Study or deed, kept open.** The Tanya (ch. 5) calls the knowing of Torah a union like no other, and the manual's closing sittings follow the deed. The Rashab holds both and ranks them only by what each does: the deed wakes the Essence, learning reveals it (line 6272); learning is greater "because it brings to doing". He suggests that learning under the yoke ranks above doing under the yoke, and then withdraws it: "all Israel have a share in the World to Come" (line 6354). This reading does not settle it either.

**It ends in the community.** The Gate of Unity is a solitary contemplation; Hitpa'alut asks for learning with a friend. The written conclusion of Samach Vav makes the unity of Israel a condition: the shofar drew the inner light of the mitzvot only when priests, Levites and Israelites were "bound together in one band" (line 6454), and it ends on "gather our exiles" (line 6468). The deepest point of the series is not reached alone.
