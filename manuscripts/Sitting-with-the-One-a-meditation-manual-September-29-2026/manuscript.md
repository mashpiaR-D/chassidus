## What it is for

Chassidus says G-d's oneness can't be understood, only glimpsed, and the glimpse comes in contemplation (hisbonenus): thinking one truth through, slowly, until it is as real to you as something you see. People don't come looking for G-d. They come looking for themselves, and in finding their true self they find Him. So the manual starts with you, climbs the rungs of the oneness, and comes back down into your day.

## The shape of every sitting

1. **Arrive** from what's already moving in you.
2. **Get quiet** by calmly watching your thoughts for a minute, without pushing.
3. **The one line**, said softly.
4. **Think it through** in breadth, length and depth, then down into your own table, hands, street and people.
5. **The picture**, and where it breaks.
6. **Come back** when the mind drifts, without arguing with the stray thought.
7. **From inside**: what it's like, described, never demanded. If nothing moves, the thinking still counts.
8. **Your own words** to Him.
9. **The lips**: a verse, or for a Jew the Shema with eyes covered, drawing out Echad on its ches and dalet.
10. **One act** for today, and one sentence to carry.

The test comes afterward, not during: *am I a little quieter, a little less full of myself, a little more my own master?*

## Guards

- Jewish contemplation (hisbonenus), not mindfulness: no breath-watching, body scan or relaxing. The short quiet is the Piaseczner's watching of thoughts, kept to a minute or two and always ending in the line. - Never drink as a practice. Outside real danger, no referral; in danger, stop and use the crisis line. - If a sitting leaves you frightened or feeling things are unreal, stop, go back to sitting 1 or 13, and do something plain with your hands. - Where the sources disagree (mind first or body first, force or never force, watch stray thoughts or ignore them), the manual gives both and says when each fits.
