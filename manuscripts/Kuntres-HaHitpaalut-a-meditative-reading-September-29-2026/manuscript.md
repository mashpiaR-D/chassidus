## 1. The book as one meditative journey

Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, the Tract on Ecstasy, walks a person from a soul asleep to a soul that sings without hearing itself sing. It is two letters of the Mitteler Rebbe to the chassidim, written in 5574 (1813/14). The first letter begins with sleep: worry over a living has emptied minds and hearts, and the Mitteler Rebbe calls that distraction "the exile of the Shechinah" and "sleep" (line 12). The last lines of the tract come back to the same word: "this is the main exile, likened to sleep" (line 899). Everything between is about waking, and about how to tell a real waking from a dream of one.

Most books in this index hand a person something to think about: God filling and surrounding the worlds, the chain of worlds, the letters of creation. This one hands him a mirror. Its object is the person's own inner movement in prayer, hitpa'alut, being moved. Its rule is one sentence: the arousal should be "only Godly arousal, and not the arousal of the life of flesh" (line 135). The rest unpacks the word "Godly". Under all effort lies a bond the person never made: cleaving, the soul's constant tie to the Essence, in every Jew (lines 144-154). Next to it stands its double, the heat that looks the same and is "serving oneself" (line 167). The key is a song. A niggun comes by itself. It is felt, and the singer does not feel himself singing (lines 207-219).

Then the book climbs two ladders. The first is the natural soul's answer to contemplation: hearing from afar, good thought joined to deed, the heart moved, intent that holds the whole, and the simple will above reason (lines 225-311). Then the floor drops out. All of that was "serving God with the body", because the soul in itself is "a Godly power, literally" (lines 312-313). The second ladder is the Godly soul's own, and it starts at the very bottom: good done by nature, a thief's faith, a child walking after his father out of Egypt, the martyr who will not be "separated entirely from the One" (line 531). It climbs to the heart of flesh that sings, to Shabbat's "voice that is not heard" (line 577), and to the yechidah's "one will in which all wills are included" (line 606). At the top stands the book's great rule: "the deeper the arousal of the essence, the less he will feel it" (line 556). The last third lays the map on real people: the cold, the one who can only cry out, the one who says "aha" and stops, the one whose heart runs ahead of his mind, the young man whose deep "kvell" leaves him full of himself (line 808), and the simple man whose plain joy "reaches the essential delight in the yechidah" (line 882).

So the road goes from "I want to feel something" to "I do not feel myself at all, and something in me sings to the living God". The contemplation it asks for has two hands. One hand is ordinary hisbonenus, contemplation that thinks a truth through: how God fills and surrounds all worlds, "all before Him is as nothing" (lines 59, 291, 640). The other hand is honest self-examination, done with "the open eye of the mind" on oneself, always (line 628). Its images are song, the cry, the broken heart, a bowl with no spoon, a flame in the air, meat cooked soft. It asks for tears over one's distance, and defends the tear of the one who cannot think anything through (lines 660-666). It asks for learning aloud with a friend, a hundred times (line 97). It asks that every arousal be brought into a deed, because "light has hold only in a vessel" (line 769). And it gives its tests afterward, not during: what the moment left behind, how you take an insult, whether your hands reach toward God through the day (lines 808, 829, 833). It is a demanding book and it says so. It is also a merciful one: the dullest keeps "the essence of his Jewish nature" (line 647), and in the deepest place "the unlearned and the scholars are exactly equal" (line 623).

## Section 1: First letter: the soul asleep (lines 1-21)

**The movement.** It names the dullness the reader already feels, and names it without blame: not death, but sleep. It turns a person who is close to giving up into a person who can be woken.

> כך טרדת הפרנסה שנקרא גלות השכינה נקרא שינה
> *So the distraction of making a living, which is called the exile of the Shechinah (God's presence), is called sleep.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. The letter describes a whole community. Worry over a living has filled their minds and hearts (line 3). The hard labour has become so habitual that they no longer even sigh over it (line 5). Then step by step. A sleeper is not dead. Wake him and he is alive "exactly as before" (line 10). The life was only hidden in his mind and heart, and waking brings it into view. "There is nothing new" (line 11). Then to the root. The Mitteler Rebbe calls this very distraction the exile of the Shechinah (line 12). What sleeps in you is God's presence in you. He quotes the verse "I sleep, but my heart is awake" (line 12). Bring it home. Think of last week. How many hours did the bills, the phone and the next deal take? Count them without scolding yourself. That is what sleep looks like now. The picture holds and then breaks. A sleeper wakes when someone shakes him. Here the letter asks a person to set aside times, now and then, to wake himself (line 6). And it forbids despair, because the soul's nature "to be moved always" has not changed in its essence (line 19).

**From inside.** Relief first: nothing is lost, only covered. Then a quiet tenderness toward yourself, the way you look at someone you love who has fallen asleep in a chair. A readiness to be woken.

**At the edge.** It points there without going. "Israel is not widowed from the living God" (line 20): under the sleep, the bond was never cut. The book comes back to this bond in section 7.

**Into the day.** Before you open the first worry of the day (the email, the accounts), say once: "I was asleep. I am waking."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Section 2: First letter: two errors about being moved (lines 22-40)

**The movement.** It pulls apart a false choice between thinking and feeling. Chassidus is meant to be received with being moved: first a deep hearing in the mind, then the heart.

> והן בהתפעלות הלב אחר כך כמו שכתוב והשבות אל לבבך. וזהו אלקי בקרבי ממש
> *And in the arousal of the heart afterward, as it says, "return it to your heart". And this is "God within me", literally.*

**Contemplating it.** The letter names two kinds of confusion. One person hears and understands, and then asks what he is supposed to do with it in prayer. There is "no connection" between what he hears and what he does (lines 25-26). The other kind forbids any arousal in the heart, "like a piece of forbidden meat" (line 28), and then forbids it in the mind too, until he thinks stray thoughts or dozes (line 30). The man who once cried out in prayer is laughed at, and learns to sit and sleep (lines 39-40). The foundation, he says, is that these words be fixed in the soul with arousal: in the mind, which is called good hearing (in Yiddish, "he hears it very deeply"), and afterward in the heart (line 33). That is "God within me", literally. His picture is from ordinary life. A person thinking about something that matters to him is so drawn in that he feels nothing else (line 37). This is called cleaving of thought. Bring it home. Remember one thing you once really heard, a sentence you could not put down. You did not decide to be absorbed. The picture breaks where it must: a worldly thought pulls you out toward the thing. Here the thing is God, and He is found within.

**From inside.** Permission to be moved. A mind that leans in instead of filing the idea away. The sense that learning can land somewhere in you.

**At the edge.** Not yet. It names the goal, "God within me, literally". The book spends the rest of its pages on what that means and what imitates it.

**Into the day.** After you learn one line of Chassidus today, ask: did I hear it, or only understand it?

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 3: First letter: the cry that is His, and the cry that is noise (lines 41-75)

**The movement.** It trains the ear to tell a cry that rises from the depth of the heart from a shout. It says the difference is not the volume but the source.

> אם הוא מחמת שמתבונן בה׳ אחד איך שנפשו רחוקה בתכלית מיד יתמרמר לבו בקרבו
> *If it is because he contemplates the One God, how his soul is utterly far, at once his heart grows bitter within him.*

**Contemplating it.** There are two cries that sound the same. One is a physical shout "without any intent at all, only to raise the voice" (line 53). It is not to God. It is to be heard, or to drive away a stray thought (line 55). The other comes from contemplating the One and seeing how far one's soul is. At once the heart grows bitter, and he cries out suddenly, without meaning to cry (line 57). This is teshuvah (return) "from the depth of the heart": "from the depths I called You" (line 57). It is the pouring out of the soul (line 58). The same happens from joy. Contemplate "even a little" of how He fills every world and surrounds every world, and the heart is moved to longing and gladness (lines 59-60). This, he says, is the light of God revealed in the heart (line 61). The more strongly it breaks out in a cry, the more truly the light was received (line 64). He ends with a verse: "I was a beast with You" (line 66). Below knowledge, and still with You in wondrous cleaving. Bring it home. Think of your last sigh in prayer. Was it pointed at Him, or at being heard? The picture breaks here: from outside the two cries cannot be told apart. Only the one who cries knows where it came from, and even he has to look.

**From inside.** A cry that surprises the one who cries it, and afterward quiet, not pride. The heart feels both how far it is and how near it wants to be.

**At the edge.** It points below the mind: the "beast" that is with God below knowledge (line 66). The book returns to this at its end (line 871), where that low point turns out to be rooted above knowledge.

**Into the day.** When a sigh comes in prayer today, let it come. Do not make it louder, and do not stop it.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 4: First letter: blessing, and the service of the heart (lines 76-86)

**The movement.** A short, practical turn. It makes success in business into a test of the heart.

> אך לא ירום לבבו לומר כחי ועוצם ידי כו׳ ולא יגבה לבבם בכל הטוב לשכוח עבודה שבלב
> *But let him not lift his heart to say "my strength and the might of my hand", and let their heart not grow proud in all the good, to forget the service of the heart.*

**Contemplating it.** Since the Alter Rebbe's passing, he writes, the chassidim have slackened, and many are busy with trade (lines 78-79). God, in His mercy, has blessed those in business (line 80). Then the warning. Do not say "my strength". Do not let the good make you forget prayer, which he calls the service of the heart (line 81). "Give me neither poverty nor riches" (line 82). He speaks, he says, "for my own sake". He knows his place and will not wear a garment that is not his (line 85). The one who warns against pride first watches his own. Bring it home. Think of a good month. Did prayer grow shorter in it? The passage has no picture to break. It is a plain warning.

**From inside.** Gratitude without a claim on it. The money arrives and the heart stays where it was.

**At the edge.** It does not go there. It is a practical warning.

**Into the day.** When money comes in this week, say "from Him" before you decide what to do with it.

## Section 5: First letter: learning together until it holds (lines 87-115)

**The movement.** It takes contemplation out of the lonely head and puts it among friends. What is said aloud and repeated together is what stays.

> לחזור הדבר עד מאה פעמים עם חבירו דוקא ואיש את רעהו יחזיק לבו
> *To repeat the matter up to a hundred times with his friend, specifically, and each one strengthen his fellow's heart.*

**Contemplating it.** Many chassidim live far away and cannot travel to Liadi. Only one in ten goes. The nine who stay home are often the truer ones, he says, truer than those who travel and boast of the discourses they heard (line 95). Travelling in little groups of two or three brings "division of hearts", because one lifts himself over another (line 96). The foundation of fixing Chassidus in the soul is much study and speaking it aloud, repeating it a hundred times with a friend (line 97). Then there is "complete oneness" among them (line 98), "as one man" (line 99). He offers to come and sit a month with any region that gathers (line 106). Bring it home. Who could you say one line of Chassidus to this week, and hear them say it back? The picture has a limit that the index keeps. The "oneness" here means oneness among friends. Hearing it as God's oneness is our reading, not the text's.

**From inside.** Warmth. The words become shared and a little less "mine". A friend's voice saying it back makes it real in a way your own voice does not.

**At the edge.** Not reached. But the self that lifts itself over another (line 96) is the same self the whole tract will examine.

**Into the day.** Call one friend and read one sentence aloud together, twice.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 6: Second letter: a full bowl and no spoon (lines 116-132)

**The movement.** It names the gap between knowing and receiving, and promises a way across it. The problem is not the food. It is the vessel.

> והרי זה דומה לקערה מלאה כל מעדני מאכלים טובים המשיבים את הנפש ואין לו כף במה ליקח אל פיו
> *This is like a bowl full of every good dish that restores the soul, and he has no spoon with which to bring it to his mouth.*

**Contemplating it.** The second letter begins with confusion "from one end to the other": people call light darkness and darkness light (line 117). Some know it all by heart and are hungry and thirsty, "but without light or life in the soul". They finish learning and ask what use it was (line 125). Others think hearing from afar is all of Chassidus. For them any arousal is a grave sin, because it is "feeling oneself" (lines 126-127). He asks them to look at these two opposites side by side (line 128). His aim, he says, is that Godliness be revealed in their souls (line 132), and that a lasting building be set in the soul, rooted and knotted so it never shakes (line 122). The picture is simple. A bowl of good food, and no spoon. The soul faints from longing only because it has no vessel to receive (line 123). Bring it to your own table. The food is there and your hand is there. The picture breaks here: a spoon is outside you, while the vessel this book will give is inside you. It will turn out to be the deed, and the broken heart.

**From inside.** The hunger gets a name. Then hope: nothing is missing but the way of receiving.

**At the edge.** Not here. It sets the task.

**Into the day.** At a meal today, hold the spoon a moment before you eat and say, "I need a way to receive."

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 7: The rule, and cleaving that is already there (lines 133-159)

**The movement.** It moves the reader from effort to what is already given. Beneath every arousal a person must work for lies a bond he never made and cannot lose.

> שהדביקות הוא חיות והתקשרות הנפש בחיי החיים עצמות אור אין סוף ברוך הוא
> *Cleaving (devekut) is the life and bond of the soul with the Life of life, the Essence of the Infinite Light, blessed be He.*

**Contemplating it.** First the rule of the whole book. The Alter Rebbe's whole aim was that the chassidim's arousal be "only Godly arousal, and not the arousal of the life of flesh" (line 135). Then a question. The Torah says to love God "for He is your life". If He is already your life, why is love needed (lines 138-142)? The answer is that love and cleaving differ. Love is an arousal that needs toil. Cleaving is the soul's life and bond with the Essence itself (line 144). "You who cleave" means: from the root of your soul you already cleave, of yourselves, in an essential and constant bond, not a feeling that comes and goes (line 146). This is the soul's deepest level, yechidah, the "one knot" (lines 147, 153). It is in every Jew, even one whose soul is only the lowest level (line 148). So the Shema comes first, to give the soul over to the One. Only after it comes "you shall love" (lines 149-150). The proof is ordinary. Even a simple person, hearing how God fills and surrounds all worlds, is deeply stirred (line 156). That is the Godliness in his soul drawn after its source, "as the spark is drawn after the flame" (line 158). "And this is called Godly arousal proper" (line 159). Bring it home. Think of someone plain in your family whose face changes at a Jewish song. The picture breaks where the text wants it to: a spark and a flame are two. This spark was never cut off.

**From inside.** Rest. Not having to produce it. A pull, like a spark leaning toward a fire, that comes from below the part of you that tries.

**At the edge.** Yes. It names the Essence and the yechidah, which is "above the power of wisdom in his soul" (line 157). The mind does not make this bond. It can only let it show.

**Into the day.** Say the first verse of the Shema as a giving-over, before you ask yourself to feel love.

## Section 8: Strange fire: serving oneself (lines 160-192)

**The movement.** It turns the lamp inward and removes a false picture: that heat means holiness. It is sobering, and it clears the ground.

> וזהו הנקרא עובד את עצמו
> *And this is what is called serving oneself.*

**Contemplating it.** There is a cleaving that looks exactly like the true one (line 164). It is the outward cry of the heart of flesh, from empty imagining, "without inner light and life, and not to God at all" (line 161). There is no hearing in the mind behind it (line 162). People call it cleaving and fervour, but it is "counted as dead" (line 165). Its name gives it away: "serving oneself" (line 167). It is not even "serving God with the body", which at least loves God with the natural soul (lines 168-173). It is strange fire, from the heat of the blood, "to warm himself with feeling that he is moved" (lines 175-176). It ends in the boast "I, and there is none besides me" (line 181), in pride and in falls (lines 182-183). The more a person is bound to the real thing, the more he hates this (line 184). And yet, in great hiding, a little Godly light is mixed in, a trace left over from contemplation (line 192). Even the counterfeit is not wholly empty. Bring it home. Remember a time you felt very spiritual and then snapped at someone an hour later. The picture is fire. Strange fire looks like fire. You cannot tell them apart by the heat. Only by where each comes from and what each leaves behind.

**From inside.** A clean honesty. Not shame, but sobriety. The heat cools, and what is real is still there underneath.

**At the edge.** The text names the boast "none besides me" (line 181). Reading it as the self standing where only He should stand is our reading. The index marks it so.

**Into the day.** After a moving moment today, ask once: was that for Him or for me? Let the answer be whatever it is.

## Section 9: The song that sings itself (lines 193-224)

**The movement.** It holds two opposites until they meet: an arousal that is felt, and a self that is not felt in it. The mind is brought to a real puzzle and given a song for an answer.

> והוא להיות שההתפעלות הוא אמיתית רק מצד עצם הדבר הטוב שנפשו קשור בו
> *For the arousal is true only from the very good thing to which his soul is bound.*

**Contemplating it.** The key comes from a teaching of the Alter Rebbe. In holiness the vessel, the light's own "somethingness", is completely nullified. In the other side (nogah, the mixed glow) the vessel is felt and spreads past its light (line 197). So the righteous "say little and do much" (line 199), and the outward fervour that exaggerates comes from a swollen vessel (lines 201-202). Then the surprise: "the more it is reduced and not felt, the more light" (line 204). But how can something felt be completely unfelt? "This is two opposites in one subject" (line 207). The answer is melody. Moses sang in prayer "with every kind of melody", because every soul rises to its source through song (lines 209-210). A niggun comes suddenly, without choosing. It is felt, and he does not feel himself in it (lines 213-214). His picture is joy at good news. The hands clap by themselves, and he does not know he is clapping (lines 215-217). It is true because it comes from the good thing itself (line 218). The opposite is the arousal made on purpose: he waits for it, is sad if it does not come, glad when it does (lines 221-222). Bring it home. Remember a time you clapped at good news, or found yourself humming. The picture breaks here: good news is about something outside you. Here the good thing is His nearness, and what claps is the heart.

**From inside.** Lightness. Attention rests on the good thing, not on the clapping. The song is there, and nobody is standing beside it watching.

**At the edge.** Yes. The bitul (self-nullification) of the vessel. The less of "me" in it, the more light. This is the seed of the book's great rule at line 556.

**Into the day.** The next time a tune rises in you today, let it. Do not watch yourself singing.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Section 10: The natural soul, below the first rung: wanting to feel something (lines 225-232)

**The movement.** It is gentle with the lowest place. It finds a hidden love inside even the wish to feel something for one's own sake.

> רק עם כל זה בהעלם עיקר רצונו שיהיה לו התפעלות לה׳ דוקא ובלתי לה׳ לא היה רוצה כלל בכל זה
> *Yet with all this, hidden, his main wish is that his arousal be for God, specifically; if it were not for God, he would not want any of it.*

**Contemplating it.** Here begins the first ladder, the natural soul's answer to contemplation. Below its first rung stands a person whose whole wish is to be moved and to get some vitality from it (line 226). He is near the strange fire (line 227). He does not aim at Godliness. He wants "some vitality, to be a something" (line 228). Even so, some good is hidden in it: the hidden love, dressed in a strange garment (line 229). Deep down he wants the arousal to be for God, and would not want it otherwise (line 230). It never occurs to him in so many words (line 231). "The nearness of God is good for me" (line 232). Bring it home. Think of the times you came to prayer hoping for a spiritual lift. The text does not mock that hunger. It looks underneath it. The picture is a garment. A garment covers the body, and the body is still there.

**From inside.** Compassion for your own hunger, and a way of seeing through it. The wanting is not the enemy. It is a love wearing the wrong clothes.

**At the edge.** It points to the hidden love, the soul's inborn pull toward God, which it does not yet name as the soul's root.

**Into the day.** Say it plainly once: "I want to feel something. I want it to be You."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Section 11: The natural soul, second rung: hearing from afar (lines 233-241)

**The movement.** It honours the dry beginning. The wanting of nearness, even with nothing felt, is already the true start.

> ואין זה לעצמו רק לאלהות בלבד כי אך קרבת אלהים יחפוץ שיהיה קבוע בנפשו דוקא
> *And this is not for himself but for Godliness alone, for he wants only the nearness of God, that it be fixed in his soul.*

**Contemplating it.** He hears and thinks about Godliness and understands it well. In Yiddish, "it lies very well in the mind" (line 234). But it is far from him, and he asks what good it will do his soul (line 235). It is precious to him. He acknowledges it and exalts it in his mind and even his heart. He wants it felt and open, "not hidden and from afar". Again in Yiddish: "it is very far from him, it does not belong to him" (line 236). He toils and nothing moves in heart or mind (line 239). Yet it is "not for himself, but for Godliness alone" (line 240). This, says the text, is the real beginning of those who seek God in truth (line 241). Bring it home. Think of a prayer where every word was true and every word was far away. The picture is distance. It breaks because the one who is far and wants to come near has already turned around.

**From inside.** Dignity in dryness. The wanting is the thread you hold while nothing moves.

**At the edge.** Not reached. It is the start of the road.

**Into the day.** In a dry moment today, say: "It is still far, and I want it near."

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 12: The natural soul, third rung: a treasure that is someone else's (lines 242-267)

**The movement.** It moves an idea from "true" to "mine". It shows how a thought can move a person from his place.

> וכך יובן בהתבוננות אלהות במחשבה כזאת שנדבק היטב בה כענין הנוגע לעצמו
> *So it is understood in contemplating Godliness with such a thought, that cleaves to it well, as a matter that touches oneself.*

**Contemplating it.** There are two kinds of thought. One is about a great treasure in someone else's hands, or the honour of a king. It is precious, and it does not touch him at all. This is called cold thought (lines 246-247). The other is about a good that touches him: a profit he makes, an honour he gains (line 248). Such a thought moves him from his place (line 249). Good news about a deal takes over his whole mind. This is called hearing in thought (line 250). Contemplate Godliness like that, as a matter that touches you (line 252). This is "good thought joined to deed". It gives birth to love and awe that reach action (line 253). Cold thought gives birth only to shame over one's distance and the lowness of this world, with a sigh (lines 255-256), and to agreement to do good (line 257). Even that, he says, can bring a person to the lower Garden of Eden, because some Godliness is in it (line 259). Bring it home. Compare how you think about your pay and how you think about God's nearness. The picture breaks here: a profit is yours as something apart from you. His nearness touches you because you are His.

**From inside.** A shift from the head to the chest. The idea stops being a fact about the world and becomes news.

**At the edge.** Not reached. It stays in deed, and the text says so.

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah today and think of it as your own gain, the way you would think of a good deal.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 13: The natural soul, fourth rung: the heart moved, and the gap (lines 268-288)

**The movement.** It presses the mind toward the heart and admits honestly how hard the passage is. It names why.

> והוא עיקר המצוה בעבודה שבלב לייגע את עצמו במחשבה כל כך עד שיתפעל הלב דוקא
> *And this is the main mitzvah in the service of the heart: to toil in thought so much that the heart, specifically, is moved.*

**Contemplating it.** From contemplation that has moved the mind, the heart is moved, with more light and more inwardness (line 268). "With all your heart" (line 270). This is the main mitzvah of the service of the heart: toil in thought until the heart moves (line 271). It is real labour (line 272). In worldly matters the heart moves at once. A good thought comes and the heart catches fire (line 273). In serving God it is not easy (line 274). The reason: the Godliness in this contemplation is not drawn openly into the heart. It stays surrounding the mind, makif (line 275). It is around you and not yet in you. When it does come, it becomes zeal in deed, like a merchant who does everything that helps his business eagerly and guards against anything that harms it (line 283). Love is the root of the 248 commands to do, awe the root of the 365 commands not to do (lines 284-285). Bring it to your desk. Notice how quickly you get excited there. The picture is the merchant. It breaks because the merchant gains for himself, and here the gain is to be near.

**From inside.** Warmth that goes into the hands. Doing a mitzvah with the eagerness usually kept for other things.

**At the edge.** Yes, by name: makif, what surrounds the mind and has not entered (line 275). And the text marks the limit: even this love is still called "natural" (line 277), not yet the soul's own (line 278).

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah today quickly and gladly, the way you would close a good deal.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Section 14: The natural soul, fifth rung: intent that holds the whole (lines 289-302)

**The movement.** It surprises. The heart's arousal turns out to be a narrowing. Beyond it is a mind held whole inside the idea, where love is no longer a result but the idea itself.

> ודחילו ורחימו שכליים אינו נבדל מעצם ההתבוננות כלל, אלא ממילא ומאליו באה בהכרח בלי בחירה ורצון כלל
> *Intellectual awe and love are not separate from the very substance of the contemplation at all; they come of themselves, necessarily, without choice or will at all.*

**Contemplating it.** In the moment the heart is moved, the whole long idea shrinks to the summary that touched it (line 290). The summary is "all before Him is as nothing" (line 291). Next to intent that holds the idea in its full length and breadth, that is smallness (lines 292-293). His picture: a man set on some great venture, his whole soul drawn after it, so taken up that he cannot yet get excited about it (line 294). Natural love and awe come as a conclusion: "so therefore" (line 297). Intellectual love and awe are not separate from the contemplation at all (line 298). They come of themselves, like joy that claps the hands (line 299). The sign is that they last. They do not fall and have to be woken again (line 300). Above even this is the simple will, from which the intellect itself is born (line 302). Bring it home. When have you thought about someone you love so fully that you were not emotional at all, only there? The picture breaks here: the venture is outside the man. Here the "thing" is Him.

**From inside.** A stillness that is full. No highs and lows. The idea and the love are one thing, and nothing needs to be squeezed out of it.

**At the edge.** Yes. The simple will above contemplation (line 302), where there is no reason, only wanting.

**Into the day.** Take "all before Him is as nothing" and hold it for a minute without waiting for any feeling to come.

## Section 15: Five rungs, five names of the soul (lines 303-311)

**The movement.** It gathers the ladder and pins each rung to one of the soul's five names. It ends above reason.

> שלמעלה מן הטעם ושכל לגמרי כי אין טעם לרצון כלל
> *Which is entirely above reason and intellect, for there is no reason for will at all.*

**Contemplating it.** All of this is born of preparation, that is, of contemplation (line 303). The wish only to be moved is not counted as a rung at all. It is near the strange fire (line 304). The five rungs match the soul's five levels (line 305). Nefesh is the agreement to act (line 306). Ruach is good thought, the good hearing (line 307). Neshamah is when understanding lights up in the heart (line 308). Chayah is intent in wisdom and understanding themselves, before they spread into the heart (line 310). Yechidah is the simple will of the self, above reason and intellect, "for there is no reason for will at all" (line 311). Bring it home. Ask yourself why you want the thing you most want. Keep asking. At some point there is no reason. That is what "will" means here.

**From inside.** The clarity of a map. You can see where you stand today without having to be anywhere else.

**At the edge.** Yes. A will without a reason. The mind can reach it only by running out of reasons.

**Into the day.** Notice which rung this morning's prayer stood on. Do not judge it. Just note it.

## Section 16: The turn: all of that was the body (lines 312-316)

**The movement.** The floor drops out. Everything so far, even the highest rung, belonged to the natural soul. Under it lies something of another kind.

> כי בחינת הנשמה מצד עצמה הרי היא כח אלהי ממש
> *For the soul, in itself, is a Godly power, literally.*

**Contemplating it.** All five rungs, even intent and simple will, belong to the intellectual soul, which is called the natural, living soul. All of it is called "serving God with the body, not serving God with the soul" (line 312). For the soul in itself is a Godly power, literally (line 313). All five of its own levels are Godly arousal, and not bodily arousal at all. It is only clothed in the physical mind and heart (line 314). The lowest of these is when the heart feels the soul's Godly arousal (line 315). The main thing now is the cleaving spoken of before, which is called essential life (line 316). Bring it home. The prayer you thought was your soul at its best may have been your good natural self at its best. That is not a loss. The picture breaks entirely here, and the text means it to: nothing in the first ladder was a picture of what is coming.

**From inside.** Humility and wonder together. Something truer lies under the best of me, and it is not mine to build.

**At the edge.** Yes. "A Godly power, literally." What the soul is in itself is beyond the whole ladder of effort.

**Into the day.** When a good feeling comes today, say: "Even this is a garment. Under it is a part of Him."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Section 17: Why one hears and another does not (lines 317-326)

**The movement.** It takes away both the credit and the blame that belong to talent. The ear for Godliness is not cleverness and not piety.

> רק שזה בא מכח הנשמה עצמה שיש בה כח אלהית והתפעלותה התפעלות אלהית יקרא
> *Only that this comes from the power of the soul itself, which has Godly power in it, and its arousal is called Godly arousal.*

**Contemplating it.** A wonder: one mind is deeply moved hearing words of the living God (line 318). It is not because it is a deeper mind. There are deeper and broader minds (line 319). One hears and another does not hear at all (line 320). It is not because his heart is better at serving God either. A person can be God-fearing and whole in his service and have no sense of this hearing at all (line 322). So it must come from the soul itself, which has Godly power (line 323). When it hears Godliness, the Godly soul is moved "from the source it was hewn from" (line 324). There are levels: from nefesh one is moved less, from ruach more (line 326). Bring it home. Think of someone in your community who lights up at a teaching, and someone devout who never does. The picture is hearing. It breaks because this ear is not in the head.

**From inside.** Freedom from comparing. The one who hears more has not earned it, and the one who hears less has not failed.

**At the edge.** It points to the soul's source and does not go further here.

**Into the day.** When someone near you is moved and you are not, bless it silently. It is his soul, not his brain.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Section 18: A soul that needs no fixing (lines 327-338)

**The movement.** It gives comfort at the root. The soul itself is whole. What needs work is the garment it wears.

> דהנשמה מצד עצמה אין צריך תקון כי היא מעולם התקון
> *The soul in itself needs no repair, for it is from the world of repair.*

**Contemplating it.** The Godly soul's arousal is Godly even when it comes through understanding, because its understanding is Godly understanding (line 328). The natural soul, too, is moved only by the Godliness in what it grasps. But it grasps through a garment of nogah that hides the Godly light (line 329). Three partners make a person, the sages say. Father and mother give the natural soul, its traits and mind. God gives the Godly soul (line 331). The soul in itself needs no repair. It is from Atzilut, the world of repair (line 332). Even a simple person who has only the lowest level of soul has a glimmer of Atzilut in it (line 333). It came down only to refine the garments of nogah (line 334). Bring it home. Think of the part of you that has always been all right, under every failure. The picture is a garment. It holds well: you wash a garment, not the one wearing it.

**From inside.** A steadiness under your mistakes. Something in you was never stained.

**At the edge.** It points to Atzilut in the soul and stops there.

**Into the day.** When you fail today, say: "The soul was not damaged. I will wash the garment."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Section 19: Eden, and light through a screen (lines 339-369)

**The movement.** It gives a picture of Godly feeling without hiding, and then shows that the soul carries a share of it here.

> ומפני שנמצאו מאין האלהי כניצוץ מן השלהבת והוא חלק אלוה ממש
> *And because they come from the Godly nothing, like a spark from the flame, and it is a part of God, literally, therefore it enjoys the radiance.*

**Contemplating it.** In the Garden of Eden, souls enjoy the radiance of the Shechinah. Their grasp and their delight are Godly light "without any hiding or contraction" (line 342). That grasp is empty of all "something". It is in the Godly nothing, the ayin (line 344). Why can they enjoy it? Because they come from the Godly nothing like a spark from a flame, a part of God, literally (line 353). He gives a ladder of pleasures: money and food, then honour, which is finer, then the Godly delight of a soul in a body, then the soul's delight in Eden. Each is physical beside the next (lines 348-350). Nogah is like light shining through a screen. It gives light, but it is acquired light, not the light itself (lines 362-363). The Godly soul at its root receives the light itself without a garment (line 365). Even in Eden it wears a garment only "so that it not be nullified out of existence" (line 366). In the body, too, the garment does not hide from it (line 367). Bring it home. Look at a lamp through its shade, then at the bulb. The picture breaks where the text says it does: a shade dims the same light, but the natural soul's arousal has "no comparison at all" to the soul's (line 368).

**From inside.** A sense of a brighter light behind the ordinary one. The feeling that you are not only the shade.

**At the edge.** Yes. The Godly nothing, and a light so close that without a garment the soul would be nullified.

**Into the day.** Look at a lampshade today and say: "There is a light that does not come through a screen, and a part of it is me."

## Section 20: The delay tells (lines 370-380)

**The movement.** It gives one simple sign to watch, without pressure: how long it takes to be moved.

> הרי מאחר שכבר חלף ועבר רגע אחד ושתים שלא נתפעל מגופה של ההשגה עצמה מזה מורה על היות שבחינת אלהות שבהשגה זו מוסתר בה מאד
> *Since a moment or two has already passed in which he was not moved by the grasp itself, this shows that the Godliness in this grasp is very hidden in it.*

**Contemplating it.** When the natural soul hears a Godly idea and is not moved, and only later is moved by the summary through toil (line 371), the delay itself tells something. The Godliness in that idea is very hidden for him (line 372). What moves him then is only the short conclusion, the "so therefore" (lines 374-375). If he is moved at once by the grasp itself, the Godliness is less hidden (line 376). Still, all of it is Godliness hidden in a garment of nogah (line 376). The Godly soul's arousal in the very same grasp is not the arousal "of something separate from Godliness" (line 379). Bring it home. Notice, in your next learning, how long it takes after a line lands. The picture is a pause. It breaks because this is not a test you pass. It only tells you how thick the covering is today.

**From inside.** Information, not judgment. You learn to read your own pauses.

**At the edge.** It names the hiding, and does not go past it here.

**Into the day.** In prayer today, when a phrase lands at once, stay with it. When it lands late, do not force it.

## Section 21: Hearing the essence (lines 381-405)

**The movement.** It moves the mind from many explanations to the one core that holds them, and then out again. The mind widens from its centre.

> כי מתפעל מיד מתמציתו האלקית וממילא נעשה אצלו רוב הסבר לאין שיעור לאורך ורוחב ועומק גדול
> *For he is moved at once by its Godly core, and of itself a great deal of explanation comes to him, without measure, in length and breadth and great depth.*

**Contemplating it.** Two people hear the same Godly idea (line 381). One is moved by the explanation, by how well it is argued (line 383). The other is moved at once by the Godliness in it (line 384). His sense holds all the explanation inside it, so that he supplies most of it himself (lines 385-386). There is a counterfeit. Someone is moved only by the idea's "spirituality", which is empty imagining from heat of the blood (lines 387-388). This is the most common error among the chassidim, he says (line 389). The sign of the real is that he sees the innermost point in every detail (line 391), and from it understands one thing from another, twice and twice over (line 392). The one who only grasped the words keeps them "like lumps of matter without form", and they shrink until only the outline is left (lines 393-394). Another error: someone is moved by the core at once and cannot bear the explanation. That is not the Godly grasp. It is the natural piety every Jew has, the smallest level of the Godly soul (lines 398-402). The true sense is the "essence" of the idea, like a concentrate that flavours many drinks (lines 403-404). Bring it home. One drop of syrup flavours a whole jug. The picture breaks because the syrup is added from outside. Here the core is already in every detail.

**From inside.** Expansion. Ideas giving birth to ideas. The pleasure of seeing one thing everywhere in a teaching.

**At the edge.** It comes close: the simple matter before any form, the hyle (line 405). The core is not another detail. It is what the details are made of.

**Into the day.** After you learn today, say the one core in one sentence, then see what else that sentence opens.

## Section 22: The least of the soul, greater than the best of the self (lines 406-416)

**The movement.** It reverses the ranks a person usually keeps. The smallest thing in the soul outweighs the greatest thing in the self.

> אך זהו מצד בחינת עצמיות נשמות ישראל כידוע גדולה הרבה גם מהיותר עליון שבנפש הטבעית
> *But this is from the essence of the souls of Israel, as is known, and it is far greater even than the highest in the natural soul.*

**Contemplating it.** The distance between being moved by an explanation and being moved by its Godly core is small beside the distance between the natural soul's Godly arousal and the Godly soul's (line 406). The natural one is only a garment that hides the other (lines 407-408). Even the lowest of the Godly soul, the smallness of following God without a reason, as in the Exodus, "like a child who walks after his father" (line 410), is far greater than the highest of the natural soul, the simple will above reason (line 411). Still, the soul's ten powers are clothed in the natural soul's ten powers, delight in delight, will in will, mind in mind (line 412). Inside the natural soul's understanding is the Godly soul's understanding, reaching down from its root in Eden (lines 413-414). Bring it home. Picture a child holding his father's hand through a crowd, and a professor making a fine argument. The picture breaks because the text does not despise the argument. It is the garment the child walks in.

**From inside.** Humility, and new respect for the plain. The small, unreasoned turning toward God is not the bottom. It is the most precious.

**At the edge.** It points to the essence of Jewish souls, and stops there.

**Into the day.** Keep one plain custom today without asking why, the way a child follows.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Section 23: As natural as hunger (lines 417-436)

**The movement.** It makes Godly arousal natural instead of an achievement. The soul is drawn to God the way a body is drawn to bread.

> כך ממש התפעלות נפש האלהית מעניני אלהות בכל פרטיהם בבחינת עצמית וטבעית
> *So exactly is the Godly soul's arousal by Godly things, in all their details, essential and natural.*

**Contemplating it.** The soul is a part of God, literally, so its arousal must differ in kind from the natural soul's (line 419). It is essential Godly arousal, not a separate one (line 420), because Godliness is its nature. That is where its being is taken from (line 421). In Yiddish he says it: "Godly arousal, not human arousal: the soul is moved, not the body; the soul hears" (line 422). Like the angels, called sons of God, whose arousal is only Godly (line 423). As the natural soul is moved by bodily things by nature, because that is where it comes from, so exactly the Godly soul is moved by Godly things (line 424). No one expects the Godly soul to want food (line 425). And the natural soul reaches toward God only with great toil (line 426). Even so it can, because nogah too draws its life from "the Godly power that brings all into being" (line 427). All such arousal is called serving God with the soul (line 432). "You who cleave", of yourselves, by nature (line 433). Even the lowest soul has the yechidah, the "one knot" (line 434). Bring it home. Your hunger at noon comes without effort. The picture is hunger. It breaks at one point: hunger wants to take in, and the soul's pull wants to give itself over.

**From inside.** Ease. The pull toward Him is native. You are not forcing a stranger's feeling. You are letting your own nature speak.

**At the edge.** Yes. The yechidah, the bond in every soul (line 434), and the one line here that speaks of the world: the Godly power that brings all into being (line 427).

**Into the day.** Before you eat today, pause once: "My body wants this by nature. My soul wants Him by nature."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Section 24: The Godly soul, first level: good done by nature (lines 437-458)

**The movement.** The second ladder starts at the very bottom and finds the soul there: in deeds done without thinking.

> כי אם רק מצד בחינת יהודית העצמית דוקא
> *But only from his essential Jewishness, specifically.*

**Contemplating it.** Every good deed depends on choice, and on each person's love and awe (line 439). But there is also something like nature in every Jew, in doing good and avoiding evil in actual deed (line 442). It comes from the root of the Godly soul, not from choice or effort (line 443). Here the one who serves and the one who is light-headed are equal (line 444). Not desecrating Shabbat, not serving idols, not the gravest sins. Matzah on Pesach, sukkah, tefillin, tzitzit. For the simple person too, these are like nature, without reason (line 445). Not from habit learned by rote, but from his essential Jewishness (line 446). Those who are careless do so from the evil in the natural soul, which overpowers the Godly soul's nature (line 449). Without this nature in every spark of Israel, piety would long ago have run out (line 453). Service and awe strengthen it (line 454). Even the sinners of Israel are "full of mitzvot like a pomegranate" (line 458). Bring it home. Think of something you would never do, and never had to think about. The picture is nature. It holds: this is not a feeling at all. It is what you are.

**From inside.** Respect for your own plain faithfulness. The quiet things you have always done start to shine a little.

**At the edge.** It points to the essence, "his essential Jewishness", and does not go further.

**Into the day.** At one plain mitzvah today, tell yourself: "This came from the root, not from me."

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Section 25: The thief at the tunnel (lines 459-480)

**The movement.** It shows faith whole and alive, and cut off from the deed. It lets a person feel his own split without despair.

> שה׳ הוא העוזר לכל כי הוא ברא את הכל והוא אחדות פשוט
> *That God is the helper of all, for He created all, and He is simple oneness.*

**Contemplating it.** The good deed that comes from the soul's nature is born of a decision in the mind, and that decision is faith (line 464). It is very far and very hidden (lines 465-466), like the name of God that is on everyone's lips all day: "blessed be He, He helps" (line 467). The sages' picture: the thief at the mouth of the tunnel calls to God to help him (line 468). If he believes God helps, why steal against His will? Because for him they are "two separate things" (line 469). The faith is real and rooted in his Godly soul: God helps all, He created all, He is simple oneness (line 470). The theft comes from the natural soul's desire (line 470). He may truly ask God to make the theft succeed (line 471). This proves the faith is natural, inherited from the fathers (lines 472-473). Martyrdom shows how far inward it can shine (line 475). Even one who gives charity "so that my son may live" is called completely righteous (line 477). Bring it home. Where do you pray one thing and act against it the same day? The picture is harsh. It breaks because we would call the thief a hypocrite, and the text calls his faith real.

**From inside.** A sober recognition, and then the first move toward joining the two halves. Not shame, but seeing.

**At the edge.** It holds the book's one plain sentence about God and the world, "He created all and is simple oneness", in the mouth of a thief. The edge here is the gap itself: faith above reason, living beside a deed it has not reached.

**Into the day.** Pick one place where your faith and your deed are "two separate things", and bring them one step closer today.

## Section 26: Below that: not kicking (lines 481-494)

**The movement.** It tests the root by what happens under pressure: when a mitzvah is taken away, does the heart rebel or let go?

> כי הנפש האלקית עיקר רצונו רק לעשות רצון האלקי מצד עצמו כנזכר לעיל וכשמצטער פטור ולא מבעט כלל
> *For the Godly soul's main will is only to do the Godly will for its own sake; and when he suffers he is exempt, and does not kick at all.*

**Contemplating it.** Below even the good deed done by nature there are those who do good only from what they saw, like the mixed multitude who followed the miracles and then made the calf (lines 482-483). There are Jews who do good only for a name, sure they are completely righteous (lines 484-485). There are hypocrites (line 486). Yet even there Godliness is hidden. Under great suffering they weep (line 487). At Moses' rebuke they said "we have sinned" (line 488). A divorce given under pressure is valid, and people are compelled to give charity, because underneath they want to (lines 489-490). The sages tell of the sukkah in the future. When the sun beats down, the nations kick the sukkah and leave. Israel leaves and does not kick (line 491). The Godly soul wants only to do His will. When it suffers it is exempt, so leaving is His will too (line 492). Bring it home. When you are sick or excused, how do you leave the mitzvah you cannot do? The picture is a man stepping out of a hot sukkah. It holds: the difference is only in how he steps out.

**From inside.** A tender dignity. What you wanted was His will, not your part in it, so you can let go of the part.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there.

**Into the day.** When you are excused from something today, leave it without resentment.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Section 27: The Godly soul, second level: a child after his father (lines 495-504)

**The movement.** It turns faith into movement of the feet. What cannot give its reasons still walks.

> כמו התינוק שהולך אחר אביו בהתפעלות חזקה לא יועילו כל להפרידו מאביו
> *Like a child who walks after his father with strong feeling; nothing can separate him from his father.*

**Contemplating it.** Above the deed is ruach, the spirit: an arousal of inner thought and will (lines 495-496). At the Exodus the whole people went out, children and women, by the simple faith in their souls (line 497). At first they could not listen to Moses "from shortness of spirit", but when their spirit returned they believed (line 498). It moved them from their place (line 499). "I remember the kindness of your youth, your going after Me in the wilderness" (line 501). Without reason and knowledge, and still essential and natural (line 502). Like a child who walks after his father with strong feeling. Nothing can separate him from his father (line 503). Bring it home. Remember holding a parent's hand as a small child in a crowd. The picture breaks where it should: a child grows out of following. This does not.

**From inside.** Trust that moves the feet before the mind has finished. A going-after that feels like belonging, not like obeying.

**At the edge.** It is below reason and above it at once: "without reason and knowledge", yet from the essence.

**Into the day.** Take one step today that you know is right, without waiting until you understand all of it.

## Section 28: What wakes when separation is asked (lines 505-537)

**The movement.** It brings the reader to the strongest point in the soul by asking what it would do at the one demand it cannot meet.

> אך ליפרד לגמרי מאחד בזה מעורר כח היותר פנימי ועצמי
> *But to be separated entirely from the One: that wakes the most inward and essential power.*

**Contemplating it.** Converts: sparks fallen among the nations for hundreds and thousands of years wake up (line 505). A man sunk in every evil longs to become a Jew. It is "a Godly spirit stirring in him, and he does not know what it is" (lines 506-507). Those who return: Rabbi Elazar ben Durdaya wept until his soul left him (line 511). On Yom Kippur even the lightest and the heretics weep (line 514). Not from choice (line 515), but because God is revealed to every Godly spark that seeks Him (line 516), and the spark weeps of itself (line 517). Highest is martyrdom. Rabbi Akiva longed for it (line 528). A man who might commit many sins, when asked to be separated entirely from the One, wakes the most inward power and gives his life (line 531). This cannot come from toil (line 532). It comes only from the essential bond of the soul with its source, which cannot be separated in any way (line 533). If this power shone openly always, he would never sin even lightly (line 535). Bring it home. Think of one line you would never cross, whatever it cost. The picture breaks: we think we chose that line. The text says it was never effort. It was the bond.

**From inside.** A floor under you. Steadiness. The knowledge that something in you will not let go, whatever else does.

**At the edge.** Yes. The essential bond, the yechidah shining down into the deed (line 530), that "cannot be separated in any way" (line 533).

**Into the day.** At the Shema today, say in your heart, "I will not be separated from You", and let one small choice follow from it.

## Section 29: The Godly soul, third level: the heart sings, and does not know it (lines 538-563)

**The movement.** It states the book's central rule. Depth and unfeltness grow together. The mind is brought to hold a feeling that does not feel itself.

> וזהו הכלל גדול דכל שההתפעלות העצמות עמוקה יותר כמו באהבה ורצון ותענוג היותר פנימית, יותר לא ירגיש בה

> *Only let each one do according to what he is in essence.*

**Contemplating it.** Each one should recognize his place and rung, and not fool himself by reaching for what is beyond him (line 890). If he does not go up, at least he will not go down (line 891). Do not think ill of your friend, and do not envy his rung. Each has his own place, and envy makes a hard mixture in all a person's ways (line 892). Let each one do according to what he is in essence. In Yiddish: "natural, not imitated" (line 893). There is much more to say, he writes: on learning Torah for its own sake, on the mitzvot, on charity (lines 895-897). And on the permission people have given themselves, deep in the heart, to spend all their days on livelihood (line 898). That is the main cause of the fall, until a spirit is poured on them from above and they wake from their sleep (line 898). "This is the main exile, likened to sleep" (line 899). Bring it home. Whose prayer do you envy? The picture is sleep again. It holds. The book ends where it began, and now the reader knows what waking looks like from inside.

**From inside.** Relief. Permission to be yourself before God. No borrowed fervour.

**At the edge.** It points to a waking that comes by "a spirit from on high" (line 898), and does not describe it.

**Into the day.** Pray today in your own way, not someone else's.

## Section 43: Closing: not half a word (lines 900-912)

**The movement.** It seals the tract with truthfulness. There is no hidden layer. All of it came from the point of the heart.

> האם יש בכל הדברים שפירשתי שיחתי לפניהם אפילו חצי דיבור שלא יצא מתוך נקודת לבבי
> *Is there, in all the things I have explained before them, even half a word that did not come from the point of my heart?*

**Contemplating it.** He asks the chassidim not to say there are hidden secrets in it, revealed only to the modest (line 903). That, he says, is the talk of people who want to be proud: "my secret is mine" (line 904). He swears by his life that not even half a word came from anywhere but the point of his heart (line 905). All of it is understandable to anyone who has tasted Chassidus from youth (line 906). He learned it over twenty years in his father's holy hall (line 907), and checked every heart and every error himself (line 908). Bring it home. Think of your own words to God. Did they come from the point of your heart? The picture is an oath. It holds: the whole book asks for one thing, and here the author gives it.

**From inside.** Trust. Plainness. Nothing held back, and nothing to hunt for behind the words.

**Into the day.** Say one sentence to God today that is wholly true.

**Ladder.** None. It seals the book rather than deepening a sitting.

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations

1. **Section 29, line 556:** וזהו הכלל גדול דכל שההתפעלות העצמות עמוקה יותר כמו באהבה ורצון ותענוג היותר פנימית, יותר לא ירגיש בה. *The book's great rule: depth and unfeltness grow together. The truest love does not watch itself.* Love someone today without narrating it to yourself, and notice that the love did not shrink.

2. **Section 7, line 144:** שהדביקות הוא חיות והתקשרות הנפש בחיי החיים עצמות אור אין סוף ברוך הוא. *Cleaving is not produced. It is the soul's constant bond with the Essence, already there in every Jew.* Say the Shema's first verse as a giving-over, before trying to feel anything.

3. **Section 9, line 218:** והוא להיות שההתפעלות הוא אמיתית רק מצד עצם הדבר הטוב שנפשו קשור בו. *The niggun: an arousal is true when it comes from the good thing itself, the way hands clap at good news.* Let one tune rise today and do not watch yourself singing it.

4. **Section 28, line 531:** אך ליפרד לגמרי מאחד בזה מעורר כח היותר פנימי ועצמי. *The one demand the soul cannot meet shows the bond that cannot be cut, and it is not effort.* At the Shema, say "I will not be separated from You", and let one choice follow.

5. **Section 31, line 606:** אלא רק רצון אחד שכל הרצונות נכללים וטפלים בו. *The yechidah: one will before all opposites, where wife, children and livelihood are included and not lost.* Before the day's wants begin, say once: "One thing I want."

6. **Section 39, line 808:** וסימן לזה שמיד שחלף זה ועבר, בא לכלל הרגשת העצמיות מזה עצמו דוקא. *The sharpest test: bitul that leaves you full of yourself afterward was the self in disguise.* After a high moment, do one plain kindness and tell no one.

7. **Section 33, line 666:** ובאותה מדריגה עצמה תתמרמר הנפש האלהית ותבכה מצד ריחוקה מאחד. *Weeping over distance and joy in nearness are one arousal of one soul, and the cry of one who cannot contemplate counts.* On a dry day, say one honest sentence of distance to Him. Not on a crushed day.

8. **Section 41, line 882:** הרי חיות עונג בשרי זה מגיע לבחינת עונג העצמית שביחידה. *The lowest point of the heart, the "beast" below knowledge, is rooted above knowledge and reaches the yechidah.* Say one whole "thank You" today with nothing added.

9. **Section 30, line 577:** ונקרא קלא דלא משתמע כלומר בלתי מורגשת כלל ואינו נפסק לעולם. *The inner song "not heard, and never stops": a voice below sound, opened on Shabbat and in the Shema.* Give one minute at the Shema or on Shabbat to not making any sound.

10. **Section 35, line 708:** ואז נהפך האנחה שלו והמרה שחורה הטבעיות הנזכר לעיל לשמחה ועונג רק מצד אלהות ששורה על נפשו ממש. *The ache for God's light is a vessel. The Source of all life dwells in it and turns the sigh to joy.* Name one real lack of light, then do one plain thing with joy. Stop if it flattens you.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

*Compared with the Chabad method as mapped in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`, and with the other books in this index. The differences are kept, not smoothed.*

**The object is the self's own movement.** The hisbonenus ontology (`Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`) lists five objects of contemplation: the immanent and transcendent light, the order of descent, absolute unity, God's greatness, and the practitioner's own soul. The Gate of Unity takes the chain of worlds as its object. This book takes the practitioner's own arousal. Its content of contemplation is the usual one (how God fills and surrounds, lines 59, 291, 640), but what it asks a person to look at, again and again, is what that content did to him and where the movement came from. The Gate, by the same author, says the same thing in one closing chapter (Gate lines 2699-2703). Here it is the whole book.

**Self-awareness in the state is not an obstacle but the test.** The ontology lists "being aware of yourself in the state" as the subtlest obstacle, and prescribes "return attention to the object". This book is where that comes from, and it makes it harsher. "The deeper, the less felt" (line 556) is the mark of the real. Bitul felt as an achievement is its opposite (lines 808, 814). The manual's after-test, "a little less full of myself", is gentle. This book's after-tests are an insult (lines 813, 829), money (line 822) and whether the love reaches the hands all day (line 833).

**Contemplation is not the only door.** The ontology says the method is addressed to the animal soul, "since the divine soul does not need persuading". This book agrees and takes it further. All five rungs born of contemplation, even simple will, are "serving God with the body" (line 312). Then it gives a whole second ladder of the Godly soul that contemplation does not produce: good done by nature (line 446), a thief's faith (line 470), the child after its father (line 503), the martyr (line 531), the simple cry (line 660). The Gate of Unity asks a person to earn the whole through detail. This book says the highest Godly arousal "is above the mind, so the unlearned and the scholars are exactly equal" (line 623), and is found "more among simple people" (line 887). Most of it still demands toil (lines 271, 640, 728). Both stand.

**Felt arousal defended, and suspected.** The ontology records that the received picture had the Mitteler Rebbe defending arousal and R. Aharon of Strashelye insisting on bitul, and that the passages run closer to the reverse. This book holds both sides itself. It forbids forbidding arousal (line 743), and mourns those who have forbidden it (lines 28-43) and defends the sudden cry (lines 57, 660-664). It also calls most felt fervour strange fire (lines 167-175) and warns against making a "something" of any arousal (line 741). It never settles this, and this reading does not either.

**Heart arousal: higher than the Tanya makes it, lower than kavanah.** The Tanya (ch. 16) consoles the person who cannot feel: understanding clothed in the deed already gives the mitzvot wings. This book ranks that level third of five (lines 242-267), calls heart arousal "the essence of the revelation of Godliness" (line 47), and says of those who pray dry that "the light of God never shone to them" (line 34). Yet inside the same book, heart arousal is a contraction beside the intent that holds the whole idea (lines 290-292). The index keeps both, and so does this reading.

**Sadness as a vessel, with a guard.** The ontology treats bitterness and sadness as an obstacle: take the resolution if it sharpens, stop if it flattens. This book, quoting the Maggid through the Alter Rebbe, makes an essential, natural brokenness the condition for receiving the secrets of the Torah at all (line 702), and the place where the Source of life dwells (line 704). It means the Godly soul's ache over the lack of God's light, not sadness over bodily lack (line 697). The reading keeps the manual's guard: not on a crushed day, and stop if it flattens you.

**Song as a sign, not a tool.** The Gate of Unity uses no song. The Piaseczner uses melody to wake the soul. This book puts the niggun at its centre (lines 209-219, 542, 574-577), but as the sign of real arousal, never as a way to produce it. An arousal made on purpose and waited for is the lowest rung (lines 221-222, 226). The highest song is one "not heard" at all (line 577).

**In company, and in one's own shape.** The Gate of Unity is a solitary contemplation, long and exact. This book asks for Chassidus spoken aloud with a friend a hundred times (line 97), and ends by telling each person to find his own place, not to envy, and to be "natural, not imitated" (line 893). The ontology's diagnostic layer sorts a practitioner into states. This book sorts him into people, the cold, the crier, the flash, the heart-without-mind, the false and true bitul, the simple, and asks him to recognize himself (line 626).

**The worlds almost vanish.** The Tanya's Shaar HaYichud and the Gate of Unity contemplate how the world is said into being. This book states that once, in a thief's mouth (line 470), and once as the root of the natural soul's life (line 427). Its unity is stated from the soul's side: the bond that cannot be cut (line 533), the one will (line 606), bitul. A person looking here for a picture of the cosmos will not find it. He will find himself, and what in him is God's.
