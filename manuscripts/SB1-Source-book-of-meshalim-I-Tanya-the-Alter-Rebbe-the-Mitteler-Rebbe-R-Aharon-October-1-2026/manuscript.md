## What the slice shows about how meshalim are made (ours, from the 199)

1. **Two families, two rules.** Meshalim of *being* (the word and the speaker, sunlight in the sun, soul in body, drop and ocean, flint and fire, the fly in the corner) carry nearly all the 30 stated breaks. Meshalim of *relationship and service* (king, father, teacher, craft, war, commerce) almost never state one. The authors break a mashal out loud exactly where it could make G-d into a thing among things (A3, A4, A9). Where the mashal is about how a person acts, the human picture is safe and is left whole.
2. **The self is the first source (A5).** The Alter Rebbe's proofs of unity come almost only from the soul's own powers: one word against the power of speech (Tanya ch. 20), letters still in the mind (ch. 21), the brain that feels the toe (ch. 42), the will that moves the hand (ch. 23). The world is used for relationship (king, rope, seed); the self is used for oneness.
3. **The Mitteler Rebbe adds the felt response.** His new meshalim are of what happens to a person by itself when the essence is touched: hands clap at good news, a plain scream, the miser generous at his son's wedding, secrets told in joy, the child who says 'Abba'. A5 widened from the soul's structure to its reflexes.
4. **R. Aharon practices what his preface teaches.** Of his 24 meshalim, 10 carry a stated break, often with the exact place it fits ('this fits only the hidden mind of Kesser', the flint; 'only in this is it alike', the flame in the coal). He also gives the law of the mashal itself as a mashal: the wise man who must hide his wisdom in a foreign garment (A8), the same image the Alter Rebbe uses for G-d's speech and the father teaching his son.
5. **The break is often the teaching.** In the best meshalim the failure point is the lesson: your word leaves you, His does not (Tanya ch. 21); your soul is hurt by your body, He is not changed (ch. 42); a body comes from the parents, the world from nothing (Shaar HaYichud ch. 6); a drop is still water (Torah Or, Vayechi). The mashal brings you close, and the break takes you the last step.
6. **Families recur across the Rebbeim.** Sun and its light, the word and the speaker, soul and body, the king's name, the seed that rots, the flame that pulls up, the rope, the torch seen from far, ink and parchment, the father who makes himself small. Each entry lists where the same mashal returns (in this slice, and in the depths readings of the later Rebbeim).

## Tanya (Alter Rebbe; 54)

#### SB1-001 · The child from the father's mind *Domain:* father and son · *Where:* Tanya I ch. 2, line 70

- **Mashal:** A child is drawn from a drop of the father's brain. Nine months in the mother change it step by step, yet even the child's toenails come from that same drop.
- **Nimshal:** Every Jewish soul, even the lowest, is drawn from His thought and stays tied to its source through all the levels it came down.
- **Parts:** father's brain → His wisdom (thought); the drop → the soul's root; nine months of change → the descent through the worlds; toenails → the simplest person
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A grown child becomes a separate person with his own life. The soul never stops being one with its root; that is the point the mashal cannot carry.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper), PR-L07 (not on every side: name the break), PR-L09 (from the soul's own life)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe) (1745-1812)/Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt` line 70: כמשל הבן הנמשך ממוח האב, שאפילו צפרני רגליו נתהוו מטפה זו ממש (Like the son drawn from the father's brain, so that even his toenails came into being from that very drop.)
- **Recurs:** Tanya ch. 2 line 65 (same image, 'My firstborn son'); Shaar HaYichud (Mitteler Rebbe) line 1874 (drop in the father's brain); Rebbe Rashab, Sefer HaMaamarim (RM-C04 in depths: 'a father gives himself')
- **For today:** You came from something far bigger than you, the way a child comes from a parent. However far you have travelled, the line back to where you came from was never cut.

#### SB1-002 · Hugging the king in his robes *Domain:* king and kingdom · *Where:* Tanya I ch. 4, line 105

- **Mashal:** If you hug a king, it makes no difference whether he wears one robe or many. His body is inside, and you are holding him.
- **Nimshal:** Torah and mitzvos are clothed in plain physical things, yet holding them is holding His will and wisdom, which are one with Him.
- **Parts:** the king's body → His will and wisdom; robes → the physical cases the Torah speaks about; the hug → learning and doing
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A king's robes are separate cloth; His will is not separate from Him. The mashal also hides that here the 'robes' are what make the hug possible at all.
- **Principles:** PR-L01 (a handle, not the thing), PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper), PR-L10 (take the picture off after)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe) (1745-1812)/Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt` line 105: הרי זה כמחבק את המלך דרך משל, שאין הפרש במעלת התקרבותו ודביקותו במלך, בין מחבקו כשהוא לבוש לבוש אחד בין שהוא לבוש כמה לבושים (It is like hugging the king: there is no difference in the closeness whether he wears one garment or many.)
- **Recurs:** Tanya ch. 16 line 234 ('like the one who hugs the king, mentioned above'); Torah Or line 233 (hugging a wise man through his body); Imrei Binah line 262 (the hug is outer love)
- **For today:** When you hold someone in a coat, you are still holding them. The ordinary things you do with care can be the coat the deepest thing is wearing.

#### SB1-003 · Getting a law fully *Domain:* teacher and student · *Where:* Tanya I ch. 5, line 109

- **Mashal:** When you really understand a law in the Mishnah or Gemara, your mind takes hold of it and surrounds it. And at that moment your mind is also dressed inside it.
- **Nimshal:** Knowing Torah is a two-way unity: the mind holds G-d's wisdom and is held by it, a union like no other.
- **Parts:** your mind → the person; the law → His wisdom and will; surrounding and being dressed in → the double unity
- **Where it breaks (ours):** Here the 'object' you grasp is His own will, so the closeness is not one idea among others but union with Him. That part has no human match.
- **Principles:** PR-L08 (one person, many powers), PR-L09 (from the soul's own life)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe) (1745-1812)/Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt` line 109: כשאדם מבין ומשיג איזו הלכה במשנה או בגמרא לאשורה על בוריה הרי שכלו תופס ומקיף אותה, וגם שכלו מלובש בה באותה שעה (When a person understands a law in the Mishnah or Gemara fully, his mind grasps and surrounds it, and his mind is also clothed in it at that time.)
- **Recurs:** Tanya ch. 5 line 110 ('if Reuven claims so and so')
- **For today:** When you truly get something, you hold it, and for that moment it also holds you. You are inside the understanding, not just looking at it.

#### SB1-004 · The harlot hired by the king *Domain:* king and kingdom · *Where:* Tanya I ch. 9, line 166

- **Mashal:** A king hires a harlot to tempt his son, to test him. She uses every trick, but secretly she wants the son to refuse her, since that is what the king wants.
- **Nimshal:** The force that tempts a person is sent from above; its inner wish is that the person win.
- **Parts:** the king → G-d; the son → the person; the harlot → the urge to do wrong; her hidden wish → the purpose of the test
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A real harlot has her own will; the urge in us has no power or wish apart from the One who sends it. The mashal is borrowed from the Zohar and the Tanya only points to it.
- **Principles:** PR-L01 (a handle, not the thing), PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper), PR-L10 (take the picture off after)

- **Mashal:** A king sent his only son into exile, so he would lose and forget his place of greatness completely.
- **Nimshal:** The soul's descent is meant to strip it of 'I'; that loss is what lets it receive more.
- **Parts:** the king → G-d; the son → the soul; exile → the body and the world
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A king's son may truly forget who he is; the soul's root is never lost.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 134: כשהמלך שלח את בנו יחידו שילך בגלות בשביל לאבד ולשכוח לגמרי מקום גדולתו (When the king sent his only son to go into exile in order to lose and completely forget his place of greatness.)
- **Recurs:** Tanya ch. 31 (the prince freed from prison)
- **For today:** Sometimes people are sent far from where they shine so they can grow past their pride. Being far is part of the plan.

#### SB1-141 · The covenant: one thing cut in two *Domain:* love/marriage · *Where:* Sha'arei Teshuvah, line 731

- **Mashal:** A covenant is one thing cut in two; the two are halves of one body. Even if one does ugly things, the other cannot take revenge.
- **Nimshal:** G-d's covenant with Israel holds even through sin.
- **Parts:** the two halves → G-d and Israel; ugly deeds → sin; no revenge → the bond holds
- **Where it breaks (ours):** People can break covenants; this one is like one body.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 731: ואפי' אם א' יפעל רעה לרעהו או יעשה מעשים מכוערים (Even if one does evil to the other or does ugly deeds.)
- **Recurs:** Likkutei Torah line 3525 (He bound Himself by covenant)
- **For today:** Some bonds are like being two halves of one thing. Even when one side messes up, the bond does not break.

#### SB1-142 · Father and son turned away *Domain:* father and son · *Where:* Sha'arei Teshuvah, line 718

- **Mashal:** A father and son turned their backs and walked far apart. When the son, far away, cries out bitterly until it touches the point of his soul, the father turns back.
- **Nimshal:** Teshuvah from far away reaches the essential love.
- **Parts:** the father → G-d; the son → the person; the cry → teshuvah
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A father may not hear from far; He hears the cry wherever it comes from.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper), PR-L09 (from the soul's own life)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 718: שכאשר יתעורר הבן מרחוק וצועק בקול מר עד שנגע לנקודת נפשו (That when the son is aroused from afar and cries out bitterly until it touches the point of his soul.)
- **Recurs:** Likkutei Torah line 665 (back to back); Derech Chaim line 65 (the son far from his father)
- **For today:** If you have walked away, one real cry from deep inside is enough for the other to turn around.

#### SB1-143 · The villager at court *Domain:* king and kingdom · *Where:* Sha'arei Teshuvah, line 249

- **Mashal:** A villager who never saw royal splendor loses his mind when he sees the court, but he has grasped nothing.
- **Nimshal:** First comes being stunned; real understanding comes later.
- **Parts:** the villager → the beginner; the court → G-dliness; being stunned → first awe
- **Where it breaks (ours):** The villager is stunned by what he sees; G-dliness is 'seen' by the mind, so the stunning can deepen into knowing.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 249: כמו עד"מ הידוע מאיש בן כפר שלא ראה נימוסי הוד גדולת מלכות שכשרואה יוצא מדעתו (Like the known example of a villager who never saw the ways of royal splendor: when he sees it, he loses his mind.)
- **Recurs:** Shaarei HaYichud (R. Aharon) line 34 (the city man and the villager); Talmud Chagigah 13b (source)
- **For today:** Being overwhelmed by something is not the same as understanding it. Awe is the door, not the room.

#### SB1-144 · Honey on a radish *Domain:* food · *Where:* Sha'arei Teshuvah, line 303

- **Mashal:** Honey sweetens a radish; take the honey away and the bitterness is back.
- **Nimshal:** When the G-dly will is withdrawn, the foreign will wakes up again: sweetening is not yet change.
- **Parts:** the radish → the animal will; honey → the G-dly will; removing the honey → the light leaving
- **Where it breaks (ours):** In the Gate of Unity the jam of honey and radish becomes one new taste; here the warning is that it may not.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 303: אבל מיד כאשר יסתלק אור וכח הרצון האלקי אז כח הרצון הזר חוזר וניעור כמו שהיה תחלה (But as soon as the light and power of the G-dly will withdraw, the power of the foreign will wakes again as it was at first.)
- **Recurs:** Shaar HaYichud (Mitteler Rebbe) line 1214 (the jam of honey and radish)
- **For today:** Sugar on something bitter hides it; it does not change it. Real change goes deeper than covering.

#### SB1-145 · The treasure kept for the worst day *Domain:* commerce · *Where:* Sha'arei Teshuvah, line 446

- **Nimshal:** The deepest awe is a hidden treasure that comes out only when the soul is at stake.
- **Parts:** the treasure → the deepest awe; the wedding → great joy; the threat → self-sacrifice
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A treasure is spent and gone; the deepest awe grows by being revealed.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 446: אז לא יחוס על האוצר ומטמונים שלו ויבזבזם לגמרי בהתגלות לעין כל (Then he will not spare his treasure and hidden stores, and will spend them completely in the open for all to see.)
- **For today:** Some of what you have inside only comes out when everything is on the line. You are richer than you know.

#### SB1-146 · The hidden root *Domain:* nature · *Where:* Sha'arei Teshuvah, line 518

- **Mashal:** A tree's root is hidden in the ground; the deeper and more covered it is, the stronger the tree.
- **Nimshal:** Awe hidden deep in the body is what makes the service strong.
- **Parts:** the root → the hidden awe; the earth → the body
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A root can rot unseen; hidden awe is meant to show in the fruit.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 518: ע"י שהשרש מכוס' בחומר עפר הגוף (Through the root being covered in the dust of the body.)
- **For today:** The roots you cannot see hold the tree up. What is deepest in you does not need to show to be strong.

#### SB1-147 · The king's wedding feast *Domain:* king and kingdom · *Where:* Sha'arei Teshuvah, line 196

- **Mashal:** At the king's wedding feast, the lords rejoice with him, and the awe of the king shines hidden in their hearts.
- **Nimshal:** Joy before G-d carries a hidden awe within it.
- **Parts:** the lords → the joyful servant; the king's awe → fear of G-d inside joy
- **Where it breaks (ours):** Lords fear a king's power; here the awe is of His greatness, not His anger.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Sha'arei Teshuvah (Lubavitch, c. 1817-1826).txt` line 196: כמו השרים היושבים בשמחת הסעודה של המלך ביום חתונתו (Like the lords sitting at the joyful feast of the king on his wedding day.)
- **For today:** At a great celebration you can be happy and still know whose party it is. Joy and respect can sit together.

## Ner Mitzvah VeTorah Or (Mitteler Rebbe; 1)

#### SB1-148 · The spark near the flame *Domain:* water/fire · *Where:* Ner Mitzvah VeTorah Or, line 31

- **Mashal:** A spark near the flame is drawn into it; that is no wonder. Faith draws the soul from far away.
- **Nimshal:** Simple faith pulls the soul into G-d even from far.
- **Parts:** the spark → the soul; the flame → G-d; distance → exile and lowness
- **Where it breaks (ours):** Sparks are pulled by nearness; faith pulls across distance, which the mashal can only point at.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper), PR-L07 (not on every side: name the break)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Ner Mitzvah VeTorah Or (Lubavitch, c. 1820).txt` line 31: ויצאו משם באמונה פשוטה הרי כח אמונה זאת היא המושכת את הנפש בבחי' התכללות גם מרחוק (They went out from there with simple faith; the power of this faith draws the soul to be included even from afar.)
- **Recurs:** Imrei Binah line 145 (a spark out of the flame); Tanya ch. 19 (the flame)
- **For today:** A spark near a fire jumps in. Trust is a pull that works even from far away.

## Pirush HaMilos (Mitteler Rebbe; 2)

#### SB1-155 · Song at the end of the day *Domain:* music · *Where:* Pirush HaMilos, line 591

- **Mashal:** A man sings when he finishes his work well. Joy belongs to the morning, song to the evening.
- **Nimshal:** Song is the soul's response after the work is done.
- **Parts:** the finished work → service completed; the song → praise
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A worker sings because he is done; the soul's song is never 'done' working.
- **Principles:** PR-L09 (from the soul's own life)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Pirush HaMilos (Lubavitch, c. 1826).txt` line 591: כמשל אדם ששמח בכלות מעשיהו בכי טוב שאז מרנן דוקא (Like a person who is happy when his work is finished well, and then he sings.)
- **For today:** When a job is done well, you hum. Song often comes at the end, not the start.

#### SB1-156 · The soldier who stops being afraid *Domain:* war · *Where:* Pirush HaMilos, line 503

- **Mashal:** A soldier surrounded on every side gives up on saving himself. Then his heart flips: the fear leaves and he turns to fight with joy and courage.
- **Nimshal:** Giving up on yourself can free a strength above fear.
- **Parts:** the soldier → the person; the enemies → the pulls; giving up on himself → self-giving
- **Where it breaks (ours):** A soldier's despair can turn to recklessness; this courage comes from giving oneself to Him, not from giving up on life.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper), PR-L09 (from the soul's own life)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Pirush HaMilos (Lubavitch, c. 1826).txt` line 503: והופך פניו לעמוד כנגדם במלחמה בשמחה ובגבורת הלב (He turns his face to stand against them in war, with joy and a strong heart.)
- **For today:** When there is nothing left to lose, fear can turn into courage. Letting go of saving yourself can free you.

## Imrei Binah (Mitteler Rebbe; 13)

#### SB1-157 · Noise comes from distance *Domain:* music · *Where:* Imrei Binah, line 553

- **Mashal:** The far-off angels sing with great noise; the nearer ones with less. All noise comes from distance.
- **Nimshal:** The closer you are, the less your bittul shows.
- **Parts:** noise → felt excitement; distance → being far from the source
- **Where it breaks (ours):** Noise can also be joy; the text speaks only of the noise of yearning.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Imrei Binah (Lubavitch, 1821).txt` line 553: וידוע דכל רעש הוא בא מצד הריחוק (It is known that all noise comes from distance.)
- **Recurs:** Likkutei Torah line 511 (the fish's calm)
- **For today:** The people who make the most noise about something are often furthest from it. Being close tends to be quiet.

#### SB1-158 · No one stands on a law until he stumbles *Domain:* teacher and student · *Where:* Imrei Binah, line 35

- **Mashal:** A learner does not reach the depth until he has worked so hard he almost despairs. It is the same in business: only after a man gives up on his capital does the big profit come.
- **Nimshal:** Every rise is preceded by descent and darkness.
- **Parts:** the stumble → the darkness first; the profit → the deeper light; the farmer weeping → effort without seeing fruit
- **Where it breaks (ours):** In business, despair can end in real loss; the text promises the light after the dark.
- **Principles:** PR-L04 (the lower is built like the upper), PR-L09 (from the soul's own life)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Imrei Binah (Lubavitch, 1821).txt` line 35: עד שיתייאש לומר שיפסיד הקרן אז דוקא ימצא ריוח גדול (Until he despairs, saying he will lose the capital; precisely then he finds great profit.)
- **Recurs:** Imrei Binah line 34 (one suffers before entering the king's palace); Sha'arei Orah line 101 (darkness after darkness)
- **For today:** You often get it right after you nearly gave up. The struggle is not a sign you are off track.

#### SB1-159 · Wanting to live *Domain:* the soul's faculties: will · *Where:* Imrei Binah, line 43

- **Mashal:** There is a love you do not decide on, like your will to live. You cannot split 'wanting to live' from 'not wanting to die'; they are one thing.
- **Nimshal:** The inner heart's bond to G-d is that kind of plain, undivided will.
- **Parts:** will to live → the soul's bond; not wanting to die → refusal to be cut off
- **Where it breaks (ours):** The will to live is for oneself; this will is to be bound to Him, even past oneself.
- **Principles:** PR-L09 (from the soul's own life)
- **Source:** `Chassidus-txt/R. Dovber Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe) (1773-1827)/Imrei Binah (Lubavitch, 1821).txt` line 43: כמו שאין להבדיל ולחלק במה שרוצ' לחיות ואינו רוצ' למו' דהיינו הך ממש (Just as one cannot divide between wanting to live and not wanting to die: it is the very same thing.)
- **Recurs:** Torah Or line 2214 (loving like one's own life)
- **For today:** You do not decide to want to live; it is just you. Some of your deepest wants are like that.

#### SB1-160 · Love felt from far away *Domain:* father and son · *Where:* Imrei Binah, line 258

- **Mashal:** A father close to his son does not feel the full force of his love. When the son is far, the longing burns.

- **For today:** You feel how much you love someone most when they are away. Distance can show you a bond you forgot.

#### SB1-162 · The student who makes himself small *Domain:* teacher and student · *Where:* Imrei Binah, line 326
