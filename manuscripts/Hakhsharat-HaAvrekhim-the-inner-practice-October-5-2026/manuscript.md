Line numbers are lines of the source file. Chapter and paragraph references (e.g. 4:12) follow the file's own labels. Translations are mine and kept close.

A note on the book's standing. It is the second of the Rebbe's graded manuals. It follows Chovat HaTalmidim, which was written for boys, and it was meant to prepare for a planned "Chovat HaAvrekhim". The introduction says only someone who has already worked through Chovat HaTalmidim should read it (line 20). A note dated Monday of Re'eh 5702 (August 1941), signed "Kalonimus Kalmish", records corrections made in the Warsaw ghetto, and pleads "for we are in great distress" (line 14). So the revised book we have was finished under catastrophe. Its subject, however, is the ordinary pre-war young Chassid who "knows he should want to want" and feels nothing.

## 2. The method: every concrete instruction

Grouped by what it trains. "Place" gives the line and chapter:paragraph.

## A. Finding what is already moving in you (the first step)

1. **Do not ask for new feelings. Get to know what is already happening in you.** Place: line 87 (4:6). **כי הכל בך, התרגשות בך וגם אתה איש מתרגש, ועליך להשתדל רק להכיר אותך ואת אשר בך מתרחש** ("for everything is in you: feeling is in you and you are a person who is moved; you need only strive to recognise yourself and what is stirring in you"). Give the soul a platform: **ועליך רק לתן לה מצע בקרבך שעלי׳ תתגלה ותתחזק** ("you need only give it a platform in you on which it can be revealed and grow strong").
2. **Start with the big, clear feelings, not the faint ones.** Faint stirrings cannot yet be told apart from bodily appetite. Place: line 89 (4:8). **לכן החל בהתרגשויות הגדולות והחזקות יותר** ("so begin with the larger, stronger feelings").
3. **Start with joyful feelings, not the soul's pain.** Place: line 89. **ומוטב שלא תתחיל בהתרגשויות כאבי הנפש** ("better not to begin with the feelings of the soul's pains").
4. **When a good stirring comes, stay with it and give it thought, or it vanishes** (citing Imrei Elimelech). Place: line 90 (4:9). **מחשבה חזקה היא ראשית המצע** ("strong thought is the first platform").

## B. Strong thought and picturing (*machshavah chazakah*, *dimyon*, *tziyur*)

5. **Think simply and broadly, not deeply or analytically.** Place: line 94 (4:13). **לא בשכליות ועמקיות תחשוב אז רק במחשבה פשוטה ורחבה** ("do not then think with intellect and depth, only with simple, broad thought"). 6. **Begin with occasions that change, not with daily prayer.** Shabbos, when clothes and the house look different, and festivals are easier to start with than the fixed daily prayer. Place: line 94. **וטוב יותר שבמצוה שיש בה השתניות שונות תתחיל** ("it is better to begin with a mitzvah that has variations"). 7. **Fill the thought with many different pictures.** A single image cannot hold the mind. Place: line 98 (4:17). **אבל אי אפשר למחשבה חזקה בלא ציורים בה** ("strong thought is impossible without pictures in it"). Line 101 gives the reason: thought and imagination **שאינם יכולים לשהות זמן מה במחשבה ודמיון אחד בלתי אם מוסיפים עליהם צורות חדשות** ("cannot stay a while on one thought and image unless new forms are added"). 8. **Picture what can be pictured "as if before your very eyes". Hold in strong thought what cannot**, such as words and events. Place: line 102 (4:21). **ושהם בני דמיון תדמה בדמיון כאילו הם כנגד עיניך ממש**. Line 99: **המחשבה חזקה, והדמיון, מין אחד הם** ("strong thought and imagination are one kind"). 9. **Picture your teacher's face to cleave soul to soul** (citing the Chida and the Ramak). Place: line 103. **יעיין בחכמה שלמד מרבו ויצייר צורתו ויתדבק נפש בנפש**. 10. **Two grades of strengthening thought.** First *machshavah peshutah*: think and picture exactly what you read, adding nothing. Then *machshavah mechadeshes*: widen what was given, and only much later produce new ideas. Place: lines 192-194 (6:27-29), 201. Label: **מחשב״ה פשוט״ה**. In the worked example you first picture only Noam Elimelech's words, that you are standing in the Temple, then you add everything you know about the Temple. **וחשוב במחשבה חזקה כאילו הוא ממש כך, שאתה עומד במקדש לפני ד׳ ומתפלל** (line 193). 11. **Practise in spare moments, not only at the time of prayer.** Then at prayer the stirring comes easily. Place: line 194. The same goes for walking in the street (line 191). 12. **Tanakh and midrash: take part "as if you were there"**, and weave all the midrashim into one scene. Place: line 199 (7:3). **תשתדל להשתתף בכל מאורעות הקודש שעברו כאילו גם אתה היית אז**. Honesty about invention: **הן לא תדע אם באמת כך הי׳ כפי שאתה משער לך במחשבתך** ("you do not know whether it really was as you imagine", line 201). It is permitted because it stays within the foundations of Torah and midrash. 13. **Do not do an uplifting widening and a humbling one on the same day, or at least not in the same hour.** The feelings differ, and together they produce nothing or confusion. Place: line 227 (8:2). **נכון שלא לקרא אותן ביום אחד ועל כל פנים לא בשעה אחת**. 14. **Remembering the day of death has to be widened in advance, at calm times, until you are wholly moved.** Then in the moment of temptation a brief recall is enough. Place: line 228 (8:3). **אם רק בהרהור מחשבה של רגע תזכיר לך יו״ם המית״ה לא תפעל בך** ("if you recall the day of death only as a passing thought, it will not act on you"). If your heart is too heavy for the full scene, picture a dead friend's face then and now (line 242).

## C. The worked meditation: se'udah shelishis in the dark (lines 104-112)

15. **Posture and setting.** Sit with fellow Chassidim at the close of Shabbos, **ובחושך אתם יושבים** ("and you sit in darkness", line 106). The darkness is deliberate: the body sits in darkness because the heart and eyes no longer see the world (line 107). 16. **Stretch a feeling of awe instead of letting it pass. Say it inwardly or aloud, slowly, and repeat it while it grows.** Place: line 110 (4:29). **אני ירא את ד׳. כל כך אני ירא את אלקי הגדול והקדוש** ("I fear God. So much do I fear my great and holy God"), with a Yiddish version. Then: **ובנחת חשוב או אמור זאת, וכפול אותם אם תרגיש שמוסיפים ומרחיבים את יראתך לד׳** ("think or say it calmly, and repeat it if you feel it adds to and widens your awe"). The warning: **ובלבד שלא תחשוב את התיבות בלבד שאתה אומר, רק את ד׳** ("only do not think of the words you are saying, but of God"). 17. **Let a verse arrive by itself, for example "Moshe approached the thick darkness where God was."** Place: line 111. **וכמו מעצמו מתנוצץ בך הפסוק ויבא משה אל הערפל אשר שם האלקים**. 18. **The limit on technique.** When se'udah shelishis actually comes, do not busy yourself hunting for pictures: **כי העיקר בשלש סעודות הוא להתקשר בד׳ ולשפוך לפניו את לבו** ("the essence of se'udah shelishis is to bind yourself to God and pour out your heart before Him", line 112). Fragments of pictures are enough at first: **מ״מ קטעי ציורים יעלו לך** (line 109).

## D. When a feeling is already present: do not look for images

19. **Do not go looking for an image that fits the feeling.** Place: line 182 (6:17). **תתחיל אז לשוטט בשכלך ולחפש איזה דמיון** is what he forbids. Line 184: **לא תחפש דמיונות ומשלים רק תתעכב ותתבונן בהתרגשותך הקדושה** ("do not seek images and parables; linger and contemplate your holy feeling"). The images will rise by themselves once your thought has been strengthened beforehand.

## E. Using stirrings as they arrive (ch. 9)

20. **Clothe a good stirring at once in learning or a mitzvah** (the Ramban, via Imrei Elimelech). Place: line 270 (9:24). **שכשבא איזה הרהור טוב באיש יעשה בו מצוה או ילמוד בו, ובזה ילבישו בקדושה**. On Shabbos, study that Shabbos's material. **תסיח דעתך מכל דבר ובהספר תהי׳ שקוע** (line 271). Pause in the middle to rethink it and try to add to it, and pray to be sanctified (line 272). The sign that it is working is a delight you have never had from learning before, **והיא מפני שגם נפשך נמצאת בתוך הרעיון** ("because your own soul is inside the idea", line 271). 21. **An unnamed low mood: first look at it before reaching for food or chatter.** Ask whether you recently did something wrong. Place: line 273. **הסתכל בה, אם דכאות רוח אתה מרגיש בקרבך**. If you find the cause, the heart lightens at once, and then make a real resolution with safeguards. 22. **Whatever the feeling, low or joyful, do not let it slip by.** Place: line 274. **נפשך כנף הושיטה מהר וחזקהו, אחוז בו ואל תרפהו** ("your soul has held out a wing: quickly grasp it, hold it and do not let go"). Say Psalms that match it. For low spirits he names 3, 6, 10, 13, 16, 17, 22, 25, 28, 31, 38, 42, especially 51. For joy he names 1, 8, 9, 18, 19, 23, 24, 27, 29, 30, 33, 34. Say them **רק כאילו תיקן דוד זאת במכוון בשבילך** ("as if David composed it expressly for you"). "My God, why have You forsaken me" is said picturing yourself before the Throne. 23. **If you cannot say Psalms or learn, speak to God in your own words.** Place: line 275. He gives a model in Hebrew and Yiddish: "Master of the world, my Father, oy how far I am from You ... I want and I want, and I lie as if in a swamp ...". 24. **A worldly worry or joy: turn at once to the side and stand facing the wall.** Place: line 285. **תלך תיכף הצדה ותעמיד עצמך אל הכותל**. First consider the worry and who can save you (line 286). Then picture the Throne, say a matching Psalm (e.g. 3, 69, 121), then free speech, then one verse of strength (Psalm 23). The result: **ובכל היום תרגיש אחר תפילה זו מין עונג רוחני, עונג של טהרה** ("all day after such a prayer you will feel a spiritual pleasure, a pleasure of purity", line 286). For a joy, use Psalm 18, briefly, and give thanks (line 287). 25. **Do not waste even a sigh.** Place: line 288. **אפילו מן אנחה פשוטה קטנה וקלה גופנית**. 26. **Drink (*mashke*) among Chassidim, with intention.** The drink opens part of the soul, and you say: **אחזיק בכנפה זה ולא ארפנו** ("I will hold this wing and not let go", line 276).

## F. Melody (*niggun*)

27. **Take a melody, turn your face to the wall or just close your eyes, and picture yourself before the Throne of Glory, pouring out your soul.** Place: line 279 (9:33). **קח לך איזו תנועה של ניגון, תסב את פניך אל הקיר, או רק תסגור את עיניך**. At first you sing to wake the soul, and then: **מעט מעט תרגיש שנפשך התחילה כבר לנגן מעצמה** ("little by little you will feel your soul has begun to sing by itself"). 28. **No new tunes are needed. Joyful tunes work too. Sing with the group, or at home "whenever you feel ready". A hum is enough.** Place: lines 278, 283, 284. **כי יש מי שמנגן רק המי׳ בלחש וקולו נשמע במרום** ("some only hum in a whisper and their voice is heard on high"). 29. **Let words come out while singing, even words not in the siddur.** Place: line 281. **כי כגון אלו מחצב הנפש הם** ("such words are quarried from the soul").

## G. Looking *at* rather than *in*: the technique against bad stirrings (lines 290-313)

30. **The law.** Dwelling *in* a stirring strengthens it. Thinking *about* it, even just looking at it, dims it. Place: line 293 (9:47). **אם טיפול מחשבתו הוא בהרחש, אז ע״י הטיפול יוסיף הרחש להתפעל יותר** ("if his thought's handling is *in* the stirring, it grows stronger"), and **ולא הטיפול לבד רק גם ההסתכלות בעצמה שאדם מסתכל במחשבתו על איזה רחש אשר בקרבו, אף על מחשבתו, מכהה את הרחש ואת המחשבה** ("not only the handling, but looking itself, a person looking in his mind at a stirring within him, even at his thought, dims the stirring and the thought"). 31. **An experiment to feel the law.** Picture a close relative, and the image grows as you dwell in it. Ask "what image am I picturing now?", **מן ההבטה בעצמה שאתה מביט עלי׳ מיד תכהה התמונה מדמיונך** ("from the very looking, the image fades from your imagination at once", line 294). Try the same with love for a friend: it weakens more slowly because it is more deeply rooted (line 295). 32. **Train on small permitted desires first, as soldiers practise on a friendly opponent.** At a meal with two meats, eat the one you want less (lines 296-297). Bais Aharon: **חפציך אסורים וואס דיר ווילט זיך טאר מען ניט** ("what you desire is forbidden: what you feel like doing, you may not do", line 296). 33. **The words for looking at a craving.** Place: line 298. "Is it true I want this so much? ... It is now clear I am a man of appetite." And **הן ידעתי שאיני צדיק אבל להיות כ״כ מנוול ובעל תאוה ... לא חשבתי** ("I knew I was no tzaddik, but that I am so vile a slave to appetite I did not think"). Repeat it once or twice. 34. **For a bad thought, jealousy or hatred.** Say **"אש תמיד תוקד על המזבח לא תכבה"** or the seven nations (from Tzetl Katan), and alternate them. In prayer, where you may not interrupt, look at the thought as a filthy beast. Place: line 299. **השמר והזהר שלא את המחשבה רעה תחשוב, רק אודות המחשבה** ("take care not to think the bad thought, only *about* the thought"). 35. **If you cannot break away, ask for twenty seconds.** Place: line 302. **על עשרים רגעים [סעקונדעס] רשות לי להפסיק ממנה ולחשוב רק אודותה**. If even that fails, first think of something that matters to you, such as money or fear, to break contact, then look (line 303). In that state keep it silent rather than spoken, because saying it lets you excuse yourself from thinking it. 36. **Stray thoughts during prayer: stop, calm yourself, sit or lean, and watch them.** Place: line 310 (9:64). **והרגיע א״ע [בערוהיג דיך]** ("calm yourself"), and **ודוקא במנוחת לב ולא בהתרגזות ולא בהתאמצות יתירה** ("specifically with a restful heart, not with agitation and not with excessive effort"). Then **הסתכל על מחשבותיך, והתבונן עם מחשבתך על פחזות הפראות האלה** ("look at your thoughts, contemplate with your mind the wildness of these savages"). The result: **מתחילה תכהנה ובכל רגע תקלשנה יותר עד שממש כנ״ל כמו במטאטא נקית את מוחך** ("at first they dim, and each moment they thin further, until as with a broom you have cleaned your mind", line 311). 37. **An unexplained mood or hardness: trace it back to the hour it began.** It may have started with a meal, a slight, or a frustrated wish. Place: line 313. **אז העצה שיתחיל לחפש בקרבו מאיזה שעה התחילה קשיות ערפו**. The sign you have hit the true cause is that at the first look it weakens, **עוד ירגיש מין הפקת רצון כאילו הוקלה מעט המשא מעל לבו** ("he feels a kind of relief, as if a little of the burden were lifted from his heart"). He calls this **חוק מחוקי הנפש הוא** ("a law among the laws of the soul"). If groping finds nothing, the mood is the soul itself crying over the wounds you gave it. 38. **Slowness.** Place: line 291 (9:45). The Baal Shem's reading of "and you shall perish quickly": **שצריך האדם לאבד את המהירות** ("a person must destroy haste"). And **רק בנחת ובישוב הדעת בכל דבר תסתכל, ובאמת, לא ברמי׳ עצמית** ("look at everything calmly and with a settled mind, and truly, not by self-deception").

## H. Timing and rhythm

39. **In prayer, do not examine yourself.** Place: line 148 (5:36). **כי אם יעלה בלבו חקירות כמו אלו, יתקרר וישאר בחקירותיו בלא תפילה** ("if such inquiries arise he will cool and be left with his inquiries and no prayer"). Test yourself only occasionally: **נסה את הנסיונות לפעמים ורק לפעמים** ("try the tests sometimes, and only sometimes"). 40. **Self-accounting at set times, preferably when already low. Eat a little less the day before, but do not fast.** Place: line 404. **לא שיתענה רק שימעט בהם כדי שיהי׳ לבו נכנע**. Picture the worlds ascending: **ובדמיונך שנתעלה ונתקדש הוי מציץ אל על עולם היצירה עם מלאכיו** (line 406). Review your sins, weep until the heart truly breaks, then trust that the repentance is accepted (lines 405-408). 41. **Three states of mind.** Place: lines 412-415. In worship, **בו לא תחשוב שום חשבון רק שמחה תשוקה ושאיפה לד׳** ("make no reckoning, only joy, longing and yearning for God"). During the accounting, brokenness. At all other times, **אז תהא נכנע ענו ושפל ומ״מ בשמחה מתוך התחזקות תהי׳** ("humble and lowly, yet in joy and strength"). 42. **Kriyas Shema, three times a week (Shabbos and two other days you feel ready).** First say aloud the declaration: "I accept the yoke ... I believe **שרק השי״ת הוא הנמצא היחידי** ... I subjugate ... **הנני מוכן למסור את עצמי לשריפה**" (lines 423-430, in Hebrew and Yiddish). He gives the reason for speaking it: at first it will be hard to say, which shows the thought is not yet real (line 421). Picture the fire and yourself leaping into it (line 433). **ולא תתחיל מיד את שמע רק הפסק רגעים אחדים** ("do not begin Shema immediately; pause a few moments", line 434). Raise your head for heaven, lower it for earth, turn it to the four directions, and lengthen *echad* (line 435). The frequency is limited because a stratagem used daily stops working (line 436, citing Me'or VaShemesh). 43. **Against ulterior motives in worship.** Picture yourself **ביער יחידי רחוק מבני אדם** ("alone in a forest, far from people", line 400). Test every private leniency by asking whether you would take it in public (line 402).

## I. Body and effort

44. **Use bodily force when feeling will not come.** Dance hard on Simchas Torah, pray aloud. Effort **אוחז הוא את נפשו בערפה ובע״כ מוציאה מן מחבואה** ("seizes his soul by the scruff and drags it out of hiding against its will", line 54). 45. **Stamp one foot and declare "only as the Torah says, not as my inclination".** It shows how much of the inclination's strength is bluff. Place: line 250. 46. **Read the booklet two or three times, the second time as work, not as a story.** Place: line 258.

## 3. The inner experience: what it is like inside

**The soul is hidden even from its owner and shows itself only in its actions.** Like God, "it sees and is not seen" (lines 34-36). Every act reveals a spark of it, yet we notice only the object: **רק גם ניצוץ מנפשו הנעלמה בקרבו נודע ומתגלה לו על ידי זה** (line 38). The mirror image: in a mirror you see what is outside it, never the mirror, **רק צורות העולם בחלקי נפש נראים ולא הנפש לעצמה** (line 43).

**Feeling is the moment the soul becomes visible to itself.** Ask someone in love what he feels and he answers: **לא יאמר את ראובן האהוב אני מרגיש, אך את האהבה אני מרגיש** ("not 'I feel Reuven, my beloved' but 'I feel the love'", line 44). The fire image: an ordinary love grips its object **כאש הנאחזת בעץ** ("like fire gripping wood"). Love of God is fire gripping fire: **רק באש היא נאחזת, כעין שלהבת הקשורה בגחלת הבוערת** ("it grips only fire, like a flame bound to a burning coal", line 48).

**The soul is never silent; we are simply deaf to it.** Line 84: it trembles and cries out over the blows we give it, but we hear it the way a sleeper stung by a fly dreams, as a merchant dreaming that a crate fell on him: **משל לאיש ישן אשר עקץ אותו זבוב במצחו**. The soul's remorse is felt as a vague unease and mistaken for hunger: **ויחשוב שרעב הוא, צמא** (line 85). The soul's song is also unheard. If a person heard **את שירת נפשו לד׳** ("his soul's song to God"), joined by his *ruach*, *neshamah*, *chayah* and *yechidah*, all Israel, the angels and all creation, he would sanctify everything (line 86).

**The feel of the soul being revealed: shaking, feathers, wings.** The whole aim is to make the soul tremble a little, **לזעזע קצוות נוצותי׳ בבחירתו** ("to stir the tips of its feathers at will", line 31). The soul offers a wing that must be caught (lines 274, 276). One test is whether "even one feather of your soul's wings stirred" (line 159).

**Two levels of heat.** - *Hisragshus* (being moved): you still want the world as well. - *Hislahavus* (being aflame): for the moment the world is repulsive, there is one longing, and you cannot stop. **אי אפשר לך לחדול מן התרגשותך זו ולהתפלל בקרירות** ("you cannot stop this feeling and pray coldly", line 51). His everyday example is the shofar, Kol Nidrei and Ne'ilah. - In full *hislahavus* the faculties dissolve. You need neither food nor sleep, and **כל עצמו מתבטל** ("his whole self is annulled", line 49).

**Real feeling has an unmistakable interior texture.** - Real fervour will not let you stop. If you stop your movements, **כאילו סיר נפוח וים נרגז בקרבך** ("like a boiling pot and a raging sea within you", line 145). - Real *hisragshus*, when interrupted, leaves **מין כאב הלב הבא מתוך געגועים ותשוקה אליו ית׳** ("an ache of the heart born of longing for Him", line 146), like missing parents or children who have travelled away. - A real prayer is uneven: **ואם הי׳ לך הרים ועמקים, אורות וצללים, עליות וירידות, יכול להיות שבאמת נתרגשת** ("if you had mountains and valleys, lights and shadows, rises and falls, you may truly have been moved", line 147).

**Delight as a foretaste of your own heaven.** The pleasure felt in worship is **הארת עולמך של עוה״ע** ("the light of your world-to-come", line 57), reading "you will see your world in your lifetime". Me'or VaShemesh: in prayer the soul tastes **מעט עריבות, נעימות מאור עליון** ("a little sweetness, the pleasantness of a supernal light", line 171). The soul then despises the body's bond, and even the body is refined.

**The dark room at se'udah shelishis** (lines 106-108). Darkness is where God hides (**ד׳ מסתתר בחושך**). After a whole day of searching you have found the One your soul loves: **מתקרבת היא אליו ונמסה בקדושתו** ("it draws near Him and melts in His holiness"). The room is full of the heavenly retinue, and you push through toward the holy of holies. Then two darknesses wrestle in you, the pure darkness of heaven and the darkness of the coming week: **התרי גוונא של חשוכא נאבקים עתה בך בשלש סעודות**. His image for this is a prince about to be dragged to prison, pressing close to his father in the last moment, **אוחז ומחבק, מתענג ומתגעגע** ("holding and embracing, delighting and longing", line 108). Even when you do not notice it, **גם לבך ומוחך מתנענעים ומזדעזעים מקול רעש הגלגל** ("your heart and mind shake from the thunder of the chariot wheels", line 105).

**The words turning into reality.** When thought and feeling are strong, "every knee shall bow" is no longer a translated phrase. You feel that you and the whole universe are bowing. **רק דיבוריך, מחשבתך והרגשותיך משתלשלות מתרקמות ונעשות לממש ואת ממשן אתה מרגיש עד כדי לראותן** ("your words, thought and feelings descend, weave themselves, and become real, and you feel their reality to the point of seeing it", line 178). He is explicit that this is **לא שתראה לפניך דמות של מלאכים** ("not that you see a figure of angels").

**Melody as the soul's own voice** (lines 280-282). The ups and downs of a tune are the soul's journey engraved in sound: **את נפשך נושא הניגון במעיו לשפכה ולקרבה לד׳** ("the melody carries your soul in its belly to pour it out and bring it near God"). As feeling rises, **ויותר תצא נפשך מנרתיקה לעוף אל על** ("your soul leaves its sheath to fly upward"). Sometimes there are no words and no request, only a child's clinging: **מין התרפקות היא, כילד שמתחטא** ("a kind of nestling, like a child fussing"). "Father, father." "What do you want, my son?" "Nothing." Then **רק נפשך מרננת ומשתפכת ומפלטת רק, רבש״ע, רבש״ע**. In joy one weeps and in weeping one is joyful (line 283).

**New thoughts rising unbidden.** After real practice, thoughts and feelings **אשר לא שערת אותן עד כה מעצמן מתחדשות ועולות לך מקרבך** ("that you never imagined rise up from within you of themselves", line 256). Do not wonder where they come from (lines 257, 112). The images come from **נפש המציירת** ("the picturing soul"), the same faculty that makes our dreams, which once formed can produce holy scenes **מבלי שנצטרך אנו לבקש לה מחשבות ומראות** ("without our needing to seek thoughts and sights for it", line 112).

**Relief when the cause is found.** Finding the true cause of a mood gives immediate lightness (line 313). Overcoming a craving gives a "pleasure of victory" whose hidden cause is that a part of your soul was freed from its filthy garment (lines 308-309).

**Seeing with the soul** (lines 335-338). Glimpses "through the lattice" of the essence behind the world: **רק בהראי׳ עם הנפש, בבחי׳ מציץ מן החרכים לפעמים נראה, גם את העצם וגם את המציל** ("only with seeing-with-the-soul, as one peeping through the lattice, we sometimes see both the shadow and what casts it"). Reason cannot afterwards say what was seen, **אבל בנפשנו נדע ונהי׳ בטוח, שבהארת אדון עולם בזיו העולם, הצצנו** ("but in our soul we know and are certain that we glimpsed the light of the Master of the world in the world's radiance"). He also gives its everyday seed. After hours of absorbed learning you feel a distaste on going back to the street, **מרגיש אתה אי שביעת רצון מן העולם** ("dissatisfaction with the world", line 336). Examined, it is not disgust at the world but at the way people use it. This insight, **לא שבשכל חקרת וכן מצאת, רק מעצמך ומנפשך ... בך התנוצצה** ("you did not reason it out; it sparkled in you from your own soul", line 338), is "the beginning of that seeing". The feeling is like waking from sleep: **דומה הוא לאיש אשר נעור משנתו** (line 333).

**After Shema said this way**, **ותרגיש שאחר ק״ש כזו כאיש אחר תהי׳** ("you will feel you are another person after such a Shema", line 436).

## 4. The self and God

The book's architecture matches the owner's thesis closely, although he never states it as a slogan. **Its first step is literally to find yourself, and finding yourself is the doorway to everything else.**

- **The first revelation is one's own soul.** Of the holy ones: **ראשית ההתגלות בקדושי אֵל, היתה נפשם הישראלית** ("the beginning of revelation in the holy ones of God was their own Jewish soul", line 26). Only after that, with the revealed soul, did they become a chariot for the higher revelation. Some never go further, and even that guides them to God: **שיש שנפשו עצמה בהזדככה מאוד תתגלה לאדם ותנהיגהו בכל דרכיו** ("sometimes a person's own soul, greatly refined, reveals itself to him and guides him in all his ways").
- **Estrangement from self is the disease.** **ההסתר והבלתי נרגש גם לעצמו מן המתרחש בקרבו מרחק את האיש מן עצמו אלפי פרסאות** ("the hiddenness, what stirs in him going unfelt even by himself, distances a person from himself by thousands of miles", line 84). Line 84 continues: "he does not know himself".
- **The cure is self-recognition.** **ועליך להשתדל רק להכיר אותך** ("you need only strive to recognise yourself", line 87).
- **Brokenness is the first door to the self.** A young man seized by remorse, seeing himself "as a sick man who knows his illness", has reached **הפתח הראשון אל עצמו ואל נפשו** ("the first opening into himself and into his soul", line 77).
- **Drawing near to your soul is drawing near to holiness.** After overcoming a craving, you feel you have come near to holiness, and truly have. First of all to the holiness within you: **וראשית כל אל הקדושה אשר בך נתקרבת, אל נפשך** ("and first of all you have drawn near to the holiness that is in you, to your soul", line 309).
- **Cleaving to God requires the revealed self.** **רק ע״י עבודת החסידות שהיא בנפש גלוי׳ ... אפשר להתדבק בו ית׳ דבוק ממש** ("only through the service of Chassidus, which is with a revealed soul, can one truly cleave to Him", line 418). The divine indwelling needs it too: **והשראה ממרום מוכרחה לנפש גלוי׳** ("revelation from on high requires a revealed soul", line 52).
- **The revealed self sees God in the world.** With the revealed soul **כבר הבטתנו וראיתנו אחרת תהי׳** ("our gaze and our seeing are already different", line 316), leading to the Baal Shem's "you look at God" (line 317). The very God one sought outside is the hidden essence of the world: **אכן אתה א-ל מסתתר, איך זה בכל היום עליך אני מביט, ולא אראך** ("truly You are a hidden God; how is it that all day I look at You and do not see You", line 334).
- **One's true self is greater than one knows.** **דע כי לא אתה ולא אני יכולים להשיג את גדולתך וקדושתך הרבה של עצמותך** ("know that neither you nor I can grasp the greatness and holiness of your own essence", line 257). The Jew is **כבן מלך האסור בבור האסורים** ("like a king's son chained in a dungeon"). Unknown even to himself, he rises and sings when the rags come off and he hears the king's song. Compare line 382: however high you rise, **לא תגיע עוד לקרסולי גדולתך אשר לך באמת** ("you will not reach the ankles of your true greatness").
- **Simplicity is the self present in its acts, and that is truth.** **כי תמימות היא ממשלת הנפש על האדם ומעשיו, וערמימיות היא חוסר הנפש** ("simplicity is the soul's rule over a person and his acts; cunning is the absence of soul", line 385). God's seal is truth, and truth is "being", a soul present in one's actions (line 387).

**Where he complicates the thesis, and this should not be smoothed.** The movement is not self-discovery ending in self-affirmation. 1. The self one finds is first a wounded, crying soul, and part of finding it is honest shame (lines 117, 123). His picture of the undisciplined ego is **האנכיות שלי ... שוכבת ורובצת היא, ושינת עולם ישנה** ("my 'I-ness' lies crouched and sleeps an eternal sleep", line 123). This *anochiyus* is an obstacle to be woken, not the true self. 2. The goal of revealing the soul is to hand it over. The aim is **לקשרה בקדושתו ולבטל אותה ואת עצמותנו בנשמת ש-ד-י** ("to bind it to His holiness and annul it and our very selves in the breath of the Almighty", line 278). The Shema formula is **הנני משעבד, ומכניע, ומבטל** ("I subjugate, humble and annul", line 425). *Mesirus nefesh* means handing the soul over, part by part over a lifetime (line 418). 3. Self-examination is dangerous because the self is biased (lines 142-143). So finding oneself is never done by the self alone. It needs God's gaze ("no one sees you but God", lines 114, 137), a confidant (line 371), and the group.

So, faithful to him: people come to God by first coming to their own soul. That soul is God's portion. Once revealed, it becomes a vessel for the Shechinah and an eye that sees Him in the world, and its fulfilment is to give itself back.

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

**Forcing feeling backfires.** - **אל יתעקש להתעורר דוקא** ("let him not insist on becoming aroused", line 59). Insisting makes the heart go dead: **כאילו הכל בי כבה** ("as if everything in me went out"). - Enthusiasm is "the essence of essences", but **שבשעת התפילה לא יבקשנה אף לא יתעקש עלי׳** ("during prayer he should neither seek it nor insist on it", line 61). Pray simply and it will come by itself. - The same holds for thoughts: fighting stray thoughts by force makes them stronger (line 310).

**Counterfeit fervour produced by movement.** He describes it with uncomfortable precision (lines 138-139): swaying hard, pulling the tallis down over the face, screwing up the eyes, squeezing out the words. After months of this, **אף כח ההכרה שבך לדעת מה היא התלהבות ומה לא׳ נפגם** ("even your ability to know what enthusiasm is and what it isn't is damaged"). **The test** (line 145): **נסה נא לחדול מן כל תנועתיך** ("try stopping all your movements"). If everything goes quiet and you could chat idly, it was compulsion, not fire. A prayer that felt smoothly aflame from start to finish is suspect (line 147).

**The self-deceiving examiner.** Inner looking has no external test, like baking soda for vinegar (lines 140-142). Bias twists even the signs one uses: **שראשית כל הוא להסתכל על עצמו בחשד פשוט כעל גנב** ("first of all, look at yourself with plain suspicion, as at a thief", line 143). He adds at once that this is for times of examination only, not during worship.

**Looking *in* while believing you look *at*.** One can gaze at a craving from within the craving and think one is observing it (line 300). A child dragged to class answers his teacher while his mind stays in the game. So too one can say "I've thought about it" with just **קצה אבר מן מחשבתו** ("the tip of a limb of his thought", line 301). Rabbi Asher of Stolin: **הם צועקים על היצה״ר עם היצה״ר** ("they cry out against the evil inclination with the evil inclination"). Speaking such words can also replace thinking them (line 303).

**Imagination gone astray.** - Do not hunt for images to fit a feeling. That is intellect making parables, and it kills the feeling (line 182). The goal is **לראות הנפש אשר למעלה מן השכל** ("to see the soul that is above intellect"). - He fences off fantasy: **לא דמיונות מדוחים, ולא הזיות הבל [פאנטאזיעס]** ("not banished delusions, not empty hallucinations, 'fantasies'", line 182). What he wants is the prophetic seeing latent in Jews, **ניצוץ הראי׳ של בני הנביאים** ("the spark of sight of the sons of the prophets"). - To the objection "it is only my imagination" he answers with Tanya ch. 44. Even imagined love is good, because **פנימית נפשו הקדושה מתעוררת באמת לד׳, ורק הגופנית היא המסתרת אותה** ("the inner holy soul truly is aroused toward God; only the physical conceals it", line 179). This is a claim about the soul's reality. It does not license any picture whatever.

**The same feeling can go either way.** - Nervous excitability (*atzbanus*) is the prophetic readiness misused: **ומהתרגשות שהיא הכשרה לעבודה עילאה ... ח״ו שובבות ועצבנות יכול להיעשות** ("from emotion, which is a readiness for high service ... wildness and nervousness can come", line 70). Not everyone moved is a servant of God, just as not every joyful person prophesies. - A tune opens the soul, but a cantor or a gentile musician can open it and then misuse it (line 278). - Drink stirs the heart, and the coarse person uses that for sin (line 276). - Bare emotions without a garment slide into sadness or giddiness if not directed (line 267).

**Sadness is forbidden. Bitterness is required.** **אבל עם כל זה בעצבות לא תשאר** ("but with all this, do not remain in sadness", line 149). **לדאוג ולהתמרמר, להתחזק ולשמוח בד׳ זה כלל גדול בעבודת החסידות** ("to worry and be bitter, to strengthen oneself and rejoice in God: this is a great rule in Chassidus"). The death meditation: **שלא להעציב אותך ח״ו באנו** ("we have not come to sadden you", line 243).

**Addiction to rebuke.** Some enjoy harsh *mussar* the way others enjoy burning liquor or scalding baths, **שמזה ששורף להם ... דוקא הם מתענגים** (line 160), and change nothing.

**The hope for effortless technique.** **טעות נורא ומסוכן הוא טועה** ("a terrible and dangerous error", line 162). Stratagems only help effort succeed. They also wear out and must be changed, which is why Shema is limited to three times a week (lines 161, 299, 436).

**Projection.** Dwelling with righteous disgust on another person's filth is your own craving in disguise. **דע לך כי כל המחשבות שאתה חושב עתה על זולתך שלך הן** ("know that all the thoughts you are now thinking about another are your own", line 156).

**Portraying the evil inclination as mighty makes students limp** (line 250). It is a weak robber (line 249).

**Mind without heart fails in old age.** Those who only restrained themselves by knowledge collapse when strength fades (line 73).

**Mood from the body.** A good lunch can harden the heart against uncomfortable thoughts (lines 241, 313). Check the body before trusting a mood.

**Pride in the group.** The fellowship must not be publicised, because work that reveals the soul **מתנגדת היא לשאון ופרסום** ("is opposed to noise and publicity", line 372). There are no officers (line 355). The liar cannot even repent, because his repentance is also a lie (line 359).

## 6. Comparison with Chabad *hisbonenus*

(Against `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`: contemplation working in Binah, extension and breadth, duration, mind ruling heart, and the obstacles of coldness, foreign thoughts and self-awareness in the state.)

The Rebbe engages the Alter Rebbe directly in this book: the Siddur, Torah Or/Likkutei Torah, Iggeres HaTeshuvah, Iggeres HaKodesh, and Tanya chs. 27 and 44. So several points below are his own stated positions relative to Chabad, not an outside comparison.

## Where they agree

- **Mind gives birth to feeling.** He grounds this kabbalistically: Tevunah is "mother of the children", and human thought is Malchus of Tevunah, so the traits are born from and fed by thought (line 93). Feelings without a platform of thought "are gathered in quickly".
- **Extension and duration.** His "widen" (*harchev*), "lengthen" (*ha'arech*) and "fill it with many different details" (lines 100-101) is the Chabad *orech* and *rochav*. A passing thought does nothing (lines 97, 175, 228).
- **Do not examine yourself in the middle of worship** (line 148). This agrees with Chabad's warning about "being aware of yourself in the state". He goes further and explains why: looking at a state dims it (lines 293-295).
- **Foreign thoughts are a lifelong task, not a problem to be solved once.** He cites Tanya ch. 27 and the Baal Shem story (lines 80-81).
- **Bring the arousal down into action.** Clothe the stirring in a mitzvah or learning (line 270). The Shema declaration is meant to make the day's service "rise well" (line 420).
- **Bitterness yes, sadness no.** He cites the Alter Rebbe's Iggeres HaTeshuvah ch. 11 on certain forgiveness (line 149).
- **Even flawed arousal is real** (Tanya ch. 44, line 179).

## Where he differs (differences kept)

1. **The object: not deep ideas but simple, broad, pictured scenes.** He explicitly declines the intellectual route for this stage: **לא בשכליות ועמקיות תחשוב אז רק במחשבה פשוטה ורחבה** (line 94). He then draws a distinction taken from the Alter Rebbe himself. When the *mochin* (Chochmah and Binah in their place) are revealed, the emotions are quieted and absorbed. *Machshavah*, "the lowest level of intellect, the back side of intellect", widens and enlarges the emotions instead (line 93). Chabad contemplation seeks *mochin* that reach the heart. He deliberately works one level lower, with *machshavah*, because his goal is to get the emotion moving at all. His objects are Shabbos in the dark, the Exodus as a child experienced it, a deathbed, the Temple. They are narrative, sensory and personal. Chabad contemplates the order of descent and divine unity. Only in Ch. 10 does he approach Chabad-style content (the world has no essence apart from divine light, quoting Likkutei Torah Tavo, line 324). Even there the aim is "seeing with the soul", not understanding. He says of the Baal Shem's teaching: **כי אין זאת ראי׳ רק הבנה** ("that would not be seeing, only understanding", line 317).
2. **Imagination is central, not suspect.** Thought and imagination are "one kind" (line 99). One must see things "as if before your very eyes" (lines 102, 193), and even picture a teacher's face (line 103). The goal is to reveal "the picturing soul" (line 112) and a spark of prophetic sight (line 182). The Chabad ontology as summarised has no such faculty in its technique axis.
3. **The order: start from feeling already present, then give it thought.** Chabad moves from contemplation to arousal. His first move often runs the other way. Notice what is already stirring, then give it a platform of thought (lines 87-90, 184, 274). Contemplation often serves an existing feeling rather than producing one.
4. **The target is *hisragshus*, the capacity to be moved, trained like a muscle.** The stated aim is to become an *ish misragesh* who can be moved at will (lines 64, 259). Chabad's layered states (arousal from the self, from the essence, *bittul*) are not his categories here. His scale is *hisragshus* and then *hislahavus*, measured by whether you can stop (line 51).
5. **Tools Chabad's method does not use as technique here:** melody as a key to the soul (lines 277-284), drink (line 276), bodily force (line 54), stamping a foot (line 250), turning to the wall (line 285), Psalms matched to a mood (line 274), spontaneous Yiddish speech (line 275), and head movements in Shema (line 435).
6. **Quieting and observing.** His "look at, not in" and his calm watching of thoughts in prayer (lines 293, 310-311) are a distinct, almost phenomenological technique. Chabad's prescription for intruding thoughts, as recorded in the ontology, is "return without commentary". His is "stop, calm yourself, and watch them until they thin out as if swept with a broom". Precision matters here: the word *hashkatah* does not appear in this book. What appears is *harge'a atzmecha* / *beruhig dich* ("calm yourself") and "with a restful heart, not with agitation or excessive effort" (line 310). This is the seed of the practice, not its later full form.
7. **Emotion is taken as the soul's own voice, not only as the product of understanding.** For him emotion *is* "the first revelation of the soul" (line 44). The soul is always moved; only we fail to hear it (line 84). This is a psychology of listening rather than of producing.
8. **Explicitly for a "low generation", and graded.** Not deep contemplation but "grasping a corner" of high service (line 172).
9. **The group as method.** Chevraya, a confidant, shared *se'udah shelishis*, and slow applied study (lines 341-374), citing Me'or VaShemesh that solitude works "only for its moment" (line 345).

## A shared tension worth keeping

Both schools worry about self-watching during the state. Chabad treats it as an obstacle to the state. He turns the same mechanism into a tool against bad states, which suggests the two positions are one mechanism seen from opposite sides. Watching dissolves whatever it is aimed at, so do not watch holy feeling, and do watch craving. This is my inference from his lines 148 and 293. He does not say it about Chabad.

## 7. For the guide (faithful to him)

1. "Before we try to feel anything new, let's notice what is already moving in you. It's all already inside you" (line 87). Start with the clearest, and preferably the most joyful, feeling rather than the faintest or the most painful (line 89).
2. When a feeling appears, don't analyse it and don't hunt for images. Linger in it, say one simple sentence slowly, such as "I stand in awe of God, so much awe", and repeat it only while it keeps growing. Think of God, not of the words (lines 110, 184).
3. Outside the moment of prayer, build the picture in advance: one scene, many concrete details, as if you were there. Then at prayer a brief recall is enough (lines 101, 194, 228).
4. If feeling won't come, don't force it or squeeze it out. Pray simply, perhaps aloud or with a quiet hum, and let it arrive by itself (lines 59, 61, 284).
5. If thoughts are rushing, stop, sit or lean, calm yourself, and simply watch the thoughts, without pushing, until they thin out. For a craving, look *at* it ("so this is how much I want it") rather than *into* it, even if only for twenty seconds (lines 293, 302, 310-311).
6. If a heavy mood has no name, ask "since when?" and look for the moment it began. If you find it, the heart lightens at once. If you don't, it may be your soul crying, so turn to the wall and say a psalm as if David wrote it for you, then speak in your own words (lines 274, 285, 313).
7. Keep examination for a separate time. During prayer, only joy and longing. Afterwards, honest brokenness. The rest of the day, humble joy, because your faults are the faults of a king's son (lines 148, 412-415).
8. Clothe what you felt in something done: a mitzvah, some learning, a resolution with a real safeguard. And find one friend to tell it to (lines 270, 273, 371).

*Summary* 1. This whole short book is a graded method for becoming an *ish misragesh*, "a person who is moved". It rests on the thesis that feeling is the first revelation of the soul, and a revealed soul is what receives God (lines 26, 44, 52). 2. The method is to notice what already stirs, give it a platform of strong, pictured thought built in advance, and clothe it in learning or a mitzvah. Tools include se'udah shelishis in the dark, the Exodus, a deathbed, psalms matched to mood, free speech, melody, drink and a fellowship. 3. The key original technique is to look *at* a stirring rather than *in* it, which dims it, plus calm watching of stray thoughts until they thin "as with a broom" (lines 293, 310-311). This is the seed of hashkatah, though the word does not appear. 4. Self and God: finding one's own hidden soul is explicitly the first door (lines 26, 77, 84, 87, 309), and the revealed self then cleaves to God and sees Him in the world (lines 316-335). Its fulfilment is to give itself back (lines 278, 425). 5. Compared with Chabad, he agrees on thought birthing feeling, extension and not self-watching in prayer. He differs sharply by choosing simple imaginative *machshavah* over deep *mochin*, citing the Alter Rebbe's own distinction (line 93), and by making imagination, melody and quieting central.
