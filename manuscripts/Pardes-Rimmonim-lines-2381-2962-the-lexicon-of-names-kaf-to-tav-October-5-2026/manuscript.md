# Pardes Rimmonim, lines 2381-2962: the lexicon of names, kaf to tav

This is the second half of Gate 23, the dictionary of God's names, from kaf to tav. Most entries are short and technical. But in the second half the Ramak keeps stopping to teach, and some of his clearest lines on the unity and on the inner life sit here. He writes about speech as the pattern for how anything comes to be. He calls Binah a question with no answer. He says the moon is poor and still not dark, and that the heart's cry tears up a decree. He says the edge of holiness is drawn near, not only burned. His last word on return is that everything comes back to its place, each to each.

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**The names are clothes on the Infinite.** "Shem" (name) "means in truth a garment: as the four-letter name is a garment to the sefirot, so all the sefirot are a garment to Ein Sof" (כל הספי' כלם לבוש אל הא"ס, line 2875). Each layer is shell to the one above and core to the one below: "this is a shell to that, and that is the brain to this" (דא קליפה לדא ודא מוחא לדא, line 2457). So nothing in the whole dictionary names a second thing next to God. Every name is a garment that He wears.

**Waters and waters: do not say two.** R. Akiva warned the four who entered the Orchard: "when you reach the stones of pure marble, do not say 'water, water'". The Ramak explains that the upper and lower waters are two yuds that are one in Atzilut, "and there is no separation between water and water, for all is one union" (ולית הפרשה בין מים למים דכלא יחודא חדא, line 2483). "There is no separating except in the place of impurity" (אין הבדלה אלא במקום הטומא' כדכתיב להבדיל בין הטמא ובין הטהור, line 2484). Dividing is only needed where there is something to keep out. Where there is only God, splitting Him in two is the danger.

**Speech is the pattern.** "Speech is the parable for the comings-to-be in the upper worlds" (כי הדבור הוא המשל אל ההויות בעולמות העליונים, line 2700). In Chochmah things are so hidden that "between them and nothing there is only a hair". In Binah they come out as "a voice that is not heard" (קול דלא אשתמע, line 2700), a sound with no words yet. Only in Malchut are the letters cut and the words spoken. From the upper mouth "comes out voice alone, mere breath, with no cutting of words" (ומהפה העליון יוצא קול לבד, line 2441). This is the picture the Tanya will build its Gate of Unity on (see §6).

**What? The most revealed is still a question.** Malchut is called "Mah" (What?): "though she is more revealed than all, she stands for the question, what did you see, what did you know" (ואע"ג דאתגלייא יתיר מכלא קיימא לשאלא מה חמית מה ידעת, line 2456). Binah is Tohu, "a thing with no hold, and so it is called Who, a word of asking"; "because it is a question with no answer" (ומפני שהיא שאלה בלא תשובה, line 2921). Even the lowest level of God, the one we meet, is a question to us. Higher up the question has no answer at all.

**No borders above.** Binah is "inheritance without borders" (nachalah bli metzarim). The Ramak's own reason: "a thing that is grasped has borders" (והדבר המושג יש לו מצרים, line 2584), and from Binah up there is no grasp at all, so no border. And yet in Malchut the limit is what reveals: she is called "number of days" because "from the side of the border and the count, it is revealed" (ומצד הגבול והחשבון מתגלה, line 2509). The hidden cannot be known without a limit, and what is past every limit cannot be known at all. The Ramak holds both.

**He is the place of the world.** Tiferet is "Makom" (Place) because "He is the place of the world" (הוא מקומו של עולם, line 2531), and the world is not His place. This is the same line as in the first half (line 2060), now said of the middle line.

**The one light.** Sun and moon are not two lights: "the light of the moon and the light of the sun are all one light" (כי אור הלבנה ואור החמה הכל אור אחד, line 2445). The only difference is that one takes straight from Binah and the other takes through a middle.

**A king only on his throne, a whole only with the lower half.** The Tikkunim: "He is not called king there until He rides" on Knesset Yisrael (לאו אתקרי תמן מלך עד דרכיב, line 2499). "A king without a queen is not a king" (ומלכא בלא מטרוניתא לאו מלכא איהו, line 2927). Jacob is "whole above, whole below" (שלים לעילא שלים לתתא, line 2873). God's unity, for the Ramak, is not complete in the heights alone. It is complete when the lowest level is joined to it.

**Join the middle and the whole joins.** Tzeror haChayim, the bundle of life: the Shechinah is so called "because in her all is bound" (בגין דבה אתקשר כלא, line 2748), and R. Abba says "all of it becomes one crown" (כולא אתעביד חד עטרא, line 2747). The Ramak reads it: her whole aim is to join Tiferet, and then the rest of Atzilut joins her by itself. Unity has a center. Hold the center and the rest comes.

## 3. States of consciousness

**Too bright to see (RK-S19, proposed).** The Ramak separates two kinds of not-seeing. "Closed eyes" is darkness: the lights do not shine in Malchut. But the "hidden vision" of Binah is "hiddenness of the sight from little grasp because of too much brightness" (להעלם המראה במיעוט ההשגה בה לרוב הבהירות, line 2540). And united, the Shechinah is "vision awake", with open eyes; not united, "vision in a dream" (בסתימו דעיינין אתקריאת מראה בחלום ובפתיחו דעיינין איהו מראה בהקיץ, line 2539). Inside: I cannot see Him, and I know it is not because it is dark. It is too much light for me. How to enter: when understanding fails, ask whether this is the dark of closed eyes (then work to open them) or the dazzle of too much (then stay and stop reaching). Near S35 (The edge of knowing) and S36 (Faith above knowing). What is new is how you tell the two blanks apart.

**Poor and not dark (RK-S20, proposed).** Malchut and Yesod are "poor, having nothing of their own". People object: then Malchut is nothing, and the sefirot are nine. The Ramak answers with the moon. It has no light of its own, and it is not removed from the world when it wanes. "It stays like the sapphire stone" (ותשאר כמו האבן ספיר, line 2665), which shines when the sun strikes it and is not wiped out when it does not. Inside: nothing I have is mine, it is all received, and I am not cancelled. When the light comes I shine, and when it goes I am still here. How to enter: say plainly, "I have nothing of my own", and then notice that you are still standing, a stone ready to catch the next ray. Near S58 (A ray in the sun) and S11 (The verdict: I'm my mistakes). It answers S11's lie. Being empty of your own light does not mean you do not exist.

**Everything coming home (RK-S21, proposed).** The last entry of the gate is teshuvah (return). The Zohar: when the Mother rests in joy over the children, "what was closed returns to its place, and all return one to one, and each and every one is blessed" (ומה דהוה סגיר תב לאתריה וכלהו תבין חד לחד ומתברכין כל חד וחד, line 2959), "and then it is called complete return". Inside: this is not me alone coming back. Everything that was out of place is sliding home, and I am one of the pieces. How to enter: after your own return, widen the circle. Picture each part of your life, each person you hurt or who hurt you, going back to its place. Near S48 (Returning) and S77 (The lower return). What is new is that return is shared: "one to one".

**Sadness without the face (named, not new).** "Atzvut is sadness and anger with no light of the face, when the moon is dark and blessings are not found" (עצבון עציבו ורוגזא בלא נהירו דאנפין, line 2670), and it is "the side of the other spirit that holds back blessing from the world". This is the Ramak's own description of the state the Tanya fights throughout. It ties to S15 (Angry) and S73 (Exile inside). His point is that sadness shuts the channel. It is not humility.

**Delight when all is bound (named).** Eden, the Zohar says, is when "all the limbs sit in one bond, in the delight of longing" (בעידונא דתיאובתא, line 2636). Joy follows the same shape: "the Mother sits in complete gladness" (אימא בחדוותא שלימתא יתבא, line 2878). Delight here is not a thrill. It is what being gathered into one feels like. Ties to S53 (Glad) and S50 (Joy and bittul together).

**Dawn is dark (named).** "Shachar" (dawn) is from "shachor" (black): "though it is white, compared to Chesed it is black" (אע"פ שהוא לבן, בערך החסד שחור הוא, line 2860). The first light always looks dim beside the full day. A beginner's light should not be judged by noon.

**The kiss.** "Kisses are the cleaving of spirit to spirit" (והנשיקות הם דביקות רוחא ברוחא, line 2598). Only after the kiss is she called a rose (באינון נשיקין אקרי שושנה, line 2855). Near S43 (Love like fire). The Ramak keeps this in the upper union. For the guide it names the highest closeness in prayer: breath meeting breath.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**The heart's cry (RK-T20, proposed).** Three words for calling out: shav'ah (crying out) in Malchut, anachah (the groan) in Tiferet, and tza'akah (the cry) in Binah. "The cry is greatest, for the cry is in the heart" (שצעקה היא בלב, line 2743). "Great is the cry, for it tears up a person's decree" (גדולה צעקה שקורעת גזר דינו של אדם, line 2743), and in it "there is a clothing of mercy to turn all of them to mercy". With it go the gates of tears: "there are gates of tears" (ויש שערי דמעה, line 2898), and the Ramak adds that there is no water libation greater than this. What it is for: when words have run out and judgment presses. How: not louder words. Let the call come from the heart with no words, and if tears come, let them be your libation. Guard: it is a cry to Him, not a scene before people.

**Crown Him in all ten (RK-T21, proposed).** The Ramak gives his own habit: "in our way in this book, when we mention Ein Sof, we say afterward 'King, King of kings of kings', to unite Him in His ten sefirot and to crown Him in them" (ליחדו בעשר ספירותיו ולהמליכו בהם, line 2500). With the bundle of life (line 2747): join the center and the rest joins by itself. What it is for: so that naming God never names Him apart from His world. How: whenever you name the Infinite, add at once a short phrase of kingship ("King over all"), and mean by it that He fills every level you know. Guard: said plainly, with no picturing of sefirot.

**Draw the rind near (RK-T22, proposed).** The Ramak separates milah (cutting the foreskin, removing the shell) from priah (the second step). Priah is "the drawing near of the foreskin to holiness" (הוא קירוב הערלה אל הקדושה, line 2717). It is like the seventy bulls of Sukkot, which come back to holiness by being brought near, and it is done from Chesed: "the left should always push away and the right draw near" (לעולם תהא שמאל דוחה וימין מקרבת, line 2717). What it is for: the coarse parts of a person that are not sins. Hunger, restlessness, stubbornness. How: do not only fight them. Bring them close to a mitzvah (eat with a blessing, spend restlessness walking to help someone) so that they return to holiness. Guard: real sin is cut, not drawn near. This step belongs to the right hand, after the left has done its work. Near S75 (Weekday: sorting).

**Holiness first, then union.** On the blessing "who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us": "holiness is the preparation for union" (הקדושה היא הכנה אל היחוד, line 2527), like betrothal before marriage. And the whole point of a mitzvah "is to unite her with her Husband and not to separate her" (עקרה מצוה בתורה לייחדה עם בעלה ושלא להפרידה, line 2525). So every blessing has two beats: I set myself apart, then I join. This ties to "arrive" and "binding".

**Silence at the top.** Tikku, the Talmud's "let it stand", is "the place of silence, for there is no thinking there" (שהוא מקום השתיקה כי אין להרהר, line 2933): "be silent, so it rose in thought". Oil, the flow from Chochmah, "comes in secret, in a whisper, always from the side of thought" (שמן דאיהו בחשאי בלחישא תדיר אתי מסטרא דמחשבה, line 2888). The still small voice is the voice in Malchut "that is standing in secret" (שהיא עמידה בחשאי, line 2759), which is the silent Amidah. These fit the "edge" and "quieting" techniques already on the map. At the top of a meditation, stop asking. Keep the prayer that follows as a whisper.

**The good hour.** Time has fourteen good and fourteen bad "times". So one should say "a good hour", and "take care not to say 'it will be in a bad hour', for it looks like cursing oneself" (שלא יאמר בשעה רעה יהיה ח"ו שנראה כמקלל עצמו, line 2694). "There is no judgment below unless there is one above" (כי אין דין למטה, אם לא שיהיה מלמעלה, line 2694). It is a small guard of the mouth, and it belongs with "lips".

**The limb and the will.** Ratzon (will) flows from Keter, and the sefirot take it "as a man clothes himself in will when he eats" (כאדם המתלבש ברצון בעת אכילתו, line 2825). Receiving from above feels like appetite satisfied. Eat and receive the same way, wanting it.

**A servant, a lamp.** "From his side a person must make himself as a servant" (ומצדו צריך האדם לעשות עצמו כעבד, line 2629). And the wick is called "lamp" only when it is "a vessel ready to receive the upper light" (line 2595). Make yourself the vessel. The light is not yours to make.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why do I feel far from God?** "Why, Lord, do You stand far off?": Tiferet has risen to Chochmah, "and Malchut is left alone" (ונשארה המלכות גלמודה, line 2812). Distance is not always about our sin. Sometimes He has gone up to where we cannot follow for now. And "far" is Chochmah's own name, "because of how little it is grasped". The feeling of distance can be the feeling of height.

**What does sin do, and what does forgiveness do?** "Sin damages and darkens the light of the sefirah" (כי החטא יפגום ויחשיך אור הספי', line 2616), and forgiveness is when the Forgiver "strengthens His light and repairs and lights up that place". Forgiveness is not only erasing a debt. It is light put back where it was dimmed.

**Is there evil in God?** No. The Zohar calls Malchut "the tree of knowledge of good and evil", "and one must not be tempted to say that there is evil in Malchut" (ואין להתפתות ולומר שבמלכות יש בה רע, line 2669). Evil is below her, not in her. And the flow is held back not by stinginess but by our deeds: Malchut, full, "gives only little by little" (אינה משפעת אלא מעט מעט, line 2397), because of how little merit is below.

**Must even the worst be fed?** The altar teaches that Malchut feeds everyone, even the other side: "she gives them from the dregs of the wine and the lees" (נותן להם משמרי היין ומהעכירות, line 2465). Nothing exists without some life from her. This is the root of the later teaching (in the Tanya too) that even kelipah lives from a thin thread of holiness.

**How do I return, if I don't understand?** "There is no return unless Binah comes first, that the person understand" (שאין תשובה אם לא תקדם הבינה שיבין האדם, line 2961). Return starts with seeing clearly, not with feeling bad. And it does not end with you: "each and every one is blessed" (line 2959).

**What heals?** The apple: "as the apple is healing for all, so the Holy One is healing for all" (מה תפוח הוא אסוותא לכולא אוף הקב"ה אסוותא לכלא, line 2951). It has color, scent and taste, as He has color (the traits), a fine scent (from Keter) and sweetness (from Chochmah and Binah). Healing here means being joined to what is whole.

**Can I ask even if I don't deserve it?** Tachanah (plea) is from "chanun" (gracious): "the poor man speaks pleas" (תחנונים ידבר רש, line 2930), and it is answered "even though he is not worthy", from Binah, "where there is a free gift". You do not have to earn the right to ask.

**What does Torah do?** "Torah is not without wisdom, and wisdom is not without Torah" (לאו אורייתא בלא חכמתא ולאו חכמתא בלא אורייתא, line 2927). And it is called Torah "because it teaches and reveals what was hidden". Each level shows the one above it. The Torah is how the hidden reaches us.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Speech and the Gate of Unity.** The Ramak's line 2700 is the frame of Sha'ar HaYichud VeHa'Emunah. Being comes out of hiding the way speech comes out of thought: first a silent voice, then cut letters. The Tanya takes the Baal Shem Tov's "Your word stands in the heavens": the letters of "let there be a firmament" "stand and remain forever within the firmament" (הן נצבות ועומדות לעולם בתוך רקיע השמים, Tanya line 785). The Ramak gives the ladder of speech. The Tanya adds that the speech never stops, and if it stopped for a moment the world would be nothing. The Ramak's picture is emanation. The Tanya's picture is constant creation. It is the same parable pushed one step further.
- **Teshuvah as "tashuv heh".** The Ramak ends the gate with "return, heh" (תשוב ה' כי שבה ה' אל מקומה, line 2961) and with Binah as the upper return. The Tanya builds Iggeret HaTeshuvah on exactly this: "tashuv heh, the lower heh, lower return; the upper heh, upper return" (תשוב ה׳ ה׳ תתאה תשובה תתאה, Tanya line 920). It also uses the Ramak's image of Binah as the mother over the nest: "Binah is upper return, and the mother rests on the chicks" (דבינה איהי תשובה עילאה והאם רובצת על האפרוחים, Tanya line 979; the Ramak, line 2631). This is full agreement. The Tanya turns the map into a practice: upper return is Torah learned in awe and love.
- **Garments and tzimtzum.** "All the sefirot are a garment to Ein Sof" (line 2875) is close to the Tanya's language of garments. But for the Ramak the garment is real and lasting, and the unity is that it is His garment. For the Tanya the garment hides Him only from our side, while from His side the world is nothing. The Ramak does not say that here. He says the names are His, not that they are nothing.
- **Sadness.** Line 2670 (atzvut belongs to the other side and stops blessing) is the Ramak's version of the Tanya's war on sadness (chapters 26-31). Both agree it is not humility. The Tanya makes it a daily discipline.
- **Drawing the rind near.** Priah (line 2717) is an early form of the Chassidic idea of raising sparks from the permitted world (kelipat nogah). The Tanya's version: the permitted is raised when used for Heaven. The Ramak supplies the "right hand draws near".
