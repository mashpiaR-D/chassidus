## The word is still being said (continuous creation)

- **What it says:** The Besht reads Psalms 119:89, "Forever, G-d, Your word stands in the heavens," as a statement about right now. The words "Let there be a sky" were not said once and finished. They are still standing inside the sky, and they are what keeps it there. His reason is the difference between two kinds of making. A human craftsman makes a vessel out of silver that already existed, so when he walks away the vessel stays. G-d made the world from nothing. If His giving of life stopped for one moment, everything would go back to the nothing it was before. The Maggid says the same: the power of the maker stays inside the made thing, and "through the power of this speech it stands and has existence."
- **What it adds:** This is the root of all the other angles. It moves "being held" from a comforting idea to a description of how anything exists at all. You are not held the way a baby is held after being born. You are held the way a sound is held by the one speaking it: stop speaking and there is no sound.
- **The picture:** The silversmith and his vessel (BST, Bereshit 50 and 51). The vessel does not need the smith; the world does need its Maker. Where it breaks: the mashal teaches by contrast. We picture G-d as the smith and then correct the picture, because in the case of G-d there is no leftover silver that could keep the thing going without Him.
- **How the source says to realize it:** No set practice in these passages. The Besht's teaching works by the contrast itself: look at a made thing, notice that it does not need its maker, and then say to yourself that you and the world are not like that. **(ours)** A person can do this slowly with any object on the table.
- **From inside:** A quiet shock, then a softening. The ground under you is not solid in itself; it is being given. Many people feel less alone, since the Giver is present in the giving at this very moment.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "If I stop pushing, everything falls apart." After: "I'm not what keeps things existing. That's being done right now, for me too."
- **Sources:** - BST line 189 (Bereshit 50, from Heichal HaBracha): "שאם אומן עושה כלי אזי הכלי אינו צריך לאומן" - BST line 190 (Bereshit 51, from Be'er Mayim Chaim): "אם יסתלק חלילה השגחת הבורא מהם, אף על רגע כמימריה" and "היו כל העולמות כמו שהיו קודם בריאתן והיו לאפם ואין ממש" - MDL line 64 (Maggid Devarav leYaakov 17:1): "וע"י כח הדיבור הזה הוא עומד ויש לו קיום" - Note: BST line 187 gives the same teaching but is copied from Tanya, Shaar HaYichud (Chabad), so it is left to the Chabad file.

## The small things too (providence down to a coin)

- **What it says:** The Besht explains a Talmud line: what counts as "suffering" that atones? A man reaches into his purse for a gold coin and pulls out a smaller one. How can that little annoyance count? Because a person who believes in individual providence, hashgacha pratis (G-d's personal attention to each detail), knows that "all the events of a person come from Him, whether a small thing or a great one." So even the wrong coin is a message from the King, and when he takes it that way and turns to G-d, it counts. The person who says "my strength and my own hand did this" has, in the Besht's words, pushed the King's rule under a cover. In the days of Mashiach that cover will be removed and His providence will be seen "in every tiny detail." Tzava'as HaRivash: "even the lightest of light things that happens in the world, all is from His providence." R. Levi Yitzchak: at every hour one must believe "there is not one thing in which His providence is not present," even when it looks like nature.
- **What it adds:** It brings being held down to the scale of a normal day. Not only the big events. The coin, the missed bus, the found object.
- **The picture:** The wrong coin in the purse (KST 1:36). A second picture from R. Yaakov Yosef's teacher: you find a coin where many people walked, and no one else saw it. Think: He covered their eyes so that you would find it. Then, the teaching says, do not focus on the coin but on His love and attention to you (KP, Tzav 72). Where it breaks: the wrong coin passage is about atonement and sin, which can make providence sound like a system of fines. The found coin passage balances it: the same attention is love.
- **How the source says to realize it:** When something small goes against you, say it came from Him, turn your thought to Him, and let that be enough (KST). When something small goes for you, look past the object to the love behind it, and let it warm you toward Him more than others (KP). R. Levi Yitzchak ties this to Purim: reading the Megillah "sets into our thoughts" that everything is from Him and not from nature, and whoever keeps this thought always has a clear, awake mind on Pesach night.
- **From inside:** The day stops being random. Small frictions feel less like an attack and more like being addressed.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "Of course that went wrong. Nothing goes my way." After: "Even that small thing came from Him. So I'm not alone in it."
- **Sources:** - KST line 50 (Keter Shem Tov 1:36): "הושיט ידו לארנקו ליטול דינר זהב ונטל מטבע פחותה"; "המאמין בהשגחה פרטית של הש"י ויודע שכל מאורעותיו של האדם הם ממנו ית' הן דבר קטן או גדול" - KST line 514 (Keter Shem Tov 2:60): "הכל בהשגחה פרטית ממנו ית'" - TZR line 153 (Tzava'as HaRivash 84:2): "ואפילו דבר קל שבקלים שנעשה בעולם הכל הוא מהשגחתו ית'" - KP line 125 (Ketonet Pasim, Tzav 72): "שסימא עין חבירו שלא ימצאו כי אם הוא דוקא"; "לא יתן לב על עצם המציאה רק על אהבתו יתברך והשגחתו עליו" - KL line 1445 (Kedushat Levi, Rules of Miracles 23): "צריכין להאמין בכל עת ושעה שאין לך שום דבר ודבר שאין בו השגחתו יתברך שמו"

## The leaf, and how much of it you receive

- **What it says:** The Radziner defends the Chassidic belief that "even the falling of a leaf from the tree" is by providence, and squares it with the Rambam, who seems to deny this. His answer: "The truth is that from G-d's side everything is by providence, and only from the side of the creature is providence received according to its vessel." The Rambam's point, as he reads it, is that the person whose mind never leaves G-d has providence on him at all times; when his mind turns away, he receives less of it (not none).
- **What it adds:** It answers the objection "then why do I feel unheld?" The holding is total from His side. What varies is how much of it reaches your awareness and your life, and that is tied to where your mind is. This links being held to the next angles about thought.
- **The picture:** The leaf falling from the tree. Note: the famous story of the Besht and the leaf turned over by the wind is not in this library. What is here is the Radziner's use of the falling leaf as the test case, taken from the Rambam's Guide 3:17. Where the picture breaks: a leaf has no mind, so it cannot show the second half of the teaching, about receiving more by turning toward Him.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Keep the mind on G-d. The Radziner cites the Zohar: He "is known and cleaved to according to what one measures in his heart."
- **From inside:** Relief that nothing has been withdrawn, and a gentle push: the door is on my side.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "Maybe G-d looks after big things, not someone like me." After: "He's holding all of it, me too. I can open up to more of it."
- **Sources:** - RAD line 79 (Introduction to Beit Yaakov 16:1): "אפילו נפילת העלה מן האילן" - RAD line 80 (16:2): "והאיש השלם בהשגתו אשר לא יסור שכלו מהשם תמיד, תהיה ההשגחה בו תמיד" - RAD line 81 (16:3): "האמת היא שמצד השי"ת הכל בהשגחה, ורק מצד הבריאה מקבל ההשגחה כפי כלי קבולו"

## No place empty of Him: the life inside each thing

- **What it says:** The Maggid's disciples open his book with this: "First of all one must know that the whole earth is full of His glory and there is no place empty of Him (leis asar panui minei)." He says you can see it with your senses. Every thing has in it a taste, a smell, a look, or something lovable or awesome. Strip away the physical part and think only of that quality, the taste itself, the beauty itself. It is clearly not something the hands can hold. It is the life of the Creator dwelling in the physical thing "like a soul in a body." The Besht gives the same exercise for beauty: when you see something beautiful, ask "where does this beauty come from?" If the person or thing lost its life, the beauty would go. So its root is a divine power, and "there is in every place the life of G-d from above."
- **What it adds:** It turns "He is everywhere" from an abstract claim into something you can check with your own senses right now. And it shows that what holds you is not far off. It is the very sweetness in the food.
- **The picture:** Soul in a body (MDL, Introduction 7). Where it breaks: a soul can leave a body, and then the body is still there as a corpse. With G-d, if the life left, the thing would not remain at all (see the first angle).
- **How the source says to realize it:** The Maggid: "when we strip it of the physical and think only of the spiritual," the taste or smell itself, you will see it is the Creator's life. Then think the same about your own traits: your love, your fear, your speech and thought. Each is drawn from its root above. The Besht: when you see beauty, ask where it comes from; when you eat, think that the taste and sweetness come from the life above.
- **From inside:** The world looks lit from within. Ordinary pleasures stop being private property and become contact.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "G-d feels far. I can't find Him anywhere." After: "Even the taste of this coffee is His life. He's right here."
- **Sources:** - MDL line 7 (Introduction 7): "ולית אתר פנוי מיני׳"; "כי בודאי יהיה בדבר ההוא טעם או ריח או מראה או אהבה"; "וכאשר נפשיט אותה מן הגשמיות ונחשוב רק רוחניות לבד" - BST line 557 (Lech Lecha 14): "יחשוב במחשבתו מנין לה זה היופי"; "נמצא שיש בכל מקום חיות אלוה ממעל"

## Equanimity: all of it comes from Him (hishtavus)

- **What it says:** Tzava'as HaRivash opens its practice with Psalms 16:8, "I have set (shivisi) G-d before me always." The Besht reads shivisi as hishtavus, sameness or equanimity: whatever happens, it should all be the same to you, whether people praise you or shame you, whether you eat fine food or plain food. The reason is in the next line: "and about everything that happens to him, let him say: this is from Him." The second passage adds what makes it possible: constant cleaving to G-d. A person busy holding on to Him "has no free time to think about these things."
- **What it adds:** It shows the emotional result of being held. If everything comes from the same hands, then praise and insult lose their power to throw you. Equanimity here is not coldness. It comes from being so taken up with Him that the rest cannot grip.
- **The picture:** No mashal in the text, only the two pairs: praise and shame, fine food and plain food. **(ours)** It is like a child in a parent's arms who hardly notices the jostling of the crowd.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Say, about each event, "this is from Him." Keep your mind attached to Him so there is no room left to weigh how people see you. The text calls this "a very great level," which means it is a direction to grow in, not a test to pass on day one.
- **From inside:** A kind of evenness. The sting of an insult arrives and does not take over.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "Their comment ruined my whole day." After: "It came, it's from Him, and I'm still with Him. It doesn't run me."
- **Sources:** - TZR line 3 (Tzava'as HaRivash 2:1): "שויתי לשון השתוות בכל דבר המאורע הכל שוה אצלו" - TZR line 4 (2:2): "וכל דבר שיארע לו יאמר הלא זה הוא מאתו יתברך" - TZR line 17 (10:1): "ודבר הגורם לזה הוא הדביקות בהבורא תדיר" - Parallel: KST line 262 (1:189) and BST lines 1023 to 1024 carry the same text.

## Handing over the plan ("roll your deeds onto G-d")

- **What it says:** Tzava'as HaRivash 4 calls this "a great rule": Proverbs 16:3, "Roll your deeds onto G-d and your thoughts will be set right." Whatever comes up, think that it is from Him. And when you ask Him for something, ask Him to arrange for you "what G-d knows is for your good, and not what seems good to people by their own reasoning," since what looks good to you may be bad for you. "Just throw all your matters and needs onto Him." Section 84 adds: whether something happened as you wanted or not should make no difference to you, "since everything is from the Creator and He knows that it is good for him that it did not happen as he wanted."
- **What it adds:** It reaches into the place where people hold on hardest: their own idea of what should happen. Being held means you can even let go of the plan.
- **The picture:** The verb "roll" (gol) is itself the picture: a weight you roll off your back onto someone stronger. Where it breaks: you still have to pray and act; rolling is not dropping (see the angle on doing your part).
- **How the source says to realize it:** In prayer, ask for what He knows is good for you rather than for your specific outcome. After things happen, remind yourself it is from Him and that He knew it was better this way.
- **From inside:** The jaw loosens. A person feels the relief of not having to be the one who knows.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I need this exact thing to happen or I'll fall apart." After: "I'm asking Him for what He knows is good for me. That's safer than my plan."
- **Sources:** - TZR line 8 (Tzava'as HaRivash 4:1): "כלל גדול גול על ה' מעשיך ויכונו מחשבותיך"; "שיבקש מהשי"ת שיזמין לו תמיד מה שהשי"ת יודע שהוא לטובתו ולא מה שנראה לב"א ע"פ שכלו"; "רק ישליך הכל כל עניניו וצרכיו עליו יתברך" - TZR line 153 (84:2): "והוא יודע שכן טוב לו שלא יהי' נעשה כמו שהי' ברצונו"

## You are not the one speaking (the vessel and the craftsman)

- **What it says:** Tzava'as HaRivash 127: "When he speaks, let him not think that he is the speaker, but the life in him is the Creator, speaking in him." And the text draws the conclusion itself: "in this too equanimity is included, for speech is the same with my friend as with me, all is from Him." The Maggid, in the same book, gives the picture of a tool: "let him think he is only like a vessel, like a craftsman who strikes the stone with a hammer." The hammer does not want to strike. If it had its own desire, it would be separate from the craftsman.
- **What it adds:** The other angles say G-d holds the world. This one says He is the living power in your own acts, your talking, eating, moving. You are not a separate engine that He props up from outside. That takes away both the pride of "I did it" and the crushing weight of "it all depends on me."
- **The picture:** Hammer and craftsman (TZR 101:3). Where it breaks: a hammer has no choice and no heart, and the same book asks you to choose and to love (127:1 opens "first of all, choice"). The picture teaches who the power belongs to, not that you are a thing.
- **How the source says to realize it:** While speaking, think that the life in you is the Creator speaking. While eating, have in mind to lift the life in the food to its root. "And so with all other things."
- **From inside:** A lightness in speech, and less self watching. Some people say words come out more gently, as if there is less at stake for "me."
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "Everything depends on me getting the words exactly right." After: "It's His life that talks through me. I'll say it and let it go."
- **Sources:** - TZR line 259 (Tzava'as HaRivash 127:2): "אל יחשוב שהוא המדבר אלא החיות שבו הוא הבורא ית' המדבר בו" - TZR line 206 (101:3, "in the name of the Maggid of Mezritch"): "יחשוב שהוא רק ככלי כמו אומן שמכה בפטיש על האבן"

## From "something" to "nothing" (yesh and ayin)

- **What it says:** The Maggid: "A person must think of himself as nothing (ayin) and forget himself completely, and ask everything in his prayer for the sake of the Shechinah (the Divine Presence)." Then he can reach the "world of thought," above time, "where all is the same: life and death, sea and dry land." He reads a Zohar about the Israelites at the sea: they had to "let go of themselves and forget their trouble" in order to come there. And the reason: "when he thinks of himself as something (yesh) and asks for his needs, then the Holy One cannot clothe Himself in him," because the Infinite fits in no fixed vessel. Only when he thinks of himself as nothing.
- **What it adds:** It explains why the self that grips cannot feel held. A full hand cannot receive. Ayin (nothing) here is not low self worth. It is an empty space that the Infinite can fill. The Maggid also says ayin is the place of change: between egg and chick "there is a moment when it is neither egg nor chicken," and that moment is ayin. R. Chaim of Amdur: include yourself in the nothing first, and know there is only the Maker's power in the made, "like a seed that is sown and rots first and afterward grows."
- **The picture:** Egg and chick (MDL 27:2); the seed that rots before it grows (AMD 9:1). Where they break: the egg really does stop being an egg, but the person does not disappear. He comes back as himself, now open to more.
- **How the source says to realize it:** In prayer, think of yourself as nothing, forget yourself, and ask for the Shechinah's sake rather than for your own needs. In trouble, let go of yourself and forget the trouble for a while, the way Israel did at the sea.
- **From inside:** A letting go that feels like falling at first and then like floating. Problems do not vanish but lose their edge, because "all is the same" in that place.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I have to keep holding myself together." After: "I can put myself down for a minute. Something bigger fills the space."
- **Sources:** - MDL line 156 (Maggid Devarav leYaakov 68:6): "צריך האדם לחשוב א"ע כאין וישכח א"ע מכל וכל"; "שהיו צריכין להפקיר א"ע ולשכח בצרתן"; "וכן כשחושב א"ע ליש ומבקש צרכיו אז אין הקב"ה יכול להתלבש בו" - MDL line 82 (27:2): "ויש שעה שאינו לא ביצה ולא תרנגל" - AMD line 14 (Chayyim VaChesed 9:1): "כמו גרעין הנזרע ונפסד תחילה ואחר כך צומח"

## Where your thought is, there you are

- **What it says:** R. Yaakov Yosef: "I heard explicitly from my teacher: in the place where a person thinks, there he is, all of him." So when a person binds his thought to G-d, "all his limbs and sinews are drawn after the thought." One student version adds the other side: whoever thinks bad, heavy thoughts is standing in hell right now. The Degel adds that even though in fact the body does not stand where the mind is, "the body is drawn after the head." Tzava'as HaRivash gives the working form: your real home is above, with G-d. When you must deal with worldly things, think of it like "a person who goes out of his house and means to come right back."
- **What it adds:** This is the practical hinge for being held. The holding is always there; what changes is where you are standing inside yourself. If your mind lives in the worry, you live in the worry. If it rests in Him, you are there, even with your hands busy.
- **The picture:** Leaving the house meaning to come right back (TZR 84:5). Where it breaks: a person really does leave his house. Here the "home" never left you. Only your attention did.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Bind your thought to Him, and the rest of you follows. During work and talk, keep the feeling of someone who has stepped out briefly and is on the way home, and return the thought "at once to the first attachment."
- **From inside:** You notice you are not stuck where the problem is. Returning to Him feels like walking back in your own front door.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "My head is stuck in this mess all day." After: "I can put my mind back with Him. That's where I actually live."
- **Sources:** - TOL line 656 (Toldot Yaakov Yosef, Chayei Sara 69): "במקום שחושב האדם שם הוא כולו"; "כל רמ"ח איבריו ושס"ה גידיו גרורין אחר המחשבה" - KST line 60 (Keter Shem Tov 1:42): "במקום שאדם חושב במחשבה שם הוא כולו" - DEG line 611 (Degel, Masei 5): "אף שבעצם אינו עומד במקום מחשבתו" - TZR line 156 (Tzava'as HaRivash 84:5): "כמו האדם שהולך מביתו ולחוץ ודעתו לחזור תיכף"

## The stream still joined to its spring (the soul bound at its root)

- **What it says:** The Degel describes the tzaddik who is "always attached to the source," even when he has fallen to a small level. His picture: "just as a stream flows and spreads out from the gushing spring," even though the stream is outside the spring, "the stream is attached to the source and the source to the stream, and they count as one." The Maor VaShemesh gives the reason G-d feels our pain: "because they are attached to His very essence, as it says, 'For G-d's portion is His people, Yaakov is the rope (chevel) of His inheritance,'" since "they have in them a part of G-d above (chelek Eloka mima'al)." The Degel, in the king parable, has the son know "that his soul was hewn from Him."
- **What it adds:** The other angles say He holds you from outside and inside. This one says you are connected at your root. Being held is not only care given to you; it is a bond that runs through your own soul, the way the Torah calls Israel a rope tied at the top.
- **The picture:** The stream and the spring (DEG, Masei 5); the rope of inheritance (MVS, Vayeilech). Where it breaks: a stream can be cut off by a dam. The Degel's point is that from the root it is not cut: even at the far end, "there is no break at all."
- **How the source says to realize it:** Keep the mind attached to the source even at a low level, knowing the far end is still one with the spring (DEG). No step by step practice is given.
- **From inside:** You feel less like a separate unit that has to earn contact, and more like a branch that was never unplugged.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I've drifted so far there's no way back." After: "The far end of the stream is still the stream. I'm still tied at the top."
- **Sources:** - DEG line 611 (Degel, Masei 5): "כמו שהנחל נמשך ונתפשט ממעין הנובע"; "הנחל דבוק למקור והמקור לנחל ולאחד יחשב" - MVS line 763 (Maor VaShemesh, Vayeilech): "מפני שהם דבוקים בעצמותו ית"ש כדכתיב כי חלק ה' עמו יעקב חבל נחלתו"; "שיש בהם חלק אלקי ממעל" - DEG line 695 (Degel, Haftarah Ki Tavo 1): "ויודע שנשמתו נחצבה ממש ממנו"

## Nothing but Him can help or harm, so fear only Him

- **What it says:** Chovos HaLevavos gives the logic: a servant with many masters, each able to help him, cannot fully trust any one of them. If only one master can help or harm him, he trusts that one alone. "So when a person feels that none of the creatures can help him or harm him except with the Creator's permission," his heart turns away from fearing them and hoping in them. Tzava'as HaRivash turns this into a prayer: "I trust in Him, who created all the worlds with His words from nothing," and "I trust and fear only Him." It even says that every fear you feel is a fallen spark of His, so think: "why should I fear one spark from Him that is in a bad form?"
- **What it adds:** It uses being held to loosen fear of people and things. If they are not independent powers, fear can go back to its root.
- **The picture:** The servant with one master (CHOV 3:10), and the prisoner in the pit wholly in his master's hands (2:8). Where it breaks: the pit is a harsh image. Chovos uses it only to show that one party alone holds the key, and then adds that this One is compassionate beyond any other.
- **How the source says to realize it:** When a fear comes, ask where it comes from; it is from Him. Tie the fear to Him instead, as awe (TZR 127:1). Before fasting or hard effort, say the prayer of trust in TZR 43.
- **From inside:** The threat shrinks to its real size. The fear does not vanish, but it turns toward G-d and becomes something steadier.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "My boss holds my whole life in his hands." After: "He can only do what G-d allows. I'll do my job and fear Him, not the boss."
- **Sources:** - CHOV, Fourth Treatise on Trust 3:10: "וכן כשירגיש האדם שלא יועילנו ולא יזיקנו אחד מהנבראים אלא ברשות הבורא יתברך" - CHOV 2:8: "כעבד האסור אשר הוא בבית הבור ברשות אדוניו" - TZR line 258 (Tzava'as HaRivash 127:1): "ולמה לי לירא מניצוץ א' ממנו שבבחינה רעה" - TZR line 75 (43:7): "ואני בוטח בו שהוא ברא כל העולמות בדבריו יש מאין" - TZR line 288 (137:5): "ואין אני בוטח ומתירא רק ממנו ית'"

## Every step is led

- **What it says:** The Degel, from his teachers, on Psalms 37:23, "A man's steps are set by G-d, and He desires his way." All of a person's steps come from G-d for some purpose. The person wants to go this way for his own reasons, "but in truth it is all G-d's way, and G-d made him want to go this way" for a reason the Master needs, often to lift sparks of holiness waiting in that place. The Maggid: "everything a person sees and hears, and every chance event, all come to wake him up."
- **What it adds:** Being held includes your movements and wants, not only what happens to you. Even your "own" choices of where to go are carried inside a larger path.
- **The picture:** No mashal beyond the verse. **(ours)** A child who thinks he picked the road, while the parent carrying him chose the trip.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Pay attention to whatever you meet, since it came to wake you (MDL 105:1). The Maggid tells how Shlomo found a teaching for G-d's service in every tree and stone.
- **From inside:** Wherever you are stops feeling like the wrong place. There is a reason you are here.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I ended up here by accident. This isn't my life." After: "My steps were set. Something here is waiting for me."
- **Sources:** - DEG line 611 (Degel, Masei 5): "ובאמת הכל הוא דרך הקב"ה וה' עשה שיחפוץ לילך בדרך זה" - MDL line 226 (Maggid Devarav leYaakov 105:1): "כל מה שרואה ושומע וכל המקריות שיקרה לאדם הכל הם באים להתעורר אותו"

## The contraction is the love (tzimtzum)

- **What it says:** The Maggid, reporting the Besht: every flow of good from above has two sides, the love that makes Him give and the contraction (tzimtzum) that lets the gift come down into a small world. At the first creation the two came "at once, with no separation at all," so that "the love with which He loved Israel is itself the contraction." Without it the worlds could not bear His light, and without His light they could not exist "for one moment." His picture: "a father who loves his son, even though the son is very far from him, shrinks his mind" and pictures the son's face in front of him. And another: a small boy wants to ride a stick as if it were a horse. A horse is supposed to lead a man, and here the boy leads it; the father enjoys this and hands him the stick. So G-d contracted Himself into worlds so that the righteous could have the joy of "leading" them.
- **What it adds:** It answers the feeling "if He is holding me, why is He so hidden?" The hiding is the form the holding takes, sized to what you can bear. A parent bending down is not less present than a parent standing tall.
- **The picture:** The father who shrinks his mind to picture his far off son (MDL 102:1); the stick horse (MDL 4:1). Where they break: a human father really is limited when he shrinks his mind. G-d is not diminished; the contraction is only from our side.
- **How the source says to realize it:** No separate practice. **(ours)** When He feels small or far, read it as the size He chose so you could stay in one piece.
- **From inside:** Distance stops meaning rejection. It can even feel tender, like being spoken to in a child's words.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "If He cared He wouldn't be so hidden." After: "The hiding is how He lets me stand near Him without being overwhelmed."
- **Sources:** - MDL line 94 (Maggid Devarav leYaakov 33:2): "שאהבה שאהב את ישראל הוא בעצמו הצמצום" - MDL line 219 (102:1): "האב שאוהב את בנו אעפ"י שהוא רחוק ממנו הרבה מצמצם את שכלו" - MDL line 15 (4:1): "כדמיון אב שיש לו בן קטן והבן קטן רוצה ליקח מקל לרכוב עליו"

## The father teaching his child to walk

- **What it says:** The Besht on Psalms 48:15, "He will lead us like youths": "A parable of a father teaching his small son to walk. When the child walks two or three steps toward his father, the father moves himself further away, so that the child will walk more." That is why G-d is called "a hidden G-d." When a righteous person serves Him it seems to him he is not yet whole and is far from G-d, so that he will come closer. The Chernobyler tells the same parable with one more detail: the father steps back "and the child falls." Why? Because otherwise the child could only walk that little bit; now he gets strong on his legs. The Maggid gives a matching one: a father shows himself to his son who is playing with small children, and when the son drops the games and runs after him, the father "hides his face and goes on his way," so that the son's longing will grow and he will call "Father, Father" louder.
- **What it adds:** It speaks to the person who feels G-d moving away exactly when they try hardest. The distance is a sign of being trained and wanted, not of being dropped. The arms are right there, a step ahead.
- **The picture:** The first steps (KL, Shemot 27; ME, Ha'Azinu 11); the father who hides so the child runs (MDL 83:1). Where it breaks: in the parable the child can see the father. In life you often cannot, which is the whole difficulty. The parable asks you to trust that the father is there even when the space looks empty.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Take the feeling "I am still far and not whole" as the father's step back, and walk further (KL). The Chernobyler places the parable inside his teaching on faith at night: see the next entry.
- **From inside:** The ache of distance turns into motivation. Falling stops meaning "I failed" and starts meaning "I'm learning to walk."
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "The harder I try, the further He gets." After: "That's what a father does when his kid starts walking. He's right there."
- **Sources:** - KL line 380 (Kedushat Levi, Shemot 27): "משל לאב המלמד לבנו הקטן לילך"; "ועל דרך זה השם יתברך נקרא אל מסתתר" - ME line 639 (Me'or Einayim, Ha'Azinu 11): "והבן נופל ולמה עושה האב כן" - MDL line 183 (Maggid Devarav leYaakov 83:1): "משל אב כשרואה את בנו משתעשע עם ילדים קטנים"; "אז מסתיר פניו והולך לדרכו" - Also BST line 356 (Noach 8, copied from Kedushat Levi).

## Faith for the night shift

- **What it says:** The Chernobyler: when a person is lifted up and attached to G-d, he is a Temple; when he falls from his level, that is the Temple's destruction. Even then he must "strengthen himself and go to G-d from the level he is on now, as when he was on the high level," and this is done "by believing that on the level he is on now, G-d is also there, since there is no place empty of Him." He reads Psalms 92:3, "to tell of Your kindness in the morning and Your faithfulness at night": in the morning, when the mind is clear, you can draw down kindness; "Your faithfulness at night" means "in the time of darkness of the mind," it must be done by faith.
- **What it adds:** It gives being held a job for the bad days. You do not wait to feel close. "No place empty of Him" covers the low place too, so you can start from where you are.
- **The picture:** Morning and night (Psalms 92). The Temple and its ruin inside a person, with the custom of taking the lulav "in memory of the Temple." Where it breaks: night passes on its own. A fallen state does not always pass by itself; the text says you must strengthen yourself.
- **How the source says to realize it:** In a dark time, do not wait. Go toward G-d from the level you are on, believing He is there too, and keep praising Him "from the rising of the sun to its setting."
- **From inside:** Not a warm feeling. A quiet stubbornness, like holding a hand in the dark.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I'll pray again when I feel something." After: "He's here in this low place too. I'll start from here."
- **Sources:** - ME line 639 (Me'or Einayim, Ha'Azinu 11): "בהאמינו שבמדריגה שהוא עכשיו יש גם כן השם יתברך כי לית אחר פנוי מיניה"; "ואמונתך בלילות היינו בעת חשכות המוחין"

## The King behind walls of illusion

- **What it says:** The Besht's parable, told before the blowing of the shofar and preserved by his grandson and his students. A great king made, by illusion, many walls one inside the other, with rivers, armies, bears and lions, so that not just anyone would approach him. He had heralds call out that whoever reaches him will be made a minister. Many turned back at the first wall; some went further and took the money scattered at the gates and went home. But the king's son, whose heart yearned for his father, cried out, gave himself over, and pushed through. Then his father removed the illusions, "and he saw there was no wall and no screen dividing," only level land and gardens, and the king on his throne. The king told him: I did all this "only to test you." Keter Shem Tov draws the lesson: "there is no partition dividing between a person and Him, with this knowledge."
- **What it adds:** It says the separation itself is not real. The walls that make you feel unheld are made of appearance. The king was in reach the whole time.
- **The picture:** The illusion walls (DEG, Ki Tavo; KST 1:39). Where it breaks: in the story the walls truly frighten and cost the son his treasure. The parable does not pretend the fear is fake while you are in it, only that it is not a real barrier.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Know that all the screens are from G-d and are illusion, cry out with a broken heart to your Father, and do not stop at the gifts along the way. The Degel reads Psalms 89:16, "Happy is the people who know the teruah (the broken shofar blast)," as those who understand that the dividing screens are from G-d and are only illusion.
- **From inside:** The wall that seemed solid thins out when you stop treating it as the end. Some people describe it as sudden open space.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "There's a wall between me and G-d I can't get past." After: "The wall is part of the test. He's right behind it, closer than it looks."
- **Sources:** - DEG line 695 (Degel, Haftarah Ki Tavo 1): "למלך שעשה מחיצות רבים באחיזת עינים זו לפנים מזו"; "וירא אין שם חומה ולא שום מסך מבדיל" - KST line 56 (Keter Shem Tov 1:39): "ואין שום מחיצה מבדיל בין האדם ובינו ית' בידיעה זאת" - DEG line 695 on teruah: "שמבינים שכל המסכים המבדילים הם מאת ה' והם ממש רק אחיזת עינים" - Other tellings: Ben Porat Yosef, Miketz 37 and Shabbat HaGadol 1:129 (R. Yaakov Yosef); BST lines 1526 and 1529.

## Once you know it is a hiding, it is no longer hidden

- **What it says:** A student complained to R. Pinchas of Koretz about weak faith in providence, "since in a time of pressure for a living it looks like a hiding of the face." R. Pinchas first said such talk is for young people, but a Jew should be ashamed to need it; faith should be strong in the heart. Then he reminded him: "when one knows that it is a hiding, it is no longer a hiding." The Degel reads Deuteronomy 31:18, "I will surely hide My face," the same way: this is the illusion of the king's walls, "and in truth there is no hiding at all." When you understand "that I (Anochi) am in the hiding," the descent itself is for your good, and G-d who goes with you "in descent and in ascent" does not leave you: "even when I hide at times, it is not to push you away."
- **What it adds:** This is the most direct turn from not feeling held to being held. The hiding is not overcome by effort only. It is dissolved by knowing who is in it.
- **The picture:** Anochi (I) hidden inside haster (hiding). Where it breaks: knowing does not always bring feeling. The student still had the money pressure. What changed was what the pressure meant.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Say to yourself, in the hiding itself: He is here, this is Him hiding. Keep the faith strong in your heart (MP).
- **From inside:** The same situation, but it now has someone in it.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "When money is tight it feels like G-d turned away." After: "This is Him hiding, and I know it. So He's here."
- **Sources:** - MP line 290 (Midrash Pinchas 6:24): "שבשעת דוחק פרנסה נראה כהסתרת פנים"; "כשיודעין שהוא הסתרה שוב אינו הסתרה" - DEG line 706 (Degel, Vayeilech 2): "אין זה כי אם אחיזת עינים והאב רוצה לנסות אם ישתדל הבן לבוא אליו ובאמת אין שום הסתרה"; "כשתתן לב להבין שאנכי היא בהסתרה"; "אפי' כשנסתתר לפעמים מכל מקום לא להרחיק ממך"

## He is with you in the trouble (the Presence in exile)

- **What it says:** The Besht, in Keter Shem Tov: in every kind of pain, physical or spiritual, there is a holy spark from Him, dressed in many garments. "When he takes to heart that here too He is with him, the garment is removed and the pain is cancelled." R. Yaakov Yosef reads Isaiah 63:9, "in all their trouble He was troubled" (lo tzar), which is written with an aleph, "no trouble": once a person knows that in all his events G-d is with him, as it says "I am with him in trouble" (Psalms 91:15), then "there is no trouble at all." The Piaseczno Rebbe, in the Warsaw Ghetto: the suffering Jew "thinks only he is in pain," but the verse says He is pained in all our pain, and far more than we are.
- **What it adds:** Being held does not mean being kept out of pain. It means not being alone inside it. The pain is shared by the One who holds you, and that sharing changes it.
- **The picture:** The spark in the garment of pain (KST 1:67). A second picture from the Toldos (line 1226): a poor man's clay jug falls into a well; then a princess's golden jug falls in too, and he is glad, because now they will search the well and find his as well. Where it breaks: the Besht says the pain is "cancelled," which can sound like denial. The Piaseczno Rebbe, who wrote in real horror, does not say that. He says G-d's pain is beyond ours; the comfort is companionship, not that the suffering is unreal.
- **How the source says to realize it:** In pain, "take to heart" that He is here with you (KST). Then set out His praise, that the whole earth is full of His glory and He is troubled in all your trouble, and pray; "the trouble is cancelled by itself once he knows how to set out His praise" in this way.
- **From inside:** The loneliness goes out of the pain first. Sometimes the pain itself eases.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "No one understands what I'm going through." After: "He's in this with me. It hurts Him too."

## When it is too heavy, He is the one carrying ("I made and I will carry")

- **What it says:** The Apter Rav quotes the Midrash on Isaiah 46:4, "I made and I will carry": "I made Israel... and I will carry, 'I bore you on eagles' wings'; I will bear it when they sinned with the calf." He then explains that a soul pressed down in this low world cannot, on its own, long to rise. It needs help from G-d, who sends it a gleam of His light, and only as much as it can bear. The Piaseczno Rebbe, in the Ghetto, 1940: there is a level of "I am with him in trouble," where the trouble is on Israel and He suffers with us. But when the trouble is so great that Israel could not stand it by their own strength, and they survive only because He gives them strength, then "the great burden is, as it were, placed on Him," "since it is not Israel's strength that bears and withstands, only the strength of G-d that He gave to Israel."
- **What it adds:** For the person who says "I can't carry this": you are right, and you are not the one carrying it. The fact that you are still standing is itself His strength in you.
- **The picture:** Eagles' wings (the Midrash); the burden that shifts onto the Carrier. Where it breaks: the Piaseczno Rebbe does not use this to say all is well. He uses it to demand mercy: since the burden is on Him, Heaven must hurry to save.
- **How the source says to realize it:** The Piaseczno Rebbe says how to "unload" the burden in such times: "with return, with prayer, with charity, and with compassion for one another."
- **From inside:** Something like collapsing into arms that were already there. The effort does not stop, but the sense of being the only support does.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I can't hold this up anymore." After: "I was never the one holding it. The strength I have left is His."
- **Sources:** - OY line 247 (Ohev Yisrael, Yitro 6:1): "אני עשיתי ואני אשא"; "אני אסבול כשחטאו בעגל" - OY line 249 (Yitro 6:3): "צריך לה סיוע מאת השי"ת היינו שהשי"ת שולח לה הארה" - EK line 324 (Esh Kodesh, 5701, Vayishlach 3): "המשוי הגדול כאילו עליו ית׳ נתונה"; "כיון שלא כח ישראל הוא הסובל ועומד בהם, רק כח ד׳ אשר נתן בישראל"; "בתשובה בתפילה ובצדקה וברחמנות זה על זה"

## The eye that sees you (being seen and watched over)

- **What it says:** Tzava'as HaRivash 137: "Let him think that the Creator fills the whole earth with His glory, and His Shechinah is always near him," He is master of everything and can do whatever He wants, "and so it is not fitting to trust anyone but Him." And: "Let him always be in joy, and think and believe with full faith that the Shechinah is near him and guards him. And he looks at the Creator, and the Creator looks at him." The Maggid's disciples, quoting Chovos HaLevavos: "Do not rebel against your Master while He is watching you." Chovos itself makes it the second premise of trust: know that the Creator "looks down on him," and nothing of him is hidden, inside or out.
- **What it adds:** Being held is also being seen. For many people the loneliest part of pain is that no one sees it. Here the gaze is mutual: you look at Him and He looks at you.
- **The picture:** Two people looking at each other. Where it breaks: we cannot see Him the way He sees us. The text asks you to picture it "as he looks at physical things," which is a practice of the imagination, not a claim that you see His face.
- **How the source says to realize it:** This is given as a practice for a person at a "small" level (katnus), when he cannot rise in thought to higher worlds: think that the whole earth is full of His glory and He is near you, and "just as he looks at physical things, so he looks at the Shechinah that is near him; this is service in smallness." The text says that even in smallness he "serves with great attachment." Then keep in joy, and trust and fear only Him (137:5).
- **From inside:** The feeling of being watched turns from threat to company. Some describe a warmth on the back of the neck, as if someone is standing behind them kindly.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "Nobody sees what I'm carrying." After: "He sees me right now. I'm looking back."
- **Sources:** - TZR line 284 (Tzava'as HaRivash 137:1): "יחשוב שהבורא מלא כל הארץ כבודו ושכינתו תמיד אצלו" - TZR line 285 (137:2): "ויחשוב כמו שמסתכל על דברים גשמיים כן מסתכל בשכינה שהוא אצלו וזהו עבודה בקטנות" - TZR line 288 (137:5): "ויחשוב ויאמין באמונה שלימה שהשכינה אצלו ושומרת אותו"; "והוא מסתכל על הבורא ית'. והבורא ית' מסתכל בו" - TZR line 122 (67:1): "רק שמחשב שמלא כל הארץ כבודו והוא קרוב אצלו"; "ומ"מ אף שהוא עובד בקטנות עובד בדביקות גדול" - MDL line 7 (Introduction 7): "אל תמרה את אדוניך והוא רואה אותך" - CHOV, Fourth Treatise on Trust 3:17: "כי הבורא יתברך משקיף עליו"

## Why trust Him, and what trust is (Chovos HaLevavos, Gate of Trust)

- **What it says:** Rabbeinu Bachya defines it: "The essence of trust is the rest of the soul of the one who trusts," that his heart relies on the one he trusts to do what is good and right for him. We trust a person when seven things come together: he has compassion for us; he does not ignore us or get lazy about our need; he is strong and cannot be stopped; he knows what is truly good for us, hidden and open; he has taken care of us from the start of our life; our whole matter is in his hands alone, like a prisoner in the pit in his master's power; and his generosity never stops. "When we search for these seven conditions, we find them not at all in created beings, and we find all of them in the Creator." When a person grasps this, "he will hand himself over to Him and leave his management to Him," and not suspect His judgment.
- **What it adds:** It is the reasoning behind being held. The Chassidic masters speak from the inside; Chovos gives the clear case that makes the rest reasonable. It also shows trust is a state of the heart, rest (menuchas hanefesh), not only a belief.
- **The picture:** A person we lean on, measured by seven questions. Where it breaks: every human helper fails at least one of the seven, which is Chovos's point. The picture works by showing its own limits.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Five premises "by whose clarifying and truth a person's trust in G-d becomes complete." First, believe and make clear to yourself that the seven conditions are in G-d. Second, know He sees your heart, so trust must be inside and not only in words. Third, trust Him alone and not Him plus someone else. Fourth, work hard to do His will, as the Mishnah says, "do His will as your will so that He will do your will as His." Fifth, understand how His decree and the chain of causes work together (see the entry on doing your part). Chovos also says to "judge Him favorably in all His judgments and acts."
- **From inside:** A settling. Chovos lists the results: "rest of the heart from worldly worries," and calm from the soul's restless wanting.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I can't trust anyone, not even G-d." After: "He's the only One who has all of it: He cares, He knows, He can. I can lean."
- **Sources:** - CHOV, Fourth Treatise on Trust 1:1: "אך מהות הבטחון היא מנוחת נפש הבוטח" - CHOV 1:2: "ויחשב עליו הטוב במה שלא התנה לו ולא ערב עשהו שיעשהו נדבה וחסד" - CHOV 2:8: "כעבד האסור אשר הוא בבית הבור ברשות אדוניו" - CHOV 2:10: "ולדון אותו לטוב בכל דיניו ומפעליו" - CHOV 2:11: "לא נמצאם כלל בברואים ונמצאם כלם בבורא" - CHOV 2:17: "וימסר אליו ויניח הנהגתו עליו" - CHOV 3:22: "עשה רצונו כרצונך כדי שיעשה רצונך כרצונו" - CHOV, Introduction 39: "מהן מנוחת הלב מן הדאגות העולמיות"

## The mother bathing her baby (joy as the sign of trust)

- **What it says:** Among the fruits of trust, Chovos lists "his joy in every situation he is moved into, even if it goes against his nature, because he trusts G-d will do only what is good for him in every matter, as a compassionate mother does for her child, in washing him and swaddling him, tying and untying him, against his will." He cites Psalms 131:2, "I have quieted my soul, like a weaned child on its mother." The line before: he knows "the Creator arranges his affairs better than he can." Tzava'as HaRivash makes joy the frame of the whole practice: "Let him always be in joy," knowing the Shechinah guards him. Prayer in great joy is more accepted than prayer in sadness and weeping. Even after a sin, do not sink into sadness: be sad over the sin and "return to rejoice in the Creator."
- **What it adds:** It explains why joy and trust belong together. Joy is not a mood you force; it is what trust looks like from the outside. And it honestly admits that the good thing may feel bad, like a bath to a baby who cries.
- **The picture:** The mother washing, swaddling, tying and untying her crying baby (CHOV, Introduction 47). Where it breaks: a baby grows up and understands the bath. We may not understand our "bath" in this life. The picture asks us to trust the hands, not to see the reason.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Remind yourself that the Creator arranges your matters better than you would (CHOV). Keep in joy; do not add sadness on top of sadness; pray in joy (TZR 46, 107, 137).
- **From inside:** Crying and trusting at the same time becomes possible. The protest quiets without being silenced.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "This is awful and I hate it." After: "It feels awful, like a bath to a baby. The hands washing me are kind."
- **Sources:** - CHOV, Introduction 46: "יודע כי הבורא יתברך מתקן ענינו יותר ממנו" - CHOV, Introduction 47: "ומהן שמחתו בכל ענין שיעתק אליו ואם יהיה כנגד טבעו"; "כאשר תעשה האם החומלת לבנה ברחיצתו וחתולו וקשירתו והתרתו על כרחו"; "אם לא שויתי ודוממתי נפשי כגמל עלי אמו" - TZR line 288 (137:5): "ויהי' תמיד בשמחה" - TZR line 219 (107:1): "התפלה שהוא בשמחה גדולה בודאי יותר מקובל לפניו" - TZR line 90 (46:2): "ואפי' נכשל בעבירה לא ירבה בעצבות"

## This too is for the good (finding the kindness inside the judgment)

- **What it says:** The Besht, in Keter Shem Tov, asks why the Talmud has two sayings: R. Akiva's "all that Heaven does, it does for good," and Nachum Ish Gam Zu's "this too (gam zu) is for the good." His answer: to sweeten a harsh judgment at its root, a person finds "some kindness in the root of the judgment," and then the judgment is sweetened and "everything becomes kindness." Nachum could see the kindness in the root, so it turned to kindness at once. An ordinary person who does not see the root should still be used to saying "all that Heaven does is for good," even without knowing how. R. Yaakov Yosef adds: "the moment he thought 'this too is for the good,' he entered the attribute of kindness and goodness," woke the same above, "and it truly became good." R. Levi Yitzchak: accept whatever He brings "calmly and with joy," "trust and believe that there is great good in it, for no evil comes from Him," and through "your pure and faithful thought, that you believe He does everything for good," the judgment turns to good. He reads Yaakov's wish "to dwell in peace" (Genesis 37:1) as this very habit: to take everything calmly and for the good, even in fear.
- **What it adds:** Two things the other angles do not. First, the good is hidden inside the hard thing, not only after it. Second, the way you think about it is not only a way of coping; in these sources it takes part in turning it. This is the nearest thing in this circle to the later saying "think good and it will be good."
- **The picture:** Nachum Ish Gam Zu, whose chest of gems for the emperor was stolen and replaced with dirt, and who said "this too is for the good" (the Degel tells it, DEG line 156). Water from flint (Deuteronomy 8:15), which R. Levi Yitzchak reads as kindness drawn out of the hardness of judgment. Where it breaks: the story ends well, with the dirt turning out to be miraculous. Life does not always show the ending. The Besht's two levels answer this: if you cannot see the kindness, say "it is for good" anyway.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Make it a habit ("let a person always be used to saying"). If you can, look for the kindness in the root of what happened. If you cannot, say the plain line and trust it. Accept it "calmly and with joy." Note a tension: R. Levi Yitzchak once says that when a bad thing comes a person should "do no physical action, only trust in G-d" (Vayeshev 17, reading Yosef in prison), which pulls against Chovos on doing your part. **(ours)** We read it as a teaching for a high level and a particular case, not as the general rule these sources give.
- **From inside:** A shift in the story you tell yourself, sometimes followed, in time, by the facts shifting too.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "This is the worst thing that could have happened." After: "I can't see how yet, but this too is for the good. There's kindness inside it somewhere."
- **Sources:** - KST line 35 (Keter Shem Tov 1:24): "למתק הדינין בשרשן ע"י שימצא בשורש הדין איזה חסד"; "משא"כ בסתם בני אדם שאינו יודע שורש החסד מ"מ יהי' רגיל לומר כל דעבדין מן שמיא לטב עבדין" - BPY = `R. Yaakov Yosef of Polonoye (c.1710-1784)/Ben Porat Yosef (Polonne, Ukraine, 1760-1780).txt`, line 1069 (Chayei Sara 111): "דמיד שחשב גם זו לטובה נכנס במדת החסד והטוב"; "ונעשה כך באמת טובה" - KL line 251 (Kedushat Levi, Vayeshev 3): "ויבטח ויאמין שיש בזה טובה גדולה, כי מאתו לא תצא רעה"; "על ידי מחשבתך הטהורה והנאמנה שאתה מאמין בו יתברך שהכל הוא עושה לטובה"; "ביקש לישב בשלוה" - KL line 265 (Vayeshev 17): "אזי לא יעשה שום פעולה גשמיות רק יבטח בה' ובוודאי יהפוך לו לטובה"

## Worry is a kind of forgetting

- **What it says:** Chovos: the fourth fact to know is that G-d watches over all of a person's affairs, does not leave them, and "one thing does not make Him forget another." And the soul's agitation to speed up what He delayed, or to increase what He made small, is "weakness of recognition of the truth of His knowledge." R. Elimelech of Lizhensk, on Leviticus 25:20, "and if you say, what will we eat": when G-d created the world He opened channels of good that flow without stopping. The flow stops only when a person "falls from trust to think 'what will he eat'." Then the Torah's advice: do not act this way, "trust in G-d with all your heart, and the flow will go on without any break." On the manna, "Behold I (hineni)" means always: "I am always, at every moment, ready and prepared" to give each person his bread.
- **What it adds:** It names worry for what it is in these sources: not caring too much, but forgetting for a moment who is holding things. And it says worry is not neutral; it can itself narrow what flows to you.
- **The picture:** The channel of blessing that runs by itself unless blocked (NE, Behar 2). Where it breaks: said crudely, this can turn into blaming the worrier for their troubles. R. Elimelech's point is an invitation to trust, not a verdict on anyone's poverty.
- **How the source says to realize it:** When the thought "what will I eat" comes, answer it with "I am always ready" (NE). Recall that nothing makes Him forget you (CHOV).
- **From inside:** Worry notices itself. The grip loosens when you remember you were never alone in it.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "I can't stop thinking about how we'll pay the bills." After: "I forgot for a minute who's providing. I'll do what I can and let it flow."
- **Sources:** - CHOV, Fourth Treatise on Trust 3:8: "ולא ישכיחהו דבר את דבר" - CHOV 3:14: "חלישות ההכרה באמתת ידיעתו" - NE line 924 (Noam Elimelech, Behar 2:1): "כאשר חלילה יפול מן הבטחון לחשוב מה יאכל" - NE line 595 (Noam Elimelech, Beshalach 7:1): "אני תמיד בכל עת מוכן ומזומן"

## Doing your part (hishtadlus) while being held

- **What it says:** Chovos, fifth premise: everything that comes about in the world comes from two things, the Creator's decree, and causes and means, near and far, open and hidden, all running to finish what was decreed "with G-d's help." Picture a water wheel that raises water from a deep well: the man ties the animal to the wheel; between him and the water are the animal, the wheels and the rope. If one link fails, the water does not come up. So a person must act. Why did G-d make us work for our bread? One reason is to test the soul in serving Him. The other: without the need to work, a person would kick and chase sins. And "it was from the Creator's compassion on man that He kept him busy with his world." But the result is not in the effort: "what the Creator decreed shall be completed for a person will be completed" as the causes fall into place. Tzava'as HaRivash adds the Father's side: a father whose beloved son is being tested by a guest cannot bear the son's struggle, so he quietly "opens an opening for him" in the hard law. The guest then asks harder questions, and "the son, since he trusted in his father, grew wise on his own" and answered them all. G-d, too, "created the evil inclination and created the Torah" and gives us the strength to win, and still He takes pride in us "as if we did it by the strength of our own hand."
- **What it adds:** It keeps being held from turning into passivity. You act fully. What you let go of is the belief that the outcome rests on you. And the Besht's parable shows that trust makes a person more capable, not less.
- **The picture:** The water wheel and its links (CHOV 3:28 to 3:29); the father who opens the way and the son who, trusting him, grows wise (TZR 138); the Maggid's stick horse, which the boy thinks he is steering (MDL 4:1). Where they break: in the water wheel the man is the "far cause" and seems to be the source. Chovos's frame puts the decree above even him.
- **How the source says to realize it:** Do the work in the form that gives your body more rest, your heart more space, and your service more time, knowing the means "will not add to his portion" beyond the decree (CHOV, Introduction 45). Keep G-d's will; that is the fourth premise. And, with the Besht, take on hard things as the son who trusts his father.
- **From inside:** Effort without dread. You work hard, and when you stop, you actually stop.
- **How someone says it in a chat, before and after:** Before: "If I don't control every detail, it'll fail and it'll be my fault." After: "I'll do my part as well as I can. The result isn't on my shoulders."
- **Sources:** - CHOV, Introduction 45: "מה שיש בו יותר מנוחה לגופו ושם טוב לו ופנאי ללבו"; "כי הסבה לא תוסיף לו בחקו" - CHOV, Fourth Treatise on Trust 3:28: "כהוצאת המים ממעמקי הארץ בגלגל" - CHOV 3:29: "ואם יקרה פגע לאחת מן הסבות הנזכרות לא יגמר הענין" - CHOV 3:33: "ומה שגזר הבורא שיגמר לאדם מהם יגמר וישלם בהשלמת הזדמנות הסבות" - CHOV 3:36: "היה בועט ורודף אחר העברות" - CHOV 3:37: "והיה מחמלת הבורא יתעלה על האדם שהטרידו בעניני עולמו" - TZR line 290 (Tzava'as HaRivash 138:2): "פתח לו פיתחא בהאי הלכה" - TZR line 291 (138:3): "והבן ההוא בהיותו בטוח על אביו התחכם מעצמו" - TZR line 289 (138:1): "ואתה מתפאר בנו כאילו עשינו הדבר מעוצם ידינו" - TZR line 295 (138:7): "שהוא המעורר אותנו ונתן לנו כח לעבדו" - MDL line 15 (Maggid Devarav leYaakov 4:1): "וברא הקב"ה את העולמות כדי שיתענגו בהנהיגם"

## Walking someone through it (ours)

This order is our own synthesis from the entries above, not a sequence any one source gives.

1. **Start with the fact, not the feeling.** The word is still being said (continuous creation). Nothing needs to be felt yet. Use the silversmith: a vessel does not need its maker; you do.
2. **Make it touchable.** The Maggid's exercise: take one taste, smell or beauty in front of you and ask where it comes from. The Besht: "where does this beauty come from?"
3. **Bring it to the small events of today.** The wrong coin and the found coin. Say about one thing that happened: "this is from Him."
4. **Name where the mind is standing.** "Where your thought is, there you are." Step out to deal with things the way you step out of your house, meaning to come right back.
5. **If they cannot feel it, do not argue.** Use the hiding angles in this order: the father teaching to walk (distance is training), faith for the night shift (He is on this level too), "once you know it is a hiding, it is not hidden," then the King's walls.
6. **If they are in pain.** "He is with you in the trouble," and, when it is beyond their strength, "the burden is on Him." Offer the Piaseczno Rebbe's four ways to lighten it: return, prayer, charity, compassion for one another.
7. **Loosen the grip.** Roll the plan onto Him: ask for what He knows is good. Then the Maggid's step: put the self down as ayin for a moment, in prayer.
8. **Send them back to work.** Doing your part: act fully, like the son who trusted his father and grew wise; the result is not on your shoulders.
9. **Watch for the sign.** Joy, even through tears, like the bathed baby. Equanimity when praised or shamed. These show the teaching has landed.
