## The short answer

Ibn Gabbai wrote one idea three times, at twenty six, at fifty and at fifty eight: **the service of G-d is a need on high** (tzorech gavoah). The mitzvot and prayer are not only for us. They repair the Glory (the Kavod: the divine attributes as they turn toward the world) and unite it with the great Name. That is why the world was made, why the soul came down, and why man and not the angel is the purpose.

1. **The world was made so His unity could be known.** "Without creatures, the glory of the Ein Sof, His unity and His Godhead would not be recognized" (Derekh Emunah ¶35). And His glory is seen "only on creatures of matter, made up of all sides" (¶35), not on angels.
2. **The soul came down for a need on high.** "Man's coming into this world is a need on high, and it is the repair of the Glory" (Avodat HaKodesh line 827). The work can only be done here: "this world was created to be the place of the service" (line 3).
3. **The one who unites is the purpose of all being.** "The one who unites and the complete servant is the purpose of existence" (line 1110). And this is a will without a reason: "there is no reason in a will" (line 1114).
4. **The sefirot are G-d Himself, not tools.** "What is emanated is not separate from the Emanator, and not outside Him" (line 53); "He is they, and they are He, and all is one" (line 53). All change is from the side of the receivers (Derekh Emunah ¶102).
5. **He desired a dwelling below.** The book ends with the very midrash that Chabad makes its center: "The Holy One desired that just as He has a dwelling above, so He should have a dwelling below" (line 2212).

> כי כבר התבאר בזה החלק כי ביאת האדם לעולם הזה צורך גבוה, והוא תקון הכבוד (Avodat HaKodesh 2:33:1; line 827)

> כי עם שהרצון העליון היה זה ואין לשאול ולהשיב עליו כי אין טעם ברצון, הנה התכלית ידוע למבקשי יי' והוא ייחוד השם הגדול בכבודו שהוא צורך גבוה (Avodat HaKodesh 3:6:21; line 1114)

> נתאוה הקב"ה שכשם שיש לו דירה למעלה שיהא כך דירה למטה (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:10; line 2212)

*Ours.* Of all the pre-Lurianic kabbalists, ibn Gabbai is the one who stands closest to the Chabad "why" of creation. He has the dwelling below, the will without a reason, the preference for embodied man over the angel, the deed as the completion of the Thought, and the sefirot as G-d Himself. What he lacks is the Essence. For him the need is in the Glory and the Name; the Ein Sof stays untouched. Chabad moves the desire into the Essence itself.

## His core teaching on the unity

**One root, no separation.** Avodat HaKodesh opens its first part ("the Part of Unity") with the claim that governs the rest: the ten sefirot are "the very essence of the Godhead", and emanation means the emanated is not separate and not outside (line 53). G-d "did not create the world with anything other than Himself and His truth" (line 91), and after creation "He was always in that same oneness" (line 88). Derekh Emunah says it in the Tanya's own formula, taken from the Rambam: "He is the wisdom, He is the understanding, He is the knowledge, and they are nothing other than Himself" (¶53).

**Power in the limit, too.** "Ein Sof is completeness without lack. If you say He has power without limit and has no power in limit, you make His completeness lacking" (line 93, from R. Azriel; again Derekh Emunah ¶37, ¶115). The limit was made "so that they could hold His light" (Derekh Emunah ¶115).

**The same light, a different wick.** The flame of the Zohar: a white light above that never changes, a blue light below that changes with the wick (line 124). "The creatures cause the change ... and in the act itself there is no change" (Derekh Emunah ¶102). "From His side there is only good" (line 127).

**He is all, and there is none besides Him.** The unity of prayer brings the one Soul "from level to level to the end of all levels ... to know and to show that He is all, and He alone, and there is none besides Him" (line 471; again line 1923). And from the Zohar: one who cuts this world off from Him "is like one who cuts off this soul, and it seems there is another soul besides this one" (line 1923).

**Nothing outside.** He is "within that world, and above it and outside it; He surrounds it and bounds it from every side, and nothing surrounds or bounds Him" (Derekh Emunah ¶29). "If He is without limit, there is nothing outside Him" (¶31). He fills heaven and earth, and "when He wishes, He dwells between the two cherubs" (line 2156).

**Why a world, and why then.** "Since there was no recognition of the Glory while things were in that state, the hidden simple Will came to build a palace for His Glory" (line 2201). The Exodus below mirrors this "upper Exodus" (line 2202). And why then? "That is what will is: He wills when He wills" (Derekh Emunah ¶131).

## The need on high, from first book to last

**Tola'at Yaakov (1507).** In the section on tzitzit: "You must know that the mitzvot are not only a common need, but a need on high as well" (scan p. 14, our reading). He answers the Midrash that "the mitzvot were given only to refine the creatures": the need is not of the One Lord, but "of the Glory ... and this is the secret of refining the creatures: the joining and binding of the lower ones to the upper" (p. 14). "Nothing benefits Him ... yet as through food and drink the bond continues, so through the services the flow continues" (p. 10). "The repair of this Glory and its completion depend on the repair of pious human beings" (p. 41).

**Avodat HaKodesh (1530).** The introduction: the servant "does not ask or try for his own needs at all, but all his intention is for the needs on high" (line 1). "This world was created to be the place of the service", to complete "the upper will, which can only be completed in it, for it is the world of deed" (line 3). The deed is "the end of the Thought and its completion", and one who serves so "unites the head of the Thought with its end" (line 3). Part 2 argues it at length (reports 2-5); Part 3 makes it the purpose of existence ("all creatures to serve man, and man to serve his Maker", line 1092); Part 4 applies it to prophecy ("the desire in creation was that this power be in the hand of man, to repair the Glory by his deeds", line 2033) and ends the book with it: "a need on high, nothing else ... this is the whole of man and his purpose in his creation" (line 2209), and "He desired a dwelling below" (line 2212).

**Derekh Emunah (1539).** The simplest form: "as it were, for Him, for His need; for were it not for His emanation, His unity and Godhead would not be recognized" (¶58). "When people do kindness they arouse that attribute and give it room, and as it were add strength to it" (¶56).

**How Chassidus takes it up.** *Ours.* The Chabad line of thought runs through the same points, mostly without naming ibn Gabbai:

- **The dwelling below.** Tanya ch. 36 opens from the same midrash (Tanchuma, Naso 16). Ibn Gabbai reads "as above, so below"; Chabad reads "a dwelling in the lowest world", where the Essence shows more than above.
- **A desire without reason.** "There is no reason in a will" (line 1114) is the same move as Chabad's "a desire has no reason" (as we recall it, in the Rebbe Rashab's discourses and in Bati LeGani).
- **Matter, not angels.** "His glory is seen only on creatures of matter" (Derekh Emunah ¶35) is a root of the Chabad claim that the purpose is the physical world and the body of a Jew, not the angels (Tanya ch. 36-37).
- **Arousal from below.** "As one arouses below, so one arouses above" (Derekh Emunah ¶56; Avodat HaKodesh line 2077) is itaruta diltata.
- **The limbs as a chariot.** "When he sanctifies and purifies his limbs below, that repair rises above" (line 2035) is Tanya ch. 23.
- **For His sake, not reward.** "Not to intend any benefit or reward, but to repair the Glory" (Tola'at Yaakov p. 42) is the avodah "for the sake of the Shechinah" of Tanya ch. 41.

**Where they part.** For ibn Gabbai the need is in the sefirot: Tiferet and Malchut, the Name and its Glory, which can be joined or parted. The Ein Sof is "above will and intention" (Derekh Emunah ¶34) and has no need. Chabad keeps the language of the Name and the Shechinah, then goes one step further: the desire for a dwelling is in the Essence itself, and the lower world is where the Essence is found. Ibn Gabbai's purpose is a unity above that we complete from below. Chabad's purpose is the Essence revealed below. The first is a repair of the upper; the second is a home in the lowest.

## The states and meditations he adds

- **The sent stranger** (line 1): living here on purpose, with a task, and a heart turned home.
- **Serving the need on high** (line 1): turning the question from "what do I need?" to "what does He want done?"
- **Fear of not being worthy** (line 324): longing and awe in one feeling, always adorning the soul.
- **The hour worth heaven** (line 700): an hour of return here is worth more than the soul's whole life above.
- **Alive in the task, return as rest, His joy in me, eyes meeting, partner in creation** (reports 5-8).
- **The flash in the heart** (line 1980): the closeness that comes and goes, and its going is part of it.
- **One in all my parts** (line 2204): even the evil inclination turned into a lover.
- **The colorless sapphire** (Derekh Emunah ¶175): emptiness as the vessel that can hold every color.
- **Thought returned to its source** (Tola'at Yaakov p. 36): the mind rising to the wisdom it came from, one with it, then returning with light.

- **The flame of two lights** (lines 124-125): the steady white over the changing blue.
- **Two witnesses** (lines 128, 139): the sun with many acts, then the one heart with two pulls.
- **The Soul through all the levels** (line 471): the one Life through every level, until "He is all, and He alone".
- **Fixing the thought in the Infinite** (lines 734-735): after the words, let the will rest toward Ein Sof "where all wills and thoughts are fixed".
- **The prayer after the prayer** (line 449): a few words asking nothing for oneself.
- **The Shema from root to kingdom** (reports 11, 13); **crowning Him over each limb** (Tola'at Yaakov pp. 29-30).
- **A melody to wake the spirit** (lines 2072-2078); **eating to raise** (report 5); **the image of G-d in the other** (lines 2206-2208); **take the good in it** (line 2222).

## Where he agrees with Chabad, and where he differs

- The sefirot are G-d Himself; emanation brings nothing new (Tanya's Iggeret HaKodesh 20 and Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah hold the same, with more precision about the vessels). - The purpose of creation is the service of man in this world, and specifically embodied man. - The mitzvah binds the limb and the world to the divine; the deed completes the Thought. - All change is from our side; from His side there is only good. - Joy is itself service (lines 1015-1020; Tanya ch. 26, 31). - Teshuvah is older than the world and a foundation of it (line 851). - Spreading the inner Torah brings rest to the Shechinah and the redemption (line 1363). - Knowing comes before serving (Derekh Emunah ¶189; Tanya ch. 42).

- **Where the need lives.** In the Glory and the Name, not the Essence (see above).
- **The method.** Ibn Gabbai's service is kavanah on the sefirot, knowing which attribute each word and act joins. Chabad teaches contemplation of G-d's unity (hitbonenut) that brings love and awe, and holds that the simple deed with bittul reaches higher than any knowledge of the sefirot.
- **The received and the reasoned.** Ibn Gabbai attacks philosophy hard (reports 7-8) and rejects the Rambam's view of prophecy and the "created voice" at Sinai (lines 2160-2192). Chabad uses the Rambam's formulations and limits them rather than rejecting them.
- **Before the Ari.** He knows nothing of tzimtzum as the Ari taught it, nor of the breaking of the vessels, nor of sparks to be raised. His "repair" (tikkun) is the joining of attributes, not the gathering of fallen sparks.
- **Suffering and the other side.** His account of evil is mostly the older one: from the left side, and from man's sin separating the attributes.

## What the guide should take (ours)

1. **The need on high as the answer to "why am I here?"** Taught plainly: your deed is wanted above; the world was made so that it could be done here. Offer it with the Chabad step beyond it: He wanted a home, and this place is it.
2. **The flame of two lights** as a first unity meditation, especially for someone who feels G-d has changed toward them (the same light, a different wick).
3. **Serving the need on high** and **the prayer after the prayer** as a way to lift prayer out of self-concern without denying need.
4. **Fear of not being worthy** as the healthy form of awe, joined with longing.
5. **Thought returned to its source** as a short practice of ratzo vashov before a request.
6. **Crowning Him over each limb** in the Shema.
7. **The flash in the heart** to comfort someone whose closeness faded.
8. **The image of G-d in the other** and **take the good in it** for daily life.
9. **Guards.** The warnings against philosophy are not the guide's tone; the guide keeps the positive side ("the proof is the answer", line 1299). No self-torment in fasting; ibn Gabbai's fast is a humbling of the heart, and the guide never recommends physical harm. Kavanot on the sefirot are not taught to beginners.
