This part is a handbook of Talmud method. The Shelah wrote it in Jerusalem (line 10293). It is built on 27 "rules", one for each letter of "for by these words I made a covenant with you" (line 10294). Most of it is technical. It covers the thirteen rules of interpretation, the ways of the Mishnah, the Tosefta and the *baraitot*, and the terms of the Talmud from *aleph* to *tav*. It gives the rules for deciding the law and the lineage of the sages. It was all read. Only a small part bears on the guide. But that part matters. Line 10699 holds the book's plainest statement of a Chabad idea: the "plain sense" of a word is above, and our use of it is only a garment. Lines 10697-10698 add that "the whole Torah is His Names, and so are the words of our sages". And the chapter on *iyun* (deep study, lines 10700-10718) is a method of learning. It asks for effort and prayer together, for learning in pairs, aloud and with joy, and for "clear" words "before the Presence".

## 1.1 The whole Torah is His Names

In the chapter "It is the glory of G-d to conceal a thing", the Shelah goes past the Rambam's praise of *aggadah*. Above the secrets the Rambam saw are "the secrets of the Kabbalah in the secret of His Names":

> כמו שהתורה כולה שמותיו כן דברי רז"ל
> "Just as the whole Torah is His Names, so are the words of our sages." (line 10697, Torah Shebe'al Peh, Kelal Kevod Elohim 1)

> כי לדבר ממדותיו של הקב"ה בסוד שמותיו אין לדבר סוף ותכלית
> "For speaking of G-d's traits in the secret of His Names has no end and no limit." (line 10697)

> כלל הענין כשהם מדברי' בתחתונים רומזים בעליונים
> "The rule is: when they speak of lower things they hint at upper things." (line 10697)

The same line reads "it is not an empty thing for you" (Deut. 32:47) to mean this: what looks empty or exaggerated looks so only "from our side below". In its root above it is "very great". Line 10538 says the order of the verses is exact, "not only by the secret that the whole Torah is His Names" (לא מבעיא על פי הסוד שהתורה כולה שמותיו).

## 1.2 The plain sense is above; ours is the garment

This is the key line of the range. The Shelah reverses the usual idea of "literal" and "borrowed". We think the hand of a person is the real "hand", and G-d's "hand" is a figure of speech. The truth is the reverse:

> ועל האמת יד האדם בעצמו היא בהשאלה ממקום גבוה הנקרא יד
> "In truth, a person's own hand is a word borrowed from a high place called 'hand'." (line 10699, Kelal Kevod Elohim 3)

> נמצא פשוטן של דברים כלומר עצמיותיהן הוא למעלה ומה שאנחנו סוברין שהוא פשוטו הוא התלבשות
> "So the plain sense of things, that is, their essence, is above, and what we take as the plain sense is a garment," (line 10699)

and a garment of a garment. He sends the reader back to his introduction (*Toldot Adam*) on why Hebrew is "the holy tongue". The letters and words of the Torah "are above", and the physical is "the clothing of the spiritual".

## 1.3 In heaven and on earth

The rule "two verses that contradict, until a third decides" has a classic example. One verse says G-d spoke "from heaven". Another says He "came down on Mount Sinai". The Shelah, quoting the *Korban Aharon*, calls it a full contradiction:

> שזו היא סתירה גמורה אחר שאם הוא בשמים אינו בארץ ואם הוא בארץ אינו בשמים
> "This is a full contradiction, since if He is in heaven He is not on earth, and if on earth not in heaven." (line 10404)

Reason is not allowed to settle it. Only the third verse may: "from heaven He made you hear His voice... and on earth He showed you His great fire" (Deut. 4:36). *Ours:* the guide can use this as a picture of the unity. What looks like a contradiction to the mind is one in Him, and only His own word can say how.

## 1.4 "As it were"

On the word *kiveyachol* ("as it were"), Rashi says it is used "because it is a hard thing to say that G-d is weary of Israel" (line 10616, לפי שהוא דבר קשה לומר שהקב"ה קץ בישראל). The *Halichot Olam* reads it as a note:

> התורה שנתנה בכ"ב אותיות יכולה לומר דבר זה אבל לנו אי אפשר לאומרו
> "The Torah, given in twenty-two letters, can say this, but we cannot say it." (line 10616)

## 1.5 These and these

The oneness of the Torah holds disagreement inside it. From Rashi:

> ואיכא למימר אלו ואלו דברי אלקים חיים הם
> "And one may say: these and these are the words of the living G-d." (line 10545)

There is "no lie here", for each says his own view, and "the reason turns with a small change in the facts". The Shelah adds that even a view the Talmud drops "stays true for another matter":

> דע כי לא נזכר דבר א' לבטלה בתלמוד שלנו הקדוש והכל הוא אמת
> "Know that not one thing in our holy Talmud is said for nothing, and all of it is true." (line 10648)

He points to his longer treatment of "these and these" in *Toldot Adam* and *Shevuot*.

## 3.1 Effort and prayer together (proposed: `ask_for_understanding`)

From the chapter on *iyun*. Go over it "again and again", for each time something new appears. But:

> ועם כל זה צריך גם כו לבקש רחמים שיסייעוהו מן השמים ואל יאמר כחי ועוצם ידי עשה
> "And with all this he must also ask for mercy that heaven help him, and not say 'my strength and the might of my hand did it'." (line 10700)

> וג"כ בוטח בה' ולא בכח שכלו וגבורתו אשריו
> "And trusts in G-d and not in the power of his mind and strength; happy is he." (line 10700)

The Rambam's introduction, quoted at line 10691, says the same with "open my eyes that I may see wonders from Your Torah":

> ואין לאדם אם החכמה ודרישתה וההשתדלות אלא לעזוב ענינינו ביד הבורא ולהתפלל אליו
> "With all wisdom, seeking and effort, a person has only to leave his affairs in the Creator's hand and pray to Him." (line 10691)

*Tie:* `own_words`, `lips`; the R14 `with_his_help`. *Ours:* before learning, one sentence asking for help to understand, and after, one sentence of thanks.

## 3.2 Read aloud, with joy; learn in pairs (proposed: `learn_aloud_in_pairs`)

> בראשית תקרא הלשון שנים או ג' פעמים בשמחה ובקול רם
> "First read the words two or three times with joy and aloud." (line 10706)

Choose an hour "that your mind be free of all worldly matters" (line 10705, שיהיה שכלך פנוי מכל ענייני העולם). Do not lean on your own reading; ask a friend, "for no one sees fault in himself" (line 10704, כי אין אדם רואה חובה לעצמו). The one who asks and the one who answers in the Talmud are not fixed roles. They are:

> אלא הענין הוא אלו שני בני עליה עסקו ביחד בפלפולם להבין ולירד לעומק העניינים
> "Rather, these two great ones worked together in their debate to understand and go down to the depth of things," (line 10491)

"as two people do who learn Torah together" (כדרך שנים שעוסקים בתורה ביחד). And to his sons:

> והפלפול יהיה של אמת וכל אחד יודה לדברי חבירו אם האמת אתו
> "And the debate should be one of truth, and each should admit his friend's words if the truth is with him." (line 10717)

*Tie:* `chazarah`, `lips`. *Ours:* this is the *chavruta* (study partner). The Shelah's rule is to argue for truth, not to win.

## 3.3 Clear words before the Presence (proposed: `clear_words_before_him`)

From the Zohar, in the Shelah's last advice on study:

> כשאתה עומד לפני השכינה דהיינו כשהוא עוסק בתורה או בתפלה אל ימהר להוצי' מפיו אלא דבר ברור והגון
> "When you stand before the Presence, that is, when learning Torah or praying, do not rush to bring out of your mouth anything but a clear and fitting word." (line 10718)

> השינון הוא בלב ואח"כ יוציא מפיו בקול רם
> "The sharpening is in the heart, and then he brings it out of his mouth aloud." (line 10718)

*Tie:* `before_whom`, `lips`; R15's `inner_speech` (line 10176). *Ours:* heart first, then mouth.

## 3.4 Read for good; the lack is in me (proposed: `read_for_good`)

The Shelah met harsh words between sages in the Talmud. His practice:

> ומימי טרחתי ת"ל כשמצאתי כן בגמרא לפרש על דבר טוב ועלתה בידי
> "All my days, thank G-d, when I found such a thing in the Gemara, I worked to read it for good, and I succeeded." (line 10681)

And on a text that seems to lack something:

> כי רוח אלהים עמה לא חסרה דבר והחסרון הוא בחיקנו ודעתינו
> "For the spirit of G-d is with it; it lacks nothing; the lack is in our lap and our mind." (line 10498)

The Rambam says it too: "he should think that the lack comes from his own mind" (line 10691, אבל יש לחשוב שחסרון בא משכלו). *Tie:* `discern`, the R15 good eye (line 9524). *Ours:* the same good eye applies to people.

## 3.5 Make the views agree

> וכן יתבונן האדם לראות בעומק שכלו להשוות הדיעות החלוקות בכל מה שיוכל להשוותם
> "So a person should look with the depth of his mind to make the divided views agree as far as he can." (line 10634)

Line 10574 gives the reason: "one should try to bring the two views together as far as possible and make the dispute smaller" (ראוי להשתדל להסכים שתי הסברות בכל מה שאפשר ולמעט המחלוקת). *Tie:* S86. *Ours:* a habit of mind that serves peace.

## 3.6 A clean mouth

The Torah spent eight extra letters to avoid saying "unclean":

> לעולם אל יוציא אדם דבר מגונה מפיו
> "A person should never let an ugly word out of his mouth." (line 10680)

## 4. The big human questions

- **Why do the sages disagree if the Torah is one?** "These and these are the words of the living G-d" (line 10545). Every view is true in its place (line 10648).
- **Is Torah study a skill or a gift?** Both. Toil "again and again", and pray (lines 10700, 10691).
- **What is better, doing or refraining?** A "do" is service from love, a "do not" from fear, "and service from love is better" (line 10546, דעשה עבודה מאהבה הוי ול"ת עבודה מיראה ועבודה מאהבה עדיף טפי).
- **Why did the law follow Beit Hillel?** "Because they were gentle and humble" (line 10747, מפני שנוחין ועלובין היו).
- **Is language about G-d only a metaphor?** No. Our language is the metaphor; His is the real (line 10699).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **Letters above, words as garments.** Line 10699 is very close to *Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah* ch. 1 and to the Baal Shem Tov's teaching there. The letters of the Ten Utterances stand in the heavens and give life. The physical is their garment.
- **Torah as His Names.** Lines 10538 and 10697 repeat the Ramban's introduction, which Tanya ch. 4 and 23 build on (the Torah as His will and wisdom).
- **Negative theology.** Lines 10616 ("we cannot say it") and 10404 (only His word can settle it) fit the Rambam's caution, which Chabad keeps for the Essence.
- **"These and these".** Chabad reads it the same way. Each side draws from a different attribute above.
- **Tzimtzum.** No new statement in this range.
- **Not carried.** The technical rules of interpretation, the rules for deciding law, and the lineage of sages (10353-10403, 10405-10496, 10499-10535, 10547-10679, 10719-10781) were read and not carried. Also not carried: disgrace told "as punishment" (10682); R. Zeira testing himself in fire (10765). The "cup" and "wine" in legal examples (10460, 10513) are law only; the guide never uses drink.
