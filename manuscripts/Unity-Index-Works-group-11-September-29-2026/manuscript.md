## Summary

Group-11 has twelve works: seven Chabad (the Rebbe's Inyanah; three by the Tzemach Tzedek; the Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch; the Rashab's Ayin Beis; the Maharash's Toras Shmuel) and five from other schools (Breslov, Arvei Nachal, Ropshitz, Lizhensk, Hillel Zeitlin). Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus is the thesis itself. It says Chassidus is one point, the drawing down of Ein Sof, 'the essential', and that 'ein od milvado' means the worlds as they are, in time and space, are united with the Essence, not that they vanish. Its essence shows most at the lowest point (2:3, 18:5, 19:4). The three large Chabad maamar and mitzvah works state the idea throughout, each from its own side. Derekh Mitzvotekha states the commandment of unity and makes sovev the source of matter, 'and from this the virtue of the practical mitzvot is understood'. Hemshech Ayin Beis (OCR, parts 1-3) puts the Essence above both limit and the unlimited and derives the yesh 'precisely from the Essence', so that the worlds' own bitul is 'ein od'. Toras Shmuel (OCR) says the worlds are 'only a mere radiance' and that the dwelling below is made where darkness turns to light. The spine's kept dispute on the source of creation (radiance against Essence) runs between these last two. The Tzemach Tzedek's Igros Kodesh (OCR) state the idea only in a few pastoral letters: 'above and below are equal', 'the purpose of comprehension is bitul'. Thin, and said plainly: his Chiddushim al HaShas (halachic novellae; the unity terms occur only in halachic senses) and Shulchan Aruch HaRav with Divrei Shalom (a code; only the standard 'the whole earth is full of His glory' of OC 1-2). Outside Chabad, Arvei Nachal states the idea strongly ('no existence to anything except through the Name Havayah dwelling in it'; continuous creation). It also keeps two positions against Chabad: God relates to creation only through the tzaddikim's souls, and He is king even without a people. Likkutei Etzot states it as counsel (Godliness in every physical thing, in trade and among the kelipot; bitul through hitbodedut), but its center is the tzaddik and faith. Noam Elimelekh's center is the tzaddik as channel. It touches the unity in its own wording, including spatial tzimtzum ('to make room'); thin as a whole. Imrei Shefer is thin. Zeitlin's essays restate the idea in modern Hebrew (the ayin that unites opposites; 'the Shechinah in every single detail'; 'the belief in unity is the whole essence of Judaism'). His Zohar histories are thin.

## Signature (**stated**)

This is the one work in the corpus that states the index's own thesis about all of Chassidus. It says Chassidus is not a sum of virtues but one essential point: the drawing down of Ein Sof, which is 'the essential', with every other quality only a branch caused by it (2:3). Its method is a demonstration: it takes one small subject, Modeh Ani, reads it in pshat, remez, drush and sod, and then shows that Chassidus adds no fifth reading but the life of each, from the yechidah (9-17). Its one addition to the spine's precision is that the unity 'ein od milvado' does not mean the worlds cease to exist. It means the worlds 'as they are found in their existence, defined in time and space' are united with the Essence (18:5). Its last move is that the point shows itself most fully at the lowest point: in transforming the animal soul and refining one's share of the world, and in the four cubits of halacha (19:4, 20:5-22:1). That the earlier Rebbeim's definitions are branches of this point is the Rebbe's reading (spine §1). The text says it, so it is stated here as his claim.

## Structure

Four parts. (1) 1-3: four received definitions of Chassidus (the Besht's revival, lifnim mishuras hadin, changing one's nature, making Godliness graspable), then the claim that they must be branches of one point, and that the same holds for Torah as a whole. (2) 4-8: the point explained through Moshiach (yechidah) and the image of oil (separate from all, so in all). (3) 9-19: the Modeh Ani demonstration, ending in the union of particulars with the Essence (18:4) and 'the lower, the more its essence shows' (19:4). (4) 19:6-23: Torah as 'spice' for the yetzer, the four-cubits halacha, and spreading the wellsprings outward. Appendix (25-30): why the deepest Chassidus is given in the lowest generations.

## Signature (**reading**)

The book does not carry the one idea on its pages. It is Talmudic and halachic analysis in the manner of the acharonim: Rashi, Tosafot, the Rif, the Shach and Taz, and the author's grandfather the Alter Rebbe cited as 'אאזמו"ר'. A search for the unity vocabulary returns only halachic senses. The single kabbalistic aside found (Yevamot, on the fifteen women: the Name Yah between man and wife) touches the Shechinah but not the unity. Any tie to the spine comes from outside the book: the Rebbe's claim that Chassidus is the life of every part of Torah, including halacha (Inyanah 8:5-8, 20:5-22:1; spine F10), and the Tzemach Tzedek's own Derekh Mitzvotekha, where a single author joins the reasons of mitzvot to the unity. To read these novellae as an expression of the one idea is our reading, and it is thin.

## Structure

By order and tractate (Berachot to Niddah and Mikvaot). Each entry is a question on a Gemara, Rashi or posek, and a resolution. There are no Chassidus sections.

## Signature (**stated**)

Derekh Mitzvotekha shows the unity made actual commandment by commandment. Its angle is that the mitzvot are not added to the unity; they are how the unity is lived in a body. It states the mitzvah of unity itself: 'to know the truth that He is one and besides Him there is none', against the 'shittuf' of those who honor God's ministers (119). It then gives the book's most distinctive claim, the spine's F04: the 'main vitality comes from sovev'. Form comes from the filling light, but the very yesh of matter cannot come through any chain of cause and effect, only through His will that surrounds all and is omnipotent. From this 'the virtue of the practical mitzvot is understood' (120). The same point recurs in the individual mitzvot. Matzah is the belief that heaven and earth are batel like sunlight in the sun, 'and this is the secret of ein od' (51). Shabbat and the working week are the turning of the separate yesh to bitul, 'for which the Holy One desired a dwelling below' (13). The link to the unity is on the page throughout. That each mitzvah in the book is one expression of the same point is our reading of the whole, and it is well supported.

## Structure

By mitzvah, roughly following the Torah; each entry moves from the plain commandment to its root in the sefirot and the unity. The central entries for the spine are faith in God (95-118) and the unity of God (119-120). The Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah is a treatise on prayer as contemplation of memale and sovev (e.g. 267-268). The discourses and supplements continue the same themes.

## Signature (**stated**)

Ayin Beis shows the one idea from the Essence downward, with the precision that Essence is neither the infinite nor the finite. Its most important statement for the spine is that the power of limit is as much the Essence's power as the power of the unlimited, and the unlimited is 'not the Essence itself', because the Essence 'is above limit and above the unlimited, and both are equal to Him' (4226-4227). That is the spine's F05 (the Essence beyond both) in its source. From this it draws two things. The yesh and the existence of the worlds come 'precisely from the Essence', so the bitul of the worlds themselves is of the kind 'ein od', higher than the bitul of light, which is only 'as nothing' (6775). And by drawing the Essence of Ein Sof into Malchus and below, 'the essential intent, to be for Him a dwelling below, is completed' (10419). The method is a long, systematic hemshech that works through reshimu, kav, lights and vessels and Adam Kadmon. It follows the Rashab's own warning that the deep matters are to be reached through detail, not slogans. The Rebbe cites the early chassidim's saying that the Rashab is 'the Rambam' of Chassidus, for his ordered and graded explanation (Inyanah 28:4-29:1); that is said of his maamarim in general, and Inyanah singles out the hemshech 5666, not this one. ## Signature (**reading**)

The one idea is present in Likkutei Etzot as counsel, but it is not the book's center. The center is the true tzaddik and faith (צדיק 294 lines, אמונה 128, against אין עוד מלבדו 0). Where the idea appears it is stated clearly, and always as advice for someone far away. 'One must know that the whole earth is full of His glory... memale and sovev'; even someone in trade among the gentiles can find Godliness there, since without it nothing would have life; only, the lower the level, the more contracted and garbed it is (358). Even someone sunk in the kelipot can find Him 'in your place' (359). Bitul is reached through hitbodedut, until one's soul is included in its root 'and the whole world is included with him in the unity of God' (332). Breslov's angle is F03 and F08 lived from below: the distance is real and felt, and the tzaddik is the way through it. The spine's limits section says Breslov is organized around emunah and hitbodedut, not this point. That holds here, and linking the whole book to the one idea is our reading.

## Structure

Alphabetical headings (Emet ve'Emunah, Achilah... Teshuvah, Temimut), each a list of short counsels with a reference to Likkutei Moharan. The unity statements sit under Knowledge (268), Strengthening (358-359), Hitbodedut (331-332), Divine Attainments (393-404) and Prayer (1188, 1217).

**Thin.** Partly thin: the organizing center is the tzaddik and faith, not the unity. The unity is stated in a small number of counsels.

## Signature (**stated**)

Arvei Nachal states the unity in the plain Torah-homily mode of the Maggid's circle. It takes the verse 'know today... Havayah is Elokim... ein od' and reads it as 'there is no existence to anything except through the Name Havayah dwelling in it' (274). His kingship is 'over absence' now as before creation, since if His providence stopped for an instant all would cease, and 'there is no existence besides His' (2570). Its angle differs from Chabad's in two ways. God has 'no relation' to any created thing except the souls of Israel's tzaddikim, who are 'a part of God above'; the worlds were made on the way to them, as the king builds inns on the road to his son, 'and below the earth nothing was made' (563-566). And against Shaar HaYichud 7 ('there is no king without a people', spine F03), he says that unlike a human king He is king even when alone (2568). The tie is stated on the page. That the whole book is one expression of it is our reading.

## Structure

By parsha, each parsha a series of derushim that open with a difficulty in the verse or a Midrash and resolve it by a principle of Kabbalah or musar. The strongest unity passages are in Noach 3 (272-276), Chayei Sarah 2 (561-566) and Balak 2 (2568-2572). Much of the rest is ethical and eschatological.

## Signature (**reading**)

Imrei Shefer does not carry the one idea in any sustained way. Its homilies are ethical and kabbalistic in the Galician manner, centered on the tzaddik, the attributes and prayer. The nearest approaches are the Vayetze homily and the Terumah homily. In Vayetze, the tzaddikim of the Torah were engaged in this world because man is 'matter and form' and 'must set his eyes below and his heart above', descending to know what hinders people and then returning to cleave above (6). In Terumah, one cannot cling to a single trait all one's life; one must take up gevurah and tzimtzum at times as well as love (7). To read the first as the dwelling below (F07, G05) or the second as the unity of opposites kept distinct in conduct (F09, G10) is our reading. The Karliner's saying that Avraham's whole service was 'Anochi', revealing His Godliness (30), comes near the Maharash's Anochi, and it is a single line. The tie is thin.

## Structure

Parsha homilies, then sayings of other tzaddikim, then letters and a will. Nothing in the structure is organized around the unity.

## Signature (**reading**)

Noam Elimelekh is organized around the tzaddik, not the unity. Its 'true unity' is mostly the tzaddik's inner state, the channel through which he draws abundance to all Israel: 'the complete tzaddik who walks always in true unity bestows good abundance and blessing on all Israel' (81). The spine (§4, §5) finds no stated tie between this doctrine and the unity and warns against forcing one. The book does touch the idea in passing, and in its own wording. His attributes are 'complete unity' in Him, while we are each divided in ours (55). And He, whose glory fills the earth, 'had to contract Himself to make room' for creation, so a person must contract himself (737). The second is the spatial tzimtzum wording that the spine keeps against SH 7 (F03, with Tzava'at HaRivash); here it serves a lesson in humility. To call the book one expression of the one idea is our reading, and it is thin.

## Structure

By parsha, each parsha a set of short homilies, usually ending in the tzaddik's service or its effect on Israel. The Tzetel Katan (introduction) is a practical regimen of self-examination and mesirut nefesh.

## Signature (**stated**)

Zeitlin's book restates the one idea from outside, in modern Hebrew, as the key to Chassidus and to Judaism. In 'The Foundations of Chasidut' he takes the Maggid's ayin as its center. What cannot be named or bounded 'always unites opposites': 'separations and opposites belong to the yesh, while the ayin is always equal unity' (14). A person can recognize 'the absolute unity within all this separation... the error of the yesh', and must know not only the Godliness that fills the worlds 'but also its Shechinah in every single detail' (112-113), close to the Mitteler Rebbe's formula (spine §1). In the historical essays the unity becomes his test: 'the belief in unity is the whole essence of Judaism', and the question he puts to the Kabbalah is how ten sefirot are compatible with it (342). The later parts are historiography of the Zohar and argument with Graetz and Shadal, and there the tie is thin. His angle is a synthesis across schools, which Chabad does not claim; the phrasing is his own, not the Rebbeim's.

## Structure

Five essays. 'Foundations of Chasidut' (6-273): ayin and yesh, tzimtzum, the breaking of the vessels, raising sparks, service in the physical. 'Origins of Jewish Mysticism' (274-438): Kabbalah and the unity, the dispute over the sefirot. 'History of the Zohar' I-II (439-963): dating and authorship, with a close reading of the four who entered Pardes as unity against dualism. 'Introduction of Zohar' (964-1033): commentary on Patach Eliyahu.

## Disagreements kept

- Zeitlin asks how 'the ten are one' can differ from 'the one is three' (342-343) and then defends the Kabbalah. The objection is his framing; the Chabad texts answer it through the unity of lights and vessels ('איהו וגרמוהי חד').

**Thin.** The Zohar-history essays (439-963) are historiography; there the tie to the unity is thin except for the reading of the four who entered Pardes.
