## 1. The mechanics: how an understood truth becomes part of the person

**1.1 The definition: what is understood in the mind must come down into the heart, and from there into what you actually do.** *Why:* "Chassidus is that what one understands in the mind should come down into the heart, and there should be a union of mind with heart, so that it comes down into actual avodah in deed." Source: SH 5687, Shabbos Pinchas 16 Tammuz, 174:4 · `SH:1473` · «חסידות איז אַז דאָס וואָס מ'פאַר­שטייט אין מוח זאָל אַראָפּקומען אין לב» … «אַז ס'זאָל אַראָפּקומען אין עבודה בפועל ממש».

**1.2 The whole point of Chassidus is to change the *nature* of one's traits, not only the traits.** The Tzemach Tzedek asked the Alter Rebbe at yechidus "what is the point (ענין) of Chassidus?" The answer: to change "the natural state of his middos" (טבעיות מדותיו). The Rayatz stresses that this is different from changing "his natural middos". All the depth of Chassidus is aimed at this, and Chassidus both demands it and teaches how to do it. *Why:* it describes "the upper end, the true purpose" of Chassidus. "All the richest concepts … are aimed at changing the nature of his middos." Source: LD vol. 1 p. 82 (sichah of 12 Tammuz, retelling the yechidus note of c. 5565-5568), OCR · `LD:565` «שכל ענין החסידות הוא לשנות טבעיות מדותיו» · `LD:569` «חסידות דורשת לשנות את טבע המדות» · `LD:572` «מכווניס לשנות את טבעיות מדותיו».

**1.3 The change happens only through daily inner work in all three garments: thought, speech and deed.** When actual avodah is missing, the whole of Chassidus is missing. Learning that does not reach actual avodah has been "twisted". *Why:* "a thing done only through inner, day-by-day avodah in all three garments of the soul: thought, speech and deed." Source: LD vol. 1 p. 82 ¶10, OCR · `LD:574` «דבר הנעשה רק על ידי עבודה פנימית יוס יומית בכל שלושת לבושי הנפש: מחשבה, דיבור ומעשה».

**1.4 Chassidus that does not make you a Chassid is not Chassidus.** At the very least it must make you someone who recognises Chassidic middos in himself. Otherwise call it knowledge, understanding or wisdom, but not Chassidus. Source: LD vol. 1 p. 43, OCR · `LD:316` «כי חסידות עושה אדס לחסיד» … «אפשר לקרוא לכך ידיעה, הבנה, חכמה, אבל אין זו חסידות».

**1.5 Pnimi vs. chitzon: the inner person takes time.** The *pnimi* "cannot hurry. First he learns, then he reflects on what he learned, he takes in what he learned inwardly, until he warms from it", and all this takes time. He knows that "a great deal of toil is needed until one works over a trait". Even after he has changed a trait, he must keep watching himself so that it does not come back (see 3.1). The *chitzon* learns, davens warmly, and even looks warmer than the pnimi, but "the natural middos remain in their nature". The pnimi "strives to become more and more a vessel for the matter he is engaged in". Source: LD vol. 1 pp. 80-81 (sichah on makif and pnimi), OCR · `LD:557` «אין הוא יכול למהר, בתחילה הוא לומד, לאחר מכן הוא מהרהר במה שלמד» · `LD:560` «יש צורך לעמול הרבה עד שמעבדיס מדה» … «והמדות הטבעיות נשארות בטבען» · `LD:554` «הוא חותר להיות יותר ויותר כלי לענין שהוא עוסק בו».

**1.6 Inspiration without an inner vessel breaks you.** Will and delight (the *makif* powers) raise the intellect far beyond its own level, but only when the inner faculty has been prepared. "If the preparation and fitting in the inner are missing", the surrounding light does not strengthen the inner one. "It breaks it and destroys it." This is why the makif is said to "strike" the pnimi. Source: LD vol. 1 p. 79, OCR · `LD:545` «אס חסרות ההכנה וההכשרה בפנימי» … «אלא הוא ישבור אותו ויאבדו».

**1.7 There is a wall between mind and heart, and between heart and deed, and in avodah the crossing takes time.** For worldly desire there is no wall: you understand, you want, you act. In avodah you understand, sometimes brilliantly, "but it takes a while until it affects the middos, until one begins to want; and once there is a wanting it takes a while until the actual deed". His picture of such a person: "he davens and learns and does a mitzvah, does a Jew a favour and is a Chassid, but he does not budge from his place." Source: SH 5691, Shabbos Chukas-Balak 12 Tammuz ("קרית סיחון"), 253:5-7 · `SH:5501` «קיר איז אַ וואַנט וואָס איז מפסיק בין מוחין למדות» · `SH:5503` «אין עבודה איז פאַראַן אַ הפסק בין מוחין למדות ובין המדות אל הפועל, וואָס עס איז פאַראַן אין דעם אַ שהיות זמן» … «אָבער ער רירט זיך ניט פון אָרט».

**1.8 What breaks the wall: an honest accounting, "without self-love", followed by davening.** "The first davening that comes after a just accounting breaks the wall between mind and heart", and at least for a time the deed improves. The fire "goes out from the accounting" (אש יצאה מחשבון). Source: same sichah, 253:9-254:1 · `SH:5505` «אדעת' דנפשי' אָן אהבת עצמו» · `SH:5506` «צוברעכט דעם קיר המפסיק בין המוח אל הלב».

**1.9 Why understanding stays barren, and the remedy.** A person can grasp and even explain a G-dly idea well, yet it produces no lasting love or fear, or only a brief flash. The reason: he has taken in only the *intellectual* side of it, and "the G-dly in it was not absorbed by him". The remedy is to linger: "he should dwell at length in the contemplation and contemplate it several times at length, and then the matter will be well absorbed." Source: SM 5692, Shabbos Mishpatim, ch. רכג · `SM:435` «ואינו נקלט אצלו האלקי שבזה» … «שיאריך בההתבוננות ויתבונן כמה פעמים באריכות, ואז יוקלט הענין אצלו בכי טוב».

**1.10 If the heart is not moved, the understanding was not real understanding.** The middos are the test of the mind, just as the deed is the test of the middos. Source: SH 5703 (OCR), p. 73 · `SHo:4008` «שהלב אינו מתפעל שזוהי הוראה שהשגתו אינה השגה».

**1.11 Discernment matters even more than comprehension or feeling.** "Intellectual discernment (הבחנה שכלית) matters more for actual avodah than intellectual grasp, and even more than the excitement of the heart." In every matter, always ask "who is the speaker": the G-dly soul, the intellectual soul, the natural soul or the animal soul. The yetzer hara can put on a pious face. Source: SH 5703 (OCR), p. 77 · `SHo:4054` «הבחנה שכלית איז נוגע אין עבודה בפועל מער» · `SHo:4055` «מען בעדארף אלעמאָל און בא יעדער זאך וויסען» (continues: «װער איז דער זאָגער»).

**1.12 Prayer is the vessel through which understanding becomes conduct.** "Prayer is the vessel for the light of actual avodah", in the way the brain is the vessel for wisdom. It is "the avodah of prayer that brings the grasp of the mind into the feeling of the heart, and both together into actual avodah". It is also the offering of your *natural* traits and habits, and it starts from knowing what you lack. Source: KTC (OCR), pp. 7, 9, 21 · `KTC:180` «התפלה היא כלי להאור דעבודה בפועל» · `KTC:47` «עבודת התפלה היא היא המביאה השגת המוח בהרגשת הלב» · `KTC:59` «דהקרבן עצמו הוא הקרבת המדות טבעיים והרגילות» … «ראשית הכל הוא אשר התפלה היא על החסרון».

**1.13 The avodah of Chassidus is "to know yourself and to work with yourself".** Good middos, even good middos by the standard of Chassidus, are "quite good, but that is not yet a Chassid". The work of a Chassid is to know "himself", and a niggun opens that door: "a niggun shows him where he is, where he should be and where he can be." Source: SH 5706, Chag HaGeulah 12 Tammuz, 52:5 · `SH:6507` «דער ענין פון עבודת החסידות איז קענען זיך, און טאָן מיט זיך» … «אַ ניגון ווייזט אים וואו ער איז, ווי ער דאַרף זיין און וואו ער קען זיין».

**1.14 Even the good traits must be "worked out".** "The alef-beis of Chassidus is the avodah of prayer and the refining of the middos." You must distance the bad middos, and "the good middos too must be worked out" so that they have a real taste. Avodah comes from the same root as *oros avudim*, tanned hides: a hide is coarse and smells by nature, and avodah is the tanning of the body and the animal soul. Source: SH 5707, Simchas Torah meal, 77:6 · `SH:6696` «דער אל"ף­בי"ת פון חסידות איז עבודת התפלה און בירור המדות» … «נאָר אויך די מדות טובות דאַרפען זיין אויסגעאַרבעט»; SH 5707, Shabbos Nitzavim, 153:2 · `SH:7420` «עבודה מלשון עורות עבודים, אויסגעאַרבעטע פעל».

**1.15 Tikkun hamiddos is the beginning, and there are two kinds of repair.** Some traits, such as pride, anger and lying, cannot be repaired at all: they must be broken and replaced, and you fight yourself with every tactic until no trace is left. Other traits are good at root and need only washing: bringing the light of the mind into them, so that each makes room for the others (this is the 7×7 of the Omer). *First* you must "light up the place where you stand", your true overall condition as only you know it, because in the dark a treasure and a pit look the same. *Why this matters for integration:* the same maamar describes people who toil in hisbonenus before and during davening, feel the "taste and refinement" of the idea, and yet "it works no salvation in their souls in actual avodah to transform their middos". The reason it gives is that the vessel of their thought has not been purified. Source: SM 5692, Lag BaOmer (and again Shavuos night) · `SM:684` «ראשית העבודה הוא תיקון המדות לבררם» · `SM:683` «וכמו מדת הגאוה הכעס שקר והדומה, שאין שייך בזה ענין התיקון כלל» · `SM:686` «תחלה צריך להאיר את מקום עמידתו» · `SM:685` «ומ"מ אינו פועל ישועות בנפשם בעבודה בפועל להפוך מדותיהם».

**1.16 Even a naturally good trait has to be put under the mind.** Someone kind by nature gives to everyone without distinction, even to animals, and sometimes the excess harms the receiver. "When the mind leads the middos, then he gives only to the fitting." This is what the maamar means by the mind ruling the heart, so that the middos stop being *natural* and become *intellectual*. Source: SM 5692, Lag BaOmer, ch. שס · `SM:687` «וכאשר השכל מנהיג את המדות אז ישפיע רק להראוי».

**1.17 The order of self-repair, like a craftsman fixing a vessel.** The steps: (1) look over the vessel, which means contemplating yourself: what is happening to you and what will become of you. This alone weakens the middos in general, "like the first cold water that stops the boil". (2) Then comes breaking, done with intelligence and according to Torah. (3) What is bad (a bad eye, a bad mouth, lying, envy) is thrown out entirely. What is good is cleaned of what has grown onto it. Source: SH 5691, Purim, 203:3-204:1 · `SH:5136` «די התבוננות אין זיך מכח זיך» · `SH:5137` «לאחר דער התבוננות דאַרף זיין די שבירה».

**1.18 Itkafia means going against your own nature and habit.** "Do exactly what is hard; go against your nature. Even if by nature he is cold, he must fulfil the mitzvos with warmth, and go against his habit." Keep the mitzvos that have reasons in the same spirit of *kabbalas ol* as the chukim. Source: SM 5692, Shabbos Mikeitz, ch. קפז · `SM:373` «הגם ער איז בטבע אַ קר הנה המצות צריך לקיים בחמימות».

**1.19 Acquired nature: trained habit becomes nature, until you can trust your own body.** At the 5685 rabbinic assembly the Rebbe Rashab told the Gerer Rebbe that he relied on his "not-wanting": "From childhood I trained my body not to want what the Torah forbids, and to want every limb to do its mitzvah; by much habit it became nature, until I trust my body." Source: SH 5685, summer, 91:11-92:1 · `SH:708` «אני הרגלתי את גופי לא לרצות את האסור» · `SH:709` «וברוב רגילותי הוטבע ונעשה טבע, עד אשר הנני מאמין להגוף».

**1.20 Why avodah is the essence even for the thinker.** At a farbrengen in his childhood, a learned guest argued that "Chassidus is intellect, and intellect is good at any time, so what does it have to do with prayer?" The elders answered that avodah is the essence. The Rebbe Rashab explained why: the physical brain can "take hold of" G-dly intellect "only through avodah of the heart in prayer". "Without tears and without the sweat of a mitzvah one cannot be a servant of G-d in prayer." He described the *maskil* who learns deeply, does all the preparations, and when he davens delights in the intellectual pleasure but does not feel the G-dliness. Heaven tells him: "You want to be a maskil? Be a maskil. But G-dly light is not given to him." Source: LD vol. 1 pp. 172-174, OCR · `LD:1378` «הרי זה אפשרי רק על ידי עבודה שבלב בתפלה» · `LD:1380` «בלי דמעות ובלי זיעה של מצוה, אי אפשר להיות עובד הי בתפלה» · `LD:1384` «אבל אור אלקי לא נותנים לו».

**1.21 Love is commanded because the contemplation is in your hands.** The Maggid asks how love can be commanded when it is a feeling of the heart. The answer: "through hisbonenus one can come to love, and the command is on the hisbonenus." Source: SH 5691, meal of 13 Tammuz, 264:3 · `SH:5581` «דורך התבוננות קען מען צוקומען צו אהבה, און דער ציווי איז אויף דער התבוננות».

**1.22 The Chassidic "nature": gifts that count only when put to work.** The Rebbe Rashab (retold 5687): Chassidim "are by nature clever, by nature firm in their minds; a Chassid has a *brenn* (a burning), the power of bittul, a sense of smell". These are "great virtues *if* one uses them in avodah". Other statements of what a Chassid is: - A Chassid is "*yekod esh* within and calm (במתינות) without" (the Alter Rebbe's disciple R. Aharon of Strashelye). - Joy of soul and friendship are Chassidic traits that are always there in essence. At times they are hidden, and they are revealed by learning Chassidus and contemplating "what a Jew is". - Chassidus does not value gifts; it values "powers one has toiled for". - "A Chassid is not a maskil and not an oved but a Chassid", which of course includes being both. Sources: - SH 5687 reshimah, 157:5 · `SH:1302` «אַ חסיד איז בטבע אַ קלוגער, איז בטבע אַ תקיף בדעתו» … «גרויסע מעלות אויב מען ניצט זיי אויס אין עבודה» - SH 5704 (OCR) p. 166 · `SHo:7910` «אַז אַ חסיד איז, װאָס תוכו איז יקוד אש» - IK vol. 14, letter ה'שה · `IK:3213` «ולעולם ישנם במהותם, ובמציאות, אלא שיש זמנים שהם מתעלמים וצריכים לגלותם» - LD vol. 3 p. 85 (OCR) · `LD:4714` «חסידות אינה מעריכה כוחות וחושים שניתנו במתנה, חסידות מעריכה כוחות שעמלו עליהס» - SH 5707, Shabbos Chol HaMoed Sukkos, 70:5 · `SH:6635` «חסיד איז ניט משכיל, ניט עובד, נאָר אַ חסיד».

## 1b. The old Chassidim as models of integration (what each story teaches)

"Every movement of an old Chassid is a light in life, a firm foundation in the way of life" (`SH:5551` «יעדער תנועה פון איין אַלטן חסיד איז אַ ליכט אין לעבן»). He also says the stories are what turn children into "good vessels for darkei haChassidus" (see 2.14).

| # | Story (source) | What it teaches about integration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | **R. Yekusiel of Liepli.** At his first yechidus the Alter Rebbe said only "and You give life to them all" (the verse is quoted in the same line, `SH:442`). He "was turned into another man in understanding and in avodah", although he could barely follow a verse of Chumash with Rashi (SH 5684, 55:1 · `SH:442` «ותומ"י נתהפך לאיש אחר בהשכלה ובעבודה»). The Rayatz's comment: this was a *gift* from the Rebbe, but "one must come to light and revelation by one's own power, through avodah in order and gradation", rung by rung (SH 5691, 251:4 · `SH:5487` «מ'דאַרף צו קומען צו אור און גילוי בכח עצמו, דורך אַ עבודה בסדר והדרגה»). | A moment of transformation is real, but it is only the start. What holds is built step by step. For his study regimen, see *Limud HaChassidus* E6-E7. |
| S2 | **R. Shmuel Munkes and the treif food.** At a farbrengen he refused to share the food and then threw it into the slop water. Soon word came that it was treif. Pressed, he explained that before his first yechidus he had resolved: "a bodily thing that I *want*, I do not do" (SH 5691, 259:5 · `SH:5549` «אַז אַ ענין גשמי וואָס עס ווילט זיך זאָל ער ניט טאָן»). | One act of itkafia, held as a rule for life, became a second nature and a clear sight that looked like ruach hakodesh. The Rayatz: the first step into yechidus was "with a resolution and a boundary". |
| S3 | **R. Moshe Vilenker's five months.** He sat in Liozna "hearing and learning" for five months until he felt fit to enter yechidus. A maamar was repeated "fifty, sixty times" (SH 5691, 259:8-260:1 · `SH:5552` «פינף חדשים איז ער געזעסן און געהערט און געלערנט» · `SH:5553` «האָט מען געחזרט פופציק זעכציק מאָל»). | Absorption needs preparation and repetition. Every word heard from a Chassid "was taken into the head and into the heart". |
| S4 | **R. Pesach Malastovker and R. Zalman Zezmer.** At a farbrengen R. Pesach, "a great oved", hugged the great maskil R. Zalman, wept, and said: "Zalmanke, if only your heart were a true vessel for your head…" R. Hillel was moved by R. Zalman's *midas haChassidus* and by R. Pesach's *midas ha'emes* (SH 5691, 260:4 · `SH:5556` «אַך ווען דיין האַרץ וואָלט געווען אַ כלי אמיתית צו דיין קאָפּ»). | Head and heart can come apart even in giants. Integration is the heart becoming a vessel for the head, and friends can say this to each other's face with love. |
| S5 | **R. Aizik on King Solomon.** Wiser than all, yet he had no Rebbe. Had he travelled to a Rebbe, "he would have had Chassidic friends and been at a Chassidic farbrengen" (SH 5691, 261:2 · `SH:5559` «וואָלט ער האָבן חסידישע חברים, און זיין אויף חסידישן פאַרברענגען»). | Wisdom alone does not make a Chassid. Integration happens in a community: a Rebbe, friends, the farbrengen. |
| S6 | **R. Hillel envies Shalom Yosef the builder.** The simple builder wept in davening because he had heard that every soul is from *Chochmah d'Atzilus*: "Chochmah d'Atzilus in such a loafer!" R. ## 2. The practices and their place in the day, week and year

**2.1 The whole day of a businessman who is a tzaddik (the Rayatz's own model day).** Given at Simchas Torah 5685, step by step: - **On waking:** wash the hands, say *Modeh Ani* with complete *kabbalas ol*. "If you sleep like a Jew, you get up like a Jew." - **A general boundary on the body:** "all matters that belong to the body should not happen without a permit". So he is simply not involved in lashon hara, rechilus, lying or mockery. - **Before davening and davening.** After davening he eats, "and the eating too is in a different way". - **Then to business.** "A businessman too can be a tzaddik." Standing in the market with sacks of flour, not looking at "market things", knowing "He gives you the strength to do valour", "he is a chariot for G-dliness". - **Mincha in the middle of the deal** is the hardest moment to tear away, and the vital one (75:6-76:2). - **Between Mincha and Maariv,** learn something. **After Maariv,** learn something. - **Kerias Shema she'al hamittah** with a real accounting, "truthfully, without self-love". - **Then Tikkun Chatzos.** Source: SH 5685, Simchas Torah, 75:2-76:5 · `SH:569` «אַז מען שלאָפט ווי אַ איד, שטייט מען אויף ווי אַ איד» … «זאָלן זיך ניט טאָן אָן אַ רשיון» · `SH:570` «אשר גם האכילה היא באופן אחר» · `SH:571` «וואָרום אַ בעל עסק קען דאָך אויך זיין אַ צדיק» · `SH:572` «איז ער אַ מרכבה לאלקות» · `SH:576` «באמיתית אָן אהבת עצמו» · `SH:578` «דערנאָך הויבט זיך אָן תיקון חצות».

**2.2 The four parts of the avodah of prayer, and the main thing.** "(a) Learning Chassidus before davening; (b) preparation for davening; (c) hisbonenus within the prayer; (d) the meaning of the words. After all that, the main thing is to actually say the words with life." Source: SH 5708, Pesach, 204:10 · `SH:7956` «א) דער לימוד החסידות פאַר'ן דאַוונען, ב) הכנה צו דעם דאַוונען, ג) די התבוננות בתוך התפלה, ד) דער פירוש המלות» … «אַז מען דאַרף טאַקע זאָגן די ווערטער מיט אַ חיות».

**2.3 What learning Chassidus before davening does.** "When one learns Chassidus before davening, one becomes truly broken by the general hisbonenus of who is standing and before Whom one stands." The old Chassidim's toast was "that in old age we become *baalei avodah*". Source: SH 5707, 19 Kislev, 90:9-91:1 · `SH:6815` «און אַז מען לערנט חסידות פאָרן דאַוונען ווערט מען באמת צובראָכן».

**2.4 The minimum: "who is praying, and before Whom".** True submission needs preparation (bedtime Shema, Tikkun Chatzos). At the very least, spend a little hisbonenus on these few words, and it will give the davening some life. Source: SH 5691, eve of Simchas Torah, 152:1 · `SH:4789` «מי הוא המתפלל ולפני מי הוא מתפלל». *(For anyone.)*

**2.5 "The Chassidic corner".** "Once there was the Chassidic corner. One would go into the beis midrash, sit down in a corner and begin to consider what is happening with oneself, who is going to daven and before Whom." Today, he says, one gets up and starts rushing, and the avodah of prayer is forgotten. Source: SH 5706, Pesach, 36:8 · `SH:6373` «אַמאָל איז געווען דער חסידישער ווינקעל».

**2.6 Hisbonenus as a systematic curriculum, like learning Gemara.** The old Chassidim "did in Chassidus and avodah as one does in learning nigleh: today one learns Gemara, tomorrow the halachah in Choshen Mishpat". So today the hisbonenus is on the wonder and exaltedness of the Ein Sof, today on the worlds before the tzimtzum, another day on the lights of *or yashar* or of *or chozer*, another day on "his heart was lifted up in the ways of Hashem". Source: SH 5691, 12 Tammuz, 255:2 · `SH:5516` «היינט לערנט מען גמרא, מאָרגן לערנט מען די הלכה בחו"מ».

**2.7 Davening at length: how much and when.** To a student: "davening at length should be on weekdays at intervals, and on Shabbos it is fitting". The same letter tells him to learn more nigleh. Of the old Chassidic types (rav, shochet, melamed, learned and working householders): they "davened at length, some at great length and some at moderate length, and all davened *bemesinus*" (unhurried). Source: IK vol. 14, letter ה'רפג · `IK:3007` «ובעניני אריכות התפלה צריכה להיות בחול לפרקים ובשבת ראוי הוא»; LD vol. 3 p. 83, OCR · `LD:4699` «האריכו בתפלה, מהס באריכות גדולה ומהס באריכות ממוצעת וכולס התפללו במתינות».

**2.8 The texture of Chassidic davening: quiet, like dry wood.** Wet wood hisses and flares noisily. Dry wood burns quietly, and when the flame catches it gives a shudder and burns through. "So too in Chassidic davening: quiet, without noise." When the flame catches, "his middos break over", but all without noise. Source: SH 5706, Pesach, 36:6 · `SH:6371` «אַזוי איז אויך אין אַ חסידישן דאַוונען, שטיל אָן רעש».

**2.9 Singing in davening, and the niggun's place.** "Chassidim sing in davening because the voice arouses the intention." When something settles in for a Chassid during davening, it sings itself. But "a niggun is a preparation for avodah; it is not yet avodah." Do not rush a niggun. It is not shouting; it is life. Source: SH 5706, Sukkos, 6:2 · `SH:6128` «חסידים זינגען אין דאַוונען, ווייל קול מעורר הכוונה»; SH 5706, 12 Tammuz, 53:2 · `SH:6511` «ניגון איז אַ הכנה צו עבודה, דאָס איז נאָך ניט עבודה».

**2.10 Learning Chassidus: daily, like nigleh, and down into the heart.** *(Extends Limud HaChassidus F1-F5.)* - "One must learn Chassidus the way one learns nigleh", and then "bring it down into avodah of the heart: no chopped-off heads". It must be "avodah and toil". - Without learning Chassidus, "there is nothing to daven with". A Chassidic saying and a story are good and necessary, but they cannot take the place of learning to grasp a G-dly concept. - What to learn first: "maamarim in which hisbonenus in the unity of Havayah is explained". - The Rayatz's standard request in letters: a daily shiur, so that it bears fruit "in avodah of the heart and in refining the middos". - Hiskashrus through learning, from the Rebbe Rashab to someone who asked to be *mekushar*: "every hiskashrus is through Torah: learn the Chassidus I say and you will be mekushar." **2.12 Speech, and business as a place of G-dliness.** - **Permitted speech:** according to Chassidus "the permitted is very limited". If a thing is not *needed* and you merely fancy it, the wanting itself is bad. Permitted talk leads to idle talk, and idle talk to forbidden talk. - **The business person:** business people have an advantage over the scholar in the tent. Trade-chatter, though, blinds them to it. - **The practice:** every business person should have "a book in his pocket" while in the shop or market (Chumash, Tanya, Mishnayos, Tehillim) and learn a verse or a few lines in every free moment. **2.14 The farbrengen.** - **Its place.** It is "one of the foundations of darkei haChassidus". It "washes out the worldly mud, drives out bad middos, makes the body purer, and awakens the *nekudas halev*". It has made many baalei teshuvah and many Chassidim in avodah of the heart. "The foundation of Chassidus and of Chassidim is love of friends." - **When.** Fix farbrengens on Shabbos Mevarchim, on every festival and on days off, "where each one arouses his fellow in all matters of darkei haChassidus". - **What to speak about.** Answering a question on "a Chassidic farbrengen": the main talk "should be on tikkun hamiddos and ahavas re'im, that one must help another". In the middle of the talk, explain how to run a home "calmly, without anger". Do not make large demands of people; give them life according to their measure. - **The inner mechanics.** At farbrengens of old "the mashpia was broken within himself, and the listener was broken by the mashpia's brokenness". Words should be firm and short, with a sting, but "with the sting pointed at oneself". - **The order.** The young ask and the elders answer. "To be a student is itself work", and the more one toils at being a receiver, the more of a vessel one becomes. - **The stories.** The stories told at a farbrengen make the youngest children of Chassidim "good vessels for darkei haChassidus", and make the past a living present. **2.15 The chaver, the mashpia and the elder Chassid.** - **The chaver (the Mitteler Rebbe's institution, c. 5560).** When one tells another the wounds of his heart, or sees a bad trait in the other, and the two discuss it and look for ways to get rid of it, "they are two G-dly souls against one animal soul", and so they actually carry out the repair. - **The mashpia.** The Tzemach Tzedek added that the town mashpi'im should guide the young men. The Rebbe Maharash sent young men for set periods to the elder mashpi'im. - **What people took to the mashpia.** He was "the Chassidic crown of the town". People consulted him "how to be rid of a bad trait and how to become accustomed to a good one", how to make davening real, how to say bedtime Shema, and how to prepare for yechidus. - **The planter.** A true mashpia is like someone planting: he prepares the soil, then hoes, weeds and waters, each in its time, "because the good of the fruit touches his very soul". A lazy planter does not merely grow less; he grows *spoiled* fruit. - **The mashpia's own work.** He must not only say "do this". He must "work with himself to the degree that it will affect the other", "because every movement of his touches the soul of the one he educates". - **Why guides are needed.** The Alter Rebbe told his son and grandson that the true way of Chassidic education is older Chassidim as guides who can give "the true guidance how to be truly given over to a Rebbe, and then one becomes a *mekabel penimi*", an inward receiver. - **The elder's holy duty.** To raise a young man in darkei haChassidus, both in knowledge ("from light to heavy") and in the avodah of tikkun hamiddos, "that one should go in an ordered order". First he sweeps out the dry dirt. Then he washes with hot water and scrapes out with a knife the ingrained habits of "the bitter, dark, baked-in leprosy called 'I'". - **The mashpia's real task.** The mashpi'im concentrated on giving students a *taste* for the idea. They should have made them "sense the *untastiness* of themselves". - **The old saying.** "An old Chassid has more sense than a young man who is both maskil and oved." - **The teacher's role (LD).** The work of the rav is to *make* the student into a receiver: to widen his senses and abilities, and to pray that he will succeed in this. **2.16 Hiskashrus.** - From the first elders, the Alter Rebbe's disciples: "the essential being of a Chassid is hiskashrus: bound-ness and given-over-ness, with all the powers of his soul, to the Rebbe." - In its highest form (the "luminary and light"), what matters is "the inner and essential hiskashrus". Each completes the truth of the other, and it shows itself as inner love. - In practice it comes through Torah (2.10), preparation (story S3) and self-effort (S1). Source: SH 5708, Shavuos night, 230:1 · `SH:8213` «אַז דער עיקר מהות פון חסיד איז התקשרות»; LD vol. 1 p. 75, OCR · `LD:511` «העיקר הוא ההתקשרות הפנימית והעצמית».

**2.17 Ahavas Yisrael: where to begin, and a daily test.** - **Where to begin.** The Rebbe Rashab: there are love of G-d, love of Torah and love of Israel, and "one must begin with ahavas Yisrael". The Alter Rebbe, after Mezritch: love all Jews, "even simple people". That brings love of Torah, "and then one has a sense of love of G-d". The three must be braided together "like a havdalah candle". - **A daily test.** Even if one learns and understands Chassidus and davens with fire, "that is not yet all; there must be ahavas Yisrael", and it must be lived. "When a day passes and he has not revived a Jew, simply in material things, he should feel fallen." - **Its rank.** "Doing a Jew a favour in material things is higher than understanding a deep matter in learning." - **How.** Love a Jew with no distinction, scholar or simple, as a brother. Do not force yourself on people, but "put the greatest effort into everything so that the other will be better off". See the good in the other, and "understand it from yourself": you know how hard it is for you to break your own bad heart. - **The summary.** "The essence of Chassidus is tikkun hamiddos and to hold a simple Jew dear." Sources: - SH 5706, Shavuos, 50:1 · `SH:6485` «אַז אָנהויבן דאַרף מען פון אהבת ישראל» - SH 5707, 13 Elul, 149:9 · `SH:7393` «אַז מען דאַרף האָלט האָבן אַלע אידן, אפילו אנשים פשוטים» - SH 5708, 19 Kislev, 183:9 · `SH:7745` «אויסגעפלאָכטען צוזאַמען אַזוי ווי אַ נר הבדלה» - SH 5707, Purim, 101:4 · `SH:6895` «ווען עס גייט דורך אַ טאָג און ער איז ניט מחי'» - SH 5707, 19 Kislev, 96:1 · `SH:6850` «אַ טובה טאָן אַ אידן בגשמיות איז העכער ווי פאַרשטיין אַ טיפן ענין אין לערנען» - SH 5690, Simchas Torah, 90:1 · `SH:4232` «אַז יענעם זאָל ווערען בעסער» - IK vol. 14, letter ה'קכה · `IK:1085` «לראות גם בהזולת צד הטוב, ולהבין זה מעצמו» - LD vol. 3 p. 178, OCR · `LD:5447` «עיקר החסידות הוא תיקון המדות ולייקר יהודי פשוט». *(For anyone.)*

**2.18 Simcha: action, not sighs.** - **The letter.** To an uncle in low spirits: "better one action than a thousand sighs". A chapter of Tanya, a chapter of Tehillim, a verse of Chumash with Rashi, "restore the soul". In every sadness what matters is the joy that comes after it, and there is much to contemplate for joy, "first of all Tanya ch. 33". - **Hidden, not lost.** Joy of soul and friendship are never absent from Chassidim, only hidden. Learning Chassidus reveals the joy. - **Joy as an identity.** R. Zalman Zezmer told his former teacher, who wanted to become a Chassid: "a Chassid is a ball of fiery joy; you were raised on cold melancholy." - **Tears are not sadness.** The Tzemach Tzedek: for a Chassid "the niggun is not merely joy and the weeping is not sadness; the weeping is *merirus*". **2.19 Week and year: making the weekdays a festival.** The Alter Rebbe read *ve'asisa chag shavuos* as "make the *weeks* into a festival". Davening at length belongs especially to Shabbos (2.7), and so do farbrengens on Shabbos and on days off (2.14). Shemini Atzeres and Simchas Torah are the time to "set oneself in a good movement and stand in that good movement with strength all year" (see 3.9). **2.20 A Chassid everywhere, and learning from everything.** "A Chassid is a Chassid everywhere: in the market, at home, in the store, in the street." "That is darkei haChassidus: nothing is a lump. Everything one sees speaks and tells; one must learn something from it for avodah." (The Rebbe Rashab, at a shop window of machines: "the light of Chassidic understanding can and must use everything in avodah.") Hillel's name, the four things a Chassidic child receives from a Chassidic father or melamed: 1. a Torah-saying or story heard from a Rebbe; 2. a niggun heard in a Rebbe's name; 3. retelling what one saw and heard; 4. a teaching, custom or movement of an elder Chassid. Seclusion is a preparation, and a great one. But "for all its greatness, hisbodedus is not the intention of the soul's descent into the body". The way of the Baal Shem Tov combines withdrawal and involvement. ## 3. Growth over time

**3.1 It takes time, and relapse is expected.** The pnimi knows that "avodah needs time". After he has changed a trait "he must still check well and watch himself so that it does not come back to life". (See 1.5 and 1.7 on the time lag between mind, heart and deed.) Source: LD vol. 1 p. 81, OCR · `LD:560` «עדיין עליו לבקר היטב ולהשגיח על עצמו שלא יהיה חוזר וניעור».

**3.2 How long one trait takes.** Everyone knows his own wounds: one is a liar by nature, one hot-tempered, one envious, one cold, one wants everything for himself. Breaking one bad trait "takes a proper length of time and great toil". Exchanging it for a good trait "blackens the face": "one gets thoroughly blackened before one comes to a bit of truth". (He gives this as the reason to judge others kindly.) Source: IK vol. 14, letter ה'קכה · `IK:1085` «עולה זמן נכון וביגיעה גדולה» … «מיא וערט גוט פאר שווארצט ביז מיא קומט צו א שטיקל אמת».

**3.3 The layers come off in order, and truth comes drop by drop.** The Rayatz uses Chassidus's parable of the well. The water is always there; the work is only to remove the cover. First the stones, which must be cracked into pebbles so light gets in. Then the pebbles. Then soft earth, whose softness shows that the spring is near. "Each covering needs its own work to remove it, and an ordered order from heavy to light." When the spring is uncovered it does not come like a storm of rain: "it flows drop by drop", but it is living water. Only "an oved who is busy working with himself, in the hisbonenus before davening and the devotion during it, comes in the course of time to the truth". That takes "an ordered avodah", with "strict supervision of every step" and "strict, penetrating self-criticism". Source: IK vol. 14, letter ה'שכו · `IK:3461` «כל כיסוי וכיסוי דורש עבודה מיוחדת להסרתו וגם סדר מסודר» · `IK:3463` «נאר עס פליסט טיפין טיפין» · `IK:3464` «קומט צו במשך הזמן צום אמת» · `IK:3465` «טאן מיט זיך מיט א שטרענגער השגחה אויף יעדן שריט און טריט».

**3.4 The stages of Chassidus itself: mussar, then inquiry, then life.** Even though the higher wisdom is higher, it is acquired from below upward. "The beginning of the avodah must be from the *mussar* within Chassidus" (the power), then the inquiry within Chassidus (the light), then the life-force (the essence). Source: KTC p. 17, OCR · `KTC:138` «דתחלת העבודה צריך להיות מהמוסר שבחסידות».

**3.5 A nuance: basic character first.** "Before Chassidus there must be tikkun hamiddos." One should not need Chassidus for the basic "turn from evil" of bad traits, or even for ordinary good traits and ahavas Yisrael. Chassidus is for more than that. (Compare 1.15, where tikkun hamiddos is "the beginning of avodah". Read together: ordinary decency is the floor, and Chassidus aims at changing the *nature*, 1.2.) Source: LD vol. 1 p. 270, OCR · `LD:2261` «לפני חסידות צריך להיות תיקון המדות».

**3.6 How to measure real change: have you moved from your place?** The test of davening is *kiryas Sichon* (from *hazazah*, to budge): has he moved at all, "whether in becoming better in fear of Heaven or in good middos", or does he not stir? (1.7-1.8). A second test is whether excitement leaves anything behind. Every excitement that is "born of intellect brings a *bechen* (therefore)", a practical result in understanding and in conduct. Excitement for its own sake "cools at once and was as though it never was". The Rebbe Rashab: "We need Chassidim of Wednesday, not Chassidim of Simchas Torah." Source: SH 5691, 12 Tammuz, 253:8 · `SH:5504` «הן בעסער ווערין אין יראת שמים, און הן אין מדות טובות, טוט ער זיך ניט אַריר»; LD vol. 1 p. 270, OCR · `LD:2259` «כל התפעלות כשהיא תולדה משכל, מביאה לייבכןיי» · `LD:2257` «אנו זקוקיס לחסידים של ימי הרביעי בשבוע ולא לחסידי שמחת תורה».

**3.7 Falling and getting up: do not collapse into self-punishment.** Young men who build their own programme of avodah often go wrong. When they find their mistakes, "their spirit falls" and they take on fasts and self-affliction. The animal soul wants exactly this, to confuse them. "They must strengthen themselves with every kind of strengthening", and whoever sets his heart on it is helped from Heaven. For the turn itself, the Rebbeim hold that one inner turn ("איין קער"), in which a person turns over "from end to end", can place him above a lifelong tzaddik. So "one may not push a Jew away; one must bring him close". One must also demand real Chassidic middos of him, and not bring Chassidus down where it does not belong. Source: LD vol. 3 pp. 178-179, OCR · `LD:5450` «צריכיסם להתחזק בכל מיני חיזוק»; SH 5685, Simchas Torah, 76:6-8 · `SH:579` «אינווייניק קערט זיך באַ אים איבער מן הקצה אל הקצה» · `SH:581` «מען טאָר ניט מדחה זיין, מ'דאַרף אים מקרב זיין».

**3.8 A picture of the years of adolescence (from a bar-mitzvah maamar).** In one of the bar-mitzvah discourses printed in *Kuntres Bar Mitzvah*, the good inclination is given help from above from 13 on, "little by little I will drive it out". This "continues seven years, until all seven middos of the yetzer tov are complete", and only then is it clear that the mind shines in them. *Attribution note:* this passage comes from the Rebbeim's bar-mitzvah maamarim that the Rayatz was told to learn and repeat. It is not his own sichah, and I could not settle from the OCR which maamar it belongs to. Source: KBM p. 42, OCR · `KBM:258` «ונמשך ז' שנים עד שיהיי כל ז' מדות דיצ"ט בשלימות».

**3.9 The Rayatz's own beginning, and the yearly reset.** From 5651, when he was 10-11: "my father began to learn Chassidus with me, and I began to feel in my soul that there are understandings and knowledge that make one not want the things a person wants", and want what he does not want. Each year at Shemini Atzeres and Simchas Torah, "the *absorption* shall be yours": set yourself in a good movement "and stand in it with firmness and strength the whole year". Source: LD vol. 1 p. 140, OCR · `LD:1093` «התחלתי להרגיש בנפשי שישנן הבנות וידיעות המשפיעות שלא לרצות בדבריס שהאדס רוצה»; SH 5705 (OCR) p. 61 · `SHo:10480` «און אין די תנועה טובה זאָל ער שטעהן מיט אַ תוקף און אַ חוזק כל השנה».

## 4. Counterfeits of integration

**4.1 Knowledge only leads away, even from being a simple Jew.** "Those who held only to the intellect of Chassidus later became, sadly, distanced", not only from the ideal of a Chassid, who "passes over his middos", but even from being "a plain Jew". Even when they kept Torah and mitzvos, "in the end they caused a separation, in themselves or in others". Source: SH 5687, Shabbos Pinchas, 174:5 · `SH:1474` «די וואָס האָבן געהאַלטן נאָר באַ די השכלה פון חסידות» … «זיינען זיי נתרחק געוואָרן פון אַ פּראָסטן אידן».

**4.2 The "chitzon": warm, fast and fooled.** He learns a matter and thinks he already knows it. He is already warm, "and the warmth too is external". He gives opinions before he has absorbed anything. "As he swallows it, so he spits it out." (Contrast 1.5.) Source: LD vol. 1 p. 81, OCR · `LD:558` «והחמימות היא גס כן חיצונית».

**4.3 Excitement from excitement: "Simchas Torah Chassidim".** The Rebbe Rashab greatly valued excitement when it came from the intellect, or from a simple person's *temimus*. "But excitement that comes from excitement he called externality." These are people too lazy to toil at understanding "who want to discharge their duty with mere excitement". Source: LD vol. 1 p. 270, OCR · `LD:2258` «אך להתפעלות שבאה מצד התפעלות היה קורא חיצוניות».

**4.4 The Chassid on wooden stilts.** Young men will not listen; each thinks he is different. He knows what a Chassid is, but "his animal soul, the one wise for evil, tells him that he is a Chassid". Working on davening and on good middos is small stuff to him; he is above it. How is he higher? He stands "on *kavilles*, on wooden legs": his height is wooden and dead, and brings life neither to understanding nor to actual good middos and ahavas Yisrael. What it looks like: - When he does daven at length, "he looks to all sides to see whether people see that he is an *oved*". - He does not answer *amen yehei shmei rabba*, "because he is a Chassid who does nothing for reward". - He knows that Chassidus is bittul, "only not to nullify himself, but the other". - At a farbrengen, especially when he sings a Chassidic niggun, "he thinks that he too is a Chassid". Source: SH 5707, Pesach, 115:6-116:3 · `SH:7034` «זיין נפש הבהמית — דער חכם להרע — זאָגט אים אַז ער איז אַ חסיד» · `SH:7036` «ער שטייט אויף "קאווילעס", אויף הילצערנע פיס» · `SH:7037` «קוקט ער אין אַלע זייטן צי זעט מען אַז ער איז אַ עובד» · `SH:7044` «מיינט ער שוין אַז ער איז אַ חסיד אויך» · `SH:7045` «נאָר ניט מבטל זיין זיך, נאָר יענעם».

**4.5 The Chassidic sigh and the Chassidic dance.** "Chassidus is not with makifim, being satisfied with little": he davens, on Shabbos he davens a bit longer, "he gives a Chassidic *krechtz*, he gives a dance; that is not a Chassid; there must be avodah and toil." Some young men think singing and dancing "are part of the essentials of darkei haChassidus. They make it easy for themselves and want to discharge the essential with it … they discharge their duty by mumbling a chapter of Tanya." Source: SH 5706, 12 Tammuz, 53:1 · `SH:6510` «ער גיט אַ קרעכץ אַ חסידישן קרעכץ, ער גיט אַ טאַנץ, ניט דאָס איז אַ חסיד»; SH 5708, 12 Tammuz, 249:4-5 · `SH:8372` «מען איז יוצא מיט אָפּבראָקן אַ פרק תניא».

**4.6 A saying and a story in place of learning.** "They make do with a Chassidic *vertl* and a story." Both are good and even necessary, but they cannot replace learning to grasp a G-dly concept, and without learning "there is nothing to daven with". Source: SH 5703 (OCR) p. 66 · `SHo:3947` «מען איז יוצא מיט אַ חסידישן װערטל און אַ סיפּור».

**4.7 Collecting other Chassidim's sayings to build an image.** "There are those who collect from R. Aizik's and R. Hillel's books and say little *pshetlach* without understanding what they say. That is lying, the opposite of Chassidus, especially since it is for their own *yeshus*." Chassidus is something essential (עצמי). Source: SH 5684, eve of 20 Kislev, 61:4 · `SH:486` «ובפרט שזהו בשביל ישות ומציאות עצמם».

**4.8 Talk before prayer, then "avodah".** He chats before davening, misses amen and barchu while talking, and when he stands up to daven, it doesn't daven. "Yet he thinks that this is avodah and that this is a Chassid." Source: SH 5691, eve of Simchas Torah, 152:1 · `SH:4789` «נאָר ער מיינט אַז דאָס איז עבודה און אַז דאָס איז אַ חסיד».

**4.9 Chassidic outside, empty inside.** Some young men raised in Chassidic homes and by Chassidic teachers are Chassidic outside, "but their inside is empty". The cause: they never accepted the yoke of a teacher and guide. They educated and guided themselves, "and from such education grew wild fruit, beautiful and shiny outside and rotten inside". The same thing on the scale of a community (5689): "we are Chassidim without Chassidus"; we leaf through the books and carry the yoke with love, "and with all that, from our material place we do not budge". Source: LD vol. 2 p. 161, OCR · `LD:3767` «חיצוניותם של אותס אברכים היא חסידית, אבל פנימיותסם ריקה» · `LD:3769` «יפה ומבריק מבחוצ ורקוב מבפנים»; IK vol. 16, letter ו'קכב · `IK:8252` «ובכל אלה ממקומנו החומרי אין אנו זזים» · `IK:8254` «חסידים אנחנו בלי חסידות».

**4.10 "Circumcised but not *pria*": coarse spirituality.** The Mitteler Rebbe as a child read the Mishnah *mal velo para* as a teaching about avodah. Someone who is stirred to love and fear, "but does not trouble and weary himself with the toil of turning from evil and doing good", and thinks he is already circumcised, "is as though he were not". His spirituality is coarse because *he* is coarse. As long as he has not purified his mind, middos and garments, he can learn Chassidus, understand it well, know the ways of prayer, contemplate until love or fear stirs in his heart, "and with all this he is still entirely outside". Source: KTC p. 20, OCR · `KTC:170` «שאינו מטריח ומייגע את עצמו ביגיעת עבודה בסור מרע ועשה טוב» · `KTC:172` «הנה בכל זה הנה הוא עדיין מבחוץ לגמרי».

**4.11 Knowledge that feeds the ego.** For those coarse of spirit by nature, "the more knowledge of Torah and fear of Heaven they add, the more important they are in their own eyes", the way food feeds a sick man's illness. Learned, G-d-fearing people can use their Torah knowledge "to purify the sheretz" for themselves (the Ramban's *naval birshus haTorah*). Source: KTC pp. 17-18, OCR · `KTC:153` «כל אשר יוסיפו ידיעה בתורה וביראת שמים, הנה עוד הם נחשבים יותר בעיני עצמם» · `KTC:142` «וחכמתם בידיעת התורה תעמוד להם לטהר את השרץ».

**4.12 Avodah for one's own sake.** This is the Alter Rebbe's teaching heard by R. Hillel at an inn in 5571, on the four types of *Avos* 5:10, read as relations between the G-dly and animal souls. When the G-dly soul refines the animal soul, both gain. But "the virtue of the Chassid is that he acts with kindness toward his Maker … and does not think about the advantage that comes to him from this avodah". Whoever works "only for the advantage that comes to him", however high he climbs, leaves "the materiality of the world in its physicality". Source: SH additions, reshimah, 416:3 · `SH:10172` «מעלת החסיד הוא שמתחסד עם קונו» … «ואינו חושב אודות היתרון שנעשה בו מצד עבודה זו» … «הנה חומריות העולם נשאר בגשמיותו».

**4.13 Harshness.** The Rashbatz: "Chassidim forgive quickly." And: "Chassidim must be sharp, but not with the sharpness toward the other, only toward oneself." At home, conduct must be "calm and restful, without anger". When someone sees great and harsh evil in another, and denounces it in public instead of rebuking privately with love, "truly it is his own evil". R. Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev saw the good in everyone. Source: SH 5705 (OCR) p. 61 · `SHo:10479` «חסידים דאַרפן זיין שאַרף, נאָר ניט מיט די שאַרף צו יענעם נאָר צו זיך»; IK vol. 16, letter ה'תתסד · `IK:5904` «שלא ברוגז וכעס»; SH 5691, Purim, 203:1 · `SH:5133` «איז באמת איז דאָס זיין אייגענער רע».

**4.14 Sadness and sighing passing for piety.** - "The Chassidic *baalei avodah* of today have forgotten the true 'alef' of avodah received from the earliest Chassidim: less sighing and more doing." Those who groan over the low state of avodah should groan over their own. - A single action is better than a thousand sighs (2.18). - Young men who find their own faults fall into fasting and self-affliction, and that is the animal soul's trap (3.7). Source: SH 5709, 287:11-12 · `SH:8749` «ווייניקער געקרעכצעט און מער געטאָן»; LD vol. 3 pp. 178-179, OCR · `LD:5448` «שמסדרים עניני עבודתס ולימודס בעצמם» · `LD:5449` «ומקבליס עליהס כמה עניניס של סיגופים ועינוי הגוף».

**4.15 A blessing instead of work.** After the talk on makif and pnimi, someone asked for a blessing. The answer: of course one needs a blessing, but "with a blessing alone we will not get through the world". A blessing is good when it comes with doing: plough the "I", clear the stones, sow. "The beginning of the work is with the 'I'": make a true accounting of who and what I am. Source: LD vol. 1 p. 83, OCR · `LD:576` «שבברכה בלבד לא נעבור את העולס».

**4.16 Showing off.** "Among Chassidim there should be no *bletos* (displays) at all. When one goes to the mikveh no one needs to know." The Rebbe Rashab (5662): "an *oved* goes on his feet and a *maskil* goes on his head. When the maskil walks, it is heard and seen; when the oved walks, it is not noticed at all." Source: LD vol. 3 p. 87, OCR · `LD:4730` «בחסידיס לא צריך להיות בכלל הענין של בליטות» · `LD:4732` «וכאשר ייעובדיי הולך לא ניכר כלל».

## 5. For anyone

The Rayatz addresses many of these instructions explicitly to *every* Jew, and says so. He tells business people: "I say this not as a guide warning one section of Israel but as one who loves all Israel, as good advice" (LD:737). The items marked *(For anyone)* above are collected here, with a few more. The practices are Jewish practices and are named as such.

- **A businessman can be a tzaddik.** In the market, knowing who gives the strength to earn, he is "a chariot for G-dliness" (2.1: `SH:571`, `SH:572`).
- **Simple people come close through Tehillim and ahavas Yisrael.** Through "the simplicity of their service in saying Tehillim, even without knowing the meaning of the words, and ahavas Yisrael", simple people draw inwardly close to G-d, "for the main thing is the deed and the service in simplicity of heart" (KTC p. 5, OCR · `KTC:22` «על ידי תמימות עבודתם באמירת תהלים»). Compare *Limud HaChassidus* A2: those who cannot follow the explanations can take the mussar part.
- **Simple Chassidim took *chayus* in avodah bepoel,** and it raised them to "a truly important level" (SH 5707, 95:4 · `SH:6846` «אפילו גאָר גאר פשוט'ע אידן חסידים האָבן מקבל געווען אַ חיות אין דרכי עבודה ופועל»). See the Polotsk shopkeeper (S14) and Shalom Yosef the builder (S6).
- **The overseas craftsman's morning.** A shoemaker, tailor or butcher "would get up at 2 in the morning, go to the beis midrash to say Tehillim and daven early", then take his pack on his shoulders and think Pirkei Avos or Tehillim as he walked (SH 5703 (OCR) p. 23 · `SHo:3549` «געהן אין ביהמ"ד זאָגען תהלים און דאַװנען בהשכמה»).
- **Before prayer:** a moment on "who is praying and before Whom" (2.4).
- **During the day:** a book in the pocket and a line in every free moment (2.12). Guard permitted speech (2.12). Learn something from whatever you see (2.20).
- **Every day:** revive another person, "simply in material things"; if a day passes without it, feel it (2.17).
- **At night:** an honest accounting without self-love; "tomorrow should be different from today" (2.11).
- **In low spirits:** one action rather than a thousand sighs, a chapter of Tehillim, a line of Tanya, and then contemplate reasons for joy (2.18).
- **With others:** see their good. Understand how hard change is from your own struggle (2.17, 3.2). Be sharp only with yourself (4.13).

## What this means for integration (our synthesis, not the Rayatz's words)

1. **For the Rayatz, integration *is* the definition of Chassidus, not an application of it.** "What is understood in the mind should come down into the heart … into actual avodah" (1.1). "The whole point of Chassidus is to change the nature of his middos" (1.2). "Chassidus that does not make a person a Chassid is not Chassidus" (1.4). So the unity idea mapped in SPINE and ONE-PROCESS is, on his terms, only half-received until it has changed what a person *wants* (3.9, LD:1093). His test is not how well you understand, but whether you have "moved from your place" (3.6).

2. **He gives a working model of the gap and how to cross it.** There is a wall between mind and heart and between heart and deed, and the crossing takes time (1.7). Three things break through: - **absorption** by lingering: repeated, unhurried hisbonenus (1.9; the Mitteler Rebbe's "hisbodedus, iyun, hiskakvus", 2.22); - **an honest accounting without self-love** at night, followed by the next morning's davening (1.8, 2.11); - **prayer** as "the vessel for actual avodah", the place where understanding turns into resolve and resolve into the day (1.12). Integration is therefore a daily cycle, not an event: learn → linger → account → daven → carry the resolution into the market → account again (2.1).

3. **The target is the *nature*, and nature can be acquired.** Changing "the nature of the middos" (1.2) is made concrete in three ways: - the Rashab's "by much habit it became nature, until I trust my body" (1.19); - R. Shmuel Munkes' single resolution kept for life (S2); - going against one's temperament, "if cold by nature, do mitzvos warmly" (1.18). The order he gives for middos is diagnostic before therapeutic: first "light up where you stand" (1.15), then contemplate yourself, then break, then discard or wash (1.17). Some traits cannot be fixed, only replaced; good traits must also be "worked out" and put under the mind (1.14, 1.16).

4. **No one integrates alone.** The chaver ("two G-dly souls against one animal soul"), the mashpia who works on *himself* so that it affects others, the elder who leads "from light to heavy", and the farbrengen where the speaker is broken and so the listener breaks: these are not extras but the mechanism (2.14-2.15). Self-made programmes produce "fruit shiny outside and rotten inside", or collapse into self-punishment (4.9, 3.7). The stories are part of the method: they carry a lived movement where an idea alone would not reach (1b, 2.21).

5. **The counterfeits are the same failure seen from different sides: the "I" left intact.** All of these leave the ego standing: - knowledge that feeds the ego (4.11); - excitement born of excitement (4.3); - the stilts of "I am above davening and middos" (4.4); - sigh, dance and saying in place of toil (4.5-4.6); - avodah done for one's own spiritual gain (4.12); - sharpness aimed at others (4.13). His counter is a set of plain signs of the real thing: quiet ("dry wood burns without noise", 2.8), not being seen (4.16), speed to forgive (4.13), and above all *ahavas Yisrael in deed*, "higher than understanding a deep matter" (2.17).

**The five integration teachings that matter most in these sources:** 1. The purpose of Chassidus is to change the *nature* of one's middos, by daily inner work in thought, speech and deed (1.2-1.3). 2. The wall between mind, heart and deed, the time lag, and how it is broken: an honest accounting without self-love, then davening (1.7-1.8). 3. Prayer as "the vessel for actual avodah", prepared by learning Chassidus and hisbonenus: the four-part avodah of prayer and the minimum "who prays and before Whom" (1.12, 2.2-2.4). 4. Habit made into nature: "by much habit it became nature, until I trust my body", with itkafia against temperament (1.18-1.19, S2). 5. Integration through others: the chaver, mashpia, elder and farbrengen, and ahavas Yisrael as both the gate and the test (2.14-2.17).
