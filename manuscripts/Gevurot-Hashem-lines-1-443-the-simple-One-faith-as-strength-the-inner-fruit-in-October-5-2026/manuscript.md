Gevurot Hashem ("The Mighty Acts of G-d") is the Maharal's book on the Exodus and the Pesach night. It was printed in Krakow in 1582. It opens with three long introductions about miracles and nature. Then it walks through the story from Avraham to the sea, and it ends with the seder and the Haggadah. This first range holds the introductions and the chapters up to Moshe as a young man.

What matters here for the guide is not the story. It is the frame the Maharal builds before the story starts. He says G-d is "simple being", and that because He is simple, nothing can be outside Him. He says oneness does not mean "one thing among many" but "He is all, and there is nothing outside Him". And he says faith is not an opinion. Faith is the strength of a person's existence, and it brings joy.

## 2.1 Simple, so nothing is outside Him

The Maharal's starting point is not the Rambam's "G-d is intellect". He rejects that. G-d is not called "the Intellect, blessed be He", but "the Holy One, blessed be He" (34). He is

- "פשוט בתכלית הפשיטות" (34, Second Introduction 14): "utterly simple".

And from simplicity he draws the unity at once:

- "ומזה בעצמו שהוא בתכלית הפשיטות, אין דבר נבדל ממנו" (34): "and because He is utterly simple, nothing is separate from Him."

The reasoning is plain. Anything with a definition (גדר, a boundary that says what it is and is not) has an outside. G-d has no definition. So there is no "outside" for anything to stand in. His essence is

- "עצמותו הויה פשוטה" (37, Second Introduction 17): "His essence is simple being."

And then the sentence that the whole book leans on:

- "והכל אפס זולתו, והוייתו יתברך נקרא הוייה, וזולתו אפס" (38, Second Introduction 18): "All is nothing apart from Him. His being is called being, and apart from Him is nothing."

This is close to the Chabad "there is nothing else" (אין עוד, F05, F06). But note the road. The Tanya reaches it through the letters of speech that keep saying the world into being (F01). The Maharal reaches it through simplicity: what has no edge leaves no room outside.

## 2.2 Separate, and so present

The Maharal keeps two words side by side all through the book: נבדל ("separate", set apart, not mixed with matter) and כולל ("inclusive", holding everything). In the First Introduction the secret of the Chariot is that He is "נבדל מכל הנמצאים" (12): "separate from all beings", and in the same breath all beings stand by Him. His picture is the soul and the body. The soul is not the body and is not mixed with it, and that is exactly why it can give the body life (13). Separateness is not distance. It is the condition for holding everything.

This is his answer to a question Chabad answers with tzimtzum (contraction). How can the One be both above and within? The Maharal does not use tzimtzum here. He says: because He is not a thing among things, He can be with every thing. We come back to this in the later ranges, where he says it outright (see the report on lines 983-1863, the chapter on "His greatness is His humility").

## 2.3 "Oneness means He is all"

The clearest definition of unity in this range is in chapter 5, about Avraham, who was born to Terach the idol maker. Light came out of darkness. How can opposites come from each other? Because the One who makes them is the One who holds everything:

- "יחידו של עולם המאחד את המציאות" (162, 5:19): "the Only One of the world, who makes existence one". - "ואף המציאות הרע הוא מאחד" (162): "even the existence of evil He makes one". - "וזהו ענין האחדות, שאין לענין האחדות רק שהוא הכל, ואין חוץ ממנו" (163, 5:20): "This is what oneness means: only that He is all and there is nothing outside Him."

This is the key sentence for the guide. Oneness here is not a number. It is "all, with nothing outside". It matches F09 (the unity of opposites). The Maharal adds that because the Actor is One who is all, His work must also be a whole, and a whole includes opposites. That is why good and bad, light and dark, both come from Him (we see this again at 734 and 1446 in the next ranges).

## 2.4 The Shema: alone in every direction

In chapter 12 the Maharal reads the six words of the Shema as the six directions of space (up, down, and four sides):

- "שאנו מודים שהוא יחיד בכל הצדדין" (334, 12:11): "we affirm that He is alone in every direction". - "שאין כאן צד אחר כלל" (334): "that there is no other side at all".

The six directions are "the division of existence". To say He rules all six is to say there is no "other side" at all. The seventh point, the middle, is the holy point with no direction (72, and "דמיון הנקודה שבעיגול" (336): "like the point in a circle"). This gives a clear meditation for the Shema (see 4.2).

## 2.5 Where the unity is hidden

The Maharal is honest that the unity is not yet seen in the world:

- "וכל זמן שזרעו של עמלק בעולם, אין כאן אחדות בעולם" (82, Third Introduction 12): "As long as Amalek's seed is in the world, there is no oneness in the world."

Amalek, for him, stands for the principle of "two", of opposition for its own sake. So the oneness is true in itself but not yet revealed below. This matches the guide's F11 and S68 (the hiding seen as His). The unity is a fact; its showing is a task and a promise.

## 2.6 Only He is truly one

- "אין האחדות כי אם להקב"ה" (292, 9:35): "Oneness belongs only to the Holy One."

Every other "one" has lack in it. A person can be "one" only by being joined to Him. We will see this worked out as a practice in the next ranges (the whole Pesach lamb, the united family).

**Caution (ours).** The lines about a weak body bringing blessing (326, 370) describe what suffering *can* do. They are not a call to weaken the body. The guide must never tell anyone to fast, go without sleep, or harm themselves. Use these lines only to give meaning to hardship that has already come.

## 4. Meditation and practice

The Maharal is not a writer of techniques. But this range gives five clear practices.

## 4.1 The simplicity look (proposed technique, ours)

Take the chain of 34-38 as a contemplation. It fits the guide's `depth` and `edge` moves:

1. Anything I can define has an edge. Past the edge is "not it".
2. He has no definition (34). So He has no edge.
3. So there is no "past the edge" where I could stand apart from Him (34).
4. "His being is called being, and apart from Him is nothing" (38).

This is a different entry from the Tanya's "letters saying the world" (F01). It works for a mind that likes logic more than pictures. **Proposed id: `mh_simple_no_edge`.**

## 4.2 The Shema in six directions (proposed technique, ours)

From 334-336: while saying the six words of the first verse, turn attention (not the body) to each direction in turn: above, below, front, back, right, left. With each, rest in "no other side here". On "One" (אחד), come to the middle point, which has no direction at all. This joins `before_whom` to `breadth`. **Proposed id: `mh_six_sides`.**

## 4.3 Chesed as clinging

One who does kindness "הוא דבק במדת טובו יתברך" (180, 6:14): "clings to His measure of goodness". Kindness is not only good behaviour. It is a way of being joined to Him. This matches `act` and `carry`. For the guide: after an act of kindness, pause to notice "I just did something of His." (Ours.)

## 4.4 Amen with all your strength

"גדול העונה אמן יותר מן המברך" (283, 9:26): "the one who answers amen is greater than the one who blesses". Why? Because answering amen is pure faith, and "כי האמונה אשר הוא מאמין הוא הדבוק בו וחוזקו" (287): "the faith he believes is his clinging to Him and its strength". The practice (ours): say amen slowly, as a small act of leaning your whole weight on Him. It fits `lips`.

## 4.5 Offering as drawing near

"אין קרבן זולת הקירוב והדבוק בו יתברך" (217, 8:8): "an offering is nothing but drawing near and clinging to Him". And "כי אין לעולם קיום רק על ידי הדבוק בו יתברך" (224): "the world has no standing except by clinging to Him". Study of the offerings is itself the drawing near. In the last range he builds this into a full teaching (the offering as the return of the effect to its Cause, 1741-1743).

## 5. The big human questions

**Purpose.** Things are made to be complete, and a whole includes opposites (162-164). Man's path runs from youth, where matter rules, to maturity, where form rules (134, 317). (His Israel/Egypt "form/matter" language, 118-137, is his framing of peoples. The guide takes only the human arc from it, per D28.)

**Suffering.** Three answers in this range. First, evil does not come down from His side: "אין רע יורד לעולם מצד עצמו" (186): "no evil comes down into the world from His side". Bad comes through the receiver. Second, evil has no fit existence of its own: "ולרע אין ראוי מציאות כלל" (400, 16:13). Third, hardship can renew a person: He brings pain "to cleanse", until they think they are lost, and then "ויחדש אותם בריה חדשה" (257): "He makes them a new creation". And absence itself opens room for a new form: "ההעדר דבק בנמצאים, ומצד ההעדר שדבק בנמצאים תחול צורה אחרת" (425): "absence clings to beings, and from that absence a new form arrives".

**The soul.** The inner always grows out of an outer shell: "כל דבר שיש לו מדריגה פנימית, הוא יוצא מתוך דבר שהוא חיצון לו" (429, 18:7): "everything with an inner level comes out from inside something outer to it". Like fruit inside its peel. This is a gentle reading of a person's rough outer life: the shell is not the enemy, it is where the fruit forms.

**Providence.** Miracle and nature are one order: "הדברים אשר הם טבעיים ואשר הם בלתי טבעיים ענין אחד" (30). And what is set can change: "שהמסודר יכול להשתנות על ידי תשובה ורחמים" (42): "what is set in order can change through return and mercy". So providence is not fate.

**Trust.** Faith gives a person solid existence (200). It frees him: the one who trusts has no other master (284).

**Joy and sadness.** Faith and joy go together; anger and sadness show a lack of faith (206-207). Moshe's anger at the rock is read this way: "כי האמונה מביא לידי שירה ושמחה" (206): "faith brings song and joy".

**Love and awe.** Kindness is clinging (180). The Shema as "no other side" (334). Awe, in this book, comes from His separateness; love comes from joining (worked out at 789 in the next range).

**Narrowness and width.** "כי אין צרות רק בגשם" (67): "narrowness exists only in the physical". At the Temple, where people bowed to the separate level, "ושם הוא הכל מרווח" (66): "and there all is wide". A real image for the guide: anxiety is tightness; bowing to the One is room. (Ours to use; his to say.)

## 6. With Chabad: first notes (ours)

- **Agree.** "All is nothing apart from Him" (38) and "He is all and nothing outside Him" (163) say the same unity as Tanya's Gate of Unity. The pure core under sin (220) matches the Tanya's teaching that the divine soul is never touched by sin.
- **Differ in road.** The Maharal's unity comes from simplicity and "no definition" (34), not from continual speech (F01) or tzimtzum. He also holds "separate" (נבדל) and "including" (כולל) together without needing a contraction.
- **Differ with the Rambam.** He says plainly that G-d is not "intellect" (34-37). The Rambam's "He is the knower, the knowing and the known" is set aside. Only negative speech fits (35). On this point he is close to the Rambam's negative attributes but denies the Rambam's intellect model.
