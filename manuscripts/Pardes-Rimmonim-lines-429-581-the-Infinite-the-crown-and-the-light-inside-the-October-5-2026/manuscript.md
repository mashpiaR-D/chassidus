# Pardes Rimmonim, lines 429-581: the Infinite, the crown, and the light inside the vessels

*Text read:* ramak/_text/pardes-rimmonim.txt (plain export of Pre-Lurianic Kabbalah/Pardes Rimmonim/corpus.json, one Sefaria segment per line)  
*Range:* lines 429-581: Gate 3 (Is Ein Sof the Crown?) and Gate 4 (Essence and Vessels)

---
These two gates are the heart of the Ramak's teaching on the unity of God. Gate 3 separates the Infinite (Ein Sof) from the first of the sefirot, the Crown (Keter), and says what sets the Infinite apart. Gate 4 asks the question that decides everything else: are the sefirot God Himself, or are they tools He uses? The Ramak's answer, "both, and they are one", with its parables of water in colored cups, sunlight through colored glass, a great fire wrapped in spheres, and the soul in the body, is the teaching the Alter Rebbe cites by name in the Tanya. Here the Ramak also says, in so many words, that nothing has existence apart from Him, and that He is "inside every being... and without Him it has no existence".

## 2.1 What sets the Infinite apart (lines 433-436)

The Ramak names three differences between the Emanator and everything emanated.

- **Time.** "He brought time into being, and is not a being in time" (הוא המציא הזמן ולא שהוא בעל זמן, line 433, Pardes 3:1:5). Everything emanated had a "time" before which it was not. He never did.
- **Need.** Every level needs the one above it. The Zohar says the sefirot "take counsel" with one another ("let us make man"), but the Cause of causes asks no one: "See now that I, I am He, and there is no god with Me." He is "the source of life, and life comes from Him, until He is the life of life" (אלא הוא מקור החיים והחיים נמצאים ממנו עד שהוא חיות החיים, line 433).
- **Name.** Each sefirah has a Name that bounds it. Of Him, "there is no name and no point by which we can call Him" (שאין שם ונקודה שנוכל לכנותו בה, line 435). Even "Ein Sof", "without end", is chosen "because inquiry into Him has no end" (מפני שהחקירה בו אין לו סוף, line 436).

The Crown, however high, is on the other side of that line. "Even thought is forbidden in Him" (כי אפי' המחשבה אסורה בו, line 439), and His Godliness "is revealed only by means of the sheath of His light and His garment, which is the Crown" (line 443).

## 2.2 Why the ten are not God's tools, and not only our perspectives (lines 499-525)

The Ramak sets two teachers against each other.

**Recanati** said the sefirot are like a craftsman's tools. God does not change; the tools do. His parable: a king tells one servant to reward the diligent and another to strike the lazy. "The king does no harm and no good to either of them; all is by his command" (והנה המלך אינו מריע ואינו מטיב לאחד מהם אלא הכל במצותו, line 504).

The Ramak's answer is sharp. God's knowing is not like ours. "He is the knowledge, and He is the knower, and He is the known" (אלא הוא הדעת והוא היודע והוא הידוע, line 518). If the sefirot were outside Him, He would be wise with a wisdom outside Himself. Worse: reward and punishment would come from the tools by their nature, and "God would have left the earth in the hands of the measures" (אין באלקות יכולת המשכיר והמשגיח אלא עזב ה' את הארץ ביד המדות, line 518). A person who sinned would have to pray to the servant. "This is only the view of the philosophers." And then, with great courtesy: "it is clear to us that R. Menachem was one of those sunk in complete faith" (line 518); he cannot have meant it.

**R. David** (in Magen David) said the opposite: the sefirot are God's essence, and they are ten only in relation to the ones who receive from Him. He quotes the Zohar: "He is all, He is one without division" (הוא כלא, הוא חד בלא פרודא, line 508). The Ramak answers: then there is no order, no channel, no reason one sefirah should receive from another; the Shema's work of uniting the sefirot means nothing; the sefirot would exist only while they act. And the soul, whose "longing is only for them" (אשר ודאי תשוקת הנשמה אינה כ"א אליהם, line 523), would have nothing to cleave to. "He knocked down a wall to bring in a mat" (line 525).

## 2.3 The Ramak's own view (lines 526-536)

He begins with the Infinite in itself. He is not blessed by anyone; He is the one "who blesses and praises and glorifies and gives life, from the first point of His emanation to the last point, from the horns of the wild ox to the eggs of lice" (line 526). Of Him we can say "no judgment and no mercy, no anger and no wrath, no change and no limit and no measure at all, not before the emanation and not now after it":

> לא דין ולא רחמים לא רוגז ולא כעס לא שנוי ולא גבול ולא שום מדה לא קודם האצילות לא עתה אחר האצילות (line 526, Pardes 4:4:1)

Then: at the start of emanation He emanated "ten sefirot that are from His own essence, united with Him; He and they are all one complete unity" (עשר ספירות אשר הם מעצמותו מתיחדות בו והוא והם הכל אחדות שלימה, line 526). These are the soul, and they are clothed in ten sefirot that have names and are vessels.

**The water and the cups.** Water poured into cups of white, red and green seems to take their colors. But "the change of color is not acquired by the water" (אין שינוי הגוון ההוא קנוי במים, line 529). It is so "by accident, not in essence", and only in the eyes of the one who sees. So the light of the Emanator in the sefirot: it looks like judgment here and mercy there, but the change belongs to the vessels, "according to the receivers, not according to themselves".

**The ten attics.** Picture ten rooms stacked one above another, each with a window in the middle, all in a line, so that sunlight comes down through all of them. If one room tilts, its window no longer faces the light, and it and every room below it go dark. "In truth the darkness is not from a lack of light... it comes from the bad readiness of the one who receives":

> והנה באמת אין החשך מהעדר האור וחשכו בעצם האור אמנם נמשך מפני רוע הכנת המקבל (line 534, Pardes 4:4:9)

"For the Holy One rests His Presence and His providence only in a whole place" (line 534). When people act badly, a sefirah "turns its face away and does not look toward the light, from its shame and embarrassment" (line 534). That is what judgment is: not His anger, but a face turned away.

## 2.4 One, but not as a number (lines 537-545)

The Ramak reads Elijah's prayer (Patach Eliyahu) word by word. "You are One and not in number" means one "in the way of negation, that He is one in that there is no second":

> אלא הכוונה על צד השלילה שהוא אחד בערך שאין שני (line 542, Pardes 4:5:6)

"One" said of a man bounds him; said of God it only says "not two". He quotes Ibn Gabirol: "You are One, and in the secret of Your oneness the wise of heart are astonished." And from the prayer itself: "No thought can grasp You at all" (לית מחשבה תפיסא בך כלל, line 537); "apart from You there is no unity, above or below" (ובר מינך לית יחודא בעלאי ותתאי, line 537); "when You withdraw from them, all the names remain like a body without a soul" (line 537).

He adds a point the guide needs: the sefirot both reveal and hide. To the upper worlds they reveal Him; "and by them You are hidden from human beings" (ובהון אתכסיאת מבני נשא, line 545). The hiding is mostly for us.

## 2.5 The light inside is Him (lines 550-561)

This is where the Ramak comes closest to Chassidus. The essence that spreads through the sefirot and the Infinite "are all one thing, united. There is only a difference in our grasp of Him, between before His spreading and after it":

> והעצם ההוא והא"ס הכל דבר אחד מיוחד. אלא שיש חלוק אל השגתינו בו בין קודם התפשטותו אל אחר התפשטותו (line 550, Pardes 4:6:4)

"Like water that stays one with its spring even after it spreads" (כדמיון המים המיוחדים במעיין אפי' אחר התפשטותם, line 550). So the heavens and the earth were made by the simple essence itself, not by tools. And it all came from Him "in a chain of will and love, and not by necessity at all, only as a free gift" (וממנו נמשך הכל בשלשלת הרצון והאהבה ולא בהכרח כלל רק בנדבה, line 550). Of His essence itself we know only "that all hangs on Him like an amulet on the arm" (אלא שנדע ונכיר שהכל תלוי בו כקמיע בזרוע, line 550).

The Zohar's "light that does not stand in light" is dark only from too much brightness: "the darkness of the Infinite is from the height of brightness, which is not grasped" (line 552), unlike the darkness of the lowest sefirah, which is a lack. And he ends the chapter: "the cause of life is the cause of cleaving" (כי סבת החיים הוא בסבות הדביקות, line 561), on "you who cleave to God are all alive today".

## 2.6 He made them a place inside Himself (lines 569-574)

How can the Infinite, which does not change, be one with sefirot that were emanated and so "had a time before"? The Ramak's parable: picture a fire so great and bright that anything placed in it would burn at once. Within it He made a sphere to hide the fire on all sides; within that, another; ten spheres, then more, until at the center something could live in a sweet, even warmth. "All this is not because of the light and the heat, but because of the weakness of the creature" (אין כל זה לסבת האור והחום אלא לחולשת הבריאה, line 571). The fire itself did not change: "as its strength was then, so is its strength now" (שככחו אז כחו עתה, line 572). What was new was only the spheres, and for them "He made a place in His own essence" (המציא להם מקום בעצמותו, line 573). "Length here is sin and guilt" (line 574).

This is the Ramak's tzimtzum, long before the Ari's: not a withdrawal, but covers inside the fire, for our sake.

## 2.7 Nothing but Him (lines 575-581)

The last chapter reads the Raaya Mehemna on the Shema. All His names are for the sake of creatures: "before He created the world, for whom would He be called merciful or judge?" He fills His names like the soul fills the body, "and no limb is empty of it" (ולית אבר פנוי מנה, line 575). He was "before every being, and He is inside every being, and He is after every being" (line 575). And the Ramak explains:

> הוא סבת כל הויה וקיומה וחיותה, וזולתו אין לה מציאות. והוא נשמת ההויות וקיומן (line 581, Pardes 4:10:7)

"He is the cause of every being, its standing and its life, and without Him it has no existence. He is the soul of all beings and their standing." And a few lines before: the sefirot "all draw from Him and have no existence apart from Him" (מפני שכלם שואבים ממנו ואין להם מציאות זולתו, line 580).

And one more line that is the guide's own F03 in a sentence: "the revealing is the cause of the hiding, and the hiding is the cause of the revealing" (וזהו שההתגלות סבת ההעלם, וההעלם סבת ההתגלות, line 580).

## 3. States of consciousness

**The colored glass (RK-S02, proposed).** Inside: what hurts me looks like His anger, but I know the red is in the glass, not in the light. The light that reaches me is one light. How to enter: take the harsh thing and ask, "what is the glass here, and what is the light behind it?" (lines 529-530). This sits between S68 (The hiding seen as His) and S85 (Seeing Him in what happens), but it adds something neither has: the harshness is real, and it is also not His. Guard: the Ramak does not say the pain is unreal; he says it is "by accident, not in essence".

**The tilted window (RK-S03, proposed).** Inside: I am in the dark, and the dark is not that the sun went out; my window turned. The light is exactly where it was. How to enter: the parable of the ten attics (line 534). What moves a person out of it: turning the face back toward the light, which is teshuvah. This gives S04 (Low and heavy) and S11 (The verdict: I'm my mistakes) a way out that does not deny the fault: "the darkness comes from the bad readiness of the one who receives". It also has the most human image in the range: a sefirah that turns its face away "from shame". Shame is what turns the window.

**Hanging on Him like an amulet (S62 Only Him, S63 The world held).** The Ramak's "all hangs on Him like an amulet on the arm" (line 550) and "without Him it has no existence" (line 581) are S62 and S63 in his words.

**Knowing that you do not know (S35, S36).** "One not in number" (line 542) and "no thought grasps You" (line 537): the mind reaches the place where the word "one" only means "not two". Ibn Gabirol's "the wise of heart are astonished" is the feeling of it.

**The humility of the teacher (S59 True lowliness).** He praises the Ramban for writing "I do not understand this passage" rather than break with what he received: "he chose to say he did not understand, rather than overturn the reason of his tradition" (line 461). And for "blimah" in Sefer Yetzirah: "a person should close his mouth and not multiply words about their essence" (line 461).

## 4. Meditation and practice

**The ladder up and the ladder down (RK-T03, proposed).** The clearest contemplative instruction in the range (line 550). "This is the order of grasping hidden things: from the later to the earlier, and then from the earlier to the later":

> כי כן סדר השגת הנסתרות מן המאוחר אל הקודם ואחר כך מן הקודם אל המאוחר (line 550, Pardes 4:6:4)

From the world of doing we come to understand the world of forming, then creating, then emanation; from emanation we recognize the essence spreading in it; then we recognize how all is bound to the First Cause. Then we come back down: emanation bound to creating, creating to forming, forming to doing, "all one, united, like a strong chain and a lamp of joined pieces" (הכל יחיד מיוחד כשלשלת חזק ואמיץ ומנורה של פרקים, line 550). His proof text is Job's "from my flesh I see God". The way up starts with what is in front of you.

How (ours, from his words): begin with one physical thing. Ask what forms it, what makes it this and not something else, what wisdom is in it, and what gives it being at all. Stop at "all hangs on Him like an amulet". Then come back down the same steps and see the thing again, now held. What it is for: to see the world as one chain, not as a pile. Ties to `walk_down` and C1, but it adds the upward half first, and the return, which the guide's technique lacks. Guard: of His essence itself "there is no grasping", only that all hangs on Him (line 550).

**Coming back to the same passage (line 550).** "Let him labor at the doors of study once, twice and three times, and he will find rest for his soul" (ישקוד על דלתי העיון פעם ופעמים ושלש וימצא לנפשו נופש). This is `chazarah`, and its fruit is rest.

**Few words (lines 461, 574).** Twice he stops himself. "Length here is sin and guilt... and brevity is beautiful" (line 574). The guard on `edge`: at the top of the ladder, say less.

**The Shema (line 521).** The Ramak's kavanah: in "Hear, Israel" to unite the sefirot, and in "One" "to unite the Kingship with all of them and all of them with the Kingship, and to raise her by the steps by which she came down". The one who says "One" lifts what fell.

**The deed and God's knowledge (lines 565-568).** On the old question of how God can know what we will do while we choose freely, the Ramak gives a Kabbalist's answer. God's knowing lives in Da'at; human choice comes from Malchut, "the house of choosing". Before the deed, His knowing "makes no impression" above. Only after the deed does the knowing become actual, which is why God said to Abraham after the binding, "now I know" (עתה ידעתי כי ירא אלקים אתה, line 566), knowing in the sense of union. And choice can overpower what was known: people "strengthen the choice over the knowledge" (line 568). For practice: the deed is where it happens. Ties to `act` and S81.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why does the world exist?** Not from need: "only a free gift, His measure being to do good to another, not that He was forced, only by will and love" (אלא רק נדבה שנדבו מדתו להטיב אל זולתו ולא שהוכרח רק ברצון ואהבה, line 569). And "He needed, out of His love for the creation, to emanate a vessel for His work" (הוצרך לאהבתו להבריאה להאציל כלי למעשהו, line 568). Everything, down to the vessels, is love taking a form.

**Suffering and judgment.** Judgment is not a change in Him. It is light held back when a window turns (line 534), or light seen through red glass (line 530). Even when a sefirah is "clothed in strong judgment", the constant flow from Him "never lessens and never grows" (הראשון הוא התמידי אשר לא יחסר ולא יעדיף, line 580); without it the sefirah would not even be able to judge.

**Being held.** "He is the life of life" (line 433), "no limb is empty of Him" (line 575), "without Him it has no existence" (line 581). And His rule reaches everything "whether they go far or come near" (אם ירחקו ואם יקרבו, line 466).

**Love.** "For the love of Israel He narrowed His Presence between the two poles of the ark" (לאהבת ישראל היה מצמצם שכינתו בין שני בדי הארון, line 462), and: "this is the power of our Maker's greatness, that without limit He limits Himself and narrows Himself for the good of those below" (line 462). The narrowing is itself love.

**The soul.** It longs for the sefirot (line 523); it lives by cleaving (line 561); and its likeness to God is only in this, that it rules the whole body and no limb is empty of it (lines 575-576).

**Free will.** Answered above (section 4): choice is real because God's knowing does not act until we do.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **The Tanya cites this gate five times.** For "He is the knower, the knowledge and the known", the Alter Rebbe writes "and the Kabbalists agreed with him, as is written in the Pardes of the Ramak" (והודו לו חכמי הקבלה, כמו שכתוב בפרדס מהרמ״ק, Tanya line 88; also lines 636, 708, 836). The Ramak's own words are at line 518 and line 580 ("He and His wisdom are all one essence").
- **But the Tanya limits it.** "Without the tzimtzum and the clothing, it does not apply at all to say 'He is the knower'", for the Infinite is above knowing altogether (אבל בלי צמצום והלבשה הנזכר לﬠיל לא שייך כלל לומר, Tanya line 860). The Ramak himself says of the Infinite in itself "no measure at all, not before and not after" ( line 526), so the two are closer than they look; but the Alter Rebbe puts the line higher.
- **Cause and effect is not creation.** In Iggeret HaKodesh 20 the Alter Rebbe writes: "what the Ramak wrote about the chain of cause and effect" (ומה שכתב הרמ״ק ענין השתלשלות עילה ועלול, Tanya line 1287) is true only "in the chain of the sefirot among themselves" (בהשתלשלות הספירות בספירות עצמן, Tanya line 1287). The vessels of Atzilut are Godliness "to create something from nothing like the Infinite, and not only by a chain of cause and effect" (Tanya line 1286). The Ramak's whole Gate 2 and Gate 4 are built on cause and effect; the Alter Rebbe keeps it for the sefirot and says the world itself comes only from the Essence, from nothing. The Ramak's fire and spheres (line 569) is the nearest he comes to the Alter Rebbe's view.
- **Flint and ember.** The Ramak says the attributes were hidden in Him before emanation "like a flame hidden in the coal, like fire hidden in a flint" (line 580). R. Aharon of Strashelye, in Sha'arei HaYichud VeEmunah, records that he heard "explicitly from our holy Rebbe" (the Alter Rebbe) that even this parable fits only the hidden root of Keter, not Ein Sof (see shaarei-hayichud/lines-97-281.md, on line 132 of that book). Chassidus refuses to place the attributes in the Infinite even as hidden potential.
- **Where they agree.** "The revealing is the cause of the hiding, and the hiding the cause of the revealing" ( line 580) is F03. "Like water that stays one with its spring" (line 550) and "without Him it has no existence" (line 581) are F01 and F04 before Chassidus. The Ramak's fire and spheres "for the weakness of the creature" (line 571) is the Tanya's reason for tzimtzum in Shaar HaYichud.
