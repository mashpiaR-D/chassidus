Netzach Yisrael ("The Eternity of Israel") is the Maharal's book on exile and redemption. It asks a hard question. If a people is bound to G-d, why does it suffer so long? The first half, covered here, is mostly about exile: why it came, why it lasts, and why it cannot be forever.

For our purposes the book matters because of what it says on the way. To explain exile the Maharal has to explain how many things come from One, how the caused stands to its Cause, and what kind of bond with G-d can never be undone. Those answers are general. They are about any person and any created thing, and we read them that way.

**A note on D28.** This book compares Israel and the nations many times, often sharply (for example 24, 66, 118, 323-330, 432, 447-450). Those passages are not used. Where the Maharal speaks of "Israel" we take only lines whose logic is about the soul, the caused and the Cause, or the bond with G-d, and we say so when we widen them.

## 2.1 He is the only real cause

The book opens with a rule about causes. A builder is not the true cause of the house, and a father is not the true cause of the son, since the house and son go on after them. The true cause must stay with its effect: "אבל סבת קיומו מסבה אחרת, הוא השם יתברך, אשר הוא סבת הכל, ואין סבה זולתו" (2, Introduction 2): "but the cause of its standing is another cause: G-d, who is the cause of all, and there is no cause besides Him."

That one sentence holds a whole teaching. Everything that stands is being held up now. The builder only moved the beams. What keeps anything in being is G-d, and nothing else.

## 2.2 How can many come from One?

The philosophers asked: if G-d is simple and one, how does a world of many different things come from Him? Some answered with a chain of intermediaries, each one making the next. The Maharal says this is a serious error: "ואין לך דמיון עבודה זרה יותר מזה" (29, 3:4): "there is no likeness of idolatry greater than this." If the world came through middlemen, G-d's glory would be taken out of it.

His own answer (30-39): from the One comes first a single thing, a "beginning" (reishit). Once there is a receiver, more can be added to it, because every created thing lacks and needs completing. So the many come "מצד המקבל", from the side of the receiver: "והרי חלוף ורבוי הנמצאים הם מצד המקבל" (30, 3:5): "the difference and manyness of beings come from the side of the receiver."

He sums it up: "בעבור כי הוא יתברך אחד, ומאתו נמצא גם כן אחד" (39, 3:14): "because He is One, and from Him too comes one." Every giving of "the first" to G-d (first fruits, the tithe, challah, the first bite of bread) is a way of saying this. The first belongs to Him because the first is one.

## 2.3 The One includes opposites; two cannot stand in His place

When an enemy set up an idol in the Temple, he set up two, one for fire and one for water, to "complete" it. One fell on the other and broke its hand. The Maharal: "והאחד הוא יכול על הכל, אף על ההפכים, כי דבר שהוא אחד כולל הכל" (186, 8:6): "the One is able over all, even over opposites, for what is one includes all." And "כי קצר מצע האחדות מלהשתרע שם השניות" (187, 8:7): "for the bed of oneness is too short for two-ness to stretch out on."

This is the Maharal's picture of G-d's oneness. It is not one thing beside others. It is the one that holds opposites together. Many powers side by side can never add up to Him.

## 2.4 The caused falls by itself; it rises only from Him

"שכל אשר הוא עלול יש בו הנפילה מצד עצמו, רק הקמתו מצד העלה" (313, 13:22): "for whatever is caused has falling from itself; its rising is only from the Cause." That is why Ashrei has no verse for nun, the letter of falling, and why the psalm says "סומך ה' לכל הנופלים" (311): "G-d holds up all who fall."

And the other side of the same truth: any break in the bond is from our side only. "כי אין ההסרה שלהם רק מצד העלול עצמו, ולא מצד העלה" (249, 11:4): "for any turning away is only from the side of the caused, not from the side of the Cause." The Maharal says it again in philosophical terms: the caused "אפשרי מצד עצמו ומחוייב מצד עלתו" (276, 11:31): "is possible from itself and necessary from its Cause." From my side I may or may not be. From His side I am held.

## 2.5 Nothing comes from Him without being joined to Him

"כי לא יבא דבר מדבר רק אם יש לו צירוף אליו" (286, 12:1): "for nothing comes from something unless it is joined to it." He repeats this at 440: "שלא יבא דבר מדבר אם אין לו התיחסות אליו בדבר מה": "nothing comes from something unless it has some relation to it." So the world has two sides: a bodily side from itself, and a side beyond the body that comes from its Maker (440). Cleaving to G-d is not something added to creation. It is written into having come from Him.

And who is closest? "וכל זה מפני כי הוא יתברך פשוט בתכלית הפשיטות, ולפיכך בחר בשפלים שהם פשוטים" (286): "all this because He is simple in the utmost simplicity, so He chose the lowly, who are simple." Simplicity again (compare Netivot Olam 944). The humble are near because they are plain.

## 2.6 He is seen as the receiver can see

The sages say G-d "weeps" over the ruin. The Maharal explains: "כי השם יתברך נראה ונגלה בעולם כפי מה שהוא המקבל" (214, 9:13): "for G-d is seen and revealed in the world according to what the receiver is." At the sea He appeared as a warrior, at Sinai as an elder teaching. Nothing changes in Him. What changes is the one who receives.

## 2.7 He is not acted upon

When a blasphemer mocked Him in the Temple, He was silent. Why? "והוא יתברך, קדוש ונבדל מכל עניני הגשם, לכך אין לו התפעלות הגשם" (100, 5:48): "He is holy and apart from everything bodily, so He is not acted upon as bodies are." The sages read "who is like You among the mighty (elim)" as "among the mute (ilmim)". A person who is insulted must hold back his anger by force. G-d does not need to: "כמו האלם אין צריך לכבוש את הדבור שלא יצא" (101, 5:49): "like the mute, who does not need to hold back speech from coming out."

## 2.8 The middle binds, and has no end

The Maharal returns often to "the middle". In the word גאל (redeem) the aleph, the letter of one, sits in the middle, "כי לעולם האמצעי מאחד ומקשר הכל" (11, 1:7): "for the middle always unites and binds everything." The ends of a thing have an end. The middle does not: "והאמצעי, שהוא הישר, אין לו קצה" (446, 21:7): "and the middle, which is the straight, has no end." That is why "Jacob our father did not die." The straight path, which leans to no side, is the one that lasts.

**Proposed MH-S15 "Held from the other side".** Inside: I am not holding on by my own strength. From my side I am always able to fall. From His side I am always held, and nothing I do can cut the bond from His end. Any distance is only mine, and so it can be undone. How to enter: say inside, "From my side I can fall. From Your side I am held." Stay with the second half. Source: Netzach Yisrael 249, 276, 313. (Ours: the Maharal says this of the people as a whole and of every caused thing; we use the general form.)

**Proposed MH-S16 "Nothing of my own".** Inside: I have no being that is mine apart from Him. What I am, I am in Him. The Maharal: "עד כי בטל מציאות עצמם אל השם יתברך" (292, 13:1): "until their own existence is nullified to G-d." Of Isaac, who gave himself on the altar, there is no "remembering", because "הרי אין לו הבדל מאתו יתברך" (300, 13:9): "he has no separation from Him." How to enter: in the Shema or after prayer, let the sense "I" rest in "only You"; do not try to feel small, only to feel held. Source: 292, 300-302. Close to S62, but grounded in handing oneself over (Isaac), not in contemplation of the world.

## 4.1 Ashrei three times a day (new: `mh_ashrei_life`)

Why does the sage promise the world to come to one who says Ashrei three times a day? Because it praises G-d as the giver of life to all: "You open Your hand and satisfy every living thing with will." The one who says it "הוא דבק בחיים הבאים מן השם יתברך" (303, 13:12): "clings to the life that comes from G-d." It goes by the alphabet, and "אלו כ"ב אותיות יש בהם הפשיטות הגמור" (305, 13:14): "these 22 letters have full simplicity", so the praise reaches simple, non-bodily life. Three times, "שצריך התעלות ג' עולמות" (306, 13:15): "for one must rise through three worlds."

How: say Ashrei morning, afternoon and evening. At "פותח את ידך" pause and feel that your very life is being given now, from His open hand, "with will" (ratzon), not only with bread. Source: 303-306.

## 4.2 Pray with others (`arrive`; new: `mh_gathering`)

"כאשר הם מתפללים עם הצבור, והם מתאספים ומתקבצים אל ה'" (241, 10:21): "when they pray with the congregation, and gather together to G-d", that itself is leaving the scattering. The Maharal adds Torah and kindness (242-244) as the two things in "His right hand" that raise a person. How: when you pray with others, think of it as many scattered people being gathered into one before the One. Source: 241-244.

## 4.3 Eye before mouth (`lips`, `discern`; new: `mh_eye_before_mouth`)

Lamentations puts the letter pe (mouth) before ayin (eye) as a sign of the spies' sin. The eye sees what exists. The mouth should follow the eye. "והדבר שהוא רע כאילו אינו נמצא כלל" (205, 9:4): "and what is bad is as if it does not exist at all." So one should speak only of what truly is, like Torah and mitzvot, and turn the eye away from bad. Slander does the reverse: it gives words to what has no real being, and it splits people (203). How: before speaking of another person, ask: did my eye see this, and is it worth giving being to with my mouth? Source: 203-205.

## 4.4 Do not harm yourself (guard)

"לא תתגודדו", do not gash yourselves over the dead. The Maharal's reason: "ודבר שיש לו מציאות גמור אמיתי אין ראוי שימצא בו השחתה" (252, 11:7): "a thing that has full, true existence should not have destruction found in it." One who is bound to G-d has true being, and self-harm denies it. This belongs beside Netivot Olam 1056 as the Maharal's guard against self-harm. The guide should keep it.

## 4.5 Give the first (`act`)

The first of the dough, the fruit, the grain, the first bite of bread (the crust, over which we say hamotzi) belongs to G-d because the first is one (36-41). How: before eating, make the blessing on the best, first part of the bread and think: from the One comes one, and the rest is added. Source: 36-41.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why do the best fall hardest?** "One who has a level beyond the usual measure draws the power that would cancel it" (19). And a great level held by a body is never held fully, so it is open to lack (22). The Maharal gives a picture: "אבל הטעות הנמצא בשכל הוא למעלתו ולרוממותו במה שאינו טבעי" (25, 2:13): "but the error found in the mind comes from its height, in that it is not natural." The mind errs more than the eye, not because it is lower, but because it reaches higher.

**What is the soul, and what breaks it?** The soul is one with many powers (46). Three great sins break its powers one by one. Baseless hatred breaks the soul as a whole, because "שנאת חנם, אשר הוא החילוק והפירוד, הוא הפך עצם הנפש, אשר הוא אחד" (48, 4:6): "baseless hatred, which is division and separation, is the opposite of the soul's very self, which is one." Hatred is not only wrong. It is against what a soul is.

**Why do I fear?** "כי הדבר שהוא שלם, מפני שלימותו אינו מוכן להפסד" (55, 5:3): "for what is whole, because of its wholeness, is not open to loss." Fear is a sign of a crack (see Netivot Olam 1212). The one fear that is healthy is fear for one's learning, since no one owns Torah fully (55).

**What does good news do to a person?** It changes his very self. "שמועה טובה תדשן עצם" means "רק רוצה לומר עצמו של אדם" (88, 5:36): "rather it means the person's very self." The sage with one shoe that would not come off and another that would not go on was too full of joy to settle.

**Is suffering the end of me?** No. Blessings in the Torah run from aleph to tav, in order. Curses run out of order, and "וזה לפי שהדבר שהוא הפוך אינו קיים" (319, 14:4): "for what is reversed does not last." Punishment comes "divided", "לכך מביא המכה מחולקת" (336, 14:21): "so He brings the blow divided", so that a person can bear it. And dust, the lowest of things, is the most lasting: "והארץ לעולם עומדת" (333).

**Why does the good come slowly?** "Grass sprouts fast and is quickly lost" (428). "אבל דברים שאינם גשמיים לא ימצאו בשלימותם ובטובתם בתחלה, רק באחרונה" (417, 19:5): "but things that are not physical are not found complete and good at the start, only at the end." Payment for good deeds comes at the end, "כי התשלומין הם בסוף ובהשלמה" (422, 19:10): "for payment comes at the end, at the completion." To ask for all of it now is to ask for one's end now.

**What of growing old?** "זקני תלמידי חכמים, כל זמן שמזקינין דעתן נוספת" (351, 15:12): "old scholars: the older they grow, the more their mind increases." Youth lives in the body, the middle years hold body and mind side by side, old age belongs to the mind (351).

**How should I thank?** "כי ההודאה שהכל הוא אל השם יתברך" (425, 19:13): "for acknowledging is that all belongs to G-d." And "His faithfulness in the nights": "כי אף על גב שהוא בצרה, הוא מאמין בו יתברך" (426, 19:14): "for even though he is in trouble, he trusts in Him."

**What is faith?** "המאמין באחד דבק בו לגמרי כאשר מאמין בו" (437, 20:7): "one who believes in the One cleaves to Him fully as he believes." Bodily things rise to a peak and then fall. What rises toward G-d does not fall: "כל אשר הוא עולה הוא מתקרב אל השם יתברך" (436, 20:6): "whatever rises comes nearer to G-d." And the purpose of a life is with Him: "כי הוא יתברך תכלית להם" (438, 20:8): "for He is their end and purpose." That is the Maharal's reading of peah: leave the edge of your field for the poor, and your "end" is given to G-d, so it has no end (438-439).

**What is a person?** An upside-down tree: "כי העץ שורשו למטה תקוע בארץ, ואילו האדם שרשו למעלה" (165, 7:26): "for a tree's root is below, stuck in the earth, while a person's root is above." Tefillin "plant" a person in G-d.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **One cause, held now** (2) is the Maharal's form of the Gate of Unity's teaching that the world is made anew each moment and would return to nothing without the word of G-d. The Tanya speaks of speech that keeps creating. The Maharal speaks of a cause that must stay with its effect. The logic is the same: nothing stands by itself.
- **Many from One through the receiver** (30-39) is close to, but not the same as, the Tanya's tzimtzum. The Tanya explains the many by G-d's hiding and limiting His light so that separate things can appear. The Maharal explains it by the receiver: from the One comes one "beginning", and the many are "additions" that the receiver needs because it lacks. Neither uses a chain of independent powers. Both say that a chain of middlemen with their own power would be like idolatry (29). This is an agreement.
- **He is not acted upon** (100-101) fits the Rambam's teaching that no emotion or change can be said of G-d, which the Tanya also accepts. The Maharal uses it for comfort: His silence is not weakness.
- **He is seen as the receiver can see** (214) is the same move the Chabad teachers make when they say that the change is only in the one who receives the light. The Maharal applies it to G-d "weeping".
- **The break is only from our side** (249, 276, 313) matches Tanya ch. 18-19 on the hidden love that never leaves a soul, and the teaching that the soul's root is never cut off. The Maharal frames it as cause and effect, not as the soul's root in G-d's wisdom. For the guide, both can be said together.
- **Bittul** (292, 300): "their own existence is nullified to G-d". This is very near the Chabad idea of bittul, and Isaac's ashes that are "always before Him" is a strong image for it.
- **The Ramak's formula** ("He is all, and all is not He") does not appear. The Maharal's nearest statement is 186: the One includes all, even opposites, and two-ness cannot stand in His place.
