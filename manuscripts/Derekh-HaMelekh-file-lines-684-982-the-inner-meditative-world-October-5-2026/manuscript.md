Source: `Chassidus-txt/R. Kalonymus Kalman Shapira of Piaseczno (1889-1943)/Derekh HaMelekh (Warsaw, 1931).txt`, lines 684-982. Every Hebrew line quoted below was checked with `find_he.py verify ... --line N` and came back FOUND at the line given. Nothing unverified is quoted.

This range covers the last of the Torah-portion talks (Balak to Ha'azinu, several from 5696/1936) and then Part II, the festival talks: nine Rosh Hashanah talks (5686-5691), three for Shabbat Shuvah, one on the Thirteen Attributes, and the opening of Motzaei Yom Kippur 5686. The Rosh Hashanah and Shabbat Shuvah talks are some of the most interior writing in the book. Again and again they describe the hidden soul, the feeling of God's nearness when He cannot be seen, crying, song, and the stone on the heart.

## 1. What the range contains

- **Balak 6-11 (684-689).** Fear can be constant (Abraham's), not just fear when a sin comes along. Its root is love with fire. The fire a Jew raises in himself at Shema, by picturing himself thrown into fire for God, is the base of all service. Even a man busy earning a living must make "free times" to remember why he came into the world.
- **Pinchas (690-698).** "Wisdom sings in the street": the *good* of Torah is a taste that shows itself mainly in melody (*niggun*). It needs a holy place to appear. Two movements: going deep (narrow) and then widening. Every good thought starts narrow and must be widened by repeating it. This is *ratzo vashov*.
- **Shabbat Chazon 5696 (699-707).** God "believes in" a person who throws himself fully into his service, and that makes him steady. Rising and falling is unavoidable. Doubts that are pushed away still wear down faith from inside, like water on stone. The cure is to reduce one's ego (*yeshus*) in everything.
- **Vaetchanan/Nachamu 5696 (708-716).** God used the "commoner's vessels" of the Temple out of love. Now He lives in the love itself, in the Jew. Heat of the evil inclination can be turned into holy fire, the "consuming fire" of service.
- **Eikev (717-728).** Idleness kills the soul's showing itself. A soul that shows and hides by turns cannot come out in service. Practice: split the work into parts, take one tractate or one trait, and picture the joy of finishing it.
- **Ha'azinu (729-741).** "Know and return it to your heart": knowing that God alone exists must become habit in the whole body. A *flame* (*shalhevet*) of excitement does not hold its place. A *coal* (*gachelet*), a fixed self set in holiness, does. The root of both inclinations is one "spark of selfhood" drawn from the King's "body."
- **Rosh Hashanah 1 (5686, 742-754).** Knowing (*hakarah*) builds the knower. The self is revealed only by what it knows, as the moon shows only by the sun. A self "born" of holy knowing has thoughts that act. Sometimes a person feels "like an angel was added to him," and sometimes he stays "like a stone." Sighs gathered over the year are clothed in the shofar.
- **Rosh Hashanah 2 (743-770).** Learning the *secrets of Torah* means uncovering your own hidden soul as you learn, even in a simple Talmud passage. Give yourself to the Torah and it serves God through you. Humility is true for the self but a lie about the Torah. The "son" is marked by longing to see his Father.
- **Rosh Hashanah 3 (771-800).** Fear of the Sinai kind, from God showing Himself. Awe at Rosh Hashanah. Serving by *feeling* God's will, not by knowledge alone (the "tree of knowledge of good" is also a flaw). The Infinite is found in the Jewish heart. People worn out by longing are "lovesick." The parable of the hidden king in the next room: "the spirit of my father."
- **Rosh Hashanah 4 (5686, 801-820).** Hidden wisdom (*chochmah stima*) is in everything, and we close it off with ourselves; "move yourself aside." Shame before Mashiach. The parable of the prince who forgot the King's language and rings the emergency bell (the shofar). The soul speaks to God in "thought," a language the accusers cannot read.
- **Rosh Hashanah 5 (5690, 821-834).** Song (*shirah*) is the soul seeing past the crack of the intellect. Joy widens the soul. Practical rule: grab hold of whatever corner of the soul is awake now, even a prayer for your own needs, and let it rise to real prayer. Parable of the prince who sinks into melancholy.
- **Rosh Hashanah 6 (5691, 835-852).** Cry, but not in mourning. Every sin now comes from being "abandoned." Do teshuvah for the abandonment as a whole, not item by item. A Jew is "shir El," a song of God, who brings the light itself and does not only report it. At Shema no word is enough: the "voice of Shema," beyond speech.
- **Rosh Hashanah 7 (5691, 853-871).** God remembers Israel as He remembers Himself. The model inner prayer: "I want only You." The stone on the heart splits. Rising *before* the evil inclination (Jacob "passed before them," Joseph's vision of his father). "Waiting an hour" after and before prayer.
- **Rosh Hashanah 8 (5691, 872-888).** Divine thought has two halves: descent into "being," then return to "nothing." A person who does not really know where he stands does not reach the second half. Longing builds the vessels of *tikkun* for oneself and others.
- **Rosh Hashanah 9 (889-905).** Weigh every mitzvah *before* doing it (the "judge" in a person). This draws down its soul. A mitzvah without thought first is a body without a soul. Decide firmly to do it, then think only about *how* to do it best.
- **Rosh Hashanah 10 (906-914).** Only a bad will is "other than God." A contemplation of fear: you stand before a volcano with the fate of the King's palace in your hands. From it the self dissolves.
- **Shabbat Shuvah 1 (915-931).** "Faces" of the King hidden behind walls of darkness. The Jew is the *face*, the first revealing. Every thing has a face that a Jew can uncover. Longing is the key: "my heart said for You."
- **Shabbat Shuvah 2 (5690, 932-945).** "Fear from love": God suffers when we spoil His love. A heaviness with no bodily cause is God's voice calling. Joy comes only while you are *drawing near*. "Hang on a great tree": picture a tzaddik's path and join it.
- **Shabbat Shuvah 3 (5691, 946-969).** The shame of a wise child. Prayer for needs is a need of Heaven. Emotion makes intention hold. Every mitzvah needs a "holy place," which is made by committing to it.
- **Thirteen Attributes 5686 (970-973).** True prayer is when "God calls to God," when the divine part in a person prays.
- **Motzaei Yom Kippur 5686 (974-982).** Form (*tzurah*) is not seen by the eyes. Soul passes to soul. See the world "in one look" as letters of God's name. Humility that says "I'm only matter" is the evil inclination's humility.

## 2. The method: concrete instructions

1. **Picture yourself thrown into fire, at Shema (686-687).** Resolve in your mind and picture giving your life. The *arousal itself* does the work, and it must come from you. "שמחליט בדעתו ומצייר עצמו שמשליך א"ע באש לשם ד" (686). "הראשית והעיקר הוא מה שהאיש יעורר מעצמו בעצמו בעבודתו" (687).
2. **Set free times, and set them early (688).** A working man must still make "free times" to remember what he is here for and to stir up love and fear, and build this before the chase begins. "צריך הוא לעשות לו זמנים פנויים שיזכור שלא בא לעולם רק לשם משא ומתן" (688).
3. **Learn and pray with melody (692, 694).** The taste of Torah is beyond reason and speech and shows mainly in *niggun*. It needs a holy place (synagogue, beis midrash, shtiebel). "הטוב שבתורה נתגלה בעיקר בנגינה" (692).
4. **Widen every small good stirring by repeating it (696).** An idea, a good thought or a breath of teshuvah comes "short." Say it over in mind and speech several times until you see it. "כל הרהור טוב ורצון טוב ורוח התשובה הבא אל האדם מתחילה הוא קצר וצריכים לעבוד להרחיבה" (696).
5. **Divide the work and picture its end (723).** Take one tractate or one trait. Picture how happy you will be when you finish. When you finish, do not rest; take the next one. "ויצייר בעצמו איך יהיה מאושר כשיגמור ויזכור מסכת" (723).
6. **Turn what you know into habit in the body (734).** Knowing God is everywhere must become habit: go back again and again, add Torah and mitzvot, "simple doings" (734, paraphrase).
7. **Think "there is nothing but holiness" (745).** The world's hiddenness is itself God's gift. This thought actually brings one's material side into holiness. "ע"י שבמחשבתם שאין דבר רק קדושה אז מחשבתם מועלת" (745).
8. **Guard what you know and feel (752).** The Baal Shem Tov's path in place of fasting: guard the mind so that you feel something good from everything, and feel pain at anything bad. "הבעש"ט ז"ל גילה לנו את דרכו בקודש ע"י שמירת הדעת מההרגשה וההכרה" (752).
9. **Study as uncovering your soul (759).** Even in "one who admits part of a claim" or "the ox that gored the cow." "לימוד סתרי אורייתא הוא המגלה בלימודו את נשמתו המוסתרת עתה" (759).
10. **Start feeling God's will with a known mitzvah (780).** Do not start with unclear cases. Before praying, feel a *longing* to pray; that longing is the feeling of God's will. "אך גם ירגיש תשוקה להתפלל ותשוקה זו היא הרגשת רצון ד'" (780).
11. **Picture the Temple service before praying (794).** "ויצייר בעיניו ויחשוב איך הייתי עומד על קרבני" (794): the kohen in his garments, the Levites singing, fire coming down. Then ask how I should stand now, in prayer, which is greater.
12. **Stand before your heart like a closed ark and look a long time (797).** Look long at God being hidden inside you until heartache wakes and you cry. Then the gates open. "והאיש הישראלי עומד לפני לבו כלפני ארון הקודש מסוגר וצועק ובוכה" (797). "כשמסתכל הרבה בזה יתעורר בו כאב לב ויצעק ע"ז ואז סוף כל סוף יפתחו לו שערי לבו ואור ד' יופיע עליו" (797).
13. **Before the shofar, picture yourself on trial (809).** Picture being asked why you screened out God's light with your body and held back Mashiach. Weep from shame (809, paraphrase).
14. **Wake up for the shofar (820).** A soul half asleep prays in a "sleep-thought" that the accusers can read. The shofar's call is "עורו ישינים הקיצו מתרדמתכם" (820).
15. **Seize whatever corner of the soul is awake (828-829).** If what is awake now is a prayer for your own needs, do not shut it down while waiting for a "holier" arousal. Pour it out from the heart. From that pouring out, real song and prayer will rise. "מוטב שיאחז בקצה הזה של הנפש שמצא עתה" (828); "ויתפלל את תפלתו מקירות לבו ובהשתפכות הנפש" (828). Psalm 39 read as a warning against silence (829).
16. **Use joy to lift the soul out of its garments (830).** "ע"י השמחה אשא ואעלה את נפשי מן לבושיה" (830).
17. **Say praises until the spark widens (845).** Even alone: "מתחילה היה בו רק ניצוץ וכשמדבר ואומר תהלות ד' מתגלה ומתרחב בקרבו" (845).
18. **At Shema, lengthen the "One" without more words (848).** "רק הקדושה כמו שהיא והנפש כמו שהיא וקורא בקול שמע" (848). Draw out *echad*.
19. **Wordless prayer, repeated (859-860).** The model prayer: "רק אותך רוצה אני. נפשי תפתח ותסתכל בהודך" (859), "הסר את המחצלת של קנים שקוברת את נפשי" (859). Then: "בלא דיבור רק במוח ולב וכל קרביו. עוד ועוד פעם, מרגיש שהאבן מתפוצץ" (860). Order: first the stuck *Nishmas*, then the inner cry, then the stone splits, then *Nishmas* can be said.
20. **Rise above the evil inclination before the test (861-866).** Do not fight as an equal. First look at the light from far off (the "spyglass"). "מלחמת היצה"ר קשה היא אבל ביטול היצה"ר קל מזה" (861).
21. **Wait an hour after prayer, then an hour before the next (867).** Spread the delight of a good prayer through the day, "like someone who enjoys a good meal for hours," and use that thread to wait before the next prayer. "העיקר תלויה ראיה זו בשוהין שעה אחת אחר התפלה" (867).
22. **Know your low state fully, not with a light sigh (881).** Stir up worry and brokenness until you are "wholly in" your low state. A light sigh brings despair (881).
23. **Stop before every mitzvah and weigh it (893-896).** "קודם כל מצוה צריכין לשהות ולהתבונן ולשפוט עליה" (893). There is no fixed content: "אז באים לו מחשבות ומשפט אמת בלבו מה שישקול ומה יחשוב" (893). Decide first *that* you will do it, then weigh only *how*. A bad feeling may stay; the decision is separate: "כי הרגש הלב והחלטת הדעת הם שני דברים מחולקים" (896).
24. **The volcano contemplation (911).** "יתבונן האדם כאילו העמידוהו לפני הר אשר בבטנו נמצא אש וגפרית" (911). Your limbs shake and you forget yourself. Do this "always": "וכשיתבונן זאת תמיד תתבטל עצמותו" (911).
25. **Look inward until the cry breaks out (919).** See your soul's lowness as a desecration of His name: "וכשמביט האיש בקרבו זאת צעקה מתפרצת ממעמקי נפשו" (919), and pray "for Your sake."
26. **Check an unexplained heaviness (937).** First rule out bodily causes (hunger, insult), then lack of Torah. If nothing is found, hear it as God's call.
27. **"Hang on a great tree": picture a tzaddik's path (942-944).** "מצייר אני בדעתי מין קדושה ודרך זה" (942). Choose a way, start at its lowest step, and take concrete resolutions: "לא סתם אהיה טוב. רק מעתה כך וכך אתנהג" (944).
28. **See the world "in one look" (979-980).** Combine all things into one combination of divine letters, and look through your soul, not your eyes. "אם מביטים בראיה אחת אז רואים את אלקים כביכול" (979); "כל הבטה שמביט בעולם. בנפשו הוא מביט" (980).

## 3. The inner experience, in his images

- **The soul that shows and hides.** "כ"ז שנפשו של אדם נראית ואח"כ מטמינה א"ע ושוב נראית ושוב נטמנת" (722). While it keeps flickering it cannot come out in service.
- **Rising and falling is built in.** "כי תמיד מוכרח האיש להיות בעולה ויורד" (704).
- **Blowing on coals.** "יש איש מנפח בגחלים כדי שיתבערו וידלקו מעצמם" (720). Outside pressure starts the fire but cannot keep it going.
- **Flame and coal.** "השלהבת שמתלהב לפעמים אינו בטוח שכבר תישאר בו" (735). The coal "takes its place."
- **A formless selfhood inside.** "והאיש רק מרגיש שבקרבו ישנה הארה של בחי' ישות שממנה מתחלת והיא בלא צורה" (736).
- **Knowing another reveals yourself.** "האדם כשמכיר דבר זולתו לא את זולתו בלבד מכיר אלא אף את עצמו מכיר" (746). The moon image.
- **Two kinds of knowing holy things.** "לפעמים מרגיש שנעשה לאיש אחר וכמו מלאך נתוסף בו" (750): lifted from the body, a higher delight, yearning to rise, even after a dry Talmud page. Or "כאבן שאין לו הופכין" (750), even after all the mussar books.
- **The Jewish sigh.** In Yiddish: "איודישער קרעכן איז אין הימעל זעהר טייער" (753), "a Jewish groan is very dear in heaven," if it comes from the inside of the heart.
- **Outside the Garden.** "והעני בדעת שעומד בחוץ ורק מסתכל בתורה בעין שכלו בלבד" (758) feels no excitement: "to delight in Eden you must be in Eden."
- **Lovesick and worn out.** "רק מפני שנלאו ונחלה נפשם והיא שוכבת וקשה לה לקום" (791). "למי שרץ בתשוקה רבה ... ונפגע בכותל" (792): like one who runs with great longing and hits a wall.
- **Feeling the hidden Father.** "מה זאת שאת רוח אבי אני מרגיש כאן כאלו היה בקרבתי" (798); "וישאף עמוק נשמתו אל קרבו. רוח אבי אהה" (798). Then: "והן האיש הישראלי מרגיש את קרבת אביו אבל לא ידע איפה הוא" (799). The father weeps too, and they embrace.
- **The heart melting at the shofar.** "הלב מתמוגג כמעט מבכי, והנשמה וכל רוחנו ונפשנו מתגלים ומספרים לד'" (817). Its opposite: "כ"כ יבשות בלי שום הרגשה ובלי שום אור" (817).
- **Speaking in the King's language.** "הנשמה מספרת עם ד' בלשון המלך לשון אצילות בחי' מחשבה ומתקשרת עם דודה" (818).
- **Peering through the crack of the intellect.** "נפשו מצומצמת וטמונה בגופו. ורק דרך שכל האנושי שלו מציצה ומסתכלת בקדושתו" (825); "והביטה נפשו רק ע"י סדק, ע"י שכלו" (826). Song brings "a new seeing that he cannot explain in speech or grasp with the mind" (827).
- **The cramped spirit cries.** "נפשו בקרבו תזעק על שרוחה שפלה ומצומצמת" (831).
- **A spark widening until the body is drawn in.** (845) "עד שכל גופו מתפעל ובטל אליה". "ואם אין. הלב שומע ומתפעל הוא בעצמו ולעצמו גילה" (845): even with no listener, the heart listens.
- **The self seen at Rosh Hashanah.** "ואז עצם האיש נתגלה גם לו לעצמו ובקרבו" (850); "ובוכה מקירות לבו איפה אני" (851). This rises to "עד שלא יוכל הפה לדבר. בחי' קול דק"ש" (851).
- **The stone on the soul.** "אבן גדולה סותמת את נפשו ולוחצת אותה" (858). The soul "buried in a reed mat" (859). The stone "bursts" (860).
- **Delight that lasts after prayer.** "כמו איש שמתענג איזה שעות אחר מאכל שערב לו" (867).
- **The volcano.** Limbs knocking, sweat, trembling, "he remembers nothing of himself" (911).
- **Heaviness as a nudge in sleep.** "כמו מי שישן ודוחפים אותו שמרגיש את הצער רק אינו יודע מהו מרגיש" (936). "אז דאגתו עתה קול ד' הוא שנפשו שומעת איך שקורא לה" (937).
- **Joy only while approaching.** "והכל רק בשעה שמתקרב. לא כשכבר קרוב" (941).
- **Born in a cellar.** "איש שנולד במרתף קר רטוב חשוך" (943) hopes only for a little window, until he tastes a bright house.
- **From the depths.** In Yiddish: "פין מיין טיפעניש רעדט זיך ארויס" (973), "it speaks itself out of my depths."

## 4. The self and God

This range backs the owner's thesis strongly, with several different reasons.

- **The self is revealed only by what it knows, so knowing holiness builds a holy self (746-750).** "ואם לא ישיג את זולתו גם א"ע לא ישיג" (746). A person "creates his selfhood" by holy knowing, and that holy self is what lasts. Finding oneself and finding God are one act of knowing.
- **The secret of Torah is your own hidden soul (759).** Uncovering the soul in study *is* reaching the hidden God in the Torah. The Torah then shows the "hidden God" (763).
- **The son longs for the Father because he comes from the Father's mind (767).** "מפני שהבן ממוח האב כנודע, לכן כל עצמותו משתוקקת לאביו" (767). Rosh Hashanah is the time "לחפש בגנזי דמלכא ולמצוא את אבינו המלך" (768). The longing of one's own essence is the search for God.
- **God is found in the heart (791, 797-799).** "את ד' אלקי ישראל אין סוף ואין תכלית יכולים למצוא בלב הישראלי" (791). Where is He hidden? "In our heart" (799). The man stands before his own heart as before a closed ark (797). Here the thesis is literal: the place to look for God is inside oneself.
- **Seeing yourself is the start of return (850-851).** At Rosh Hashanah "the essence of the man is revealed to himself." He cries "where am I," and that cry runs toward God.
- **But the self is also what closes the door (806-807).** "כשאתה נוטל את עצמך הצידה נעשה ב' ונתגלה לך החכמה בכל המציאות" (806): only when you move *yourself* aside does wisdom show. Still, the grasp comes "by union, once the person first uncovers the hidden wisdom within him" (807). The Rebbe holds both: uncover the inner self, and move the ego-self aside. The owner's thesis should keep this distinction. The self that is found is the hidden soul; the self that is set aside is the "vav," the body and traits, the *yeshus*.
- **Prayer is God in the person calling God (972-973).** Only someone connected to "his own divinity" prays like this: "רק מי שמקושר עם האלקות שלו" (973).
- **Seeing God depends on the seer's own soul (980).** "רבש"ע יודע ומרגיש אני שלא אתה התחבאת ממני אחר החטא רק ואחבא" (980): it is not You who hid, it is I who hid. Whoever uncovers his soul sees God's seal in the world.
- **The coal is one's own selfhood (736-740).** Holiness stays only when it is in one's own *yeshus*, one's essence, and not just a flame from above. So the true self is not dissolved; it becomes the place where holiness stays.

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

- **Flame versus coal (735, 738).** Uplift may be only "a creation and appearance from above" that will not stay. Test: did it settle into your self and your everyday habits?
- **Doubts pushed away but not uprooted (706).** "בפנימיותו גם בלא הרגשתו יכולה להחליש את אמונתו". The cure is to reduce ego in all things, not to argue.
- **Knowing without feeling is also a flaw (780).** "גם הדעת טוב פגם הוא. שאינו מרגיש מאומה את רצון ד'". Acting only from understanding is a sign of distance.
- **Excitement that is imagined (858).** "לא התלהבות מדומה רק התלהבות מתוך הסתכלות בהוד של מעלה שנפשו מרגשת". Real excitement comes from looking at something, not from making up a feeling.
- **Arousal from hearing versus from the spark (847).** "לא ששומע שיש אור וקדושה ומתפעל ומתענג רק ניצוץ קדושה מתלהב בו". Being moved by *hearing about* light is second-hand.
- **Forcing a holier arousal (828).** Shutting down a real prayer for needs in order to wait for a "pure" one loses both. The rule is "one *hav*" (a first cry for one's needs is allowed) but not "*hav hav*" (not staying there).
- **A light sigh leads to despair (881).** "מי שמתעורר באנחה קלה אז גם יאוש מתעורר בו". Deep brokenness turns into strength and joy.
- **Crying in mourning (838).** "הבכיה לא תהיה באבילות רק בשמחה". Mourning comes from despair. Real weeping carries hope.
- **The evil inclination's humility (762, 981).** "I am nothing, so nothing I do matters" is a trick: "וזה עניוות היצה"ר לחשוב א"ע רק לחומרי" (981). True humility sets the self aside but not the work.
- **Weighing whether to do it (894).** Weighing *whether* to do a mitzvah draws "the soul of a sin" into it. Weigh only *how*.
- **Doing mitzvot for yourself (760, 808).** Self-serving service adds to the closing. Refining one's traits "for his own sake" is a sin.
- **Intention without emotion (960).** "ואם אין בה חיות אז אף אם מכוון. הכונה קלה היא כאילו תלויה באויר". Without life in it, focus gets knocked loose easily.
- **Mistaking body for spirit (937-938).** Check for bodily causes first. You can feel the Shekhinah's pain only when you are not worn down by the body: "לזה צריכים להשקיט לו מימי הרע" (938).
- **Chasing pleasure is an empty soul (940).** "וכל הרדיפה אחר התענוגים ... הוא רק במי שאין נפשו מרגשת תענוג של התגלות קדושה". Contentment comes on its own; it is not a struggle.
- **Wanting gifts without effort (939).** Tzaddikim turned down levels given freely, because the joy is in the *drawing near*.

## 6. Compared with Chabad hisbonenus

**Where they agree.** - *Duration and extension.* His "narrow then widen" (696) matches hisbonenus's move from a point (chochmah) into length and breadth (binah). "Say it over several times until you see it" is the Chabad instruction to extend, not to add new material. - *Knowing must reach the heart and the body.* Ha'azinu's "know and return it to your heart" (731-734) is the classic hisbonenus verse, and his claim that knowledge must light up "our whole self and body" is the Chabad aim. - *Feeling is not optional.* "Knowing good is also a flaw" (780) agrees with R. Aharon of Strashelye's defense of felt arousal. - *Self-awareness and ego.* "Move yourself aside" (806) and "refining traits for oneself is a sin" (808) match the Chabad worry about "being aware of yourself in the state" and the Mitteler Rebbe's demand to reduce the self first. - *The test is change.* Flame versus coal (735) is close to the Rashab's test: has the dominant trait actually changed?

**Where he differs (kept, not smoothed).** - *The starting point.* Chabad starts with a concept (unity, the order of descent). He usually starts from a *state*: longing, heartache, a sigh, a prayer for needs, a heaviness with no cause. His object is often the person's own condition (797, 851, 919), or the felt nearness of a Father who cannot be seen (798). - *Picturing and imagination.* He tells people over and over to *picture*: picture yourself thrown into fire (686), the finished tractate (723), the Temple service (794), yourself on trial before Mashiach (809), the volcano (911), a tzaddik's path (942). The spyglass parable (865) makes seeing the goal ahead of time the method. Chabad works through intellectual analogy, not staged scenes. - *Seeing, not only understanding.* Song is seeing past "the crack of the intellect" (825-827). Form is taken in by soul, not eye (978). The Shema is "beyond speech" (848, 851). He values a seeing that "cannot be explained in speech or grasped by the mind." Chabad insists on daas, holding the idea. - *Crying and wordless prayer.* The inner cry "without speech, only mind and heart and all one's insides, again and again" (860) is a repeated emotional push, not reasoning. Chabad treats bitterness as a sharpening tool with conditions. For him crying is central, provided it is not mourning (838). - *Melody.* The taste of Torah shows mainly in *niggun* (692). Chabad hisbonenus does not make melody the carrier of understanding. - *Joy as the lever.* Joy lifts the soul out of its garments (830). Chabad hisbonenus prefers settledness and duration. - *Beginning low.* "Grab whatever corner of the soul is awake," even a selfish need (828). Chabad's "unsettled" state says do not contemplate yet; he says use exactly what is moving. - *Rising above instead of fighting.* Cancelling the evil inclination by first looking at holiness (861) is closer to the Chabad idea of "light pushes out darkness." But he gets there through a vision (Joseph's image of his father, 863), not through contemplating greatness. - *Weighing before the act.* "Waiting and judging before each mitzvah" (893) is a small contemplation on *how* to act. Chabad hisbonenus is more about God than about the act at hand.

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before we start, what part of you is awake right now, even if it's just a worry or a need? Let's start there and not wait for a holier feeling." (828)
2. "Take one small good thought you had today and say it to yourself slowly, three or four times, until it feels wider than it was." (696)
3. "Picture it clearly: where you stand, what you see, what's at stake. Then ask how you want to stand now." (794, 911)
4. "Stand in front of your own heart as if it were a closed ark. God is in there and hidden. Keep looking, and if an ache comes, let it cry; that is how the door opens." (797)
5. "If you can't find words, don't force them. Just keep reaching, with mind, heart and your whole insides, again and again, until something gives." (859-860)
6. "If you feel heavy for no reason, first check the body: hunger, tiredness, a hurt. If it's none of those, it may be a call to you." (937)
7. "Let the good you felt stay with you through the day, like the taste of a good meal. Come back to it for a few minutes before you next pray." (867)
8. "Crying is fine, but cry with hope, not with mourning. A light sigh tends to slide into despair; a real heartbreak turns into strength." (838, 881)
