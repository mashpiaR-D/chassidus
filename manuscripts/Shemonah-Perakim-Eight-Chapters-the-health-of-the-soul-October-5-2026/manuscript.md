## The shape of the work

Avot is the tractate of *chassidut*, piety, which "brings to the holy spirit" (line 1). Before explaining it the Rambam gives eight chapters of psychology, drawn from the Sages and the philosophers: "hear the truth from whoever says it" (line 1).

1. The one soul and its five powers (lines 2-11).
2. Which powers hold commandments and sins; virtues of mind and of character (lines 12-13).
3. The sickness of the soul (lines 14-16).
4. The cure: the middle way, and how to return to it (lines 17-30).
5. One goal for every power: knowing G-d (lines 31-38).
6. The pious one and the one who conquers himself (lines 39-41).
7. The partitions between a person and G-d (lines 42-47).
8. Human nature and free choice; G-d's will and G-d's knowing (lines 48-68).

## 1. The unity of G-d in this work

**One goal for the whole person.** The unity of G-d here shows up as the unity of a life. "A person should keep one goal always before his eyes: to know G-d, as far as a human can" (line 31), and every power of the soul should serve it (line 38). "Love G-d with all your heart, soul and might" means "make the goal of every part of your soul one goal: the love of G-d" (line 38).

> שתעשה תכלית כל חלק ממנה תכלית אחת והיא: לאהבת השם יתברך (line 38)

**Knower, knowing and known.** Chapter 8 gives the same teaching as YT 2:10 and the Guide: G-d does not know by a knowledge that is other than Him; "He is the knowledge, and He is the knower, and He is the known" (line 66). So we cannot know how He knows, as we cannot grasp His being: our mind falls short "as the light of the eye falls short of grasping the light of the sun", not because the sun is weak but because its light is stronger (line 67). "We only know that He knows, as we know that He exists" (line 67).

> שהוא הידיעה והוא היודע והוא הידוע (line 66)

**The last partition is the mind itself.** Faults of mind and of character are "the partitions that separate us from Him" (line 43). Each unbalanced trait is one more partition; Moses, having removed all, had "only one clear partition left: the human intellect that is not separate" (line 47). *Ties to:* C6 (From the known to the edge of knowing), S35.

**Will in the six days, not moment to moment.** The Rambam rejects the Kalam view "that He wills each thing, time after time, always": "we do not believe so; rather the will was in the six days of creation, and all things act by their natures always" (line 56). G-d willed that man be free as He willed that man stand upright (line 57). This is one of the clearest places where Chassidus departs from him (see section 5).

> לא כך נאמין אנחנו, כי אם החפץ היה בששת ימי בראשית (line 56)

## 2. States of consciousness

- **The sick soul (ch. 3, line 14).** "The soul has health and sickness as the body has." A sick soul tastes the bitter as sweet and the sweet as bitter, and craves what harms it; some sick souls do not feel they are sick. *Ties to:* S07 (Restless wanting), S09 (The pull that keeps winning), S18 (Chasing the high). Chassidus keeps the image of the soul's illness (Tanya ch. 1's "nefesh ha-bahamis", and the medicine-map in this library), but the guide should frame this as moral and spiritual, not clinical (ours).
- **The pious one and the conqueror (ch. 6, lines 39-41).** The philosophers say the *chasid*, who does good and wants to, is higher than the *kovesh et yitzro*, who does good while still wanting evil. The Sages seem to say the reverse: "the greater a person, the greater his inclination", and "one should not say 'I do not want pork', but 'I want it, but what can I do, my Father in heaven decreed on me'" (line 40). The Rambam's solution: for sins all people know to be evil (murder, theft, fraud), not wanting them is higher; for the Torah's decrees (chukim) the conqueror is higher (line 41). *Ties to:* S10 (Torn in two), S28 (Mind over heart), S34 (Two voices). This is the closest the Rambam comes to the Tanya's *tzaddik* and *beinoni* (Tanya ch. 12, 27): the Tanya holds that the beinoni who still wants and still conquers brings a delight above of its own kind (Tanya ch. 27, lines 361-365 of the Tanya file). The Rebbe reads Esau's nature through this chapter, "so that he could reach the level of 'the one who conquers his inclination'" (Likkutei Sichos vol. 30, Toldos 2, p. 114; line 854 of that file).
- **Joy, not grief (ch. 7, line 46).** "Prophecy rests not amid laziness nor amid sadness, but amid joy." Anger removes prophecy; Jacob's grief for Joseph removed it until his spirit revived. *Ties to:* S53, S04.
- **Contentment (line 44).** "Who is rich? One who is glad in his portion": satisfied with what his time gave him, not pained by what it did not. *Ties to:* S53, S84.
- **The highest level (ch. 5, line 37).** One whose every act and word is weighed by whether it leads to knowing G-d: "I do not say he is below the prophets." Reached "only by a very few, after great training". *Ties to:* S63, R-S6 (Guide).

## 3. Meditation and practice

- **The method of healing traits (ch. 4, lines 17-22).** Good deeds are the middle between two extremes; virtues are settled dispositions reached only "by repeating the deeds of that trait many times over a long time" (line 20). To cure, "we stand against it with its opposite, until it returns to the balance" (line 21): the miser practices spending freely until the miserliness leaves, then settles in generosity. The secret of the order: lean harder on the opposite of a strong vice than of a weak one, because the return from overspending to generosity is easier than from miserliness (line 22). *Ties to:* techniques `discern`, `act`, `chazarah`. *Proposed new technique* **R-T5 "The opposite for a season"**: name one trait that has gone to an extreme; for a set time practice its opposite on purpose, a little past the middle; then settle in the middle and watch it daily.
- **Weigh your traits every day (line 28).** "The complete person must remember his traits always, weigh his deeds, and examine the dispositions of his soul day by day"; "it is impossible for a person to be without a lack" (line 28). *Ties to:* technique `cheshbon`.
- **The Torah is the measure (lines 24-27).** The Rambam warns strongly against self-torment: fools who saw the pious fast and go to the desert, not knowing it was medicine, "tormented their bodies... as if G-d hated the body and wanted to destroy it" (line 24). The Torah's laws already lean us a little from the extremes: the forbidden foods toward restraint, the gifts to the poor toward a good heart (lines 26-27). *Guard* for the guide.
- **One goal in every act (ch. 5, lines 31-38).** Eat, sleep, work and rest for the body's health, and the body's health so the soul can grow in wisdom (line 32). Even beauty has a place: one troubled by *marah shechorah* (black bile; "gloom") may lift it with music, gardens and fine buildings (line 32); the mind tires "and thought grows turbid" and needs rest in beautiful things (line 36). *Ties to:* technique `carry`; "all your deeds for the sake of Heaven" (line 38). *Guard (ours):* this is a medieval view of mood and body; the guide takes the principle (one goal, rest has a place) and not the medicine.
- **Nothing is fixed (line 57).** "Let him not say he has reached a state that cannot be changed, for every state can be changed from good to bad and from bad to good, and he is the one who chooses." *Ties to:* S11 (The verdict: I'm my mistakes), which this line directly answers.

> ואל יאמר שכבר הגיע למצב, שאי אפשר לשנותו (line 57)

## 4. The big human questions

- **Am I made this way?** No one is born with a virtue or a vice, only ready for one, as a person may be born ready to learn more easily (line 48). Astrology is lies (line 49). "All a person's deeds are handed to him" (line 52).
- **Is everything from Heaven?** "Everything is in the hands of Heaven except the fear of Heaven" refers to nature (height, rain, air), not to a person's choices (line 53). Even whom one marries and what money one has are partly choice.
- **Why did Pharaoh's heart harden?** Punishment for earlier free cruelty can be the loss of the choice to repent (lines 61-63). A hard line; the guide should hold it with the Teshuvah laws that nothing stands before return (ours).
- **Who am I at the core?** One soul with many powers (line 2), matter to the form of the intellect; a soul without knowledge "is not good" (line 11).

## 5. Where Chassidus reads this work, and where it departs

- **Tzaddik and beinoni.** The Rambam's ch. 6 and the Tanya's tzaddik and beinoni treat the same question. The Rambam ranks the *chasid* higher for moral laws and the conqueror higher for decrees; the Tanya ranks the tzaddik (who has turned his evil to good) above the beinoni in level, yet gives the beinoni's conquering its own unique delight above (ch. 27, line 365: "two kinds of delight"). The Rebbe explicitly cites Eight Chapters here (line 854).
- **The powers of the soul.** The Rashab cites Eight Chapters ch. 1 on the mind clothed in the limbs and crafts (Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 11 of that file: "see what the Rambam wrote in Shemonah Perakim ch. 1").
- **Will in the six days vs. constant renewal.** The Rambam says the will was set in the six days and things run by nature (line 56). The Tanya (Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah 2, line 764 of the Tanya file) attacks the view that the world is like a vessel that no longer needs the smith, and teaches that the Creator's power must be in the creature always, "to give it life and keep it" (line 767). The Rambam also holds that all things depend on G-d for their being (YT 1:3; Guide I:69), so the difference is not that the world could stand alone, but whether each event is a new act of will. Chassidus says yes; the Rambam says no (ours: this is a real disagreement, not a difference of words).
- **Joy and sadness.** Full agreement: sadness blocks, joy opens (line 46; Tanya ch. 26-31).
- **The middle way.** Chassidus keeps the Rambam's middle path in behavior, but its inner work is less about balance and more about *transforming* the trait (iskafya and ishapcha). The Rambam's cure is habit; the Tanya's is contemplation that moves the heart plus habit (ours).

## What this work adds

- A complete, plain method for healing traits (R-T5). - The daily weighing of traits. - The answer to "I cannot change" (line 57). - The earliest clear statement of the conqueror's special worth, which Chassidus develops. - A clear marker of the Rambam's view on will and nature, for the RAMBAM.md disagreements.

## Where the thread is thin

- Chapter 1's physiology (nutritive, sensing, imagining, appetitive, rational powers) and chapter 8's temperaments are medieval science. - Chapter 4's aside on Moses' sin at Meribah (lines 29-30) is exegesis.
