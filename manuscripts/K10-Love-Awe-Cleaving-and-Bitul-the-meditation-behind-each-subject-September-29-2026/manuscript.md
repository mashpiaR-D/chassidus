## Why a meditation behind each subject

Every concept in Chassidus points to one thing, the unity of God, and that unity cannot be understood: by definition it is beyond the mind. It can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (hisbonenus). So behind each subject below is a meditation, and the meditation, not the idea, is what matters. Each one specializes the meditation of the subject's first face.

## How to use it

This is hisbonenus, the Jewish way of contemplating as Chabad does it, not mindfulness. Take one subject per sitting.

- **Hold** is one line of teaching. Say it, and fix the mind on it.
- **The move** thinks it through slowly. It opens the line detail by detail, brings it down into your own day, gives the sources' own picture and says in a few words where the picture breaks, and makes it particular: you, here, now. The pauses between its steps are time to think, not silence to watch anything.
- **Stay** binds the mind: say the line again slowly, and when the mind drifts, come back to it without arguing with the stray thought (Tanya 3 and 28).
- **From inside** describes the glimpse. It is never asked for, and if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.
- **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, then gives one mitzvah or act of Torah in your own measure, and a line to return to during the day.

## Labels

- **Tie** says whether the move rests on steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our construction (*reading*).
- **Thin** follows the entry's own marking (its 'thin?' column; 'partly' or 'split' counts as thin). A thin subject's meditation is kept modest and says why in one line.
- **Where the sources differ** keeps a disagreement the entry records, where the meditation touches it, rather than smoothing it.
- **Note** marks any other place where the meditation leans on our reading.
- **Anchors** are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly (reference, line and Hebrew), each with the entry's own gloss. *OCR* marks uncorrected machine transcription, quoted as the entry quotes it.

## S112 · Love of God · אהבת ה'

**Face:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · **also touches:** F01 The world said into being now; F09 The unity of opposites; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** He is your very life, the life in you and in every good thing you love; seeing that is how a person comes to love what seemed infinitely far.

**Hold.** 'He is our very life, and as a person loves his own soul and life, so will he love God' (Tanya, Chinukh Katan 8).

**The move.** Open it. R. Aharon of Strashelye asks how anyone can love what is infinitely far from him, and answers: only from the side of the unity. The Alter Rebbe makes faith in His unity the first foundation of love, and its content is that He is our very life. The Chernobyler turns it outward: the good you taste in anything is His life in it, and the love for it has fallen from the root love and can be lifted back. Bring it down to one good thing in your day, a child's face or fresh bread, and the life in you that enjoys it. The picture is the Tanya's, a bird that cannot fly with one wing, love without awe; it is only a picture, since in Him, the Maggid says, love and awe are one. You, here, now: He is your very life.

**Stay.** 'He is our very life', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Love for your own life, turned toward its source. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts. It is what carries the deed.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart.' Then do one mitzvah today with that love in mind: a coin to charity, or a blessing said slowly. Line for the day: 'He is our very life.'

**Where the sources differ.** Does love bring a person into the unity? Tanya 35: even the tzaddik who loves remains 'a thing in itself'; only the mitzvot, His will, are fully one with Him. The Besht: devekut is essential life. The Mitteler Rebbe: devekut is the constant bond, love only an arousal at times. Vitebsk: every felt love is 'false', only a way in; true devekut is unfelt faith.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 5 (line 5): ומצד היחוד תוכל לשער אהבה ויראה ודביקות The answer: love and cleaving become possible only 'from the side of the unity'. - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, Chinukh Katan 8 (line 750): הן דרך כלל – כי הוא חיינו ממש, וכאשר האדם אוהב את נפשו וחייו – כן יאהב את ה׳ Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, Chinukh Katan 8: the love any Jew can reach comes from contemplating that He is 'our very life', the vitality in us (F01, F06). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 35:4 (line 474): רק הוא דבר בפני עצמו – ירא ה׳ ואוהבו Tanya 35: even the perfect tzaddik who loves God is still 'a thing in itself, one who fears and loves Him'; love alone is not the full unity, only the mitzvot are (F08, F10).

## S113 · The hidden love · אהבה מסותרת

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** In every Jew, whoever he is, lies a love that does not want to be separated from the One. It is covered, never gone.

**Hold.** 'To cleave to faith in God, His unity and oneness, and not to be separated from His oneness' (Tanya 24, on what the hidden love is).

**The move.** Open it. Ein Sof is clothed in the innermost wisdom of every Jew's soul, whoever he is. That wisdom by nature sets itself aside before its source. So the soul by nature wants to cleave to Him, and that wish is the hidden love. It is called hidden because it is covered, as with sackcloth, not because it is weak, and the Tanya calls it 'truth of truth in itself'. Bring it down to the moment today when you are about to do a mitzvah half-heartedly, or to skip one. The picture is the Tanya's, a flame that keeps reaching up from the wick toward its source; it is only a picture, since the love does not burn and its source is not up in a place. You, here, now: under whatever covers it, you do not want to be separated from Him.

**Stay.** 'Not to be separated from His oneness', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** A homesickness that is really love. The Tanya says it is true even when waking it feels imagined; the work is only to bring it out into a deed. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'Hear, Israel, the Lord is our God, the Lord is One', meaning not to be separated from Him. Then do the one mitzvah today that you might have skipped. Line for the day: 'Not to be separated.'

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 18:7 (line 255): ונמצא, כי איןסוף ברוךהוא מלובש בבחינת חכמה שבנפש האדם – יהיה מי שיהיה מישראל Tanya 18: Ein Sof is clothed in the Chochmah of every Jew's soul, whoever he is; this is the root of the hidden love (F06). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 24:8 (line 323): המכסה ומסתיר ומעלים את האהבה מסותרת שבנפשה האלהית, לדבקה באמונת ה׳ ויחודו ואחדותו, ולא ליפרד חס ושלום מאחדותו Tanya 24: the content of the hidden love is to cleave to faith in His unity and not to be separated from His oneness. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 44:9 (line 643): מאחר שהוא אמת לאמיתו מצד עצמו בבחינת ״אהבה מסותרת״ Tanya 44: even if arousing it feels imaginary, the hidden love is 'truth of truth in itself'; the work is only to bring it out into deed.

## S114 · Great love and eternal love · אהבה רבה ואהבת עולם

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F04 Filling and surrounding; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** One love grows from below, from seeing that everything is as nothing before Him; the other comes from above as a gift, from where nothing is separate. Between them is His own love, which hid His light for your sake.

**Hold.** 'Eternal love comes from understanding the greatness of Ein Sof, who fills all worlds and surrounds all worlds, before whom all is as nothing' (Tanya 43).

**The move.** Open it. The eternal love is the one you can work toward: think how He fills and surrounds every world, until the love strips off every garment of pleasure and wants Him alone. The great love is not yours to make: it comes from above as a gift, from a place with no separation, and cannot be commanded. What joins them is His own love, for He hid His light through contraction after contraction for love of the lowly human being. Bring it down to the blessing before the morning Shema, 'with great love' or 'with eternal love', said at your usual place. The picture is the Tanya's, water reflecting a face; it is only a picture, since He has no face and your love is not a copy of His, but love does answer love. You, here, now: the hiding was done for love of you.

**Stay.** 'All is as nothing before Him', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** An answer more than an effort: love meeting love, the way a face answers its reflection in water. The gift love, if it comes, comes as a gift. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, tomorrow morning, the first words of the blessing before the Shema, slowly, as the answer to His love. Then say the Shema that follows, drawing out 'One'. Line for the day: 'Hidden for love of me.'

**Where the sources differ.** Whose love are 'great love' and 'eternal love'? Tanya 43: man's two loves. The Maggid: God's two loves, for the righteous (which caused the tzimtzum) and for the penitent. That the names moved from His love to ours, with 'as water reflects face' as the bridge, is our reading.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 43:7 (line 630): אך ״אהבת עולם״ היא הבאה מהתבונה ודעת בגדולת ה׳ איןסוף ברוךהוא, הממלא כל עלמין וסובב כל עלמין, וכולא קמיה כלא ממש חשיב 'Eternal love' (ahavat olam) is born of contemplating that Ein Sof fills and surrounds all worlds and all is as nothing before Him (F04, F02, F12). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 43:6 (line 629): כי אהבה זו היא מבחינת אצילות, דלית תמן קיצוץ ופירוד Its source is Atzilut, 'where there is no cutting off or separation': the higher unity felt as love (F02). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 49:5 (line 692): וגנזו והסתירו בג׳ מיני צמצומים שונים, והכל בשביל אהבת האדם התחתון Tanya 49: 'as water reflects the face': He hid His light through three contractions for love of the lowly man; the concealment is itself His love, and it calls for ours (F03).

## S115 · Awe · יראה

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F01 The world said into being now; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** The sky and the street are His garments, with Him inside giving them life; seen from higher, they and you are nothing on your own in His word. Awe is standing in that.

**Hold.** 'Just so one should fear God: by seeing heaven and earth and all their hosts, in which the light of Ein Sof is clothed to give them life' (Tanya 42).

**The move.** Open it in two grades. The lower awe sees the world as His garments, with Him hidden inside giving them life, and it comes first, as the gate. The higher, which the Tanya calls shame, sees everything being brought from nothing by His word and set aside in it; and 'let a person not exclude himself'. Berditchev states the same two grades: the lower is fear of being separated from Him, the higher is having no self left before Him. Bring it down to the window of your room and the street outside it this morning. The picture is the Tanya's, a king honoured for the life in him and not for his clothes; it is only a picture, since He has no body. You, here, now: you too are held in His word.

**Stay.** 'Heaven and earth are His garments', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Closer to not wanting to be apart from Him than to being frightened, Berditchev says of the lower awe; the higher the Tanya calls shame, the self gone quiet before Him. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'The whole earth is full of His glory.' Then keep one mitzvah today that you would rather skip, before the King who is here. Line for the day: 'His garments, with Him inside.'

**Note.** That the lower and higher awe are the lower and higher unity of this face is our mapping; the Tanya names them lower and higher awe.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 42:16 (line 619): ככה ממש יש לו לירא את ה׳, על ידי ראיית עיני בשר בשמים וארץ וכל צבאם, אשר אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא מלובש בהם להחיותם Tanya 42: one fears a king for his inner life, not his body; so one fears God by seeing heaven and earth as garments in which Or Ein Sof is clothed to give them life (F01, F03). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 43:4 (line 627): הרי השמים והארץ וכל צבאם, בטלים במציאות ממש בדבר ה׳ ורוח פיו The higher awe (shame) comes from Chochmah that sees how everything is born from nothing and is nullified in existence in His word (higher unity, F02, F01). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 43:4 (line 627): ואל יוציא אדם עצמו מהכלל 'Let a person not exclude himself': his own body and soul are equally nullified; the higher awe is the self's bitul (F08).

## S116 · Accepting the yoke · קבלת עול

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F02 The higher and the lower unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Below feeling there is a place in you that simply does not rebel against the King; taking on His yoke from there is a bond deeper than love.

**Hold.** 'This acceptance is true without any doubt, for it is the nature of every Jewish soul not to rebel against the holy King' (Tanya 41).

**The move.** Open it. He leaves the upper and the lower beings and unites His kingship upon us; we accept it in the Shema in words, and again in the Amidah's bows in deed. The acceptance is true even when no awe is felt, because not rebelling is the soul's nature. R. Aharon of Strashelye weighs it: love's self-giving is higher as a state, but the yoke's is higher in root, a bond from the essence and not from what one enjoys. So after love, take on the yoke again, 'from the side of His Essence, besides whom there is nothing'. Bring it down to the one mitzvah you will do today whether or not you feel like it. The picture is Homel's, a servant who with all his powers serves the King and not himself; it is only a picture, since truer still is how the servants stand in the King's own knowledge, where their being does not register at all. You, here, now: His servant, whatever you feel.

**Stay.** 'It is the soul's nature not to rebel against the King', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Plain and steady rather than warm, with nothing to prove or hold up; the Tzemach Tzedek says the yoke changes one's very essence. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, before the Shema: 'I accept upon myself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven.' Then do that one mitzvah today on time, without waiting for the mood. Line for the day: 'His servant, whatever I feel.'

**Where the sources differ.** Which bitul is deeper, love's or the yoke's? Strashelye: love's is higher as a state, the yoke's higher in root. The Rebbe (from the Maharash): the yoke of Heaven is bitul of the self itself, but its purpose is the yoke of mitzvot. Tanya 41: one must be both servant and son; love alone does not rise, nor awe alone.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 41:8 (line 583): וקבלה זו היא אמיתית בלי שום ספק, שהרי היא טבע נפשות כל ישראל שלא למרוד במלך הקדוש יתברך Tanya 41: even without felt awe, accepting His kingship is 'true without doubt', because not rebelling against the King is the nature of every Jewish soul (F06). - Sha'arei Avodah, Fourth Gate 17:5 (line 605): אבל שורש הביטול הזה הוא למעלה מהביטול שהוא מצד האהבה כי הביטול מצד העול מורה על עצם התקשרות נקודת לבבו מצד העצם Strashelye: the yoke's bitul is lower in state than love's but higher in root, since it shows the bond of the heart's point from the essence, not from what it enjoys (F05). - Sha'arei Avodah, Fourth Gate 17:6 (line 606): צריך לקבל עליו העול מצד עצמותו ברוך הוא אשר אין זולתו יתברך Strashelye: after the love of Shema one must accept the yoke again 'from the side of His Essence, besides whom there is nothing'.

## S117 · Cleaving · דביקות

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F01 The world said into being now; F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Cleaving to Him is being alive from your source: He is the Life of all life, and once you know He hides in whatever is in front of you, nothing screens you from Him.

**Hold.** 'The cause of true life is cleaving: one who cleaves to God, the Life of all life, lives in essence' (the Besht, Keter Shem Tov).

**The move.** Open it. Without cleaving, the Besht says, a person lives only by accident. Once you know He hides in the garments, even in a stray thought, there is no screen between you and Him. Yosher Divrei Emet, from Zbarazh, says one who longs for Him sees, in whatever he sees, God whose power gives it life. And the Tanya adds that since in the Torah He gave us His will and wisdom, 'as if He gave us Himself', nothing blocks the cleaving but the will. Bring it down to the ordinary things in front of you at work today. The picture is the Besht's, heat, which belongs to fire and only visits water; it is only a picture, since He is not fire and you are not water, but it shows the difference between borrowed life and life from the source. You, here, now: nothing screens you from Him.

**Stay.** 'Cleaving to the Life of all life', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Not a feeling that comes and goes: the Mitteler Rebbe describes a bond that is constant and from the root, and Vitebsk says the truest cleaving is not felt at all, so there is nothing to check for. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'And you who cleave to the Lord your God are all alive today.' Then learn one line of Torah today, as holding His will. Line for the day: 'No screen.'

**Where the sources differ.** Does love bring a person into the unity? Tanya 35: even the tzaddik who loves remains 'a thing in itself'; only the mitzvot, His will, are fully one with Him. The Besht: devekut is essential life. The Mitteler Rebbe: devekut is the constant bond, love only an arousal at times. Vitebsk: every felt love is 'false', only a way in; true devekut is unfelt faith.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Keter Shem Tov 1:55:4 (line 87): דסבת אמתת החיות הוא הדביקות וכשיש לו דביקות בהש"י שהוא חי החיים הוא חי' בעצם The Besht: devekut is the cause of true life; cleaving to the Life of all life, a person lives essentially, without it only accidentally (F01, F06). - Keter Shem Tov 1:55:10 (line 93): שאין שום מסך מבדיל בינו לבין אלהיו בשעת תורה ותפלה The Besht: once one knows God hides in the garments (even stray thoughts), 'there is no screen dividing him from his God' (F03). - Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 7:14 (line 146): הדבקים ממילא בהתקשרות עצמיית ותמידיית ולא בחינת התפעלות לפרקים 'You who cleave': from the root of the soul, an essential, constant bond, not an arousal from time to time (F06, F05).

## S118 · Run and return · רצוא ושוב

**Face:** F07 The home below · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** The soul runs toward Him and comes back, and the coming back is the point: it returns so that His unity can live, revealed, in this lower world.

**Hold.** 'Perforce you live': to draw higher life down from the Life of life, through the Torah of life, and make a dwelling below for His unity, revealed (Tanya 50).

**The move.** Open it. The soul by nature runs toward its source. The Besht gives two reasons it comes back: so as not to be annulled, and so that delight stays a delight. The Alter Rebbe gives the reason from the unity: a dwelling below for His unity, revealed. And the Rebbe says it in one line: the purpose of the running is the return. Bring it down to the moment you close the siddur and turn to the day's first task. The picture is the Maharash's, the holy creatures that run to be included in the light and return because 'perforce you live'; it is only a picture, since there is no distance to cross, only the soul's reaching and its turn to the task. You, here, now: this task is the return.

**Stay.** 'Perforce you live, to make Him a dwelling below', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Coming down without falling: the joy of a plain man whose King lives in his house. The Besht adds that rising and falling keeps the delight a delight. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, as you close the siddur: 'There is nothing besides Him.' Then give a coin to charity on your way out, as the first act of the return. Line for the day: 'Perforce you live.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: Tanya 50, the Maharash and the Rebbe tie the return to the dwelling below; in the Besht's own reasons (to keep existence and to keep delight alive) the tie to the unity is our reading.

**Where the sources differ.** Why return from the running? The Besht: otherwise the soul would be annulled from existence, and a constant delight is no delight. The Alter Rebbe: to make a dwelling below for His unity, revealed. The Maharash: 'perforce you live'. The Rashab: running without return is death. The Rebbe: the purpose of running is the return.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 50:6 (line 708): כדי להמשיך חיים עליונים מחיי החיים ברוךהוא למטה, על ידי תורת חיים, להיות דירה בתחתונים לאחדותו יתברך בבחינת גילוי Tanya 50: when the heart runs to expire into its source, it must return, because the soul lives in the body to draw life below and make a dwelling for His unity, revealed (F07). - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ג / בס"ד. ש"פ במדבר, מבה"ח וער"ח סיון ה'תשכ"ט:14 (line 2258): דכמו שברצוא ושוב, תכלית הכוונה דהרצוא הוא בשביל השוב שלאחרי הרצוא The Rebbe: the purpose of running is the return; kabbalat ol of Heaven (ratzo, self-sacrifice) exists for kabbalat ol of mitzvot (shov), through which the Essence's intent, the dwelling below, is completed. - Keter Shem Tov 1:88:1 (line 137): כי תענוג תמידי נעשה טבע ואינו תענוג The Besht: a constant delight becomes nature and is no delight; so a person rises and falls, and the delight is the main thing in the service.

## S119 · Fervor · התלהבות

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'; F01 The world said into being now **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**Before you start.** If fervor comes with sleeplessness, racing thoughts, or a sense of a special mission, that is not this practice; speak to a doctor.

**The glimpse.** Fervor at its truest is a fire that burns away the self, not one the self warms itself at; it rises from seeing that all is as nothing before Him, or that He is standing right here.

**Hold.** 'Through contemplating the greatness of Ein Sof, before whom all is as nothing, the soul flames up and burns' (Tanya 50).

**The move.** Open it. The flame's source, the Tanya says, is seeing that all is as nothing before Him. The Besht's circle kindles it by thinking 'the Shechinah surely stands opposite me'. And the Rashab is honest that for most people it comes another way, from seeing how far one is from Him and thirsting to come near. It cannot be constant, the Besht says; what can be constant is holding to the letters of the prayer, which are from Him too. Bring it down to the place you stand to pray tomorrow, and the line in the prayer that usually moves you. The picture is Piaseczno's: a flame, which has no place of its own and does not stay, and a coal, which holds the fire; it is only a picture, since the self is not fuel, but it shows why the flame is not the goal. You, here, now: the letters in front of you are His.

**Stay.** 'All before Him is as nothing', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** The Mitteler Rebbe's sign of holy fire is less of oneself in it, not more; if what is left is pleasure in one's own fervor, the Rashab calls that the feeling of selfhood. If nothing burns at all, the letters still hold, and the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, before you pray: 'The Shechinah surely stands opposite me.' Then pray, and when a line moves you, go on to the next word rather than stopping to enjoy it. Line for the day: 'The letters are His.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: fervor is a state of the soul and much said about it is psychology; the tie is stated for its source (Tanya 50), its test (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut) and its occasion (the Shechinah present), so the meditation holds those and does not chase the heat.

**Where the sources differ.** Is fervor the height of service or a danger? Tzava'at HaRivash: serving in fervor is complete devekut, though it cannot be constant. The Mitteler Rebbe: outward, exaggerated arousal comes from the spreading self of nogah; holy arousal is without self-feeling. The Rashab: satisfaction with one's fervor is 'the feeling of yeshut'. Piaseczno: the flame does not stay; one needs the coal.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 50:3 (line 705): דהיינו, שעל ידי התבוננות בגדולת איןסוף ברוךהוא, דכולא קמיה כלא ממש חשיב – תתלהט ותתלהב הנפש Tanya 50: the love 'like fiery coals' is kindled by contemplating that all is as nothing before Ein Sof (F12, F02). - Tzava'at HaRivash 68:2 (line 124): כי מסתמא השכינה בודאי עומדת לנגדי. ומכח זה יבא להתלהבות גדול Tzava'at HaRivash: in prayer one thinks 'the Shechinah surely stands opposite me', and from this comes great fervor (F01). - Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 9:11 (line 203): הוא בא בתכלית בחינת הבטול בהעדר הרגשת עצמו לגמרי In holiness even the felt arousal comes with complete bitul, without feeling oneself at all (F08).

## S120 · Nullification · ביטול

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F05 The Essence beyond both; F07 The home below **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Everything is as nothing before Him; bitul, setting yourself aside, is agreeing to that from your side, and holiness rests only where you stop standing as a thing on your own.

**Hold.** '"We thank You" means we concede that the truth is as it is before Him: above is the true being, and below is nothing' (the Tzemach Tzedek on Modim).

**The move.** Open it. The Tanya says that not setting oneself aside is a denial of His true unity, since in truth all is as nothing before Him. Likkutei Torah says the purpose of every mitzvah is to turn 'something' into 'nothing', as the parchment of tefillin becomes a vessel for 'One'. It is frank that the bitul a person makes is not yet the true one, since he is still someone setting himself aside; but it draws the true one from above. And the Rebbe adds that the bitul that counts reaches your very existence, and that is what completes His dwelling below. Bring it down to the bow at 'Modim' in today's Amidah. The picture is the Tanya's, a ray inside the sun, which is not a thing of its own there; it is only a picture, since you are not a ray of light and He is not a star. You, here, now: bowing, you concede it.

**Stay.** 'The truth is as it is before Him', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** The lightness of a ray inside the sun, with nothing to hold up; and no need to have seen it yet, since conceding is how it begins. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, at Modim in today's Amidah: 'We thank You', meaning 'we concede', and bow slowly. Then give a coin to charity today, something of yours made a vessel for Him. Line for the day: 'The truth is as it is before Him.'

**Where the sources differ.** Should the self be abolished? Tanya 22: to regard oneself as a thing apart is idolatry. The Degel, from the Besht: keep only as much selfhood as the world's existence needs. Piaseczno: a Jew's selfhood is a spark of the King, the root of choice, to be made holy. The Rebbe: the highest bitul is within existence, and it completes the dwelling below.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 23:1 (line 240): וענין מודים אנחנו לך היינו שאנו מודים שהאמת הוא כמו שהוא קמי' ית' שלמעלה היש ולמטה האין The Tzemach Tzedek: bowing at Modim concedes the higher knowledge, that above is the true yesh and below is nothing; but only as concession, not as sight. - Likkutei Torah, Behar 2:19 (line 997): אמנם ביטול זה אינו אמיתי שהרי מ״מ יש הוא אלא שמבטל א״ע But this bitul is not true bitul, since he is still a yesh nullifying himself; it is an arousal from below that draws the true ayin from above. - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ג / בס"ד. ש"פ במדבר, מבה"ח וער"ח סיון ה'תשכ"ט:15 (line 2259): שהביטול דיום הזה (שהאהבה היא בביטול) הוא גם בהמציאות דהאדם. ושעי"ז דוקא נשלמת הכוונה דדירה בתחתונים The Rebbe: the higher bitul is the one that reaches the person's existence itself, and only through it is the dwelling below completed (F07).

## S121 · The separate self · ישות

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; F06 The soul as part of the One; F05 The Essence beyond both **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** The sense of standing on your own is real as a sense, not as the truth: it is His life hiding in you. Taken for the truth, it says 'I, and none besides me'.

**Hold.** 'You need only take out the chametz, which is selfhood and haughtiness, and bring in the matzah, which is bitul' (the Alter Rebbe to a learned newcomer, as the Rayatz tells it).

**The move.** Open it. Things look as if they stand on their own only because His life hides in them. The other side takes that look for the truth and says 'I, and none besides me', which the Tanya calls other gods. Even learning can feed it: Chassidus learned without the work of prayer, the Rashab warns, makes a person more of a self. Bring it down to one conversation today in which you are tempted to be the hero of the story. The picture is the Alter Rebbe's, leavened bread, puffed up, and flat matzah; it is only a picture, since the self is not dough. And the sources differ on what becomes of it: the Degel keeps as much of it as the world needs, and Piaseczno calls it a spark of the King to be made holy. You, here, now: what feels like 'I' on its own is His life, hidden.

**Stay.** 'Take out the chametz of selfhood; bring in the matzah of bitul', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Relief more than loss: less to inflate and defend, the lightness of a ray inside the sun. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'There is none besides Him', the reverse of 'I, and none besides me'. Then give a coin to charity today where no one sees it. Line for the day: 'Matzah, not chametz.'

**Where the sources differ.** Should the self be abolished? Tanya 22: to regard oneself as a thing apart is idolatry. The Degel, from the Besht: keep only as much selfhood as the world's existence needs. Piaseczno: a Jew's selfhood is a spark of the King, the root of choice, to be made holy. The Rebbe: the highest bitul is within existence, and it completes the dwelling below.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 22:7 (line 300): ואדרבה, מגביה עצמה כנשר, לומר: ״אני ואפסי עוד״ Tanya 22: kelipah is 'other gods' because it is not nullified and says 'I, and there is none besides me', the inversion of 'ein od milvado'. - Kuntres Toras HaChassidus (scans, OCR), id 15658, scan p. 21 ¶4 (line 176) *(OCR)*: רק צריך אתה להוציא את החמץ שהוא ישות וגסות הרוח, ולהכניס מצה שהוא ביטול The Alter Rebbe to a learned, God-fearing newcomer (via the Rayatz): remove the chametz of selfhood, bring in the matzah of bitul (OCR). - Derekh HaMelekh, Torah, Ha'Azinu 8 (line 736): והאיש ישראל שנאחז בגופא דמלכא לכן גם הארת ניצוץ חלק ישות נמשכת בו עד שמתחיל בו רצונו But a Jew, held by the King's 'body', receives a spark of yeshut from above, so his will begins in himself; this is the root of free choice and of both inclinations (F06).

## S122 · Humility · ענוה ושפלות

**Face:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F05 The Essence beyond both; F09 The unity of opposites **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Humility is what nearness looks like: the nearer to Him, the more one is nothing before Him, and that lowliness is where the Essence rests. It is not the crushed self.

**Hold.** 'All is as nothing specifically "before Him"; so whoever is more "before Him" is more as nothing' (Iggeret HaKodesh 2).

**The move.** Open it. Every kindness brings a person nearer, before Him, so with every kindness one becomes lower, like Yaakov's 'I am small from all the kindnesses'. The Besht says: to praise, praise God; to disparage, disparage yourself; for the one who praises affirms the unity, and the one who disparages others separates from it. And Yosher Divrei Emet, from Zbarazh, says the more one cleaves, the more one feels one's poverty. Bring it down to one kindness you received this week, from a person or from Him. The picture is the Mitteler Rebbe's, a pauper, crushed and still full of self; it is only a picture of the lowliness that is not meant, since the other lowliness, in the essence of the soul, is roomy and becomes a vessel for His height. You, here, now: small before the One who gave it, and, Hayom Yom adds, able to rise high.

**Stay.** 'Whoever is more before Him is more as nothing', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Not being crushed: the second lowliness has room in it, a vessel for something higher. Small and high at once. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'I am small from all the kindnesses and all the truth You have done.' Then, when someone praises you today, pass it on to Him in a word, 'thank God'. Line for the day: 'Nearer, smaller.'

**Where the sources differ.** Does humility mean belittling oneself? The Besht: disparage yourself, praise God. The Mitteler Rebbe: the crushed self is one lowliness, essential lowliness another. Piaseczno: one already fixed in service need not belittle himself in the hour of greatness; one still guarding against sin must. Hayom Yom: how small, and how high.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 2:2 (line 997): וכנודע, דכולא ״קמיה״ דווקא – כלא חשיב, ואם כן, כל שהוא ״קמיה״ יותר – הוא יותר כלא ואין ואפס Iggeret HaKodesh 2: with every kindness one must become lower, because all is as nothing specifically 'before Him', so whoever is more 'before Him' is more nothing (F02). - Toras Chaim, שמות ח"ב / פרשת משפטים / לא תהי' משכלה / רצט, ד:1 (line 4095): כי יש בשפלות [ג"כ] ב' אופנים, א' ביטול הישות והגסות כעני מדוכא שנעשה שפל ע"י העוני. והב' שפלות עצמו בעצם הנפש The Mitteler Rebbe: two lowlinesses, the crushed self of a pauper and lowliness in the essence of the soul; the second becomes a vessel for the Essence's exaltedness (G05). - Hayom Yom, אייר / יט אייר, לד לעומר:1 (line 288): דער פארשטאנד וואס ווייזט דעם מענטשן ווי קליין ער איז, און ווי גרויס ער קען זיך דערהויבן Hayom Yom: Chassidus shows a person how small he is and how high he can rise.

## S123 · Self-sacrifice · מסירות נפש

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F04 Filling and surrounding **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** This is about the point in the soul that will not deny Him, lived in plain daily things; it is not about wanting to die. If thoughts of not wanting to be here are around, put this aside and reach a person now: your local emergency number or a crisis line.

**The glimpse.** In every Jew, learned or not, is a point that will not be separated from the One at any price. It is not bravery or effort; it is what the soul is.

**Hold.** 'The One God shines in the whole soul and gives it life, clothed in its wisdom', which is why even the unlearned give their lives rather than deny Him (Tanya 18).

**The move.** Open it. It is done without reason or argument, even by those who know little, because the light in the soul's innermost wisdom is above understanding. The Tanya says it includes the fear of being separated from His oneness 'even at the cost of life'. The Mitteler Rebbe says the demand to be separated from Him wakes the deepest power, the yechidah, and it comes from the soul's bond with its source, not from effort. And it holds even for a moment: a Jew will not bow to an idol for one moment and repent afterward. Bring it down to the Shema you say every day, and to one small mitzvah today that costs you something. The picture is the Rayatz's: mind and feelings are garments over the soul's essence, which is why simple people show this more; it is only a picture, since the essence is not a body under clothes. You, here, now: that point is in you, whoever you are, and it asks you not for your life but not to be separated.

**Stay.** 'The One God shines in the whole soul', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Not bravery: more like finding a floor that was always there, a core no one has touched. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'Hear, Israel, the Lord is our God, the Lord is One', meaning for that line not to be separated from Him at any price. Then keep one small mitzvah today that costs you something. Line for the day: 'Not separated, not for a moment.'

**Where the sources differ.** Self-sacrifice for which unity? Tanya 18-25 and the Mitteler Rebbe: not to be separated from His oneness. The Tzemach Tzedek: it draws the surrounding light below. R. Nachman: it effects the union of the Holy One and His Shechinah. R. Tzadok: Avraham's refusal and Yitzchak's offering are two kinds. That these are different senses of 'unity' is our reading.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 18:14 (line 262): והיינו, משום שה׳ אחד מאיר ומחיה כל הנפש, על ידי התלבשותו בבחינת חכמה שבה Tanya 18: even the unlearned and sinners give their lives rather than deny the one God, because 'the one God' shines in the soul's Chochmah, above reason (F06). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 25:2 (line 335): שלא ליפרד בשום אופן מיחודו ואחדותו יתברך, אפילו במסירת נפש ממש Tanya 25: the hidden love includes the fear of being separated from His oneness 'even at the cost of life'. - Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 28:27 (line 531): אך ליפרד לגמרי מאחד בזה מעורר כח היותר פנימי ועצמי לבוא לידי מסירת נפש בפועל ממש The Mitteler Rebbe: being asked to separate entirely from the One awakens the innermost, essential power, the yechidah, to actual self-sacrifice.

## S124 · Truth · אמת

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** What is really so is that He alone is; every other truth is as nothing beside His. To be true is to concede that, and in the end to see it.

**Hold.** 'The truth of "the Lord God is truth" is His unity: He alone is, and there is literally nothing besides Him' (Tanya 35).

**The move.** Open it. The Tzemach Tzedek makes it a commandment: to know the truth that He is one and there is nothing else. Iggeret HaKodesh 6 says His seal is truth, and all creaturely truth is as nothing before Him. And yet each level's truth, the Tanya says, is real for that level, and as nothing only before the one above. For now, the Tzemach Tzedek adds, we concede the higher truth when we bow at Modim; it is concession, not yet sight. Bring it down to one small matter today where a slight untruth would be easier. The picture is the Tanya's, truth as an inheritance without borders; it is only a picture, since truth is not land, but it shows that no level is its ceiling. You, here, now: conceding that He alone is.

**Stay.** 'He alone is', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Standing on what is really so without needing to see all of it yet: true at your level, with always more above. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, at Modim today: 'We thank You', as conceding that He alone is. Then, in one small matter today, say the true thing where a slight untruth would be easier. Line for the day: 'His seal is truth.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: for truth as His name and as the higher knowledge the tie is stated; for truthfulness as a trait it runs only through 'His seal is truth' and is largely our reading, so the act of honesty at the end is ours.

**Where the sources differ.** Is truth seen or conceded? The Tzemach Tzedek: at Modim the higher truth is conceded, not seen. Tanya 13: each level has its own truth. R. Tzadok: this world is falsehood; truth is complete unity, reached by union and simplicity. Sefat Emet: truth in a person is itself a Godly power, reached through Torah.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 35:5 (line 475): כי אמיתת ״ה׳ אלהים אמת״ – הוא יחודו ואחדותו, שהוא לבדו הוא ואפס בלעדו ממש Tanya 35: the truth of 'Havayah Elokim is truth' is His unity: He alone is, and there is literally nothing besides Him. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 13:14 (line 209): ומדת אמת היא ״נחלה בלי מצרים״, ואין לה שיעור למעלה עד רום המעלות Tanya 13: the trait of truth, Yaakov's, has no upper measure; each level's truth is 'as nothing' before the level above it, so a beinoni's love is 'true' for him. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 23:1 (line 240): אלא שזהו בבחי' הודאה לבד ולא בראיה והשגה נגלית ממש The Tzemach Tzedek: at Modim we concede that the truth is as it is before Him, above the yesh and below the ayin, but only as concession, not sight (F12).
