## What this cluster shows

These twelve subjects are the one idea as it is lived from inside: felt (joy, sadness, bitterness), suffered (suffering, trials), held (faith, trust, simplicity), lost and regained (doubt, despair and hope), and met at the edge (death, reward and punishment). The spine anchors only one of them in so many words (G16: joy in faith in the unity, Tanya 33), but the sources state far more. Iggeret HaKodesh 11 is the hinge of the cluster. From continuous creation out of the Ayin (F01) it derives joy at every moment, calls sadness 'like heresy', dissolves suffering into good not grasped, defines faith as 'no place empty of Him', and names the purpose of man as being tested. The Rebbeim then add their own sides: the Mitteler Rebbe places true simplicity and the bitter cry in the yechidah (F06); the Maharash reads bitterness as the altar ash that rises into the unity and sadness as the ash thrown out; the Rashab makes the broken heart a house for the King (F07) and warns that lingering bitterness is sadness; the Rayatz calls a trial 'not a thing in itself'; the Rebbe grounds joy in bitul and trust in 'no support besides Him', and reads histalkus as the revelation of light above the worlds and the resurrection as the Essence revealed in the body.

Two things keep the cluster honest. First, the Breslov and Polish voices do not merely echo Chabad. R. Nachman states hope as the unity itself ('even in the lowest Sheol God is found') more directly than any Chabad text found here, and though his doctrine of heresy speaks of a space 'as it were' empty of God, he says in the same teaching that 'in very truth Godliness is there too'; the difference from the Alter Rebbe (SH 7) is how the tzimtzum may be spoken of, not whether any place is without Him. The Piaseczno Rebbe in the Warsaw Ghetto keeps IH 11's faith that all is for good and adds what IH 11 does not say: God Himself suffers in the pain. Several links first written as stated are now marked as our reading or qualified: the Rebbe's 'all is from the Creator' is his first, provisional explanation of trust; the 'hope itself' is a Midrash he cites; Breslov's 'no despair' is grounded in the tzaddik, not in the unity as such. Second, two subjects are partly thin: despair and hope (the stated tie is Breslov's) and death as such (stated only for martyrdom, the tzaddik's histalkus and 'ratzo without shov').

## S138 · Joy · שמחה

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F07 The home below · F01 The world said into being now · F09 The unity of opposites **Search terms:** שמחה · בשמחה · עבדו את ה' בשמחה · שמחת לבב

> Joy is the unity of God as it is felt: the heart's answer to 'there is nothing besides Him, even below', and a share in His own joy in His dwelling here.

## Derivation

1. The contemplation of Tanya 33 is that the world and all it holds are nullified in their source, as the sun's light is in the sun. When one goes deep into this, 'his heart will rejoice… in this faith, for it is nearness to God itself' (Tanya 33:3-4). (*stated*)
2. What one rejoices in is named precisely: the inheritance of 'the true unity of God, that even in the earth below there is nothing besides Him, and that is His dwelling below' (Tanya 33:6). Joy has an object, and the object is the one idea. (*stated*)
3. So Chabakuk's 'the righteous lives by his faith' means joy in faith in the unity, as if it were the only mitzvah, and that joy lifts the soul over everything that blocks the 613 mitzvot (Tanya 33:7-8, spine G16). (*stated*)
4. The joy is doubled: besides the soul's own joy in nearness, one rejoices in God's joy that the other side is subdued and darkness turned to light in this physical world (F11), 'Israel rejoices in its Makers', in His dwelling in the lowest (Tanya 33:9-12, F07). (*stated*)
5. The same unity grounds joy in every hour: He gives life and does good at every moment (F01), so 'first of all a person should rejoice at every time and hour' (IH 11:4). Sadness is then the claim that something is missing (S139). (*stated*)
6. The Rebbe adds that joy in the open must rest on bitul (F08), so that the felt and the nullified are joined, 'a union of two opposites' that comes from above division (F09); and because joy breaks bounds it prepares for a revelation above even sovev (Chayei Sarah maamar, lines 501-505). (*stated*)
7. Outside Chabad the command of joy is as strong but grounded differently. The Besht's circle ties it to faith that the Shechinah is with him (Tzava'at HaRivash 137:5). Breslov argues it from health and the future dance (Likutei Moharan II 24); the one line there that names the Shechinah as the sick person's only life is a wordplay proof, not a derivation. The Breslov tie to the unity is ours. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 26: joy (zerizut) is needed to win against the yetzer, as a wrestler cannot win while heavy. Tanya 31: the soul's joy at leaving exile, like a prince freed from prison. Tanya 33: joy in faith in the unity and in God's joy in the dwelling below; this is the chapter the spine anchors (G16). IH 11: joy at every moment follows from His giving life at every moment.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 41:28-29: delight in God, when it comes in temimut without self-feeling, reaches the essential delight of the yechidah. He places true joy by its source in the soul, not by its intensity.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located for joy as such in this pass. The spine's 'Chassidus is chayus' (Hayom Yom 24 Teves) is the nearest.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5641 (Vayar Yisrael): joy comes after bitterness, through contemplating 'even darkness is not dark to You', and so the lowliness is included in the unity (see S140).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR), scan p. 24: day is light and joy, night is din; after bitterness one should gladden the soul with Tanya 31 and 33 and learn Chassidus 'in order to pray in joy'.
- **Rayatz.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Chayei Sarah (20 Cheshvan): joy founded on bitul; felt joy with bitul is a union of opposites; 'joy breaks every fence', and so is a vessel for the level above sovev, to be revealed in the Third Temple.
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov circle: Tzava'at HaRivash 137:5 (constant joy with faith that the Shechinah guards him); 107:1 (prayer in great joy is more accepted than prayer in weeping). Breslov: Likutei Moharan II 24, 'a great mitzvah to be always joyful', argued from illness and the future dance. The Concepts pillar (06) also records Berditchev (joy in God's joy, Kedushat Levi, Purim 3) and the Sfas Emes (sorrow entered only with the sin); not re-verified here. The difference: Chabad derives joy from the content of the unity; Breslov makes joy a practice to be forced, even by foolishness (pillar 06), without deriving it.

Not thin in Chabad: Tanya 33 states the tie in so many words. In Breslov and the Polish schools joy is a mode of service and the tie to the unity is our reading.

## S139 · Sadness and melancholy · עצבות

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** עצבות · מרה שחורה · עצב · עצבון

> Sadness is the unity of God denied in feeling: it takes His concealment for His absence and one's own lack for a real lack.

## Derivation

1. Worldly sadness rests on something seeming bad. The Alter Rebbe's answer: 'this too is for good', only not visible, because it comes from the hidden world, the yud-heh of the Name, which is higher than the revealed world (Tanya 26:5). What grieves is concealment, not absence (F03, G03). (*stated*)
2. The concealment is from below only: from above there is only good. 'There is no evil that descends from above' (IH 11:2). So the person who is sad and complains 'shows that he has a little evil… and this is like a heretic' (IH 11:4). IH 11 sets this against 'the essence of faith', that no place is empty of Him (11:3). That sadness is thereby a denial of the higher unity in the spine's sense (F02) is our naming. (*stated*)
3. Tanya 31:2 places sadness in kelipat nogah, not holiness, since 'strength and gladness are in His place' and the Shechinah rests only amid joy. The same line qualifies it: sadness over heavenly matters is 'from the good in nogah', and one who comes to great sadness through long contemplation of his lowness 'should not worry' (31:1). The 'therefore' that ties this to IH 11 is ours; the two texts do not cite each other. (*stated*)
4. Its inner root is self-concern. The Besht's circle answers sadness over a stringency: 'my intent in service is not for myself but to give Him pleasure' (Tzava'at HaRivash 44:2). That this is the bitul of F08 is our naming; the text states the self-removal. (**reading**)
5. Sadness must be told from bitterness (S140). The Rashab warns that bitterness which lingers 'is in truth sadness' (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan p. 46), and the Maharash makes sadness the ash thrown outside the camp because it makes a person despair (S147). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 26: sadness is heaviness that loses the wrestle; worldly sadness is cured by 'this too is for good' from the hidden world; spiritual sadness during business is the yetzer's ploy, and even real grief over sins belongs only at fixed times. Tanya 27: even when bad thoughts fall in, rejoice at turning away. Tanya 31: sadness is from kelipat nogah. IH 11: the sad person is 'like a heretic'.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39:19 speaks of natural melancholy (marah shechorah) in the delicate; not read in full in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5641: sadness is the ash taken outside the camp, 'the threshold trodden by all sins', because it makes a person despair entirely.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah (OCR), scan p. 46: bitterness held for a long time is really sadness. Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR), scan p. 24: the night is din, bitterness and sadness from the gevurot; not to be carried into the day.
- **Rayatz.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass; see his joy maamar under S138.
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov circle: Tzava'at HaRivash 44-46: the yetzer invents sins and stringencies to make a person sad; after a real sin grieve briefly, then return to joy in the Creator. Breslov: Likutei Moharan II 24: all illness comes from damaged joy; drive away sadness and melancholy with all one's strength. The Concepts pillar (06) records the Chozeh (sadness drove off Elisha's prophecy), Sochatchov and Radomsk; not re-verified here.

## Thin or forced?

Not thin in Chabad: IH 11 states that sadness contradicts faith in the unity. Tanya 31 itself allows sadness from heavenly matters as 'the good in nogah', so the rule is not absolute. In Breslov the stated ground is health and distance from God, not the unity; the tie there is ours.

## S140 · Bitterness, not sadness · מרירות

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F09 The unity of opposites **Search terms:** מרירות · לב נשבר · מרירות הנפש · במרירות

> Bitterness is the unity of God as it measures distance: the pain of a living heart that sees the worlds as mere radiance before Him and itself far, and so is emptied to receive Him.

## Derivation

1. Bitterness is born from the higher unity. The Alter Rebbe: one arouses great mercy by bitterness 'when he contemplates how the life of all worlds is only radiance and Name', while He Himself is unchanged and alone; then he pours out his soul over how far he is (Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 13:15, F02, F05). (*stated*)
2. So it is not sadness. Sadness is a heart dead as stone; bitterness and a broken heart have life, the life of holy gevurot, while joy is chesed, and the heart holds both (Tanya 31:6). Bitterness is a form of chayus. (*stated*)
3. Its place is fixed and limited: set times, best at midnight or when one is already sad for worldly reasons, turned into 'the reckoning' (Tanya 26:8, 31:8). The Rashab: not every night for most people, and in private (Kuntres HaTefillah, scan p. 24). (*stated*)
4. Its turn into joy runs through the soul as part of God: 'all this is I alone, the body… but within me is literally a portion of God', in exile; freeing it is the joy (Tanya 31:9-12, F06). (*stated*)
5. What bitterness does is empty the house for the King: 'then he becomes a vessel for God's light to dwell within' (Rashab, Kuntres HaTefillah, scan p. 23, F07, F08). The Maharash: bitterness is the ash absorbed at the altar, which rises 'to be included in the unity of God' (Toras Shmuel 5641). (*stated*)
6. At its deepest it reaches the yechidah: the true penitent's bitter cry without self-feeling touches the yechidah, as R. Elazar ben Durdaya's soul left him weeping (Mitteler Rebbe, Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 41:26-27). (*stated*)
7. Bitterness and joy are opposites joined in one heart and in one service ('in all sadness there is advantage': the joy after it, Tanya 31:5). That this is F09 in the soul is our naming. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 26 and 31 fix the distinction and the timetable; Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 13:15 derives bitterness from contemplating that the worlds are radiance only, and places maror in the middle line of the seder plate because bitterness arouses great mercy. The Concepts pillar (06) records Likkutei Torah, Beha'alotcha 1:10 (midnight bitterness reveals Godliness over the whole day); not re-verified here.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 33:3-4 (bitterness and lowliness over the coarse body and sins of youth) and 41:26-27 (the bitter cry that reaches the yechidah). He tests bitterness by whether it is free of self-feeling.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5641, Vayar Yisrael: the two parts of the altar ash. Bitterness over distance rises and becomes 'under my head', lifting to love; sadness is thrown out. The method is contemplating 'even darkness is not dark to You'.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR), scan pp. 23-24: the reckoning breaks the heart and makes it a vessel; only at times, best at midnight, alone; afterwards learn Chassidus to pray in joy. Kuntres HaAvodah (OCR), scan p. 46: bitterness that stays becomes sadness. He also warns (scan pp. 42 and 46) that bitterness and arousal from general contemplation alone stay 'surrounding' and 'imaginary', and change nothing in the natural traits.
- **Rayatz.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass.
- **Other schools.** The Concepts pillar (06) records Krakow (Maor VaShemesh: grief brief and deep, then joy at once; ascetics end in melancholy), R. Nachman (bitterness as medicine dosed by God) and Radzin (feel the maror fully). None re-verified here. The real difference: Chabad schedules bitterness deliberately; Breslov keeps a person 'always joyful' and leaves bitterness to God's dosing.

> — The Mitteler Rebbe: the true penitent's bitter cry, without self-feeling, reaches the yechidah (F06); his example is R. Elazar ben Durdaya (next line). Text reads תשבוה for תשובה.

## Thin or forced?

Not thin. Tanya 31, Likkutei Torah and the Maharash state that bitterness comes from, and rises into, the unity. Its joining with joy as 'unity of opposites' is our naming.

## S141 · Suffering · יסורים

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F11 Darkness turned to light · F09 The unity of opposites **Search terms:** יסורים · ייסורים · יסורין של אהבה · צער

> Suffering is the unity of God as hidden good: the same continuous creation from the Ayin, too great to be grasped, which faith includes and raises into the good it already is.

## Derivation

1. Every creature is brought into being from nothing at every moment from His wisdom, the ayin (F01, IH 11:2). (*stated*)
2. That ayin is 'the source of life, good and delight'. So one who pictures his being as continuous creation from it cannot think 'it is bad for me' or that he suffers; suffering seems so only because the good is not grasped, 'there is no evil that descends from above' (IH 11:2). (*stated*)
3. The not-grasped good is the hidden world, higher than the revealed (Tanya 26:5), so rejoicing in suffering is love of nearness to God more than life, a nearness greater in concealment that will shine openly in the future (Tanya 26:6). Suffering is a case of G03: concealment for the sake of a greater revelation. (*stated*)
4. The Besht gives the same structure from the side of creation: creation came through din, the tzimtzum, so human suffering is a 'body' to spiritual life; accepted in love and joy it joins body and soul and the din is annulled (Ba'al Shem Tov al HaTorah, Vaetchanan 16:1). (*stated*)
5. Faith is not only a reading: 'by this faith the imagined bad is truly included and raised into the hidden higher good', and it becomes good openly (IH 11:9). This is darkness turned to light (F11) at the scale of one life. (*stated*)
6. In practice suffering has no second author: in distress no other power rules; even a deserved punishment is from Him and is a kindness that cleanses (the Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos 36, Shemos 1, 3:5). He offers this as a first ('lechora') explanation and then calls it insufficient for trust in its plain sense, which expects revealed good (4:2-4:4); the premise itself is not withdrawn. And the soul's suffering here spares it Gehinnom's scouring (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 12, see S149). (*stated*)
7. A second voice is kept, and it is not a flat denial. The Piaseczno Rebbe in the Warsaw Ghetto holds the same faith as IH 11: self-sacrifice in faith is to believe even in the hiding 'that all is from Him for good… and all the sufferings are full of God's love for Israel' (Esh Kodesh 5702, Hanukkah 4), and suffering serves to crush human knowing so that knowledge of God may be revealed (Yitro 9). Weeks later he adds what IH 11 does not say: God Himself suffers in Israel's suffering, and because He is without limit His pain is without limit and so hidden from the world (Mishpatim-Shekalim 2). The pain is not dissolved; God is placed inside it. That the two are two sides of one idea is ours. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** IH 11 (the whole derivation); Tanya 26:5-6 (the hidden world; 'those who rejoice in suffering'); Iggeret HaTeshuvah 11:13-12:3 (receive all events in joy; bodily suffering is a great good for the soul, sparing Gehinnom, since 'the world is built on kindness').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 41:23 uses the cry of one under torture only as an image of unmixed feeling).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass; his 'think good' belongs to S144.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Hayom Yom 15 Teves (1942): 'the sufferings coming upon us are the birth pangs of Moshiach'; do not trust in man. The suffering is read as part of redemption, not explained away.
- **The Rebbe.** Likkutei Sichos 36, Shemos 1: trust is founded on faith that everything is from the Creator; in distress no one else rules; even punishment is His kindness to cleanse.
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov (anthology, from a disciple's 'my master'): suffering as the din of the tzimtzum, annulled by acceptance in love. Piaseczno (Esh Kodesh): God weeps 'in secret places'; faith in hiddenness as self-sacrifice; questions allowed as prayer, not as argument (5702, Hanukkah 4). The Concepts pillar records the Yismach Moshe's paid debt (suffering seen as providence is borne in joy); not re-verified. The real difference is narrower than it first looks: the Esh Kodesh also asks faith that all is from Him for good (Hanukkah 4); what it adds, and IH 11 does not say, is that God suffers in the pain and that the pain is not dissolved.

## S142 · Trials · נסיון

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F06 The soul as part of the One · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** נסיון · נסיונות · ניסיון · בנסיון

> A trial is the unity of God as a concealment set up by Him and for us: a thing with no reality of its own, placed so that the soul's hidden faith in the One wakes and the concealment dissolves.

## Derivation

1. Man is created in this world 'to test him with these trials' and know what is in his heart: whether it turns after 'other gods', the body's desires from the other side, or to 'the living God' (IH 11:7). The text frames the trial in the language of idolatry, 'other gods' against 'the living God'; that this makes every trial a trial of the unity itself is our gloss on that framing. (*stated*)
2. The tempter has no power of its own. The other side has no doubts; it is permitted to confuse, like the harlot the king sends to test his son, 'by the King's permission' (Tanya 29:26). There is no second power in the trial. (*stated*)
3. So the Rayatz: 'the trial is not a thing in itself, only something set up to conceal and prevent'. Unlike physical refinement, a trial is refined within the person, by arousing essential trust, because 'nature itself is above nature, only it is hidden light' (Sefer HaMaamarim 5693, 12 Tammuz; F03, G14). (*stated*)
4. What the trial wakes is the soul's chochmah, where Or Ein Sof is clothed. In a trial of faith in the One God that chochmah wakes from sleep, and then 'all kelipot are nullified… as nothing before God' (Tanya 19:11-12; F06, F02). (*stated*)
5. Every event, not only temptation, is sent to arouse: a frightening thing is the King's messenger dressed in fear, 'and in truth it is nothing' (Maggid 105; G12). (*stated*)
6. The last exile has more concealment and more trials, which itself shows the greater power given to stand in them (Rayatz, 5693). That the lowest generation has the greatest trials because the Essence shows most at the lowest point (spine thesis, F11) is our joining. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** IH 11:7 (man created to be tested); Tanya 19 (trial of faith wakes the chochmah); Tanya 29:26 (the harlot parable from the Zohar).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5693, 12 Tammuz (his own redemption day): a trial is concealment only; last exile, more trials, more strength ('nes lehitnoses', a banner). Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 5 Kislev: Avraham stood all trials so his seed would not be moved by nature.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass.
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov (Keter Shem Tov 1:39): one who has entered and left a trial need not face it again. Maggid (Maggid Devarav LeYaakov 105): every event comes to arouse; the fear is a messenger and 'nothing'. Piaseczno (Esh Kodesh 5702, Hanukkah 3): the Akeidah was the greater trial, but righteousness is written of faith, on which all self-sacrifice depends.

> — Man is created in order to be tested, and the test is whether the heart turns to 'other gods', the body's desires, or to the living God. The framing is idolatry against the living God; calling it a test of the unity is our gloss.

## S143 · Faith · אמונה

**Faces:** F12 Knowing the unity · F06 The soul as part of the One · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** אמונה · אמונה פשוטה · מאמינים בני מאמינים · אמונתו

> Faith is the unity of God present in the soul before it is understood: the One who shines in the soul's chochmah, holding what knowledge cannot, that no place is empty of Him.

## Derivation

1. Chochmah is 'what is not grasped', and so Or Ein Sof, which no thought grasps, is clothed in it (Tanya 18:10). (*stated*)
2. Therefore every Jew, even the unlearned, believes: faith is above knowledge, and before Him all are simple. They die rather than deny the One God 'because the One God shines in and gives life to the whole soul through the chochmah in it' (Tanya 18:11-14). Faith is the unity already present in the soul (F06). (*stated*)
3. Its content is the unity: 'the essence of faith for which man was created is to believe that no place is empty of Him' (IH 11:3), and Chabakuk stood all 613 mitzvot on faith, read as joy in the unity (Tanya 33:7-8). (*stated*)
4. The Rebbeim disagree on where faith ends and knowledge begins. The Tzemach Tzedek: that God exists and enlivens the world 'is not faith but real knowledge', known from His acts as the soul is known from the body; faith is for His essence (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Mitzvat HaEmunah 2:1). The Alter Rebbe: how He is one with His attributes is beyond knowledge, 'we have no business with hidden things', only to believe (SH 9:7). The spine keeps this disagreement (F12). (*stated*)
5. The Rayatz sharpens the object: the simple faith fixed in every Jew's heart is in Havayah, above nature; the 'soul-faith of the pious of the nations' is only in Elokim, the source of worlds, and such faith 'is in truth comprehension', since Elokim is grasped from the worlds (Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, 5 Kislev; G14). His contrast is between Israel and the nations, not between two stages of one person. (*stated*)
6. Breslov gives faith the task knowledge cannot do: to cross the vacated space, where He cannot be found, by believing He surrounds even it (Likutei Moharan 64:2; F04). Here faith is set against inquiry, not completed by it. (*stated*)
7. The Mitteler Rebbe shows faith and deed can be apart: the thief prays to God for success; faith that God is 'simple unity' is fixed and natural in the divine soul even when hidden, and shows fully in martyrdom (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 25). The Tzemach Tzedek uses the same thief (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Mitzvat HaEmunah 2:2) for a sharper point: faith is only 'surrounding', like a crown, and does not move the traits; hence the need for knowledge. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 18-19 (faith above knowledge, from chochmah where Or Ein Sof is clothed); Tanya 33 (faith as joy in the unity); SH 9:7 (believe what is above comprehension); IH 1 (faith as the 'loins' that hold up the head); IH 11:3 (the essence of faith: no place empty of Him).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 25-27: natural faith in the divine soul, inherited from the fathers; the Exodus came through the people's simple faith.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Mitzvat HaEmunah: 'to know and to believe'; God's existence and giving life are knowledge, not faith; faith is for His essence.
- **Maharash.** The spine records that he places the higher knowledge (all is as nothing before Him) in faith (F12l). Not re-read under this subject.
- **Rashab.** Hayom Yom 12 Teves (5648): in the heels of Moshiach, keep Torah in simple faith, not by following the intellect. This sits beside his demand for contemplation in prayer (spine F12g); both are his.
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692: faith in Havayah above nature versus faith in Elokim that is really comprehension. Hayom Yom 5 Teves: understanding has a limit, faith is a feeling without limit. The spine records his insistence on 'to know' (Limud HaChassidus).
- **The Rebbe.** Likkutei Sichos 36, Shemos: trust is founded on faith that everything is from the Creator, offered as a first explanation and then completed (S144). The spine records his placing the simple feeling of the unity in the yechidah (Inyanah 15:1).
- **Other schools.** Breslov (Likutei Moharan 64): faith, not inquiry, crosses the heresy of the vacated space; Israel are 'Ivrim' because they cross over all wisdoms by faith. Piaseczno (Esh Kodesh 5702, Hanukkah 3-4): faith is God's light in the Jew, not a substitute for knowing; it must be held with self-sacrifice in the hour of hiding. The Concepts pillar (14) records Chernobyl, Karlin, Ropshitz, Sochatchov and others; not re-verified here.

## S144 · Trust · בטחון

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** בטחון · בוטח · בטח בה' · בטחון בה'

> Trust is the unity of God leaned on: having no support in the world besides Him, and so living as if the good He brings into being each moment were already here.

## Derivation

1. The Rebbe's first ('lechora') explanation: trust is founded on faith that everything is from the Creator; in distress no one else has power over a person, so one is at rest even if the distress is deserved (Likkutei Sichos 36, Shemos 1, 3:5). He then calls this insufficient, since trust in its plain sense expects revealed good (4:2-4:4). (*stated*)
2. The Besht's circle gives the ground: all goods and all judgments are rooted in Him, and His flow and vitality are in everything (F01, F09), so 'I trust and fear only Him' (Tzava'at HaRivash 137:5). (*stated*)
3. The Rebbe then distinguishes trust from faith. Calm because all is from Him is not yet trust. Trust is a service of its own: to lean on Him until one casts one's whole lot on Him, 'having no support in the world besides Him' (Likkutei Sichos 36, 4:9-5:1). That this is 'ein od milvado' made into a stance is our naming of what the text states. (*stated*)
4. And trust is causal: the Tzemach Tzedek's 'think good and it will be good' means that the trust itself brings visible good, measure for measure, even to one not otherwise worthy (Likkutei Sichos 36, 5:4-5:6). That trust causes the good is stated, as the Rebbe's reading of the Tzemach Tzedek; its tie to the unity is ours. (**reading**)
5. It reaches through nature: the Rayatz asks for 'complete trust in providence above nature' in all times (Sefer HaMaamarim 5692), because nature itself is hidden supernatural light (5693; F03). (*stated*)
6. How far one may use natural means is disputed. The Maharash, with Rabbeinu Bachya, holds that for a tzaddik like Yosef even asking the butler was a fault, since God is 'master of all causes'; he then raises Chovot HaLevavot's duty to engage the means (Toras Shmuel 5633). The Rebbe asks for complete trust 'together with doing all that depends on him in the natural way'. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** IH 13:11: the 'baalei bitachon' are of the right, the side of chesed revealed without contraction. Not a full teaching on trust in what was read.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** 'Think good and it will be good' (טראכט גוט וועט זיין גוט), said to one who begged him to pray for a dangerously ill person, as the Rayatz retold it and the Rebbe explains it.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5633, VeLo Zachar Sar HaMashkim: Yosef's two years; trust in 'the master of all causes'; the question from Chovot HaLevavot's duty to use means, answered by the difference between Yosef, busy in the world, and the shepherd patriarchs who were a 'chariot'.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass. (Hayom Yom 16 Sivan, which asks for 'hope and complete trust' in the healing of the soul, names no speaker and is not assigned to him here.)
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaMaamarim 5692 and 5693: trust in providence above nature; a trial is refined by arousing essential trust until the obstacles are nullified.
- **The Rebbe.** Likkutei Sichos 36, Shemos 1: trust is a distinct service, not a by-product of faith; it brings visible good; Moshe's fear 'the thing is known' caused Pharaoh to hear; Israel was redeemed in the merit of trust and will be again in the merit of hope.
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov circle (Tzava'at HaRivash 137:5): trust and fear only Him, in whom all goods and judgments are rooted. Esh Kodesh 5701, seventh day of Pesach, opens 'the first lesson to learn from suffering is faith in God and strong trust in Him' (seen in search, not read in context here). The Concepts pillar records R. Tzadok (livelihood sorrow is self-made; trust removes it); not re-verified.

## S145 · Doubt and heresy · ספק / כפירה

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** ספקות · כפירה · עמלק · אפיקורסות

> Heresy is the unity of God denied in thought, and doubt is its cooling first step: the world imagined as able to stand without the word that makes it, and the self as able to stand apart from His oneness.

## Derivation

1. SH 2 names 'the root of the error' of those who deny providence: they picture God's making of heaven and earth like a smith's vessel that no longer needs the smith. The answer is creation from nothing, which needs 'the power of the maker in the made' always (SH 2:1-4; F01). Heresy is the craftsman model. (*stated*)
2. Any transgression is separation from His unity, 'as if denying His oneness' more than idolatry does (Tanya 24:6). So heresy is not one sin among many; it is the limit of what every sin is. Its root is regarding oneself as a thing apart (F08, Tanya 22). (*stated*)
3. Doubt is not the Jew's own. Doubts in faith are 'words of the spirit of the other side alone', which itself has no doubts and is only permitted to confuse; Israel are believers, children of believers (Tanya 29:24-26). (*stated*)
4. The Alter Rebbe's Purim maamar (as the Rebbe retells it from the Rayatz): Amalek equals 'safek' in gematria and cools, until one doubts 'whether the service is service' (Toras Menachem 5727). The Rebbe's order: coldness, then doubt, then 'iron' that cuts the bond in practice (Purim 5727). The tie of doubt to the unity is not stated in these lines. (**reading**)
5. R. Nachman divides heresy in two. One comes from outside wisdoms, from the breaking of the vessels; it holds sparks, and God can be found there. The other comes from the vacated space, where 'as it were' there is no Godliness; it has no answer and is crossed only by faith that He surrounds even that space (Likutei Moharan 64:2). (*stated*)
6. Here the schools come close and still differ. R. Nachman's second heresy comes from a space where 'as it were' there is no Godliness, yet he says in the same teaching that 'in very truth there is certainly Godliness there too, for nothing is without His life', and that the space is 'is and is not', not to be grasped until the future (Likutei Moharan 64:1:4-5). So he does not hold a space truly empty of God. The difference from the Alter Rebbe is in how the tzimtzum may be spoken of: for R. Nachman its emptiness is real from our side and no inquiry passes it, only faith; for the Alter Rebbe the tzimtzum is concealment only, and to take it literally is a serious error (SH 7, spine F03). That R. Nachman's wording falls on the literal side of the spine's F03 disagreement is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** SH 2 (the root error of the deniers of providence); Tanya 24 (sin as denial of His oneness); Tanya 29 (doubts are the other side's voice). His Purim 5547 maamar on Amalek as doubt is known here only as the Rebbe retells it.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass beyond the spine's note on his Sefer HaChakirah (Hayom Yom 28 Teves).
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not located as a separate teaching; the Rebbe notes that the Rashab said the maamarim 'Zachor' and 'Chayav inish' in 5679 on the basis of the Alter Rebbe's.
- **Rayatz.** Hayom Yom 11 Teves: living in an 'air of heresy' is a sick life; the air is purified by saying letters of Torah. He also retold the Alter Rebbe's Amalek maamar (Purim 5696).
- **The Rebbe.** Toras Menachem 5727 (Purim and Tazria): Amalek's sequence of coldness, doubt, iron; remember it daily at the Shema.
- **Other schools.** Breslov (Likutei Moharan 64): two heresies, sparks from the breaking versus the vacated space, where Godliness is absent only 'as it were' and 'in very truth' present (64:1:5); the tzaddik's niggun raises souls from the second. Piaseczno (Esh Kodesh 5702, Hanukkah 4): in the ghetto some whose faith was whole asked 'why have You abandoned us'; said as prayer it is good, held as a question it damages faith.

## S146 · Simplicity · תמימות ופשיטות

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** תמימות · תמים · פשיטות · תמים תהיה

> Simplicity is the unity of God in the soul's single point: the yechidah, which holds to Him without self-feeling and without needing to understand.

## Derivation

1. Before Him who is beyond intellect all are simple: 'I was brutish… as a beast before You, and I am always with You', and it is by being 'brutish' that one is always with Him (Tanya 18:11). Simplicity is the stance fitting to the One who is beyond knowledge. (*stated*)
2. The Mitteler Rebbe places true temimut in the soul: it is 'the yechidah shining in the heart's inner point', and its sign is feeling 'without any self-feeling at all' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 41:20-22; F06, F08). (*stated*)
3. The Besht opened the way for simple Jews: through the temimut of their Tehillim, even without knowing the words, and love of Israel, they come to inner closeness to God (Rayatz, Kuntres Toras HaChassidus, scan p. 5; spine G19). (*stated*)
4. In the last generations the Rashab asks for temimut and simple faith rather than following the intellect (Hayom Yom 12 Teves), and Hayom Yom says Moshiach will reveal the virtue of simple people's service (24 Iyar, 5 Teves; both entries name no speaker, and the compilation draws mostly on the Rayatz). (*stated*)
5. Why the simple point is highest: the yechidah is where every Jew is united with the Essence (spine F06, Inyanah 15), and the Essence shows most at the lowest (G05). That simplicity's hidden virtue is this is our reading. (**reading**)
6. Kept disagreement: Chabad demands comprehension (the Rayatz's 'to know', spine F12), while R. Nachman says 'wisdoms are not needed at all, only faith, temimut and simplicity' (Likutei Moharan II 78:7:5). The spine records the same tension inside Chabad (Simplicity or comprehension?). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 18:11: 'all are as simple before Him'; faith of women and the unlearned.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 39:12 and 41:17-31: false temimut (self-exaltation 'in the inner temimut unknown to him') against true temimut of the yechidah without self-feeling. The sharpest Chabad text on the subject.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass (Toras Shmuel has 12 lines with תמימות, not read).
- **Rashab.** Hayom Yom 12 Teves (5648, when appointed gabbai of the Chevra Kadisha): even men of intellect must set it aside and keep Torah in temimut and simple faith. Hayom Yom 4 Tishrei (a 'short explanation of my father', i.e. the Rashab): 'tamim tihyeh', teshuvah through temimut, the 'earnestness' of the heart.
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus (OCR; simple Jews and the Besht). Hayom Yom 5 Teves (no speaker named): Moshiach will show the virtue of temimut; understanding has a limit, faith is a feeling without limit.
- **The Rebbe.** Compiled the Hayom Yom entries; not located as a separate teaching in this pass.
- **Other schools.** Breslov (Likutei Moharan II 78:7): even a simple man who cannot learn receives life from Torah through the 'great simple man', the tzaddik; wisdoms mislead, simplicity leads to great joy. The Concepts pillar (14) records Radzin's definition (no order of one's own, carried like an infant); not re-verified.

## S147 · Despair and hope · יאוש ותקוה

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F11 Darkness turned to light · F06 The soul as part of the One **Search terms:** יאוש · ייאוש · תקוה · להתייאש

> Hope is the unity of God at the lowest point: since He is found even in Sheol, no place is outside Him, and so no fall is beyond return; despair is the forgetting of this.

## Derivation

1. Breslov states the ground of hope as the unity: even one who has fallen to the lowest Sheol must not despair, 'for even in the lowest Sheol God is found, and there too one can cleave to Him' ('if I make my bed in Sheol, You are there', Likutei Moharan 6:11:3, 6:14:1). (*stated*)
2. The very asking 'where is the place of His glory?' from the place of the fall is knowing that He is there, hidden; the kelipot live only from that hiding, and the search gives holy life in the place of the fall (Likutei Moharan II 12:2; F03). (*stated*)
3. 'There is no despair in the world at all', said with force for all generations; whoever strengthens himself in what he is still has hope (Likutei Moharan II 78:7:3), and one must begin again each time 'as if today he began' (Sichot HaRan 48:3). The ground given in LM II 78 is not God in Sheol but receiving life from Torah through the tzaddik, the 'great simple man' (78:7:1-2); that tie runs through the doctrine of the tzaddik, which the spine marks as thin. (*stated*)
4. In Chabad the same point is made through the soul: even 'the lightest of the light' has a portion of God within him, in exile, and freeing it is great joy (Tanya 31:9; F06). That this is the Chabad answer to despair is our reading; Tanya 31 speaks of bitterness, not despair. (**reading**)
5. The Maharash names despair as what makes sadness deadly: it is 'the threshold trodden by all sins, because it makes one despair entirely' (Toras Shmuel 5641). (*stated*)
6. Hope is itself redemptive: the Rebbe cites the Midrash that Israel 'are worthy of redemption in reward for the hope itself', as they left Egypt in the merit of trust (Likkutei Sichos 36, Shemos 1, 6:6). The words are the Midrash's; the application to this exile is the Rebbe's. That hope at the lowest point is F11, darkness to light, is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Not located under the words yiush and tikvah in this pass; Tanya 31:9 (the portion of God even in the lowest) is the nearest, and its use here is ours.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** 'Think good' (S144) is the nearest; not located under these terms.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5641: sadness leads to despair and despair is the source of all sins.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass. (Hayom Yom 16 Sivan, on 'hope and complete trust' in healing the soul, names no speaker and is not assigned to him here.)
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Likkutei Sichos 36, Shemos 1: cites the Midrash that redemption comes 'in the reward of the hope itself'.
- **Other schools.** Breslov owns this subject: Likutei Moharan 6 (the two expertises, running and returning; God is in Sheol), II 12 (asking 'where?' is already finding), II 78 ('no despair in the world'), Sichot HaRan 48 (begin anew many times a day; the Besht's 'I love God without the World to Come'). The explanatory section of Likutei Moharan 6 cited here speaks of 'Rabbeinu' in the third person and appears to be R. Nosson's.

> — The Maharash: sadness is the threshold for all sins because it makes a person despair entirely; bitterness instead rises to be included in the unity.

> **Likutei Moharan, Part II 12:2:3** (R. Nachman of Breslov, Likutei Moharan; line 6239)
> אבל תכף כשמבקשין איה מקום כבודו, נמצא שיודעין עלכלפנים שיש אלוק יתברך, רק שהוא נסתר ונעלם
> — Asking 'where is the place of His glory?' from the place of the fall is already knowing He is there, hidden (F03); the kelipot live only from the hiding.

> **Sichot HaRan 48:3** (R. Nachman of Breslov, Sichot HaRan; line 260 · **reading**)
> ויתחיל בכל פעם מחדש, כאלו היום התחיל להתקרב להשם יתברך
> — Hope as beginning again each time. That this mirrors the world renewed every moment (F01) is our reading.

> **Likkutei Sichos vol. 36, Shemos 1, 6:6** (Lubavitcher Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos; line 25015)
> כדאי הן לגאולה בשכר הקיווי (עצמו)
> — The Rebbe cites a Midrash ('as it is in the Midrash'): Israel are worthy of redemption for the hope itself, as they were redeemed from Egypt in the merit of trust. The wording is the Midrash's; the Rebbe applies it to the last exile.

## Thin or forced?

Partly thin. The stated tie is Breslov's (God is found in Sheol). In Chabad the words yiush and tikvah rarely carry the argument; the Chabad tie through the soul's portion of God is our reading.

## S148 · Death · מיתה / הסתלקות

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F05 The Essence beyond both · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** מיתה · הסתלקות · מלאך המות · פטירה

> Death is the unity of God met at the edge of the vessel: the soul, whose life was faith and love of the One, freed from place; for the tzaddik, a revelation of light above the worlds; and never a goal in itself.

## Derivation

1. The one death the Tanya asks of a Jew is to die rather than deny the One God; even sinners do it 'without reason', because the One God shines in the soul's chochmah (Tanya 18:12-14; F06). (*stated*)
2. What dies is only the vessel. 'The life of the tzaddik is not the life of flesh but spiritual life: faith, awe and love' (IH 27:11). After his passing his spirit is no longer in a vessel or a physical place, so those close to him can receive it more, and he is 'found in all the worlds more than in his life', this world included (IH 27:9-16). (*stated*)
3. The Rebbe names it: 'histalkus' is the revelation of a light 'not in the category of worlds at all', and 'therefore the passing of tzaddikim is called histalkus' (Basi LeGani 5711; F05). (*stated*)
4. In one of the maamar's two readings, at death the soul 'becomes a goer', because the unlimited that Torah and mitzvot drew into it below is now revealed (the Rebbe on a Mitteler Rebbe maamar, 10 Kislev 5746, sections 5 and 7; the first reading places the 'going' in the soul's life below). (*stated*)
5. But dying into Him is not the aim. Nadav and Avihu died of 'running without returning'; 'running without return is death'. The first tzimtzum's intent is revelation below (Hayom Yom 27 Adar II, the Rashab via the Rayatz). That this is the higher unity sought without the lower (F02, F03) is our naming; the text says 'running without return is death' and 'the first tzimtzum's intent is the revelation'. (*stated*)
6. At the extreme, bitterness itself can take the soul: R. Elazar ben Durdaya's soul left him weeping, a cry that reached the yechidah (Mitteler Rebbe, Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 41:26-27). The text holds it up as unmixed truth, not as a path. (*stated*)
7. Death as such, the ordinary end, the angel of death, is not tied to the unity in what was read. The ties run through martyrdom, the tzaddik's histalkus and the warning against ratzo without shov. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 18 and 24-25 (martyrdom for the unity; even a bow to idolatry without faith is refused); IH 27-28 (the tzaddik's life and passing; his students receive his spirit).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 41:27 (R. Elazar ben Durdaya). The Rebbe's 5746 maamar builds on a Mitteler Rebbe maamar on 'haolam dein'.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass (Toras Shmuel has many lines with מיתה, not read).
- **Rashab.** Hayom Yom 27 Adar II (via the Rayatz): ratzo without shov is death; the tzimtzum's intent is revelation; the entrance is bitul, 'doing what Chassidus calls for, not what the intellect says'. The Rebbe (Toras Menachem 5727) also records the Rashab on the tzaddik's influence after passing: from above downward, first on the yechidah.
- **Rayatz.** His own histalkus is the occasion of the Rebbe's Basi LeGani; his maamar Basi LeGani 5710 is its base.
- **The Rebbe.** Basi LeGani 5711 (histalkus as revelation above the worlds; the passing of tzaddikim harder than the destruction of the Temple, so that His glory be exalted in all worlds); 10 Kislev 5746 (the soul becomes a 'goer' at death; resurrection reveals the Essence in the body).
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov (Keter Shem Tov 1:39): 'I shall not die but live', read as not needing to descend again once the trial is passed. Piaseczno (Esh Kodesh 5702, Hanukkah 7): R. Akiva's students asked 'our master, until here?' fearing for their faith at his bitter death; he answered from 'all my days I was pained'. Breslov was not checked for this subject in this pass.

## Passages

> **Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 18:12** (Alter Rebbe, Tanya; line 260)
> ולכן, אפילו קל שבקלים ופושעי ישראל מוסרים נפשם על קדושת ה׳, על הרוב, וסובלים עינוים קשים שלא לכפור בה׳ אחד
> — The one death the Tanya asks for: to die rather than deny the One God, drawn from the soul's chochmah where He shines (F06).

> **Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 27:11** (Alter Rebbe, Tanya; line 1427)
> כנודע, שחיי הצדיק אינם חיים בשרים כי אם חיים רוחניים, שהם אמונה ויראה ואהבה
> — Life is defined by faith, fear and love, not flesh; so death removes only the vessel.

> **Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 27:14** (Alter Rebbe, Tanya; line 1430)
> יכול כל הקרוב אליו לקבל חלק מבחינת רוחו שבגן עדן, הואיל ואינה בתוך כלי ולא בבחינת מקום גשמי
> — After passing, the tzaddik's spirit is no longer bounded by a vessel or physical place, so he is 'found more' even in this world.

> **Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, vol. 2, Basi LeGani, 10 Shevat 5711:10** (Lubavitcher Rebbe, Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat; line 1255)
> ולכן גם פטירת צדיקים נק' בשם הסתלקות, כי הסתלקות הוא גילוי אור נעלה ביותר
> — The Rebbe: 'histalkus' means the revelation of a light 'not in the category of worlds at all' (F05); therefore the passing of tzaddikim is called histalkus.

> **Hayom Yom, 27 Adar II** (Hayom Yom (compiled by the Rebbe from the Rayatz, 1942); line 193)
> ורצוא בלי שוב, היא מיתה
> — The Rashab (via the Rayatz): the sons of Aharon died of running to God without return; the first tzimtzum's intent is revelation. Calling this the higher unity without the lower (F02, F03) is our naming.

> **Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, vol. 2, Vayetze, 10 Kislev 5746:12** (Lubavitcher Rebbe, Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat; line 791)
> שבשעת פטירתו של אדם, כשהנשמה נפטרת מהגוף ומעולם הזה, דאזלינן מיני', אזי נעשה בה הענין דאזלינן, שהיא נעשית בבחינת מהלך
> — The Rebbe (on a Mitteler Rebbe maamar), in the second of two readings: at death the soul becomes a 'goer', because Torah and mitzvot done below had drawn the unlimited into it; now it is revealed.

## Thin or forced?

Partly thin. Stated for martyrdom, for the tzaddik's histalkus and for 'ratzo without shov is death'. For death in general (mortality, the angel of death, burial) no verified passage ties it to the unity; do not force it.

## S149 · The afterlife: reward and punishment · גן עדן וגיהנם / שכר ועונש

**Faces:** F07 The home below · F04 Filling and surrounding · F05 The Essence beyond both · F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual **Search terms:** גן עדן · גיהנם · שכר ועונש · עולם הבא

> Reward and punishment are the unity of God as its revelation is measured: Gan Eden is a graded radiance, Gehinnom a cleansing, and the true reward is the Essence revealed in this physical world, which the mitzvah already is.

## Derivation

1. The World to Come is enjoying 'the radiance of the Shechinah', the delight of comprehension, and no creature grasps more than a radiance. But when a person grasps Torah and mitzvot he grasps the Holy One Himself. Hence 'one hour of teshuvah and good deeds in this world is better than all the life of the World to Come' (Tanya 4:12; F10). (*stated*)
2. Gan Eden is limited by definition: souls there enjoy Him through chochmah-binah-da'at, and could not receive the infinite without being nullified from existence (Tanya 48:2). Its revelation is 'filling all worlds', graded to each (IH 17:5; F04). (*stated*)
3. The ultimate reward is the resurrection, when surrounding light is revealed without measure in a refined body, 'the end embedded in the beginning' (IH 17:6-8; F04, G05). And the fullness of the days of Moshiach and the resurrection is 'the revelation of Or Ein Sof in this physical world', which depends on our deeds now (Tanya 37:1; F07). (*stated*)
4. So the reward is not other than the deed: 'what causes the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah itself', since doing it draws Or Ein Sof into physical things (Tanya 37:2); the Alter Rebbe in Liozna: 'the mitzvah in its very essence is the reward, and the revelation of the Essence will be in the future' (Hayom Yom 25 Iyar). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe completes it: at the resurrection 'the drawing of the Essence through the mitzvot in this world will be revealed'; now it is received only by the soul leaving the body, then in the body itself (10 Kislev 5746; F05). (*stated*)
6. Punishment is cleansing: Gehinnom scours the soul, and light suffering in this world, where 'the world is built on kindness', saves it from that scouring (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 12:1-2). The Rebbe says of the Torah's punishments generally that they are His kindness, to cleanse (Likkutei Sichos 36, 3:5, within his first explanation of trust). Naming this F03, chesed in the form of gevurah, is ours. (*stated*)
7. At the top, reward itself is set aside for the Essence: the Alter Rebbe, 'I do not want Your Gan Eden, I do not want Your World to Come, I want only You Yourself' (Toras Menachem 5713). The Besht, fearing he had lost the World to Come: 'I love God without the World to Come' (Sichot HaRan 48). The parallel is ours; the Breslov telling serves hope, the Chabad telling serves the Essence beyond every radiance. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 4 (World to Come as radiance; this world grasps Him); Tanya 37 (reward is the mitzvah; the purpose is revelation in this physical world); Tanya 48 (Gan Eden limited); SH 5:3 (the reward of tzaddikim in Gan Eden is comprehension of the expansion of chesed and gevurah, the 'secret of the 22 letters'); IH 17 (Gan Eden memale, resurrection sovev); IH 27 (the air of Gan Eden and of Gehinnom around every person); Iggeret HaTeshuvah 12 (suffering here instead of Gehinnom); Hayom Yom 25 Iyar; 'not Your Gan Eden' (Toras Menachem 5713).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** The Rebbe (Basi LeGani 5711) cites him: lights added in Atzilut by mitzvot are there 'as if placed in a box', for the sake of the lower world. Not read in the Maharash's own text here.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Not located as a separate teaching in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** 10 Kislev 5746: the three 'hilula' unions, the highest being soul and body at the resurrection, when the Essence is revealed. Toras Menachem 5713: the Alter Rebbe's 'only You', and that it obliges his followers in practice: to leave one's own existence and limits.
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov via Breslov (Sichot HaRan 48:7): love of God without the World to Come, told as the model for serving 'without receiving reward' even when one fears Gehinnom (48:5-6). Piaseczno (Esh Kodesh 5702, Hanukkah 5): souls judged in Gehinnom rise purified; one whose faith is damaged is like a soul that added sin in Gehinnom. The difference: Chabad's highest reward is revelation in the physical world; Breslov here stresses service without reward.
