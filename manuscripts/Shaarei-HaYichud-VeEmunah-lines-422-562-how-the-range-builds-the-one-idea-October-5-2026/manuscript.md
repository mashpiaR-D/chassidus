## The shape of the range

The whole range is one argument, and it is almost all *stated*. That is why so few lines are structural and only one is *none*. R. Aharon keeps asking one question: how can God be described at all? His answer runs through every chapter. There are two sides, "relative to us" (לגבי דידן) and "relative to Him" (לגביה). Whatever divides, hides, contracts or is "known" is on our side. With Him "all is in total equality" (בהשואה גמורה). By the end he has three measures: the worlds, the root of the worlds in His power (Adam Kadmon), and Ein Sof Himself.

That is why F05 (the Essence beyond both) leads the count by far. F02 comes second, because nearly every line sets the lower unity (ours) against the higher (His). F03 comes third, because the contraction is always "of the light, not of the Essence." F12 and F08 rise at the end, where the teaching turns into service: first unite the worlds through Da'at, then be nullified above Da'at.

F01 appears mostly as providence over every detail. F10 appears only as "Shema, prayer, Torah and mitzvot" that unite the worlds. F06 and F11 are almost absent.

Two words carry the range. **השואה** ("equality") is his name for the higher unity: all levels in His Essence without distinction. **פלא** ("wonder") is his name for how two opposites hold in one subject: Keter, the power that joins what cannot be joined.

## Ch. 23:5-24:7 (422-431): Malchut, Da'at and counting to ten

Line 422 sets the range's first principle. Malchut, whose task is to hide, is not another essence: > שבחינת המקבל הוא גם כן עצמותו ברוך הוא (line 422)

The hiding power is one of His own middot. Only "relative to us" is it called lack (line 423, F03 + F02).

Chapter 24 defines Da'at (line 426). Da'at is not a content of its own. It is the felt knowing that binds mind and traits to the soul. Its root is the soul's will, the "wonder" that joins soul and body though they have no likeness. This definition holds up everything that follows. The same wonder above is Keter: > ושרשם הוא הכתר שהוא כח הפלא המייחדם ומקשרם (line 427)

Line 428 is the chapter's center. Da'at above is the felt revelation of Godliness that brings creatures into being (F01), and it does not change Him (F05). Whether it is revealed depends on the deeds below (F10): > כי כאשר מייחדים העולמות על ידי קריאת שמע ותפלה ותורה ומצות אזי נגלה יחודו (line 428)

When the worlds are not united, only nature shows: אבל אינם נגלים כי אם החיצוניות שהוא הטבע (line 428). Da'at is counted among the sefirot from our side. From His side Keter is counted instead, because feeling and binding need two essences: > אבל באין סוף יתברך הרי אין דבר זולתו (line 429)

"In general they are all one" (line 431). Lines 424-425 are the counting puzzle itself and are marked structural.

## Ch. 25 (432-435): exile as hidden unity

In Egypt Ze'ir Anpin was "in gestation," without Da'at. The worlds showed that He creates them, but not His unity: > כי במצרים לא היה התגלות בחינת יחודו יתברך בעולמות (line 432)

Line 433 names the purpose of all worlds in this book's own words (F07): > ולגלות יחודו יתברך בעולמות (line 433)

Line 433 gives the parable of a man who hides his aim until he acts on it. The exodus is the aim revealed. Reading this as F11 is ours (433.3, *reading*). Line 434 makes the turn depend on Israel's own bitul, an awakening "in nullifying the yesh and the worlds to unite them to Him" (F08).

## Ch. 26 (436-440): "He is not a body" means the worlds are not separate

This chapter is the range's clearest statement of the unity. Da'at, body and vessels are ours. His knowing is not like a soul's knowing of its body: > כי מאחר שבאמת אין זולתו ואין דבר שחוץ ממנו (line 436)

How He "knows" and yet is unchanged "is not in the power of intellect to recognise" (line 436, F09 + F12). Line 437 gives the Rambam's knower, known and knowledge. Line 439 then sets a trap for "those lacking knowledge," who say He is not a body but the worlds are. That view "cannot escape two sides." Separate worlds would divide Him, and worlds He spreads into would change Him. The answer is stated as faith: > והוא יתברך והעולמות הכל אחד (line 439)

And yet He is unchanged, "as before creation, so after it." Line 440 goes further: > אבל לגביה אין עולם כלל (line 440)

Even the hiding is His power, and in it "He and His life are one" (איהו וחיוהי וגרמוהי חד בהון, line 440). How this is so ends in "you have no business with hidden things."

## Ch. 27 (441-449): light as a garment; Atzilut and "before Atzilut"

Even light cannot be said of Him, because all revelation is to another: > ומאחר שאין זולתו יתברך איך יתכנה אליו גילוי (line 441)

So the light is itself created from nothing, "not by way of hishtalshelut" (line 442, F05 + F01). Line 443 is F09 stated. The light is like a garment, foreign to Him, and yet "He and His Name are one": > והוא מדברים הנסתרים איך יהיו שני הפכים בנושא אחד (line 443)

The gloss in line 443 reads the Arizal's disciples' levels "before the tzimtzum" as a way of denying Him even light. Line 444 defines the two terms. His unity in the light, as it is for us, is Atzilut. His simplicity as it is for Him is "before Atzilut." Line 447 reaches the high point of the Gate. Before Atzilut the levels are so equal in Him that not even "unity" applies, since unity means two: > ואפילו בחינת יחיד אין לתארו (line 447)

It also gives F04 in the Raaya Mehemna's words, אנת הוא מלגו כל עלמין ומלבר כל עלמין (line 447), and it forbids inquiry: "run back to your place." Line 448 closes the loop: נמצא שבאמת אינו גוף אפילו מצד העולמות.

## Ch. 28 (450-462): the warning, the purpose, and Keter in two halves

Line 450 guards against the opposite error. One must not think the light and the sefirot exist only for us: > אין לך בחינה קטנה שבקטנות שלא נעשה בכוונה מאתו יתברך ובהמשכת עצמותו ברוך הוא בהם דייקא (line 450)

Line 451 states that even the light is His Essence, and it names the purpose (F07): > כל תכלית כונת בריאת העולמות למהוי עילא ותתא שוין (line 451)

Line 453 reads "Israel rose first in thought" as the power of revelation itself (F06 + F07). All contractions pertain to revelation, not to Him: ולכן כל הצמצומים אין שייך אלא מצד הגילוי (line 453). Line 454 explains why the revelation stops at the union of Atzilut. Full equality would nullify the creatures, and the aim is revelation: > כי אם יהיה התאחדותם והתכללותם בהשוואה גמורה הרי יהיו בטלים לגמרי (line 454)

Line 457 reads "I Havayah have not changed": "I" is the Essence and Havayah is Atzilut, and כי אצלו שני ההמשכות הם בהשוואה גמורה. Lines 458-462 split Keter into Atik (the Emanator's side) and Keter (the emanated side). The wonder is what makes the two one: והשני בחינות המה בחינה אחת שהוא פלא להשוות שני הבחינות (line 458). Line 458 also gives providence in every detail (F01): המורים על גילוי עצמותו בכל פרט דייקא.

## Ch. 29-31 (463-485): Atik, Arich, Ze'ir; the borrowed name "Adam Kadmon"

Here the book maps its doctrine onto the partzufim. Much of it is structural: the parables of father and son (467, 472-473) and of man's many parts (478), and the naming (470, 475, 477). The stated points are these.

- Atik is "the head that is no head." The Essence cannot even be called head: אבל עצמותו ברוך הוא לא נודע מהותו כלל ואין מהות אחר זולתו (line 464). - Ze'ir Anpin is the father contracting his mind to the child (line 467). Here the contraction is set up as love, and it is kept structural. - Arich Anpin is the one place where the book speaks the SPINE's own precision: > ואין לכנות אל בחינה זו אפילו אחד המורה על התאחדות שני מהותים כי אם תואר יחיד (line 469)

Its providence is "an open eye that does not sleep" (line 469, F01). - In His Essence the levels are one, beyond even "inclusion" and "pleasure": עד שאין נופל לייחס עליהם אפילו לשון התאחדות והתכללות אפילו בבחינת תענוג (line 476). - "Adam Kadmon" is a borrowed name (lines 480-482). Wisdom and middot are named in the source from their effect, but Da'at is never borrowed, not even as a loan (line 482). - The tzimtzum is described only so that the levels can be revealed: מוכרחים אנו לתאר צמצום (line 483). The revelation is "the least of the least" of His power, the kav (line 485). And "all is one power alone" (line 485).

## Ch. 32 (486-494): two measures, and the service they ask for

Line 486 states the method outright: > שצריכין אנו להעריך באין סוף ברוך הוא שני בחינות (line 486)

From the worlds' side, nothing exists "not made by His intention and His drawing into it in actual fact" (line 486). From His side one may not even say "He is one in them." Line 487 then gives the lower unity as our work (F10): > וזהו עבודתינו לייחדם בקריאת שמע ותפלה (line 487)

The higher measure is beyond our reach. That is why the sages say "cleave to His attributes" (line 487). Line 488 is the range's strongest F08 + F12. All our binding is still to Ein Sof Himself, "to give over the soul in unity" (ולמסור נפשו ביחוד), and: > צריכין לידע יותר שמהותו לא נודע וליבטל אליו בבחינת דלא אתיידע (line 488)

This is "inwardness": stripping the garments of the partzufim. The more one strips them, the more the unity shows, "from Asiyah to Ein Sof" (line 489). Line 491 marks where understanding stops: > כי היחוד על ידי השכלה הרי עדיין יש מי שמשכיל ביטולו (line 491)

Line 493 names the wonder, "with Him yesh and ayin are equal and come forth together," and then withdraws even the word "wonder." Line 494 maps the morning prayer onto the ladder. The section before Baruch She'amar is the bitul of Asiyah, and the Amidah is the bitul of Atzilut.

## Ch. 33 (495-506): inner and surrounding reversed

From below, Ein Sof is the innermost: עד שנשכיל שהכל הוא אין סוף לבדו (line 495). From above, it is the reverse. Asiyah is innermost, and He surrounds all (line 495). That is the Etz Chaim's circles (line 496). Lines 497-499 give the parable of a sage who contracts his wisdom; his hidden remainder "surrounds" what the student gets. The stated result: > וכאשר תסתכל תבין שהפנימי והמקיף הוא בהשואה גמורה הוא אין סוף ברוך הוא לבדו (line 502)

Line 505 reads "I am first and I am last" as inner and surrounding, and says הן מצד הפנימי הן מצד המקיף הכל הוא אין סוף. Line 506 gives the definition: "what we cannot grasp is called surrounding."

## Ch. 34 (507-512): even the contractions are Him; bitul is equal for all

> ובאמצעיתא אפילו הצמצומים הוא אין סוף ברוך הוא לבדו (line 507)

The revelation depends on the awakening from below: וזה תלוי באתערותא דלתתא (line 508). The Names in the Torah follow the receivers' grasp, but לית ליה שם ידיעא (line 509). Line 510 makes a point the Tanya range does not make in this form. Grasp differs from person to person, but bitul does not: prince, servant and commoner are nullified to the king alike. > אבל בביטולם כולם שוים שבטלים לעצמותו ברוך הוא (line 510)

Line 511 places Moshe's grasp in Beriah, "where there is full bitul of yesh into ayin." No man ever grasped Atzilut. Line 512 limits our awakening to uniting Ze'ir and Nukva.

## Ch. 35 (513-521): the body without a soul; tikkun; the wall's other side

The worlds as they appear are "a body without a soul" (ונראים כגופא בלא נשמתא, line 513). Uniting them means: > שרואין בכל הנפעלים גילוי אורו יתברך בהם בלי שום שינוי (line 513)

Da'at is then the feeling שנהיה מרגישים אור עצמותו יתברך המתמשך בהעולמות (line 514). Line 515 explains how a unity made below reaches what the lower cannot reach. His intention is that upper and lower be equal, so: > ולכן כאשר התתאין מבטלין העולמות ומייחדים אליו בערכינו ממילא נתעלה יחוד הזה עד בחינת ההשתוות אשר בכח האין סוף ברוך הוא (line 515)

Lines 516-517 give the teacher-and-student parable: the teacher, delighted, raises himself. Line 518 describes the contraction in two steps, first to make room for "yesh" and then to hide the equality in vessels. Line 519 defines tikkun as a likeness of His equality drawn into the vessels "through pleasure." Line 520 restates the service: זהו עבודתינו לייחד היש והגבול בבחינת העולמות אליו יתברך.

## Ch. 36-37 (522-536): "like the appearance of a man"; borrowing; the void; the wall

The four worlds are a person: Asiyah the body, Yetzirah the traits, Beriah the mind, and Atzilut the life that vivifies all (lines 522-523): > נמצא כד תסתכל אשר בכל העולמות לא נמצא רק כחו יתברך לבדו (line 524)

Atzilut is "Adam of Asiyah" because אבל בלי העשיה אין מקום כלל להתגלות כחו יתברך (line 526, F07). Lines 527-534 set rules for how far speech may go. Parable is allowed for Atzilut. Borrowed names are allowed up to Malchut of Adam Kadmon, but not inquiry. Above Keter of Adam Kadmon not even borrowing is allowed: כי אם עצמות המאציל ברוך הוא בזה אין לנו עסק כלל (line 533). Line 531 adds the soul's side: its own powers show only as the body's vessels are nullified, which is what attaining nefesh, ruach and neshamah means (F08 + F06).

Line 535 reads the Etz Chaim's "void" as distance, not absence, like the space between heaven and earth: הבחינת חלל כביכול המפסיק בינו ובין העולמות. Line 536 is the practical close of the section: > ולכן כל עבודתינו להסיר הכותל אפילו לגבי דידן על ידי היחוד ומסירות נפש בקריאת שמע ותפלה ותורה ומצות (line 536)

The wall is "our" wall, which is the contractions.

## Ch. 38-39 (537-552): three measures; the Idra Rabba

The three measures are the worlds as "a man" (537), their root in His power as Adam Kadmon, which is man only by borrowing (541-543), and Ein Sof Himself, where not even borrowing applies (544). Line 538 contrasts man, who changes and grows, with the Creator, who does not, שאין דבר חוץ ממנו. Line 539 makes all change in the world "from our side," like a potter's wheel. Line 545 grounds the image of man in His perfection, which includes upper and lower. Line 546 reads breaking and repair: the breaking reveals the "yesh," and the repair binds it back: > והתיקון הוא לקשרם ולייחדם ביחודו יתברך בכדי לגלות שלימותו (line 546)

Line 548 quotes the Idra's "all is in one balance," then states לגביה יתברך הוא זעיר אנפין הוא עתיקא בלי פירודא. Line 549 is G14 stated: > ובאמת הוי"ה הוא אלקי"ם שהכל הוא כח אחד (line 549)

Lines 550-551 read "He blew into his nostrils the breath of life." The repairs exist so that Ein Sof Himself is drawn "from level to level to the end of all levels." Line 552 is an editorial note (*none*).

## Ch. 40 (553-562): the conclusion

> כי אם שמריש כל דרגין ועד סוף כל דרגין הכל הוא עצמותו לבדו (line 553)

The repairs exist only to reveal אשר אין עוד מלבדו (line 554). When they succeed, the revealed things return to their source: > ולא נגלה כי אם עצמותו לבדו ברוך הוא כמו שהוא לבדו קודם בריאה (line 555)

That revelation "is made through the service of the lower ones" (line 556). The order of service runs from below up. First comes unity through Da'at, and then bitul above it (line 557). Line 558 reconciles "cleave to His attributes" with "to Him and not to His attributes" as two stages. The first is F12 in the SPINE's own verse, ועל זה נאמר וידעת היום והשבות אל לבבך. The second is להבטל למעלה מן הדעת ולהבין שהכל הוא לבדו יתברך. Line 559 cites the Alter Rebbe's gloss (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 9:6 in the Liozna copy, line 832): knower, known and knowledge apply only in Atzilut, not before it. Line 562 closes with a warning. These rules are: > כולם מקובלים פה אל פה בלי סברת שכל אנושי (line 562)

## What this book adds to the Tanya, or says differently

1. **A method, not only a doctrine.** Tanya's Shaar HaYichud gives the higher unity (SH 3) and the lower unity (SH 7) as two teachings. Strashelye turns them into a rule for reading every statement about God: "relative to us" and "relative to Him" (lines 423, 428, 440, 486). He then adds a third measure between them, the root of the worlds in His power (lines 537-544).
2. **Past 'yachid'.** The SPINE's precision is that the unity is 'yachid', not 'echad'. Strashelye says exactly that of Arich Anpin (line 469). He then goes further. Of the Essence even 'yachid' is said only from our side (line 447), and not even "inclusion" or "unity" applies (lines 447, 476, 486). The SPINE's precision holds here, but for him it describes a level below the Essence.
3. **A guard on both sides.** The Tanya refutes the literal tzimtzum (SH 7, Tanya lines 837-838). Strashelye agrees that the contraction is "of the light, not the Essence" (lines 440, 453, 483). He also warns against the opposite error, that light and sefirot are only our perception: "no aspect so small that it was not made with intention... by the drawing of His Essence" (line 450). And he adds that the light and the contractions themselves are His Essence (lines 451, 507).
4. **Inner and surrounding reversed.** Tanya 48 says sovev is not spatial but "unknown." Strashelye agrees (line 506), and he adds that the order flips with the direction of looking. Seen from below, Ein Sof is the innermost; seen from above, Asiyah is the innermost and He surrounds it (lines 495, 501-505).
5. **Bitul above Da'at as the main thing.** The Tanya's F12 is Da'at as binding (ch. 42), with faith taking over where grasp ends (SH 7). Strashelye makes Da'at the first stage and names bitul above it as "the main bitul" (line 558), because understanding still leaves "someone who understands his bitul" (line 491). He also says bitul is equal for all, though grasp is not (line 510).
6. **The limit of the awakening from below.** Our awakening reaches only to uniting Ze'ir and Nukva (lines 512, 520). What lies beyond rises "of itself" (ממילא), because His intention is that upper and lower be equal (line 515).
7. **Rules for speech.** Strashelye grades how far language may go: parable for Atzilut, borrowed names up to Malchut of Adam Kadmon, and silence above Keter of Adam Kadmon (lines 526, 533, 544, 562).

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- One line is *none*: line 552, an editorial pointer to the Idra. - The partzuf mechanics are structural: the counting puzzle (424-425), the parables of father, son and man (467, 472-473, 478), the naming (470, 475, 477), and the borrowing rules for the soul (528-530).
- *Reading* is used twice. At 433.3 the exodus is read as F11. At 503.2 "He looked into the Torah" and the souls standing before Him are read as F10 and F06. In both places the text uses the sayings for a different point. - F10 here means only "Shema, prayer, Torah and mitzvot unite the worlds" (lines 428, 487, 536). The range never ties a particular mitzvah or ruling to the unity, as Tanya 40 and 53 do. - F11 is effectively absent. "Breaking and repair" (line 546) is about revealing and re-binding the "yesh," not about turning darkness to light, so it is not marked F11. - F01 appears as providence over detail (lines 446, 458, 469, 486), not as "Your word stands" or continuous speech. The Besht's formula does not appear in this range. - Line 537 lists "Yetzirah and Asiyah" as His traits and intellect. The context (lines 523-524) calls for Yetzirah and Beriah, so this is probably a slip in the text. It is left as printed.

## Disagreements with the Tanya, kept

- **The soul-body analogy.** The Tanya refuses it at the root for the Essence (SH 7, Tanya line 821). Strashelye uses it constantly for Atzilut ("like the appearance of a man, and not a man," lines 538, 540) and refuses it only for the Essence (lines 436, 438, 507, 542). This is a difference in how much the analogy may be used, not a contradiction. But Strashelye's line 513, that soul and body are joined by His wonder "because before Him they are one essence," goes further than the Tanya range does.
- **Where the lower's effort reaches.** Tanya 41 has the person "draw His light" onto himself by the mitzvah. Strashelye says the awakening cannot reach "before Atzilut" at all (line 515). What reaches it is His own intention, working through the unity made below. The two are compatible, but they place the initiative differently.
- **How much is known.** Both authors hold that the Essence is known only by faith (Tanya line 834; Strashelye lines 439, 447, 562). Strashelye states this more sharply, as a ban on inquiry ("run back to your place," "it is forbidden to ask," lines 447, 464). He ends by grounding his rules in tradition "without human reasoning" (line 562). That sits with the Alter Rebbe's "we have no business with hidden things," not with the Rayatz's demand to toil to understand (SPINE F12). Strashelye also asks for understanding along the way ("one who reflects will find them reasonable," line 562).
- **The purpose of the worlds.** The Tanya's F07 is "a dwelling below." Strashelye's is "that upper and lower be equal" and that His perfection be revealed (lines 451, 496, 515, 546). The idea is the same, but the emphasis is on equality revealed, not on the home.
