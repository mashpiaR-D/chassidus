This is the last part of Derekh Chayyim: chapter 5 of Pirkei Avot ("with ten sayings the world was made") and chapter 6, the added chapter on acquiring Torah. It holds the Maharal's longest direct argument about G-d's attributes, aimed at the Rambam and the philosophers. It also holds his teaching on "a dispute for the sake of Heaven," where G-d is the One who unites opposites.

Six teachings matter most for the guide:

- **He is the One who unites opposites.** "ומה שהוא סבה לשני הפכים דבר זה בעצמו אחדותו יתברך" (1288): "that He is the cause of two opposites is itself His oneness." That is why a dispute for Heaven lasts.
- **The One is not a number.** "אשר האחד אינו מספר, ואינו נכנס בכלל מספר" (1297): "the One is not a number and does not enter into number." And "כי האחדות הוא הקיום בעצמו" (1299): "oneness is lasting itself."
- **Toil and time are from the receiver's side.** He made the world with no toil and no time; "the toil and effort are from the side of the receiver" (1077). The ten sayings, not one, are for the world's sake.
- **No wall between the worlds.** "כי העולם הטבעי הזה קשור עם עולם הנבדל" (1163): "this natural world is tied to the separate world," "ולא נעשה מחיצה ביניהם" (1163): "and no wall was made between them."
- **Against the Rambam on His knowing.** His knowing is not His essence; calling His essence "mind" gives Him a definition, "and He is not defined at all" (1171). He is "simple with the utmost simplicity, and so He holds all" (1172).
- **He alone is One, and beings have nothing of their own.** "ואין לנמצאים מצד עצמם דבר, כי אם מה שמשפיע להם השם יתברך, ובזה השם יתברך אחד ואין זולתו" (1570): "beings have nothing from their own side, only what G-d pours to them, and by this G-d is One and there is none besides Him."

## 2.1 Ten sayings: one from the Maker, many from the receiver

Why ten sayings, when one would do? The ten show "והשם יתברך שכינתו בעולם הזה" (1069): "that G-d has His Presence in this world." The Maharal reads "for with Yah (yud-heh) G-d formed the worlds": this world was made with heh. The heh is a dalet (length and width, the spread of matter) with a yud inside it. "ואין היו"ד נוגע, כי אין הדבר שהוא נבדל מעורב עם הגשמי" (1071): "the yud does not touch, for what is separate does not mix with the physical."

The world must be one, because "אי אפשר שלא יהיה פעולתו גם כן אחת, כי כל פעולה מתדמה אל הפועל" (1072): "His work must also be one, for every work resembles its maker." But "כי אין אחד רק השם יתברך" (1072): "none is one but G-d." So the world is one and many at once: nine sayings for its many parts, and the tenth, "In the beginning," for the Maker's side, "שהוא אחד כולל הכל" (1073): "who is One and holds all."

From His side one saying would do. The ten are for the receiver: "לא היה עמל ויגיעה מצד השם יתברך, אבל העמל והיגיעה הוא מצד המקבל" (1077): "there was no toil or effort from G-d's side; the toil and effort are from the side of the receiver." "כי הוא יתברך יכול לפעול הכל בלא המשך זמן כלל" (1077): "for He can make all with no stretch of time at all." Even the rest of the seventh day is for the receiver.

## 2.2 No wall between the worlds

The Rambam read the "ten things made at twilight" as a condition G-d set at creation, so His will would not seem to change later. The Maharal says the sages did not fear that. The condition means "כי העולם הטבעי הזה קשור עם עולם הנבדל" (1163): "this natural world is tied to the separate world," "ולא נעשה מחיצה ביניהם" (1163): "and no wall was made between them." He did not leave this world fully in the hands of nature.

## 2.3 Against the Rambam on His knowing and His attributes (1164-1175)

This is the Maharal's fullest statement on the attributes. In order:

1. **Knowing is His act, not His essence** (1164). The philosophers said His knowledge is His essence, so it cannot change, so He cannot know changing things as they change. The Maharal: knowing is a deed of His ("and G-d knew," Ex. 2:25). Like the soul, which knows but whose knowing is not its essence (1165).
2. **This makes no many in Him.** The change is not in Him: "השנוי הזה הוא מצד המקבל" (1166): "this change is from the side of the receiver."
3. **Soul to His attributes.** He quotes the sages of Kabbalah: "כי הוא נשמה למדותיו" (1167): "for He is a soul to His attributes." His attributes are to Him as a body is to its soul: He is in them and not bounded by them.
4. **Negation alone cannot stand** (1168). If one says, with the philosophers, that we may speak of Him only by what He is not, one cannot also say what the Kabbalists say. People think the Kabbalists' view harms His oneness; "this is nothing."
5. **The sefirot are from the side of the receivers** (1169). The many names and the count of the sefirot do not make many in G-d; they follow the many who receive. And "והנה הוא יתברך עלת העולם הזה ופועלו" (1169): "He is the Cause and Maker of this world." Can one say that this is not an attribute at all?
6. **The sun and its rays** (1170). Quoting Ma'arechet HaElokut: the attributes are not added to Him, as a ray is not a thing added to the sun.
7. **Calling Him "mind" defines Him** (1171). Those who say His essence is mind give Him a definition, "והוא יתברך לא יוגדר כלל" (1171): "and He is not defined at all."
8. **Simple, so He holds all and knows all.** "כי הוא יתברך יודע גבור ויכול, הכל בכח אחד, הוא עצמו" (1172): "for He knows, is mighty and able, all by one power, which is Himself." "כי הוא יתברך פשוט בתכלית הפשיטות, ומפני כך כולל הכל" (1172): "for He is simple with the utmost simplicity, and so He holds all."
9. **He is all, so nothing is hidden from Him.** "כי אשר הוא הכל, אי אפשר שיהיה נעלם ממנו דבר" (1173): "for He who is all, nothing can be hidden from Him." If something were hidden, it would be apart from Him, and He would not be all.

## 2.4 The One who unites opposites

A dispute for Heaven lasts; a dispute not for Heaven does not. Why? Opposites cannot stand in one subject, and the world is one subject, because it is from the One (1286-1288). So how do fire and water stand? "כי הוא יתעלה ויתברך הוא המאחד שני הפכים" (1288): "for He is the One who unites two opposites." And "ומה שהוא סבה לשני הפכים דבר זה בעצמו אחדותו יתברך" (1288): "that He is the cause of two opposites is itself His oneness." If He caused only fire, there would have to be another cause for water. So from His side, "אם הם הפכים הם מתאחדים" (1289): "even if they are opposites, they become one."

The same for the sages' disputes: "these forbid and these permit," but both were "given from one shepherd." "והטעמים הם מן השם יתברך" (1291): "and the reasons are from G-d." The ruling of the law comes from a level above the dispute, the fully simple mind, "שלא שייך בשכל פשוט לגמרי" (1292): "that has no place in the fully simple mind" (dispute has no place there). Why were only Hillel and Shammai "the words of the living G-d"? "כי החיות הוא אמתת המציאות" (1294): "for life is the truth of being," and "כי החיים מתפשטים לימין ולשמאל" (1294): "for life spreads to the right and to the left."

A dispute not for Heaven is plain loss: "כי הפירוד והחלוק הוא התחלת ההפסד" (1296): "for split and division are the start of loss." Dispute and Gehinnom were both made on the second day.

## 2.5 The One is not a number

Chapter 5 counts tens, sevens and fours. Then it turns to dispute and love. Why? To teach "the rank of oneness": "אשר האחד אינו מספר, ואינו נכנס בכלל מספר" (1297): "the One is not a number and does not enter into number." "שאי אפשר רק שיהיה הכל אחד, ודבר זה הוא קיום העולם" (1297): "it cannot be but that all is one, and this is the lasting of the world."

Love that hangs on nothing lasts forever for the same reason: "כי האחדות הוא הקיום בעצמו" (1299): "oneness is lasting itself," and "אין בטול לדבר שהוא אחד בעצם משום דבר" (1299): "nothing can undo what is one in its essence." Such love does not need to be "for Heaven," because it is already oneness (1300).

## 2.6 Division is from the body, oneness from form

Ten is the separate world, seven the middle, four the physical. "והאחדות הוא מצד הצורה, שאין בה חלוק כלל" (1271): "and oneness is from the side of form, which has no division at all." Man binds the three: "והוא מקשר את כל ג' עולמות, עד שהעולם אחד מקושר לגמרי" (1267): "and he binds all three worlds until the world is one and fully bound." Seven is six sides and a middle that has no distance; "כי כאשר יש שבעה, יש כאן חבור הקודש אל הגשמי" (1273): "when there is seven, here is the joining of the holy to the physical": six weekdays and Shabbat.

## 2.7 He alone is One; life flows by cleaving

The fullest line on unity in chapter 6 (1570): "כי הוא יתברך אחד בלבד ואין זולתו" (1570): "for He alone is One and there is none besides Him." So life and being come to all only from Him: "ואין לנמצאים מצד עצמם דבר, כי אם מה שמשפיע להם השם יתברך, ובזה השם יתברך אחד ואין זולתו" (1570): "beings have nothing from their own side, only what G-d pours to them, and by this G-d is One and there is none besides Him." "כי המקור אין לו הפסק כלל" (1570): "for the source has no break at all." Life comes through cleaving, as branches live from the root; karet (being cut off) is being cut from the cleaving. And "כי אין לך דבר שהוא קרוב אל השם יתברך כמו התורה, שהיא אצולה מן השם יתברך" (1571): "nothing is as near to G-d as the Torah, which is drawn from G-d."

## 2.8 What is all has no other with it

A full acquisition must be single, with no partner (1624). "אבל דבר שהוא הכל, אי אפשר שיהיה עוד אחר עמו" (1628): "but what is all cannot have another with it." So the Torah is one. Heaven and earth are one "כמו העיגול והמרכז הם ביחד דבר אחד" (1629): "as the circle and the center are together one thing."

## 2.9 Against the Rambam on "for His honor" (1663-1667)

"All that G-d made He made for His honor." The Rambam (Guide III:13) said G-d takes nothing from others, so "for His honor" must mean "for His will, which is His essence." The Maharal: "so the Rambam was pushed" to strain the words. The sages meant it plainly. "רק שהאדם נברא שהשם יתברך מלך עליו" (1664): "only that man was created so that G-d be King over him." A king's honor comes from ruling, not from what the people give. And since "against your will you are born and live and die," "כי מאחר שהכל מצד השם יתברך, ולא מצד האדם, לא נחשב שהוא מקבל דבר" (1665): "since all is from G-d's side and not from man's, He is not counted as receiving anything."

Why create at all, if He is whole? "ואין ספק כי הדבר שהוא בפעל הוא יותר שלם ממה שהוא בכח" (1666): "surely what is in act is more whole than what is in potential," "עד שמלכותו יתברך הוא בפעל, ולא שיהיה זה בכח בלבד" (1666): "so that His kingship be in act, and not only in potential." "כי בלא הנבראים לא שייך לומר" King (1662): "for without creatures one could not say" King. The Maharal adds (1667) that the Rambam wrote for a generation drawn to the philosophers.

He also answers the Rambam's reading of "G-d wanted to make them worthy, so He gave them much Torah and many mitzvot" (1670-1693). The Rambam said: with many mitzvot, one is sure to do one fully for love, and earn the World to Come. The Maharal tests this against the Talmud at length and reads the mishna plainly: "כי הוא יתברך צדיק, וחפץ בזכאים וצדיקים" (1692): "for He is righteous and wants the worthy and the righteous." The mitzvot are His decree, given so we will be righteous in deeds.

## 2.10 The goal of all is Him

"כי תכלית הכל הוא הקב"ה" (1652): "for the goal of all is the Holy One." Beriah (creation) "נאמר על הצורה הנבדלת האלהית שדבק בנבראים" (1655): "is said of the separate G-dly form that cleaves to creatures." And "הן אמת שאין יתברך גוף וגשם, ומכל מקום הוא עשה הגוף" (1661): "true, He is not body or matter, and yet He made the body."

- **MH-S39 "No wall between" (1071, 1163).** Inside: I live in nature, and I feel it is not closed off. The separate world is right here, like the yud inside the heh that does not touch the lines around it. Nothing is "only natural." How to enter: look at an ordinary thing and say: this world is tied to the separate world, and no wall was made between them. It differs from S63 The world held (the world kept in being). This state is the openness of nature to what is above it.
- **MH-S40 "Opposites held in the One" (1288-1292).** Inside: two things pull against each other, two views or two needs, and I do not have to pick one to be at peace. I feel both as from Him, the One who unites opposites. How to enter: name the two sides. Say: both reasons are from Him (1291); His oneness holds them (1288). Then act by the better one, knowing the ruling comes from a place above the dispute (1292). It is the cure of S10 Torn in two, not by cutting off one side but by finding the One who holds both.

## 4.1 Hold both reasons (`mh_both_from_one`, new)

In a disagreement, whether with another person or inside yourself, say: "והטעמים הם מן השם יתברך" (1291): "the reasons are from G-d." Then look for the level above: the simple mind where there is no dispute (1292). Check yourself: is this for Heaven, or to win? A dispute to win is split, and split is the start of loss (1296). This is MH-S40.

## 4.2 Love that hangs on nothing (`mh_love_hangs_on_nothing`, new)

Love that hangs on a thing ends when the thing ends. Love that hangs on nothing is oneness itself, and lasts (1299). Practice: think of someone you love. Ask: what would be left if what I get from him were gone? Love that.

## 4.3 Leopard, eagle, deer, lion (`mh_four_stages_of_doing`, new)

Ben Teima: "be bold as a leopard, light as an eagle, run like a deer, mighty as a lion, to do the will of your Father in heaven." The Maharal reads four stages (1342-1344). Bold: wanting, not being "like a person asleep in the service of his Creator" (1343): "שלא יהיה נחשב כמו אדם ישן בעבודת בוראו." Light: waking, so nature does not weigh you down. Run: moving toward the mitzvah. Mighty: doing it fully. Practice: when a mitzvah is before you, go through the four. And "ואצל עבודת השם יתברך, כל שמוסיף אין זה יציאה מן הסדר" (1348): "in the service of G-d, whatever one adds is not a going out of order." But "אבל יהיה עז פנים בעבודתו יתברך, ויהיה בעל בושה בענייני העולם הזה" (1394): "let him be bold in His service, and bashful in the matters of this world."

## 4.4 Your self is the Temple (`mh_self_as_temple`, new)

"One who has knowledge, it is as if the Temple were built in his days" (1355). "הנה האדם עצמו הוא בית המקדש" (1355): "the person himself is the Temple." Practice: before prayer, stand still and say: this body and mind are a house for Him. Then keep that house holy for the hour.

## 4.5 Something new every day (`mh_new_every_day`, new)

"Turn it and turn it, for all is in it." "בודאי תמצא בכל יום ויום חידוש" (1379): "surely you will find something new every day." The Torah is "what brings one to G-d, to cleave to Him in oneness" (1380). Practice: in each day's learning, look for one new thing, and write it down.

## 4.6 No pain for its own sake (`mh_no_needless_pain`, guard)

"By the pain, the reward." But the Maharal limits it: reward follows nearness (1382), and pain counts only when one pushes past what blocks a mitzvah. "אבל אם רוצה לקיים המצוה בצער, אף שיכול לקיים אותו שלא בצער, נראה שאין כאן שכר יותר" (1382): "but if he wants to do the mitzvah with pain when he could do it without pain, it seems there is no extra reward." The same with "bread with salt": it speaks only of one "able and can bear it" (1469), and "שאם יאכל פת במלח שלא לצורך, אין זה נקרא שממית עצמו על התורה" (1470): "if he eats bread with salt with no need, that is not called killing himself for the Torah." The guide must never suggest self-harm or needless hardship.

## 4.7 Awe and smallness before learning (`mh_greatness_and_smallness`, new)

Before learning or prayer, do two things: "שיהיה מכיר ערך רוממות השם יתברך וגדולתו יתברך, ויכיר גם כן שפלות ערכו" (1494): "that he know the height and greatness of G-d, and know also his own lowness." "אבל מי שהוא בעיניו חשוב ובעל מעלה, אין לו הכנה לקבל כלל" (1495): "one who is important and great in his own eyes has no readiness to receive at all."

## 4.8 Learn with joy (`mh_learn_with_joy`, new)

"כי אין ראוי לאדם שלימות האלקי הזה, היא התורה, כי אם על ידי שמחה, שהיא שלימות נפשי" (1497): "a person is fit for this G-dly wholeness, the Torah, only through joy, which is the wholeness of the soul." Joy is not laughter: "ואין השחוק דומה לשמחה, שעיקר שמחה בלב בלבד" (1514): "laughter is not like joy, for joy is mostly in the heart alone." Practice: before learning, find one thing to be glad of, and begin from there.

## 4.9 The wick near the flame (`mh_wick_to_flame`, new)

Serving the wise is greater than learning from them, "כי אין ספק כי כאשר תקרב הפתילה אל הנר, שהנר מדליק את הפתילה" (1499): "for surely when you bring the wick near the flame, the flame lights the wick." And learn with a friend: "ראוי שיוציא השכל אל אחר, ואז הוא שכל נבדל שאינו עומד בגוף האדם" (1500): "he should bring his mind out to another, and then it is a separate mind that does not stand in the body." Practice: do a small service for a teacher or a wise person this week. Learn with a partner.

## 4.10 Carry the yoke with a friend (`mh_carry_with_a_friend`, new)

Four ways among the forty-eight (1528-1529): carry the burden with your friend, judge him well, bring him to truth, bring him to peace. "ומורה דבר זה שהוא אדם טוב, כאשר נכנס בעול עם חבירו" (1528): "this shows he is a good person, when he takes on the yoke with his friend." Practice: when a friend is in trouble, step into it with him.

## 4.11 Say it in the name of who said it (`mh_in_the_name_of`, new)

"One who says a thing in the name of the one who said it brings redemption." Esther told the king in Mordechai's name, and did not take the credit. "כי בודאי היתה אסתר יודעת כי כל אשר נעשה הוא מאת השם יתברך" (1542): "for surely Esther knew that all that was done was from G-d." Each person's insight is his own gift from Him: "כך נתן לכל אדם שכלו ודעתו" (1538): "so He gave each person his own mind and knowledge." Practice: when you repeat a good word, name its source. When good comes through you, say: it is from Him.

## 4.12 Review as harvest (`mh_review`, extended)

Already in the Derush al HaTorah report. Here the Maharal gives its reason: learning not reviewed is seed left in the ground; review is the harvest that lifts it out of the body (1551). Rabbi Akiva: "sing every day." A person is made for toil, "כי האדם נשאר בכח תמיד" (1553): "for a person always remains in potential." Practice: review yesterday's learning before new learning.

## 4.13 Silence as a sign (`mh_silence_for_mind`, extended)

From the 1-419 report. Add: "כי השתיקה היא סימן חכמה" (1512): "for silence is a sign of wisdom," and speech in excess stops the mind from working.

## 5. The big human questions

**If G-d is One, why is the world full of opposites?** Because He is the cause of both, and that is itself His oneness (1288). From His side the opposites become one (1289).

**Must I be torn when two good things conflict?** No. Both reasons are from Him (1291). Choose by the better one, knowing the choice comes from a level above the dispute (1292).

**Why does love fade?** Because it hung on a thing. Love that hangs on nothing is oneness, and oneness lasts (1299).

**Is nature closed off from G-d?** No. There is no wall between this world and the separate world (1163).

**Can we speak of G-d at all?** Yes. He is not defined (1171), but He knows, is mighty and able, all by one power, which is Himself (1172). The attributes are like rays of the sun, not added to Him (1170).

**Why did He make the world, if He lacks nothing?** Not for a need. What is in act is more whole than what is only in potential, so His kingship is in act (1666). He takes nothing from us, because all is from His side (1665).

**Should I make things hard for myself to earn more?** No. Pain earns more only when one pushes past what blocks a mitzvah (1382). Bread with salt is not for one who has no need of it (1470).

**What goes with me when I die?** Only Torah and good deeds (1605-1610): the Torah for the soul, the deeds for the body.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **The Rambam's attributes (the main disagreement).** The Rambam: His knowledge is His essence; we may speak of Him only by negation. The Tanya (ch. 2, ch. 48) and Gate of Unity (ch. 7-9) quote the Rambam's "He is the knower, the knowing and the known, all one," and add that this is beyond our grasp. The Maharal says the opposite: knowing is His act, not His essence, because calling His essence "mind" defines Him (1164, 1171). They agree on the result: He knows all, and there is no many in Him (1166, 1172). The Maharal also rejects "negation only" as unable to stand with Kabbalah (1168). Chabad keeps the Rambam's words and puts Kabbalah above them. The Maharal argues against the Rambam's words directly.
- **The sefirot.** "He is a soul to His attributes" (1167) and "the sefirot are from the side of the receivers" (1169). Tanya and the Zohar say "He and His life are one" (the sefirot are one with Him as light with the sun). The Maharal's "sun and its rays" (1170) is the same image Gate of Unity uses (ch. 3). Both refuse to make many in Him.
- **Tzimtzum.** Not named. The Maharal's parallel is "from the side of the receiver" (1077, 1166), and the yud inside the heh "that does not touch" (1071). Tanya (Gate of Unity ch. 7) says tzimtzum is not real from His side; the Maharal says the toil, time and change are not from His side. Both put the limit on the receiver's side.
- **The Ramak's formula** ("He is all, and all is not He"). The Maharal says "He who is all" (1173), "what is all cannot have another with it" (1628), and "He is simple, and so He holds all" (1172). With 1570 ("beings have nothing from their own side"), this is the first half of the formula. The second half is his "from the side of the receiver."
- **"For His honor."** Chabad's reason for creation is "He desired a dwelling in the lower worlds" (Tanya ch. 36). The Maharal's reason is that kingship must be in act (1666). They meet: both say the world is for His kingship to be real below. Both reject the idea that He needs it.
- **Joy** (1497) is close to Tanya ch. 26 and 31: sadness blocks, joy opens. **Humility and awe before learning** (1494) matches the Chabad custom of preparing for prayer by thinking of His greatness and one's smallness.
- **The One who unites opposites** (1288) is close to the Chabad teaching that the Essence holds opposites (the Rashba's "He is able" to be both infinite and finite, as the Rebbe often cited). The Maharal gets there through dispute and law.
