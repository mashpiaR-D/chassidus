*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Long maamarim on the parshiyos from Yisro to Pekudei. They are dense Kabbalistic exposition: ratzo and shov, Malchus rising and descending, nahar dinur, Amalek as a kelipah, and delight clothed in its opposite. But the Mitteler Rebbe keeps grounding each structure in what "we see with our senses" in a person. That gives extended asides, often in parentheses, on waking up in smallness or expansiveness, rises and falls in spiritual life and livelihood, bitterness before prayer, teshuvah that turns into joy, arrogance as the root of desire and envy, ecstasy that is really self-feeling, and the humble yoke of plain deeds.

**How much of it is medicine.** some: most of the volume is Kabbalistic cosmology, but several maamarim (Tetzaveh, Vayakhel, the livelihood section) hold sustained, concrete psychology of moods, falls, teshuvah, ego and toil.

**Its model of the person.** A person has a G-dly soul bound inside a coarse animal soul and body. When one grows stronger, the other weakens. Inner life pulses in ratzo and shov, like a heartbeat or a candle flame, and it alternates between katnus (smallness of mind and heart: dullness, sadness, irritability) and gadlus (expansive joy). Change comes through its opposite. Humility and bitterness come before light, the way a seed rots before it sprouts. Toil and despair give a delight greater than delight that is simply given. The deepest root of vice is excess self-feeling (gasus, yeshus). Real humility is not feeling oneself at all. Any strength of one's own comes from an awakening from above.

**Its philosophy.** Divine life-force reaches every creature in ratzo and shov, contraction and expansion. So there is no light without prior darkness and no growth without prior nullification, and time itself is a cycle of renewal. Divine delight goes down into its opposite (pain, toil, distance) so that turning darkness into light gives a greater delight than light alone. The human cycles of night and day, falling and rising, bitterness and joy are this same structure lived out.

**What it adds.** A phenomenology of mood cycles: waking in smallness or expansiveness, night-time irritability and illness, rises and falls in spirit and livelihood, all read as ratzo and shov, with explicit instruction against both despair and pride. Also a sharp split between felt ecstasy (possibly self-feeling) and the humble, joyful yoke of plain deeds. Arrogance and chutzpah (Amalek) are made the single root of craving and envy. And toil and despair are shown to produce a greater delight.

## Some mornings I wake up heavy, sad and irritable for no reason, and my prayer is dead

Days when the mind grasps nothing, like a small child. Heaviness, laziness, bad temper that cannot bear anyone. One can pray only with brokenness and no delight, while other days one wakes up joyful and expansive.

**What is really going on.** Not a verdict on the person. The soul lives in a rhythm like night and day: smallness comes before greatness, as night comes before day. It is part of how life-force comes and goes in everything.

**Program: Serving in smallness.** (1) Notice that you woke up in smallness and don't force ecstasy. (2) Let the day's prayer be humble brokenness, 'like dust to all'. (3) Say Shema and learn simply to do the Creator's will, even without mind or heart, as in the bedtime Shema said half asleep. (4) Know that this rises too, and wait for the day to return. *When:* On any day, or any hour, of smallness. Night and bedtime are the model. *For:* Anyone whose mood swings between expansion and dullness. *Not for:* Not a reason to stop trying for depth on days of expansiveness. `TC4·Q03` · `TC4·Q04` · `TC4·Q02` · `TC4·Q05`

**Program: Holding steady through rises and falls.** (1) When you fall, remind yourself this is a world of change and that after descent comes ascent. (2) Do not say 'it's hopeless'. (3) When you rise, don't be carried away or imagine it will last. (4) Trust in G-d alone and ask not to be cast away. *When:* Whenever a fall or a high comes, spiritual or material. *For:* People battered by repeated setbacks, or inflated by success. *Not for:* Not a substitute for practical action or help during real crises. `TC4·Q05` · `TC4·Q06` · `TC4·Q07` · `TC4·Q08`

Smallness is the night that comes before a new day.** Smallness gets placed inside the universal rhythm of ratzo and shov, so it is no longer read as a verdict. The person can serve inside it instead of despairing, and night even carries its own intense longing. `TC4·Q02` · `TC4·Q04` · `TC4·Q05` · `TC4·Q08`

## I keep falling, spiritually and financially, and I'm ready to give up

Repeated falls in Torah, prayer and teshuvah, or in health, children and income. Each one feels final and pulls toward despair.

**What is really going on.** This is a world of change. Nobody falls completely: after descent comes ascent. The descent itself prepares the greater ascent, since there is no light without darkness first.

**Program: Holding steady through rises and falls.** (1) When you fall, remind yourself this is a world of change and that after descent comes ascent. (2) Do not say 'it's hopeless'. ## My sadness slides into despair and then into giving in to cravings

Going through the motions of mitzvos with no inner life, like something dead. Sadness turns into hopelessness, and hopelessness into indulgence.

**What is really going on.** Sadness that falls into despair stands at the back side of holiness, right next to impurity. It is not humility. It leads down into bad desires.

**Program: Teshuvah that turns into joy in the mitzvah.** (1) Do teshuvah from the heart's depths: bitterness over distance until the hard, brazen heart softens. (2) Know your low place truthfully. (3) When you come to a mitzvah, reflect that through it G-d draws near to your lowly soul and gives light even in darkness. (4) Rejoice in the mitzvah and in G-d, not in yourself. *When:* Teshuvah first, then each mitzvah that follows. The two must come together. *For:* Someone carrying guilt who has forgotten how to be glad. *Not for:* Not teshuvah alone without deeds. The work says teshuvah without good deeds does not rise as high. `TC4·Q11` · `TC4·Q12` · `TC4·Q13`

**Reframe.** From *"Feeling low and hopeless is right, given who I am."* to **Sadness that falls into despair is not humility. It borders on impurity and leads to indulgence. Longing-bitterness is different.** Distinguishing productive brokenness from despair keeps the person from calling collapse piety. `TC4·Q10` · `TC4·Q27`

## I've done wrong, I feel far and ashamed, and I don't see how joy is possible again

Bitterness over one's distance from G-d, and a feeling that brokenness and joy cannot live together.

**What is really going on.** The bitterness is born of hidden love and is compassion for one's own soul. Teshuvah from the depths softens the hard heart, and from it a greater joy comes, like moving from death to life.

**Program: Heaviness of heart before prayer.** (1) Before prayer, settle into seriousness (koved rosh). (2) Take account of your distance from G-d through your faults and attachments until bitterness, lowliness and submission come. (3) Know that this bitterness comes from love and is compassion for your own soul, in proportion to your awareness. (4) Then contemplate G-d's greatness through Pesukei d'Zimra, Shema and Amidah, where love and awe can now be born. *When:* Daily, just before morning prayer, as its first stage. *For:* Someone whose coarseness or hard heart keeps contemplation from moving him. *Not for:* Anyone depressed or given to self-contempt. The work itself warns that sadness which becomes despair leads down. `TC4·Q01` · `TC4·Q09` · `TC4·Q30` · `TC4·Q31`

## I feel nothing in prayer; for me it's only a heavy yoke

One cannot reach felt love or ecstasy, finds no satisfaction in one's service, longs to be close, and is not let in.

**What is really going on.** Real lowliness admitted truthfully. The yoke path is not second-rate: a deed done in simple faith without feeling stands higher than felt ecstasy, and toil later turns into a greater delight.

**Program: The humble yoke when there is no feeling.** (1) Say truthfully to yourself that you cannot reach devotional heights. (2) Commit to Torah and mitzvos in actual deed, like an ox to the yoke, all day. (3) Rejoice out of that humility, like a poor man glad for his bread. (4) Trust that a deed done in simple faith is higher than felt ecstasy, and that toil later becomes delight. *When:* All day, in plain deeds. *For:* People who feel nothing in prayer, and people whose highs have turned into self-feeling. *Not for:* Not a grim, joyless self-punishment. ## I can't bear it when others rise, and nothing I get is ever enough

*Its own term:* גסות וחוצפא (קליפת עמלק)

## I work myself to exhaustion, see no success, and money worry fills my mind

Toiling without results until one's spirit is short and one says it is hopeless. Business takes over the inner mind and heart.

**What is really going on.** Despair in toil is the seed rotting before it sprouts. The deeper mistake is putting the depth of one's heart into business instead of only one's hands.

**Program: Work with the hands, not the depth of the heart.** (1) During the week, work with your hands but keep your inner mind and heart out of business. (2) Cast your burden on G-d with faith and trust. (3) Your mind will be free of worry for Shabbos and prayer. (4) When despair in toil comes, know it is the seed rotting before growth. *When:* The six weekdays of work, leading into Shabbos. *For:* People consumed by work and money worry. *Not for:* Not a reason to ignore real financial trouble or skip practical steps. `TC4·Q29` · `TC4·Q28` · `TC4·Q21`

**Reframe.** From *"All my toil with no success means I've failed."* to **Despair in toil is the seed rotting before it sprouts, and blessing grows from it.** Toil and its opposite outweigh the original delight. Nullification is the vessel for renewal from nothing. `TC4·Q28` · `TC4·Q21` · `TC4·Q08`

## Cautions

- 'Broken heart' and 'loathing his life' language must never be given to a depressed person. The text itself separates this from sadness that sinks into despair. - 'Humility' described as being despised in one's own eyes can feed self-hatred. The work's highest humility is not feeling oneself, not self-contempt. - 'Say it's hopeless' in business and toil should not be read as a reason to ignore real financial trouble or to give up practical help. - Framing suffering, illness or loss as a necessary descent should not be pressed on someone in acute pain as a reason it had to happen. - The Amalek passages about people who hate the devout, and about luxury as chutzpah, should be applied inward only, never used to judge or label others. - The yoke path without felt satisfaction could become grim self-punishment. The text requires joy that comes from humility.
