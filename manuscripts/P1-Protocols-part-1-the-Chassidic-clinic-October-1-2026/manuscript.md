## How to read this

Each protocol is what a mashpia would hold in mind for one kind of person. It has a **formulation** (where it sits in the soul, which state on the map, how it traces to the root problem of a self that holds itself up, what is blocked, what state is needed, and which kind of healing fits) and a **protocol** of 4 stages. Each stage gives the exact practice, the reasoning, the mashal and its limit, the hour, the length, the sign it is working, the sources' own fallback if stuck, and when to move on. Then the shape of the day, an honest duration, the guards, and what the guide actually says to start each stage.

**The body is inside the stages, not beside them.** The Rebbe, quoting the Maggid: «אז א קליינע לעכעלע אין גוף גורם א גרויסע לאך אין דער נשמה», *a small hole in the body causes a big hole in the soul*, so 'guard your health plainly, in eating, drinking and sleep, and it will be good for you in the spirit too' (Igros Kodesh vol. 7, letter 2055; `Chassidus-txt/R. Menachem Mendel Schneerson (Lubavitcher Rebbe) (1902-1994)/Igros Kodesh (Berlin, Paris, and Brooklyn, 1928-1972).txt` line 28618). So lunch eaten with a blessing, daylight, water, breath, bedtime and a checkup each appear as steps with their reason. None is an afterthought, and there are no supplements or doses.

**Common rules (ours, from MAP Part 6, VS and the constitution as cited there).** Danger overrides everything (S21, D03). Low days get the low-day minimum, never self-examination (S04). Never demand a feeling (D25). Never promise a state. A real practical problem gets a practical step and a person (D07). Ask before a sitting. A no is a no.

## P1-01 · Heaviness: the afternoon slump, low energy

**Presenting.** My afternoons are heavy. Around two o'clock I just sink. No energy, everything weighs a ton, and anything spiritual feels like one more thing I'm failing at.

## Formulation

- **In their words.** "My afternoons are heavy. Around two I sink. I tried meditating and it doesn't do anything. I end the day feeling lazy and like I'm wasting my life."
- **Where in the soul.** Mainly the animal soul, in its heaviness. The Tanya places laziness and sadness in the element of earth in the natural soul (T82): weight, sinking, staying put. The G-dly soul is not missing; its light simply does not reach the body in the afternoon. Faculty: da'as. In the morning the mind was bound, at least a little, to something; by midday that binding has let go, so knowing no longer reaches the heart. The middos are flat rather than hurt. Garments: thought drifts, deed slows, speech turns to complaint ('I'm lazy'). Level: nefesh, the level of plain doing. The repair starts there, with the body and a small act. It does not start with feelings at the level of ruach or neshamah. The body is part of the picture from the start: a tired, under-fed, under-slept or unwell body is itself a 'hole' that lets the soul's light leak out (IK7a). (Stated: T82, IK7a. Applying it to the afternoon is ours.)
- **State now.** S04 Low and heavy (atzvus), entered each day from S08 Run by the natural soul. In the morning it is often S01 Scattered.
- **Root.** The root problem (yeshus, the self that feels it holds itself up) takes two forms here. First, the day is felt as running on my own energy. When that energy ebbs, the ebb reads as a verdict: 'I'm failing, nothing is here.' But the Tanya describes the ebb as the normal tide of every in-between person. After the prayer-time of an expanded mind, the mind withdraws and the animal soul wakes again (T208). Second, the sadness itself is about me. The Mitteler Rebbe: this sadness 'is not from G-dliness at all, only that his soul is not whole in some completeness that would honor his name' (DC381). So the heaviness carries a hidden self-measure ('I should be more than this'), and that measure is the weight. (Stated: T208, DC381. Seeing both as one root is ours.)
- **What is blocked.** Quickness (zerizus) is blocked by heaviness (kevedus), which the Tanya says comes from sadness and a stone-dull heart and cannot win (T371). Da'as has slipped. This is the ordinary midday hesech hada'as, the attention gone elsewhere, not a sealed heart. It is atzvus, not merirus: flat, with no life in it (T452a), not the alive pain of a broken heart (T452b). It is also not true timtum. Timtum is a heart closed even in prayer, all day (T403). This is a daily tide. The Tanya names exactly this weight: sometimes a person cannot even hold back in what is permitted 'because of the heaviness in his heart' (T404). Also blocked: the body's own part in the service. Lunch is eaten at the desk without a thought, there is no daylight, and sleep is short.
- **State needed.** S53 Glad (simcha), reached through S33 Before Whom and S65 Given right now, done 'in smallness', and landing in S81 The deed in the hand. Later, S80 Prayer: mind into heart, at the afternoon prayer.
- **Kind of healing.** Simcha, joy, first, because the Tanya's rule is that heaviness only loses to quickness, and quickness comes only from joy (T371). It comes by way of hamtakah, sweetening: the afternoon is named as the hour when judgments grow strong and 'need sweetening' (MVS128). So we do not fight the heaviness. We bring it, as it is, into prayer (Z73). There is a small dose of iskafya at one point only: standing up mid-day against everything that holds you back, to pray. The Maharash calls this very dear (TS163). Not teshuvah or self-accounting on a low afternoon (MAP S04 guard). Not bittul teachings. Not 'let go'. (Stated: T371, MVS128, Z73, TS163. Choosing and ordering them for this case is ours.)

## 1. Name the tide, wake the body (week 1)

**Aim.** From 'I'm lazy and failing' to 'this is the known afternoon tide; it says nothing about me'. From sinking in the chair to one quick bodily act that is itself service.

**Practice.** When: at the moment of the sink (for most people 1:30-3:30 pm). How long: 3-4 minutes. Steps. (a) Notice and name it out loud, quietly: 'This is the afternoon tide.' The reasoning, which is stated: the Tanya says that after the morning's prayer-time of expanded mind, the mind withdraws and the animal soul wakes again. It describes this as the normal life of the in-between person, not a fall (T208). (b) Apply the test the Tanya gives for heaviness that comes in the middle of the working day. Real sorrow out of love of G-d does not show up in the middle of a business afternoon. So this heaviness is not a message about your soul, and you do not need to argue with it (T377). (c) Wake the body, as service and not as a break from service. Stand up. Drink a full glass of water slowly. Go outside or to a window with daylight for two minutes. The Baal Shem Tov's order is body first: 'first rouse yourself with your body, with all your strength, so the power of the soul can shine in you' (Z58). The Zohar's line, as the Tanya quotes it: a log the flame won't catch, you strike it (T405). (d) Outside, one look in 'smallness'. Look at one plain thing, a tree or a parked car or your own hand, the way you would normally look at it. Say one sentence: 'The whole earth is full of His glory, and He is right here.' That is all. The Baal Shem Tov calls this 'service in smallness'. You look at the Presence near you the way you look at physical things, like a small child whose mind is little but whose closeness is real (Z137b, Z67a, Z67b). Mashal: two wrestlers. The heavy one is thrown even if he is stronger (T371). Its limit: the opponent is the heaviness, not you. You are not wrestling yourself. Anchor: for someone who keeps mitzvot, the glass of water gets its blessing, said slowly. If a 'what's the use' voice rises, the Mitteler Rebbe names it as the inclination's counsel, 'whose aim is to fell him completely' (DC89). The Piaseczner: 'even so, do not stay in sadness' (HA149). (Stated: T208, T377, Z58, T405, Z137b, Z67a-b, T371, DC89, HA149. Water, window and the 2-4 minute timing are ours.)

**Signs of progress.** They can name the tide without adding 'I'm lazy'. The verdict sentence shows up less in their own words. Some days the heaviness is shorter or lighter after the body act. A plain, small 'okay' instead of a glow.

**If stuck.** If they cannot get up: do (a), (b) and (d) sitting, feet flat on the floor, eyes on one object, and drink the water there. The Baal Shem Tov: if you cannot pray, do not believe that today you surely won't (Z72a). Do the smallest version, not none. If the heaviness is not a tide but sits on the whole day, has lasted weeks, or comes with hopelessness or poor sleep, this is not the protocol. Go to the guards (doctor, and S04/S21 safety).

**Move on when.** On at least 4 of 7 days they did the pause once and can say the reframe in their own words ('it's the tide, not a verdict').

**The guide says** *(ours, 128 words)*. "This week, just one thing. When the afternoon sinks in, around two, name it out loud: 'This is the afternoon tide.' Everyone who takes their inner life seriously has a morning with some light in it and an afternoon where it pulls back. It's not a sign you're lazy. Heaviness in the middle of a working day isn't telling you anything true about you, so don't argue with it. Then stand up. Drink a full glass of water slowly. Step into daylight for two minutes. Waking the body is part of this, not a break from it. Look at one plain thing, a tree or your own hand, and say one sentence: 'The whole earth is full of Him, and He's right here.' That's it. Small is fine."

## 2. Pray from the heaviness: the afternoon sitting (weeks 2-3)

**Aim.** Turn the heaviness from a wall into the material of prayer (hamtakah). Give the afternoon a fixed 'tearing away' that is precious because it happens mid-day.

**Practice.** When: once each afternoon, at a fixed time just after the usual sink. For a Jew this is set at Minchah, the afternoon prayer. For anyone else it is a short set prayer or psalm of their own. How long: 10-12 minutes. The reasoning, which is stated: the Maharash says the afternoon prayer carries more iskafya than the morning one. In the morning a person is fresh and free. In the afternoon he is busy all day and still stands up against everything that holds him back to pray, and 'this is very dear to G-d' (TS163). The Rashab calls it the tearing-away: tearing yourself out of all your affairs in the middle of the day (SHM22645). A non-Chabad master adds the reason the afternoon feels heavy: then 'the forces of judgment begin to grow strong, and they need sweetening' (MVS128). Steps. (1) Two doorways (1 min): do not go straight from the desk into prayer (KHT88a). Stand. Put the work down the way you put it down every night to sleep (KHT88b). It will be there when you come back. (2) Breath (1 min): three slow breaths. With each one, notice that the breath leaves and comes back without your making it. The Alter Rebbe uses exactly this: with every breath there is a withdrawing and a spreading out, the life running back to its source and returning (LT2256). (3) Say the heaviness to Him in your own words (2 min), out loud and low: 'It's heavy. I'm dragging. I don't feel anything.' (4) Turn it (2 min). The Baal Shem Tov: 'pray about the heaviness in your head, for what you lack is lacking in the Shechinah' (Z73). Your heaviness is not a private failure. It is one piece of the whole world's midday sleep. The Mitteler Rebbe calls the distraction of earning a living 'the exile of the Shechinah, called sleep' (KH12). So ask for the whole of it to wake: 'Let this office, this city, this tired afternoon wake up to You. Me with it.' (5) The set prayer (4-5 min). For a Jew, Minchah. Say the first blessing of the standing prayer word by word, low, with full strength (Z33). The rest at your normal pace. Anyone else: a short psalm the same way. (6) Close with one quick deed within ten minutes: the next task done promptly, or one kindness. Mashal: the King's guard. The Baal Shem Tov says that when you cannot pray with intent, know there is extra guard around the King, which means the King is here (Z72c). Limit: this does not make heaviness holy. It means the heaviness is no proof that He is absent. Second mashal: the rope. Move the top end and the bottom moves by itself (DC56). Limit: you are working the bottom end and do not see the top. (Stated: TS163, SHM22645, MVS128, KHT88a-b, LT2256, Z73, KH12, Z33, Z72c, DC56. The 10-12 minute shape and the order of the six steps are ours.)

**Signs of progress.** A sigh during step 3 or 4. The heaviness feels like 'ours' rather than 'my failure'. Prayer feels more like talking than performing. They return to work a little quicker (zerizus) even if no warmer. The afternoon has one point they look forward to, or at least don't dread.

**If stuck.** No words come at step 3: say only 'It's heavy', then go straight to step 5, saying only the first line, by plain decision (kabbalas ol). That still counts (T668, T257). If they feel 'it's fake': the Tanya says not to worry even if it seems like imagination (T665). If they miss days: no making up and no guilt. Tomorrow, the same time. If step 2 (breath) brings panic or unreality, drop it and use the hand on the table instead.

**Move on when.** The sitting has happened on most weekdays for two weeks, and at least once they report that the heaviness 'changed color' during or after it.

**The guide says** *(ours, 129 words)*. "Now let's give your afternoon a fixed point. Once a day, right after the slump, take ten minutes. First, put the work down the way you put it down every night to sleep; it'll be there after. Take three slow breaths and notice that each one leaves and comes back without you making it. Then say the heaviness out loud to Him, plainly: 'It's heavy. I'm dragging.' Then widen it. Your tiredness is one small piece of this whole half-asleep afternoon, the office, the street, everyone. Ask for all of it to wake up, you included. Then say a short set prayer slowly, the first line with full attention. End by doing the next task right away. Standing up mid-day to do this is worth more than it feels."

## 3. The King in your house: joy from the unity (weeks 3-5)

**Aim.** Open a real, quiet joy that comes from the thing itself, not from a pep talk. This is the joy that makes quickness possible (T371).

**Practice.** When: three times a week, 10 minutes, at a fixed calm time (late morning, or right after the afternoon sitting). Not late at night. The reasoning, which is stated: the Tanya gives this exact contemplation for set times when a person sees his soul 'needs to be cleaned and lit up with gladness' (T470). Steps. (1) Sit where you are. Picture the space before anything existed: only He was here. (2) Now, in this same space, with the desk, the window and you in it, He is here exactly as before, unchanged, filling it. (3) The sun mashal from the same chapter: the sun's light inside the sun is not a separate thing. It is just the sun. Everything around you is like light inside its source. (4) Bring it home with the Tanya's own picture: how great is the joy of a plain, low person when a king comes close and lodges with him in his own house (T476). Put it in your terms: someone you deeply admire is not visiting you. They have moved in, and they want to be here. (5) Stay with that for two or three minutes. Let the comparison work slowly, by itself. (6) Close with thanks: 'How good is my portion.' (Jews know this line from the morning prayers. Anyone can say it.) Limit of the mashal: a human king can leave. And you will not feel this like a guest in the room. The joy here is quiet, of the soul, and often shows only as lightness afterward. (Stated: T470, T476, T371. The modern 'moved in' image and the timing are ours.)

**Signs of progress.** Lightness after, not excitement. A smile at nothing. They can recall the one picture ('He moved in') in thirty seconds during the afternoon sink, and it helps. Quickness in the next task.

**If stuck.** Nothing moves: picturing it in the mind is enough, and the deed counts as if done with love (T668, T257). If they start chasing a feeling ('I want that high again'), name it gently as serving oneself (KH167) and go back to the plain deed. If the day is low, skip stage 3 and do only stage 1.

**Move on when.** They can call up the picture during the slump on their own, and the slump has visibly shortened on most days.

**The guide says** *(ours, 122 words)*. "Three times this week, at a calm hour, ten minutes. Sit where you are. Picture this room before anything existed. Only He was here. Now the desk, the window and you are in it, and He is still here exactly as before, filling it. Not far off. Here. Now picture someone you deeply admire. They aren't visiting you. They've moved into your small apartment, and they want to be there. Think how that would feel, and then that this is truer than that. Stay with it two or three minutes. Don't push for a feeling; if any comes, it's usually quiet, a lightness afterward. Close with: 'How good is my portion.' Later, when the slump comes, call up just that one picture."

## 4. A day that carries it: food, light, sleep, and the evening (from week 4, ongoing)

**Aim.** Make the whole day a vessel, so the afternoon is held from both ends. The body's acts become part of the service: lunch, daylight, sleep. The ebb becomes a known rhythm, not a daily defeat.

**Practice.** Lunch, 1 minute before and after. Eat sitting down, away from the screen if you can. Before: one sentence, 'This food came to me. I'm eating it to have strength for what and whom I care about.' For a Jew, the blessing, said slowly. The reasoning, which is stated: food gives life because its root is higher than man. When a person eats so that with that strength he will learn and pray, he 'raises the spark' in it (TS4280a, TS4280b). R. Aharon of Strashelye: from the blood made by eating, the heart is fired in Shema and prayer (SA6). The Alter Rebbe's Hillel went to eat as 'doing a kindness to the poor one: his body' (T409). Eat to be strong, not stuffed. A heavy lunch is half of the slump. A 5-10 minute walk in daylight after lunch, with one line: 'Lift your eyes and see Who created these.' 'These' is what you see; 'Who' is the hidden one giving it (LT82). Sleep as handing the soul back. At bedtime, the two-line check: 'What moved me today? What did I do with it?' No court case, no accounting on a low day. Then the bedtime Shema for a Jew, or for anyone the plain verse 'Into Your hand I entrust my spirit', and lights out at a set hour. On waking: 'I thank You' (Modeh Ani). The day begins there, and that thanks stays whole whatever else is missing (HY84a, HY84b). The checkup: if tiredness lasts weeks, or comes with poor sleep, breathlessness, weight change or low mood, book a plain checkup. The Rebbe, quoting the Maggid: a small hole in the body causes a big hole in the soul. So guard health plainly, in eating, drinking and sleep, and it will be good for you in the spirit too (IK7a, IK7b). Keeping the body whole is one of the ways of G-d (IK7c). And the doctor was given permission to heal (IK3), so following an expert's instructions is part of the path (IK10). Going is making a vessel for the blessing. It is not a lack of faith. One quick kindness mid-afternoon (a message, a favor). The Rebbe's advice for low mood is to turn outward through real activity, above all a kindness (engine condition C13). (Stated: TS4280, SA6, T409, LT82, HY84, IK7, IK3, IK10. The daily arrangement, the walk-with-a-line and the checkup triggers are ours.)

**Signs of progress.** Lunch is eaten, not inhaled. The walk has become a habit. Bedtime is steadier. The slump is shorter and has lost its verdict. On bad days they do the stage 1 minimum without being told.

**If stuck.** On a bad week, drop to stage 1 alone plus the water and the walk. Never skip; shorten (inner-world 40-day rule). If sleep is the problem, start with bedtime, not with the afternoon.

**Move on when.** No graduation. After about 40 days, review: is the afternoon now the point they keep, not the point they lose? Then go deeper only by choice (for example, the full afternoon prayer daily, or adding a fixed ten minutes of learning after it).

**The guide says** *(ours, 126 words)*. "Let's make the whole day hold the afternoon. Evening: the two-line check, bedtime Shema or a verse, lights out at a set time. Total: about 20 minutes on a normal weekday. ## Worked example: the heavy afternoon, pushed all the way

*A full exchange in the guide's voice. The person is the owner's test case: an ordinary American, Jewish by birth, not observant, who keeps pushing. **Person.** My afternoons are heavy. Like around 2 I just sink. I've tried meditating and it doesn't do anything.

**Guide.** Two o'clock, every day, like a tide going out. Before I suggest anything, let me ask a few things so I give you the right practice and not a generic one. Is it only the afternoons, or does the weight sit on the whole day? And how long has it been like this?

**Person.** Mornings are fine actually. Coffee, I get stuff done. It's after lunch. Maybe a year? It's not like I'm depressed. I just feel useless till like 5.

**Guide.** Good, that helps. Three more. How are you sleeping, and when did you last have a checkup? What do you usually eat for lunch, and where? And when the slump hits, what do you say to yourself?

**Guide.** Last two. When it's heavy, is there any pain in it, like missing something or being far from something? Or is it just flat? And is there anything in your afternoon that's yours, any pause, any prayer, anything at all?

**Person.** Just flat. Nothing. And no, I'm Jewish but I don't pray. I don't really believe in that stuff.

**Guide.** Here's how I see it. Your mornings have some light in them, and after midday that light pulls back. On top of the tide you lay a verdict, 'I'm lazy, I'm wasting my life', and that verdict is most of the weight. The flatness isn't telling you the truth about you. It's what heaviness does when nothing in the day lifts it. And your body is part of this: six hours of sleep and a sandwich at the desk make the tide deeper. So we won't fight the heaviness. We'll give the afternoon one small act that lifts, and build from there.

**Guide.** This week, one thing. When the slump hits, say quietly: 'This is the afternoon tide.' Then stand up, drink a full glass of water slowly, and go to a window or outside for two minutes. Look at one plain thing, a tree or your own hand, and say one sentence: 'The whole earth is full of Him, and He's right here.' Three or four minutes. That's the whole practice. You don't need to believe the sentence yet. Treat it as an experiment.

> *Mashpia's note:* [Stage 1. Z58 body first; T405 the log that won't catch; Z137b service in smallness; T377 heaviness mid-business is not true information. The 'experiment' framing respects his unbelief (MAP S12: no forced seeing).]

**Person.** ok but how do I feel that. Like what am I supposed to experience. Guide me through it in detail.

**Guide.** Fair. Here it is, step by step, with what usually happens. Stand up. Your legs will feel heavy; that's fine, it's the point. Drink the water slowly. Notice the cold going down, and that your body takes it in without you running anything. At the window, pick one thing, say the tree. Don't try to see anything special. Look at it exactly the way you'd look at a tree. Now ask one question: is this tree here because it was put here once, or because it's being kept here right now, this second? Hold the question for three breaths. Don't answer it. Then say the sentence, slowly, once. What most people feel is small: a slight loosening, a breath that goes deeper on its own, a sense that the afternoon isn't only your problem. Sometimes nothing. Nothing still counts. The sign it's working isn't a feeling in the moment. It's that you go back to your desk a bit quicker and skip the 'I'm lazy' speech.

**Person.** Prove it though. I'm a regular American. Why would I think anything is 'keeping' a tree there? It just grows.

**Guide.** I won't pretend to prove it like a lab result; the deepest part of this is honestly beyond proof. But here's the reasoning, and you can check each step. When a carpenter builds a table and goes home, the table stays. It was made from wood that already existed; he only changed its shape. Most of us picture the world like that table: made once, now running on its own. But nobody ever made existence out of something. There was nothing to shape. So existence is less like the table and more like a wall of water held up by wind. The second the wind stops, the water falls. On that picture, the tree isn't 'just there'. It's being given its being, every instant. Science tells you beautifully how the tree grows. It doesn't tell you why there's anything at all, this second, rather than nothing. You don't have to agree yet. Just notice that 'it's just there' is also a belief, and not an obviously better one.

This is the key move: the heaviness stops being your private failure and becomes the thing you pray about. Five, four minutes: a short set prayer, slowly. Jews have had an afternoon prayer for thousands of years, said exactly at this hour, in the middle of the day's business. I can show you the first few lines; say just the first one with full attention. Six: within ten minutes, do the next task right away. What should change, over two to three weeks: not a high. The slump gets shorter. The 'I'm lazy' voice gets quieter. You come back to work quicker. Some days there's a sigh in step three, and that's a good sign. If you miss a day, no guilt, same time tomorrow. And this week, book a checkup and get to bed thirty minutes earlier. That's part of the method, not an extra.

## P1-02 · Anxiety and worry

**Presenting.** I can't stop worrying. Money, the kids, my health. I lie awake running through everything. If I let go for a second, it all falls apart.

## Formulation

- **In their words.** "I can't stop worrying. I lie awake running through everything. If I don't handle it, nobody will."
- **Where in the soul.** The animal soul's fire. The Alter Rebbe: the fretting worry comes 'from the element of fire and heat in the animal soul' (LT4036). The faculty that is overworked is binah, which builds pictures and runs them forward into futures, in the garment of thought. The fear that is meant to stand before Him is spent on outcomes instead. The body carries it: shallow breath, a clenched jaw, broken sleep. That is why the treatment goes through the breath, the hands and the bed. Level: nefesh and ruach. The thought grips and keeps coming back on its own, but in the wrong direction. (Stated: LT4036. The faculty reading is ours.)
- **State now.** S02 Holding it all up. At night it often slides toward S20 if there is panic or no sleep.
- **Root.** Yeshus in its plainest form: 'I am the one keeping my life in being.' This is the smith's picture of reality, applied to oneself. The vessel, once made, stands on its own, and so my life stands only as long as I hold it (T792). The sources answer not 'stop caring' but 'you were never the one holding it'. Being is given at every moment (T785, T1144). (Stated: T792, T785, T1144. Reading worry as this form of yeshus is ours, following MAP S02.)
- **What is blocked.** Da'as of being given right now (S65) is missing. So trust (bitachon, S84) has nothing to stand on. Fear (yirah) is pointed at outcomes, not at Him. The Baal Shem Tov's line is the reverse: 'I trust and fear only Him' (Z137e). Sleep is blocked, which feeds the loop.
- **State needed.** S65 Given right now, then S84 Trust, carried into S81 The deed in the hand. Do the practical step, and leave the holding to Him.
- **Kind of healing.** Birur first: sort the practical concern (which needs a step and maybe a person) from the 'holding-up' (which needs letting go). Then bittul of the first grade only: the holding self is set down for this moment, through one fact from their own body. This comes out as bitachon. Kabbalas ol does the practical step without the fire. Not simcha by command, and never 'worry is a lack of faith' (MAP S02). (Stated: T376, T1144, Z137e, KH837. Arrangement ours.)

## 1. Sort the step from the holding (days 1-7)

**Aim.** Separate what needs action from what needs releasing, so the real problem gets a real step and the rest loses its fuel.

**Practice.** When: once a day, early evening, never in bed. How long: 10 minutes, on paper. Steps. (1) Write each worry as one line. (2) Next to each, write the one next practical step, or 'none possible today'. (3) Do or schedule the steps that exist. If it is money or health, the step usually includes a person: a doctor, an advisor, a call (D07). (4) For each line, say out loud: 'I've done my part for today.' The Mitteler Rebbe's model: do the business with your hands, only what is needed, and 'the whole depth of his heart is not given over to his business' (KH837). Body: do the sorting sitting at a table with both feet on the floor and a glass of water. Then a 10-minute walk, so the body learns the worry was handled and finished, not carried. (Stated: KH837. The written sort and the walk are ours.)

**Signs of progress.** Fewer lines each evening. The real problems have moving steps. The night loop is a little shorter.

**If stuck.** If every line feels urgent, pick only one to act on today. If a worry is an emergency (danger, abuse, a medical red flag), deal with it now with a person, and drop the practice for the day.

**Move on when.** They can tell 'a step' from 'holding it up' without the paper.

**The guide says** *(ours, 106 words)*. "Let's start by sorting, not calming. This evening, at a table, not in bed, write each worry as one line. Next to each one, write the next practical step, or 'nothing I can do today'. Do or schedule the steps that exist. If it's about money or health, one step is usually a call to a real person who can help. Then, for each line, say out loud: 'I've done my part for today.' That's the whole first week. You're teaching yourself the difference between handling something and holding it up. Afterwards, take a ten-minute walk, so your body knows the work is done for today."

> **Sources.**
> «ולא יהיה כל עומק לבבו במסירת נפשו במשא ומתן», *and the whole depth of his heart is not given over to his business* (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 837)

## 2. The beat you don't make: given right now (weeks 2-3)

**Aim.** One felt fact that undoes the holding: you are being given this moment; you are not producing it.

**Practice.** When: once a day at a calm hour, and again briefly when the worry spikes. How long: 8 minutes. Steps. (1) Sit. Feet down. Hands open on the knees. (2) Breath, 2 minutes: notice the breath leaving and returning by itself. The Alter Rebbe: with every breath there is a withdrawing and a spreading out (LT2256). You have never once run it. (3) The reasoning, 3 minutes. A man makes a vessel, walks away, and the vessel stays. But the heavens are made from nothing, so their being is held up the way the wind held up the wall of the split sea. If it stopped for an instant, the water would fall (T792, T794). 'If the letters withdrew for an instant, all the heavens would be truly nothing' (T785). The same holds for your child asleep, your income this month, and you. (4) The Tanya's turn, 2 minutes: 'Let him picture his own coming into being from nothing at every single moment: how can he think it is bad for him?' (T1144). Limit: this does not say nothing hard will happen. It says you are not the one holding the world up. 'All is utterly good, only it is not grasped' (T1150) is said quietly to yourself, never to someone else about their pain. (5) Close: name the worry, and say, 'You are holding this. I'll do my part tomorrow.' Mashal: the wall of water and the wind. Its limit: the sea wall was a miracle against nature. Here 'nature' itself is the wind. (Stated: LT2256, T792, T794, T785, T1144, T1150. Timing and wording ours.)

**Signs of progress.** The body lets go a little: a longer out-breath, a dropped shoulder. A moment of 'oh, I'm not running this'. The worry is still there, but lighter in the hands.

**If stuck.** If attention to the breath or the pulse raises anxiety (panic, health anxiety), drop it. Use the hand on the table instead, or a plain object: 'Who keeps this here now?' If the reasoning stirs an argument, do stage 2 as a question only, with no answer needed.

**Move on when.** They can call up 'given right now' in under a minute when the worry spikes, and it helps more often than not.

**The guide says** *(ours, 119 words)*. "Now one fact from your own body. Sit, feet down, hands open. For two minutes, notice your breath going out and coming back without you running it. You've never once made it happen. Then think about this. When someone builds a table and goes home, the table stays. But nobody built existence out of anything. It's held, the way a strong wind could hold up a wall of water. If the wind stops, the water falls. You, your kids, this month's bills: all held, this second. Now ask yourself honestly: if you're being given this moment, fresh, right now, who's really holding it all up? Then name your worry and say, 'You're holding this. I'll do my part tomorrow.'"

> **Sources.**
> «וכך הוא בכל רגע על כל נשימה ונשימה יש הסתלקות והתפשטות», *and so it is every moment: with every single breath there is a withdrawing and a spreading out* (Likkutei Torah, Chukas; line 2256)
> «כי כאשר יצא לצורף כלי – שוב אין הכלי צריך לידי הצורף», *once the smith has made a vessel, the vessel no longer needs the smith's hands* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2; line 792)
> «ואילו הפסיק ה׳ את הרוח כרגע – היו המים חוזרים וניגרים במורד כדרכם וטבעם», *had G-d stopped the wind for an instant, the water would have run back downhill as is its nature* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2; line 794)
> «כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש», *if the letters withdrew for an instant and went back to their source, all the heavens would be truly nothing* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1; line 785)

> «הכל טוב בתכלית רק שאינו מושג», *all is utterly good, only it is not grasped* (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 11; line 1150)

## 3. Fear in its place: hands working, heart trusting (weeks 3-5)

**Aim.** Point the fear back to its right object, and act without the fire.

**Practice.** When: through the day, at each spike. How long: 30 seconds, plus the act. Steps. (1) Notice the spike in the body: chest, jaw, breath. (2) Say the Baal Shem Tov's sentence: 'I trust and fear only Him' (Z137e). He describes the same person being glad and knowing He is near and guarding him. (3) Ask: 'Is there a step now?' If yes, do it with the hands, quickly and plainly (M57). If no, put it on tomorrow's list. (4) Drink water, unclench the jaw, and go on. Anchor: before morning prayer, or before starting the workday, give a coin to charity. Giving from your own earnings loosens the grip on them (M34). (Stated: Z137e, M57 basis KH837 and LT4036. The spike routine is ours.)

**Signs of progress.** The spikes are shorter. Action happens sooner and with less dread. Real problems are handled better, not worse.

**If stuck.** If the spikes are constant and physical (racing heart, can't eat, can't work), this is beyond a practice. A doctor or therapist, now (guards).

**Move on when.** The sentence comes to them by itself at a spike.

**The guide says** *(ours, 102 words)*. "When worry spikes during the day, use a thirty-second routine. Notice where it is in your body: the chest, the jaw, the breath. Say one sentence: 'I trust and fear only Him.' Then ask: is there something I can actually do right now? If yes, do it with your hands, quickly and plainly. If no, it goes on tomorrow's list. Drink some water, unclench your jaw, and go on. One more thing: each morning, give a coin or a small donation before you start work. Giving a little from what you earn loosens the grip that says it's all up to you."

> **Sources.**
> «ואין אני בוטח ומתירא רק ממנו ית'», *I trust and fear only Him* (Tzava'at HaRivash 137; line 287)
> «ולא יהיה כל עומק לבבו במסירת נפשו במשא ומתן», *and the whole depth of his heart is not given over to his business* (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 837)
> «אבל הדאגה שאדם מיצר ודואג על זה הנה הוא מיסוד האש והחמימות שבנפשו הבהמית», *but the worry a person frets with comes from the element of fire and heat in his animal soul* (Likkutei Torah, Sukkot; line 4036)

## 4. Handing it over at night (from week 2, ongoing)

**Aim.** Sleep as the nightly practice of letting go. The body learns trust in the bed.

**Practice.** When: at bedtime, every night. How long: 5 minutes. The reasoning, which is stated: the Rashab points out that every night you set the worry down so you can sleep. You can already do it for the body (KHT88b). Steps. (1) Screens off 30 minutes before. (2) Write tomorrow's one step, so the mind has nothing to guard. (3) For a Jew, the bedtime Shema. For anyone, the verse 'Into Your hand I entrust my spirit'. Say it slowly, as a real handing-over: 'I'm going to stop holding for eight hours, and everything will be held anyway.' (4) Breath: three long out-breaths. (5) On waking: 'I thank You' (Modeh Ani), you were given back (HY84a). Body: a set bedtime is the vessel. Short sleep makes every worry louder. (Stated: KHT88b, HY84a. The rest is ours.)

**Signs of progress.** They fall asleep sooner. Fewer 4 a.m. runs. Waking with 'thank you' before the list.

**If stuck.** If they lie awake more than 20 minutes, get up, write the worry down, say the verse again, and go back to bed. Ongoing insomnia: a doctor.

**The guide says** *(ours, 104 words)*. "Bedtime is practice too. Every night you already set your worries down so you can sleep. Let's do it on purpose. Screens off half an hour before bed. Write tomorrow's one step, so your mind doesn't have to stand guard. Then say slowly, 'Into Your hand I entrust my spirit', and mean it: for eight hours you'll stop holding, and everything will be held anyway. Take three long breaths out. In the morning, before the list, say thank you for being given back. If you're awake after twenty minutes, get up, write the worry down, say the line again, and go back to bed."

> **Sources.**
> «הלא בעניניו הגשמיים כמו בשינה», *look: in his bodily affairs, as in sleep (he sets the worry down and sleeps)* (Kuntres HaTefillah 11 (the Rashab); line 88, OCR)
> «דער סדר פון טאג הויבט זיך אן מיט מודה אני», *the order of the day begins with Modeh Ani ('I thank You')* (Hayom Yom, 11 Shevat; line 84)

**Daily shape.** Morning: 'I thank You'. A coin to charity, then the workday or morning prayer. Day: the 30-second spike routine as needed, plus real practical steps. Early evening: the 10-minute sort (week 1), then the 8-minute 'given right now' sitting (from week 2), and a short walk. Night: the 5-minute handing-over. About 25 minutes in total.

**Duration.** 4-6 weeks to a noticeable change in the night loop. Worry is a lifelong trait for some temperaments. The aim is that it stops running the day, not that it never comes. Real problems stay real. The practice makes you better at them, not free of them.

**Guards.** Real money, health or safety problems need a practical action and a person. Never a spiritual reframe in place of one (D07). Never say or imply that worry is a lack of faith (MAP S02). Panic attacks, generalized anxiety, OCD-type checking, or health anxiety need professional care. The pulse and breath exercises are not for them. Never use 'all is good' about someone else's suffering (D03). Never promise an outcome. Sleeplessness with racing thoughts and grand certainty is the danger line (S20, S21).

## P1-03 · Numbness: feeling nothing

**Presenting.** I don't feel anything. I pray, I read, I hear something beautiful, and nothing lands. Not sad, not anything.

## Formulation

- **In their words.** "I don't feel anything. Nothing lands. I go through the motions."
- **Where in the soul.** The G-dly soul is whole, but its light does not reach the heart. In the Tanya's words, the soul's light does not shine enough to rule the coarseness of the body and heart, even when the person understands (T405, T407). Faculty: da'as, the binding that turns knowing into feeling, does not hold (KHT61). The love sits hidden, an inheritance in every heart (T272). Level: neshamah, the light that reaches the heart, is closed off. Yechidah is untouched. That is where the way back starts. The body matters here too. Exhaustion, poor sleep and some medicines flatten feeling, and the stone heart is easily confused with depression. (Stated: T405, T407, KHT61, T272.)
- **State now.** S03 Numb, the stone heart (timtum halev). Check it against S04 Low and heavy and against depression. It sits close to S16 Knowing it, not feeling it.
- **Root.** Yeshus as coarseness: 'the cause is the coarseness of the husk, which lifts itself up over the light of the G-dly soul' (T407). Often there is a second form. The self demands to feel, and measures the service by its own feeling. The Mitteler Rebbe calls making the arousal the point 'serving oneself' (KH167). (Stated: T407, KH167. Pairing them is ours.)
- **What is blocked.** Timtum halev: the heart 'has become like stone, and he cannot open his heart in any way' (T403). It is not atzvus. There is no despair, only absence (BMT46). It is not merirus, which is alive (T452b). Da'as is not binding. The mouth and the deed are still open.
- **State needed.** S81 The deed in the hand and S55 Own words to Him first, then S41 The hidden love wakes, and S29 Good thought joined to deed.
- **Kind of healing.** Kabbalas ol: serve without feeling, because the deed counts as if done with love (T257, T668). Then iskafya in the Tanya's specific form for the stone heart: 'striking the log' at set times, a humbling look that ends in joy (T405, T455). Only if the person is not low or depressed. Then the hidden love is woken by its own test (T232, T286). Teshuvah only in the gentle sense of turning toward. Never self-punishment.

## 1. Sleep, not death: stop measuring by feeling (week 1)

**Aim.** Take the verdict out of the numbness and keep the deeds going.

**Practice.** When: every day. How long: one deed, plus 2 minutes. The reasoning, which is stated: the Mitteler Rebbe calls this state sleep. A sleeper is not dead; 'when one wakes him he is awake at once and alive exactly as before' (KH10). 'Let him not say it is hopeless, for the very nature of his G-dly soul is to be moved always' (KH19). The Tanya: if he cannot bring the love into his heart, he can still do the deed for its own sake (T668), and it counts 'as if done with awe and love in his revealed heart' (T257). Steps. (1) Choose one plain good deed for the day: a call to a parent, a coin given, a blessing said. (2) Do it precisely and on time, without checking how it feels. (3) Afterward, one sentence: 'Done, whether I felt it or not.' Body: a sleeper wakes faster when the body is rested. Keep a set bedtime this week, and get daylight in the morning. (Stated: KH10, KH19, T668, T257. The one-deed form is ours.)

**Signs of progress.** Less self-checking ('do I feel anything?'). The deed is done without drama.

**If stuck.** If even one deed feels pointless, make it smaller and physical: put a coin in a box, light a candle. Mixed is not false (BMT247).

**Move on when.** A week of daily deeds without measuring them.

**The guide says** *(ours, 100 words)*. "Feeling nothing isn't the same as having nothing. It's more like sleep than like death: someone sleeping is fully alive, they just aren't awake yet. So this week, stop measuring by feeling. Pick one plain good deed a day: call your mother, give a coin, say one blessing. Do it carefully and on time, and don't check how it felt. Afterwards, say: 'Done, whether I felt it or not.' A deed done without feeling still counts, fully. And help the sleeper: a set bedtime this week, and some daylight in the morning. A tired body keeps the heart asleep longer."

> **Sources.**
> «שהרי כשיקיצנו אותו מיד נעור וחי כמקדם ממש», *for when one wakes him he is awake at once and alive exactly as before* (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 10)
> «לא יאמר נואש לנפשו כי עצם מהות טבעיות נשמתו ורוחו האלקית להתפעל תמיד», *let him not say 'it is hopeless', for the very nature of his G-dly soul is to be moved always* (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 19)
> «אך אם אינו יכול להוציאה אל הגילוי בלבו, אףעלפיכן, יכול לעסוק בתורה ומצות לשמן», *if he cannot bring it out into his heart, even so he can do Torah and mitzvot for their own sake* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 44; line 668)
> «כאלו עסק בהם בדחילו ורחימו ממש אשר בהתגלות לבו», *as if he had done them with awe and love in his revealed heart* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 16; line 257)
> «כי בין עשרה התלהבויות רמיות תמצא אחת אמתית», *among ten deceptive fervours you will find one true one* (Bnei Machshava Tova (the Piaseczner); line 247)

## 2. The mouth before the heart (weeks 2-3)

**Aim.** Open the garment that is still open, speech, so the heart can follow.

**Practice.** When: once a day, morning or evening. How long: 5 minutes. Steps. (1) Choose one line: one line of prayer, or a plain sentence like 'You are here, and I am here.' (2) Say it in a low voice, with all your strength, word by word. The Baal Shem Tov: say the words 'with all his strength, as it says, all my bones shall say it' (Z33). Let the body join: stand, or sway gently. The Baal Shem Tov's order is body first, so the soul's power can shine (Z58). (3) Then one minute of your own words to Him: 'I feel nothing. I'm here anyway.' (4) If you cannot pray, the Baal Shem Tov: 'let him not believe that today he surely will not pray' (Z72a). When you can't pray with intent, there is extra guard around the King, which means the King is here (Z72c). Mashal: the guard around the King. Limit: it does not explain the numbness. It only says that the numbness is no proof He is absent. (Stated: Z33, Z58, Z72a, Z72c.)

**Signs of progress.** A sigh. A crack: a moment of caring, a tear, a 'huh'. The Piaseczner: when the heart seems sealed, in truth even now it is full of holiness (DM1240).

**If stuck.** Keep the line the same for a week, and don't swap it for a better one. Add a slow, wordless tune if they have one. Melody reaches the animal soul without asking for a feeling (M26).

**Move on when.** At least one crack has happened, or two weeks have passed with the practice kept.

**The guide says** *(ours, 109 words)*. "The heart is closed for now, but your mouth isn't, so we'll start there. Once a day, choose one line. It can be a line of prayer, or just 'You're here, and I'm here.' Stand up. Say it in a low voice, slowly, word by word, with all your strength, as if your whole body were saying it. Then one minute in your own words: 'I feel nothing. I'm here anyway.' Keep the same line all week. Some days it will feel like nothing; that's fine. When you can't get through to someone important, it's often because there's a guard at the door. A guard means someone is there."

> **Sources.**
> «ויאמר הדבר בין בזמירות בין בלימוד בכל כחו כדכתיב כל עצמותי תאמרנה», *let him say the words, in the songs or in study, with all his strength, as it says 'all my bones shall say it'* (Tzava'at HaRivash 33; line 57)
> «וצריך לעורר עצמו בתחלה בגוף שלו בכל כחו כדי שתאיר בו כח הנשמה», *first he must rouse himself with his body, with all his strength, so the power of the soul can shine in him* (Tzava'at HaRivash 58; line 111)
> «לפעמים אם אינו יכול להתפלל לא יאמין שהיום בודאי לא יתפלל», *sometimes, if he cannot pray, let him not believe that today he surely will not pray* (Tzava'at HaRivash 72; line 128)
> «וכן כשאין יכול להתפלל בכוונה ידע שיש שם שמירה יתירה סביב המלך יש כאן המלך», *when he cannot pray with intent, let him know there is extra guard around the King: the King is here* (Tzava'at HaRivash 72; line 130)
> «ונדמה לו שלבו אטום. ובאמת גם עתה מלא קדושה הוא», *it seems to him his heart is sealed; in truth even now it is full of holiness* (Derekh HaMelekh (the Piaseczner); line 1240)

## 3. Strike the log, then joy, at a set time only (weeks 3-5; skip if low)

**Aim.** The Tanya's own remedy for the stone heart: a brief, honest humbling of the coarse self, then real joy.

**Practice.** When: once or twice a week, at a set quiet time. Never daily, and never late at night. How long: 10 minutes, with the last 3 for joy. The reasoning, which is stated: the advice in the Zohar is that 'a log the flame won't catch, you strike it' (T405). The cause is the coarseness that lifts itself over the soul's light (T407). The striking is at set times (T405 context). Then the Tanya turns at once to joy. The coarse self 'is only me, the body with the life-soul in it', and the G-dly soul is something else (T455). Steps. (1) Name, without drama, where the self has been the center this week, in two or three plain facts. (2) Say: 'That's the coarse part. It is not the whole of me.' (3) Turn: 'And the soul in me is a part of Him, untouched.' (4) Three minutes of gladness: you can still come close. Close with a quick deed. Body: do it sitting up, by daylight if possible. Food and sleep come first. This is not done tired or hungry. (Stated: T405, T407, T455. The 10-minute form is ours.)

**Signs of progress.** A broken-but-alive feeling (merirus, T452b), followed by lightness. The stone has turned into something that can hurt, and that is progress.

**If stuck.** If it turns into self-hatred, or leaves them lower afterward, stop this stage for good and stay with stages 1, 2 and 4.

**Move on when.** After 2-4 sittings that ended in lightness.

**The guide says** *(ours, 106 words)*. "Once or twice this week, at a set quiet time, never late at night and never when you're feeling low, take ten minutes. Sit up straight. Name two or three plain facts from this week where you put yourself at the center. No drama. Then say: 'That's the coarse part of me. It isn't all of me.' Then turn: 'There's a part of me that's from Him, and it's untouched.' Spend the last three minutes being glad that you can still come close, and do one small deed right after. If this ever leaves you lower instead of lighter, we stop it and keep the rest."

> **Sources.**
> «אעא דלא סליק ביה נהורא – מבטשין ליה», *a log that the flame won't catch: you strike it (and it catches)* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 29 (quoting the Zohar); line 405)
> «והסיבה – היא גסות הקליפה, שמגביה עצמה על אור קדושת נפש האלהית», *the cause is the coarseness of the husk, which lifts itself up over the light of the G-dly soul* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 29; line 407)
> «אך כל זה הוא אני לבדי, הוא הגוף עם נפש החיונית שבו», *but all this is only me: the body with the life-soul in it* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31; line 455)
> «אבל מרירות ולב נשבר, אדרבה, הרי יש חיות בלבו להתפעל ולהתמרמר», *but bitterness and a broken heart: on the contrary, there is life in the heart, to be moved and pained* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31; line 452)

## 4. The hidden love: its own test (weeks 4-6)

**Aim.** Find the love that is already there, under feeling. Not make a new one.

**Practice.** When: 3 times a week, 10 minutes. The reasoning, which is stated: the Tanya says there is a hidden love in every heart, an inheritance (T272). It is like a candle flame that always flickers upward by its nature (T286). Even the lightest of the light can give their life for the holiness of G-d (T232). On Yom Kippur even those with no preparation weep, 'not through preparation and not in proportion to their deeds' (SO195). Steps. (1) Ask: is there anything you would refuse to do even if you were paid well? Deny who you are, betray the people you love, say a lie about G-d out loud? (2) Notice the refusal. It isn't a feeling. It's a plain 'no' from somewhere deep. (3) Say: 'That no is the love. It was there the whole time.' (4) Picture the candle flame. It reaches up even when nobody is watching. (5) Close with one deed done from that 'no', for example keeping a promise. Mashal: the candle flame. Limit: a flame can be smothered. The point is only that its direction never changes. (Stated: T272, T286, T232, SO195. The 'refusal' test as a modern exercise is ours, built on T232.)

**Signs of progress.** A quiet 'oh'. A sense of being more than the numbness. Steadier deeds.

**If stuck.** If no refusal is found, the guide does not press. Go back to stages 1-2. The rope's top end is tied above even when the bottom feels nothing (DC56).

**The guide says** *(ours, 107 words)*. "Here's a test for a love you may not know you have. Ask yourself honestly: is there anything you'd refuse to do even if someone paid you well? Deny who you are? Betray the people you love? Say out loud that there's nothing true and good in the world? Notice the refusal. It isn't a feeling. It's a plain no from deep down. That no is the love, and it was there the whole time, like a candle flame that keeps reaching up even when nobody's watching. Sit with that for a few minutes. Then do one thing today because of that no, like keeping a promise."

> **Sources.**
> «שהיא ״אהבה מסותרת״ שבלב כללות ישראל, שהיא ירושה לנו מאבותינו», *the hidden love in the heart of all Israel, an inheritance from our fathers* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 18; line 272)
> «כאור הנר שמתנענע תמיד למעלה בטבעו», *like a candle flame that always flickers upward by its nature* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 19; line 286)
> «ואפילו קל שבקלים יכול למסור נפשו על קדושת ה׳», *even the lightest of the light can give his life for the holiness of G-d* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 14; line 232)
> «שלא ע"י הכנה ולא לפי ערך מעשיהם», *(they weep on Yom Kippur) not through preparation and not in proportion to their deeds* (Sha'arei Orah (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 195)
> «כמשל המניע לחבל בראשו ינוע קצהו התחתון ממילא מאיליו», *like one who moves a rope at its head: its lower end moves by itself* (Derech Chaim (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 56, OCR)

**Daily shape.** Morning: daylight, 'I thank You', and the one line said low with full strength (stage 2). Day: the one plain deed, done precisely. Evening: one minute of own words. Set bedtime. Weekly: stage 3 once or twice (if fit), and stage 4 three times. About 10-15 minutes a day.

**Duration.** Weeks to months. The sources say a person may stay numb for a long time and that waking often comes suddenly, at a time of favour, not on schedule (MR1-C01, DC95). The protocol keeps the vessel ready; it cannot produce the waking. Honest expectation: the first crack within 2-6 weeks for many people; steadiness in months.

**Guards.** Never read depression as a closed heart (MAP S03 safety). If the numbness covers everything (food, people, pleasure), has lasted weeks, or comes with hopelessness, or started with a new medication: a doctor and a person. Stage 3 is never for someone low, depressed or self-punishing (PS-18 guard). Never ask 'how does it feel?' over and over (D25). Never demand a feeling. Exhaustion and burnout make the heart numb. Sleep and rest come first, as part of the path.

## P1-04 · Dry, mechanical prayer

**Presenting.** My prayer is mechanical. I race through the words and my mind is somewhere else. It's a chore I check off.

## Formulation

- **In their words.** "I say the words fast and my mind's elsewhere. It's a checklist."
- **Where in the soul.** The garment of speech is running without the garment of thought. The Mitteler Rebbe: 'his prayer is in his mouth, from the lips outward, and his heart is wholly far away' (DC145). Faculty: da'as. No contemplation came first, so nothing in the mind is there to come down into the heart. Prayer is meant to bring 'the mind's grasp into the heart's feeling, and both together into deed' (HY568). Level: nefesh is intact; they still show up and say the words, and that is real. Ruach is missing. The body is part of it: prayer done slumped, rushed, on an empty stomach, or with a phone face-up. (Stated: DC145, HY568.)
- **State now.** S16 Knowing it, not feeling it, in the setting of prayer, close to S03 Numb. The flat moment inside a sitting is S26 It goes flat.
- **Root.** Yeshus as hurry: my time, my day. Prayer becomes a task the self gets through, not a standing before Someone. The Tanya's first instruction before prayer answers exactly this: 'G-d stands over him... and He looks at him' (T600). (Stated: T600. The 'hurry' reading is ours.)
- **What is blocked.** Hachanah, the preparation. They come straight in from the street (KHT88a). Hesech hada'as during the words. Stray thoughts are fought or followed, never let go (T394). No detail: the Rashab says contemplation has to be at length and in detail (KHT85).
- **State needed.** S33 Before Whom, then S80 Prayer: mind into heart. S55 Own words to Him at the end, and S29 Good thought joined to deed.
- **Kind of healing.** Kabbalas ol at the start: stand before the King first (T600). Birur of attention through one detail contemplated (KHT85). Simcha in the saying itself: the Baal Shem Tov speaks of praying with joy and with all one's strength (Z33, Z51). Not more speed, and not guilt.

## 1. Before Whom: sixty seconds at the door (week 1)

**Aim.** Change who you are talking to before changing how you talk.

**Practice.** When: before every prayer or set prayer time. How long: 60 seconds. Steps. (1) Don't start the moment you come in (KHT88a). Put the phone in another room. Stand with your feet together. (2) Put down what you came in carrying, the way you do every night to sleep (KHT88b), so that 'heart and mind be free to receive' (KH6). (3) Say inwardly the Tanya's opening for prayer: 'G-d stands over me. The whole earth is full of His glory. He is looking at me' (T600). (4) Then begin, and say the first line slower than all the rest. Body: stand straight, don't slump on a couch, and don't pray on an empty stomach if that makes you rush. The body's posture is the first word of the prayer. (Stated: KHT88a-b, T600. The 60-second form is ours.)

**Signs of progress.** The first line is said like a sentence to Someone. A felt shift between 'before' and 'after' the minute.

**If stuck.** If the minute itself becomes mechanical, change one physical thing: face a wall, close your eyes, or put a hand on your heart.

**Move on when.** The minute happens on its own most days.

**The guide says** *(ours, 109 words)*. "Before you change how you pray, change who you're talking to. Starting today, give yourself one minute at the door. Don't start right after walking in. Put your phone in another room. Stand with your feet together. Set down whatever you came in carrying, the way you set it down every night to sleep. Then say quietly: 'He's standing right here, and He's looking at me.' Take that in for a few breaths. Then start, and say the first line slower than everything else. Stand up straight; how your body is standing is part of the prayer. That's the whole change this week. One minute, then one slow line."

> **Sources.**

> «הלא בעניניו הגשמיים כמו בשינה», *look: in his bodily affairs, as in sleep (he sets the worry down and sleeps)* (Kuntres HaTefillah 11 (the Rashab); line 88, OCR)

## 2. One part, at full strength (weeks 2-3)

**Aim.** Not the whole prayer slowed down, which nobody sustains, but one part fully alive.

**Practice.** When: in the daily prayer. How long: 3-5 extra minutes. Steps. (1) Choose one part: the first line of Shema, the first blessing of the standing prayer, or for someone outside Jewish practice one psalm. (2) Say it in a low voice, word by word, with all your strength. The Baal Shem Tov: 'with all his strength... all my bones shall say it'. Pray in a low voice, and if you cry out, cry in a whisper (Z33). (3) Stray thoughts: don't fight them and don't follow them. Turn from them at once (T394). Arguing with them is like wrestling a filthy man, and you get dirty too (T401a). The Baal Shem Tov: train yourself at least to begin without one (Z19). (4) The rest of the prayer at your normal pace, with no guilt. Mashal: the halls of the palace. At each door a stray thought is checked and sent away, and you walk on (Z87). Limit: there is no perfect prayer. Getting in is the point, not a spotless record. (Stated: Z33, T394, T401a, Z19, Z87.)

**Signs of progress.** That part has become 'theirs'. Some days the words land. Fewer fights with stray thoughts.

**If stuck.** If they can't pray with intent: the guard around the King means the King is here (Z72c). Don't believe today is lost (Z72a). Say just the one line again.

**Move on when.** Two weeks with the one part kept alive.

**The guide says** *(ours, 114 words)*. "Don't try to slow down the whole prayer. Nobody keeps that up. Pick one part: one line or one short paragraph. Say that part in a low voice, word by word, with all your strength, as if your whole body were saying it. When other thoughts come in, don't fight them and don't follow them. Just come back to the next word. Fighting them only gives them more hold. Think of walking through a palace: at every door some thought tries to come along, it gets turned back, and you keep walking. Say the rest at your normal pace, with no guilt. One part fully alive is worth more than the whole thing half-there."

> **Sources.**
> «ויאמר הדבר בין בזמירות בין בלימוד בכל כחו כדכתיב כל עצמותי תאמרנה», *let him say the words, in the songs or in study, with all his strength, as it says 'all my bones shall say it'* (Tzava'at HaRivash 33; line 57)
> «אל ישית לב אליהן, אלא יסיח דעתו מהן כרגע», *let him pay them no attention, but turn his mind from them at once* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27; line 394)

> «וכן כשאין יכול להתפלל בכוונה ידע שיש שם שמירה יתירה סביב המלך יש כאן המלך», *when he cannot pray with intent, let him know there is extra guard around the King: the King is here* (Tzava'at HaRivash 72; line 130)
> «לפעמים אם אינו יכול להתפלל לא יאמין שהיום בודאי לא יתפלל», *sometimes, if he cannot pray, let him not believe that today he surely will not pray* (Tzava'at HaRivash 72; line 128)

## 3. Feed it: one idea in detail before you pray (weeks 3-6)

**Aim.** Give the mind something real to bring into the heart, so the words carry content.

**Practice.** When: 5-10 minutes before prayer, 3-5 times a week. The reasoning, which is stated: the Rashab says the cold prayer comes from contemplation that is too general. He asks for contemplation 'at length, in detail specifically' (KHT85). He also says feeling is what da'as is: a person can understand well and still not feel (KHT61). Steps. (1) Take one idea the prayer itself contains. For example, from the morning blessings: He 'renews in His goodness, every day, always, the work of creation'. (2) Go into detail. This morning's light. This morning's breath. The person you love woke up. Each is renewed, not left over from yesterday (T785). (3) Stay with one detail until it is clear, not just heard. (4) Then pray, and when you reach those words, slow down. Body: do this after drinking water and with a little food if you need it. R. Aharon of Strashelye: from the blood made by eating, the heart is fired in Shema and prayer (SA6). (Stated: KHT85, KHT61, T785, SA6. The choice of example is ours.)

**Signs of progress.** The prayer has 'places' in it now. The idea comes back during the day by itself (S25).

**If stuck.** If the detailed version leaves them colder than the general one, the Rashab names this too: the person is not yet a vessel. Keep it shorter and lighter, and keep stage 2.

**Move on when.** Ongoing. Rotate ideas every week or two.

**The guide says** *(ours, 106 words)*. "A prayer goes dry when there's nothing in the mind for it to carry. So a few mornings a week, take five or ten minutes before you pray. Pick one idea the prayer actually says, for example that the world is renewed fresh every day. Then go into detail: this morning's light, your breath this morning, the person you love waking up. Not left over from yesterday; given today. Stay with one detail until it's clear, not just heard. Have some water and a bite first if you need it. Then pray, and when you reach those words, slow down. They'll have something in them now."

> **Sources.**
> «יתבונן באריכות ההתבוננו" בפרטיות דוקא», *let him contemplate at length, in detail specifically* (Kuntres HaTefillah (the Rashab); line 85, OCR)
> «וההרגשה זהו"ע הדעת, כי יכול להיות שמבין את הדבר היטב ומ"מ אינו במורגש אצלו», *feeling is what da'as is: a person can understand a thing well and still not feel it* (Kuntres HaTefillah (the Rashab); line 61, OCR)
> «כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש», *if the letters withdrew for an instant and went back to their source, all the heavens would be truly nothing* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1; line 785)
> «ומהדם מתלהב לבו להוי"ה בקריאת שמע ותפלה», *and from the blood his heart is fired for G-d in Shema and prayer* (Sha'arei Avodah (R. Aharon of Strashelye); line 6)

## 4. Close in your own words, then a deed (from week 2, ongoing)

**Aim.** Carry the prayer out of the room.

**Practice.** When: right after prayer. How long: 2 minutes. Steps. (1) One minute in your own words to Him, in any language. (2) One resolution for today, small and concrete, said out loud. (3) Do it within the hour. The Rebbe's line: prayer brings the mind into the heart, and both together into deed (HY568). (Stated: HY568.)

**Signs of progress.** The day feels connected to the morning.

**If stuck.** If no words come, say: 'Thank You for letting me stand here.'

**The guide says** *(ours, 100 words)*. "Don't leave the prayer in the room. When you finish, take one more minute and talk to Him in your own words, any language, any way. Then say one small, concrete thing you'll do today because of it: call someone, hold back one sharp comment, give something. Say it out loud. Then do it within the hour. That's what turns a prayer from words into something real: the mind brings it to the heart, and the heart brings it to the hands. If no words come, just say 'thank You for letting me stand here', and that's enough for today."

> **Sources.**
> «עבודת התפלה היא המביאה השגת המוח בהרגשת הלב, ושניהם יחד בעבודה בפועל», *prayer brings the mind's grasp into the heart's feeling, and both together into deed* (Hayom Yom; line 568)

**Daily shape.** Before prayer: water, phone away, and on 3-5 days the 5-10 minute detail contemplation. At the door: 60 seconds before Whom. In the prayer: one part at full strength. After: one minute of own words and one resolution. Later in the day: the resolution done. Total added time: 5-15 minutes.

**Duration.** The first change (a living line) often comes in 1-2 weeks. A prayer that has become 'yours' takes months. Dry spells will come back. They are part of the shape (inner-world AR-11).

**Guards.** No guilt about speed or missed days. For people who do not pray in a set form, the protocol uses a psalm or their own words, and never pressures them toward observance. If the dryness is part of a wider numbness or low mood, see P1-03 and the S04 safety rules. Scrupulosity (repeating words over and over because they weren't 'right') is a sign to stop, and possibly OCD. Then a person is needed, and 'full strength' is not for them.

## P1-05 · Scattered, racing mind

**Presenting.** My mind is all over the place. I can't hold one thought for three seconds. When I try to meditate it gets worse.

## Formulation

- **In their words.** "My mind is a train station. I can't focus. Meditating makes it worse."
- **Where in the soul.** The garment of thought, flooded. The Piaseczner names the flood as 'thoughts and wishes, which flood a person without end' (DM1555). This is the animal soul's busyness (tirdah). The Mitteler Rebbe calls the distraction of earning a living 'sleep' (KH12). Faculty: chochmah's quiet point is covered by noise, so da'as cannot bind to anything long enough. Level: nefesh and ruach. The body runs the same speed as the mind: caffeine, screens, no pauses, shallow breath. The body is where the slowing starts. (Stated: DM1555, KH12.)
- **State now.** S01 Scattered.
- **Root.** Yeshus as 'my wants and my managing'. The flood is made of wishes, not only thoughts (DM1555), and the self keeps every plate spinning. Forcing quiet makes it worse. The Piaseczner's student: saying it forcefully 'can only rouse his ego' (DM1556c). (Stated: DM1555, DM1556c.)
- **What is blocked.** No settled mind (yishuv hada'as). The person wrestles the thoughts, which the Tanya says only dirties you (T401a). Stopping is not felt as allowed. The Piaseczner: 'this time of stopping is also service' (DM224).
- **State needed.** S32 Quiet, then S25 The line takes hold. Later S33 Before Whom.
- **Kind of healing.** Quieting (hashkatah), as a road to the first grade of bittul. Birur of thought by not engaging: turn away, don't wrestle (T394, T401b). Then kabbalas ol in the plain act of coming back to the line, again and again. Not iskafya by force.

## 1. Watch, don't fight (week 1)

**Aim.** Let the flood slow by looking at it, not by stopping it.

**Practice.** When: once a day, at a calm time. Morning before the phone is best. How long: 5 minutes. Steps. (1) Sit. Feet down. Put coffee and the phone out of reach. (2) For 3 minutes, ask yourself only: 'What am I thinking?' Notice the thought, then the next, like cars passing. The Piaseczner's student: 'let him begin to look at his thoughts for a short while, a few minutes: what am I thinking?' (DM1556a). (3) 'Then slowly, slowly he will feel his head emptying' (DM1556b). (4) When there is a little room, say one short verse, lightly, not forcefully (DM1556c). For example: 'The whole earth is full of His glory.' Mashal: the sleeper and the fly. The soul's call comes through the noise looking like something else (HA84). Its limit: not every thought is the soul calling. Body: one glass of water before, and coffee after the sitting, not before. (Stated: DM1556a-c, HA84. This is a student's record of the Piaseczner; say so when it matters. Water and coffee timing ours.)

**Signs of progress.** Gaps between thoughts. The thoughts feel less like you and more like traffic.

**If stuck.** A second way in from the same advice: watch the small hand of a clock for a minute (VS-07). If watching thoughts makes things feel unreal or frightening, stop (S20 guard).

**Move on when.** Most days they get a moment of room for the verse.

**The guide says** *(ours, 102 words)*. "Don't try to stop your thoughts. That makes them louder. Tomorrow morning, before your phone and before coffee, drink a glass of water and sit for five minutes. For three of them, just ask yourself: 'What am I thinking right now?' Notice the thought, then the next one, like watching cars go by from a bench. You aren't trying to stop the cars. Little by little, most people find their head starts to empty a bit. When there's some room, say one short line, lightly: 'The whole earth is full of His glory.' Then have your coffee. That's it for this week."

> **Sources.**
> «שיתחיל האיש להביט על מחשבותיו שעה קלה לערך איזה רגעים היינו מה אני חושב», *let him begin to look at his thoughts for a short while, a few minutes: 'what am I thinking?'* (Derekh HaMelekh, quieting advice (a student's record); line 1556)
> «אז ירגיש לאט לאט שראשו מתרוקן ומחשבותיו עמדו משטפן הרגיל», *then slowly, slowly he will feel his head emptying and his thoughts stopping their usual flood* (Derekh HaMelekh, quieting advice (a student's record); line 1556)
> «ובאמירת בתוקף יכול הוא רק לעורר את האנוכיות שלו», *saying it forcefully can only rouse his ego* (Derekh HaMelekh, quieting advice (a student's record); line 1556)
> «משל לאיש ישן אשר עקץ אותו זבוב במצחו», *like a sleeper stung by a fly on his forehead (who dreams a crate fell on him)* (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim (the Piaseczner); line 84)

## 2. One line, and return (weeks 2-3)

**Aim.** Train the plain act of coming back. Each return is the practice, not a failure.

**Practice.** When: same sitting, extended. How long: 8 minutes. Steps. (1) Take one line for the whole week. (2) Say it softly every few breaths. (3) When a thought pulls you away, don't argue and don't analyse. 'Turn your mind from them at once' (T394). 'Act as one who does not know or hear the thoughts that fell into him' (T401b). (4) Come back to the line. Again. Mashal: the halls of the palace. At each door a stray thought is checked and turned away, and you go on (Z87). Limit: you will not be thought-free. Even the Baal Shem Tov says it is not possible to pray with no stray thought at all (Z19). Body: breathe out slowly as you say the line. The out-breath slows the body, and the body slows the mind. (Stated: T394, T401b, Z87, Z19.)

**Signs of progress.** Shorter wandering. Returning gets easier and less annoyed.

**If stuck.** If they are angry at themselves for wandering, that is wrestling too. The only instruction is 'back to the line'.

**Move on when.** They can stay with one line for a minute at a time.

**The guide says** *(ours, 108 words)*. "This week, one line, and coming back to it. Same sitting, eight minutes. Pick one line and keep it all week. Say it softly every few breaths, on the out-breath. When a thought pulls you away, and it will, don't argue with it and don't figure it out. Act as if you didn't hear it, and come back to the line. Every time you come back, that's the practice working, not failing. Picture walking through a palace: at every door some thought tries to come in with you, it gets turned back, and you keep walking. Nobody ever has zero stray thoughts. Coming back is the whole skill."

> **Sources.**
> «אל ישית לב אליהן, אלא יסיח דעתו מהן כרגע», *let him pay them no attention, but turn his mind from them at once* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27; line 394)
> «רק יעשה עצמו כלא יודע ולא שומע ההרהורים שנפלו לו», *let him act as one who does not know or hear the thoughts that fell into him* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 28; line 401)
> «וכשיבא לו מחשבה זרה מגרשין אותו כי דנין אותו בכל היכל אם ראוי לכנוס», *when a stray thought comes, it is sent away, for at every hall he is judged whether he may enter* (Tzava'at HaRivash 87; line 159)
> «הגם שאי אפשר להתפלל בלי מחשבה זרה ירגיל עצמו בלי מחשבה זרה בתחלה», *though one cannot pray with no stray thought at all, let him train himself to begin without one* (Tzava'at HaRivash 19; line 37)

## 3. Let the line fill the room (weeks 3-5)

**Aim.** From holding a line to being held by it.

**Practice.** When: 3 times a week, 10-12 minutes. Steps. (1) Quiet as in stage 1 (3 min). (2) Take one thought, for example 'I am being given this moment', and go into one detail of it: this breath, this light (3 min). (3) Let it widen until, in the Piaseczner's words, 'his room and all around him fill with this thought' (CT228). (4) The Piaseczner: if a person stopped the flood for a while, the G-dly part would be seen without a garment (DM90). Don't chase that. Just stay. (5) Close with one deed. Body: by daylight, or with a window open; a slow walk can replace sitting on restless days. Walking one line is the same practice. (Stated: CT228, DM90.)

**Signs of progress.** The line comes back during the day by itself. More steadiness in work and talk.

**If stuck.** If 'filling' feels like nothing, stay with stage 2.

**The guide says** *(ours, 93 words)*. "Three times this week, take ten or twelve minutes. Settle first the way you've been doing. Then take one thought, for example 'I'm being given this moment', and go into one detail of it: this breath, this light on the wall. Stay with that one detail. Let it slowly widen until it seems to fill the room around you. Don't chase anything special, just stay. If you're restless, walk slowly instead of sitting and repeat the line as you walk; that counts the same. Finish by doing one small good thing right away."

> **Sources.**
> «עד שגם חדרו אף כל סביבו מתמלאים מן מחשבה זו», *until his room and all around him fill with this thought* (Chovat HaTalmidim (the Piaseczner); line 228)
> «ובאם היה האדם מעמיד ומפסיק על שעה את כל שטף מחשבותיו ורצוניו, היה חלק אלקות נראה לו בלא לבוש», *if a person stopped the whole flood of his thoughts and wants for a while, the G-dly part would be seen by him without a garment* (Derekh HaMelekh (the Piaseczner); line 90)

## 4. Learning with strength and joy (from week 2, daily)

**Aim.** Give the mind good food, which crowds out the flood better than emptiness alone.

**Practice.** When: daily. How long: 10 minutes. Steps. Read a short piece of Torah or wisdom out loud, with energy. The Baal Shem Tov: 'let study be with strength and great joy; this lessens stray thoughts' (Z51). Choose one line from it to carry. Come down several times a day to rest from the thought (Z135): stand, stretch, look out a window for a minute. (Stated: Z51, Z135.)

**Signs of progress.** Fewer stray thoughts in the sitting. The mind has somewhere to go.

**If stuck.** Make it 5 minutes, but out loud.

**The guide says** *(ours, 82 words)*. "A busy mind also needs good food, not just quiet. Each day, take ten minutes and read something wise out loud, with energy, like you mean it. Reading with strength and some joy is one of the oldest ways to thin out stray thoughts. Pick one line from it to carry through the day. And come down a few times a day: stand up, stretch, look out a window for a minute. Rest isn't a break from this; it's part of it."

> **Sources.**
> «הלימוד יהי' בכח ובשמחה גדולה וזהו ממעט מחשבות זרות», *let study be with strength and great joy; this lessens stray thoughts* (Tzava'at HaRivash 51; line 99)
> «וצריך לירד למטה כמה פעמים ביום לנוח עצמו ממחשבתו מעט», *he must come down several times a day to rest a little from his thought* (Tzava'at HaRivash 135; line 281)

**Daily shape.** Morning: water, then the 5-8 minute sitting before the phone or coffee. Day: one carried line, and three 1-minute 'come down' breaks (stand, stretch, window). Evening: 10 minutes of learning aloud. Screens off before bed. 3 times a week: the longer stage 3 sitting.

**Duration.** Noticeable within 2-3 weeks for most people. A settled mind is built over months. On very full days, the 3-minute version is enough.

**Guards.** Racing thoughts with no sleep, big ideas and a sense of mission are the danger line, not scatter (S20, S21). Watching thoughts is not for anyone who feels unreal or detached. Intrusive, distressing thoughts that won't leave (OCD-type), ADHD, or anxiety disorders need a professional. The practice can go alongside, not instead. Never call wandering a spiritual failure.

## P1-06 · Doubt: 'I don't believe. Prove it.' (the reasoning-first path)

**Presenting.** I don't believe in any of this. I'm a regular American. Prove it to me, or why should I bother?

## Formulation

- **In their words.** "Prove it. I'm a regular American. Why would I believe anything is there?"
- **Where in the soul.** Chochmah and binah, the mind, honest and working. For this person the mind is the open door (VS decision table: 'Curious, debating... teach, one step at a time'). The animal soul listens through plain reason and its own experience. The sources say to explain step by step, with pictures, and through what it has seen with its own eyes (M01, M12). The body is the first piece of evidence they already own: the heartbeat they don't run, the breath, waking up. Level: binah. Deeper down, the simple will (yechidah) is untouched by the argument either way. (Stated: M01, M12 bases. The door-reading is ours.)
- **State now.** S12 The flat world, nobody there, or S22 Hearing from afar if they are curious.
- **Root.** The yeshus of the world: things look self-standing. The Tanya: through the hidden face, 'the life hides in the creature's body, as if the body were a thing in itself' (MAP S12). And the smith's picture: the world is made once and runs by itself, like a vessel that no longer needs the smith (T792). The person is not foolish. The eye of flesh simply does not see the life in things (T800). (Stated: T792, T800, MAP S12.)
- **What is blocked.** Not intelligence. What is missing is one question: 'what keeps this in being now?' It is missing because 'it's just there' feels like the neutral view. There is also an expectation that faith must be proven like a theorem. The sources say the core is above the mind (T834) and still meant to come down into knowing (IB4).
- **State needed.** S22 then S23/S24 Understanding, then S65 Given right now. Later, only if they choose, S35 The edge of knowing and S36 Faith above knowing. Two knowings held at once (T1290).
- **Kind of healing.** Teaching first, as birur through reason: the long road, answering each claim in its own terms (M10). Da'as is built slowly. Then, if they choose, emunah: a deed done before belief (M33), with no pressure. This is not a 'cure' of doubt. Doubt is treated as a respectable starting place.

## 1. Take the question seriously: the table and the wall of water (week 1)

**Aim.** Remove the hidden assumption that existence is 'just there', without claiming a lab proof.

**Practice.** When: in conversation, then 10 minutes alone with the questions. Steps. (1) Ask what they would count as evidence. Honor the answer. (2) The reasoning, from the Tanya. A smith makes a vessel. When his hands leave, it stays, because he only reshaped silver that already existed (T792). But existence itself is 'something from nothing' (T793). Nothing was there to reshape. So its being is more like the sea wall held by the wind. 'Had G-d stopped the wind for an instant, the water would have run back downhill' (T794). (3) The question to sit with: is being a one-time event, or is it given now? (4) Honest limit: this is reasoning that removes an obstacle and points. It is not a laboratory proof. The sources themselves say the core is 'above the mind and above grasp' (T834). Mashal: the table and the wall of water. Its limit: the wind is still a thing in the world. What gives being is not a thing among things. Body: do the 10 minutes on a walk. The trees, the cars and your own legs are the material. (Stated: T792-794, T834. The modern framing ours.)

**Signs of progress.** 'Huh, I never thought of it that way.' They argue with the idea instead of dismissing it. Curiosity.

**If stuck.** If they want a scientific proof, say plainly that this is not one, and that science answers 'how', not 'why anything at all'. Offer stage 2 as an experiment instead.

**Move on when.** They can say the question back in their own words.

**The guide says** *(ours, 116 words)*. "Good question, and you don't have to switch off your brain for this. I won't pretend there's a lab proof, but here's some reasoning you can check yourself. When a carpenter builds a table and goes home, the table stays, because he only reshaped wood that was already there. But existence itself wasn't reshaped from anything. There was nothing to start with. So maybe it's less like the table and more like a wall of water held up by a strong wind: stop the wind for one second, and the water falls. Here's the question to take on a walk this week: is the world just there, or is it being given its being right now?"

> **Sources.**
> «כי כאשר יצא לצורף כלי – שוב אין הכלי צריך לידי הצורף», *once the smith has made a vessel, the vessel no longer needs the smith's hands* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2; line 792)
> «למעשה שמים וארץ, שהוא יש מאין», *(unlike) the making of heaven and earth, which is something from nothing* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2; line 793)
> «ואילו הפסיק ה׳ את הרוח כרגע – היו המים חוזרים וניגרים במורד כדרכם וטבעם», *had G-d stopped the wind for an instant, the water would have run back downhill as is its nature* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2; line 794)
> «רק להאמין באמונה, שהיא למעלה מהשכל ומהשגה», *only to believe, with a faith above the mind and above grasp* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 7; line 834)

## 2. Test it on your own body (weeks 1-2)

**Aim.** From an idea to something seen with their own eyes (M12).

**Practice.** When: daily, 5 minutes, any time. Steps. (1) Two fingers on the pulse for ten beats. Ask of each beat: did I make that? (W05). (2) Notice the breath leaving and returning by itself. The Alter Rebbe describes the life in every breath running back to its source and spreading out again (LT2256). (3) Tomorrow morning, notice waking up. You did not do it; you were given back. That is why the day starts with 'I thank You' (HY84a). (4) Each time ask, without answering: kept, right now, or just there? Limit: a heart has its own causes, and the teaching doesn't deny them. The point is that the whole chain of causes is itself being given (W05 'Breaks'). A pulse is not G-d. (Stated: LT2256, HY84a, W05 basis. The exercise is ours, from WAYS.)

**Signs of progress.** A moment of wonder that isn't argued away. The questions come back to them on their own.

**If stuck.** Not for anyone with health anxiety or panic. Use a plain object instead: a cup, a tree.

**Move on when.** They report one moment where 'just there' felt less obvious.

**The guide says** *(ours, 107 words)*. "Let's test it on the evidence closest to you: your own body. Put two fingers on your pulse for ten beats, and ask of each one: did I make that? Then notice your breath going out and coming back without your running it. Tomorrow when you wake up, notice that you didn't do that either. You were given back. Your heart has its causes, and science explains them well. The question is underneath that: is the whole chain of causes just there, or kept, right now? Don't answer it. Just ask it a few times a day, and see whether 'it's just there' still feels as obvious."

> **Sources.**
> «וכך הוא בכל רגע על כל נשימה ונשימה יש הסתלקות והתפשטות», *and so it is every moment: with every single breath there is a withdrawing and a spreading out* (Likkutei Torah, Chukas; line 2256)
> «דער סדר פון טאג הויבט זיך אן מיט מודה אני», *the order of the day begins with Modeh Ani ('I thank You')* (Hayom Yom, 11 Shevat; line 84)

## 3. Two knowings: you don't have to give up science (weeks 2-4)

**Aim.** Remove the false choice between being reasonable and being open.

**Practice.** When: 10 minutes, 2-3 times a week. The reasoning, which is stated: the Tanya holds two knowings at once. 'From below upward, the created thing is completely separate' (T1290): the world as science studies it is real and separate. From above, it is held by the One. If the eye were allowed to see the life in every created thing, it would see that life (T800). Steps. (1) Look at one thing, a tree, through the first knowing: cells, water, sunlight, how it grows. (2) Then through the second: given, this second. (3) Notice that you don't have to give up either. Mashal: a word and the voice speaking it. You can study the word's grammar forever. It is still being spoken. Limit: no mashal fits; He is not a voice. Body: do this outside, standing near the thing you look at. (Stated: T1290, T800. The word-and-voice mashal is ours, close to Shaar HaYichud's letters, T785.)

**Signs of progress.** Ease. They stop feeling they must choose between being smart and being open.

**If stuck.** If the idea of 'above' still feels like a cheat, stay with stage 2 and the open question.

**Move on when.** They can hold both views for a minute without strain.

**The guide says** *(ours, 105 words)*. "You don't have to choose between being reasonable and being open. A few times this week, go outside and look at one tree. First look at it the way a biologist would: cells, water, sunlight, how it grows. All true, all real. Then look again and ask: is this being given, this second? Hold both. Think of a sentence someone is saying. You can study its grammar all day, and it's still being spoken. The comparison isn't perfect, and nothing will be. But you'll notice the two ways of seeing don't actually fight. One tells you how. The other asks why there's anything at all."

> **Sources.**
> «אבל בידיעה שממטה למעלה – היש הנברא הוא דבר נפרד לגמרי», *but in the knowledge from below upward, the created thing is completely separate* (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 20; line 1290)
> «אילו ניתנה רשות לעין לראות ולהשיג את החיות ורוחניות שבכל נברא», *if the eye were allowed to see the life and spirit in every created thing* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 3; line 800)
> «כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש», *if the letters withdrew for an instant and went back to their source, all the heavens would be truly nothing* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 1; line 785)

## 4. The edge: where reason stops, an experiment of the heart (week 4 on, only if they choose)

**Aim.** Reach the honest edge of knowing, and offer, without pressure, a deed done before belief.

**Practice.** When: once, as a conversation, then 30 days if they say yes. The reasoning, which is stated: the sources say the core is a faith above knowing (T834), and that this faith is meant to come down into understanding over time (IB4). Even a believer may 'believe even then, only not feel it' (EK578). The Rebbe's way with a hesitant mind: do first, then understand (M33). Steps. (1) Name the edge: 'Reasoning brought you to an open question. It can't close it for you.' (2) Offer one small act for 30 days as an experiment, not a proof. For a Jew, perhaps candles on Friday night, or the morning 'I thank You'. For anyone, one daily kindness given as if to Someone. (3) At day 30, ask what changed, if anything, in them, not in the argument. Body: the act is done with the hands and the mouth, not only thought. (Stated: T834, IB4, EK578, M33 basis.)

**Signs of progress.** Whatever they report honestly. Often: 'I'm still not sure, but I'm different when I do it.'

**If stuck.** If they say no, it is a real no. Stay a friendly teacher, and keep answering questions.

**The guide says** *(ours, 111 words)*. "Reasoning has taken you to an honest open question, and that's as far as reasoning can go. It can't close the question for you. If you want, here's an experiment, not a proof. For thirty days, do one small thing as if Someone were there: say 'thank you' when you wake up, or do one kindness a day as a gift to Him. Do it with your hands and your mouth, not just in your head. After thirty days, we'll look at what changed in you, if anything. If the answer is no to all of this, that's completely fine, and I'm still glad to keep thinking it through with you."

> **Sources.**
> «רק להאמין באמונה, שהיא למעלה מהשכל ומהשגה», *only to believe, with a faith above the mind and above grasp* (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 7; line 834)
> «שיבא כח האמונה זו שלמעלה מן הדעת לידי גלוי בדעת והבנה», *that the power of faith above knowing come to be revealed in knowing and understanding* (Imrei Binah, introduction (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 4)
> «מאמין הוא גם אז רק שאינו מרגישה», *he believes even then, only he does not feel it* (Esh Kodesh (the Piaseczner); line 578)

**Daily shape.** 5 minutes of the body test, at any time. A 10-minute question walk 2-3 times a week. Conversations whenever they have questions. If they choose stage 4, one small deed a day.

**Duration.** As long as their questions last. The first shift (a real question in place of 'it's just there') often comes in 1-2 weeks. Belief is not the measure of success. An honest, open question is.

**Guards.** Never pressure, never 'you must believe'. A no is respected and not followed by persuasion (VS 'Always ask'). Never claim a scientific proof of G-d. Never explain anyone's suffering or a death as 'His hiding' (MAP S12 guard, D03). If the doubt sits on grief or trauma ('where was G-d when...'), meet the pain first (S14), not the argument. Not for someone in crisis.

## P1-07 · Loneliness

**Presenting.** I'm lonely. Nobody really knows me. I come home and there's no one.

## Formulation

- **In their words.** "Nobody really knows me. I come home and it's empty."
- **Where in the soul.** Both souls. The animal soul needs people, and that need is real and good, not a spiritual flaw. The G-dly soul is rooted with all other souls: 'they are all matched, and there is one Father for all of them' (T466b). 'Only the bodies are divided' (T466a). The Piaseczner says of the soul's ache that 'it is not the man who feels this pain, only the G-dly spark within him' (DM518): part of this longing is the soul's own. Faculty: chesed, love with nowhere to go. Garments: speech has no listener. The body feels loneliness as tiredness, poor sleep, eating alone and badly, and too much screen. Caring for it, a shared meal and a walk among people, is part of the healing. (Stated: T466a-b.)
- **State now.** S13 The unnamed longing, with touches of S12 The flat world. MAP has no node of its own for loneliness, and the closest goal is S86 The other held. The VS decision table row 'Lonely, no one to tell' applies.
- **Root.** Yeshus in its social form: the feeling of standing apart, which is the very feeling the sources call the root problem. The bodies really are divided. The error is taking the division for the whole truth (T466a). And there is a real lack of people, which is not an error at all and needs a real answer. (Stated: T466a. The reading is ours.)
- **What is blocked.** Love has no vessel. The giving and receiving between people has stopped. The Tanya: as water gives back face to face, so the heart of one person to another (T680). Also the sense of His nearness 'in the house' (T476).
- **State needed.** S55 Own words to Him, then S86 The other held. Practically: one human connection.
- **Kind of healing.** Simcha of nearness: He dwells with the crushed (KH704), and the King lodges in your house (T476). Is'hapcha, turning: the longing for company is turned into love given outward (T680, W25). Practical chesed and the friend: two G-dly souls against one (RY30653). Honest gap: the corpus gives no road for 'feeling unseen' as such (ChassidusIntelligence frame note). We build from nearness and the friend, and say so.

## 1. Met, and one person (week 1)

**Aim.** Take the lack seriously as a real lack, and aim at one human contact.

**Practice.** When: this week. Steps. (1) The guide names it plainly: 'This is meant to be done with people near. A chat isn't a substitute' (VS-14). (2) Choose one person to contact: an old friend, a neighbor, a relative. Send one specific message today. (3) Look up one class, circle, synagogue, community meal or volunteer slot nearby, and pick a date. The Rayatz on why a friend matters: two G-dly souls against one natural soul (RY30653). The Mitteler Rebbe asks for learning repeated 'with a friend specifically' (KH97). Body: eat at least one meal a day at a table, not standing or in front of a screen, and if you can, with someone. (Stated: RY30653, KH97. The steps are ours, following VS-14.)

**Signs of progress.** One message sent. One date on the calendar.

**If stuck.** If there is truly no one: start with a structured place (a class or a volunteer shift), where the setting does the introducing.

**Move on when.** One real contact is made or scheduled.

**The guide says** *(ours, 111 words)*. "Being lonely is a real lack, not a flaw in you, and it needs a real answer, not only an inner one. This week, two things. First, send one specific message today to someone you used to be close to: not 'how are you', but something real, like 'I was thinking about that trip; can we talk this week?' Second, find one class, community meal, or volunteer slot near you and put a date on your calendar. These things are meant to be done with people near you. And eat at least one meal a day sitting at a table, not standing over the sink. It matters more than it sounds."

> **Sources.**
> «צוויי נפש אלקיתין אויף איין נפש הטבעית», *two G-dly souls against one natural soul* (Igros Kodesh (the Rayatz), from notes of Elul 5652; line 30653, OCR)
> «לחזור הדבר עד מאה פעמים עם חבירו דוקא», *to repeat the matter up to a hundred times, with a friend specifically* (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 97)

## 2. Not alone in the room: own words at the lonely hour (weeks 1-3)

**Aim.** Fill the empty hour with real speech to Someone near.

**Practice.** When: at the lonely hour, usually evening on coming home. How long: 5 minutes. Steps. (1) Before turning on a screen, sit at the table. (2) Talk to Him out loud, in your own words, like to a close friend: what happened today, what hurt, what was good. (3) The reasoning, which is stated: the Tanya's picture is the joy of a plain person when a king lodges with him in his own house (T476). The Tanya again: a great king showing his great love to a plain, lowly man (T681). The Mitteler Rebbe: He dwells with the crushed, 'to revive the spirit of the crushed' (KH704). (4) Close: 'Thank You for being here.' Then make dinner. Body: light on, a real meal, and sit at the table. The body learns that this home is lived in. Limit: this does not replace people, and it is never offered as a reason to stay alone. (Stated: T476, KH704.)

**Signs of progress.** The evening has a beginning. Less screen-drift. A sense of company that is quiet, not dramatic.

**If stuck.** If talking to Him feels like talking to the ceiling, keep it to one sentence a night: 'I'm home. I'm lonely. You're here.' The Piaseczner, writing in the ghetto: He weeps with the one who weeps (EK585). Use that only where there is real pain, never as a slogan.

**Move on when.** It has become a habit on most evenings.

**The guide says** *(ours, 94 words)*. "Let's give the lonely hour a shape. When you come home tonight, before you turn on any screen, turn on the lights, sit at the table, and talk to Him out loud for five minutes, like you would to a close friend. What happened today, what hurt, what was good. Picture it this way: someone great has come to stay in your home and is glad to be there. Then say 'thank you for being here', and make yourself a real dinner. This isn't a replacement for people. It's company while you're finding them."

> **Sources.**
> «כמה גדולה שמחת הדיוט ושפל אנשים, בהתקרבותו למלך בשר ודם המתאכסן ודר אתו עמו בביתו», *how great is the joy of a commoner, the lowest of men, when a flesh-and-blood king comes close and lodges with him in his own house* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 33; line 476)
> «אם מלך גדול ורב, מראה אהבתו הגדולה והעצומה לאיש הדיוט ונבזה ושפל אנשים», *if a great king shows his great love to a common, lowly man* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 46; line 681)
> «אז ישכון בו ממקור כל החיים מקורא דכולא להחיות רוח נדכאים», *then He dwells in him, from the source of all life, to revive the spirit of the crushed* (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut (the Mitteler Rebbe); line 704)
> «ושם בוכה ומילל עמו כביכול יחד», *and there He weeps and wails with him, as it were, together* (Esh Kodesh (the Piaseczner); line 585)

## 3. Love answers love: hold someone, be held (weeks 2-5)

**Aim.** Turn the longing into giving. This is the turn that opens the door for others to come close.

**Practice.** When: one act a day. How long: 5 minutes. The reasoning, which is stated: 'as water gives back face to face, so the heart of one person to another' (T680). Love given wakes love. Steps. (1) Each day, one small, concrete help or warmth offered to one person: a check-in message, a favor, a meal shared. (2) Expect nothing back that day. (3) Once a week, do something with others in body: a walk with a neighbor, a shared Shabbos or Sunday meal, a volunteer shift (W25). Guard: for someone who already over-gives, the act is to ask for help, not to give (W25). (Stated: T680. W25 is ours from the being-held strand.)

**Signs of progress.** Replies. Small returns of warmth. A name or two that becomes regular.

**If stuck.** If giving feels empty, make it physical and in person rather than a text.

**Move on when.** One or two relationships have begun to move.

**The guide says** *(ours, 102 words)*. "Love works like a reflection in water: the face you show is the face that comes back. So each day, offer one small, concrete thing to one person: a check-in message, a favor, an invitation for coffee. Expect nothing back that day. Once a week, do something in person with others: a walk, a shared meal, a volunteer shift. Over a few weeks, some of it comes back, and a name or two becomes a regular part of your life. One note: if you're someone who always gives and never asks, then your act this week is to ask someone for help."

> **Sources.**
> «כמים הפנים לפנים, כן לב האדם אל האדם», *as water gives back face to face, so the heart of one person to another* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 46; line 680)

## 4. One root: the contemplation at a shared table (week 4 on)

**Aim.** See others, even strangers, as matched to you at the root.

**Practice.** When: at any meal with others, or in a crowd, for 2 minutes. Steps. (1) Look at the people around you. (2) Say inwardly the Tanya's line: they are all matched, and there is one Father for all of them (T466b). They are truly siblings from the root of the soul; only the bodies are divided (T466a). (The Tanya says it of Israel. The owner's reading, D28, carries the structure to every person.) (3) Let one of them become a little less of a stranger: a smile, a word. Limit: a shared root does not mean anyone owes you closeness. It means you are not, at bottom, apart. (Stated: T466a-b. D28 is the owner's.)

**Signs of progress.** Less feeling of 'everyone else has people'. Easier small talk.

**The guide says** *(ours, 89 words)*. "Next time you're in a room full of people, at a meal, on a train, in a waiting room, try this for two minutes. Look around and tell yourself: at the root, we all come from one place, and we have one Father. Only the bodies are separate. That doesn't mean anyone owes you closeness. It means that at bottom you're not as apart as it feels. Then let one person become a little less of a stranger: a smile, a word, a question. Small is how it starts."

> **Sources.**
> «ולכן נקראו כל ישראל ״אחים״ ממש, מצד שורש נפשם בה׳ אחד רק שהגופים מחולקים», *so all of Israel are called truly brothers, from the root of their souls in the One G-d; only the bodies are divided* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 32; line 466)
> «שכולן מתאימות, ואב אחד לכולנה», *they are all matched, and there is one Father for all of them* (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 32; line 466)

**Daily shape.** Morning: 'I thank You', and one message to someone. Evening: lights on, the 5-minute talk to Him at the table, then a real meal. One small kindness a day. Weekly: one in-person activity with others. Screens off at a set hour, and a set bedtime.

**Duration.** Connection takes months to build. The first relief (an evening that has a shape, one reply) often comes in 1-2 weeks. Loneliness may return in seasons. The practices are meant to be kept.

**Guards.** Loneliness with hopelessness, 'nobody would notice if I was gone', or not wanting to be here is danger (S21): a person now. Elderly or isolated people may need practical services: the guide points to them. The guide must not become the relationship. If they say 'only you understand me', or chat late every night, it gently points to people and limits sessions (VS decision table, dependence row). Don't press people-pleasers to give more (W25 guard). Never say loneliness is a spiritual failure.

## Gaps (kept honest)

- The corpus gives no road for loneliness or 'feeling unseen' as such. P1-07 is built from nearness (T476, KH704), love answering love (T680), the friend (RY30653) and the shared root (T466). Its arrangement is ours. - 'The afternoon as the hour of judgments that need sweetening' (MVS128) is from Maor VaShemesh, a non-Chabad Chassidic master. The Chabad sources here give the afternoon prayer's preciousness (TS163, SHM22645) and the ebb after morning prayer (T208), but not the 'judgments' reading. - The quieting method (DM1555-1556) is a student's record of the Piaseczner, not his own pen. - Tzava'at HaRivash is a compilation of the Baal Shem Tov's and the Maggid's teachings, not written by either. - The 'refusal test' in P1-03 stage 4 and the 'word and voice' mashal in P1-06 stage 3 are ours, built on T232 and T785. - Durations are judgments from the sources' own pacing (habit becomes nature, 40-day path, set times) and are not stated by any source for these conditions. - Several protocols fit a person outside Jewish practice by swapping the mitzvah anchor (afternoon prayer, Shema, blessings) for a psalm, own words or a verse. This follows the owner's reading D28 and is not stated in the sources.
