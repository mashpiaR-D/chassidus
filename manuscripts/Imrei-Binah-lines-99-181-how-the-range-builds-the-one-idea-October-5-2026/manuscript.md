## The shape of the range

This is the heart of the book's commentary on the first verse of the Shema. The range asks one question in many forms: how can the One who is alone (yachid) also be the One of all things (echad), with no change between them?

- **§3-5 (99-110): the body as a map.** The heart, the lungs, the stomach and the diaphragm are read as a map of Atzilut. The "firmament" that divides the upper from the lower waters is the organ that separates the fine from the coarse. The point that matters: the separation was willed for the sake of the lowest end (line 102), and Torah comes down into the mixed world to sort it (lines 104-110).
- **§6-11 (111-136): the Essence and its two names.** The large *ayin* and *dalet* of the Shema point into the Essence. Then comes the book's central distinction, *echad* against *yachid* (lines 120-124), and the claim that Israel is the "intermediary" that makes them equal (lines 129-132). Section 11 explains why the tzimtzum changes nothing.
- **§12-16 (137-155): "With You is the source of life".** A long sorting of analogies (hidden and revealed, cause and effect, creation from nothing, power and act, body and garment) tests how the sefirot can be one with the Emanator.
- **§17-23 (156-181): the lower unity is higher.** Baruch Shem, the unity in the created worlds, has an advantage over the unity of the first verse, because creation from nothing is the Essence's own power. The range ends in the earth, which bears new things from nothing because "the end holds the power of the beginning".

F05 (the Essence) leads by a wide margin. F07 (the home below) comes second at line level, because the end of the deed that "rose first in thought" runs through the whole range. F12, knowing the unity, is rare here: the range argues the unity rather than teaching how to contemplate it. The one place it speaks of knowing (line 158) is important, and is taken up below.

## §2 end to §3 (99-103): the firmament is made for the lowest

Lines 99-101 set up the analogy of the heart and the "supernal man" of Atzilut. Line 101 gives F01 and F02 in one breath. The worlds come from the letters of supernal thought and speech, "from nothing to something", and they are called worlds only because those letters are garments: > משום דכלא חשיבי ממש לגבי אור עצם מדות (line 101)

The same line reads the three Shabbat rests as a return of the many to the one. On the third rest, "vayinafash", the return reaches His very Essence: > וז"ש וינפש שבא לכלל בחי' עצמותו ממש (line 101)

Line 102 is the first strong F07 in the range. Why divide the upper waters from the lower? Because the simple will wanted the lowest creation first: > ומפני שסוף מעשה עלה ורצון הפשוט בתחלה שיהי' דוקא תכלית שפלות הבריאה (line 102)

The divider itself must be a joining of opposites (line 102, F09). Line 103 gives the image: the pillar of cloud between Israel and Egypt, "made of two opposites, darkness and light".

## §4-5 (104-110): choose life; Torah sorts the pleasures of the world

"Choose life" is read two ways, choosing good within the Tree of Knowledge and choosing the Tree of Life itself. The text ends: "both ways are true and all is one" (line 104). Water carries a hidden pleasure, and every physical pleasure comes from the "lower waters", which mix good and evil. Eating for the soul's sake lets the good rise: > והטוב שבהן עולה במעלות עונג העליון שמוסיף חכמ' העליונה האלקית (line 105)

This is F11 in its everyday form. At the end of days nothing will be refuse (line 106): "the pig will be purified", because everything will have been sorted.

Line 109 gives the range's first plain statement of the unity as such, and it gives it as a law of Shabbat. Sorting is forbidden on Shabbat even between the hidden and the revealed Torah: > משום דבאמת הכל א' דאא"ס כמו שהוא אין שיעור למעלה כך אין לו שיעור למטה (line 109)

Torah's root is the Essence's own delight. Line 110 says the speech of Torah clothes "the essence of the supernal delights" in His primordial wisdom. So "man does not live by bread alone": Torah as "bread" sorts and nourishes, but its life comes from what "comes from God's mouth" (F10 + F05).

## §6-7 (111-119): the source of the source; the end that is the beginning

The large *ayin* of "Shema" is the essential middot, before they go out into thought and speech. Line 112 uses a soul analogy for F02. Love goes out in many acts, and every act "reaches only the edge" of the love itself. Line 114 then lays out four levels: the spread into the lower worlds, the lights of Atzilut, the ten sefirot hidden in the Essence, and the Essence itself: > והד' בחי' עצמו' המאציל ממש דלאו מכל אינון מדות כלל (line 114)

The Essence "measured in Himself" what would be divided, yet "He, His life and His causes are one without any change". Line 115 gives the reason for everything as a "desire for kindness" hidden in the Essence, "as the nature of the good is to do good".

The large *dalet* (§7) is Malchut of Ein Sof, "I will reign", the end of thought in which the beginning is wedged. Line 117 gives the principle that the rest of the range will use: > והוא מה שאא"ס למעלה מעלה עד אין שיעור ע"כ מתפשט למטה עד אין תכלית (line 117)

So Baruch Shem is Malchut of Ein Sof coming down to Malchut of Asiyah (line 117, F02 + F04). Line 118 adds F10: the first will is fulfilled only "by their Torah and mitzvot".

## §8-9 (120-128): echad and yachid

This is the range's most important passage for the index. The SPINE records the Rebbe's distinction between *echad* and *yachid* as the "precision before anything else". Here the Mitteler Rebbe states it in full, a century and a half earlier. *Echad* is a counted one. It can stand next to a second, or hold many hidden as a thousand holds its parts. *Yachid* has no second and no parts: > ולזה יקראוהו בל' יחיד זאין לו שיתוף וחיבור במספר כלל וכלל (line 120)

Applied above (lines 121-122): the ten sefirot hidden in the Essence are "simple unity", an *echad* that holds many. The Essence itself, "You are one but not in number", "not of any of these middot even hidden", is *yachid*: > הנה זהו הנק' בשם יחיד כמו היחיד שאינו בגדר מספר (line 122)

Line 123 adds "a new thing". Each essential middah holds the whole Essence, because a simple essence cannot be divided. Of His very Essence one cannot even say "He and His life are one": > משא"כ בעצמו ממש לא שייך לומר איהו וחיוהי (line 123)

The soul analogies here (the father's bond to a son, the love of Jonathan and David, Abraham at the binding) are tagged F08 where they speak of real self-sacrifice. They are not tagged F06, following the Gate of Unity reports.

§9 reads the two Havayahs of the Shema this way. The first Havayah is *yachid*: > הוי' הא' הוא בבחי' יחיד בעצמות אא"ס טרם שצמצם א"ע כו' שאין עוד מלבדו כלל (line 124)

The second is the sefirot "like a flame bound to a coal". Then comes the human side (lines 126-127, F08). Self-sacrifice goes to the Essence, "to Him and not to His middot", yet the sages say to "lengthen *echad*". The answer is that one who gives the whole soul into the *echad* is thereby given into the *yachid* above it: > אזי ממילא נמסר נפשו ביחיד שלמעלה מא' (line 126)

"Because he desired Me" means Me in My Essence. That desire has no opposite in the kelipah, so "I will rescue him" (line 127).

## §10-11 (129-136): Israel as the intermediary; why the tzimtzum changes nothing

After the tzimtzum He is still *yachid*, "nothing besides Him" (line 129). What makes *echad* equal to *yachid*? > מפני שישראל דוקא הוא בחי' הממוצע בין יחיד לאחד (line 129)

The general thought sees all worlds "in one glance", like a sphere around a line (line 130, F04). Israel is its inner intent, "behold it was very good" (line 131). Israel rose even in the delight of the will within the Essence. So when Israel comes down and makes the unions below, nothing has changed: > ע"כ ודאי מובן שאין שינוי כלל בין יחיד בעצמותו לאחדות ה' באבי"ע (line 132)

§11 grounds this in the Essence itself. Multiplicity must itself be in the utmost simple unity (line 133), because He "can do all" and holds ascent and descent as equal powers. Line 134 gives the trace (reshimu) as ascent and the line (kav) as descent; both were in the Essence before the tzimtzum. Therefore: > וא"כ אין שינוי כלל בין קודם הצמצום לאחר הצמצום (line 134)

Then what is the tzimtzum? Line 136 answers: > שאין זה הפסק גמור רק העדר גילוי אור העצמי (line 136)

This agrees with the Tanya's non-literal tzimtzum (F03). It also adds a boundary the Tanya does not draw here. Between Atzilut and Beriah there *is* a real screen that interrupts the line (line 136.3), and that screen becomes the key to §17-18.

## §12-15 (137-152): "With You is the source of life"

These lines are long and largely structural. Line 138 sets out three kinds of coming-forth, and gives the sign of true creation from nothing: > שנק' התהוות יש מאין עכ"פ כידוע וסימנו שמהווה לאין שיעור ולא יכלה כוחו כלל (line 138)

This sign returns at line 179. Lines 139-147 test the images of body and garment against the sefirot. The unity is stated at 143: "He grasps all worlds and none grasps Him", "He is the place of the world and the world is not His place": > הרי ב' הפכי' בנושא א' (line 143)

When He "withdraws", that is no change, because no one grasps Him (line 143). Line 149 carries the whole section: whether as spread, as clothing or as hidden-revealed, the lights are one with the Emanator, > שאין לך דבר חוץ מן העצמות (line 149)

and even the vessels come from Him, "He and His causes are one". In three modes the sefirot are "with You", nullified in His Essence (line 149.4, F08 + F05). So line 150 concludes: > ונמצ' מובן מכל זה דאין הפרש כלל בין בחי' יחיד לבחי' אחד (line 150)

Line 151 gives the phrase "alive but not with life", and the image of a candle at noon: the lesser light is "in" the sun and only becomes noticeable when the sun is withdrawn (lines 151-152). Here the tzimtzum is what lets the created light be seen at all.

## §16 (153-155): "our God" and the soul

"Elokeinu" is "ours" because Binah is revealed in every divine soul by its measure (line 153). Israel comes from the inner vessels of Z"A and angels from the outer ones. The divine soul is born "in His image" from the inner mind (line 155). So the section closes on F06 as a stated claim, not an analogy: > הכל א' מצד ישראל דווקא שעלה במח' והוא בחי' הממוצע (line 155)

## §17-18 (156-163): the lower unity surpasses the higher

The two unities are equal, and "the screen does not interrupt" (line 157). The tribes said to Jacob, "as there is only One in your heart, so in ours". In exile the Shechinah is in the dust, yet: > ולא עוד אלא שעיקר היחוד הוא בבשכמל"ו (line 157)

Why do Pesukei deZimra praise God through creatures and not through Atzilut? Because more of the Essence is in the lower unity: > ושבח עצמו' אור הא"ס יותר מבחי' יח"ע דאצילות (line 158)

Line 158 then turns this into F12. The command to know is "know that Havayah is Elokim", the lower unity: > עכ"ז אדרבה עיקר מצות הידיעה לידע האמת איך שהוי"ה ואלקים כולא חד ממש (line 158)

It is precisely from His works, the name of action (Adnut), that "the Holy One Himself" is known, though no thought grasps Him. Line 159 extends the advantage even to exile, "with more hiding of the Face" than the Temple.

The reason is given in §18. The lower unity is creation from nothing, "a complete novelty". It comes through the real screen, "as light excels from darkness": > ואמנם יתרון מעלתו אינו רק מצד שהארת עצמות א"ס ממש נמצא שם יותר מבהארת הקו (line 160)

Line 162 finishes the argument with chesed and gevurah. The first tzimtzum is itself kindness, and the light of kindness breaks every screen and shines in Malchut of Asiyah "as in Malchut of Ein Sof". That gives the equality of the two unities. But the firmament of the second day hides completely, and that hiding is higher than the first kindness: > והן הן גבורותיו שיוכל לצמצם ולהסתיר לגמרי כדי שיהי' בריאה יש (line 162)

That is F03 at its strongest: the hiding is the Essence's own power, and it is the reason the lower surpasses the higher.

## §19-22 (164-178): the will lives in the deed; heaven, sea and earth

The screen comes from the gevurot that hide the Essence so that creation from nothing can happen (line 164). The crown of Malchut stays in Atzilut: "forever, God, Your word stands in the heavens" (line 164, F01). Will and deed are one (line 165). Every particular of the will is in the deed, as a hand's drawing is one with the thought (line 166). So "Blessed is He who spoke and the world was" means no change before and after creation: > והיינו פי' אתה הוא משנבר' כו' ואתה הוא קודם שנברא כו' בלי שינוי כלל כי אין המעשה נבדל מן הרצון (line 168)

The end of the deed holds the inner desire because it completes the whole in act (lines 169-170). Line 170 then gives the fulfilled will as "the main cause of the renewal of the work of creation every day".

§21-22 read heaven, earth and seas. Heaven is the firmament that hides His self-will "so that there can be creation from nothing" (line 172). Earth is the end of the deed (line 172). The sea is the hidden world and the land the revealed one. The souls in the letters of thought are "like fish swallowed in the sea", in essential bitul, so one gives the soul "in echad" (line 173, F08). Speech needs a contraction of thought, as a teacher contracts himself for a child (lines 173-174, F03).

Line 176 then says the heavens' daily renewal "is not new at root". Real renewal is in the earth: > אבל הנה בארץ אנו רואי' דבר חדש שיש בה כח התחדשות הבריאה בהתהוות מציאות חדשה ממש (line 176)

Lines 177-178 are natural philosophy (rain, heat, stars, dust). They end with "both are true": the earth grows by its own power, and the stars and the rain still help.

## §23 (179-181): the end holds the power of the beginning

The answer is the range's clearest G05, "find the highest in the lowest": > הוא מבחי' כחו של אא"ס שבתחלה כי הארץ סוף הכל שאין עוד למטה הימנה לכך נמצא בה כח התחלה (line 179)

The likeness is a woman bearing a child. The father's drop is nullified like a sown seed, and her power grows the child, but only with "a divine power", "for I am holy", the Shechinah between them (lines 179-180). Line 180 restates the principle: > לפי שבסוף הכל נמצ' כח התחלה ויותר מזה והוא מה שמחדש מאין בלי הפסק (line 180)

Line 181 carries it into Torah (F10). The Oral Torah renews laws with no hint in the Written, as a child grows from its mother. The supernal unions are pictured as male and female, not as a rich man giving to a poor one, because: > הוא בא מכחו של א"ס עצמו והיינו כדמיון דכר ונוק' (line 181)

## What this range adds to the Tanya, or says differently

- **Yachid and echad, named and argued.** The Tanya's Shaar HaYichud says "Havayah is Elokim" and that the earth is "not even called *od*" (Tanya lines 818-819 in the Tanya report). It does not name the two words. Here the distinction is the backbone of the Shema: the first Havayah is *yachid*, the second *echad* (lines 120-124). It is the SPINE's "precision" in the Mitteler Rebbe's own words.
- **Israel as the intermediary.** In the Tanya, Israel "rose in thought". Here that rising is what makes *yachid* and *echad* equal after the tzimtzum (lines 129, 131-132, 155).
- **The lower unity is higher.** Tanya SH 7 presents the lower unity as the Essence filling the earth "below as above". Here Baruch Shem surpasses the first verse (lines 157-162), and exile surpasses the Temple (line 159). The reason given is creation from nothing, the Essence's own power, which works through the complete hiding (gevurah above chesed, line 162).
- **Knowing the Essence through His works.** The main command to know is "Havayah is Elokim" (line 158). It is precisely from the name of action that "the Holy One Himself" is known. This is F12 moved into the lower unity.
- **The end holds the beginning.** The earth, lowest of all, has the power to create from nothing (lines 176, 179-180). This matches the SPINE's F05 anchor from Iggeret HaKodesh 20 as the SPINE records it (creation "only He, by His power"). Here the reason is framed as "beginning wedged in end" and as the returning force that is stronger than the outgoing one (line 179).
- **Torah's two halves as upper and lower waters.** Written Torah is a measured light; the Oral Torah "renews" without limit, "a woman of valor is her husband's crown" (line 181). The Tanya has "Torah and the Holy One are one" (F10) but not this ranking.

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- **Anatomy and natural philosophy.** Lines 99-100, 104-107 (heart, stomach, pleasure in water), 138-141 and 144-146 (a taxonomy of analogies), 153-154 (cup, knife, telescope, brain) and 176-178 (rain, heat, stars, dust) are mostly *structural*. The unity is why they are there, and the text says so at the end of each section, but most sentences do not state it.
- **The soul as analogy.** Following the Gate of Unity reports, F06 is left off the many lines where the soul only explains God (for example 113, 120, 145-146). F06 is kept where the text speaks of the actual souls of Israel (127, 129, 131-132, 153, 155, 173).
- **Two *reading* sub-entries.** 104.4, where hidden pleasure in water is linked to the source of created life, and 176.3, where the earth's renewal is linked to F07 before the text itself does so at line 179.
- **F11 is tagged on sorting (birur).** Lines 105-110 speak of sorting good from evil in food and pleasure. Calling that "darkness turned to light" is the index's usage, as in the Gate reports.
- **Kabbalistic mapping.** Parts of lines 100, 112, 116, 164 (the partzufim, the name Ban, netzach-hod-yesod) carry no face.

## Disagreements kept

- **Is the daily renewal creation from nothing?** Tanya SH 1-2 (F01): all things are created from nothing constantly, and would return to nothing without "the power of the Maker in the made". Line 176 says the heavens' daily renewal "is not new at root", only renewed light, like the daily renewal of a person's life, and that true renewal from nothing is in the earth. Line 170 also calls the fulfilled will "the cause of the renewal of the work of creation every day". The text does not reconcile these.
- **"As light excels from darkness."** The Mitteler Rebbe's Gate of Unity (line 2414 there) says light in darkness only *looks* brighter to the eye. Here the lower unity's advantage is called > כיתרון אור מתוך החשך דוקא יתרון ממש על אור העצם (line 160)

"a real advantage over the light of the Essence". The same line limits the first tzimtzum: it is not a screen, only the absence of the first light. So the "real advantage" belongs to the second-day screen. The two statements may speak of different hidings, but neither text says so. - **Is the tzimtzum a hiding or not?** "Not a tzimtzum of complete hiding" (line 136.1), and no change before and after (line 134). Yet there is a real screen between Atzilut and Beriah where "the bond to the Essence" is cut (line 160), and the hiding of the firmament is "complete" (line 162). Both are kept: the first tzimtzum is a lessening, the screen below Atzilut is a true hiding, and the second is the higher one. - **Are the two unities equal, or is the lower higher?** "As they are united above, so below", "and not more" (line 162), against "when is He great? in the city of our God" (lines 159, 161). The text itself sets out this contradiction and resolves it by chesed against gevurah (lines 161-162). The resolution is the text's; the tension is recorded because it is the SPINE's F07 question of whether the purpose lies above or below. - **Faith or knowledge?** The Tanya says that His not changing is known "only by faith" (SH 7). This range argues it at length (lines 133-136, 149-150, 168), as the Gate of Unity does. It keeps the limit only for the Essence, "no thought grasps You" (line 147), and still says that "the Holy One Himself" is known through His works (line 158).
