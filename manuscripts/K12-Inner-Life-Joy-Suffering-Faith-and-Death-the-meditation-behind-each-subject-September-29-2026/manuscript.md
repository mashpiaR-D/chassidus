## Why a meditation behind each subject

Every concept in Chassidus points to one thing, the unity of God, and that unity cannot be understood: by definition it is beyond the mind. It can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (hisbonenus). So behind each subject below is a meditation, and the meditation, not the idea, is what matters. Each one specializes the meditation of the subject's first face.

## How to use it

This is hisbonenus, the Jewish way of contemplating as Chabad does it, not mindfulness. Take one subject per sitting.

- **Hold** is one line of teaching. Say it, and fix the mind on it.
- **The move** thinks it through slowly. It opens the line detail by detail, brings it down into your own day, gives the sources' own picture and says in a few words where the picture breaks, and makes it particular: you, here, now. The pauses between its steps are time to think, not silence to watch anything.
- **Stay** binds the mind: say the line again slowly, and when the mind drifts, come back to it without arguing with the stray thought (Tanya 3 and 28).
- **From inside** describes the glimpse. It is never asked for, and if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.
- **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, then gives one mitzvah or act of Torah in your own measure, and a line to return to during the day.

## Labels

- **Tie** says whether the move rests on steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our construction (*reading*).
- **Thin** follows the entry's own marking (its 'thin?' column; 'partly' or 'split' counts as thin). A thin subject's meditation is kept modest and says why in one line.
- **Where the sources differ** keeps a disagreement the entry records, where the meditation touches it, rather than smoothing it.
- **Note** marks any other place where the meditation leans on our reading.
- **Anchors** are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly (reference, line and Hebrew), each with the entry's own gloss. *OCR* marks uncorrected machine transcription, quoted as the entry quotes it.

## S138 · Joy · שמחה

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F07 The home below; F01 The world said into being now; F09 The unity of opposites **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Joy has an object: that even here, in the plain earth, there is nothing besides Him, and this is His home. And you are joining in His own joy at living here.

**Hold.** 'Even in the earth below there is nothing besides Him, and that is His dwelling below' (Tanya 33).

**The move.** Open it. The world and all it holds are set aside in their source, as the sun's light is in the sun. Going deep into this, the Tanya says, the heart rejoices in this faith, because it is nearness to God itself. And there is a second joy, His: He rejoices in His dwelling in this physical world, and you can rejoice in His joy. Iggeret HaKodesh 11 adds that He gives life and does good at every moment, so there is joy at every hour. Bring it down to your own kitchen or office this morning. The picture is the Tanya's, the sun's light, which is nothing of its own inside the sun; it is only a picture, since He is not a body of light, and here below He wants a home, not a sky. You, here, now: this place is His home.

**Stay.** 'Nothing besides Him, even here', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The plain man's joy that his King lives with him, and the King's joy too. Joy is not demanded here: the fact is held, and the heart answers in its own time. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'There is nothing besides Him.' Then do one mitzvah in your home today, lighting a candle or setting a place for a guest, as for His dwelling. Line for the day: 'His home, even here.'

**Where the sources differ.** Where does joy come from? Chabad derives it from the content of the unity (Tanya 33); the Besht's circle ties it to faith that the Shechinah is with one; Breslov commands constant joy and argues it from health and the future dance, and there the tie to the unity is our reading.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 33:4 (line 453): והנה, כשיעמיק בזה הרבה, ישמח לבו ותגל נפשו אף גילת ורנן בכל לב ונפש ומאד באמונה זו, כי רבה היא, כי היא קרבת אלהים ממש Joy is the heart's response to contemplating that the world is nullified before its source: 'this faith' is nearness to God itself. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 33:8 (line 457): כשיהיה לבו שש ושמח באמונתו ביחוד ה׳ בתכלית השמחה The spine's anchor G16: the object of joy is faith in the unity of God, and that joy lifts the soul above every obstacle to the 613 mitzvot. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 33:11 (line 460): יש לו לשמוח בשמחת ה׳, אשר שש ושמח בדירתו בתחתונים, שהם בחינת עשיה גשמיית ממש The joy is doubled: one rejoices in God's own joy in His dwelling in the physical world (F07). Joy is shared with the One who dwells below.

## S139 · Sadness and melancholy · עצבות

**Face:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** The sadness these texts speak of is not depression, grief or anxiety, and this sitting is not a remedy for them. If you have thoughts of not wanting to be here, put this aside and reach a person now: your local emergency number or a crisis line.

**The glimpse.** What makes a small thing feel bad is that the good in it is hidden, not absent; sadness takes His hiding for His absence.

**Hold.** 'This too is for good, only it is not seen, for it comes from the hidden world, which is above the revealed world' (Tanya 26).

**The move.** Open it. What grieves in a small setback is the look that something is missing. Iggeret HaKodesh 11 says no evil comes down from above; what is missing is only the seeing. And the Tanya keeps its own limit: heaviness over one's distance from Him is 'from the good', and one who comes to it through long contemplation 'should not worry'. Bring it down to one small disappointment from today, a plan that fell through or a missed bus, not a grief. The picture is the Tanya's, a hidden world above a revealed one; it is only a picture, since they are not two places but His hiding and His showing. You, here, now: hidden, not absent.

**Stay.** 'This too is for good', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** The relief of finding what was never gone, and the small thing staying small. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, the next time something small goes wrong today: 'This too is for good.' Then do the next mitzvah that comes your way promptly, as a wrestler who is not weighed down. Line for the day: 'Hidden, not absent.'

**Where the sources differ.** Schedule bitterness or avoid it? The Alter Rebbe and the Rashab: at fixed times, best at midnight, briefly and in private. Breslov (per pillar 06, not re-verified): always joyful, bitterness only as God doses it. The Rashab: bitterness that lingers is sadness. The Tanya's own rule against sadness is not absolute: sadness over heavenly matters is 'the good in nogah'. Breslov's rule against sadness rests on health and distance from God, not on the unity; that tie is ours.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 26:5 (line 353): כי ״גם זו – לטובה״, רק שאינה נגלית ונראית לעיני בשר, כי, היא מ״עלמא דאתכסיא״ שלמעלה מ״עלמא דאתגלייא״ The cure for worldly worry: what grieves comes from the hidden world (yud-heh of the Name), higher than the revealed world. Sadness mistakes concealment for absence (F03). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 31:2 (line 426): ואף שעצבות היא מצד קליפת נוגה, ולא מצד הקדושה, כי בצד הקדושה כתיב: ״עוז וחדוה במקומו״ Sadness belongs to kelipah, not holiness: 'strength and gladness are in His place'. Where He is revealed there is no sadness.

## S140 · Bitterness, not sadness · מרירות

**Face:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · **also touches:** F06 The soul as part of the One; F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F09 The unity of opposites **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** Only now and then, briefly and in private, and never as the door for a mood that is already low. The Alter Rebbe allows the time when one is already sad over worldly things; this meditation does not, a limit that is ours.

**The glimpse.** Seeing how great He is shows how far one is, and that seeing is alive, not dead; it clears room for Him, and it turns, because within you is a part of God.

**Hold.** 'All this is I alone, the body and its life; but within me is literally a portion of God' (Tanya 31).

**The move.** Open it. Bitterness starts from His greatness, Likkutei Torah says: the life of all worlds is only a radiance of His Name, while He is unchanged and alone, and against that one sees how far one is. It is not sadness, the Tanya says: sadness is a heart gone dead as stone, while this has life in it, and the same heart holds joy. Its turn is the line itself, 'I alone… but within me is a portion of God', in exile, whose freeing is the joy; and the Rashab adds that it empties the house to receive the King. Bring it down to a few minutes at a set time, alone, now and then. The picture is the Maharash's, the ash taken up at the altar; it is only a picture, since what rises is not ash but lowliness included in His unity. You, here, now: within you is a portion of God.

**Stay.** 'Within me is a portion of God', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Alive rather than heavy: the heart holding both, the strictness of the bitterness and the kindness of the joy. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, when the few minutes end: 'Even darkness is not dark to You.' Then learn one line of Tanya 31 or 33, as the Rashab advises, and go on to prayer or to the day. Line for the day: 'A portion of God within me.'

**Where the sources differ.** Schedule bitterness or avoid it? The Alter Rebbe and the Rashab: at fixed times, best at midnight, briefly and in private. Breslov (per pillar 06, not re-verified): always joyful, bitterness only as God doses it. The Rashab: bitterness that lingers is sadness.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 13:15 (line 4534): ולעורר בחינת רחמים רבים הוא על ידי בחי׳ מרירות כשמתבונן בשכלו איך שחיות כל העולמות הוא רק בחינת זיו ושם בלבד The source of true bitterness is contemplating the higher unity: all worlds are only radiance of His Name, while He is unchanged and alone (F02, F05). Distance is felt only against that unity. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 31:6 (line 430): אך באמת, אין לב נשבר ומרירות הנפש – על ריחוקה מאור פני ה׳ והתלבשותה בסטרא אחרא – נקראים בשם ״עצבות״ כלל The distinction: sadness is a heart dead as stone; bitterness has life, the life of holy gevurot, and the heart holds both gevurah and chesed. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 31:9 (line 433): אך כל זה הוא אני לבדי, הוא הגוף עם נפש החיונית שבו, אבל מכל מקום, יש בקרבי ״חלק ה׳״ ממש, שישנו אפילו בקל שבקלים How bitterness turns to joy: the lowness is 'I alone', while within is literally a part of God (F06) in exile, whose return is the joy.

## S141 · Suffering · יסורים

**Face:** F01 The world said into being now · **also touches:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; F11 Darkness turned to light; F09 The unity of opposites **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**Before you start.** Not for the middle of a crisis or a fresh loss. If you are in danger, reach a person now: your local emergency number or a crisis line. This is a stance a person may take up for himself; it is never to be handed to someone else as an account of their pain.

**The glimpse.** At every moment you are being brought into being from a source that is only good, and what hurts is not a second power. And, the Piaseczno Rebbe adds, He is not only behind the pain but in it.

**Hold.** 'When a person pictures in his mind his coming into being from "nothing" at every single moment': from the source of life, good and delight (Iggeret HaKodesh 11).

**The move.** Open it. You are brought from nothing at this very moment, from His wisdom, the 'nothing' that is the source of life, good and delight. From there, the Alter Rebbe says, no evil comes down, and what does not look good is good too great to be grasped; and faith like this does not only reread the hard thing but lifts it into the hidden good. The Piaseczno Rebbe, writing in the Warsaw Ghetto, held the same faith and said one thing more: God Himself suffers in His people's suffering, and because He has no limit His pain has none, which is why it is hidden. Bring it down to this moment of your own life, given now, and not to the pain itself. The Piaseczno Rebbe says it 'as it were': it is only a picture, since He has no body to hurt, but it places Him inside the pain, not outside it. You, here, now: being given life by the Good, who is in it with you.

**Stay.** 'From nothing, this moment, from the Good', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Held inside the hard thing rather than explained out of it; questions, the Piaseczno Rebbe says, may be said as prayer. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips one sentence to Him about the hard thing, as a prayer and not an argument. Then give a coin to charity today for someone else who is suffering. Line for the day: 'Given life, this moment.'

**Where the sources differ.** Is suffering 'good not grasped', or does God suffer in it? Iggeret HaKodesh 11: there is no evil from above; faith raises the apparent bad into hidden good. The Esh Kodesh keeps that faith ('all is from Him for good… all the sufferings are full of God's love') and adds what Iggeret HaKodesh 11 does not say: God's own pain is without limit and hidden; questions are allowed as prayer, not as argument. The difference is an addition, not a denial.

**Note.** That the Esh Kodesh's addition is a second form of the same unity is our reading; the meditation sets the two voices side by side.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 11:2 (line 1110): וכשיתבונן האדם בעומק הבנתו, ויצייר בדעתו הווייתו מ״אין״ בכל רגע ורגע ממש, האיך יעלה על דעתו כי רע לו, או שום יסורים מבני חיי ומזוני … אבל באמת אין רע יורד מלמעלה, והכל טוב, רק שאינו מושג לגודלו ורב טובו The whole derivation in one line: being created from nothing at every moment (F01), from the ayin that is the source of good, leaves no room for 'bad to me'; suffering is good too great to be grasped. - Esh Kodesh, 5702, Mishpatim-Shekalim 2 (line 545): כביכול הוא ית׳ מצטער ביסורי איש הישראלי, ואפשר שכיון שהוא ית׳ בלתי בעל גבול שבשביל זה אינו מושג בעולם, גם צערו ית׳ מצרות ישראל הוא בלתי גבול The Piaseczno Rebbe in the Warsaw Ghetto: God suffers in Israel's suffering, and because He is without limit His pain is without limit and so hidden from the world. A different answer from IH 11: suffering is not 'good not grasped' but shared by the Infinite. - Esh Kodesh, 5702, Hanukkah 4 (line 496): שגם בעת ההסתר יאמין בו ית׳ ושהכל ממנו ית׳ לטובה והכל בצדק, וכל היסורים הם מלאים אהבת ד׳ לישראל The Piaseczno Rebbe keeps IH 11's faith too: in the hour of hiding, believe that all is from Him for good and all the sufferings are full of His love. Questions said as prayer are good; held as questions they damage faith. So the Esh Kodesh does not reject 'good not grasped'; it adds God's own pain (line 545).

## S142 · Trials · נסיון

**Face:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · **also touches:** F06 The soul as part of the One; F02 The higher and the lower unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** A trial has no reality of its own: it is a hiding set up by Him, for you, and when the soul's hidden faith wakes, it dissolves.

**Hold.** 'The trial is not a thing in itself; it is only something set up to conceal and to hold back' (the Rayatz, 5693).

**The move.** Open it. A person is created in this world to be tried, Iggeret HaKodesh 11 says: whether the heart turns to 'other gods', the body's wants, or to the living God. The tempter has no power of its own and acts only by the King's permission. And when a trial touches faith, the Tanya says, the soul's hidden wisdom wakes from sleep, and whatever stood in the way is as nothing before Him; the Maggid widens it, that every event comes to wake a person. Bring it down to one small pull you expect to meet today, a sharp reply, the phone in the middle of learning. The picture is the Zohar's, in the Tanya: a king sends someone to tempt his son, by his own permission and for the son's sake; it is only a picture, since there is no second power at the court, only the King. You, here, now: this pull is set up, and it has nothing of its own.

**Stay.** 'Only set up to conceal', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Something like the relief of seeing the scenery for what it is: a hiding, set up by the King, for your sake. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, when the pull comes: 'Hear, Israel, the Lord is One.' Then turn from it once, and give that moment to one line of Torah. Line for the day: 'Only set up to hide.'

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 29:26 (line 406): רק שניתן לה רשות לבלבל האדם בדברי שקר ומרמה, להרבות שכרו, כפיתויי הזונה לבן המלך בשקר ומרמה ברשות המלך The Zohar's parable of the harlot sent by the king: the tempter acts only by the King's permission, for the son's sake. There is no second power in the trial. - Sefer HaMaamarim (Rayatz) 5693, 12 Tammuz, 533:1 (line 1037): דהנסיון אינו דבר מצד עצמו רק שהועמד דבר להעלים ולמנוע … כי האמת הוא דטבע עצמו הוא למעלה מן הטבע אלא שהוא אור מוסתר ונעלם The Rayatz: a trial has no reality of its own; it is set up only to conceal. Its refinement is done within the person, by arousing essential trust, because nature itself is concealed supernatural light (F03, G14). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 19:11-12 (line 273): אך כשבאים לידי נסיון בדבר אמונה, שהיא למעלה מהדעת, ונגעה עד הנפש – לבחינת חכמה שבה, אזי היא ניעורה משנתה … הרי כל הקליפות בטלים ומבוטלים והיו כלא היו ממש לפני ה׳ A trial of faith in the One God wakes the chochmah of the soul, where Or Ein Sof is clothed, and then all kelipot are as nothing before Him (F06, F02).

## S143 · Faith · אמונה

**Face:** F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart' · **also touches:** F06 The soul as part of the One; F02 The higher and the lower unity; F04 Filling and surrounding **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** Faith is not a guess to fill a gap: it is the One Himself shining in the innermost point of your soul, holding what knowing cannot, that no place is empty of Him.

**Hold.** 'This is the essence of the faith for which man was created: to believe that no place is empty of Him' (Iggeret HaKodesh 11).

**The move.** Open it. Every Jew can hold it, learned or not, because the One God shines in the soul's innermost wisdom, which is above understanding, the Tanya says. The Rayatz says Israel's simple faith is in the Name above nature. And where knowing stops, the Tanya says, 'we have no business with hidden things', only to believe. Bring it down to the room where you are sitting and the street outside it. The picture is R. Nachman's, the vacated space where He seems absent, crossed only by faith that He surrounds even it; it is only a picture, since, he says, even that space came into being from His wisdom. You, here, now: this place is not empty of Him.

**Stay.** 'No place is empty of Him', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Not holding on by effort: the Piaseczno Rebbe says faith is God's light already present in the Jew. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, as you pass the mezuzah today: 'No place is empty of Him', and touch it. Then say the Shema at its time, drawing out 'One'. Line for the day: 'Not empty of Him.'

**Where the sources differ.** Where does faith end and knowledge begin? The Tzemach Tzedek: that God exists and gives life is knowledge, not faith; faith is for His essence. The Alter Rebbe: how He is one with His attributes is to be believed, 'no business with hidden things'. The Rayatz: faith in Elokim alone is really comprehension; faith proper is in Havayah. Breslov: faith, not inquiry, crosses the vacated space.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 11:3 (line 1111): וזהו עיקר האמונה שבשבילה נברא האדם, להאמין ד״לית אתר פנוי מיניה״ The content of faith is the unity itself: no place is empty of Him. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 18:14 (line 262): והיינו, משום שה׳ אחד מאיר ומחיה כל הנפש, על ידי התלבשותו בבחינת חכמה שבה, שהיא למעלה מן הדעת והשכל המושג ומובן Why every Jew believes, even without knowledge: the One God Himself shines in the soul's chochmah, above comprehension (F06). Faith is the unity present in the soul. - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 9:7 (line 833): והנה, אין לנו עסק בנסתרות, אך ״הנגלות לנו״, להאמין אמונה שלימה ד״איהו וגרמוהי חד״ The Alter Rebbe's limit: how He is one with His attributes is beyond comprehension and held by faith (the spine's F12 disagreement).

## S144 · Trust · בטחון

**Face:** F01 The world said into being now · **also touches:** F02 The higher and the lower unity; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** Trust is leaning your whole weight on the One who is making you now, with no other support in the world.

**Hold.** 'That a person lean on the Holy One until he casts his whole lot into His hand, having no support in the world besides Him' (the Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos 36).

**The move.** Open it. The Besht's circle starts from the ground: every good and every judgment is rooted in Him, and His life is in everything, so there is no one else to trust or fear. The Rebbe says calm because all is from Him is not yet trust; trust is a service of its own, leaning on Him with your whole weight. And the Rayatz asks for complete trust in providence above nature at all times, because nature itself is hidden light. Bring it down to the one matter you are carrying today, a bill, a result, a job: the matter, not the worry. The picture is the verse's, casting your burden on Him; it is only a picture, since the work is not lifted from your hands: you still do what the natural way requires, only you do not lean on it. You, here, now: no support besides Him.

**Stay.** 'No support besides Him', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being held, not holding. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'Cast your burden upon the Lord.' Then do the one practical thing the matter needs today, as the Rebbe asks trust to go together with the natural way. Line for the day: 'Think good and it will be good.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the core is stated (the Rebbe's 'no support besides Him', the Besht's circle's 'only Him'); that trust itself brings the good ('think good') is the Rebbe's reading of the Tzemach Tzedek, and its tie to the unity is ours, so it appears here only as the line for the day.

**Where the sources differ.** Trust and means: the Maharash, with Rabbeinu Bachya, holds that for a tzaddik like Yosef even asking the butler was a fault; Chovot HaLevavot (as the Maharash cites it) requires engaging the means; the Rebbe asks for complete trust together with doing all that nature requires.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tzava'at HaRivash 137:5 (line 288): ובו ית' מושרשים כל הטובות והדינים שיש בעולם. שבכל דבר יש שפעו וחיותו. ואין אני בוטח ומתירא רק ממנו ית' The Besht's circle: all goods and judgments are rooted in Him and His vitality is in everything (F01, F09), so one trusts and fears only Him. - Likkutei Sichos vol. 36, Shemos 1, 4:9-5:1 (line 25002): שהאדם יסמוך וישען על הקב"ה, עד שישליך כל גורלו ביד השם, כמ"ש "השלך על ה' יהבך", שאין לו שום משען … בעולם בלעדו ית' The Rebbe defines trust as its own service: to lean on Him until one has no support in the world besides Him. That this is 'ein od milvado' lived is our reading, but 'besides Him' is the text's. - Likkutei Sichos vol. 36, Shemos 1, 4:7 (line 25000): טראכט גוט — וועט זיין גוט The Tzemach Tzedek's saying (via the Rayatz): 'think good and it will be good'. The Rebbe reads it as trust itself bringing visible good, measure for measure.

## S145 · Doubt and heresy · ספק / כפירה

**Face:** F01 The world said into being now · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; F04 Filling and surrounding **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**Before you start.** If doubts come as a loop you keep checking and cannot settle, that loop is not this practice: hold the line once, and set the checking down.

**The glimpse.** The world is not a vessel that stands after its maker goes home: it needs His word every moment. And the doubts that come are not yours.

**Hold.** 'The power of the Maker in the made', at every moment: what is made from nothing needs it, the answer to those who picture the world as a smith's vessel (Shaar HaYichud 2).

**The move.** Open it. Shaar HaYichud names the root of every denial of providence: picturing His making of the world like a smith's vessel, which stands after the smith goes home. But what is made from nothing needs the Maker's power in it always, or it would not be there. Heresy is only the far end of what any separation is: the Tanya says every transgression is 'as if denying His oneness'. And doubts that fall into the mind, it says, are the other side's words alone; it has no doubts itself and is only allowed to confuse, for Israel are believers, children of believers. Bring it down to the cup or the chair in front of you. The picture is the smith's vessel; it is only a picture, and the one that fails, since He is not a craftsman who could step away. You, here, now: the world in front of you is being made from nothing.

**Stay.** 'Made from nothing, now', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** Solid ground under the question: 'believers, children of believers', the Tanya says of Israel; the doubt is a voice passing through. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips, when a doubt comes today: 'Believers, children of believers', and go on with what you were doing. Then learn one line of Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah today. Line for the day: 'It needs Him now.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: for heresy the tie is stated (Shaar HaYichud 2, Tanya 24, Likutei Moharan 64); for doubt as Amalek's cooling the texts speak of coldness toward service, and the tie to the unity is ours, so doubt is taken here only through Tanya 29.

**Where the sources differ.** How may the vacated space be spoken of? R. Nachman: the second heresy comes from the vacated space, where 'as it were' there is no Godliness, yet 'in very truth Godliness is there too'; no inquiry crosses it, only faith. The Alter Rebbe: the tzimtzum is concealment only, and to take it literally is a serious error. Both deny a space truly empty of Him; they differ on whether its emptiness is to be held as real from our side.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 2:1 (line 764): והנה, מכאן תשובת המינים וגילוי שורש טעותם, הכופרים בהשגחה פרטית ובאותות ומופתי התורה, שטועים בדמיונם הכוזב, שמדמין מעשה ה׳ The root of heresy is the craftsman model: the world imagined as a vessel that no longer needs its maker. Continuous creation (F01) is the answer. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 29:25-26 (line 405): שנופלים לו במחשבתו ספקות על אמונה, כי הם דברי רוח הסטרא אחרא לבדה … וגם הסטרא אחרא עצמה, אין לה ספיקות כלל באמונה Doubts are not the Jew's own; they are the voice of the other side, which itself has no doubts and is only permitted to confuse. Israel are 'believers, children of believers'.

## S146 · Simplicity · תמימות ופשיטות

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity; F05 The Essence beyond both **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**The glimpse.** Before Him, who is beyond every mind, all are simple; the simple point in the soul, which holds to Him without needing to understand or to feel itself holding, is the deepest there is.

**Hold.** 'By being "brutish" and "as a beast" before You, "I am always with You"' (Tanya 18, on Psalm 73).

**The move.** Open it. Before the One who is beyond intellect, the Tanya says, all are simple. The Mitteler Rebbe finds true simplicity in the yechidah, the soul's single innermost point, shining in the heart, and its sign is that no self-feeling is in it at all. And the Besht opened the door for simple Jews, who through the sincerity of their Tehillim, even without knowing what the words mean, come close to God from within. Bring it down to one psalm said today on the bus or in the kitchen, without stopping to understand it. The picture is the Psalm's, 'as a beast before You'; it is only a picture, since you are not a beast, but it shows a closeness that does not wait on understanding. You, here, now: always with Him.

**Stay.** 'I am always with You', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Plain, with no self in it to check on: the Mitteler Rebbe's sign of the real thing is that the self is not in it. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips one psalm today the way simple Jews say it, meaning it for Him. Then say Modeh Ani tomorrow on waking, before anything else. Line for the day: 'Always with You.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie is stated in the Mitteler Rebbe (simplicity is the yechidah); elsewhere simplicity is praised without an explicit tie to the unity, and that tie is ours.

**Where the sources differ.** Simplicity or comprehension? The Mitteler Rebbe: true temimut is the yechidah. The Rashab (Hayom Yom 12 Teves): simple faith, not the intellect, in the last generations, yet he demands contemplation in prayer. Breslov: 'wisdoms are not needed at all'. The Rayatz: 'to know'.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 18:11 (line 259): כלומר, שבזה שאני ״בער״ ו״בהמות״ – ״אני תמיד עמך״ Before Him who is beyond intellect all are simple; being 'as a beast' before Him is the very condition of 'I am always with You'. - Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 41:20-22 (line 874): והוא להיות שזהו בא מצד בחינת התמימות האמיתית שבלב … ותמימות זו היינו בבחינת יחידה דוקא שמאיר בלב בנקודה הפנימית … בלי הרגש עצמו בזה כלל וכלל The Mitteler Rebbe: true temimut is the yechidah shining in the heart's inner point (F06), and its sign is the absence of self-feeling (F08). - Kuntres Toras HaChassidus, scan p. 5 ¶11 (line 22) *(OCR)*: גם אנשים פשוטים הנה על ידי תמימות עבודתם באמירת תהלים -- אף גם כשאינם יודעים פירוש המלות -- ואהבת ישראל מתקרבים המה בהתקרבות פנימי אל ה' The Rayatz on the Besht: simple Jews reach inner closeness to God through sincerity, without understanding (spine G19), in the same kuntres that demands comprehension.

## S147 · Despair and hope · יאוש ותקוה

**Face:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · **also touches:** F11 Darkness turned to light; F06 The soul as part of the One **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**Before you start.** This is for the 'I will never change' of the spiritual life, not for hopelessness about life itself. If you have thoughts of not wanting to be here, stop and reach a person now: your local emergency number or a crisis line.

**The glimpse.** There is no place so low that He is not there, so no fall is beyond return; even asking 'where is He?' from the bottom already knows that He is hidden there.

**Hold.** 'Let him never despair, for even in the lowest Sheol God is found' (Likutei Moharan 6).

**The move.** Open it. Even there, Likutei Moharan says, one can cleave to Him. The very question 'where is the place of His glory?', asked from where one fell, already knows He is there, only hidden; and the other side lives only from that hiding. So begin again each time, as if today you were starting. Bring it down to one thing in your service you have quietly given up on, a habit you keep falling back into. The picture is the Psalm's, making one's bed in Sheol and finding Him there; it is only a picture, since the depths are not a place but the fall itself. You, here, now: He is where you fell.

**Stay.** 'Even in the lowest depths, God is found', said again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The dignity of starting again, with no need to have climbed first. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'If I make my bed in the depths, You are there.' Then start that one thing again tomorrow morning, as if it were the first day. Line for the day: 'Begin again, as if today.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the stated tie is Breslov's (God is found even in Sheol); in Chabad the words despair and hope rarely carry the argument, and the Chabad tie through the soul's portion of God is our reading, so the meditation keeps to the Breslov lines.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Likutei Moharan 6:11:3 (line 328): גם כן אל יתיאש עצמו לעולם, ותמיד יחפש ויבקש את השם יתברך, ויחזק עצמו בכל מקום שהוא בכל מה שיוכל, כי גם בשאול תחתיות נמצא השם יתברך Breslov states the ground of hope as the unity itself: God is found even in the lowest Sheol, so one can cleave to Him there. (This explanatory section of LM 6 speaks of 'Rabbeinu' in the third person, apparently R. Nosson's.) - Likutei Moharan, Part II 12:2:3 (line 6239): אבל תכף כשמבקשין איה מקום כבודו, נמצא שיודעין עלכלפנים שיש אלוק יתברך, רק שהוא נסתר ונעלם Asking 'where is the place of His glory?' from the place of the fall is already knowing He is there, hidden (F03); the kelipot live only from the hiding. - Sichot HaRan 48:3 (line 260): ויתחיל בכל פעם מחדש, כאלו היום התחיל להתקרב להשם יתברך Hope as beginning again each time. That this mirrors the world renewed every moment (F01) is our reading.

## S148 · Death · מיתה / הסתלקות

**Face:** F06 The soul as part of the One · **also touches:** F05 The Essence beyond both; F02 The higher and the lower unity **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** yes

**Before you start.** This is about the passing of tzaddikim and the life that goes on in their teaching; it is not about wanting to die, and not for a fresh loss carried alone. If thoughts of not wanting to be here are around, reach a person now: your local emergency number or a crisis line.

**The glimpse.** What dies is the vessel; a life made of faith, awe and love is not bound to it. And dying into Him is never the aim: the aim is His revelation here, in life.

**Hold.** 'The life of the tzaddik is not the life of flesh but spiritual life: faith, awe and love' (Iggeret HaKodesh 27).

**The move.** Open it. What passes is the vessel. After his passing, no longer held in a vessel or a physical place, the tzaddik is found in all the worlds more than in his life, and those close to him can receive more of his spirit. The Rebbe adds that the word for his passing, 'histalkus', means the revelation of a light higher than the worlds. And the Rashab sets the limit: running toward Him without coming back is death; what is intended is the revelation here below. Bring it down to the yahrzeit candle of a tzaddik, or of a teacher you learned from, and one thing he taught. The picture is spirit leaving a vessel; it is only a picture, since faith, awe and love were never kept in the vessel. You, here, now: his teaching is alive in you.

**Stay.** 'Faith, awe and love', said again slowly. If other thoughts come, go back to the line and do not answer them.

**From inside.** What Iggeret HaKodesh 27 describes is closeness: the one who passed is found more, not less, by those who take up his teaching. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips one teaching of that tzaddik or teacher, aloud. Then do the one thing it asks, today. Line for the day: 'Faith, awe and love live on.'

**Thin.** Partly thin: the tie is stated for martyrdom, for the tzaddik's passing and for 'running without return is death'; for death in general (mortality, the angel of death, burial) no verified passage ties it to the unity, and this meditation does not force it.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 27:11 (line 1427): כנודע, שחיי הצדיק אינם חיים בשרים כי אם חיים רוחניים, שהם אמונה ויראה ואהבה Life is defined by faith, fear and love, not flesh; so death removes only the vessel. - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 27:14 (line 1430): יכול כל הקרוב אליו לקבל חלק מבחינת רוחו שבגן עדן, הואיל ואינה בתוך כלי ולא בבחינת מקום גשמי After passing, the tzaddik's spirit is no longer bounded by a vessel or physical place, so he is 'found more' even in this world. - Hayom Yom, 27 Adar II (line 193): ורצוא בלי שוב, היא מיתה The Rashab (via the Rayatz): the sons of Aharon died of running to God without return; the first tzimtzum's intent is revelation. Calling this the higher unity without the lower (F02, F03) is our naming.

## S149 · The afterlife: reward and punishment · גן עדן וגיהנם / שכר ועונש

**Face:** F07 The home below · **also touches:** F04 Filling and surrounding; F05 The Essence beyond both; F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual **Tie:** stated · **Thin:** no

**The glimpse.** The reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah: in it you hold Him Himself, while the World to Come is a radiance. The whole end is His revelation here, in this physical world.

**Hold.** 'What causes the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah itself' (Tanya 37).

**The move.** Open it. The World to Come, the Tanya says, is enjoying a radiance of the Shechinah, and no creature grasps more than a radiance; but in a mitzvah in this world one grasps the Holy One Himself. So one hour of teshuvah and good deeds here is better than all the life of the World to Come. The end of it all is the revelation of His light in this physical world, which depends on what we do now; and the Rebbe adds that at the resurrection, what the mitzvot drew down will be revealed in the body itself. Bring it down to the next mitzvah you will do today, the coin, the candle, the bread for a guest. The picture is reward as wages after work; it is only a picture, since here the work is the reward. You, here, now: holding Him in the mitzvah.

**Stay.** 'The mitzvah is the reward', said again slowly. When the mind wanders, return to the line; the stray thought gets no reply.

**From inside.** Wanting Him more than anything He gives: the Alter Rebbe's 'I do not want Your Gan Eden… I want only You Yourself'. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with the lips: 'I want only You Yourself.' Then do one mitzvah today with no thought of reward. Line for the day: 'The mitzvah is the reward.'

**Where the sources differ.** What is the highest reward? Tanya 4: this world's mitzvah over all the World to Come. Tanya 37, Iggeret HaKodesh 17 and the Rebbe (5746): the resurrection in the body, above Gan Eden. The Alter Rebbe and the Besht: not reward at all, only Him.

**Note.** The meditation leaves Gehinnom aside: the entry reads it as cleansing, not revenge, and ties nothing more of it to the unity.

**Anchors** (the entry's verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 4:12 (line 104): כי עולם הבא הוא שנהנין מזיו השכינה, שהוא תענוג ההשגה, ואי אפשר לשום נברא, אפילו מהעליונים, להשיג, כי אם איזו הארה מאור ה׳ The World to Come is only a radiance; Torah and mitzvot in this world grasp the Holy One Himself. Hence 'one hour of teshuvah and good deeds in this world is better than all the life of the World to Come'. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 37:1-2 (line 495): תכלית השלימות הזה של ימות המשיח ותחיית המתים, שהוא גילוי אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא בעולם הזה הגשמי … כי הגורם שכר המצוה – היא המצוה בעצמה The ultimate reward is the revelation of Or Ein Sof in this physical world (F07), and the reward is caused by the mitzvah itself (F10). - Toras Menachem 5713, Nitzavim-Vayelech, 35 (line 15559): אינני רוצה את הגן-עדן שלך, אינני רוצה את העולם הבא שלך כו', רוצה אני אותך בעצמך The Alter Rebbe in devekut: not Your Gan Eden, not Your World to Come, only You Yourself. The Rebbe (next line) reads it as leaving one's own existence and limits.
