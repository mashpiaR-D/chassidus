*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** Two pastoral letters from 1814 to the Chassidim. The first is short: people are worn down by earning a living, many are close to despair, and he offers to travel and hold study gatherings for followers who live far away. The second is the Kuntres itself, a detailed guide to the feelings that contemplation and prayer produce. It separates real divine feeling from counterfeit, lays out five levels in the natural soul and five in the divine soul, sorts people into five types, and names the self-deceptions at each level. It attacks two opposite errors: forbidding all feeling in the name of depth, and chasing the feeling itself. It gives signs a person can use to test himself.

**How much of it is medicine.** core. Nearly the whole work diagnoses inner states: dryness, craving highs, inspiration that fades, spiritual pride, numbness, melancholy. It sets these inside a Kabbalistic map of the soul's levels and addresses Chassidim of 1814.

**Its model of the person.** A person has two souls. The natural soul comes from the parents, is made of "nogah" (a mix of good and bad) and has ten powers. It can be moved by divine ideas only indirectly and through effort. It has five levels: plain assent, cold thought, "good thought" that touches the self, the heart being moved, and deep intent in the mind, with simple will above. The divine soul is "a part of G-d" and needs no repair. Its feeling is essential and unaware of itself. At its core, the yechidah (the soul's innermost point) is bound to G-d in everyone. It breaks through in martyrdom, on Yom Kippur and in true return. Change comes from laboured contemplation carried from mind to heart, fixed in action, on the ground of a broken heart. How much light a person holds depends on how self-nullified his "vessels" are.

**Its philosophy.** G-d is "your life". Being attached to Him is life itself, and the soul is attached by nature. Being moved by G-d is as natural to the divine soul as bodily desire is to the body. Holiness means the vessel is nullified. Its opposite (nogah) means the self swells past the light it carries, "saying much and doing little". So genuine feeling does not feel itself, and a feeling that inflates the self is counterfeit, however strong it is.

**What it adds.** The Chabad library's dedicated phenomenology of spiritual feeling. It maps levels of feeling in both souls and gives testable signs for telling real from counterfeit: real feeling is unaware of itself, lasts, flows into action, and survives insult. Its central rule is that the deeper the feeling, the less it feels itself. It defends emotion against intellectual suppression and also exposes the ego in chasing highs. Sharpest of all, it diagnoses self-deception by the fruits a feeling leaves.

## Work and money have drained me; I've drifted so far I think it's hopeless for me

*Its own term:* טרדת הפרנסה; יאמר נואש לנפשו

Years of business pressure. Chassidus feels far away, the heart and mind feel empty, and the person starts to write himself off.

**What is really going on.** Nothing has changed in his essence. His mind and heart are emptied and busy, so the life in them has gone dormant, like a sleeper's. The real cause of falling is letting a livelihood take over the depth of the heart.

**Program: Set times of awakening, review with a friend, and one-on-one guidance.** (1) Now and then, turn your heart to how you might wake your soul, so mind and heart are free to take in the teachings. (2) Fix a free time that suits everyone, and meet as a group, not two or three apart. (3) Go over the teaching aloud with a friend, up to a hundred times, each strengthening the other's heart. (4) Do not lord it over the one teaching; stay as one. (5) Seek private guidance (yechidus) aimed at the point of your own soul. *When:* Periodic fixed times through the year. For distant communities, one set month a year at a regional centre. *For:* Busy working people who are far from a teacher and at risk of forgetting altogether. *Not for:* Not a substitute for personal inner work: hearing discourses alone, or hearing them to boast, is criticised. `KH·Q07` · `KH·Q06` · `KH·Q01`

**Reframe.** From *"I've fallen too far; whatever I had is gone."* to **You are asleep, not dead. Your soul's nature to be moved has not changed at all, and a waking touch revives it at once.** The life is hidden, not gone. Seeing this blocks despair, which the work treats as the gateway to falling for good, and it makes awakening something that can happen now. `KH·Q01` · `KH·Q02`

## My prayer is dry. I understand the ideas but they do nothing to me

He knows the teachings, but prayer is pacing, empty and dry. He asks: what use is any of this to my soul?

**What is really going on.** He never learned how to take the ideas into the soul. His thought stays cold and far off, never touching him the way something about himself would, and he has not laboured until the heart is moved. At bottom he lacks a broken heart.

**Program: Contemplation laboured until it reaches the heart, then returned to deepen the mind.** (1) Contemplate a divine idea at length, in breadth and depth, not just its gist. (2) Let it grip you the way news about your own affairs grips your thought. (3) Labour in thought until the heart itself is moved. (4) When the heart is moved, do not forbid it; also do not make it into 'something' or dwell on feeling it. (5) Turn the heart's arousal back into strength for the mind to widen and deepen, so it does not cut the depth short. (6) Keep at it constantly, so the words are fixed in the soul and not just grasped. *When:* Chiefly at prayer, and continually, with diligence. *For:* Anyone who has heard the ideas but whose prayer is dry or whose feeling fades. *Not for:* Not for forcing a feeling for its own sake; the work calls craving the feeling the lowest level. `KH·Q13` · `KH·Q25` · `KH·Q14` · `KH·Q24`

**Program: Build on a broken heart.** (1) Base your service on real brokenness and lowliness over being far from G-d. (2) Pray from seriousness and submission of the heart. (3) Contemplate how far your soul is, and let bitterness and return come. (4) Expect that this sighing, when G-d rests on the soul, turns into joy and delight. *When:* As the underlying ground of all service, especially at prayer. *For:* The self-satisfied, and 'whole' people whose teaching never goes in; the text says the great need it more than the small. *Not for:* Not for people who are clinically depressed or lean to self-loathing; see cautions. `KH·Q20` · `KH·Q23` · `KH·Q05` · `KH·Q21`

**Reframe.** From *"I understood it, I felt something, so I'm done; I'm a Chassid."* to **Understanding is only the start. The living words give life only when laboured into the soul and carried into action.** Light holds only in a vessel, and without constant work the idea shrinks to a hollow outline. Seeing this sends the person back to the work. `KH·Q24` · `KH·Q19` · `KH·Q08`

## I'm afraid to feel anything in prayer; I think emotion is shallow and will spoil my understanding

Sophisticated people suppress every feeling or cry as if it were forbidden. The result is thinking about other things or falling asleep in prayer.

**What is really going on.** A rumour that the old ways of Chassidus rejected felt emotion. It confuses the counterfeit (a loud, bodily cry) with real divine feeling, which is the very aim of Chassidus.

## I chase the spiritual high, and when it doesn't come I'm crushed

He prays to feel something, waits for the rush, and is pained without it. When it comes he enjoys it. He takes the heat of it for closeness to G-d.

**What is really going on.** What he wants is the feeling, not G-d. It is self-feeling and bodily heat. The lowest level, near 'strange fire', it leads to pride and to falls into desire.

**Program: Honest self-diagnosis by the signs.** (1) Find your own place and level; do not reach for levels above you. (2) Check whether the feeling is aware of itself: real feeling is not. (3) Check the aftermath: are you full of yourself, or truly lowly? (4) Test it with insult: does a slight crush you? (5) Look for being drawn all day to closeness in deed, with less indulgence and calm in business. (6) Do not forgive yourself out of self-love, and do not envy others. *When:* Ongoing self-watching, especially after moments of strong feeling. *For:* Anyone who has 'tasted' Chassidus and sincerely seeks closeness. *Not for:* Not for the scrupulous or anxious, who could turn it into endless self-suspicion. `KH·Q29` · `KH·Q28` · `KH·Q16` · `KH·Q30` · `KH·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"If I don't feel it strongly, nothing happened."* to **The deeper the arousal, the less it feels itself: like a child's longing for his father, or joy at good news that moves the hands by itself.** Essential feeling is not aware of itself. Wanting to feel it is self-service. Letting go of the need to register the feeling moves the person from serving himself to serving G-d. `KH·Q16` · `KH·Q11` · `KH·Q09`

**Reframe.** From *"I feel so moved, so I must be close."* to **Much of what feels divine is bodily heat. The test is what it leaves: self-importance and fragility under insult, or real lowliness.** In holiness the vessel is nullified; in the counterfeit the self swells. Judging by fruits rather than intensity exposes self-deception. `KH·Q10` · `KH·Q28`

## I get inspired, and an hour later it's gone, or I do the opposite

Moments of real arousal that vanish right away. Sometimes he acts against them straight after.

**What is really going on.** The arousal was shallow and passing. Then there is no action to hold it: light holds only in a vessel. The heart also cut short the mind's depth, and there is no broken heart underneath.

**Program: Give the light a vessel: act on it at once.** (1) When love or awe is roused, accept the yoke of Heaven with love then and there. (2) Turn it straight into a concrete deed: a commandment done, a wrong avoided. (3) Guard the heart's air from frivolity and bad desire, which smother the light. *When:* Right after any arousal in study or prayer. *For:* People whose inspiration fades quickly, or who do the opposite right after. *Not for:* Not a replacement for inner depth; action holds the light and does not produce it. `KH·Q26` · `KH·Q27`

## I can't feel or understand anything at all; I'm numb

Weighed down by the body and business, he cannot hear divine ideas even from a distance.

**What is really going on.** He is weighed down by the body, worries about business and indulgence. Yet the essential nature of the Jew remains in him, and his own numbness can become the start of return.

**Program: The simple cry of return for one who cannot contemplate.** (1) Notice honestly that you cannot hear or be moved, even in the mind. (2) Let this bring bitterness over the coarseness of the body and remembrance of past wrongs. (3) Cry out to G-d from the depth of the heart, in truth, with no contemplation needed. *When:* At prayer, whenever you find yourself numb. *For:* The busy and the dulled who cannot contemplate. *Not for:* Not for someone who would turn bitterness into self-hatred or despair. `KH·Q18` · `KH·Q05`

**Reframe.** From *"I can't contemplate, so my cry to G-d is worthless."* to **G-d looks to a heart that cries in truth, even without contemplation. It is the divine soul's own arousal, coming from the side of distance.** Weeping for distance and joy in closeness are the same movement of the divine soul. The numb person's cry is therefore a real divine act and not a lesser one. `KH·Q18` · `KH·Q05`

## I'm heavy and melancholy by temperament

A heaviness of spirit that feels like part of his nature.

**What is really going on.** The work separates two things. The natural soul's melancholy is over bodily lack. The divine soul's bitterness is over being far from G-d, and that is the true broken heart, which turns into joy when G-d rests on the soul.

**Program: Build on a broken heart.** (1) Base your service on real brokenness and lowliness over being far from G-d. (2) Pray from seriousness and submission of the heart. (3) Contemplate how far your soul is, and let bitterness and return come. (4) Expect that this sighing, when G-d rests on the soul, turns into joy and delight. *When:* As the underlying ground of all service, especially at prayer. *For:* The self-satisfied, and 'whole' people whose teaching never goes in; the text says the great need it more than the small. *Not for:* Not for people who are clinically depressed or lean to self-loathing; see cautions. `KH·Q20` · `KH·Q23` · `KH·Q05` · `KH·Q21`

## Cautions

- The broken-heart ideal, as the Maggid is quoted, reaches 'loathing his life constantly, every hour'. That is dangerous to offer anyone depressed or suicidal. - Its distinction between natural and soul melancholy needs clinical judgement. It should not be used to reframe clinical depression as spiritual depth. - Constant self-suspicion ('you are deceiving yourself', the insult test) can feed scrupulosity, anxiety or harsh self-doubt. - It dismisses visions and 'the soul leaving' as blood-heat and a defect of the mind's vessels. Such experiences may need medical attention, not moral verdicts. - It includes claims that contrast Jewish and gentile souls (lines 493, 504-506). These cannot be carried into a universal guide as they stand. - The ideal of minimal involvement in business is not licence to neglect livelihood duties or dependents. - 'Labour until the heart is moved' can produce forced emotion. The text itself warns against craving the feeling. - It criticises the 'new' young who began with wisdom and no broken heart. Applied naively, this shames young seekers.
