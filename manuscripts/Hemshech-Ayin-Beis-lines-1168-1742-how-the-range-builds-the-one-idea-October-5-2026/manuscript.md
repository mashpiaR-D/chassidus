## The shape of the range

The earlier ranges of this series asked how the limited comes from Ein Sof and still shows it. This range asks how anything connects to the Essence (atzmus) itself. The theme appears in many forms:

- the "intermediary" (memutza) between Ein Sof and the worlds: the line, Atzilut, Adam Kadmon, and finally Keter and hidden Chochmah; - the two unions "He and His life" and "He and His bones"; - the three knots: the Holy One, Torah and Israel; - will and delight; - the surround that is hidden only from us, against the surround that is hidden in itself.

That is why F05 dominates the count (213 of 575 line entries). F10 (Torah and mitzvah) is second, because the recurring answer is that Torah is where the Essence is found. The Tanya's cosmological faces (F01, F02, F04) come last in number. Yet the range states the higher unity as strongly as any text in the index: "there is nothing outside His Essence" (1223), "Havayah is One: everything is in complete oneness" (1480). What is new is a hard limit on who can know that, and how (1548-1566).

## Chukat end and Balak (1168-1210): a ladder of contemplation, and why the world was "established"

The Chukat maamar ends with the red heifer. It is wisdom above Atzilut, and through Torah and mitzvot "the very essence of wisdom is drawn" (1168, F10).

Chapter 150 is a full ladder of contemplation, as clear as anything in the Kuntres HaAvodah: - The "something" must come from the Divine nothing, because an endless chain of somethings is impossible (1179). - The world is composite, so it cannot exist from itself (1180). - The world lives, so its life is Godliness: > וכמו"כ יבין בעולם שרואה שהעולם חי הרי יש בו חיות המחי' אותו והחיות הוא אלקות (line 1184) - All of it is only a radiance beyond comparison to the Essence (1188). - Above that, the Essence is exalted. That very separateness is why He is below as above: > דהגם דעצמות אוא"ס מובדל לגמרי מעולמות התחתונים והעליונים כו', ה"ה הנותנת שנמצא למטה כמו למע' (line 1189)

The ladder also separates two roots of creation. Creation by the Divine nothing stands in some nearness to what it makes. The true root is the Essence, "He alone in His power and ability", and that is utterly beyond comparison (1180). This is the Iggeres HaKodesh 20 formula that the previous range did not state (SPINE F05a). Here it is stated, and again at 1217: "in making something from absolute nothing" the Essence's power shows.

Chapter 151 is psychology. The logic of contemplation only "surrounds" the heart. What moves the heart is the Godly *feeling*, because the divine soul is drawn to Godliness by nature (1193).

Balak's answer to the Midrash ("God sought to establish the world") is a direct F07 statement: > שהרי אין הכוונה בהתהוות העולם בעצם מציאו' היש, כ"א תכלית הכוונה הוא שבעולם יאיר גילוי אלקו' (line 1207)

The Patriarchs "earned" it (zachu). By their service they drew from Chochmah, above the "line of measure", so the light reached even Malchus and the husks could not feed (1204, 1208).

## Pinchas (1211-1253): He and His life, He and His bones; nothing outside the Essence

Chapter 153 introduces the Tikkunim pair. "He and His bones are one" is Ein Sof united with the vessels in order to create, "since creating is by the power of Ein Sof alone" (1213). The vessels are limit. Limit is as much the Essence's power as the unlimited is, and the Essence is above both: > שהרי עצמו' הוא למע' מבחי' גבול ומבחי' בל"ג ושניהם שוין לגבי' (line 1216)

From the unlimited we know *that* the Essence exists, never *how* it is (1219-1220). This is F12's limit, stated as a limit.

Chapter 154 states the higher unity without qualification. The creature's *feeling* of being a something is still real, and it has a source: > והגם שאין לך דבר שחוץ לעצמותו שהרי אוא"ס מאיר לאחה"צ כמו קוה"צ (line 1223)

> וממילא אין העולמות בבחי' מציאות (line 1223, sentence 1)

> אינם בבחינת מציאות כלל כו', מ"מ הרי הנבראים מרגישים א"ע למציאו' יש (line 1224)

That feeling, and the life that keeps each creature existing, come from the power of limit in Ein Sof (1224-1225). The Tanya's F02 is thus given a mechanism for the lower unity.

Chapter 155 has the soul come down as "the lamp of God" to light the world. Only Torah and mitzvot draw the Essence, which is what turns darkness to light (1230). It cites Tanya 35 on bitul: > משא"כ המצות שהן פנימי' רצונו ית' כו', מיוחד ונכלל ברצונו ית' והיו לאחדים ממש ביחוד גמור (line 1231)

The maamar ends with Nadav and Avihu. The Shema's three names are read with the Rokeach: "Ein Sof is alone and unique after the tzimtzum as before" (1249). The contemplation brings a running toward the Essence. But the running must be followed by a return, into Torah, "where the Essence is found" (1250). Pinchas's self-sacrifice was itself a mitzvah. So he returned Israel "not only from separation but into His very unity" (1251, F11).

## Matot and Masei (1254-1330): measure, Torah's strength, resting, and no equality with Ein Sof

Every revelation is measured (1258-1259). There are two measures: the "line" (kav, gevurah), which limits so the next sefirah can exist, and the "reed" (kaneh, chesed), which sweetens judgment (1269). Torah's measure is gevurah as *overwhelming strength*. The Essence is not by nature drawn down, so the mitzvot split into many only because of that strength: > וא"ז התחלקות כי הכל מהות א', וההתחלקות הוא רק מצד התגבורת (line 1273)

This is the clearest statement in the range that the many commandments are one Essence.

Chapters 160-161 ask how there can be an intermediary between the infinite and the finite, when "before the infinite the smallest and largest limits are equal" (1281). The answer is the line. It is Ein Sof "estimating itself" to shine in limit, and it bears the imprint of the unlimited (1283-1286). Atzilut is "like and in proportion to" the Emanator (1289). Its vessels too are "Godliness itself" (1291-1293). The intermediary serves creation and also *knowledge*. What reaches BYA through it is only knowledge that Godliness exists, and ordinary souls reach even that only as surmise. Through Torah and mitzvot there is real revelation (1294-1295).

Masei introduces "resting" (hashra'ah) against "clothing" (1306-1310). Ein Sof rests only where there is bitul. Resting is the light itself, hidden. Clothing is a grasped radiance. Tanya 35's "the One God rests on him" is read as knowing and feeling the unity, not seeing His essence (1309).

Chapter 164 has the strongest philosophical statement of the range. The earlier kabbalists explained everything as cause and effect, which leaves some "equality" between emanator and emanated. The Ari's tzimtzum removes that: > ובאמת לא יתכן כלל לו' שום השתוות לגבי א"ס ב"ה (line 1322)

> דמש"א אין ערוך לך היינו לגבי עצמות דוקא דכל ההתהוות היא באין ערוך לגמרי לגבי העצמות (line 1326)

## Devarim and Va'etchanan (1331-1427): Adam Kadmon, time, singleness, and the humiliated king

Adam Kadmon is the "whole" that contains all worlds and all 6000 years, but without existence: > אך מה שכולל הכל אינו בבחי' מציאות כלל עד שכולם הם בו בהשוואה דאצי' ועשי' שוין ממש (line 1339)

Adam Kadmon is time and not-time: one glance, yet no before and after (1370-1372). Here the Rashab gives his own version of the SPINE's yachid/echad distinction: > דבד"כ ענין היחידות שולל ענין המציאות (line 1371)

Whatever exists is by nature many and could be doubled. Adam Kadmon is called "one thought", echad not yachid, and that shows it is not yet existence. Torah comes from this level and so is above time, while mitzvot are bound to their time and place (1373). Mitzvot then are particular revelations, and Torah even in its details is "essential" (1374).

Va'etchanan reads Torah as "the parable of the Ancient One" in the strong sense (1383, F05/F10): the meaning itself is clothed in it, not a radiance. The purpose of creation is to draw Ein Sof into the worlds, through Torah (1388). Chapter 172 gives the range's sharpest F11 idea: > וכנודע דהגבלה רצונית אינו הגבלה וכמו"כ צמצום רצוני אינו צמצום (line 1402)

Every contraction in the chain is willed, and so is not really a contraction. Sin makes it *as if compelled*, because He bound Himself to Israel: "the king is bound in tresses". This is exile of the Shechinah, and it is the reason for bitterness at midnight (1402-1403).

Chapter 173 contrasts inner Torah with outer: "before Ein Sof all worlds are as nothing, and so before inner Torah" (1406). Chapter 174 draws the servant whose own delight is the Master's delight, "because he has set aside his self and his whole being is the Master's" (1420).

## Eikev, Shoftim and Ki Tetze (1428-1531): three knots, the bitul of "ein od", and "Havayah is One"

"Torah itself is the Essence of Ein Sof", so only Torah can receive the Essence (1432). At Sinai they saw the unity of God "with physical sight" (1433). The Zohar's three knots are ordered. Binding (hitkashrut) joins separate things. Cleaving (dveikut) belongs to light and source. Israel binding to Torah is what binds Torah to the Holy One: > והתקשרות זאת דתו' בהקב"ה נעשה עי"ז שישראל מתקשראין באורייתא (line 1438)

After prayer, study "for its own sake" is not to quench one's thirst but "to draw the Essence below" (1446-1447). Prayer's contemplation, even of "the Essence of Ein Sof", reaches only the Ein Sof that relates to worlds, "for contemplation does not reach the Essence" (1444).

Chapter 178 explains the advantage of the soul below. It is a separate thing, a "bride" (kallah, from kilayon, expiry), and its love burns. Then comes a precise F02/F08 point: > והביטול דאין עוד הוא בבחי' יש דוקא (line 1458)

The bitul of "as nothing" belongs to light. The bitul of "there is nothing else" belongs to a something that sets itself aside, and that exists only below.

Shoftim states the higher unity through the Tanya's own reading of "I God have not changed": > וממילא הרי גם לגבי ש' הוי' הכל בטל ממש שאינו בבחי' מציאו' כלל שז"ע הוי' אחד שהכל הוא בבחי' אחדות גמורה (line 1480)

It treats the failure to be moved by this as a husk (the "donkey", cold even in Tammuz) that only toil in Torah melts (1479-1483).

Ki Tetze corrects the earlier distinction. Even outer Torah is "all one", for two reasons: the essential delight reaches every level of it, and "the very wisdom of Torah, its knowing and grasping, is the Essence of Ein Sof" (1505). Kelipat nogah's end is not annihilation but transformation: > כ"א שתצא מטומאתה ומחולאתה ותתהפך לקדושה, והוא הענין שלעתיד יתהפך הגוף ויהי' אלקו' (line 1521)

Messiah will reveal inner Atik, "as seeing the essence with the eye, equally in every Jew" (1525).

## Ki Tavo (1532-1570): the higher unity cannot be grasped by a creature

This is the range's most important and most sober section. The higher knowledge is Ein Sof's own knowledge: > דהדעה העליונה היא איך שכולא קמי' כל"ח והיא הדיעה דאוא"ס ב"ה (line 1548)

> ואיך יכול היש לתפוס את האין (line 1548, sentence 1)

A creature cannot form it. Grasping is itself a "something". What we picture as "nothing" is really existence (1549). Light is "nothing" only relative to its source, and that "nothing" we do not reach even by surmise (1550). One can *understand* that worlds are nothing before the Essence, but: > זהו רק ההשגה לבד, אבל שיהי' בדרך הנחה במוחו ובהרגש הענין ומכ"ש שיהי' הביטול באופן כזה, א"ז בנבראים מכח עצמן (line 1551)

> אבל בחי' ד"ע והביטול שבזה א"ז בנבראי' מצ"ע כ"א כאשר נותנים מלמע' ידיעה והשגה זו וביטול זה (line 1552)

It is given from above, only to the divine soul, through the Written Torah (the name mah) (1553-1554). Even souls of Atzilut need Torah for it (1556-1558). Yet every divine soul is of itself "nullified in Ein Sof like a candle before a torch". Only its clothing in the animal soul dulls it (1558). So souls of Asiyah mostly lack the higher-unity bitul, while souls of Beriah and Yetzirah, like the Tannaim and Amoraim, can have it (1559). The Oral Torah makes bitul in the animal soul too, "even a heart of stone melts" (1563). The Talmud says Israel made Him "one entity" and He made Israel "one nation" (1533-1534). The maamar was to link these, but the explanation is missing in the manuscript (1569).

## Rosh Hashanah and Shabbat Teshuvah (1571-1663): will and delight, teshuvah, and the intermediary to the Essence

Chochmah can hide even the essential delight, because the one who hides must be near what he hides: "not a foreign thing". The hiding is for revelation (1578-1579, F03). Chapter 193 reads "He is not a man that He should relent" as the essential will that has no reason and never changes. The will of the mitzvot is of that kind. "Mitzvot will be void" means that before the revealed delight they will take up no place (1583). Teshuvah is not God changing His mind. It reaches the essential delight above the will of the mitzvot, and the will is drawn anew (1583-1585), "because Jewish souls are rooted in the Essence of Ein Sof" (1586).

Chapters 194-196 build the last intermediary. It is not for creating but so that the emanated may *connect* to the Essence. "Emanated something" already denies real somethingness: > הרי א"ז בבחי' יש ממש ח"ו דמאחר שנק' יש נאצל הרי ש' אצי' שולל מבחי' יש (line 1588)

> ובאמת הרי גם עצמו' האור דאוא"ס שלפה"צ נמצא ונמשך גם לאחה"צ (line 1590)

The intermediary must be a third level of one essence, with something of each side (1604). Atik and Arich are too far apart for this. It is hidden Chochmah: its inside is the Essence as wisdom, and its outside is the source of the emanated (1607-1610). Souls, made "with a connection to Ein Sof", can therefore receive every light of Torah and mitzvot "without being nullified out of existence" (1594).

The Shabbat Teshuvah maamar gives the essential will its human face. The soul's bond with God is will without pleasure, seen in self-sacrifice (1645). It never changes, "even amid many physical pleasures" (1649). The distance of sin is only outer: > ובאמת גם הריחוק הזה הוא רק בחיצוני' דהרי גם מחיצה של ברזל אינה מפסקת בין ישראל לאביהם שבשמים (line 1642)

At the height of Rosh Hashanah the whole chain "takes up no place", so He must "remember" it, through Israel (1640).

## Sukkot and Shemini Atzeret (1664-1742): hidden in itself, the knot, ten not eleven

Before the tzimtzum there is "Havayah without letters", the essential hiddenness (1680). The Rashab now distinguishes two kinds of hiddenness: hidden from receivers, which can come out, and hidden in itself, which by nature hides further (1688-1689). Messiah's revelation belongs to the second kind, which is why "he comes unawares". Its "revelation" is by the Essence's own power, "a gift in secret" (1691). Radla is the "supernal knot" that binds the Essence to worlds and chiefly to Israel: > וזהו אין מזל לישראל דהפי' הוא שהאין הוא המזל דישראל (line 1671)

> וע"ד שא' שכשאתה תופס במקצת מן העצם אתה תופס בכולו (line 1671, sentence 1)

Essential light, unlike a radiance, "is above hidden and revealed and is drawn as it is" (1704).

The last maamar explains why holiness has ten sefirot "and not eleven": the lights are truly one with the vessels (1715). The vessel for Godliness is bitul, "whatever is nullified to Godliness is its vessel" (1717). The other side has no bitul ("I am, and there is none besides me"), yet it must draw life from Ein Sof "or it would not exist at all" (1718). It has that life only as a distant surround, so it is in essence absence. That is why a test vanishes entirely when resisted (1719-1720). Offerings are the outer heart: "as the soul fills the body, the Holy One fills the world" (1729-1732). Incense is the inner heart, "whom have I in heaven", the wish only to be included in the Essence (1733). It is through incense that the husk's surround, and so the husk, is nullified (1741). The range ends in the middle of the explanation of the inverted four worlds of the other side (1742).

## What this range adds to the Tanya, or says differently

- **The higher unity is known only as a gift.** Tanya Part II and ch. 33 invite contemplation of "all is as nothing before Him". Here the Rashab says a creature can understand this but cannot hold it or be nullified by it "from its own power". It is given from above, only to the divine soul, through the Written Torah (1551-1554). Even souls of Atzilut need Torah for it (1556). The Tanya does not set this limit.
- **Two kinds of bitul.** The bitul of light is "as nothing". The bitul of "ein od" belongs precisely to a something (1458). This tells the SPINE's F08 which bitul the lower soul actually has.
- **Contraction by will is no contraction.** Sin makes the tzimtzum "as if compelled", and that is exile (1402-1403). The Tanya has the humiliated king (ch. 24) but not this account of why exile is worse than the chain itself.
- **Mechanism for the lower unity.** Creatures are no existence, yet they feel themselves to be something. That feeling, and their continued existence, come from Ein Sof's power of limit, "He and His bones" (1224-1225).
- **The intermediary to the Essence.** The Tanya's Iggeres HaKodesh 20 is extended: there must be a level holding the Essence as wisdom, so that the emanated and souls can connect to the Essence itself (1588-1610).
- **Singleness negates existence.** The Rashab's own yachid/echad point: "one thought" is echad, not yachid, and still not existence (1371). This supports the SPINE's precision paragraph from a different author.
- **Nogah transformed, not destroyed.** "It will leave its impurity and turn into holiness; in the future the body will become Godliness" (1521). This matches SPINE F11 and adds the body.

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- 17 lines are *none*: 16 maamar headings (1170, 1211, 1254, 1302, 1331, 1379, 1428, 1467, 1500, 1532, 1571, 1601, 1630, 1664, 1696, 1725) and 1569, where the manuscript is missing. - 117 line entries carry no face. Most are *kitzur* summaries, the questions that open each maamar, and kabbalistic mappings: the four elements and Akudim-Nekudim-Berudim (1347), the hairs of Z'A and the Nukva (1492-1495), the thirteen attributes (1699-1700), and the sukkah under a tree (1692). - Long psychological passages are structural, not *stated*: how a teacher teaches (1196, 1384-1386, 1542-1543), the sharp and the measured scholar (1615-1617), pleasure in a house built to one's design (1656-1657), the two surrounds in learning (1682-1686). The text uses them to build the sefirot. It does not tie each line to the unity. - Only 2 rows are *reading*: 1186 (the soul's three levels mirrored in Godliness, F06) and 1359 (the small one who "takes up no place" as bitul, F08). Elsewhere the text states its tie. - Where a line cites the Tanya (5, 18, 19, 21, 23, 24, 35, 36) the row says so in "how". The tie is still the Rashab's own use of the Tanya.

## Disagreements kept, not smoothed

- **Torah over mitzvot here; deed over study in Samach Vav.** In this range Torah is "He and His life", truly one, and above time. Mitzvot are "limbs" and "He and His bones", bound to time (1373, 1470). The Samach Vav reports record the same Rashab ranking the deed's reach into the Essence above study's revelation. Both stand. This range also has mitzvot giving the true bitul that even a perfect tzaddik lacks (1231, from Tanya 35).
- **Outer Torah: bound, or one?** Chapters 179-182 say outer Torah is only "bound" and only its inner delight is "all one" (1489, 1503). Chapter 183 then says that even outer Torah is truly one, because its wisdom itself is the Essence (1504-1505). The Rashab gives both and reconciles them only by "two reasons". The rows follow each line.
- **Can a low soul have the higher unity?** 1558 says every divine soul, "even the lowest", is of itself nullified like a candle before a torch. 1559 says souls of Asiyah do not have the higher-unity bitul. The Rashab's own bridge is that clothing in the animal soul dulls it. Both are kept.
- **Tanya and Iggeres HaKodesh against the Pardes and the Ma'arechet on vessels.** For the Ma'arechet and Avodas HaKodesh the vessels are the Essence itself. For the Alter Rebbe they are limit (1714-1715). The Rashab concludes that on every view they are one with the lights. He keeps the views distinct.
- **Keter as intermediary.** Chapter 195 names Atik and Arich as the two sides of the intermediary. Chapter 196 rejects that as insufficient and moves the intermediary to hidden Chochmah (1603-1610). The later answer is the Rashab's, and the earlier one is marked stated where the line states it.
- **Unsure.** The chapter labels were assigned by script from the Hebrew markers, which in this range always open a line, and were checked to run in sequence from 148 to 214. The maamar dates in `where` come from the file's running heads. The range ends in the middle of chapter 214.
