## The shape of the range

1. **Why prophecy stopped** (2089-2094). Baruch ben Neriah was ready, but the Temple and the generation were not.
2. **What prophecy is** (2095-2128). Moses compared with the other prophets and with Balaam; vision and dream; prophecy comes from the unity itself, not from an angel.
3. **Angels and garments** (2129-2141). Higher things reach lower ones only through a garment.
4. **Sinai** (2142-2195). All souls stood there. The voice was not created. All Israel heard all ten words and became prophets in the whole Torah.
5. **The Exodus, above and below** (2196-2202). The Exodus is the root of the mitzvot, and it mirrors an "upper Exodus": the unfolding of the hidden Thought.
6. **The close of the book** (2203-2224). Love of G-d and love of people, the dwelling below, and a closing prayer.

This last chapter is the book's own summary of its purpose. It says in plain words what the whole book has built toward, and it uses the exact phrase Chabad builds on: G-d desired a dwelling below.

## The unity of G-d

**He is everything, and nothing is outside.** On "you shall have no other gods before Me (al panai)": the powers of existence, eternity, providence and ability are "His face", that is:

> פנימיים בו ואינם חוץ ממנו (Avodat HaKodesh 4:34:7; line 2187)
> Inner to Him, and not outside Him.

So one must not seek them from any other, "for they have no existence or standing except from Him" (line 2187). And from the Zohar on the Exodus: "not another, but I am all of it" (line 2197).

**He fills the world, and He fits between the cherubs.** How can the One who fills heaven and earth speak from between the poles of the Ark? The Midrash: like a cave by the sea. The sea storms, the cave fills, and the sea lacks nothing (line 2155). Ibn Gabbai:

> כשהוא רוצה מלא כל הארץ כבודו וכשהוא רוצה שוכן בין שני הכרובים (Avodat HaKodesh 4:30:7; line 2156)
> When He wishes, the whole earth is full of His glory; and when He wishes, He dwells between the two cherubs.

And a second picture: a man sees his face whole in a large mirror and whole in a small one (line 2157). The Temple is a small mirror of all the worlds (line 2158). The reason is His will: though He is without limit, He does limited things, "and this is from the side of the Will" (line 2159). And:

> הוא מקומו של עולם ואין העולם מקומו (Avodat HaKodesh 4:30:9; line 2158)
> He is the place of the world, and the world is not His place.

*Ours.* This is very close to the Chabad teaching of tzimtzum (contraction) as an act of His will and ability, not a limit on Him. Chabad adds that the contraction is not literal (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7, as we recall it). Ibn Gabbai does not raise that question. But he agrees on the core: the Infinite can be wholly present in a small place because He wills it.

**Voice, breath and word are one.** Against the Rambam, who said the voice at Sinai was created, ibn Gabbai says:

> קול ורוח ודבור הם דבר אחד בעולם האחדות (Avodat HaKodesh 4:31:4; line 2163)
> Voice, breath and speech are one thing in the world of unity.

Below there can be a voice without words, like iron striking iron. Above there is no voice without speech, "for there is no striking of lips". It is "the voice that speaks by itself without motion" (line 2163). This is also what the holy spirit is: one who speaks by it "does not hear a voice, but that spirit comes within him and he speaks by himself" (line 2163).

**The goal of Sinai was unity.** The whole awe of that day was "to know that the Lord is G-d, there is none beside Him" (Deut. 4:35). Ibn Gabbai calls Sinai:

> יום קבלת התורה תכלית הבריאה ושרש האמונה ופרסום הייחוד והאלהות (Avodat HaKodesh 4:31:6; line 2165)
> The day of receiving the Torah: the purpose of creation, the root of faith, and the making known of the unity and the Godhead.

**The upper Exodus.** Before emanation, all things were hidden and mixed in the closed Thought: light and dark, good and bad, like silver mixed with dross (line 2201). When the will is sorted out, "it shows that the good is the root of the will, and the bad is only for the fullness of the good" (line 2201). Then:

> ולפי שאין היכר בכבוד בהיות הדברים על תכונה זו, בא הרצון הנעלם הפשוט לבנות היכל לכבודו (Avodat HaKodesh 4:35:6; line 2201)
> And since there was no recognition of the Glory while things were in that state, the hidden simple Will came to build a palace for His Glory.

This was the "upper Egypt" and the "upper Exodus": the Thought unfolded, each thing found its place, and "it became known in truth that the Lord is G-d, there is none beside Him" (line 2201). The Exodus below mirrors it (line 2202). *Ours.* This is one of the book's deepest images for the guide: my own going out from a narrow place mirrors the first going out, when G-d's hidden will unfolded into a world. The Chabad reading of Egypt (mitzrayim) as limits (meitzarim) is close to it (Tanya ch. 47 speaks of going out of Egypt every day).

## Tzorech gavoah: the book's own conclusion

The last chapter says the purpose of the whole book in one breath:

> הוא הדעת את יי' ולזה התכלית נברא האדם והושם בזה העולם להשלים סוף המחשבה במעשיו הטובים (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:1; line 2203)
> [Unity] is the knowing of G-d; for this purpose man was created and placed in this world: to complete the end of the Thought by his good deeds.

By serving this way, a person "ties and unites the end of the Thought with its beginning" (line 2203). The mitzvot are, in his own words, a need on high, and he names his other book:

> והוא צורך גבוה לא צורך הדיוט בלבד לתקון הכבוד ועל הדרך שבארתי בספר תולעת יעקב (Avodat HaKodesh 4:35:5; line 2200)
> And it is a need on high, not only a common need, to repair the Glory, as I explained in the book Tola'at Yaakov.

> אבל כוונתו בעבודתו תהיה לתכלית שיתיחד השם הגדול בכבודו והוא צורך גבוה לא זולת (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:7; line 2209)
> Rather his intent in his service should be for the goal that the great Name be united with His Glory, and this is a need on high, nothing else.

"This is the whole of man and his purpose in his creation" (line 2209). When a person does this, he brings the upper worlds down, "and he is the cause that the Presence rests in this world, and the intent in creation is complete, and there is peace in all, for the desire is fulfilled" (line 2211). Then, the words Chabad would build a whole system on:

> נתאוה הקב"ה שכשם שיש לו דירה למעלה שיהא כך דירה למטה (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:10; line 2212)
> The Holy One desired that just as He has a dwelling above, so He should have a dwelling below.

And a line that turns the usual order around:

> וכששוכנת למטה היא הראיה ששוכנת תחלה למעלה (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:10; line 2212)
> And when she dwells below, that is the proof that she dwells first above.

*Ours.* This is the meeting point of ibn Gabbai and Chabad. Tanya ch. 36 opens from the same midrash (Tanchuma, Naso 16): "He desired to have a dwelling in the lower worlds". The difference is in the wording. Ibn Gabbai's midrash says "as He has a dwelling above, so below": the lower dwelling matches the upper one. Chabad reads it as a dwelling *in the lowest world*, where the Essence is revealed more than above, and stresses that "there is no reason for a desire" (the Rebbe Rashab and Bati LeGani, as we recall it). Ibn Gabbai stops at the unity of the Name with its Glory. Chabad goes to the Essence itself. But the claim that the person's deed completes what G-d wanted in creating the world is the same in both.

**Torah is the Name, the mitzvah is the Presence.** From the Tikkunim: "the Holy One is the Torah, and the mitzvah is the Shechinah; happy is the one who unites them" (line 2215). The positive mitzvot are the wings of the dove, "which give her strength to fly" up to her beloved (lines 2215-2216). Both are needed:

> להגות בתורה כנגד השם הגדול, ולקיים המצות כנגד שכינת עוזו, וזהו הייחוד הגמור (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:14; line 2216)
> To study Torah facing the great Name, and to keep the mitzvot facing the Presence of His strength; this is the complete unity.

## Love

**The lover becomes one with the love.** On "a friend, beloved, who loves the Omnipresent" (Avot 6:1), ibn Gabbai says the word "beloved" shows that the love took hold of him "until he and that love are counted as one thing" (line 2203). And the true lover "multiplies lovers for his beloved" (line 2203): love of G-d spreads to love of people.

**The evil inclination turned into a lover.** "With all your heart" means with both inclinations. By full service one turns the evil inclination into a lover:

> והרי הוא אחד בכל חלקיו ואין בו צד חולק (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:2; line 2204)
> And so he is one in all his parts, and there is no side in him that disagrees.

*Ours.* This is close to the Tanya's goal of the tzaddik who has turned his evil to good (Tanya ch. 10), and to the teaching that "with all your heart" includes the evil inclination (Tanya ch. 9, citing Berakhot 54a). The guide can hold "one in all my parts" as the aim of practice.

**Remove the screen.** Rabbi Akiva gave his life "out of love", his soul leaving on "One". This is how far love reaches: "to remove the screen that divides the two, to be seen with Him face to face, to cleave to Him" (line 2205).

**Love your neighbor more than yourself.** Ben Azzai's rule, "man was made in the image of G-d", is greater than Rabbi Akiva's "love your neighbor as yourself". For one who sees his neighbor as G-d's image will seek his good even at a cost to himself, "for when he does so, he is dealing with G-d" (line 2208). Moses is the model: after the Golden Calf he said "erase me" (line 2208).

## Prophecy and Sinai

**The fountain never stops.** Baruch ben Neriah was ready for prophecy, and G-d's giving did not fail, "for the fountain comes always and does not stop" (line 2091). What was missing was outside: the Temple and the generation. *Ours.* A person who feels they have been refused should know: it may not be their lack.

**Moses never lost focus.** "Moses did not know when He would speak with him", because he was always ready: "he did not take his mind away even a moment" (line 2104). A friend of the King does not know what is in the King's heart until the King reveals it (line 2103).

**How a vision comes.** The prophet goes into seclusion and gazes at the upper palaces "and his thought roams among the awesome forms ... and by this he is stripped of the body's accidents" (line 2124). Afterward he returns to his former level, "and Abraham returned to his place" (line 2124). Dreams come from the same root: from strong hitbodedut (seclusion of thought) the soul rises "as if stripped from the body" (line 2119). *Ours.* We do not give this as a technique. It describes prophets. But its shape (going up, then returning to one's place) is the same as ratzo vashov, and the guide keeps the lesson of the return.

> כוונת מציאות הנבואה לגלות על הייחוד ולהודיע טבעו ולפרסמו בעולם (Avodat HaKodesh 4:27:16; line 2128)
> The purpose of prophecy's existence is to reveal the unity, make its nature known and spread it in the world.

**All souls were at Sinai.** Every soul, born and unborn, stood there and took on the covenant (lines 2143-2147). At "I am the Lord", Torah was set in their hearts, "and they learned and did not forget" (line 2190). The goal of the giving of the Torah was "to return the crown of wholeness to its former state", to serve like the upper servants, with no lapse of mind (line 2191). All Israel stood "with one heart and one will to their Father in heaven, like one man" (line 2175).

**Freedom.** The letters on the tablets were engraved through the stone. "Do not read *charut* (engraved) but *cherut* (freedom)" (line 2170). The tablets "give freedom to those who busy themselves with them", and there is no free person except one who learns Torah (line 2170).

## The book's last prayer

The book closes on "may the pleasantness of the Lord our G-d be upon us, and the work of our hands establish" (Psalm 90:17). Our deeds are meant to match their roots above. And if we erred and the deeds were not fully matched, ibn Gabbai prays:

> קח הטוב שבו והעלם עיניך מן השגגה והחסרון (Avodat HaKodesh 4:36:20; line 2222)
> Take the good in it, and turn Your eyes from the error and the lack.

He finished the book on the second of Tevet 5291 (late 1530), having begun it at forty-two and finished at fifty (line 2223).

*Ours.* This is the right last word for the guide, too. The aim is high: to be the one whose deed completes creation. And the plea is humble: take the good, and look away from the rest.

## The big human questions

**What am I for?** "For this purpose man was created and placed in this world: to complete the end of the Thought by his good deeds" (line 2203).

**Why did G-d make a world?** Because in the hidden Thought there was no recognition of the Glory, "so the hidden simple Will came to build a palace for His Glory" (line 2201). And He desired a dwelling below, as above (line 2212).

**Is G-d far away?** He fills heaven and earth, and when He wills He dwells in a small place (line 2156).

**Can the bad in me be turned?** Yes; the evil inclination can be made a lover, until "he is one in all his parts" (line 2204).

**How should I treat another person?** As G-d's own image; "for when he does so, he is dealing with G-d" (line 2208).

**What if I get it wrong?** "Take the good in it, and turn Your eyes from the error" (line 2222).
