These are Sabbath talks (derashot) from 5685-5698 (1925-1938), weekly Torah portions from Vayigash to Balak. They are homilies, not a manual, but almost every one ends in "our work" (עבודתנו), and several hold the most concrete instructions for inner practice in the book.

## 1. What the range contains

- **Vayigash 3 (5691), 337-349.** Before creation God's will was simple and "willed Himself"; the world is that will narrowed into particulars. A person who sees the world as God's will, not as a thing standing on its own, reveals the mitzvah by doing it. Sins come from "too little contemplation and lazy thinking" (347).
- **Vayechi 1 (5690), 350-363.** The *chassid* is someone many Jews "build" out of holiness gathered from all the worlds. He is not bound by this world, so he looks at the world without limits and "is always longing." The letters of Torah and prayer carry more holiness than their meaning: "a piece of fire." Moshe's pauses between sections of Torah, the blank parchment, stand for the contemplation that could not be written.
- **Vayechi 2 (5691), 364-376.** Everything rests on *avodah*, effort offered to God, not on vision. If a person is glad at an uplifted hour because now he can serve more, that is a good sign; if he is glad only at feeling great, it is Bil'am's "vision." Mitzvot done as the King's decree reach above the chain of worlds; Torah learned for its own sake reaches its essence.
- **Shemot (5689), 377-396.** You can find direction by looking into a holy book: a line "sparkles," pierces the heart and will not rest. Every author's book is his whole spiritual "stature," so read one book deeply, not scattered bits. Teshuvah should come before sin: fix in yourself a disgust for sin.
- **Vaera (5689), 397-418.** Prayer and Torah are one union of knower and known. In prayer the light comes before it splits: you burn and understand nothing. In Torah study the same light is understood. Prayer is the Garden of Eden of "no rest," Torah is the Garden of "sitting and delighting." Without study the light of prayer stays up in the prayer hour. Stray thoughts come from a light, unanchored mind. Effort against your own will is worth the most.
- **Beshalach (5690), 419-441.** *Sod*, the secret, means God revealing His own self to someone who draws near. The first sign of nearness is real pain at wrongdoing, your own before anyone else's. Fear of sin is built by picturing it over and over. The yirei'im feel "joy and light" and cannot say what they feel; the prophets bring it into speech. Any stirring must be spoken or it fades. Personal intentions in the words of prayer. Arousal that comes from the vessels can sour into "fallen" traits; arousal from the secret must come out as song.
- **Yitro (5690), 442-455.** The light of the tzaddikim belongs to everyone who clings to them, and the clinger becomes the "body" for that light. You can tell which tzaddik's way is stirring in you now and strengthen yourself in it.
- **Shekalim 1 (5688), 456-470.** The long passage on prayer. Hope all day for the hour of prayer, pour your heart out to your Father, feel healing in the prayer itself. The soul is dragged about like a man thrown into a tavern of drunks; in prayer it tears loose and says "I have crossed every partition." The end of the prayer is the high point, and the work is to hold on after it. Teshuvah is waking the soul "out of its sheath."
- **Shekalim 2 (5690), 471-481.** "If you have abandoned, give your *da'at*." Forsaking God begins as forgetting and scattering. Pure thought cannot move the emotions; imagination can, and *da'at* can because it is the soul's own self. Will must be roused before the act.
- **Parah 1 (5690), 482-497.** New holiness is born when two opposites meet (love and fear, building and demolishing thought, the good and evil inclinations of the righteous) and each cancels the other's self. Bring your stirrings into speech, because in speech "the whole soul comes out." Stray thoughts in prayer are this same struggle gone empty.
- **Parah 2 (5698, sent to the Land of Israel), 498-510.** Written amid "bitter sufferings." Israel is God's royal robe. Besides fighting evil, a Jew must lift himself to feel *tiferet*, the beauty and purity above. The contrast of the tavern and the beit midrash. How you see yourself decides what you can feel.
- **HaChodesh 1 (5685), 511-524.** Darkness in a Jew is not an absence but a darkening thing; not feeling light is itself a symptom. A person with inner rest "feels nothing." The pain of distance is the Shechinah's pain felt by the spark within. A daily practice: purify the will by joining thought to every desire.
- **HaChodesh 2 (5690), 525-548.** Real nearness is of the whole person, body included, not understanding alone (Bil'am knew and stayed fallen). Two kinds of will: a small one to improve a little (*vayikar*), and a great one that uproots the whole self (*vayikra*). Start with the small. Lowliness is only imagination, like a dream. Do not think of yourself as coarse.
- **Tzav / Shabbat HaGadol 1, 549-558.** Where a person's thought is, he is. Set times in prayer to go deep into the greatness of God; Noam Elimelech's star-gazing and picturing yourself in the Temple. Deep thought is a real ascent of the soul even when you do not know what it saw.
- **Tzav 2 (5698, sent to the Land of Israel), 559-571.** A Jew must become Torah himself, growing it like a sapling, not holding it "like books in a crate." Test: find the one holy book that changes you when you read it long.
- **Bamidbar, 572-587.** Israel's holiness comes before them and is God's; they are His ornament.
- **Nasso 1, 588-600.** All creation sings in the light above it. Man has free will, so his service is intermittent and has to be willed each time. Rote practice does not sanctify the self.
- **Nasso 2 (5690), 601-617.** Faith has to be of the self, not a thought. The core meditation on pushing the whole soul toward God with eyes closed.
- **Beha'alotcha 1, 618-630.** Hearing: the worlds cry "God is one," but only a soul close to holy words hears them. Seeing the world's splendor directly ("mirror") versus reasoning from it ("riddles"). Advice: feel life in the words of prayer.
- **Beha'alotcha 2 (5690), 631-662.** Ulterior motives are *boser*, unripe fruit of a sweetness not yet reached. Don't fight them, work with the good part. The company of greater people helps. It is hard to know what you really are. True rebuke is a soul-conversation with yourself. Disciples should become their master's way.
- **Sh'lach (5690), 663-678.** Prayer works because the one praying argues in Israel's defense, himself included.
- **Balak, 679-683.** Guard yourself like the eye: close before the harm comes. Keep looking inward.

## 2. The method: concrete instructions

1. **Set a daily time to review your desires, the "purification of the will"** (521). When a desire arises, stop at once and ask whether you should want it, whether it is a divine will: "צריך תיכף לחשוב אם צריך אני לרצות זה". Then keep a fixed daily session: "ויקבע לו זמן בכל יום שיעיין ברצונותיו שעלו על לבו בכל המעל"ע" (521), "וזהו ענין טהרת הרצון" (521). Beforehand, study some Torah: "טוב שילמד מקודם דברי תורה, כדי שלא דעת אנושי רק דעת אלקית יחבר אליו" (522).
2. **The eyes-closed pushing of the whole soul toward God** (608-610), the most meditative passage in the range. In order: - Rouse a worry about your own smallness: "צריך הוא לעורר בקרבו דאגה של שפלות עצמו" (608). - Picture yourself walled in by mounds of matter, and cry the verse from the heart's walls: "ומקירות לבו צועק אשא עיני אל ההרים מאין יבא עזרי" (608). - Push the soul, again and again, with your thought: "דחיפה אחר דחיפה אל ד'" (608). - Do not try to grasp God; what you feel is your lack: "את ד' אין הוא משיג רק את הרגשת החוסר שלו" (608). - Posture and state: "רק עיניו סגורות ומחשבתו רצונו וכל מדותיו נכללים ונעלמים בנפשו ואת כל עצם נפשו דוחף הוא ומקרב לד'" (608). - Do it periodically, and carry it through the day: "ובאמונה והתקרבות זו שעושה לעצמו לפרקים" (610). Afterwards it comes down into thought, speech and action: "כן יוכל אח"כ להתקרב יותר בפועל במחשבה דיבור ומעשה" (609).
3. **Contemplating the greatness of God in prayer** (551). Make set times: "יעשה לו זמנים שיחשוב ויתעמק ביראה ואהבת ד'". The sequence: the wisdom in this world, the higher worlds, angels, seraphim, lights and souls, all longing for Him while He is utterly apart; then turn to yourself: "ואני עומד עתה לפניו ית' לומד את תורתו ומתפלל לפניו". He adds that this is one form among many: "כל אחד לפי ערכו יכול להעלות עצמו ע"י המחשבה" (551).
4. **Picturing (Noam Elimelech, quoted with approval)** (552, and again 594, 626). Look at the sky: "בהביטו השמימה ורואה הכוכבים במסילותם". In prayer, imagine yourself in the Land of Israel and the Temple: "כך צריך לצייר במחשבתו בעת תפילתו כאילו הוא ממש בא"י ובבהמ"ק", "וכאילו רואה הכל בחוש הראות" (552). The stars practice works best "at times when he withdraws from the world": "זמנים יש לו כשמתרחק מן העולם ועשתונותיו שקועות בקדושה" (626).
5. **Deepening, not skimming** (550). "לכן ראשית העליה היא התעמקות המחשבה בתורה": not thinking "offhand" but until the whole mind is caught in it.
6. **Put every stirring into words: spontaneous prayer in your own words** (431). Even a faint stirring should be spoken. His model: "רבש"ע משתוקק אני אליך וממעמקי המצולות", continuing "…my heart and flesh long for You, that You raise me, purify me and bring me near." Without speech it "remains a secret and a slight feeling that soon vanishes" (431). In speech "the whole soul comes out": "כשמדברה כל נפשו יוצאת וגם ההיפך יוצא" (494).
7. **Personal intentions inside the fixed prayer** (432-433). Load each phrase with your own need. On *zokef kefufim*: "כפוף אני הן בעניני הגוף הן בעניני הנפש", so straighten me up (432). This works only if the longing was already there before prayer (433).
8. **Hope for prayer all day and pour it out** (459-461). "כשאגיע אל התפלה אספר את כל אלה לאבי ומלכי" (459). In prayer, the soul's words: "רבש"ע כבר העברתי כל המחיצות שביני ובינך … אנא הדביקני בך רוחא ברוחא" (460). Voice and guarding the mind: "בקול שמעורר כוונה ובשמירת המחשבה והתעוררות התשוקה" (461).
9. **Hold on at the end of prayer** (461-463). The peak is the Amidah at the end; "לכן עיקר היחוד הוא בסוף התפלה" (461). Afterwards you may rest, as a climber rests midway up a tree, but grip: "עכ"פ בסוף תפלתו צריך לאמץ את נפשו ולהדקה אל הקדושה כיתד שלא תמוט" (463).
10. **Follow prayer with study, so the light is "learned in"** (404, 406, 411-412). Without this, prayer is "light above you" that is lost the next day.
11. **Hear your own words** (622-623). "עכ"פ שמע את דברי הקדושה שאתה בעצמך מדבר" (622). Take the words in and yourself into them: "יכניס אותם אל קרבו ויכניס א"ע בהם" (623). The advice to everyone: "שישתדל בתפילה בתהלים ובשערי ציון שירגיש חיות מהדיבורים תיבות ואותיות" (629). The Besht's teaching to bind yourself to the letters, each "a piece of fire" (361).
12. **Song** (440). An arousal from above the vessels "has to be spoken in song": "והוא צריך לדברם בשירה".
13. **Reading one book long** (388, 564). Take one or two books, as time allows, and go into them deeply: "צריכים לקחת ספר אחד או שנים כפי הפנאי שיש לו לאיש ולעיין בו הרבה" (388). Find the one book that works on you, reading it "for a long time and at the times suited to him": "כאשר מסתכל בו זמן מרובה ובזמנים המוכשרים לו" (564).
14. **In free hours, sink into the goodness of holiness and the ugliness of evil** (424-425). "צריך בשעות הפנויות להשקיע במחשבות איך שכל דבר קדושה טוב האמיתי הוא" (424). Keep picturing the evil of sin until you fear sin itself, not only punishment: "במרה שיתמיד האיש לצייר בדעתו את רעת החטא" (425).
15. **Give your da'at, your whole self, not a passing thought** (474): "להשקיע את כל עצמותו בזה".
16. **Start with the small will** (532). Improve a little, and the great uprooting will may then come: "ואם מקדש א"ע עכ"פ מעט ברצון של ויקר ומטיב את מעשיו זוכה אח"כ להשראה של ויקרא".
17. **Rouse the will before acting** (479), and anew each time, not by habit (596).
18. **Recognize which tzaddik's way is stirring in you and strengthen yourself in it** (452).
19. **Inner self-watching, like the eye** (683): "תמיד מסתכל בקרבו שמא דרכו אשר הולך בה לא טובה".

## 3. The inner experience, in his images

- **A light you hold but cannot know.** In prayer "בא לו אור ואינו משיגו וממשמש בו ואינו מכירו" (409): a light comes and he cannot grasp it, he gropes at it and does not recognize it. This is the "Garden of Eden of no rest," enjoyment and restlessness together (409). In prayer "בתפלה מתלהב ומתפעל ואין לו דבר שיבין בשכלו" (404): the light is still before the split into knower and known.
- **Unnameable joy and light.** The one drawing near "feels something and does not know what it is": "שמרגיש איזה דבר ואינו יודע מהו" (426), "רק טעם שמחה ואורה נשאר בקרבו" (426).
- **The soul rises and sees, and you do not know it.** "נפשו עולה למרום ורואה מה שהיא רואה" (553), "ואף שהוא לא ידע מה ראתה נפשו אף לא ידע אם בכלל ראתה" (553). The sign is afterwards: "אבל יראה ואהבה נתוספות לאיש אחרי עליות נשמה כאלו" (553).
- **Absorption.** "שבמקום שמחשבתו של האיש נמצאת שם כל האיש נמצא" (550): the deeply absorbed person does not see who passes or hear who calls.
- **Thought that rises by itself.** The highest level (Atzilut) is like a person's thought: "שאין האיש צריך לחשוב אני רוצה לחשוב רק מעצמה עולה המחשבה" (569). After long reading of one's book, the thoughts "act differently of themselves, not by forcing": "לא כשמכריח אותן לחשוב רק אדרבה מעצמן פועלות אחרת" (564), "ויש שדומה לו שנפתחו עיניו ומביט אחרת על כל העולם" (564).
- **The sudden flash from a book.** "דבר זה וזה נוגע עד לבבו וכמו שהבריק בו הדבר פתאום והאירו עיניו" (381). He is startled and recoils: "פתאום מתפעל מאיזה דבר ונרתע לאחוריו", "והדבר הזה נוקב והולך בלבו לא ינוח ולא ישקוט כמה שנים" (390).
- **The soul in the tavern and its escape.** Ordinary life is "like one thrown into a tavern among drunkards, each dragging him to dance": "כמו מי שנשלך אל בית מרזח בין שכורים פוחזים וריקים" (460). Prayer is where it breaks free (460). Also Yom Kippur as "a day of free weeping": "והיו"כ נראה לו ליום של בכיה חפשית והתנצלות" (477).
- **Healing in prayer itself.** "צריך הוא להרגיש תיכף בעת התפלה רפואה וחיזוק הלב והנפש" (459).
- **The soul struggling out of its sheath.** "והיא תפרכס לצאת מנרתיקה ולחבק אותו ית' כביכול" (467).
- **Lifted into the lower Garden.** In the beit midrash of true chassidim "כאילו גם הוא נתעלה מן עוה"ז והגיע אל ג"ע התחתון" (504). He feels purity and beauty within: *tiferet*.
- **The world's splendor seen.** Looking at trees and stars "נפשו מזדעזעת ורואה מעט הארת ההדור נאה זיו העולם" (626). The chassid who sees truly: "כשאתה מביט בעולם אתה מביט בהקב"ה והוא מביט בך" (357), "ותמיד הוא בתשוקין וכסופין להקב"ה" (357).
- **Restlessness as the first real feeling.** "האיש שיש לו מנוחה פנימית זהו מפני שאינו מרגיש מאומה" (517); the one who feels a little "מרגיש תמיד כח דוחף בקרבו שדוחה אותו לעלות" (517).
- **The ache is God's own.** "לא האיש הוא המרגיש את הצער הזה, רק הניצוץ אלקי שבקרבו" (518), like a cut hand: its life feels the pain of being severed from its source.
- **Fire from a thorn-bush.** Even someone dry and low can burn from the sight of his own lowness: "גם מן סנה שהוא קוץ יכולים להוציא אש" (507).
- **Words as fire, prayer as dying.** Each letter of prayer is "חתיכת אש" (361). With the Besht, it is a miracle one survives prayer, so painful is the return to the world (458, 461).
- **The taste of a mitzvah** cannot be explained, only felt: "וטעם א"א לבאר לזולתו רק כ"א כפי שמרגיש" (556).
- **Longing beyond love and fear.** When you fear even to love God, the two opposites together give birth to "תשוקה שלמעלה מאהו"י" (491).

## 4. The self and God

The range speaks to the owner's thesis directly, and more sharply than expected.

- **The seeker is looking for himself.** The joy of understanding Torah is the joy of the one who has lost something: "בעל אבידה מחזר על אבדתו, אחר עצמו מחזר" (403). When he understands, "הוא עצמו נתיחד, עצם מעצמו ובשר מבשרו" (403). The knower in a person and the known in the Torah are one divine knowing split in two. Finding the truth is finding yourself, and both are God knowing Himself (402-403).
- **Faith must be of the self, not a thought.** "ראשית היא האמונה. לא במחשבה לבדה שחושב שמאמין רק שעצמו מאמין" (612). "Anochi Hashem *Elokecha*," singular, means joining "the particularity of oneself and one's root" to God: "להדביק בו את פרטיות עצמו ושורשו" (607). If you live in your intellect alone, "לא די שהאני של מעלה לא יתגלה בו רק גם עצמותו עצמות ישראל לא תתגלה" (605). The "I" above and one's own essence are revealed together or not at all.
- **Teshuvah is returning with yourself.** "קחו עמכם דברים, עם עצמכם" (467): not words only, but return to God *with yourselves*. That means waking the soul "out of its sheath" (467), not the mind agreeing that attachment is good.
- **Forsaking God is forsaking oneself.** Falling away starts as "an abandoned person" (*an opgelozener mentsh*), scattered: "רעיונותיו מתבלבלים ונפשו מתפזרת ללא דעת וללא מחשבה" (473). Gather yourself ("give your da'at") and "it is impossible" that the soul's love will not break through "all the partitions and garments that close it in" (473).
- **Da'at is the soul's own self.** Da'at can move the emotions "מפני שהדעת הוא התגלות של עצם נפש היודע" (475).
- **How you hold yourself decides what you can feel.** "מה הוא תופס א"ע בתור ישראל" (506). Do not think "that I am low, coarse, murky matter": "ולא יחשוב א"ע שהוא נמוך גס וגשם עכור" (542). Say rather "באמת ישראל אני איש עליון רק שנפגמתי עתה בזה וזה" (542). Lowliness is a dream you wake from (540).
- **The pain of distance is God's pain in you** (518). In the moment you feel yourself lacking, what feels is the divine spark.
- **Becoming Torah** (563-566): the goal is that "his own essence becomes Torah," not Torah "like books in a crate" (566).
- **But self-knowledge is hard.** "לכן בכלל קשה שאיש ישראל ידע את עצמו מהו באמת" (646). Someone may think himself great and be low, or think himself full of bad motives when these are lights of levels about to open in him.
- **The soul cannot be known directly either**: "כמו שא"א לדעת נשמתנו אשר בקרבנו אף שכ"כ גלויה היא לפנינו" (555).

A fair summary of this range: the true self is found *in* reaching toward God (faith of the self, the soul out of its sheath), not first in introspection and then God. And what does the seeking is the self's divine root.

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

- **Feeling without effect is imagination.** Deep thought is a real ascent, "not only an arousal of his feeling, which can be imagination without effect": "ולא התפעלות של הרגשתו לבד הוא מתפעל שיכול להיות דמיון בלא פעולה" (553). The test is added fear and love afterwards (553).
- **Enjoying the elevation instead of the service.** If he is glad "at the greatness alone and not at the service he will add," that is Bil'am's vision: "משא"כ אם שמח הוא על הגדלות לבד ולא מן העבודה" (368). The real sign is "אז אם שמח הוא בזה מפני שיוכל יותר לעבוד את ד'" (368). Feeling Shabbat without keeping it is "ראיה בלא עבודה" (369).
- **Seeing from afar without moving (Bil'am).** Knowing the truth with the intellect changes nothing (529-530). Nearness must be of "the whole person" (528, 534).
- **Arousal from the vessels.** Love and fear aroused through the body (fear of punishment, love for favours) can decay into "fallen" love and fear: "כל התעוררותו מן הכלים לא מן האור" (439).
- **The feeling that fades.** A stirring not spoken "remains a slight feeling that soon vanishes" (431). The Yom Tov high that is gone the next day, "ולמחרתו של היו"ט שוב נופל למצבו" (411), because it was not drawn into study and the body.
- **Empty prayer.** "משא"כ האיש הריק בלא לב ובלא מוח בלא דעת ולא הרגשה הקודמת גם תפלתו ריקנה" (433). Study without prayer's light is "a dry calculation": "עיונו בתורה רק חשבון יבש הוא" (406).
- **Inner calm as numbness.** "האיש שיש לו מנוחה פנימית זהו מפני שאינו מרגיש מאומה" (517). The absence of light is not neutral in a Jew; it is a darkening thing (514-516).
- **Faith that is only a thought:** "שיחשוב במחשבתו אני מאמין בד' שהוא למעלה מהשגתי" is not enough (607).
- **Stray thoughts come from a light mind, not always from evil.** "הרבה מחשבות ובלבולים באים לאדם באמצע עבודתו לא ע"י יצה"ר ותאוה ח"ו רק מפני קלות דעתו", like a child's mind, "אחת מושכת את חברתה ואחת נסבכת בחברתה" (400). The first thought is hard to stop, but keeping it is in one's hands (400). Another reading: stray thoughts are the struggle of opposites that turns empty when the words of prayer have no soul in them (495).
- **Ulterior motives: don't fight them head-on.** They are *boser*, unripe sweetness. Pleasure "not from the light of the mitzvah itself but from people's praise" (636). "אל יתרגז ואל יבטל א"ע מעבודה בשביל זה" (637). Fighting them wastes the hour: "והאיש הזה אם יעסוק בדחיית הפניות אז יעבור עליו זמן העבודה לריק" (642). Instead work with the good part "וכמעט שלא ישגיח על הפניה ועל מחשבת הבלבול שבו" (642). Company helps: "וההתחברות עם אנשים יותר גדולים ממנו מועילה נגד הפניות" (643).
- **Rote.** "ואם אינו מתעורר בכל פעם ברצון רק עושה משום ההרגל" (596): the self is not sanctified.
- **Crushing self-image.** Thinking yourself low demands nothing of you (542). On the other side, sighing is not enough: "לא יסתפק באנחה לבדה או בכאב לב לבד" (507). Turn it into fire.
- **Imagination is necessary, not only a danger.** "לכן המחשבה טהורה בעצמה בלא הדמיון אינה יכולה לעורר את המדות" (475), "ורק הדמיון שמצייר לעצמו דבר של יראה" (475). Imagination moves the heart because the emotion it pictures meets the emotion in you. The danger is the same imagination unhooked from action (553), or worldly desires, which are themselves only imagination (540).
- **Truth of nearness: pain at your own wrong.** "אם אין הדבר כואב ומיצר לו סימן הוא שלא נתיחד עם קדושתו ית'" (423). Anger only at other people's sins is self-love, not holiness (423).

## 6. Compared with Chabad hisbonenus

**Agreement.** - Contemplation of God's greatness, set times, going deep until it moves love and fear (551) is close to hisbonenus. So is the tree of worlds, angels and souls as the object. So is "deepening, not offhand" (550), and his insistence that the light must be drawn down into study, body and deed (406, 411-414). That matches Chabad's final stage, "drawing down." - Da'at as the faculty that joins thought to the emotions (474-476) is the Chabad scheme almost word for word. - The test of the real by its after-effect (553; 368) resembles the Rashab's test of whether the trait actually changed. - Arousal from the body that can fall (439) sits close to the Mitteler Rebbe's warning about self-referential arousal.

**Differences, kept as differences.** - **Imagination.** For him pure thought *cannot* move the heart without imagination (475). He endorses picturing yourself in the Temple "as if seeing with bodily sight" (552) and gazing at stars (552, 626). Chabad hisbonenus works through intellectual understanding (binah) and is wary of imagery. He makes imagination a named instrument. - **Not-knowing as normal.** The core of his inner experience is a light "you grope and do not recognize" (409), joy you cannot name (426), a soul that saw you know not what (553), and a meditation where "he does not know with what, or in which part of his soul, he draws near" (608). Chabad prizes understanding that gets into the heart. He prizes a nearness that goes past understanding and returns to it later. - **The eyes-closed pushing (608).** This is not reasoning one truth through. It is a pre-verbal gathering of thought, will and emotion into the soul, then pushing that whole soul toward God from a felt lack. The engine is want, not grasp. - **Speech and song as completion.** Feeling is "secret" until spoken (429-431), sung (440), and put into your own words (431-433). Hisbonenus stresses dwelling. He stresses bringing it out. - **Restlessness as the healthy state.** Where the Chabad map treats "unsettled" as a state to settle before contemplating, in this range "inner rest" is a symptom of numbness (517), and "no rest" is the Garden of Eden of prayer (409). (Elsewhere he teaches quieting, *hashkatah*; this range does not.) - **Stray and ulterior thoughts.** The advice is to ignore them almost entirely and work the good part (642), and to treat them as unripe sweetness (636). Chabad's "return without commentary" is similar, but his reading is warmer: they are signs of a coming level. - **The tzaddik and the group.** Light comes through clinging to tzaddikim (446-452), reading their books whole (387-388), and the company of greater people (643). The inner life is social in a way hisbonenus usually is not. - **Self-image.** His instruction to hold oneself as "an exalted person, only damaged now" (542) and to feel *tiferet* (505) has no clear counterpart in the Chabad map, whose weight falls on bittul.

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before you look for God, notice what you want today. Take a few minutes, go through the desires that rose in you, and ask of each: is this something I should want?" (521)
2. "Close your eyes. Let your thoughts, your wanting and your feelings gather inward. You don't have to understand God. Feel how far you are, and from that feeling push your whole self toward Him, again and again." (608)
3. "Picture it: the sky and the stars on their courses, or yourself standing in the Temple. Picturing isn't childish. Pure thought alone does not move the heart." (475, 552)
4. "Whatever stirs in you, even a little, say it out loud in your own words: 'Master of the world, I long for You from the depths where I am.'" (431)
5. "If you felt something and can't say what it was, only joy and light, that is the real thing. What counts is whether it leaves you a little more loving and a little more careful afterwards." (426, 553)
6. "Don't fight the thought that you're doing this to feel special. Keep working with the part of you that meant it. That other thought is unripe sweetness." (637, 642)
7. "Don't think of yourself as coarse. Say to yourself: I am really something higher, only damaged in this and that." (542)
8. "When it ends, don't just drop back. Hold on to something from it, the way a climber rests but keeps his grip." (463)
