## 1. The mechanics: how a known truth becomes part of the person

**1.1 The mind rules the heart by nature, and that is where every person's work starts.** A person cannot always choose what he feels, but he can always choose what he thinks about, says and does. That built-in power is the hinge of the whole program. · *"שכל אדם יכול ברצונו שבמוחו להתאפק ולמשול ברוח תאותו שבלבו"* (every person can, by the will in his mind, hold himself back and rule the drive of desire in his heart) · Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 12, line 209. Also ch. 17, line 265: *"כי מוחו ברשותו ויכול להתבונן בו ככל אשר יחפוץ"* (his mind is in his own hands, and he can contemplate with it whatever he wants).

**1.2 The route runs mind → heart → the three garments (thought, speech, deed).** Contemplation in the mind produces love and awe; these clothe themselves in the doing of Torah and mitzvos. When the mind has reached a conclusion, the mouth and hands must carry out exactly that conclusion. · *"ופיו ולבו שוין"* (his mouth and his heart are the same), i.e. he does with his mouth and limbs what his understanding concluded · Tanya ch. 16, line 257. The three garments: Tanya ch. 4, line 115 (the soul "clothed" in thought, speech and deed of the 613 mitzvos).

**1.3 Hidden love is enough to start. You do not wait for a feeling.** Even when contemplation produces no felt fire, the understanding that remains "in the hidden recesses of the heart" still drives the doing, and the doing counts as done with love and awe. · *"וזה כלל גדול בעבודת ה׳ לבינונים"* (this is a great rule in the service of G-d for the in-between people) · Tanya ch. 16, lines 252-257.

**1.4 Knowing is not the point. Binding the mind to what is known is (da'as).** Knowledge received from books or teachers is not yet da'as. Da'as is fixing the mind on the truth "with strength of heart and mind" until the thought is tied to it as firmly as to something seen with one's eyes. · *"אך עיקר הדעת, אינה הידיעה לבדה"* (the main thing in da'as is not knowing alone) · Tanya ch. 42, line 629. Ch. 3, line 112: *"ויתקע מחשבתו בחוזק בגדולת אין סוף ברוך הוא, ואינו מסיח דעתו"* (he fixes his thought firmly on the greatness of the Infinite and does not take his mind off it).

**1.5 Surrounding (makif) vs. inner (pnimi): a general idea hovers; a detailed one enters.** The Rashab: contemplating something only in general, even something one already understands well, "is no contemplation at all," because the light stays over the mind, not in it. The excitement it produces is real for the moment and gone the next. Only contemplation in detail, at length, "presses in" (נקלט) and moves the heart of itself. · *"אבל הוא דמיון כוזב בלבד ואין לו קיום כלל"* (it is only a false imagining and has no staying power at all) · HaTefillah §2, line 49 (OCR); the kitzur, line 51: study is the preparation, contemplation "at length and inwardly" is what reaches deed.

**1.6 Tefillah is where learning is "engraved"; without it learning evaporates.** Chassidus learned and never davened with is forgotten, "and all his toil in learning is in vain." Prayer is the spine that holds up Torah and mitzvos. · *"למה זה מחיר ביד כסיל לקנות חכמה ולב אין"* (why is there a price in the hand of a fool to buy wisdom, when he has no heart), applied to learning without davening · HaTefillah §8, line 75 (OCR). Also line 49: *"כי כל הלימוד והידיעת הקודמת היא כהכנה אל העיקר"* (all the learning beforehand is only preparation for the main thing).

**1.7 The purpose of the soul's descent is to refine the animal soul, and davening is where that happens.** Contemplation that even the natural intellect can follow teaches the animal soul itself to understand and feel; contemplation above it overpowers the animal soul's traits. Work on the specific trait: "as a person knows his bad traits in a particular trait more, he must arouse in prayer the holy trait opposite it." · *"ועיקר הזיכוך והבירור הוא ע"י העבודה דתפלה דוקא"* (the main refining and sifting comes through the service of prayer, specifically) · *"צריך לעורר בתפלה ביותר המדה דקדושה שלעומת המדה רעה"* (he must arouse in prayer especially the holy trait opposite the bad one) · HaTefillah §8, line 77 (OCR). He also names three grades of animal soul: goat (stubborn, does not move at all), ox (butts, but because it moves it can be moved), sheep (soft).

**1.8 Itkafia (subduing) comes first; ishapcha (transforming) later, and the second rests on the first.** The beinoni subdues; the tzaddik transforms. Both give G-d pleasure, "like two kinds of delicacies." The Rashab: the love above reason that changes the animal soul's very nature comes "only after the work first in reason and understanding," and "whoever toiled on Friday (itkafia) eats on Shabbos." · Tanya ch. 27, lines 387-388 (two kinds of nachas ruach) · *"ומי שמרח בע"ש בעבודה שע"פ טו"ד בבחי" אתכפי' יאכל בשבת"* (whoever toiled on the eve of Shabbos, in work by reason, in itkafia, will eat on Shabbos) · HaAvodah ch. 5, line 197 (OCR). R. Hillel: after teshuvah one must strengthen oneself "בבחי' אתכפיא ואתהפכא," and itkafia means *"לקדש א"ע במותר לו או ללמוד יותר מרגילותו"* (to hold oneself back in what is permitted, or to learn more than one's habit), Pelach HaRimon, Bechukosai, line 1175.

**1.9 The natural soul is worked on, not only fenced in.** The Rashab (Umaayan): this is why man was created rather than only angels, "to strengthen the powers of the Divine soul and humble the powers of the natural soul." A stronger temper is a sign of stronger spiritual powers given to match it. · *"וזה תכלית בריאתו בעולם להגביר הכחות דנפש האלקית ולהכניע הכחות דנפש הטבעית"* (this is the purpose of his creation: to strengthen the powers of the Divine soul and humble the powers of the natural soul) · *"ומאחר שניתן לו חום טבעי חזק מסתמא ניתנו לו כחות רוחנים גם כן חזקים"* (since he was given a strong natural heat, he was surely given strong spiritual powers too) · Umaayan, maamar 13 ch. 1, line 263.

**1.10 The Divine soul is younger in the body but older in its root, so it can win.** The animal soul arrives at birth and settles into habits; the Divine soul "arrives" at bar mitzvah. It looks weaker ("a poor child"), yet its root is higher (tikkun above tohu, Yaakov the true firstborn), so the power to overcome is always there. Whether it is used depends on the person: "if his will is truly not to turn from G-d's way, he arouses in himself the strong power of the Divine soul." · *"שאם רצונו באמת לבלי לסור מדרך ה' הרי הוא מעורר בעצמו את הכח החזק של הנפש האלקית"* · Umaayan, maamar 13 ch. 2, lines 268-273; maamar 14 ch. 1, line 286.

**1.11 The two-stage purification of feeling (R. Aharon of Strashelye).** Every first arousal of love is mixed: it is love for G-d and also "self-feeling," the sense of "I am the one who loves." You cannot skip this mixed stage: arousal starts "from the side of the self (yesh)," and the sifting comes after, through deeper contemplation of the unity, "until the love remains clean without mixture." · *"וגם יש באהבה זו הרגשת עצמו"* (and there is in this love self-feeling too) · *"לכן ראשית ההתפעלות הוא דייקא מצד היש והבירור הוא אחר זה"* (therefore the beginning of arousal is specifically from the side of the self, and the sifting is after it) · Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 7, line 7 (the silver-in-the-ore parable: you must dig up the earth with the silver, then smelt it, again and again).

**1.12 Kabbalas ol is the foundation under all of it, and it works "by itself."** The Rashab: the yoke of heaven is hidden fear; felt fear is the same thing revealed. A person who feels the yoke is "contracted" in all his senses like a servant who always feels his master, and so does not see, hear, desire or get angry against G-d's will almost automatically. Without the yoke comes "openness": saying, seeing and hearing whatever one likes, because one feels "very free with oneself." · *"וסבת הפתיחה הזאת הוא מפני פריקת עול מ"ש"* (the cause of this openness is throwing off the yoke of heaven) · HaAvodah ch. 2, lines 76-78 (OCR). The Alter Rebbe: without the yoke "holiness does not rest on him" (Tanya ch. 41, line 606, quoting the Zohar's ox that is first yoked).

**1.13 A second nature is built by habit, in both directions.** Habit rules everything and becomes second nature: accustom yourself to despise the bad and it becomes a little despised in truth. The Rashab shows the same law working against us: what a child grows used to in a bad environment "is stamped into him, as the saying goes, habit becomes nature or second nature, which is very hard to be rid of." · *"ועוד, שההרגל על כל דבר – שלטון, ונעשה טבע שני"* (habit rules over everything and becomes second nature), Tanya ch. 14, line 239 · *"וכשירגיל עצמו כן תמיד הרי ההרגל נעשה טבע"* (when he accustoms himself so always, the habit becomes nature), Tanya ch. 44, line 664 · *"כמאמר הרגל נעשה טבע או טבע שני, שקשה במאד להפטר מהם"*, Umaayan maamar 13 ch. 2, line 270 · *"אלא העיקר הוא ההרגל, להרגיל דעתו ומחשבתו תמיד"* (the main thing is habit: to accustom his mind and thought always), that everything seen is the King's outer garment; "emunah" means training, like a craftsman training his hands. Tanya ch. 42, line 644.

**1.14 What "a Chassid" is.** The Rashab founded Tomchei Temimim not to produce more Talmud scholars but so that its students "will be Jews (Yidden) who fear G-d and are whole with G-d and His Torah"; learning without inner feeling is "a trade." He also asks the hasidim of his day: without learning Chassidus, "in what are we Chassidim?" · *"יהיו יהודים (אידען) יראים ושלימים עם ה' ותורתו"*, Eitz HaChaim ch. 22, line 337 (OCR) · *"ובהעדר הלמוד החסידות במה אנחנו חסידיס"*, Igros vol. 2 scan p. 322, line 5722 (OCR) · The Alter Rebbe's portrait of the whole person: *"ופיו ולבו שוין"* (1.2 above), and in the Rashab's letter to a student the goal is *"שהמעשה בפועל בעניניס הנ"ל תתאיס [תתאים] עס [עם] הידיעה"* (that the deed actually done in these matters match the knowledge), with *"וזה העיקר ללמוד ע"מ לעשות"* ("this is the main thing, to learn in order to do"), Igros vol. 4 scan p. 139, line 12812 (OCR).

## The day

**2.1 On waking: hand the soul back.** The morning blessing "My G-d, the soul You gave me… You will take it from me" becomes a decision: since it is Yours, I give it back now, to unite it with You through the words of Torah and prayer. Begin the morning blessings with this preparation. (For anyone: a sentence on waking giving the day back.) · *"לכן, מעתה אני מוסרה ומחזירה לך לייחדה באחדותך"* (so from now I hand it over and return it to You, to unite it with Your oneness) · Tanya ch. 41, lines 621-622. Ch. 44, line 660: the love of "my soul, I yearned for You at night" is "to draw Him into me through Torah study when I wake from sleep."

**2.2 Before any Torah or mitzvah: stop and stand before the King.** Contemplate that G-d fills and surrounds all worlds, sets aside the upper and lower worlds to make Himself king over *you*, stands over you and sees whether you serve Him properly. "He should deepen in this thought and lengthen in it, as far as his mind can grasp and as far as his time allows," before learning, before tallis and tefillin. Fear comes first, then love. · *"צריך להיות לזכרון תמיד ראשית העבודה ועיקרה ושרשה"* (one must always remember the beginning of the service, its main thing and root) · *"ויעמיק במחשבה זו ויאריך בה כפי יכולת השגת מוחו ומחשבתו, וכפי הפנאי שלו"* · Tanya ch. 41, lines 598-601; ch. 43, line 656 (the fixed order that depends on a person's choice: first lower fear through Torah and mitzvos, then love).

**2.3 Learn Chassidus before davening.** The Rashab's rule for his yeshiva: the propitious time for Chassidus is morning before prayer, at least two hours (7:30 to 9:30), after tefillin, Shema and tea; those who rise early learn Chassidus the whole time until davening, "because before davening one needs no other learning but Chassidus." In the Tract on Prayer, for everyone: after putting on tallis and tefillin, think through a subject in Chassidus for half an hour or more, standing or sitting in one place. For busy people: at least learn a little before davening. · *"הנה הזמן המסוגל ללימוד דא"ח הוא בבקר קודם התפלה"* (the propitious time for learning Chassidus is the morning before prayer), Eitz HaChaim ch. 25, line 348 (OCR) · *"טוב לחשוב קודם התפלה איזה ענין בדא"ח כחצי שעה או יותר"* (it is good before prayer to think over a subject in Chassidus for half an hour or more), HaTefillah §11, line 88 (OCR) · *"ויתחזק ללמוד מעמ לכה"פ קודם התפלה"* (he should make himself learn a little at least before prayer), HaTefillah §16, line 115 (OCR)

**2.4 Davening at length (avodah shebalev), in the Alter Rebbe's measure.** Those who can (the unemployed, teachers, young men supported by their parents) should daven the morning prayer about an hour and a half at least every weekday; everyone, including businessmen, on Shabbos and Yom Tov. He threatens to send "secret spies" to the minyanim to find those who could lengthen and are lazy. The heart should "pour out like water" daily. The beinoni must "fight a great war" with his body before morning and evening prayer and "toil with toil of soul and toil of flesh" during it. · *"להאריך בתפלת השחר ערך שעה ומחצה לפחות כל ימות החול"* (to lengthen the morning prayer about an hour and a half at least every weekday), Iggeret HaKodesh 1, line 1027 · *"אך בשבתות וימים טובים, שגם כל בעלי עסקים יש להם פנאי"* (but on Shabbos and Yom Tov, when all businessmen have time too), line 1028 · *"ונפשם תשתפך כמים נוכח פני ה׳"*, line 1025 · *"ולבטשם ולכתתם כעפר קודם התפלה שחרית וערבית מדי יום ביום"* (to crush them to dust before prayer, morning and evening, every day), Tanya ch. 30, line 435

**2.5 The Rashab's measure for the contemplation itself.** Real avodah is "toil of the mind in great depth and great binding… which needs a length of time of an hour or more," widening the subject into its many details until it "settles" in the mind and the heart hears it. Five preparations: (1) clear your head of worries, don't start praying the moment you come in, stand in one place (walking around while davening is "only broadening of the outer spirit and self-feeling"); (2) think Chassidus half an hour after putting on tallis and tefillin; (3) lower yourself through an honest accounting, *at times only*, best at Tikkun Chatzos, and privately; (4) if still closed, arouse great compassion on your own soul, mainly in the blessing Ahavas Olam; (5) before the Amidah, think briefly but with binding that you stand before the Essence, who is present in every place. · *"שצריכים לזה אריכות זמן כשעה ויותר"* (this needs a length of time of an hour or more), HaTefillah §4, line 57 (OCR) · *"ובכלל זה ג"כ שיעמוד בתפלה על מקום אחד"* (and included in this, that he stand in one place for prayer), §11, line 88 · *"והנה עגין המרירות והשפלות הנ"ל צ"ל רק לעתים"* (the bitterness and lowliness should be only at times), §11, line 89 · *"וגם שיחי ביחידות דוקא שלא בפני אדם"* (and only in private, not in front of anyone), line 90 · *"ועיקר התעוררות ר"ר הוא בברכת אהבת עולם"* (the main arousal of great compassion is in the blessing Ahavas Olam), line 90 · The worry-remover: you manage to set worries aside to fall asleep; if prayer were dear to you, you'd manage it for prayer too: *"הלא בעניניו הגשמיים כמו בשינה עד"ם ה"ה מסיר טרדת לבבו וישן"*, line 88.

**2.6 How to make that length possible: practise concentration outside prayer.** "The advice so that it will come easily in prayer is that a person accustom himself to deep study of some subject during the rest of the day too," setting aside an hour or two to be alone and go deep into a subject in Chassidus. Mind-toil is easier than heart-toil, "for each person's mind is in his own hands." · *"שיעשה לו עת וזמן להתבודד ולהעטיק [להעמיק] דעתו היטב באיזה ענין בדא"ת [בדא"ח] בשעה או שתים"* · HaTefillah §6, line 67 (OCR).

**2.7 Beginners and the young: simple, steady, not ambitious.** Those who haven't learned much Chassidus should not attempt long contemplation; they will fool themselves with imagined feelings and train themselves in self-deception. Instead: learn with those who know more, be humble toward them, and daven in a fixed place, from the siddur, very slowly, with the meaning of the words, a little aloud, words pronounced clearly. Even advanced people should daven this way at times, but not use it to excuse themselves. · *"יתפללו בקביעות מקום מתוך הסידור במתינות גדולה ולכוין פי' המלות"* (let them pray in a fixed place, from the siddur, with great slowness, and intend the meaning of the words) · HaTefillah §14, line 108 (OCR). **For anyone:** this is the Rashab's floor for prayer.

**2.8 The businessman's way (quoting the Mitteler Rebbe).** For those who cannot lengthen on weekdays: each morning realise how the day's business will swallow all of mind and heart, and so don't chat about business before prayer; learn at least a little; pray slowly, contemplating the words of the prayer themselves. Every extra moment is "like buying life for the whole day": its imprint (reshimu) guards him in business and draws him back to G-d whenever he has a free moment. · *"וכל מה שיאריך בתפלה אפילו רגץ [רגע] יותר הרי זה כקונה חיים לנפשו על כל היום"* (every moment more he lengthens in prayer is like buying life for his soul for the whole day) · HaTefillah §16, line 115 (OCR). **For anyone.**

**2.9 After davening: a fixed shiur, re-intended every hour.** Start the fixed daily study session right after prayer with the same self-giving intention; a person who studies many hours straight should renew the intention at least once an hour. The unifying intention is true in every Jew "at all times," but one must also fix times to contemplate G-d's greatness. · *"וכן בהכנה זו יתחיל ללמוד שיעור קבוע מיד אחר התפלה"*, Tanya ch. 41, line 622 · *"בכל שעה ושעה על כל פנים"*, line 623

**2.10 Torah study as the dwelling-place, in the measure your time allows.** Since my mind cannot contain G-d's unity, "I will make Him a dwelling: study of Torah as my free time allows, in fixed times by day and by night," even "one chapter in the morning." And rejoice that you host the King twice a day. · *"העסק בתלמוד תורה כפי הפנאי שלי, בקביעות עתים ביום ובלילה"* · Tanya ch. 34, lines 487-488. **For anyone.**

**2.11 The ratio and the manner of learning Chassidus (for his students).** On weekdays a third of study time in Chassidus and two thirds in Talmud and law (heard from his father); no less than four hours of Chassidus a day in the yeshiva; morning and night sessions plus Tanya between Mincha and Maariv. Learn in groups of five or six on one subject, in pairs within the group; review each discourse once or twice, because only the second or third time does one reach its truth; learn aloud, "for the voice arouses the intention of the heart"; learn with desire, not watching the clock, because learning that is a burden "does nothing." · *"בחול צריכים ללטוד [ללמוד] שליש חפידות [חסידות] ושני שלישים נגלח [נגלה]"*, Eitz HaChaim ch. 22, line 337 (OCR) · *"וכל דרוש שלומדים יחזרו עליו פעם או שתים"*, ch. 25, line 350 · *"ויחזרו שנים ביחד לא פחות"* (they should review in twos, no fewer), line 350 · *"אבל מקצת דא"ח צריכים ללמוד בלילה"* (but some Chassidus must be learned at night), line 356 · *"וצריכים ללמוד בקול ודבור שהקול מעורר כוונת הלב"*, ch. 26, line 365 · *"כ"א צריך ללמוד ברצון וחשק ואז לא ידקדק בחזמן [בהזמן] כ"כ"* (he should learn with will and desire, and then he won't be so exact about the time), line 362

**2.12 Learning's aim is to know the thing, not to show off novelties.** The Rashab attacks "pilpul of vanity": the desired learning is to know what one is learning, well and in its place, to be the *receiver* of the halacha, not its "giver." Wanting only to innovate is a sign of self. · *"הלימוד הנרצה חוא [הוא] אשה [אשר] תכלית לימודו יחי' לידע את הדבר שלומד"* · Eitz HaChaim ch. 27, line 369 (OCR).

**2.13 Daily practical halacha and evening musar for everyone.** Everyone, including the fine Talmud scholar, must learn the halachos needed for practice: fix a daily time for the Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim; and a fixed hour daily for books of musar that stir love of G-d, best at night after Maariv. The Alter Rebbe: between Mincha and Maariv learn Ein Yaakov in a group of ten, and "a little Shulchan Aruch." · *"והזמן המפוגל [המסוגל] יותר חוא [הוא] בלילה אחר תפלת ערבית"*, HaTefillah §17, line 119 (OCR) · *"בין מנחה למעריב כל ימות החול, ללמוד בעשרה"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 23, line 1361; *"ללמוד מעט בשולחן ערוך"*, line 1362

**2.14 Work and business as service: the day is carried by tzedakah.** Whoever has more time and means: "the rest of the whole day, in which he is busy with business, will be a dwelling for Him, through giving the tzedakah he gives from his labour." Even giving a fifth raises the other four fifths with it. In a letter: all our worldly dealings should not be "for themselves," but to sustain "souls that are parts of G-d" with free kindness, like the One whose kindness is "all day." (Wife and children come first by Torah law.) · *"וגם שאר היום כולו, שעוסק במשא ומתן, יהיה מכון לשבתו יתברך, בנתינת הצדקה שיתן מיגיעו"*, Tanya ch. 34, line 489 · *"ואף שאינו נותן אלא חומש"*, line 490 · *"כל עסקינו במילי דעלמא לא לגרמייהו"* (all our business in worldly matters not for their own sake), Iggeret HaKodesh 9, line 1126 · The Rashab adds: the blessing, not the effort, makes rich: *"ברכת ה' היא תעשיר גם במעט עסק"* (G-d's blessing makes rich even with little business), Umaayan maamar 25 ch. 2, line 530.

**2.15 Tzedakah: why it integrates more than any other mitzvah.** In other mitzvos one power of the vital soul is used for the moment of the act; money earned is a person's whole labour, so giving it lifts "his whole vital soul" to G-d. Instructions from the letters: every coin adds up; give with joy; give before praying (R. Elazar gave a coin to a poor man, then prayed); give often (the Rambam: many acts refine the soul more than one large one); and as "avodah," give beyond your natural compassion. In hard times do not tighten a hand that was always open. · *"הרי כל נפשו החיונית עולה לה׳"*, Tanya ch. 37, line 535 · *"אף צדקה כל פרוטה ופרוטה מצטרפת לחשבון גדול"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 3, line 1035 · *"כשמפזר מיגיעו ונותן לה׳ בשמחה ובטוב לבב"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 4, line 1057 · *"רבי אלעזר יהיב פרוטה לעני והדר מצלי"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 12, line 1162 · *"ליתן הרבה יותר מטבע רחמנותו ורצונו"* (to give much more than his natural compassion and will), line 1166 · *"הכל לפי רוב המעשה"* (all according to the number of deeds), Iggeret HaKodesh 21, line 1328 · *"כל עיקר עבודת ה׳ בעתים הללו בעקבות משיחא היא עבודת הצדקה"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 9, line 1127. **For anyone.**

**2.16 Eating and the physical (birurim).** Permitted things done for the body's desire, "not for the sake of Heaven," stay on the animal soul's level; eating meat and drinking wine "to broaden his mind for G-d and His Torah," or for the joy of Shabbos, lifts them "like an offering." · *"שאינן לשם שמים – אלא רצון הגוף וחפצו ותאותו"*, Tanya ch. 7, line 154 · *"האוכל בשרא שמינא דתורא ושותה יין מבושם להרחיב דעתו לה׳ ולתורתו"*, line 157

**2.17 Small daily acts of itkafia.** Delay a meal an hour (or less) and learn in that time; hold back from a word you very much want to say; drop a thought. "Even a little" below brings great holiness from above. The Rashab turns this into a program: eat a bit less, skip an outing, "and even if he does not learn in that time," the forcing itself breaks the coarseness (as long as he doesn't fill the time with another indulgence). · *"כגון שחפץ לאכול, ומאחר סעודתו עד לאחר שעה או פחות, ועוסק בתורה באותה שעה"*, Tanya ch. 27, line 390 · *"וכן אם בולם פיו מלדבר דברים שלבו מתאוה מאד לדברם"*, line 391 · *"אדם מקדש עצמו מעט למטה – מקדשין אותו הרבה מלמעלה"*, line 392 · *"וזה ביכולת כאו"א לכוף א"ע בפועל הענינים שלא יאכל כ"כ"* (this is in everyone's power: to force himself in practice not to eat so much), Eitz HaChaim ch. 18, line 318 (OCR). **For anyone.**

**2.18 Guarding eyes, ears and mouth.** The Rashab counts this as the basic fear everyone must have: shut the eyes from seeing evil, the ears from hearing mockery and idle talk, and don't speak idle words, gossip, or "widen your mouth in anything." It is hard only at first; with time one gets used to it, "and finds rest for his soul from many troubles." The Alter Rebbe: stop in the middle of a pleasant conversation or a story that puts down another, even a small true one. · *"עוצם עיניו מלראות ברע ואוטם אזנו משמוע דבר רע"*, HaAvodah ch. 2, line 66 (OCR) · *"והכבדות בזה היא רק בעת הראשונה"* … *"ובמשך הזמן יתרגל כך"*, line 70 · *"ולא ירחיב פיו ולשונו בשום דבר"*, line 76 · *"להפסיק באמצע שיחה נאה, או סיפור בגנות חבירו"*, Tanya ch. 30, line 442 · No idle talk in shul from the start of the chazzan's repetition to the final Kaddish: *"שלא לשוח שיחה בטלה"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 24, line 1372.

**2.19 Thoughts that arrive uninvited.** When a bad or foreign thought rises, push it away "with both hands" at once; during prayer don't argue with it ("one who wrestles with a filthy man gets filthy"), act as if you didn't hear it and add strength to your concentration. If it won't leave, ask G-d for mercy as a father on his child. Each push is itself the service and a mitzvah (like one who "sat and did not sin"). Do not try the tzaddik's technique of "raising" foreign thoughts. · *"מיד בעלייתו לשם, דוחהו בשתי ידים"*, Tanya ch. 12, line 215 · *"ישב ולא עבר עבירה – נותנים לו שכר כאלו עשה מצוה"*, ch. 27, line 381 · *"רק יעשה עצמו כלא יודע ולא שומע ההרהורים שנפלו לו"*, ch. 28, line 401 · Same with resentments between people (2.21).

**2.20 The accounting (cheshbon hanefesh) and bedtime Shema.** Be one of the "masters of accounting": review all thoughts, words and deeds, whether for G-d or not. The Alter Rebbe fixes the time: an hour every day or night before Tikkun Chatzos (in Iggeret HaTeshuvah), and at least once a week, before Shabbos, for one who cannot every night. The bedtime Shema includes accepting the four deaths of the court. The Rashab: this kind of bitterness is for fixed times only, not daily for most people, done alone; and the natural moment is when you are sad anyway about worldly things: turn the sadness into accounting. · *"לעשות חשבון עם נפשו, מכל המחשבות והדיבורים והמעשים"*, Tanya ch. 29, line 417 · *"לקבוע עתים להשפיל עצמו"* (to fix times to humble himself), line 408 · *"שעה אחת בכל יום או לילה לפני תיקון חצות"*, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 7, line 966 · *"על כל פנים לא יפחות מפעם אחת בשבוע, לפני יום השבת"*, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 10, line 995 · *"היא בשעה שהוא עצב בלאו הכי ממילי דעלמא"* (the right time is when he is sad anyway about worldly things), Tanya ch. 31, line 454 · Bedtime Shema: Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 7, line 972 (context, not quoted).

**2.21 Relationships and ahavas Yisrael.** In the beinoni's program: when resentment, hatred, jealousy, anger or pettiness rises from heart to mind, don't accept it; the mind does "the very opposite": kindness, open affection, bearing with the other "to the utmost," repaying the guilty with good. The easy road to "love your fellow as yourself" is to value the soul over the body (those who make the body primary can only have love that depends on something). Love even those far from Torah and draw them "with thick cords of love"; even the sinner one must hate for his evil, one must love for the hidden good in him. Letter: implant love of your fellow in your heart, and if a bad thought about another rises, "push it away like smoke," like a thought of idolatry. Every person is "more perfect than his friend" in something. The Rashab calls love of friends "the foundation of our Torah." · *"להתנהג עם חבירו במדת חסד, וחיבה יתרה מודעת לו"*, Tanya ch. 12, line 217 · *"העושים גופם עיקר ונפשם טפלה – אי אפשר להיות אהבה ואחוה אמיתית ביניהם"*, ch. 32, line 466 · *"צריך למשכן בחבלי עבותות אהבה"*, line 469 · *"יהדפנה מלבו כהנדוף עשן"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 22b, line 1349; *"שכל אחד מתוקן מחבירו"*, line 1347 · *"והארכתי בענין אהבת רעים אשר הוא יסוד תורתינו"*, Igros vol. 1 scan p. 186, line 1154 (OCR). **For anyone.**

**2.22 The friend and the mashpia.** The Rashab: a good friend sees what self-love hides from you, rebukes you and speaks to your heart, "and this is the whole of man"; a bad friend makes you do what you would never do alone, and blaming him is itself self-deception (you chose him). Young people should learn Chassidus with those who know more and be humble toward them (2.7). R. Hillel on the manner of a mashpia: the teacher who speaks with fire and loud voice is not received; teaching enters "specifically when he speaks calmly." The Alter Rebbe: if you can't understand, bring it to the wise men of your town (Compiler's Foreword, line 61, context); but don't travel from town to town to ask advice on material matters; he says only Torah and fear of Heaven are the domain of advice (Iggeret HaKodesh 22a). · *"כי אין אדם רואה נגעי עצמו מצד אהבת עצמו"*, Umaayan maamar 14 ch. 2, line 289 · *"וחברו רואה ויודע יותר ממנו ומוכיחו על זה"*, line 289 · *"כשמדבר בנחת דווקא"* … *"דברי חכמים בנחת נשמעים"*, Pelach HaRimon, Pesach, line 82 · *"לכתת רגליהם מעיר לעיר, לשאול עצות מרחוק"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 22a, line 1334 (the practice he rebukes) · The Rashab asks a student for a report: *"בקשתי להודיעני מקביעות עתו וזמנו בלימוד דא"ח וכמה הוא עוסק"*, Igros vol. 1 scan p. 201, line 1221 (OCR): the mashpia checks the fixed times, not the feelings.

**2.23 Niggun and joy in prayer.** The Levites' service was "song and music, melody and sweetness," in running and returning; the Rashab advises davening "in a joyous melody that arouses the intention." · *"בשירה וזמרה, בניגון ונעימה"*, Tanya ch. 50, line 728 · *"בקול ניגון של שמחה המעורר את הכוונה"*, Igros vol. 1 scan p. 77, line 636 (OCR)

## The week

**2.24 Shabbos.** Shabbos is "higher return" (teshuvah ila'ah), so the weekly accounting belongs on Thursday night / before Shabbos (2.20). All businessmen must lengthen prayer on Shabbos (2.4). The Rashab: Shabbos "should be wholly devoted to Chassidus"; no wasting it in idle talk or strolling ("strolling on Shabbos is not desired at all"); only sleeping a little more and eating well are allowed, and those for the honour of Shabbos, feeling "the awe of Shabbos"; Shabbos prayers and meals belong together. Between Mincha and the end of Shabbos, study the laws of Shabbos (Iggeret HaKodesh 23, line 1363, context). · *"ויום הש"ק קדוש יהי' כולו על עסק דא"ת [דא"ח]"*, Eitz HaChaim ch. 25, line 357 (OCR) · *"והטיול ביום הש"ק הוא בלתי מרוצה כלל וכלל"*, line 358 · *"על כל פנים לא יפחות מפעם אחת בשבוע, לפני יום השבת"*, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 10, line 995

## The year

**2.25 Fasting is not teshuvah; replace it with tzedakah.** Teshuvah by Torah law is leaving the sin in the heart. "Not like the view of the masses, that teshuvah is fasting." Whoever is harmed by fasting is forbidden to fast much; redeem each fast with money ("redeem your sin with tzedakah"). One who wants the highest teshuvah may complete the fasts once in a lifetime, spread over short winter days, as his strength allows. · *"ולא כדעת ההמון, שהתשובה היא התענית"*, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 1, line 895 · *"אסור לו להרבות בתעניות"*, ch. 3, line 909; *"וחטאך בצדקה פרוק"*, line 912

**2.26 Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur change nothing without the year's work.** See 4.8. The Rashab: the new light of each year and each morning's prayer is higher than before (Iggeret HaKodesh 14, line 1190, context), but it lands only in what was prepared.

## 3. Growth over time

**3.1 The beinoni is everyone's measure, available "at every moment."** Loathing evil belongs to the heart and cannot be commanded; not doing it, in thought, speech and deed, is in anyone's hands at any moment, by saying to one's heart: "I do not want to be separated from G-d." The oath is double ("be a tzaddik… do not be a rasha") because only the second is fully in our control. · *"שכל אדם יכול להיות בינוני בכל עת ובכל שעה"*, Tanya ch. 14, line 232 · *"אינני רוצה להיות מובדל ונפרד חס ושלום מה׳ אחד בשום אופן"*, line 232

**3.2 What changes first: the garments, not the essence.** In the beinoni the animal soul's essence stays "at full strength as at birth" and can grow stronger with use; what changes is that its garments no longer act. Even day-and-night Torah study is no proof the evil has moved. So do not believe praise that says you're a tzaddik. · *"ואדרבה – נתחזק יותר בהמשך הזמן"*, Tanya ch. 13, line 222

**3.3 Daily return: it wakes again after prayer, and you pray again tomorrow.** During Shema and prayer the evil "sleeps"; after prayer "it wakes again." Yet the beinoni's love in prayer is called "true" for his level, because he can re-arouse it every day "through the preparation fitting each soul." · *"אבל אחר התפלה… הרי הרע חוזר וניעור בחלל השמאלי"*, Tanya ch. 12, line 208 · *"הואיל ובכח נפשם האלהית לחזור ולעורר בחינת אהבה זו לעולם"*, ch. 13, line 229

**3.4 The measure of real work: the 101st time.** "One who serves G-d" (oved) is one who reviews his chapter 101 times, when 100 was the norm. What counts is going past your habit, because changing a habit needs arousing love by contemplation. If there is no struggle, inherited love alone is not called your service. · *"הפעם המאה ואחת, היתרה על הרגילות"*, Tanya ch. 15, line 249 · *"שהוא באמת עבודה ועמל גדול להלחם בו תמיד"*, line 241 · *"ללמוד הרבה יותר מחפצו ורצונו לפי טבעו ורגילותו"* (to learn much more than his natural desire and habit), ch. 30, line 438

**3.5 How long: perhaps all your life, and that may be why you were made.** Don't be downhearted if the war lasts all your days. Each push-away is a victory above. · *"גם אם יהיה כן כל ימיו במלחמה זו, כי אולי לכך נברא"*, Tanya ch. 27, line 385 · R. Aharon: in war you sometimes win and sometimes don't; is that a reason not to fight? *"ובמלחמה פעמים יכול לנצח ופעמים אינו יכול לנצח וכי בשביל זה לא יעשה מלחמה כלל"*, Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 9, line 9

**3.6 Do your part; the rest is given.** Contemplate G-d's greatness even knowing you'll only reach it "in imagination": "he will do his, and G-d will do what is good in His eyes." Habit makes evil a little despised in truth, and "perhaps a spirit from on high will be poured on him." Sanctify yourself a little and "they help him drive it out of his heart little by little." After tzedakah as real avodah, evil can no longer wake up so easily by itself. · *"הוא יעשה את שלו"*, Tanya ch. 14, line 238 · *"ומסייעים אותו לגרשה מלבו מעט מעט"*, ch. 27, line 393 · *"אין הרע יכול להיות עוד חוזר וניעור בקלות כל כך מאליו"*, Iggeret HaKodesh 12, line 1169

**3.7 Start small; it grows.** The Rashab: love and fear are within everyone's reach "each according to his measure, this one a little and that one much, and at first it is a little and then he adds and goes on." Contemplation that is hard at first comes easily "with much habit over time." · *"ובתחלה הוא במעט ואח"כ מופיף [מוסיף] והולך"*, Eitz HaChaim ch. 18, line 323 (OCR) · *"אבל ע"י יגיעה יכול להגיע לזה"*, ch. 17, line 315 · How long the contemplation takes differs by soul: *"כי שיעור שעה זו – אינו שוה בכל נפש"*, Tanya ch. 42, line 631; but "I toiled and found, believe it": *"יגעתי ומצאתי – תאמין"*, line 633

**3.8 One trait at a time, one prayer after another.** The Rashab's rule for measurable change: work on a single specific trait, "little by little I will drive it out… each detail separately, and when he fixes one he fixes the next." Be bitter about that detail only, never conclude that all your good is cancelled. It takes "many times in many prayers on one particular trait" before it yields. · *"ומעט מעט דוקא אגרשנו"* … *"וכשיתקן את האחד אז יתקן את הבי [השני]"*, Igros vol. 1 scan p. 205, line 1240 (OCR) · *"ולא שעל הכלל כולו יוצא לחשוב שכל הטוב בטל כנגד הרע"*, same line · *"צריכים לייגע כמח [כמה] פעמים בכמה תפלות על מדה א' פרטית"*, HaTefillah §13, line 104 (OCR)

**3.9 How to measure real change: the specific trait in the moment of test.** The Rashab's test: after davening, does the anger or the craving still win when the actual situation comes? A general resolution ("I don't want to be bad") "does not work on the detail, and does not even last itself." A resolution made in prayer about *this* trait holds when the moment comes, and the desire itself weakens. And the directive to a student: "not to be at times strong and at times weak," but steady, "that the deed actually done match the knowledge." · *"והסכם הכללי אינו מועיל על הפרט"*, HaTefillah §13, line 104 (OCR) · *"שלא להיות עתים חלים ועתים כוי"*, Igros vol. 4 scan p. 139, line 12812 (OCR)

**3.10 Relapse and return: forgiveness is immediate and unlimited.** For G-d "there is no difference between once and a thousand times." Every day we ask "forgive us" and bless "who forgives abundantly" without doubt. Hold the sin "before me" at a distance, only to keep from arrogance, not as constant sadness; and remembering it actually helps accept insults and troubles with joy, "a good remedy against anger." · *"אין הפרש בין פעם אחת לאלף פעמים"*, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 11, line 1003 · *"אין המכוון להיות תמיד עצב נבזה"*, line 1008 · *"וזו עצה טובה להנצל מכעס וכל מיני קפידא"*, line 1009 · Real teshuvah changes the person's essence, *"שעל ידי התשובה אמיתית מתהפך מהותו"*, Umaayan maamar 14 ch. 2, line 292

**3.11 When the mind can't lead: stubbornness of holiness.** For one already caught in a habit, where reasoning no longer works, the Rashab's advice is not more thinking: "not to come at all into detailed calculations," but to do the opposite of the urge "like a lion, without argument or answer," from the essence of the soul that is above reason ("stiff-necked" for the good). "When he behaves this way in practice for a period of time," the natural feeling weakens, the Divine soul's powers appear, and then he can live by holy understanding. · *"שלא לבא כלל לידי חשבונות פרטים"*, Umaayan maamar 27 ch. 1, line 564 · *"ואינו מביט כלל אם יפסיד או לא יפסיד"* (he does not look at all whether he will lose or not), about fixed prayer and Torah times, line 568 · *"שיתגבר כארי לעשות רק ההיפך מיצרו הרע בלי שום טענה ומענה"*, maamar 28 ch. 1, line 585 · *"וכאשר ככה יתנהג בפועל משך זמן"*, line 586 · The Rashab's story of a great hasid who, at his first entry to the Rebbe, resolved: whatever my nature wants, I will not do: *"שכל מה שרוצה מצד חטבעי [הטבעי] לא יעשה"*, HaAvodah ch. 2, line 94 (OCR)

**3.12 The workers climb from rung to rung.** Those whose Divine soul shines "are refined from time to time, ascent after ascent." · *"ומזדככים מזמן לזמן בעילוי אחר עילוי"*, Umaayan maamar 27 ch. 1, line 563

## 4. Counterfeits of integration

**4.1 Knowledge without prayer.** Learned Chassidus that is never davened with is forgotten and its toil wasted (1.6). Many who learn much Chassidus "pray by habit like everyone," and when stirred, only briefly and in general, "which is not service at all." · *"ועפ"י הרוכב [הרוב] הם מתפללים כפי הרגילות ככל אדם"*, HaTefillah §8, line 75 (OCR)

**4.2 Feeling without change: the davener whose traits stayed the same.** "A bad disease" among hasidim, even those who serve with mind and heart in prayer: "they did not bring salvation to their souls, to refine and correct their natural traits, and have some very ugly traits." The cause: only a general resolution against evil, no work on the particular trait (3.9). · *"שמ"מ לא פעלו ישועות בנפשם בעבודתם לוכך [לזכך] ולתקן מדותיהם הטבעים"*, HaTefillah §13, line 103 (OCR)

**4.3 Imagined arousal.** General contemplation gives a flash of feeling that "is only a false imagining"; forcing the heart to feel is "an empty imagining." Beginners who try long contemplation train themselves to deceive themselves. · *"אבל הוא דמיון כוזב בלבד ואין לו קיום כלל"*, HaTefillah §2, line 49 (OCR)

**4.4 Love that is secretly self-love (R. Aharon).** Arousal mixed with "I am the one who loves" is real but unrefined; the work is to remove the self-feeling, and a person should suspect his arousal of containing it. But the Alter Rebbe's way, he writes, was always to *affirm* each person's arousal at his level: "his dove, his perfect one," even if "deceived" by some mixture. · *"בכדי שלא יהיה התערבות טוב ורע"*, Sha'arei Avodah, Introduction 9, line 9 · *"ולאמת כל ההתפעלות של כל אחד ואחד לפום שיעורא דיליה"* (to confirm the arousal of each and every one according to his measure), same line · *"בל יחוש לעבודתו שהיא מאונה באיזה תערובת"* (let him not worry that his service is "deceived" by some mixture), same line

**4.5 Avodah for self: pride in one's davening.** The Rashab: the Torah scholar who davens at length with deep contemplation and "takes credit for himself" has fallen lower than the simple man who says Tehillim without understanding in pure acceptance of the yoke; "all his service is serving himself," like any bodily pleasure. The simple man's service, without enjoyment, only kabbalas ol, is in that respect higher. · *"הרי הוא כורה שוחה לעצמו ונופל בה"* (he digs a pit for himself and falls into it), Umaayan maamar 15 ch. 5, line 328 · *"מראה שכל עבודתו הוא שעובד את עצמו"* (shows that all his service is serving himself), line 331 · *"כי אם כל זה אצלו בדרך קבלת עול לבד"* (the simple man: all of it only by way of accepting the yoke), ch. 4, line 320 · Igros: *"אך אל יהיי שוטה להטעות א"ע בגדולות ונפלאות"* (let him not be a fool, deceiving himself with great and wondrous things), vol. 1 scan p. 78, line 639 (OCR)

**4.6 Torah learning that leaves the person unchanged.** The Rashab on yeshiva students "who learned much Torah, and in their shape, look and feeling they are like simple householders; the Torah did not rest its spirit on them at all," because learning was "like a physical trade." Learning must itself produce bittul. · *"והתורה לא האצילה מרוחה עליהם כלל וכלל"*, Eitz HaChaim ch. 12, line 277 (OCR) · *"דעצם לימוד התורה צריך לפעול בו הביטול"*, line 274 · R. Aharon: study and practice done only "from habit" reveal no Divine light: *"ואינו לומד ועושה רק מצד ההרגל"*, Sha'arei Avodah, Fourth Gate 34, line 699

**4.7 Keeping mitzvos while staying coarse.** Without real love, a person can keep all of Torah "and he is coarse and gross and soiled in disgusting things, in desires of the permitted"; his Torah has no life. · *"ועם היותו מקיים תורה ומצות ה"ה חומרי"*, HaAvodah ch. 2, line 91 (OCR)

**4.8 The High Holiday cry that changes nothing.** "We see it plainly in many people on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, who are very stirred… and it does not truly change their way all year." Even G-d-fearing people who truly weep and accept the yoke "return to their coarseness and nature" afterwards. "It is truly a wonder how the nature does not change at all." The reason: no work all year, and remorse and resolution only "in general," not about the particular things where one falls short. · *"ונפלא הדבר ממש איך שאינו משתנה הטבע כלל"*, HaAvodah ch. 5, line 187 (OCR) · *"אך סיבת הדבר הוא העדר העבודה כל השנה"*, same line · *"ומכ"ש שאינו בא בענונים [בענינים] פרטיים"*, line 191

**4.9 Sadness passing as piety.** Sadness about spiritual matters that falls during business is "certainly a trick of the yetzer," to trap him later in desires; worry over sins belongs only at fixed times; sadness about bad thoughts "comes from arrogance: he does not know his place." Bitterness (a live, broken heart) is not sadness (a heart dull as stone). Rashab: sadness with fallen spirit "is refuse, it will not be accepted… a threshold trodden by every kind of evil"; "keep far from sadness to the very end; prayer and Torah study must be in joy, specifically." · *"בידוע שהוא תחבולת היצר"*, Tanya ch. 26, line 377 · *"העצבות היא מגסות הרוח, שאינו מכיר מקומו"*, ch. 27, line 382 · *"כי עצבות – היא שלבו מטומטם כאבן ואין חיות בלבו"*, ch. 31, line 452 · *"אבל מ"ש ועצבות בנפילת רוח כוי פיגול הוא לא ירצה"* (מ"ש = melancholy), Igros vol. 1 scan p. 237, line 1445 (OCR) · *"ירחק א"ע מן מ"ש ועצבות עד קצה האחרון"*, Igros vol. 1 scan p. 86, line 679 (OCR)

**4.10 False humility: "we are too low for love and fear."** The Rashab calls it a mistake that "causes harm": love and fear are commandments, so everyone can reach them at his level, and "the cause of their lowliness is the lack of love and fear." When learning about love and fear, "God forbid he think this has nothing to do with him and his service." In a letter: just as it is forbidden to think oneself great, "it is forbidden to lower oneself too much and say one's service is nothing": that is "lowliness of falsehood and a trick of the yetzer" so that he won't contemplate. · *"באמרם מח [מה] אנו ומה עבודתנו"*, Eitz HaChaim ch. 16, line 305 (OCR) · *"חלילה לו לחשוב שאין זה שייך אליו ולעבודתו"*, ch. 20, line 331 · *"אדרבה סיבת פחיתותם היא העדר האהוי"ר"*, same line · *"כך אסור להשפיל א"ע ביותר ולומר שאין עבודתו כלוס"*, Igros vol. 1 scan p. 78, line 639 (OCR)

**4.11 Concluding from foreign thoughts that one's prayer is worthless.** "Not like the world's mistake": the foreign thoughts come because two souls are at war, and they grow stronger precisely when the Divine soul strengthens. · *"ולא כטעות העולם, שטועים להוכיח מנפילת המחשבה זרה – מכלל שאין תפלתם כלום"*, Tanya ch. 28, line 398

**4.12 Self-justification.** "He was hot-blooded," "someone else talked me into it": the Rashab calls this worse than the folly that caused the sin, because it keeps teshuvah shallow and the person unchanged. Love of self is "the source of the blemishes." · Umaayan maamar 13 ch. 1, lines 262-263; maamar 14 ch. 2, lines 291-293 (context; key line verified at 292, 3.10)

**4.13 Harshness: fasting and self-affliction as the measure of piety.** See 2.25: fasting that harms is forbidden, especially for one who learns, since it weakens his study. The accounting is at fixed times, and afterward "remove sadness entirely." The Alter Rebbe's letter also condemns the harshness of seeking material advice instead of accepting G-d's rebuke with love (Iggeret HaKodesh 22a, line 1334). · *"אזי יסיר העצב מלבו לגמרי"*, Tanya ch. 26, line 379

**4.14 Form without inner content (the Rashab's founding letter).** His yeshiva exists because "the Torah is girded in thick sackcloth… and many young men who learn Torah have no fear of Heaven at all" and go on to rule for others; hasidim who learn Chassidus "only to be yotzei" are not what he intended. · Eitz HaChaim ch. 22-23, lines 337-340 (context; the positive aim verified at line 337, 1.14)

## 5. For anyone

Items the sources give that any person can do, as the sources give them (the Jewish practices named as what they are):

- **Rule by the mind.** In any moment you can refuse to act on, speak, or keep thinking a desire; say to yourself "I do not want to be separated from G-d" (1.1, 3.1).
- **Before starting a task that serves G-d, stop for a moment** and remember before Whom you stand (2.2).
- **On waking, give the day back** (2.1; the Jewish form is Elokai Neshama and the morning blessings).
- **Learn something that speaks of G-d before praying,** even a little; pray slowly, in one place, from the text, with the meaning (2.3, 2.7, 2.8).
- **One small daily itkafia:** delay a meal, hold back a word, eat a bit less (2.17).
- **Guard the eyes, ears and mouth;** it is hard only at first (2.18).
- **Push away a resentment the moment it rises,** and do the opposite: act with kindness (2.21).
- **Give from what you earn,** often, with joy, before praying (2.15).
- **Accounting at a fixed time,** at least weekly, and when sad anyway, turn the sadness into accounting, then let it go (2.20).
- **Work on one trait at a time,** and test yourself in the actual moment (3.8, 3.9).
- **Keep company with a friend who will tell you the truth** (2.22).
- **Joy as the ground of the work,** and refuse sadness as a strategy of the yetzer (4.9).

The Alter Rebbe's own framing: the beinoni's measure is "every person's measure," at every moment (Tanya ch. 14, line 232). The Rashab on kabbalas ol: *"קבלת עול מלכות שמים הוא יסוד לכל העבודה"* (Umaayan maamar 15 ch. 5, line 330), and in HaTefillah §15 (line 111) this is "equal for every soul": *"ולא יסיח דעתו אף רגע אחת לעשות דבר בלי דעת"* (let him not take his mind off for one moment to do something without awareness).

## What this means for integration *(ours: a synthesis of the sources above, not a quotation)*

1. **Integration is a loop, not a line, and prayer is its hinge.** Learn (before davening) → contemplate at length and in detail (in davening) → resolve about a specific trait (in davening) → carry the imprint through the day (tzedakah, guarded speech, pushing away thoughts) → fixed accounting (night / before Shabbos) → back to learning. The Alter Rebbe gives the frame (ch. 12-16, 41-42); the Rashab gives the missing gear: the resolution must be about a particular trait, or nothing changes (HaTefillah §13; HaAvodah ch. 5). The one-line version: *what is known in general stays around you; what is contemplated in detail and tied to a specific trait enters you.*

2. **The floor is action, not feeling.** The beinoni's program never waits for emotion: thought, speech and deed are always in one's hands, hidden love is enough to start, and the 101st review, the delayed meal and the swallowed word are the real units of work. Feeling is welcome but is tested by one question: did the trait change in the moment of test?

3. **Two speeds, both needed.** The long road (understanding, contemplation, feeling that refines the animal soul from inside) and the short road (kabbalas ol, "stubbornness of holiness," doing the opposite without argument) are not rivals. The Rashab is explicit that when reasoning has lost to habit, the short road comes first and the long road becomes possible afterward (Umaayan 27-28). Kabbalas ol is also the guard against the chief counterfeit, serving oneself.

4. **Joy is structural, sadness is diagnosed.** Every source separates bitterness at a fixed time (live, specific, private, short) from sadness (dull, general, constant). Joy is what makes the fight winnable ("a sluggish wrestler loses even if he is stronger," ch. 26). Most counterfeits named in the sources are forms of self-focus: pride in davening, false humility, self-justification, general remorse without particular change.

5. **Time scale: a lifetime, measured one trait at a time.** The essence of the animal soul may not change for the beinoni at all, and the war may last all one's life "perhaps for this you were made." What does change, and can be measured, is the garments: which thoughts are entertained, which words are said, which traits win in a real test. The High Holidays, a great farbrengen-like moment of arousal, bear fruit only on a year of daily work.

## Gaps (for whoever builds the next layer)

- **Kuntres Eitz HaChaim ch. 29-33 are missing from the file** (the OCR stops in ch. 28), including ch. 33, "learning in order to do; the service of prayer." Worth recovering from another edition.
- **Farbrengen, hiskashrus to a Rebbe, yechidus, and the mashpia as an institution** are nearly absent from these texts. The Alter Rebbe's letters mention bringing questions to the wise of one's town; the Rashab names the yeshiva's mashpiim by name (Eitz HaChaim ch. 25, line 350, the "RSh" and "RM" who read to the students "from the book specifically"), but there is no doctrine of hiskashrus or farbrengen here. Look to the Rayatz, the Rebbe, and Sefer HaSichos.
- **Niggun** appears only in passing (Tanya ch. 50; one Rashab letter). Not a practice with instructions in these sources.
- **Bitachon** is present as theory (Iggeret HaKodesh 11: joy because "yes and no are equal" to the believer; Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 11) but with no daily instructions. Chovos HaLevavos / the Tzemach Tzedek's "tracht gut" belong to another source list.
- **Eating as birur** has principle (Tanya ch. 7-8, Iggeret HaKodesh 26 on eating that turns into Torah and prayer) but little instruction on how to eat.
- **Relationships at home** (spouse, children) are touched only in "wife and children come first" (Iggeret HaKodesh 9) and Tanya ch. 49's "not body, soul, money, wife or children" as obstacles to love of G-d. No guidance on family life as avodah.
- **Pelach HaRimon and Sha'arei Avodah** were searched, not read through. Both are long and dense; the passages above are the clearest hits for avodah practice. Sha'arei Avodah's gates on prayer and on bittul would repay a full reading for the "counterfeits" heading.
- **Igros Kodesh** (18,000 OCR lines) was searched by keyword (עבודה שבלב, עצבות, אהבת רעים, חשבון); a full reading would certainly yield more directives. The volume index at lines 17315 and 17353 (entries under מרירות and עצבות) points to further letters.
