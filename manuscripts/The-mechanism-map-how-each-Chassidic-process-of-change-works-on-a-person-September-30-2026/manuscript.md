- **What it is.** For each core Chassidic process of change: what the sources say it is, which psychological mechanisms it probably works through, how good the evidence for each mechanism is, the conversational move that engages that mechanism, who should not get it, and where the psychology and the teaching part ways.

## 1. Hisbonenus: contemplation in detail

**The process (the sources).** Take one truth about G-d, go into its details and examples, stay with it and come back to it, until the mind is bound to it the way it is bound to something it sees. A general idea, however true, gives a flash that does not last.

- «אך עיקר הדעת, אינה הידיעה לבדה» (*The main thing in da'at is not knowing alone.* Tanya ch. 42, line 629; FOUND near line 629) - «להעמיק דעתו בגדולת ה׳, ולתקוע מחשבתו בה׳ בחוזק ואומץ הלב והמוח» (*To deepen his mind in G-d's greatness and fix his thought on Him with strength and force of heart and mind.* Tanya ch. 42, line 629; FOUND near line 629) - «אבל הוא דמיון כוזב בלבד ואין לו קיום כלל» (*It is only a false imagining and has no staying power at all (the flash from general, not detailed, contemplation).* Rashab, Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR), line 92; FOUND near line 92) - «שיאריך בההתבוננות ויתבונן כמה פעמים באריכות» (*Let him dwell at length and contemplate it several times at length.* Rayatz, Sefer HaMaamarim, line 449; FOUND near line 449)

- **Concreteness (concrete versus abstract repetitive thought).** Thinking in specific, concrete, process-focused terms helps; the same amount of thinking in abstract, evaluative terms keeps distress going. 'In detail' is the concrete mode. Watkins, 2008 [in our files]: Review: repetitive thought is constructive when concrete and unconstructive when abstract and evaluative. *(review of experiments; strong)*; Watkins and Moberly, 2009 [in our files]: Concreteness training reduced dysphoria. *(pilot; weak)*
- **Elaboration and self-generation.** Material processed for meaning, linked to many details and put into one's own words is held longer and more richly than material repeated or read. Craik and Lockhart, 1972 [confirmed by search]: Deeper, meaning-level processing gives more durable memory. *(theory, widely tested; strong for memory)*; Slamecka and Graf, 1978 [confirmed by search]: Items a person generates are remembered better than items read. *(experiment, robust; strong for memory)*; Craik and Watkins, 1973 [in our files]: Mere repetition does not deepen memory. *(experiment)*
- **Two kinds of meaning (propositional versus implicational).** Knowing a sentence is true and having it 'feel true' run on different systems; felt meaning shifts through rich, specific, sensory and personally relevant material, not through more argument. Teasdale, 1993 [in our files]: Emotion follows the holistic 'implicational' level, not the propositional one. *(theory; moderate)*; Stott, 2007 [in our files]: Clinical analysis of the head-heart gap in cognitive therapy. *(theory / clinical lore)*
- **Mental imagery (the mashal).** Picturing something produces more emotion than processing the same content verbally. Holmes and Mathews, 2005 [in our files]: Imagery raised emotion more than verbal processing of the same material. *(experiment; moderate)*; Holmes and Mathews, 2010 [in our files]: Review of imagery's special link to emotion. *(review)*
- **Reappraisal.** Changing what a situation means changes the emotion it produces; it engages prefrontal control and modulates the amygdala. Webb et al., 2012 [in our files]: Reappraisal, especially reappraising the situation, changes emotion with small to moderate effects. *(meta-analysis; strong)*; Buhle et al., 2014 [confirmed by search]: Reappraisal recruits frontal and parietal control regions and modulates amygdala activity. *(meta-analysis of imaging; strong)*
- **Memory reconsolidation (reactivate, then mismatch).** An old emotional learning, once reactivated, may be open to updating if a clearly contradicting experience follows while it is active. Lane et al., 2015 [in our files]: Proposes reconsolidation as a common mechanism of lasting change across therapies. *(theory; moderate as a model)*; Schiller et al., 2010 [confirmed by search]: Reactivation before extinction prevented return of fear in humans. *(experiment)*; Chalkia et al., 2020 [confirmed by search]: A large registered direct replication of Schiller et al. did not find the effect. *(failed replication: treat the human mechanism as unsettled)*
- **Decentering (a partial fit only).** Seeing thoughts as events rather than facts. Hisbonenus does something different: it replaces the object of thought, it does not step back from thought. Bernstein et al., 2015 [in our files]: Review of decentering and related constructs. *(review / theory)*

**The conversational move.** Pick one point. Ask the person for one concrete instance from their own day where it would be true (they generate it). Give one picture (a mashal) in plain words. Return to the same point two or three times in the conversation, a little deeper each time. Never stack several ideas. Engages: concreteness, self-generation, imagery, reactivate-then-mismatch. > *Sample:* "Take just this one thing: right now, this moment, you are being given. Where exactly, in the last hour, was something given to you that you didn't make?"

**Contraindications.** - Hisbonenus aimed at the self or at one's failures turns into rumination (abstract, evaluative, self-focused). Keep the object G-d and the world, not 'what is wrong with me'. - Vivid imagery can bring up intrusive memories in someone with trauma; if a picture brings up a memory, drop the picture. - Long absorbed sessions carry the usual meditation risks (depersonalization, unusual perceptions) in a small minority; see part-b adverse effects. - Obsessive or scrupulous people can loop on 'did I really feel it'; keep sessions short and end in an act.

**Where the two part.** - Reappraisal is chosen for its effect on distress. Hisbonenus is chosen because the Chassidic sources hold it true; the feeling that follows is love and awe, not calm. - Psychology can show that detail, imagery and self-generation deepen processing. It cannot show that what is contemplated is so. - Reconsolidation is our proposed model of why the loop's order works (locate, then distinction, then experience), not the sources' account, and the human evidence is contested.

## 2. Yeshus and bittul: the self as a thing by itself, and the self that is not in the way

**The process (the sources).** Yeshus is feeling oneself to be 'a something, a thing in itself'; bittul is the self that does not stand in the way of G-d's light. When contemplation stops, the self comes back; serving one's own image is the counterfeit.

- «להיות יש ודבר בפני עצמו» (*To be a something, a thing in itself.* Tanya ch. 19, line 292; FOUND near line 292) - «שאותו דבר נכלל באור ה׳ ובטל לו במציאות לגמרי» (*That this thing is included in G-d's light and wholly nullified to Him in its existence.* Tanya ch. 35, line 497; FOUND near line 497) - «מראה שכל עבודתו הוא שעובד את עצמו» (*It shows that all his service is serving himself.* Rashab, Kuntres Umaayan, line 387; FOUND near line 387) - «ולכן כשנפסק ההתבוננות, אינו בבחי' ביטול אז, וחוזר לישותו» (*So when the contemplation stops, he is not in bitul, and goes back to his selfhood.* Rashab, Samach Vav (Shemini Atzeres 5667), line 4486; verified in our files (consciousness-map/MAP.md))

- **Self-focused attention.** Attention turned on the self goes with more negative feeling, most of all in depression and anxiety. Mor and Winquist, 2002 [in our files]: Self-focus is associated with negative affect, strongest for rumination-like self-focus. *(meta-analysis; strong (correlational))*; Ingram, 1990 [confirmed by search]: Self-focused attention is a common feature across clinical disorders. *(review)*
- **Inflated responsibility.** Believing that one alone is responsible for preventing harm drives checking, worry and guilt. Salkovskis, 1985 [confirmed by search]: Cognitive model of obsessions built on inflated responsibility. *(theory)*; Salkovskis et al., 2000 [confirmed by search]: Responsibility beliefs distinguish people with OCD from anxious and non-clinical groups. *(correlational)*
- **Quiet ego and hypo-egoic states.** Less preoccupation with self-image, with the self still present and caring. Wayment et al., 2015 [in our files]: A validated measure of the quiet ego. *(measure)*; Leary et al., 2006 [in our files]: Hypo-egoic self-regulation: control improves when self-talk and self-image drop. *(review / theory)*
- **Self-transcendence and the small self.** Experiences that shrink the felt self and enlarge connection. Yaden et al., 2017 [in our files]: Map of self-transcendent experiences. *(review)*; Bai et al., 2017 [in our files]: Awe shrinks the felt self across cultures. *(experiments)*; Graziosi et al., 2026 [in our files]: Many awe-induction effects did not replicate. *(replication problem)*
- **Spiritual self-enhancement (the counterfeit, measured).** Practice can inflate the ego instead of quieting it. Gebauer et al., 2018 [in our files]: Yoga and meditation raised self-enhancement. *(experiment)*; Vonk and Visser, 2021 [in our files]: Spiritual superiority as a form of self-enhancement. *(correlational)*

**The conversational move.** Ask how much of the weight is about how it reflects on them, and how much is the task itself. Ask which part is actually theirs to do today, and let them name what is not theirs. Move the question from 'what does this say about me' to 'what is being asked of me here'. Engages: shift attention from self-image to task and Giver, return over-responsibility. > *Sample:* "If nobody would ever know how this turned out, how much of this weight would still be here?"

**Contraindications.** - Never offer bittul to someone who already feels worthless: 'you are nothing' lands as atzvus. The Piaseczner: one who feels himself empty and vile behaves that way (Esh Kodesh, line 541, verified in consciousness-map/MAP.md). - Depersonalization or a fragile sense of self: do not deepen 'not a thing by itself'; see the measure in Sierra and Berrios (2000). - Someone being controlled or abused must not hear bittul as 'your needs don't count'. - False humility is its own counterfeit in the sources (DARKEI-HACHASSIDUS.md §7 C).

**Where the two part.** - The quiet ego keeps a balanced self. Bittul says the self is not a thing by itself before G-d, and at the same time the soul is 'a part of G-d above'; it lowers the self and raises the person at once. - Bittul is not ego dissolution in the psychedelic sense (Nour et al., 2016 measure): the person stays a servant with a task.

## 3. Bitachon and hashgacha pratis: trust, and providence in detail

**The process (the sources).** Every detail is held and directed; trust is leaning one's full weight so that one does not worry, and it is work done in the soul, not a forecast. One does one's part and does not make calculations about how it could work out.

- «שהדבר וודאי אצלו עד שהוא במנוחה גמורה ואינו דואג כלל» (*The thing is certain to him, until he is at complete rest and does not worry at all.* The Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos vol. 36, line 25020; FOUND near line 25020) - «בטחון הוא עבודה ויגיעה בנפשו» (*Trust is work and toil in his soul.* The Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos vol. 36, line 25038; FOUND near line 25038) - «מבלי לעשות חשבונות, אם אפשר לו להנצל» (*Without making calculations whether he can be saved.* The Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos vol. 36, line 25039; FOUND near line 25039) - «להרגיל את עצמו לראות ענין השגחה פרטית» (*To train oneself to see individual providence.* Hayom Yom, line 380; FOUND near line 380) - «הכל טוב בתכלית רק שאינו מושג» (*All is utterly good, only it is not grasped.* Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 11, line 1150; FOUND near line 1150)

- **Intolerance of uncertainty and worry as an attempt at control.** Not being able to bear not knowing drives worry; worry feels protective and so is kept up. Dugas et al., 1998 [in our files]: Intolerance of uncertainty is central in a model of generalized anxiety. *(correlational)*; Carleton, 2016 [in our files]: Fear of the unknown as a fundamental fear. *(review)*; Borkovec et al., 1999 [in our files]: Positive beliefs about worry keep it going. *(review)*; Newman and Llera, 2011 [in our files]: Worry keeps one braced so a bad outcome won't be a shock. *(review / theory)*
- **Secondary control.** When the world can't be changed, aligning oneself with a larger power or meaning is a form of control that lowers distress. Rothbaum et al., 1982 [in our files]: The two-process model of control. *(theory)*; Morling and Evered, 2006 [in our files]: Secondary control defined and reviewed. *(review)*
- **G-d as a secure base and safe haven.** A trusted Other lowers threat and frees exploration. Kirkpatrick, 1992 [in our files]: Attachment theory applied to religion. *(theory)*; Granqvist et al., 2010 [in our files]: Religion as attachment. *(review)*; Granqvist et al., 2012 [in our files]: Subliminal threat raised access to God as attachment figure. *(experiments)*; Coan et al., 2006 [in our files]: Holding a trusted person's hand damped the brain's threat response. *(imaging experiment)*
- **Trust versus mistrust in G-d and worry.** Trust in G-d goes with less worry; mistrust with more. Rosmarin et al., 2009 [in our files]: Trust in G-d related to less anxiety and depression in a Jewish sample. *(correlational)*; Rosmarin et al., 2011 [in our files]: Trust and mistrust in G-d fit a cognitive model of worry. *(correlational)*; Rosmarin et al., 2010 [in our files]: An internet spiritually integrated program for Jewish adults reduced subclinical anxiety. *(RCT, small)*; Knabb et al., 2017 [in our files]: Providence-focused program for recurrent worry. *(pilot)*
- **Effort plus trust (hishtadlus and bitachon).** Engaging with goals that can be reached and letting go of those that can't predicts well-being. Wrosch et al., 2003 [in our files]: Goal disengagement and re-engagement predict well-being. *(correlational / longitudinal)*; Pargament et al., 1988 [in our files]: A 'deferring' style (leave it all to G-d) did worse than a collaborative style. *(correlational)*
- **Compensatory control (weak).** The claim that low control drives belief in a controlling G-d. Kay et al., 2008 [in our files]: Original experiments. *(experiment)*; Hoogeveen et al., 2018 [in our files]: Registered replication did not find the effect. *(failed replication)*

**The conversational move.** Separate their part (the next doable act, named concretely) from the outcome. Let them do or plan the act. Then invite them to hand the rest over in their own words. Treat trust as a thing they do now, not a prediction that it will turn out well. The guide never promises an outcome. Engages: separate controllable from uncontrollable, secure base, trust as a chosen stance. > *Sample:* "What is the one thing that's actually yours to do about this today? Do that. And the part you can't reach, can you say, in your own words, 'this part is Yours'?"

**Contraindications.** - Trust used to skip needed action (health, safety, money) is the deferring style, which does worse. Bitachon includes the act. - 'All is good' said to fresh grief or injury invalidates; the sources themselves say it is good 'only not grasped'. Do not explain the pain. - In obsessive worry, trust statements can become compulsive reassurance; do not repeat reassurance on request. - A punishing image of G-d (Rowatt and Kirkpatrick, 2002; Exline et al., 2014) makes 'He is in charge' frightening; start from being held, not from control. - Danger overrides everything: trust never replaces the safety protocol.

**Where the two part.** - Psychology measures trust by what it does to worry. For the Rebbe trust is service and toil whether or not it calms. - 'Think good and it will be good' (Likkutei Sichos 36, line 25032, in being-held/sources-chabad.md) claims an effect on reality; psychology can only test the effect on the person.

## 4. Emunah and da'at: believing, and knowing in the bones

**The process (the sources).** Emunah is the soul's faith above understanding; da'at is binding the mind to what one understands until it is felt. The aim is not to replace faith with reasoning but for the faith that is above reason to come into knowing.

- «רק להאמין באמונה, שהיא למעלה מהשכל ומהשגה» (*Only to believe, with a faith above the mind and grasp.* Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7, line 834; FOUND near line 834) - «שיבא כח האמונה זו שלמעלה מן הדעת לידי גלוי בדעת והבנה» (*That the power of this faith above knowing come to be revealed in knowing and understanding.* Mitteler Rebbe, Imrei Binah, introduction, line 4; verified in our files (consciousness-map/MAP.md)) - «אך עיקר הדעת, אינה הידיעה לבדה» (*The main thing in da'at is not knowing alone.* Tanya ch. 42, line 629; FOUND near line 629)

- **Head knowledge versus felt knowledge.** A person can hold a belief and not feel it, and the two respond to different kinds of input. Teasdale, 1993 [in our files]: Propositional and implicational meaning. *(theory)*; Stott, 2007 [in our files]: Rational-emotional dissociation. *(theory / clinical lore)*; Epstein, 1994 [confirmed by search]: Two systems, rational and experiential. *(theory)*; Gendler, 2008 [confirmed by search]: 'Alief' versus belief: automatic stances that diverge from what we believe. *(philosophy)*
- **Holding two explanations at once.** People commonly keep natural and supernatural explanations side by side. Legare et al., 2012 [in our files]: Coexistence of natural and supernatural explanations across cultures. *(correlational / developmental)*

**The conversational move.** Find where the person already believes something they don't feel. Do not argue for the belief. Ask what it is like 'when you're in it'. Then use the hisbonenus moves (detail, their own example, return) to let the known thing be felt. With someone who does not believe, start from what they already sense, not from a proposition. Engages: locate the gap, experience test, da'at practices (detail, dwelling). > *Sample:* "You said you believe He's there. When you're alone at night with this, what does it feel like is there?"

**Contraindications.** - Demanding felt proof ('you should feel it') makes a performance and then a failure. Emunah stands without feeling. - Treating doubt as sin in scrupulous people feeds checking of one's own faith (Abramowitz et al., 2002). - Some things (the reason for a particular suffering) are meant to stay above da'at; do not try to make them understood.

**Where the two part.** - The sharpest divergence in the map: psychology's dual-process models treat the intuitive, felt layer as lower and faster than reason. In Chassidus emunah is above reason, not below it; da'at brings what is higher down into the mind and heart. - So the guide must never frame faith as a feeling to be upgraded by argument, nor feeling as the test of faith.

## 5. The mind rules the heart

**The process (the sources).** By nature the mind rules the heart: one can always choose what to think about, and one can stop desire from reaching thought, speech and act. The ruling runs through the three 'garments' (thought, speech, deed); the in-between person's heart may still want.

- «כי המוח שליט על הלב בתולדתו» (*For the mind rules the heart by its nature.* Tanya ch. 12, line 433; FOUND near line 433) - «כי מוחו ברשותו ויכול להתבונן בו ככל אשר יחפוץ» (*His mind is in his own hands; he can contemplate with it whatever he wants.* Tanya ch. 17, line 265; FOUND near line 265) - «קיר איז אַ וואַנט וואָס איז מפסיק בין מוחין למדות» (*Kir is a wall that separates mind from heart.* Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5691, line 5518; FOUND near line 5518)

- **The process model of emotion regulation.** Emotion can be changed at several points: choosing situations, directing attention, changing meaning, changing response. Earlier points work better. Gross, 1998 [confirmed by search]: Integrative model of emotion regulation. *(review / theory)*; Webb et al., 2012 [in our files]: Attention deployment and reappraisal work; suppressing expression of feeling has small effects on the feeling. *(meta-analysis)*
- **Cognitive control of emotion.** Prefrontal control changes how emotional input is appraised. Ochsner and Gross, 2005 [confirmed by search]: Review of the neural basis of cognitive emotion control. *(review)*; Buhle et al., 2014 [confirmed by search]: Meta-analysis of reappraisal imaging. *(meta-analysis)*
- **Reappraisal versus suppression.** Changing meaning goes with better outcomes than hiding feeling. Gross and John, 2003 [in our files]: Habitual reappraisal goes with better well-being, suppression with worse. *(correlational)*
- **If-then plans.** Deciding in advance what to do when a cue appears makes the act more automatic. Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006 [in our files]: Implementation intentions improve goal attainment (medium to large effect). *(meta-analysis)*
- **Control is not a draining muscle.** The ego-depletion effect did not replicate; the Tanya's 'always in his power' is not contradicted by a finite-willpower model. Hagger et al., 2016 [in our files]: Multi-lab registered replication found no ego-depletion effect. *(failed replication)*

**The conversational move.** Aim the person's control at the next thought, the next word and the next act, never at the feeling. Tell them the feeling may stay. Offer one if-then: 'when X happens, I will think of Y / say nothing / do Z'. Engages: attention deployment, if-then plan, scope control to the garments. > *Sample:* "You don't have to stop wanting it. What will you let your mind rest on the next time it comes, and what will your hands do?"

**Contraindications.** - Never ask someone to stop feeling something: suppression of experience rebounds (Wegner et al., 1987; Wang et al., 2020). - In flooding distress or danger, the safety protocol comes first; contemplation is not a crisis tool. - 'Rule your heart' can shame people whose feelings are strong by temperament; the Tanya itself says the beinoni's heart still wants.

**Where the two part.** - In psychology the regulator is a skill used for well-being. In the Tanya the ruling mind is the G-dly soul's contemplation of G-d's greatness; the content (awe) rules, not bare willpower. - The Tanya expects the heart to follow in time and treats the deed as valuable even if it never does; regulation research measures the feeling.

## 6. Itkafia and ishapcha: subduing, and transforming

**The process (the sources).** Itkafia is subduing the pull while it is still strong: delaying, holding back a word, pushing a thought away at once and turning to another. Ishapcha is the pull itself turned, 'bitter to sweet, dark to light'. Habit becomes second nature.

- «כד אתכפיא הסטרא אחרא בעודה בתקפה וגבורתה» (*When the other side is subdued while still in its strength and might.* Tanya ch. 27, line 387; FOUND near line 387) - «ואתהפכא ממרירו למתקא ומחשוכא לנהורא» (*And is turned from bitter to sweet and from darkness to light.* Tanya ch. 27, line 387; FOUND near line 387) - «דוחהו בשתי ידים» (*He pushes it away with both hands.* Tanya ch. 12, line 215; FOUND near line 215) - «שההרגל על כל דבר … ונעשה טבע שני» (*Habit rules over everything and becomes second nature.* Tanya ch. 14, line 239; FOUND near line 239)

- **Inhibiting a habit when it fires.** For strong habits, the only strategy that worked in daily life was vigilant monitoring (thinking 'don't do it', watching for slips); it works by inhibiting the response, not by changing the cue memory. Quinn et al., 2010 [confirmed by search]: Two diary studies and an experiment on controlling bad habits. *(diary + experiment; moderate)*
- **Thought suppression backfires, replacement does not.** Trying not to think X brings X back. The Tanya's push is immediate and turns to other content (a line of Torah), which is closer to replacement than to 'don't think of X'. Wegner et al., 1987 [in our files]: Suppression produced rebound. *(experiment)*; Wang et al., 2020 [in our files]: Ironic effects are real but small and variable. *(meta-analysis)*
- **Habit carries self-control.** People good at self-control mostly resist less; good habits do the work. Galla and Duckworth, 2015 [confirmed by search]: Beneficial habits mediate self-control's link to outcomes, with less effortful inhibition. *(correlational + longitudinal)*; Hofmann et al., 2012 [confirmed by search]: People high in self-control report fewer temptations to resist. *(experience sampling)*; Lally et al., 2010 [in our files]: Habits formed over a median of about two months, with wide spread. *(observational)*; Wood and Rünger, 2016 [confirmed by search]: Habits are cue-triggered and outlast intentions. *(review)*
- **Transformation: changing the response, not only blocking it.** Reappraisal and acceptance change the emotion's meaning; accepting negative states predicts better health than fighting them. Ford et al., 2018 [in our files]: Accepting negative emotions and thoughts predicts better psychological health. *(lab + diary + longitudinal)*; Webb et al., 2012 [in our files]: Reappraisal changes emotion. *(meta-analysis)*

**The conversational move.** For itkafia: one small, concrete, time-limited act against the pull today (delay, one unsaid word), with the replacement named in advance. For ishapcha: ask what the pull is really for; let that want find its true object. Engages: one small inhibition with a replacement ready, if-then plan, the want under the want. > *Sample:* "Not all day, just once: when it comes, wait ten minutes and do this instead. And separately: underneath the craving, what are you actually hungry for?"

**Contraindications.** - White-knuckling alone fails and ends in shame (Rav Joey Rosenfeld, DIGEST.md §3d). - Pushing away intrusive thoughts in OCD feeds them; do not coach thought-fighting for intrusive or unwanted thoughts that recur and distress. - The Tanya's 'delay a meal' must never be offered to someone with disordered eating. - Self-mortification is named as a counterfeit in the sources (DARKEI-HACHASSIDUS.md §7 C).

**Where the two part.** - Psychology values inhibition for the goal it serves. The Tanya values itkafia in itself, as a delight above, even if it never gets easier. - Ishapcha claims the evil itself becomes good. Psychology changes the response and its meaning; it makes no claim about the essence.

## 7. Teshuvah: return

**The process (the sources).** The commandment of teshuvah is leaving the sin and resolving not to return to it, not fasting. It can be done again and again. After it comes joy.

- «היא עזיבת החטא בלבד» (*It is only abandoning the sin.* Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 1, line 893; FOUND near line 893) - «ולא כדעת ההמון, שהתשובה היא התענית» (*Not like the view of the masses, that teshuvah is fasting.* Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 1, line 895; FOUND near line 895) - «אין הפרש בין פעם אחת לאלף פעמים» (*There is no difference between once and a thousand times.* Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 11, line 1003; FOUND near line 1003) - «והעצה לזה ת"ת ולא עצבות» (*The remedy for this is Torah study, not sadness.* The Rebbe, Igros Kodesh vol. 4, line 15653; FOUND near line 15653)

- **Guilt versus shame.** Guilt is about the act and leads to repair; shame is about the self and leads to hiding, anger and defensiveness. Tangney et al., 2007 [in our files]: Review of moral emotions: guilt proneness adaptive, shame proneness not. *(review; strong)*
- **Self-compassion after failure.** Kindness to oneself after a failure raises, not lowers, motivation to improve. Breines and Chen, 2012 [in our files]: Self-compassion increased self-improvement motivation. *(experiments)*; Leary et al., 2007 [in our files]: Self-compassion buffered reactions to unpleasant self-relevant events. *(experiments)*
- **Moral repair.** Repair restores shared values between wrongdoer and wronged; genuine self-forgiveness works through the wrong, while self-punishment gets in the way. Walker, 2006 [confirmed by search]: Philosophical account of moral repair. *(philosophy)*; Wenzel et al., 2021 [confirmed by search]: Value consensus drove genuine self-forgiveness; offenders' self-punitiveness went with less forgiveness from victims. *(longitudinal dyads)*
- **Scrupulosity (the failure mode).** Religious obsessions and compulsions: endless confession, checking, fear of sin. Abramowitz et al., 2002 [in our files]: Penn Inventory of Scrupulosity. *(measure)*
- **Redemptive narrative.** Stories in which bad turns to good go with better adjustment. McAdams et al., 2001 [in our files]: Redemption sequences predict well-being. *(correlational)*

**The conversational move.** Separate 'I did this' from 'this is what I am', and never let the second stand. Locate the one specific act. Ask what repair is possible (to the person, and the resolve). Name the value they are returning to. Then close: it is done, begin again. Engages: separate act from self, repair, value reaffirmation, close in joy. > *Sample:* "You did something you regret. That's not the same as being someone bad. What would repair look like here, one step? And what do you want to be true of you tomorrow?"

**Contraindications.** - Scrupulosity or OCD: do not join repeated confession or give repeated assurance of forgiveness; keep it to one act and one repair. - Depression with guilt: skip bitterness entirely; go to the value and a small act. - People blaming themselves for what was done to them: there is nothing to return from; do not use teshuvah language. - Fasting and self-punishment are not teshuvah (Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 1).

**Where the two part.** - Self-forgiveness in psychology is between a person and himself or others. Teshuvah is return to G-d; the higher teshuvah is the soul returning to its source and need not involve sin at all. - 'Sins become merits' is a claim about the past's standing before G-d; redemptive narrative research is only a parallel.

## 8. Simcha, atzvus and merirus: joy, dead sadness, living bitterness

**The process (the sources).** Atzvus is a heart dull as stone with no life in it, and it cannot win anything. Merirus (bitterness, a broken heart) has life in it, is about something, belongs to set times, and is followed by joy. The right time for accounting is when one is sad anyway, to turn that sadness into something with a direction.

- «אי אפשר לנצחו בעצלות וכבדות – הנמשכות מעצבות» (*One cannot win with sluggishness and heaviness that come from sadness.* Tanya ch. 26, line 371; FOUND near line 371) - «אין לב נשבר ומרירות הנפש» (*A broken heart and bitterness of soul are not (called sadness).* Tanya ch. 31, line 452; FOUND near line 452) - «שלבו מטומטם כאבן ואין חיות בלבו» (*His heart is dulled like a stone and there is no life in his heart.* Tanya ch. 31, line 452; FOUND near line 452) - «אזי היא שעת הכושר להפך העצב להיות מ״מרי דחושבנא״» (*Then is the right time to turn the sadness into being a master of accounting.* Tanya ch. 31, line 454; FOUND near line 454) - «והנה עגין המרירות והשפלות» (*The matter of bitterness and lowliness (should be only at times).* Rashab, Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR), line 168; FOUND near line 168)

- **Rumination versus reflection.** Anxious, abstract self-focus (rumination) harms; curious, concrete self-inquiry (reflection) does not. Trapnell and Campbell, 1999 [in our files]: Rumination and reflection are separable. *(correlational)*; Nolen-Hoeksema et al., 2008 [in our files]: Rumination prolongs and deepens depression. *(review; strong)*; Watkins, 2008 [in our files]: Concrete versus abstract repetitive thought. *(review)*
- **Sadness as adaptive, depression as stuck.** Low mood can serve a function (disengaging, re-evaluating) and mild negative mood can sharpen judgment; depression is that state stuck and generalized. Nesse, 2000 [confirmed by search]: Evolutionary account of low mood's functions. *(theory)*; Forgas, 2013 [confirmed by search]: Mild negative mood improved some judgments and interpersonal behavior. *(review of experiments)*
- **Behavioral activation.** Doing valued things, step by step, lifts depression. Cuijpers et al., 2007 [in our files]: Behavioral activation is effective for depression. *(meta-analysis; strong)*; Ekers et al., 2014 [confirmed by search]: Updated meta-analysis: effective, comparable to cognitive therapy. *(meta-analysis; strong)*
- **Joy and mixed emotion.** Positive emotion broadens attention and builds resources; weeping and joy can coexist, and mixed feeling precedes gains in well-being. Chasing happiness backfires. Fredrickson, 2001 [confirmed by search]: Broaden-and-build theory. *(theory)*; Brown et al., 2013 [confirmed by search]: The later 'positivity ratio' claim does not hold. *(critique)*; Larsen et al., 2001 [in our files]: People can feel happy and sad at once. *(experiment)*; Adler and Hershfield, 2012 [in our files]: Mixed emotion preceded well-being gains. *(longitudinal)*; Mauss et al., 2011 [in our files]: Valuing happiness can make people less happy. *(experiment)*
- **Accepting negative feeling.** Not judging one's negative feelings predicts fewer depressive symptoms. Shallcross et al., 2010 [in our files]: Acceptance predicted less negative affect and depression. *(longitudinal)*; Ford et al., 2018 [in our files]: Acceptance of negative emotion and health. *(multi-method)*

**The conversational move.** Tell apart sadness that has an object, a direction and an end from heaviness with no object where nothing matters. With living bitterness: give it a bounded time, keep it concrete, end it in a step and then joy. With dead heaviness: no contemplation of faults; one small act first, then the teaching. Engages: discriminate living from dead sadness, bound the bitterness, small act first. > *Sample:* "Is this sadness about something specific you wish were different, or more a heaviness where nothing seems to matter? (Then, if heaviness:) Let's not think about it yet. What's one small thing you can do in the next ten minutes?"

**Contraindications.** - Never induce merirus in someone who is depressed. - Never tell a sad person that sadness is a trick, from the other side, or 'like denial' (Iggeret HaKodesh 11, Tanya line 1146). These lines are for the teacher's understanding, never for the person in the heaviness. - Hopelessness or any sign of danger: the safety protocol overrides the teaching. - Forced cheer backfires (Mauss et al., 2011); joy is not demanded of the person.

**Where the two part.** - Psychology treats sadness as information and does not make joy a duty. Chassidus makes joy a service and places atzvus on the side of kelipah, while prizing the broken heart. - The guide keeps the Chassidic ranking for itself and shows the person only the useful half: bitterness with life in it is good and has an end.

## 9. The two souls

**The process (the sources).** Two souls, like two kings at war over one small city, each wants to fill the mind alone; the animal soul wants life and comfort, the G-dly soul wants closeness to G-d. The person is the one who chooses which rules.

- «וכמו ששני מלכים נלחמים על עיר אחת» (*As two kings war over one city.* Tanya ch. 9, line 185; FOUND near line 185) - «הן שתי נפשות, הנלחמות זו עם זו במוחו של אדם» (*They are two souls, warring with each other in a person's mind.* Tanya ch. 29, line 399; FOUND near line 399)

- **Dual-process cognition.** Fast intuitive processing and slow deliberate processing. Evans and Stanovich, 2013 [confirmed by search]: Dual-process theories and their critics. *(theory)*
- **Parts models and the dialogical self.** The mind as several voices or parts; relating to a part rather than being it. Hermans, 2001 [confirmed by search]: The dialogical self. *(theory)*; Shadick et al., 2013 [confirmed by search]: An Internal Family Systems program improved pain, self-compassion and depression in rheumatoid arthritis. *(small RCT, proof of concept)*
- **Self-distancing and defusion.** Seeing an urge or thought from a step back reduces its grip. Kross and Ayduk, 2017 [in our files]: Self-distancing reduces reactivity. *(review)*; Kross et al., 2014 [confirmed by search]: Using one's own name or 'you' in self-talk improved regulation. *(experiments)*; Hayes et al., 2006 [in our files]: Defusion within acceptance and commitment therapy. *(review)*

**The conversational move.** Let the person hear the pull as 'a part of you that wants X' and the other as 'the part that wants Y'. Ask which one they recognise as themselves. Name the animal soul's want kindly: it wants life and comfort, not evil. Engages: name the part, identify with the deeper want, distance without demonizing. > *Sample:* "Sounds like part of you wants to disappear under the covers and part of you wants to be there for your kid. Which of those is more you?"

**Contraindications.** - Dissociation or psychosis: do not frame inner voices as another soul or being. - 'My evil side' language breeds shame; keep the animal soul's want sympathetic. - Scrupulous people may start checking which soul each thought came from; don't feed that.

**Where the two part.** - In parts models every part is welcome and protective; the aim is harmony. In the Tanya the animal soul is to be ruled and refined, though its root is good and it can be turned (ishapcha). - The G-dly soul is not 'System 2'. It is not reason; it is 'a part of G-d above' with its own love. The animal soul has its own intelligence.

## 10. Avodah b'gashmiyus and dirah b'tachtonim: serving in the ordinary, a home below

**The process (the sources).** The purpose of the world is that G-d desired a dwelling in the lowest place; this is done in the ordinary deed. One deed is better than a thousand sighs.

- «שנתאוה הקדושברוךהוא להיות לו דירה בתחתונים» (*That the Holy One desired a dwelling in the lower realms.* Tanya ch. 36, line 506; FOUND near line 506) - «טובה פעולה אחת מאלף אנחות» (*Better one act than a thousand sighs.* Hayom Yom, line 192; FOUND near line 192) - «וזה כלל גדול בעבודת ה׳ לבינונים» (*This is a great rule in serving G-d for in-between people (hidden love is enough to act).* Tanya ch. 16, line 252; FOUND near line 252)

- **Behavioral activation.** Acting on what matters, before feeling like it, restores mood and contact with reward. Cuijpers et al., 2007 [in our files]: Effective for depression. *(meta-analysis)*; Ekers et al., 2014 [confirmed by search]: Effective, comparable to cognitive therapy. *(meta-analysis)*
- **Values-based action.** Choosing actions by values rather than by mood. Hayes et al., 2006 [in our files]: Model and processes of acceptance and commitment therapy. *(review)*; A-Tjak et al., 2015 [in our files]: ACT is efficacious for clinically relevant problems. *(meta-analysis)*
- **Sanctification.** Seeing ordinary life as holy goes with more investment and satisfaction in it. Pargament and Mahoney, 2005 [in our files]: Sanctification as a construct. *(review / theory)*; Mahoney et al., 1999 [in our files]: Sanctified marriage went with better marital functioning. *(correlational)*
- **Meaning.** Presence of meaning goes with well-being; meaning-making helps adjustment. Steger et al., 2006 [in our files]: Meaning in Life Questionnaire. *(measure)*; Park, 2010 [in our files]: Meaning-making review. *(review)*
- **Kindness.** Doing kindness raises the doer's well-being a little. Curry et al., 2018 [in our files]: Small to medium effects of kindness on the actor. *(meta-analysis)*

**The conversational move.** Take the most ordinary next act they have today (the dishes, the email, the phone call) and ask what or whom it serves. Invite them to do one of them on purpose, as a home for Him. Size it to what they can do today. Engages: activation, values, sanctification. > *Sample:* "What's the most ordinary thing you have to do in the next hour? Try doing just that one on purpose, as if you're making room for Him in it."

**Contraindications.** - Turning every act into a test breeds perfectionism and scrupulosity; one act, not all. - For someone depressed, 'make it holy' can feel like another demand; keep it tiny and optional. - Do not promise the act will feel meaningful; the Tanya says it counts even when it doesn't.

**Where the two part.** - Behavioral activation works by reinforcement regardless of meaning. Dirah b'tachtonim is about G-d's desire, not the person's mood; the deed matters even if the heart stays cold. - Psychology measures the doer's well-being. The sources measure what the deed does above.

## 11. Cheshbon hanefesh: the accounting of the soul

**The process (the sources).** An honest account of thoughts, words and deeds, at a set time, briefly bitter about one detail, then the sadness put down and tomorrow planned. Not endless review.

- «לעשות חשבון עם נפשו, מכל המחשבות והדיבורים והמעשים» (*To make an accounting with his soul, of all his thoughts, words and deeds.* Tanya ch. 29, line 417; FOUND near line 417) - «אזי היא שעת הכושר להפך העצב להיות מ״מרי דחושבנא״» (*Then is the right time to turn the sadness into being a master of accounting.* Tanya ch. 31, line 454; FOUND near line 454) - «צוברעכט דעם קיר המפסיק בין המוח אל הלב» (*(The davening after an honest accounting) breaks the wall between mind and heart.* Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5691, line 5523; FOUND near line 5523) - «במשך איזה שבועות יסיח דעת מלחשוב אודות מצבו ברוחניות» (*For some weeks, let him stop thinking about his spiritual state.* The Rebbe, Igros Kodesh vol. 4, line 15653; FOUND near line 15653)

- **Self-monitoring.** Tracking progress toward a goal improves attainment, more when written down or told to someone. Harkin et al., 2016 [confirmed by search]: Monitoring raised goal attainment (d = 0.40), more when recorded physically or reported. *(meta-analysis, 138 studies; strong)*
- **Expressive writing.** Putting experience into words has small benefits. Frattaroli, 2006 [in our files]: Average effect r of about .075 across 146 studies. *(meta-analysis; small)*; Pennebaker, 1997 [in our files]: The method and its rationale. *(review)*
- **Reflection, not rumination.** Concrete review helps; abstract self-judgment harms. Watkins, 2008 [in our files]: Concrete versus abstract. *(review)*; Trapnell and Campbell, 1999 [in our files]: Reflection versus rumination. *(correlational)*
- **Self-compassion as the condition for honest review.** Kindness toward failure makes it safe to look. Breines and Chen, 2012 [in our files]: Self-compassion raised improvement motivation. *(experiments)*
- **If-then for tomorrow.** A concrete plan for the next cue. Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006 [in our files]: Implementation intentions. *(meta-analysis)*

**The conversational move.** Short and concrete: one thing that went well today, one specific moment to repair, one if-then for tomorrow. Then close it out loud. Offer writing it down. Engages: concrete monitoring, bounded time, plan. > *Sample:* "Three lines, no more: one moment today you're glad of, one moment you'd redo, and what you'll do next time that moment comes."

**Contraindications.** - Depression, rumination and scrupulosity: shorten or skip; the Rebbe's instruction to stop reviewing for weeks applies. - Never at the peak of shame; wait for a quieter time. - Always ends in closure and a plan, never in a verdict on the self.

**Where the two part.** - Self-monitoring measures progress toward one's own goals. Cheshbon measures against G-d's will, and ends in renewal and joy, not in a score.

## 12. Hisbodedus: speaking to G-d in your own words

**The process (the sources).** Set a time alone and pour out your words to G-d in the language you speak, with arguments, pleas and reconciliation; the heart follows words that are one's own.

- «ההתבודדות הוא מעלה עליונה וגדולה מן הכל» (*Hisbodedus is a supreme level, greater than everything.* Likutei Moharan II 25, line 6344; FOUND near line 6344) - «ולפרש שיחתו בינו לבין קונו» (*To lay out his words between himself and his Maker.* Likutei Moharan II 25, line 6344; FOUND near line 6344) - «ותפלה ושיחה זו יהיה בלשון שמדברים בו» (*This prayer and talk should be in the language one speaks.* Likutei Moharan II 25, line 6345; FOUND near line 6345) - «רק בשעה מיחדת יהיה לו לב נשבר» (*Only in a set hour should he have a broken heart.* Likutei Moharan II 24, line 6343; FOUND near line 6343) - «ביום ההולדת, על האדם להתבודד» (*On one's birthday a person should be alone (to reflect).* Hayom Yom, line 244; FOUND near line 244)

- **Disclosure.** Putting experience into words to someone has small reliable benefits. Frattaroli, 2006 [in our files]: Small average benefit. *(meta-analysis)*; Pennebaker, 1997 [in our files]: Method and rationale. *(review)*
- **Naming feelings.** Labeling a feeling damps its intensity. Lieberman et al., 2007 [in our files]: Affect labeling reduced amygdala response. *(imaging experiment)*; Torre and Lieberman, 2018 [in our files]: Affect labeling as implicit regulation. *(review)*
- **Prayer uses the machinery of relationship.** Personal prayer recruits the brain's social-cognition network; practice trains the felt presence of a listening Other. Schjoedt et al., 2009 [in our files]: Improvised prayer engaged theory-of-mind regions. *(imaging study)*; Luhrmann and Morgain, 2012 [in our files]: Prayer as inner-sense cultivation. *(theory / ethnography)*; Granqvist et al., 2010 [in our files]: G-d as attachment figure. *(review)*
- **Prayer changes the one who prays.** Praying for someone increased forgiveness of them, through selfless concern. Lambert et al., 2010 [confirmed by search]: Two experiments. *(experiments; moderate)*
- **Self-talk in the second person.** Addressing oneself or another as 'you' gives distance and regulation. Kross et al., 2014 [confirmed by search]: Non-first-person self-talk improved regulation. *(experiments)*
- **What prayer research does not show.** Prayer for another's health had no measurable effect on the other's health. Masters and Spielmans, 2007 [confirmed by search]: No discernible effect of distant intercessory prayer. *(meta-analysis)*

**The conversational move.** Invite them to say the exact thing, in their own words, to Him, addressed as 'You'. Offer a first half-sentence if they are stuck ('You know that I...'). Let complaint in. Keep the words that come; don't polish them. Engages: disclosure, labeling, second-person address, attachment. > *Sample:* "Would you try saying it to Him directly, just as you said it to me, starting with 'You'?"

**Contraindications.** - A punishing image of G-d makes this feel like facing a judge; start with what they would want to tell Him, including anger. - Do not encourage 'listening for answers' or voices in someone with unusual experiences. - Do not press for disclosure of a fresh trauma: compulsory single-session debriefing did not help and was advised against (Rose et al., 2002). - The guide never claims prayer will change an outcome or heal.

**Where the two part.** - In disclosure research the active ingredient is the person's own processing. Hisbodedus holds there is a real Listener; the relationship is the point, not the benefit. - The guide can measure only what it does to the person, and says nothing about what it does above.

## 13. Niggun: the wordless tune

**The process (the sources).** A niggun strengthens hope and trust; the voice wakes the heart's intention; a niggun shows a person where he is. It is a preparation for the work, not the work.

- «א חסידעשער ניגון שטארקט די תקוה ובטחון» (*A Chassidic niggun strengthens hope and trust.* Hayom Yom, line 418; FOUND near line 418) - «חסידים זינגען אין דאַוונען, ווייל קול מעורר הכוונה» (*Chassidim sing in davening, because the voice wakes the intention.* Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5706, line 6148; FOUND near line 6148) - «אַ ניגון ווייזט אים וואו ער איז» (*A niggun shows him where he is.* Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5706, line 6527; FOUND near line 6527) - «ניגון איז אַ הכנה צו עבודה, דאָס איז נאָך ניט עבודה» (*A niggun is a preparation for avodah; it is not yet avodah.* Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5706, line 6531; FOUND near line 6531)

- **Many routes from music to feeling.** Reflexes, contagion, conditioning, imagery, episodic memory and expectancy. Juslin and Västfjäll, 2008 [in our files]: Framework of mechanisms. *(theory)*
- **Reward.** Peak musical emotion engages reward circuits and dopamine release. Blood and Zatorre, 2001 [in our files]: Chills engage reward regions. *(imaging study)*; Salimpoor et al., 2011 [in our files]: Dopamine release at anticipation and peak. *(imaging study)*
- **Mood.** Music therapy added to usual care improved depressive symptoms in the short term. Aalbers et al., 2017 [confirmed by search]: Short-term benefit; no difference in adverse events. *(systematic review)*
- **Bonding when sung together.** Group singing bonds faster; synchrony raises prosocial behavior modestly. Pearce et al., 2015 [confirmed by search]: Singing classes bonded faster than craft or writing classes. *(semi-naturalistic)*; Tarr et al., 2014 [in our files]: Self-other merging and endorphins. *(review)*; Mogan et al., 2017 [in our files]: Weak to moderate effects of synchrony. *(meta-analysis)*; Atwood et al., 2022 [in our files]: Expectancy effects may inflate synchrony findings. *(methods critique)*

**The conversational move.** In a text chat the guide can't sing. It can name a known niggun or invite 'any slow tune without words, hummed a few times', before a contemplation, and afterward ask what moved. Always followed by the teaching or an act. Engages: music-evoked emotion, episodic memory, preparation. > *Sample:* "Before we go on, if you know a slow niggun, hum it two or three times, or just any tune without words. Then tell me: where did it find you?"

**Contraindications.** - A tune tied to a loss or a trauma can flood; let the person choose the tune. - Not framed as relaxation (owner's rule); it wakes, it does not calm down. - A niggun as a substitute for the work is a counterfeit in the sources.

**Where the two part.** - Psychology explains how music moves feeling. The Chassidic claim is that the niggun is the soul's voice above words and shows a person where he stands.

## 14. Ratzo vashov: running and returning

**The process (the sources).** The soul runs toward G-d and returns to its task in the body; running without returning is death. Every rise must end in a deed.

- «רצוא בלי שוב, היא מיתה» (*Running without returning is death.* Hayom Yom, line 219; FOUND near line 219) - «טובה פעולה אחת מאלף אנחות» (*Better one act than a thousand sighs.* Hayom Yom, line 192; FOUND near line 192) - «ולכן כשנפסק ההתבוננות, אינו בבחי' ביטול אז, וחוזר לישותו» (*So when the contemplation stops, he is not in bitul, and goes back to his selfhood.* Rashab, Samach Vav, line 4486; verified in our files (consciousness-map/MAP.md))

- **Oscillation as healthy coping.** Moving back and forth between facing the loss and turning to life is adaptive; staying at either pole is not. Stroebe and Schut, 1999 [confirmed by search]: The dual process model of coping with bereavement. *(theory)*
- **Flexibility.** Being able to shift mode to fit the situation is a core feature of health. Kashdan and Rottenberg, 2010 [confirmed by search]: Psychological flexibility. *(review)*
- **The return is the repetition.** In attention training the moment of noticing and returning is the active unit. Hasenkamp et al., 2012 [in our files]: Cycles of wandering, noticing and returning. *(imaging experiment)*
- **Highs without integration.** Intense practice experiences without integration can destabilize; spiritual bypass uses highs to avoid life. Lindahl et al., 2017 [in our files]: Meditation-related challenges. *(mixed methods)*; Fox et al., 2017 [in our files]: Spiritual bypass measured. *(correlational)*

**The conversational move.** After any moment of feeling (the experience step), always the return: one act in the world today. When the person reports falling back after a high, name it as the second half of the rhythm, not a failure. Engages: end every rise in an act, normalize the fall back. > *Sample:* "That openness you felt, where does it go now? Pick one thing today it can go into."

**Contraindications.** - People chasing highs (bypass) or with a history of mania: do not intensify the rise; go straight to the return. - Depersonalization after intense experience: ground in the ordinary task, not in more contemplation.

**Where the two part.** - Oscillation in grief research doses distress. Ratzo vashov is the soul's longing to leave and its will to stay for the task; the return is itself service.

## 15. Ahavah and yirah: love and awe

**The process (the sources).** Love and awe are two wings; a bird can't fly on one. Both come from contemplating G-d's greatness; there is a lower awe (not to sin) and a higher awe (shame before greatness).

- «דדחילו ורחימו הן תרין גדפין» (*Awe and love are two wings.* Tanya ch. 41, line 610; FOUND near line 610) - «כמו שהעוף אינו יכול לפרוח בכנף אחד» (*As a bird cannot fly with one wing.* Tanya ch. 41, line 610; FOUND near line 610) - «ויתקע מחשבתו בחוזק בגדולת אין סוף ברוך הוא, ואינו מסיח דעתו» (*He fixes his thought firmly on the greatness of the Infinite and does not take his mind off it.* Tanya ch. 3, line 112; FOUND near line 112)

- **Awe.** Vastness that needs accommodation; shrinks the felt self, raises humility and prosociality. Keltner and Haidt, 2003 [in our files]: Prototype of awe. *(theory)*; Shiota et al., 2007 [in our files]: Awe's elicitors and effect on self-concept. *(experiment / correlational)*; Stellar et al., 2018 [in our files]: Awe and humility. *(experiments)*; Piff et al., 2015 [in our files]: Awe and prosocial behavior. *(experiments)*; Graziosi et al., 2026 [in our files]: Several awe-induction effects failed to replicate. *(replication problem)*
- **Threat-based awe.** Awe mixed with threat feels like fear and lowers well-being. Gordon et al., 2017 [in our files]: A distinct threat variant of awe. *(experiments)*
- **Positive emotion and being moved.** Love-like states broaden attention and bond; being moved is a measurable emotion. Fredrickson, 2001 [confirmed by search]: Broaden-and-build. *(theory)*; Zickfeld et al., 2019 [in our files]: Kama muta across cultures. *(measure)*
- **Gratitude.** Noticing what is given raises well-being a little. Davis et al., 2016 [in our files]: Small effects of gratitude interventions. *(meta-analysis)*

**The conversational move.** Love: from the detail of what is given now, to you. Awe: from vastness, with the smallness framed as safe. Pair them in one sitting; do not lead with awe for someone anxious. Engages: love from closeness in detail, awe from vastness that is safe, pair them. > *Sample:* "It's that big, and it's giving itself to this small moment of yours. Stay with both for a second."

**Contraindications.** - Anxious, traumatized, or punishing-G-d people: awe can land as threat; lead with love and being held. - Intense ecstatic states carry adverse-effect risk (see part-b). - Do not stage awe with grand imagery on demand; the replication record is weak.

**Where the two part.** - Awe research studies an emotion toward vastness. Yirah is before a Person, includes shame before greatness and fear of sin, and love is the soul's longing to merge, which psychology does not model.

## 16. The farbrengen and the mashpia: the table of friends, and the guide

**The process (the sources).** No one does this alone. The farbrengen is a foundation of the Chassidic way; a friend sees what self-love hides; the mashpia helps choose the method and must work on himself first.

- «באַ חסידים איז פאַרברענגען איינע פון די יסודי דרכי החסידות» (*Among Chassidim, farbrengen is one of the foundations of the Chassidic way.* Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5691, line 4993; FOUND near line 4993) - «כי אין אדם רואה נגעי עצמו מצד אהבת עצמו» (*A person does not see his own blemishes, because of self-love.* Rashab, Kuntres Umaayan, line 342; FOUND near line 342) - «אַז עס ווערט ווייכער דער עור הבהמה» (*So that the animal hide softens (why a little drink is at a farbrengen).* Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5691, line 4994; FOUND near line 4994)

- **Social connection.** Strong social relationships predict survival about as strongly as well-known risk factors. Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010 [confirmed by search]: Social relationships and mortality. *(meta-analysis; strong)*; Coan and Sbarra, 2015 [in our files]: Social baseline theory: others lower the cost of threat. *(review / theory)*; Coan et al., 2006 [in our files]: Hand-holding damped threat response. *(imaging experiment)*
- **Mentoring.** Mentoring relationships help modestly; quality and duration matter. DuBois et al., 2011 [confirmed by search]: Modest positive effects of youth mentoring. *(meta-analysis)*
- **The working relationship.** The quality of the bond between helper and helped predicts change across approaches. Flückiger et al., 2018 [confirmed by search]: Alliance-outcome correlation about r = .28 across 295 studies. *(meta-analysis; strong)*
- **Singing together.** Group singing speeds bonding. Pearce et al., 2015 [confirmed by search]: Faster bonding in singing classes. *(semi-naturalistic)*

**The conversational move.** The guide plays a mashpia-like role in how it listens: understand before teaching, speak calmly, never perform. After presence (not instead of it), ask whether there is one person they could say this to, or a table they could sit at. Engages: alliance first, a real person next. > *Sample:* "Is there one person you could say this to, the way you just said it to me?"

**Contraindications.** - Never recommend drink, at a farbrengen or anywhere (owner's rule), even though the source explains its old place. - Public rebuke or group shaming harms; the sources want the sting pointed at oneself. - The guide must not become a substitute for people; loneliness gets presence first, then a person, never a brush-off.

**Where the two part.** - Psychology's support buffers stress. The farbrengen aims at refinement of character, and the mashpia may help a person sense his own 'untastiness', which in psychology is confrontation and carries risk. - An AI is not a mashpia: it has no life of its own to work on, and it must say so if asked.

## What the map shows across processes *(ours)*

1. **"In detail" is the active ingredient.** The Rashab's claim that general contemplation is a flash that does not last lines up with three solid literatures: concrete versus abstract thought (Watkins, 2008), depth of processing (Craik and Lockhart, 1972) and self-generation (Slamecka and Graf, 1978), plus imagery's special pull on feeling (Holmes and Mathews, 2005). The guide's operations must be one point, one concrete instance from the person's life, produced by the person, returned to.
2. **The Tanya's 'rule' is over the garments, and that is exactly where control works.** Research shows that hiding or fighting a feeling does little or backfires, that changing meaning and attention works, and that control is not a draining muscle (Webb et al., 2012; Wang et al., 2020; Hagger et al., 2016). Chapter 12 already scopes the mind's rule to thought, speech and deed while the beinoni's heart still wants. The guide should aim every operation at the next thought, word or act and tell the person the feeling may stay.
3. **Merirus and atzvus are the same split as reflection and rumination, and guilt and shame.** The Tanya's test (living, directed, bounded, followed by joy, versus stone-dull with no life) is a usable clinical discrimination. It gives the guide a gate: accounting and bitterness only with living sadness; with objectless heaviness, one small act first (behavioral activation, the best-supported move in the map). The Iggeret HaKodesh 11 line on sadness is for the guide's understanding and must never be said to a sad person.
4. **Faith is above reason in Chassidus and below it in psychology.** Dual-process models file the intuitive, felt layer under the rational. Chassidus puts emunah above da'at. Both agree there is a gap between knowing and feeling; they disagree on which is higher. The guide should use da'at moves to let what is already known be felt, never argue faith, never test faith by feeling.
5. **The transformation loop's order matches the best model of lasting change, which is still unsettled in humans.** Reconsolidation accounts (Lane et al., 2015) say change lasts when an old emotional learning is first reactivated and then met by a vivid mismatch while active: locate the starting perception, then the distinction, then the experience. The human evidence is contested (Chalkia et al., 2020), so the guide should keep the order but claim nothing about permanence, and should repeat the loop on the same perception over time.
6. **Trust helps when it includes the act.** Trust in G-d goes with less worry in Jewish samples (Rosmarin et al., 2009, 2011), but 'leave it all to G-d' does worse than a collaborative style (Pargament et al., 1988). The Rebbe's own definition (trust is toil in the soul) and the hishtadlus-bitachon pairing make the guide's rule simple: name your part, do it, hand over the rest, promise nothing.
7. **The relationship is the most robust mechanism in the map.** The alliance predicts change across all approaches (Flückiger et al., 2018; r about .28) and social connection predicts survival (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010). Rav Joey Rosenfeld's first move (understand before anything) is the practical form. Understanding before teaching is not style; it is the mechanism most likely to carry the rest.
