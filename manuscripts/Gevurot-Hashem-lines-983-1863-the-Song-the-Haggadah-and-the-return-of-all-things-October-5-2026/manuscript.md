The last part of the book. Chapter 47 reads the Song at the Sea. Chapters 48-50 are the laws of the seder night. From chapter 51 the Maharal reads the Haggadah line by line, then Hallel. Chapters 66-72 step back and ask what the whole Exodus was *for*. The answer: so that G-d would dwell below, and so that all things would return to their Cause. The book closes with a short summary of the Pesach laws and a sharp halakhic tract against drinking non-Jewish wine.

This range holds the Maharal's strongest sentences on the unity of G-d, the most acosmic in the whole book (acosmic: the world has no being of its own before Him). It also holds his teaching that song is the longing of the effect for its Cause, and his teaching that G-d's greatness is shown most in His care for the lowly.

## 2.1 Three foundations, and the middle one is unity

At the sea "בזה נראה כי הוא יתברך בידו הכל, ואין חוץ ממנו" (994, 47:12): "by this it is seen that all is in His hand, and nothing is outside Him." The three festivals stand for three foundations. The second is

- "אמונת מציאות השם יתברך שהוא הכל, ואין דבר חוץ ממנו יתברך" (996, 47:14): "faith in G-d's existence, that He is all and nothing is outside Him." - "ומאחר שהוא הכל, שהרי ברשותו הכל, אין עוד שני" (1009, 47:27): "since He is all, for all is in His domain, there is no second."

For the Maharal, believing in G-d *means* believing He is all. There is no "G-d plus world".

## 2.2 The offering: nothing apart from Him

Chapter 69 is the high point of the book on unity. Why does the Torah call an offering "My bread"? Because

- "ואל יעלה על הדעת שהנמצאים הם דבר זולתו" (1741, 69:2): "Let it not enter the mind that beings are something apart from Him."

If they were apart, He would lack them. But He lacks nothing. So the offering is the effect returning to its Cause. And it adds nothing, like water poured into the sea:

- "משל אדם אחד נטל מים והביאם לים" (1742, 69:3): "A parable: a man took water and brought it to the sea." The sea is the root of all water; the cupful adds nothing. Yet it is still called "bringing".

Then the strongest sentence in the book:

- "באמתת מציאותו, הכל שב אליו, ואין כאן בריאה כלל. וזהו שלימותו יתברך, שאין מציאות זולתו" (1743, 69:4): "In the truth of His existence all returns to Him, and there is no creation here at all. This is His perfection: that there is no existence besides Him."

This is as far as the Tanya's Gate of Unity goes (Shaar HaYichud 3-4: from His side the world is as nothing). The Maharal gets there by the logic of cause and effect: an effect has no standing of its own, "אין הנמצאים בצד עצמם יש להם קיום כלל, רק שהם עומדים בו יתברך" (1762, 69:23): "beings on their own have no standing at all; they stand only in Him." And from our side: "כי מצד עצמינו ההעדר והחושך, רק כי הקיום לנו בו יתברך" (1831): "from our own side is absence and darkness; our standing is only in Him."

## 2.3 From the One come opposites

Chapter 60 answers the old rule "from the One can come only one":

- "כי מפני שהוא אחד הוא הכל, שהרי אין בלתו, ולפיכך מאתו באו ההפכים" (1446, 60:13): "Because He is One, He is all, for there is none besides Him; and so opposites come from Him."

And the summary of laws: "מי שהוא אחד גמור יש בו כח על שני הפכים, מפני שהוא כולל הכל" (1833): "one who is wholly One has power over two opposites, because He includes all." This is why the Pesach lamb is eaten with matza (freedom) and maror (bitterness) together. It is also why "והחילוק הוא מצד החומר" (1441): "division comes from the side of matter", not from Him. When one limb of a lamb is struck, the whole animal feels it: the living level is undivided.

## 2.4 "The Place of the world"

- "והוא יתברך מקומו של עולם, מפני שהוא כולל הכל, ואין נמצא יוצא ממנו" (1272, 53:8): "He is the place of the world, because He includes all, and no being goes out from Him."

This matches the Tanya's use of the same midrash (the world is in Him, not He in the world). The Maharal adds that He exceeds the world without limit.

## 2.5 His greatness is His humility

Chapter 67 is the most beautiful teaching in the range. The verse says He is "G-d of gods", then "He does justice for the orphan and widow and loves the stranger". Which is higher? The second:

- "רוממות השם יתברך כאשר הוא מצורף אל השפלים" (1704, 67:2): "G-d's exaltedness is when He is joined to the lowly." - "הכל אצלו, גדול וקטן, בשוה, ואל הכל הוא פונה" (1705): "to Him all, great and small, are equal, and He turns to all." - "אך כי הוא יתברך נבדל מכל הנמצאים, ואין לו יחוס אל אחר, בשביל כך פונה אל דברים שפלים" (1707, 67:5): "Because He is separate from all beings and has no relation to another, He turns to lowly things."

Here the Maharal makes his deepest point about unity. A being that is close to the angels would stay with the angels. Only One who is not a thing among things can be equally near to the orphan and to the seraph. His separateness is His nearness to the least. For the guide this is gold: the smaller you feel, the less distance there is.

## 2.6 Why He dwells below

"החבור האמיתי שיש לסבה הראשונה, שהוא עלה באמת, הוא בתחתונים" (1677, 66:3): "The true bond of the First Cause, the true Cause, is with the lower beings." Higher beings are also causes of others; only the lowest are purely effects. So the purest bond of Cause and effect is here below. This is the Maharal's form of Chabad's "dwelling place below" (דירה בתחתונים, S82 The home below), reached by a different road: not His *desire* for a home below, but the logic of Cause and effect.

## 2.7 He is present as the receiver can hold

"הוא יתברך נמצא לנמצאים כפי המקבלים" (1767, 70:4): "He is present to beings according to the receivers." Place, time, Sinai, the Temple: all are "from the side of the receiver". This is his steady rule, and it answers why the One who is everywhere is felt only somewhere.

## 4.1 The heart matters more than the words

Why is there no blessing over telling the Exodus story? "כיון דעיקר הדבר הוא מחשבת הלב" (1538, 62:25): "since the main thing is the thought of the heart". You have to understand what you say. And on the night itself: "ויחשוב בלבו כאילו הוא יצא ממצרים" (1674): "let him think in his heart as if he left Egypt", and do everything "in great joy". This is the guide's `picturing` and `heart_after`, given a clear source.

## 4.2 From the sensed to the known

"והמצות הם כמו סולם לעלות בהן אל הידיעה השלימה" (1237, 52:7): "the mitzvot are like a ladder to climb to full knowing". Knowing grows from what is sensed to what is understood. For the guide (ours): start with a thing you can touch, then climb. Fits `mashal` and `walk_down` run upward.

## 4.3 Water to the sea (proposed technique, ours)

From 1741-1743: **`mh_water_to_sea`.** Picture carrying a cup of water to the ocean and pouring it in. It adds nothing, yet it is an act of return. Then: "my day, my breath, my work, poured back. Nothing is apart from Him." This joins `mashal` and `binding`. It is a good close to a sitting.

## 4.4 The fifteen steps

Dayenu has fifteen steps, like the fifteen steps of the Temple and the fifteen days of the waxing moon (1421-1425). 15 is the Name י"ה. Three groups of five: leaving lowness, rising above the world, full clinging. A structure for a slow ascent (ours): `length` in fifteen stops.

## 4.5 Thanks as clinging

Say thanks not as payment but as handing yourself over (1619-1621). **Proposed technique `mh_hodaah` (ours):** name one good thing; then say "I am Yours in this". The Maharal says each being gives thanks "by its level" (1621), so a small thanks is still real.

## 4.6 Answer each one by his measure

"להשיב לכל אחד כפי ענין שהוא שואל" (1283, 53:19): "to answer each one according to what he asks". This is a rule for the guide's voice itself, from the four sons.

## 4.7 Return to the beginning

"ואין דבר מסלק החטא רק ההתחלה, כי ההתחלה היא נבדלת מן החטא" (1782, 70:19): "nothing removes sin but the beginning, for the beginning is separate from sin." Teshuvah (return) was made before the world. It is a return to a simple point where no sin is. **Proposed technique `mh_back_to_beginning` (ours):** in return, do not argue with each fault; go back to the simple point before the story, where "I" was first given. Fits `depth` and C7.

## 5. The big human questions

**Purpose.** To dwell below and to return all things to their Cause (1677, 1741-1743). Man is to give thanks, which is handing himself over (1621), and to do it "by his own act" (1763).

**Suffering.** "השבח שקודם לו גנות יותר שבח" (1240): "praise that is preceded by shame is greater praise". Night comes before day; the world begins in lowness. Bitterness comes "for a good end": "הוא מביא אותו לתכלית הטוב" (1458). Exile and the evil inclination have no being of their own: "וכן הגלות, אין מציאות לו בעצמו" (1306). His hidden face is felt as absence: "כל הנמצאים קיומם בו יתברך, וכאשר הוא מסתיר פניו מהם אז יקבלו העדר" (1347): "all beings stand in Him, and when He hides His face from them they receive absence". (Ours: this is a teaching on meaning, never a verdict on why a particular person suffers.)

**Death.** The righteous accept natural death because "והצדיקים במה שהם צדיקים חפצים באמת" (1613): "the righteous, as righteous, want truth", and do not ask Him to break the order of the world for them.

**The soul.** "כל מדריגה העליונה יש בה פשיטות" (1219): "every higher level has simplicity in it". The High Priest wore gold all year, but plain white inside the Holy of Holies. Going inward means becoming simpler. Freedom is simplicity: "אין ענין הגאולה רק שיוצא, ואין לו שום צירוף אל זולתו" (1215): "redemption means only that he goes out and has no bond to anything else" (no master but Him).

**Providence.** Every rescue and every birth goes through Him alone, "שהוא בלבד בפעל הגמור" (1248): "for He alone is in full act". He redeemed all generations at once, "כפי מדריגתו של השם יתברך, שהוא יכלול כל הדורות" (1256). The good that reaches each one reaches him on purpose: "נתן השם יתברך הטוב לו בעצם" (1645).

**Trust.** Sustenance is higher than redemption. "כל הנמצאים מצד עצמם... חסרים" (1658): all beings on their own are lacking, and "כל הנמצאים כולם מקבלים החיות מן השם יתברך תחלה, בלי אמצעי" (1666): "all beings receive life from G-d first, with no go-between", like limbs from the heart. At the level from which the world was made "אין צרות" (1623): there is no narrowness. At the Temple "היו עומדים צפופים ומשתחוים רווחים" (1797): "they stood crowded and bowed with room". Bowing makes room.

**Joy.** The whole night in joy (1674). Every creature sings because "ובכל נמצא יש בו מן כבודו יתברך" (1728): "in every being there is some of His glory".

**Love and awe.** "מה הוא חנון ורחום, אף אתה" (1043): "as He is gracious and merciful, so be you". Beauty for Him is likeness to Him. Awe from His separateness (1053: "for two that are partners" feel no awe of each other).

**Trials.** "לפעמים מראה האדם עצמו צדיק, ואינו באמת צדיק" (1399): "sometimes a person shows himself righteous and is not truly righteous". Trials show the true self.

**Loss of the holy.** The divine is lost easily, not because it is weak but through "הסלוק מן המקבל" (1810): "removal from the receiver", like forgotten learning. But the readiness is never lost (1812-1813). A strong comfort for one who feels they have lost what they once had.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **Agree fully:** "no creation here at all... no existence besides Him" (1743) is the Gate of Unity's teaching. "He is the Place of the world" (1272). Dwelling below (1677). Life to all things from Him with no go-between (1666).
- **Different road:** The Maharal argues from cause and effect and from "simple / separate / including". He never uses tzimtzum or the letters of speech. For him the world's nothingness is the nothingness of an effect before its Cause.
- **Opposites:** From the One come opposites *because* He is all (1446). The Tanya would say the same through "the hidden good".
- **Humility of G-d:** 1704-1707 is richer than anything in the Tanya on this point. It fits Chabad's "He is found in the lowly" but grounds it in His separateness.
- **Particularism:** The Maharal's Israel/nations framing is strong in chapters 67-68 and in the wine laws (1842-1863). The guide (D28) keeps the human truths inside it (dignity is essential, the core is untouched, readiness is never lost) and does not carry over the comparison of peoples.
