*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A collection of long, systematic maamarim on the Genesis portions from Toldos to Vayechi. Its subjects are the wells Yitzchak dug, Leah and Rachel, Yaakov and Esav, Yosef's sheaves, the cupbearer's grapes, and Yehudah's vine. Each is explained in Kabbalistic terms: chesed and gevurah, tohu and tikkun, Ma and Ban, the raising and refining of fallen sparks. Woven into this, often in parentheses, are sharp observations about the service of the heart: how joy, bitterness, contemplation, prayer and repentance work, and how they go wrong.

**How much of it is medicine.** some. Most of the range is Kabbalistic exegesis, but a dense cluster of avodah psychology (especially 1612-1621, 1646, 1739-1761) gives real diagnosis and method for joy, bitterness, a numb heart and prayer.

**Its model of the person.** A person has a divine soul and a natural, animal soul. The mind contemplates, and the heart is moved by it. That feeling can stay in the outer heart or reach the innermost point of the heart. The deepest layer is will and delight, above reason. Any feeling that comes from contemplation depends on it and fades when attention moves on. What does not fade is a bond made from the point of will, and the hidden love every Jew is born with. Each faculty is a vessel with limits: too much, or too constant, turns delight into weariness. Change comes by breaking a coarse covering on the heart through humbling and bitterness, which lets the hidden love flow out and settle into vessels through Torah.

**Its philosophy.** Everything flows from a divine delight, and its overflow falls down as waste: empty laughter, pointless worry, stray thoughts. Even that waste can be refined and raised. Light that comes out of darkness, through a repentance that turns dark to light, reaches higher than orderly righteousness. So the low and bitter states hide a love that belongs to the soul's essence, and the task is to bring it out, not to create it.

**What it adds.** Precise inner diagnostics. Joy larger than its cause is really the heart opening up, and it empties into frivolity. Constant delight becomes pain, so learning needs rhythm. A prayer arousal that reaches only the outer heart explains the pious person who cheats without feeling any contradiction. Two wines: 'intoxicating' bitterness for someone who cannot yet reach joy. Holy bitterness is love in disguise, sharply separated from despair that falls into lust and anger. Worry without substance is waste from an empty soul.

## I can't stop worrying, even about things that aren't really threatening me

*Its own term:* פחד ודאגה שאין בו שום ממשות; ריקנות הנפש

Fear and worry keep rising in the mind, attached to no real harm to body or money. When grief or fear fills thought without a break, the mind gets confused. Sometimes it ends in tears.

**What is really going on.** The work says some pain 'has no substance': worry that comes from emptiness of soul (reikanus ha-nefesh), like rot inside a seed. It is waste from the overflow, not information about danger. Tears let out the waste of sadness and worry.

**Reframe.** From *"Something bad is coming; I have to keep thinking it through."* to **Much of this fear has no substance, neither to body nor money. It is the soul's emptiness speaking, not a real threat.** Once worry is seen as waste thrown off by an empty soul rather than a signal of danger, it loses its claim on attention. The task shifts from solving the worry to filling the soul. `TC2·Q31` · `TC2·Q04`

**Reframe.** From *"Crying means I'm falling apart."* to **Tears carry the waste of sadness and worry out of heart and brain, and sweeten them.** The work treats tears as releasing the 'harsh severities'. Weeping becomes part of healing, not a sign of weakness. `TC2·Q07`

## I get high and giddy in my spiritual joy, then crash into silliness and emptiness

*Its own term:* הוללות וליצנות; שמחה יותר מכפי המדה

A burst of great feeling in prayer or learning spills into laughter and joking. When the love drains away, all that is left is an open heart that chatters frivolities and feels far from G-d.

**What is really going on.** The joy was bigger than the contemplation behind it. It came from the heart opening up, not from the substance of divine joy. Frivolity's pleasure grows from the soul's emptiness, and the work calls this 'the main fall in a person'.

**Program: Temper runaway joy with awe.** (1) Notice when your joy has grown bigger than the contemplation that produced it. (2) Recognize it as the heart opening up, not more divine joy. (3) Hold yourself back from laughter and excess glee by calling up awe: standing before the King. (4) Keep away from the setting of mockery and frivolous talk, which the text calls the source of loosening all restraint. (5) If you have already slid into frivolity, return to love before the heart fills entirely with it. *When:* In the moment, during or right after an intense prayer or learning experience. *For:* Someone whose spiritual highs regularly spill into silliness and then emptiness. *Not for:* Someone depressed or without joy, for whom any joy should be protected, not held back. `TC2·Q01` · `TC2·Q02` · `TC2·Q03`

**Reframe.** From *"The higher I feel, the better my service."* to **Joy bigger than what caused it is the heart opening up, not substance, and it drains into frivolity.** Measuring joy by the contemplation behind it, not by intensity, keeps the love from emptying out and the fall that follows. `TC2·Q02` · `TC2·Q01`

## My prayer really moves me, and an hour later I act like someone else

He is moved sincerely in prayer. Afterwards he cuts corners or does wrong in business, and feels no contradiction with his faith. He may even ask G-d to help with it.

**What is really going on.** The arousal was real for that hour, but it reached only the outer heart, not its innermost point. A love born from contemplation depends on the contemplation, so it vanishes when his mind turns to worldly matters.

**Program: Prayer from the inner point of the heart.** (1) Notice that your arousal in prayer fades and turns into its opposite during the day. (2) Recognize that it touched only the outer heart and depended on the contemplation. (3) Seek return from the inner point of the heart, above reason: a nullifying of your will to G-d alone. (4) Bind yourself from the point of will, so the love is not dependent on anything and does not lapse when your mind is busy. *When:* In daily prayer, checked afterwards by how you behave during the day. *For:* A sincere person who is troubled by the gap between his prayer and his conduct. *Not for:* A method for depth, not a license to dismiss your sincere moments as fake. `TC2·Q08` · `TC2·Q09` · `TC2·Q10` · `TC2·Q11`

**Reframe.** From *"I was sincere in prayer, so I'm fine; or else I'm a hypocrite."* to **The feeling was true for that hour but reached only the outer heart. The goal is the inner point, where love does not depend on anything.** It explains the split without self-condemnation, and points to a deeper layer (will, above reason) where change lasts. `TC2·Q08` · `TC2·Q09` · `TC2·Q10`

## My heart feels like stone; nothing moves me, not even regret

He cannot be stirred by love of G-d, and he cannot feel the broken heart of repentance either, even though he is not aware of doing anything seriously wrong.

**What is really going on.** A thick covering, the 'foreskin of the heart', forms over its inner point. It grows even without sin, from craving surplus worldly pleasure only for oneself, including permitted pleasures.

**Program: Circumcising the heart: treading out the grapes.** (1) Name the covering: craving surplus pleasure for yourself, including permitted pleasures. (2) Sanctify yourself in what is permitted, so the heart is not drawn to it so strongly. (3) Let the heart break over its distance from G-d, and humble it. (4) Humble yourself; it is not enough to wait for others or events to humble you. (5) Aim for 'let my soul be as dust to all'. Then contentment and the hidden love come out like wine from trodden grapes. *When:* An ongoing stance, deepened in the bitterness before prayer. *For:* A basically stable person who feels numb or entitled, and whose hardness comes from comfort and self-focus. *Not for:* Anyone already crushed, shamed, depressed or abused. For them 'lowering oneself to dust' can deepen the harm. `TC2·Q14` · `TC2·Q15` · `TC2·Q16` · `TC2·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"I haven't sinned, so why do I feel nothing?"* to **Even without sin, a self-directed appetite for surplus pleasure forms a covering over the heart.** It moves the cause of numbness from guilt to habits he can change, and so gives him something to work on. `TC2·Q15` · `TC2·Q14`

## I can't find any joy in prayer; stray thoughts keep flooding in

He sometimes feels love or awe, but never reaches divine joy in contemplation, and keeps falling into foreign thoughts while praying.

**What is really going on.** Divine joy belongs to the 'upright of heart'. Someone whose soul was damaged and who never did repentance with a broken heart cannot yet reach it. The stray thoughts are the sign. The day's worldly thoughts still cling to the soul.

**Program: The 'intoxicating wine' at bedtime Shema.** (1) After contemplating G-d's greatness, set your heart on how far you have drifted. (2) Let the bitterness grow until it makes you forget your attachment to the day's worldly pursuits, like wine that drowns the mind. (3) Make a clear, full accounting of your deeds, thoughts, words and desires that were not for G-d. (4) Read your state honestly: a heart hard as stone, a heart burning with forbidden desire. (5) Say the confession and accept the four deaths in your soul, as if standing before the King's gate. (6) The aim is to clear the ground so that the joyful wine of contemplation can follow. *When:* Nightly, at the Shema recited before sleep. *For:* Someone who cannot reach joy in contemplation because unresolved wrongdoing and worldly attachment block it. *Not for:* Depressed, scrupulous or OCD-prone people, or anyone at risk of self-harm. Imagining a death sentence and a harsh self-audit can do real damage there. `TC2·Q18` · `TC2·Q19` · `TC2·Q20` · `TC2·Q17`

**Program: Stripping the day's clutter before prayer.** (1) Before Shema and prayer, look into your heart as if this were the day you leave the world. (2) Strip off the worldly thoughts that have clung to your soul, even their memory and impressions. (3) Then ascend in love to delight in G-d. *When:* Daily, before Shema and prayer. *For:* Someone whose prayer is crowded by the day's business. *Not for:* Anyone with suicidal thoughts, health anxiety or morbid rumination. For them, 'as if departing the world' should not be used. `TC2·Q21`

**Reframe.** From *"Seeing my failures proves I'm hopeless."* to **Bitterness over distance is really love and joy in disguise. Despair that leads to lust and anger is its opposite.** Read as love, bitterness leads to a plea for mercy instead of collapse. This separates holy bitterness from despair. `TC2·Q24` · `TC2·Q22` · `TC2·Q23`

## When I look at my failures I sink into despair, and then I act out even worse

Looking at himself makes him bitter, then hopeless, and he falls into hot craving, anger and self-importance.

**What is really going on.** This is the bitterness of unripe grapes, the bitterness of the husk. Holy bitterness is the opposite: grief over distance that leads to submission and a plea for mercy. Holy bitterness is really love and joy wearing the clothes of their opposite.

**Program: Holy bitterness before prayer (heaviness of head).** (1) Before praying, grieve your past distance and G-d's hiddenness in your soul. Make it grief about distance, not self-contempt. (2) Let it bring you to submission and lowliness, even to tears asking for mercy, not to despair. (3) Turn it into a request from the depth of the heart ('Baruch Ata') that G-dliness shine in your soul. (4) Hold on to the fact that your good will to come close joins with deed: the distant becomes near. (5) Keep the body's sadness separate from the soul's joy. (6) Aim to hold joy and bitterness in one movement. *When:* Before daily prayer, briefly; the text ties how much light the prayer draws down to the depth of this bitterness. *For:* Someone who can feel remorse and whose remorse slides into despair or acting out. *Not for:* Clinically depressed people, or anyone whose 'bitterness' is already self-hatred. It needs a guide who can tell the two apart. `TC2·Q22` · `TC2·Q23` · `TC2·Q24` · `TC2·Q25` · `TC2·Q28` · `TC2·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"Seeing my failures proves I'm hopeless."* to **Bitterness over distance is really love and joy in disguise. Despair that leads to lust and anger is its opposite.** Read as love, bitterness leads to a plea for mercy instead of collapse. This separates holy bitterness from despair. `TC2·Q24` · `TC2·Q22` · `TC2·Q23`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm still far away, so my wanting to change counts for nothing."* to **Good thought and good will join with deed; the distant becomes near.** Counting the will itself as a first step toward closeness keeps a distant person engaged. `TC2·Q25`

## Nothing is ever enough; I always feel I deserve more

He pursues every wish and cannot settle for little or be happy with his lot. When hardship comes he feels wronged, as if it were out of proportion.

**What is really going on.** All bad desires grow from coarseness of spirit: seeing oneself as a someone, a great thing, who is owed everything. Only when the spirit is lowered is he happy with his lot.

**Program: Circumcising the heart: treading out the grapes.** (1) Name the covering: craving surplus pleasure for yourself, including permitted pleasures. (2) Sanctify yourself in what is permitted, so the heart is not drawn to it so strongly. (3) Let the heart break over its distance from G-d, and humble it. (4) Humble yourself; it is not enough to wait for others or events to humble you. (5) Aim for 'let my soul be as dust to all'. Then contentment and the hidden love come out like wine from trodden grapes. *When:* An ongoing stance, deepened in the bitterness before prayer. *For:* A basically stable person who feels numb or entitled, and whose hardness comes from comfort and self-focus. *Not for:* Anyone already crushed, shamed, depressed or abused. For them 'lowering oneself to dust' can deepen the harm. `TC2·Q14` · `TC2·Q15` · `TC2·Q16` · `TC2·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm suffering because I'm being punished, and it's out of all proportion."* to **The work says suffering is not measured punishment; its main aim is to humble coarseness so the soul's light shines.** Replacing punishment with purpose lets the humbling be accepted without resentment. Offer it only to someone who chooses this frame. `TC2·Q27`

**Reframe.** From *"I deserve it; I need a lot."* to **The sense of being owed comes from feeling oneself a 'something'. As that softens, contentment comes.** By going after the root (self-importance) and not each desire, the desires fall away together. `TC2·Q26`

## I learn and contemplate, but it leaves me dry, or it has become a chore

*Its own term:* לומד בקרירות ועצלות; תענוג תמידי אינו תענוג

Either he thinks about ideas beyond his grasp and feels nothing, and comes to loathe it; or he learns coldly, to meet a daily quota. Constant receiving makes him weary.

**What is really going on.** Contemplation beyond one's grasp gives no felt light. Constant intake wears out the vessel until delight becomes pain. Nothing is reached without binding oneself to it from the point of will.

**Program: Rhythmic, measured learning and contemplation.** (1) Fix set times, morning and evening, rather than constant intake. (2) At each session prepare your vessel, take in according to your measure, then stop. (3) Contemplate at a level you can grasp 'with good taste' until it is felt in the heart, not ideas beyond your grasp. (4) Bind yourself to the subject from the point of your will, with a strong wanting to understand. (5) Learn for the delight it gives Above, not to meet a quota. *When:* Twice daily at fixed times; stopping between sessions is part of the method. *For:* Someone whose learning or contemplation has gone dry, cold or wearying. *Not for:* Not a cure for clinical anhedonia or depression. Those need care beyond the method. `TC2·Q05` · `TC2·Q06` · `TC2·Q11` · `TC2·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"If I can't feel deep ideas, I'm just dull."* to **Contemplation beyond one's grasp gives no felt life, and constant intake turns delight into weariness. Measure and rhythm restore it.** Dryness becomes a problem of vessel and timing, not a lack of soul, and so it can be fixed. `TC2·Q06` · `TC2·Q05`

## After so long, I feel I have no love for G-d left in me

Years of distraction and exile have left him feeling that his inner fire is simply gone.

**What is really going on.** The hidden love is rooted like nature in the point of will, above reason. It cannot be put out; it is only hidden. It comes out at times by itself, with no preparation, and only needs to be brought from concealment into the open.

**Reframe.** From *"My love for G-d is gone."* to **A hidden love is rooted like nature in the will. It cannot be put out, and it surfaces at times on its own; it only needs to be revealed.** Nothing has to be manufactured; the love is uncovered. That removes hopelessness and gives a reason to keep watch for it. `TC2·Q30`

## Cautions

- The deepest 'broken heart' (1588) is compared to natural black bile: loathing one's life with no felt emotion. Never offer it as an ideal to someone depressed. - The bedtime accounting, with accepting 'four deaths' and seeing one's heart as stone, can feed scrupulosity, OCD or self-punishment; it needs a guide and the goal of joy. - 'Suffering comes to humble your coarseness' can read as blaming the sufferer. Never tell someone in pain that it is for their arrogance. - 'Look at your heart as if you leave the world today' can trigger anyone with suicidal thoughts, health anxiety or morbid rumination. - The 'worry with no substance' reframe must not be used to dismiss real dangers, trauma or a clinical anxiety disorder. - Treating stray thoughts in prayer as proof of unrepented damage can shame people with intrusive thoughts. - Holding back joy with awe fits someone who is overflowing. For an anhedonic or depressed person, protect whatever joy there is. - The hidden love is framed as belonging to every Jewish soul; offering it to a universal audience means adapting it honestly, without overclaiming.
