## The shape of the range

The book names its own subject in its first line: "the unity of Godliness in every single detail" of the chain of worlds (line 1). The rest of the range keeps to that. It has three parts.

- **Chapters 1-6 (8-286) are about how to know.** They set out what contemplation is (Binah, Tevunah, Da'at). They say it must go detail by detail and be joined to the whole. They say it must produce two opposite feelings together, joy and bitul. Most lines are *structural* definitions of the mind. F12 leads here.
- **Chapters 7-9 (287-481) give the map.** One chain runs from Malchut of Ein Sof to Malchut of Asiyah, and its end is fixed in its beginning. Shema and Baruch Shem are read against this map. The tzimtzum answers the question "I have not changed".
- **Chapters 10-15 (482-898) go before the map.** They look at the Essence before the tzimtzum (Yachid, Echad, Kadmon), then the tzimtzum, the reshimu (the trace) and the kav (the line). F05 and F03 dominate here, which is why they lead the count.

The range differs from the Tanya range in one clear way. F10, F11, F07 and F06 are nearly absent. This range is about the Essence and the tzimtzum, and about knowing them. It is not about the mitzvah or the animal soul. The many structural lines are mostly analogies from the soul (the power of motion, the teacher, the craftsman's sketch, the thrown stone). The text uses these for G06, explaining by analogy from the soul. They are tagged by what they illustrate, not as F06. See "Where the tie is thin".

## Introduction (1-7): the book names its subject

> היחוד אלקות בכל פרט ופרט בסדר ההשתלשלות (line 1)

This is the SPINE's warrant from the Mitteler Rebbe, and it sits at line 1. Line 2 adds that the knowing must be "clothed and grasped in mind and heart", from the first tzimtzum to the end of Asiyah. Line 3 names the work Kuntres HaHitbonenut, the tract on contemplation. Lines 4-7 (apology, blessing, love for seekers, signature) are *none*.

## Ch. 1-2 (8-89): what contemplation is

These chapters are a psychology of the mind, and nearly every line is *structural*. Contemplation (hitbonenut, "with the doubled nun", line 27) is a long, fixed gaze into a matter until one knows its depth (line 10). Every idea has depth, length and breadth (line 16). Its depth flows "from Chochmah, which is called ayin" (line 22). So the mind has its own yesh (Binah) and ayin (Chochmah), and between them there is no comparison:

The text does not yet apply this to creation. It is the ground on which chapter 5 later builds. Da'at is defined as "binding one's feeling to the idea" (line 46), the same definition as Tanya ch. 42. Chapter 2 separates Binah from Tevunah. Tevunah is the power that brings an understanding down into a separate matter: a ruling, or "an arousal in the heart" (line 69). Line 22.2, the Zohar's flood of wisdom in the days of Moshiach, is the range's one *reading* sub-entry.

## Ch. 3 (90-112): the object is memale

The method is now turned on Godliness. Its first example is memale kol almin, which the text identifies with creation from nothing:

> דממלא כל עלמין שהוא התהוות יש מאין הנקרא כח הפועל האלקי בנפעל (line 91)

That is F01 and F04 joined in one phrase. "Know (vyadata)" is said to refer to this comprehension:

> שהוא בהשגה זו דממלא כל עלמין דוקא (line 104)

Without Tevunah, understanding and prayer stay "two separate things" (line 112). This is F12's warning, that knowledge which does not reach the heart is cut off.

## Ch. 4 (113-160): detail and whole

Should one contemplate in general or in detail? General contemplation reaches toward the Essence, but it can deceive: "from afar God appears to him" (line 124). Detail is what fixes it in the soul. The rule is to go from each detail to the one above it until all are included, "to unite a general unity, not a particular one" (line 139). Then comes a definition of the unity itself:

> שענין היחוד האלקי הוא בחינת עומק ההשגה בביטול היש לאין ביחודא עילאה ויחודא תתאה (line 140)

Here the unity is defined as a depth of comprehension in the nullification of yesh to ayin. It is not defined as a fact about God alone. That is F02, F08 and F12 in one sentence. Line 146 gives the SPINE's "one detail relative to the Essence":

The aim is the Essence, not the attributes: אליו ולא למדותיו, "to Him and not to His attributes" (line 153). Studying a detail "as if for some learning for himself" is ruled out (line 155).

## Ch. 5-6 (161-286): joy and bitul, two lines of one power

Every contemplation of creation holds two things. The first is how the finite yesh comes from ayin, which is grasped and gives joy. The second is the Godly ayin that creates it, which is not grasped and gives bitul (lines 165-175):

> הא' בחינת ביטול היש לאין (line 166)
> והב' ענין בחינת האין האלה״י המהווה אותו (line 168)

The two are in exact proportion. One who claims either without the other is deceiving himself:

> ולפי ערך השמחה כך ערך הביטול ממש (line 199)
> דמיון שווא הוא ואין לו בחינת ביטול כלל (line 202)

Chapter 6 generalizes this as F09 in the soul. Every force has its opposite, drawn from its own strength:

> הרי כח אחד הוא ממש, רק שנחלק לב' קווין שוין ממש ושקולין (line 228)

Weeping and joy come in three levels. The highest weeping is the mind's failure before "the Essence of Or Ein Sof, which is not grasped at all" (line 242). Joy in a contracted light is still rooted in the Essence (line 256). Now joy breaks through by its opposite (line 263), and the future's laughter comes from the weeping of exile (line 266, F11). Tikkun Chatzot is the preparation (lines 267-284), and the reverse of it is casting off the yoke, "the root and source" of all rebellion (line 275). These last lines are practical, but the text itself ties them to the revelation of "essential delight" in prayer (line 268), so they are marked *stated*.

## Ch. 7-8 (287-427): the chain whose end is fixed in its beginning

> ונעוץ תחלתן בסופן וסופן בתחלתן והיו לאחדים ממש (line 294)

The chain runs from Malchut of Ein Sof ("I am first") to Malchut of Malchut of Asiyah ("I am last"). "This is the whole of man": to join every detail to the general unity (line 296). Shema's higher unity is the simple oneness before the first tzimtzum (line 317). Baruch Shem's lower unity is Malchut of Ein Sof after the tzimtzum, down to Asiyah (lines 319-320). This gives the SPINE's F02 a precise location on the map.

Then the text goes past F02. Even the joining of head to end is "one detail" next to the general surrounding circle, and that circle too is "one detail" next to the Essence:

> שלמעלה מהיות גם בחינת כלל לפרט (line 324)

That is yachid, above whole and part, stated in the Mitteler Rebbe's own words. It is the SPINE's opening precision. The practice is to make "the short out of the long", until "in one moment" (line 330) one sees in the lowest physical thing the infinite from the beginning of all:

> שבזה ממש שורה בבחינת אין סוף דוקא מבחינת תחלת הכל (line 332)

That is G05 stated. It is also why the Psalms praise God through physical heaven and earth and not through the upper worlds (lines 333-335). By count, head and end are very far apart. In truth they are "very close, with no break or concealment in between" (line 337). Chapter 8 repeats the pattern for Chochmah, the middot, thought, speech and action. Wisdom reached Shlomo in Asiyah from "the Chochmah in the Essence" (line 372). The Holy of Holies below held the Essence in physical tablets and ark (line 377, F07 with F10). The chapter ends:

> אבל הכל רק רצון ושכל ומדה ומחשבה ומעשה אחד תחשב לגבי בחינת העצמות ממש (line 402)
> איך שלא נבדל גם הפרט היותר אחרון מהמקור הכללי היותר ראשון (line 403)

## Ch. 9 (428-481): "I have not changed", and the tzimtzum as the answer

The question is this. If Malchut of Asiyah changes between judgment and kindness, and "He renews the work of creation each day", then there is newness in the Essence (line 438). The answer:

> והתירוץ לכל זה הוא ענין הצמצום (line 444)
> על כן אין בעצמות אלהו״ת שום שינוי כלל (line 446)

Change exists after the tzimtzum, "from the side of the receivers only" (line 448). So the tzimtzum is "the main point of the Godly unity" (line 452), and everyone must know it (line 451). "Before Him darkness is as light" is said of sovev (lines 453-454), but memale too is unchanged: "its upper and lower are equal" (line 457). Prayer, "may it be Your will", is placed exactly: it asks for change in the drawing-down after the tzimtzum, not in Him (line 481, F10).

## Ch. 10-11 (482-643): Yachid, Echad, Kadmon

The first thing to contemplate is the source before the tzimtzum, "there too in detail" (line 489). It has three levels:

> בחינת יחיד משמעו לבדו ממש (line 493)
> והוא למעלה מהתאחדות נפרדים שנקרא אחד (line 494)

The SPINE takes its yachid/echad distinction from the Rebbe and the Rayatz, and here it is stated a century earlier. The analogy is the soul (lines 514-572). A trait held in the soul's essence is so one with it that one "cannot even say they are united" (line 529). The same trait, ready to come out, can be called one (line 556). Once it is measured out for a particular deed, it is Kadmon. Above, the same holds: Yachid is the Essence (line 587), Echad is "simple oneness" (line 599), and Kadmon is what He "estimated in Himself in potential" (line 506, line 614). Why is there a chain at all?

The cause is the Essence's own desire for kindness. That is the range's nearest approach to F07's "why": it is a reason in the Essence, not yet a dwelling below. Line 629 reads "Blessed is He who spoke and the world came to be" in these levels.

## Ch. 12-13 (644-782): the tzimtzum is not literal

> כי זהו עיקר היחוד האמיתי בעצמות אור אין סוף (line 646)

The aim is self-sacrifice "to the Essence of Or Ein Sof alone" (line 648). The parables (the teacher who states a law briefly, the soul in the body, the craftsman's sketch, the sign, the thrown stone, the hint) all come to one point:

> ולא שנסתלק ונתעלם לגמרי (line 662; again at line 707)
> כך גם אחר הצמצום הרי ישנו לאור העצמיות כמו שהוא בבחינת אין סוף ממש, רק שלא נגלה למטה לגבי שפע המקבלים (line 710)

This is F03 on the Alter Rebbe's side of the SPINE's kept dispute. The hiding is only toward the receivers ("first darkness... relative to below", line 713), and what later "reveals deep things out of darkness" is the Essence's own light (line 717). The reshimu is the Essence's power to limit:

> כי הוא יתברך כל יכול, וכלול בו גם כן מכח המגביל (line 736)

This is the seed of F05's "as He has infinite power, so He has finite power". The text marks where its own analogy fails: אך הנה באמת אין זה דמיון אמיתי, "this is not a true likeness" (line 738). The reshimu "shows that all withdrew" (line 739), which is harder than the brief-law parable allows. Still, the trace "is what enables all to come into revelation" (line 760), and in it the point holds concealment and revelation, "two opposites" (line 774). The chapter ends on F12: "this is the whole of man, to deepen his da'at in all this" (line 781), so as to bind his soul to the Essence (line 782).

## Ch. 14-15 (783-898): the point and the line

The point and the line seem opposite, yet "in truth the point and the kav are not two opposites" (line 795). The point is hyle, above spreading, so "the whole essence is included in it" (line 808). The kav carries "a likeness of the Essence's light" (line 832), measured to the receivers (line 831). Chapter 15 holds two opposite powers in the one kav: to include left in right, and to measure. "Both are true... each depends on the other" (lines 856-857):

> ובכח האור העצמות דאין סוף ודאי יש לעשות בחינת התכללות ויחודים של כל ההפכים (line 860)
> אנת הוא דמייחד לון ומקשר לון (line 861)

That is F09 stated through the Tikkunei Zohar, the same text Tanya SH 7 cites. Every renewal of light, even the change from Temple to exile (line 875), comes "from the Essence of Or Ein Sof before the tzimtzum" (line 871). So "may it be Your will" is addressed "before Your Essence itself" (line 877). Even the power of limit comes "from the Essence alone" (line 887). The two infinite lines, upward and downward, are equal (line 892).

## Chapter 16 opening (899-905)

The kav's surrounding and inner lights are introduced. The general surrounding light "surrounds the four worlds equally" (line 901), and the general thought "sees to the end of all generations in one glance" (line 904). The range ends at "there are two kinds of will" (line 905), with the argument unfinished.

## What this range adds that the Tanya does not say (or says differently)

- **Unity as a depth of comprehension.** Tanya SH 1-7 teaches the unity as a truth about God and the world. This book defines "the Godly unity" as "the depth of comprehension in the nullification of yesh to ayin" (line 140). The unity is located in the knower as much as in the known. F12 is not a separate face that follows the doctrine. It is how the doctrine exists.
- **Yachid above Echad, stated.** Lines 493-494, 503 and 587 draw the SPINE's precision directly. The Tanya's SH 7 comes close ("the earth is not even called 'od'"), but does not name yachid over echad.
- **The joy/bitul proportion.** No Tanya passage in the earlier range says that joy in the grasped yesh and bitul before the ungrasped ayin must be exactly equal, or that either alone is "an empty fantasy" (lines 199-202). This is F09 turned into a test for whether contemplation is real.
- **The whole order as "one detail".** The Tanya has no step in which even the general surrounding light is "one detail" relative to the Essence (lines 146, 321-324).
- **Change located in the receivers, by the tzimtzum.** Tanya SH 7 says no change came about in Him by creation, and that this is known only by faith. Here the tzimtzum is argued to be the reason (lines 444-448), and the argument is carried to prayer (lines 481, 877-879).
- **Why there is a chain.** The Essence's desire for kindness (line 577). The Tanya's earlier range gives the purpose, the dwelling below. This range gives the cause in the Essence and says little about the dwelling.

## Where the tie is thin (kept thin)

- Four lines are *none*: the author's apology, blessing, love for seekers, and signature (4-7). - Chapters 1-2 (8-89) are a theory of the mind. They are *structural*, since the later chapters need them, but most carry no face. Tagging them F12 throughout would be forcing. - The soul analogies (lines 349-350, 379-384, 514-572, 594, 626-627, 673-686, 726) explain the Essence by the soul (G06). They are not claims that the soul is part of God, so F06 is left off them. F06 stays on only two lines: the Godly spark in exile (271) and wisdom reaching Shlomo's soul (376). - The kabbalistic mapping (partzufim becoming Keter of the next, screens between worlds, the nun shapes in Binah, lines 86-89, 306-309, 463-471) is *structural*. It is the chain the unity runs through. The tie is stated for the chain as a whole (lines 290, 294, 310), not in each link.
- *Reading* is used once at line level (340: the straight kav as complete inclusion, against the separated circles) and once in a sub-entry (22.2: the messianic flood of wisdom as F12/F07). The book states almost all its own ties, so *reading* is rare here. - F10 and F11 are nearly absent (10 and 4 entries). F10 appears only in prayer ("may it be Your will", lines 442, 479, 481, 876-879), the Holy of Holies (377) and the Zohar's secrets (245). The range does not discuss mitzvot. - Line 22.1 is marked structural although it continues the ayin theme, because at that point the text is still describing a river of ideas.

## Disagreements with the Tanya kept

- **Knowledge or faith on "no change".** Tanya SH 7 (the Tanya range's line 834) says that the lack of change in Him is known "only by faith, which is above comprehension". Here the same point is argued: the tzimtzum is "the answer to all this" (line 444), and "everyone must know the tzimtzum and contemplate it" (line 451). The Mitteler Rebbe does keep a place for what is not grasped: the ayin that creates "is hidden from the mind's eyes" (line 169), and is seen only "by flash". But he puts that limit inside contemplation, as the sight of Chochmah, not as a step outside it into faith. This sharpens the SPINE's F12 dispute (knowledge or faith). The Mitteler Rebbe stands nearer the Rayatz's "to know" than the Tanya's "only to believe".
- **"We have no business with hidden things."** The Tanya's range quotes this twice. This range tells everyone to contemplate the Essence before the tzimtzum "there too in detail" (line 489) and to deepen da'at in the tzimtzum and reshimu (lines 646, 781). It does warn beginners to start general (line 160) and warns against self-deception many times (lines 123, 202, 205). The warning is about doing it falsely, not about the subject being out of bounds.
- **Is the tzimtzum a complete withdrawal?** The Tanya (and this book at line 707) says it is not literal. But the Mitteler Rebbe also says his own best analogy fails, because the reshimu "shows that all withdrew from there" (lines 738-740), and he calls the absence of downward spreading "surely a complete and true contraction" (line 792) before he qualifies it (lines 804, 839). He holds both sides. This is closer to the Rebbe's position in the SPINE (a complete removal of light, not of Essence) than to a simple "only toward the creatures".
- **Who can do this?** The Tanya range makes the love of Chinukh Katan something anyone can be commanded to. This range says the joy and bitul fall away "at a little opposition" for most people (lines 212, 282-283). It says "many raised on Chassidic teaching deceive themselves" (line 205). And it makes weeping in Tikkun Chatzot a precondition (line 279). The bar here is higher, and the book says so openly.
