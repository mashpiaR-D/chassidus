## The short answer

The Maharal is the great thinker of oneness before the Ari's teachings spread. He almost never uses Kabbalistic terms. He argues from the nature of the one and the many, the simple and the made-up, the cause and what it causes. His whole teaching comes down to four sentences.

1. **He is simple, so nothing stands outside Him.** "ומזה בעצמו שהוא בתכלית הפשיטות, אין דבר נבדל ממנו" (Gevurot Hashem line 34): "because He is utterly simple, nothing is separate from Him." "והכל אפס זולתו, והוייתו יתברך נקרא הוייה, וזולתו אפס" (Gevurot Hashem line 38): "all is nothing apart from Him; His being is called being."
2. **The One is not a number.** "אשר האחד אינו מספר, ואינו נכנס בכלל מספר" (Derekh Chayyim line 1297). He is not the first of many. He is the root from which counting comes (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 38).
3. **Because He is One, the world is one.** "שאני אחד, וכך העולם שהוא שלי הוא אחד" (Derekh Chayyim line 180): "for I am One, and so the world that is Mine is one." The many come "from the side of the receiver"; from His side there is only one act (Derekh Chayyim line 614).
4. **A person reaches Him by cleaving, and twoness is the root of sin.** "יצר הרע הוא שניות ליצר טוב, ומביא לידי שניות" (Derashat Shabbat HaGadol line 49). The Shema, the mitzvot, the Torah, humility and peace all join what is split. "וכאשר יקרא קריאת שמע, ויהיה דבק באחדותו יתברך, יהיה מסלק השניות" (same line).

> אין פירוש "אין עוד" כי אין עוד אלהים, אבל פירושו כי אין עוד דבר בעולם, רק השם יתברך (Derashat Shabbat HaGadol line 2)

"'There is none else' does not mean there is no other god. It means there is nothing else in the world but G-d."

> כי אין במציאות רק השם יתברך, ואשר נמצאים מאתו (Derekh Chayyim line 38)

"For there is nothing in being but G-d and what exists from Him."

## His core teaching on the unity

**Simple, so without edge.** What can be defined has an edge, and past its edge is something else. He cannot be defined, so there is no "past the edge" where anything could stand apart (Gevurot Hashem lines 34-38). This is the Maharal's usual road: not "G-d is everywhere," but "G-d has no outside."

**Fully one from every side.** "אבל השם יתברך אחד לגמרי מכל צד" (Be'er HaGolah line 91). And that is the same as necessary being: "השם יתברך, שהוא אחד לגמרי ואין זולתו, הוא מחויב המציאות לגמרי" (Be'er HaGolah line 233). What is one only "in some way" is possible, not necessary. The lack in the world is itself a proof: "וכאשר העלול חסר ולא יושלם, מורה שהפועל הוא אחד" (Be'er HaGolah line 467).

**One in Himself, with no joining.** "Our G-d" is His bond with us. "One" says more: "כי אחדותו בעצמו מבלי צרוף כלל" (Netivot Olam line 444). He is not one by being joined to anything.

**Beings have nothing of their own.** "ואין לנמצאים מצד עצמם דבר, כי אם מה שמשפיע להם השם יתברך, ובזה השם יתברך אחד ואין זולתו" (Derekh Chayyim line 1570). "כי אין מצד הבריאה עצמה כלום, רק הכל מן השם יתברך, כי הוא יתברך הכל" (Derekh Chayyim line 1059). "כי האחד הוא הכל כאשר אין זולתו, והבן זה מאד" (Derekh Chayyim line 616): "the One is all when there is none besides Him."

**He is all, and nothing is outside Him.** The strongest line in Gur Aryeh: "וכיון שנברא בצלם אלקים, הנה כמו שהשם יתברך הוא הכל ואין חוץ ממנו, כך ראוי הוא שהאדם שהוא נברא בצלמו הוא כולל כל עולמו" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 131).

**But He is not mixed with the world.** "אין השם יתברך מתאחד עם הנמצאים, והוא נבדל מהם, רק שרצונו מתאחד עמהם" (Be'er HaGolah line 281): "G-d does not become one with the beings; He is apart from them; only His will becomes one with them." The Maharal holds both sides: nothing is outside Him, and He is set apart from all.

**The many are from the receiver.** "שיש בו אחדות מצד הפועל, ומתחלק מצד בחינת המושפע" (Derekh Chayyim line 614). The same answer returns in Gur Aryeh: "כי אצלו אין שינוי, רק השינוי מצד המקבלים, והוא יתברך משפיע תמיד" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 146), and "שכאשר יש מקבל יש נותן" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 610).

**He unites opposites.** "כי הוא יתעלה ויתברך הוא המאחד שני הפכים" (Derekh Chayyim line 1288). An angel rules fire or water. Only the One holds both. So "ומפני שהוא אחד אין מדותיו דין גמור, שהרי יש בו רחמים ודין" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 293).

**The world is one because He is One.** "שהעולם נמצא מן הש"י שהוא אחד ולכך העולם הוא אחד" (Netivot Olam line 593). Lending, giving, learning together, peace: each shows that the world is one. Refusing to give is a kind of twoness.

**Oneness is kingship.** "וזה כי האחדות הוא המלכות" (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 696). "רק כי אין אחדות אלא למלך, שהוא יחיד בעמו, ועל ידו יתקשר הכל להיות כלל אחד" (same line). So the Shema is accepting His kingship, and "וזהו מפני כי המלכות הוא האחדות" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 628): when Israel is one, His kingship rests on them.

**One now, and One at the end.** The powers of the angels and princes are lent: "לפי שהוא אינו רק דבר שהוא שאול להם לזמן, ולא יהיה לנצח" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 148). "כאשר הכל שב אל השם יתברך, בזה השם יתברך אחד" (Derashat Shabbat HaGadol line 214). "כל הנמצאים נפסדים, והוא יתברך אחד בלבד קיים" (Gevurot Hashem line 743).

**Truth is one.** "כי אין דבר שהוא אחד רק האמת הוא אחד" (Netivot Olam line 618). "אמת חותמו להודיע כי הוא אחד" (Derush al HaTorah line 206).

**The person: cleaving, not sameness.** The soul is "caused" and He is the Cause. Any turning away is only from our side: "כי אין ההסרה שלהם רק מצד העלול עצמו, ולא מצד העלה" (Netzach Yisrael line 249). The way back is cleaving, and cleaving with no barrier is itself the removal of sin: "כי המקוה מטהר הטמא כאשר יתדבק בו לגמרי מבלי שום חציצה בעולם" (Drashot Maharal line 139). And love is cleaving "until one": "כי אהבה הוא מתדבק עם האוהב עד שנעשה אחד עם האוהב" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 189).

**Twoness is the root of sin.** The evil inclination is "a twoness" (Derashat Shabbat HaGadol line 49). Anger is the opposite of faith: "ועם הבטחון השמחה. אבל עם הכעס אין כאן אמונה" (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 474). Quarrel is "all strict law" (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 391). Idolatry is making "a head" of what is under Him (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 278).

**The middle.** Anything that cleaves to another does so through a middle, and between a person and G-d the middle is the Torah: "והחבור שהוא אל אדם עם בוראו היא התורה, שהיא אמצעי בין האדם לבוראו" (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 333). "אתה אחד, ואני אחד, והתורה אחת" (Derush al HaTorah line 121).

## Where he meets Chabad, and where he parts

**Tzimtzum (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah).** The Maharal uses the word only once in passing ("צמצם שכינתו ביניים", Drashot Maharal line 68: "He contracted His Presence among us"). But his main answer to the problem of the many is the same shape as the Chabad reading of tzimtzum: nothing changed on His side; all the change is on the side of the receiver (Derekh Chayyim line 614; Gur Aryeh Devarim lines 146, 610; Netzach Yisrael line 249). Shaar HaYichud ch. 7 and Tanya ch. 48 say the hiding is only from our side. Where they part (ours): Chabad says the world, seen from His side, is truly nothing ("bittul hayesh"). The Maharal stops short of that. He says beings "have nothing from their own side" (Derekh Chayyim line 1570), and that His will, not He, is joined with them (Be'er HaGolah line 281). He keeps a real world standing, fully dependent, set apart from Him. Chabad's "all is Him" goes one step further than the Maharal's "all is from Him."

**The Rambam's negative attributes.** The Maharal takes the Rambam's key points, no body, no parts, not a number, necessary being, and uses them. His four "descriptions" of the Name are Rambam-like: "התאר הראשון שהוא יתעלה אינו גוף", "התאר השני שהוא יתעלה מחויב המציאות" (Drashot Maharal line 129). But he argues with the Rambam's reading of knowing G-d and of His attributes (Derekh Chayyim lines 1164-1173). The attributes are not only negations: they are what the receiver takes from Him, like the rays of the sun, and a person can walk in them. So he turns negative theology into a path of cleaving by likeness: "כי ההדמות אל השם יתעלה במעשיו מביא אל האדם הקירוב והדיבוק בו" (Drashot Maharal line 129). Chassidus stands with him here. Tanya ch. 4 and 23 and Shaar HaYichud ch. 8-9 treat the middot as His garments that a person can hold.

**The Ramak's formula ("He is all that exists, and not all that exists is He").** The Maharal has both halves, in his own words. First half: "כמו שהשם יתברך הוא הכל ואין חוץ ממנו" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 131); "כי הוא יתברך הכל" (Derekh Chayyim line 1059). Second half: "והוא נבדל מהם" (Be'er HaGolah line 281); the altar must stand alone "כדי להורות על מי שנבנה לו המזבח הוא מיוחד" (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 349). He never says the world is "part" of Him, and he never says it is outside Him.

**The One is not a number.** "The One is not a number" (Derekh Chayyim line 1297; Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 38) is the Rambam's "One, but not one of the counted" (Yesodei HaTorah 1:7) given a positive turn: the One is the root of all counting. Shaar HaYichud ch. 7 says His unity is more than "one and not two"; the Maharal says the same.

**Twoness and the evil inclination.** "The evil inclination is twoness" (Derashat Shabbat HaGadol line 49; Derekh Chayyim line 428) is close to Tanya ch. 22-24: sin is a separation, "like idolatry." The Maharal makes twoness the root idea for anger, quarrel, envy and pride, where Tanya speaks of the "other side."

**Love and awe.** Love is cleaving until one, awe is standing apart (Gur Aryeh Devarim line 189; Gevurot Hashem line 789: "במקום שיש חבור אין יראה"). Tanya ch. 41-46 builds the same two, with awe as the base and love as the fire. The Maharal adds that G-d cannot give awe, since every act of His is already a bond.

**The tzaddik and the middle.** Korach's "we need no middle" (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 362) is what Chabad answers with the soul of Moshe in each generation (Tanya ch. 42). The Maharal's middle is the Torah (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 333) and Moshe as its giver. Chabad adds the Rebbe as a living middle; the Maharal does not.

**Where he parts from Chassidus (kept as differences).** - He almost never speaks in sefirot or worlds. His language is philosophical. Chassidus reads him (the Rebbe often cites him) but builds with Kabbalah. - He keeps a sharper line between Israel and the nations than the guide can carry (D28). The universal core is what we use. - He sometimes ties states to physical acts the guide will not use: Purim drinking (Ohr Chadash line 979; the guide takes the sober path), fasting as lessening the body. - His joy is mostly joy in wholeness and being ("והשמחה מורה על שלימות ועל המציאות", Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 562). Chassidus puts joy at the center of service. They meet in "שעם כל אמונה שמחה" (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar line 474).

## What the guide should take (ours)

1. **The logic of no edge.** For people who distrust pictures, the Maharal gives a clean argument: what cannot be defined has no outside. Use `mh_simple_no_edge` as an entry beside the Tanya's letters (F01).
2. **"From the side of the receiver."** This is his master key for suffering, for the many, and for the hidden face. It lets a person say: nothing changed on His side; my seeing changed. Tag Derekh Chayyim 614, Netzach Yisrael 249, Gur Aryeh Devarim 146 and 610 together.
3. **Twoness as the root of the bad.** Anger, quarrel, envy, suspicion: each is a split. Each practice that joins (peace, lending, learning together, going beyond the line) is a practice of unity.
4. **Kingship is oneness.** Our unity with each other is where His oneness shows as kingship (Gur Aryeh Bamidbar 696, Devarim 628). This joins inner work to the community.
5. **Held states for hard times.** Close in weakness, He is with us in exile, He returns with me, comfort from inside, held in mercy and law. They speak to illness, exile and grief without explaining suffering to anyone. Guard: always for oneself only.
6. **Do your part, He finishes** (the menorah). A steady middle between passivity and self-reliance.
7. **Guards.** Never drink (MH-S44 is reached sober). No self-harm in grief (Gur Aryeh Devarim 293). Prayer is alongside medical care, never instead. D28 frames are left out.
