## How to read these

Every concept in Chassidus points to the unity of God, which cannot be grasped by definition; it can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (*hisbonenus*). So each subject is given here as a meditation in the Chabad way, not as an idea. **Hold** is one line of teaching from the entry's own sources. **The move** thinks it through slowly: it opens the line detail by detail, gives the source's own picture and says where the picture breaks, and brings it down to you, here, now. It uses only what the entry's derivation and passages say. **Stay** binds the mind to the line, as Tanya 3 describes da'as: the pauses are for thinking, and a stray thought is not argued with, only left for the line. **From inside** describes the glimpse and never asks for it; if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts. **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, adds one mitzvah or act of Torah in the person's measure, and gives a line to return to.

**Tie** says whether the move rests on derivation steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our own reading; the steps are listed. Where the entry marks a subject's tie as thin or partly thin, the meditation is kept modest and says so in one line. Where the sources differ on what the move touches, the difference is kept, not smoothed. Anchors are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly, OCR spelling included.

## S064 · The powers of the soul · כחות הנפש

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One; also touches F09, F05, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Your many powers, thinking, loving, fearing, speaking, are one 'you' before they are many. That is how all of creation stands before Him: many on the outside, nothing apart from its source.

**Hold.** 'Like the nullification of the letters of speech and thought in their source, the essence of the soul and its ten powers', so are all creatures before Him. (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 33)

**The move.** Open it: your soul has ten powers, matched to the ten sefirot from which they came down, and the Rashab says that in its essence all of them 'dwell together', even fire and water, because there they are not yet separate things. So all creatures stand before Him as your words stand before your soul. The picture fails exactly where it must: in you, will, wisdom and kindness are different powers of one soul, while in Him they add no multiplicity at all, and the wisdom that comes first in you is 'the end of action' for Him. Bring it down to one sentence you said today and the 'you' that said it, here, now: the sentence is as nothing before you, and nothing that is stands apart from Him.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'as the letters in their source', and in the pauses think: many on the outside, one within. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Gathered: many sides of you resting in one. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'Hear, O Israel, the Lord is One' with your lips today; learn a few lines of Tanya chapter 33; and return during the day to 'as nothing before its source'.

**Where the sources differ.** Whether the soul is a fair picture of God is held both ways: Tanya and Shaar HaYichud say where it fails; the Rebbe models the whole chain of worlds on the soul's powers; the Mitteler Rebbe says some pictures are truer than others. All of them use it and mark where it fails.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 3:1 (line 78): כל בחינה ומדרגה משלש אלו: נפש רוח ונשמה – כלולה מעשר בחינות, כנגד עשר ספירות עליונות שנשתלשלו מהן Tanya 3 opens the soul's anatomy: each level of the soul has ten powers, corresponding to and descended from the ten sefirot. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 33:2 (line 451): כביטול אותיות הדבור והמחשבה במקורן ושרשן, הוא מהות הנפש ועצמותה, שהן עשר בחינותיה: חכמה בינה ודעת כו׳ Tanya 33: the true unity (all creatures nullified in Him) is explained by the nullification of speech and thought in the soul's ten powers, their root. - Hemshech Ayin Beis, חלק ראשון / בס"ד. ש"פ שלח, תער"ב / ל:3 (line 121): והמשל בזה ידוע מכחות הנפש שבודאי נמצאים כלולים בעצם הנפש ושם ישכנו כולם יחד The Rashab: powers that are opposite when revealed (fire and water) dwell together in the soul's essence; so the sefirot are one within Or Ein Sof.

## S065 · Wisdom, understanding and knowledge in man · חכמה בינה ודעת

*Face F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'; also touches F06, F08, F01. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The highest point of your mind is where He is clothed in you. And the wise are the ones who see each thing coming to be from nothing, now.

**Hold.** 'Who is wise? One who sees what is born: who sees how each thing is born and comes to be from nothing, by the word of God.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 43)

**The move.** Open it: wisdom is 'koach mah', the power of 'what?', the flash before anything is grasped, and because it is not yet grasped, the light of Ein Sof, which no thought grasps, is clothed in it, in every Jew. Understanding then unfolds it, and Tanya names its subject from the start: how He fills all worlds and surrounds all worlds, and all is as nothing before Him. And the wise see each thing, not only the world as a whole, coming to be from nothing by His word. Bring it down to the cup on your table and the pen in your hand, here, now: not made once and left, but being born from nothing at this moment.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'one who sees what is born', and in the pauses think of one thing in front of you. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Absorbed in what is seen, with no room left to watch yourself. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'who is wise? one who sees what is born' with your lips once today; learn one law, since Tanya says knowing a law joins the mind to His wisdom; and return during the day to 'born now'.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 3:8 (line 85): כשמתבונן ומעמיק מאד בגדולת ה׳, איך הוא ״ממלא כל עלמין״ ו״סובב כל עלמין״ ו״כולא קמיה כלא חשיב״ Tanya 3: Binah's object is named as the unity itself (filling, surrounding, all as nothing before Him). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 18:10 (line 258): שהוא מה שאינו מושג ומובן, ואינו נתפס בהשגה עדיין, ולכן מתלבש בה אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא Tanya 18: because Chochmah is not yet comprehension, Or Ein Sof is clothed in it, in every Jew. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 43:3 (line 626): איזהו חכם הרואה את הנולד״, פירוש, שרואה כל דבר איך נולד ונתהוה מאין ליש, בדבר ה׳ ורוח פיו יתברך Tanya 43: the wise man sees every thing coming into being from nothing by the word of God (F01 as the content of Chochmah).

## S066 · Da'as: bond and recognition · דעת

*Face F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'; also touches F01, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Knowing Him is not having information about Him. It is a bond, held until He is as real to you as a thing you are looking at.

**Hold.** 'Until his thought is bound to God with a strong and firm bond, as it is bound to a physical thing that he sees with his eyes of flesh.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 42)

**The move.** Open it: da'as is from 'and Adam knew Eve', a bond, the mind tied to the greatness of Ein Sof and not taken off it, and without it even wisdom breeds only false imaginings. Its measure is sight, and what it sees is heaven and earth with the eyes of flesh, the light of Ein Sof clothed in them to give them life. The Besht reads 'know Him in all your ways' as joining, even in physical acts. Bring it down to the window in front of you and the tree outside it, here, now: you are sure they are there, and that sureness is the measure Tanya asks for, turned to the life clothed in them.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'bound, as to a thing seen with the eyes', and in the pauses think of the life clothed in the tree. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The heart answering, as a face answers its reflection in water: knowing that is more like seeing than thinking. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'in all your ways, know Him' with your lips before one plain act today; do that act, a meal or a walk, as joined to Him; and return during the day to 'bound, as seen'.

**Where the sources differ.** Tanya 42 says this knowing takes 'great and doubled toil'; Breslov says His greatness cannot be put into writing or speech, and each grasps it by the measure of his own heart. They also overlap: Tanya too says it is not knowledge from books, and that its measure differs from soul to soul.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 3:13 (line 90): והדעת, הוא מלשון ״והאדם ידע את חוה״, והוא לשון התקשרות והתחברות, שמקשר דעתו בקשר אמיץ וחזק מאד, ויתקע מחשבתו בחוזק בגדולת איןסוף ברוךהוא Tanya 3: da'as is bonding, the mind fastened to the greatness of Ein Sof. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 42:4 (line 607): עד שתהא מחשבתו מקושרת בה׳ בקשר אמיץ וחזק, כמו שהיא מקושרת בדבר גשמי שרואה בעיני בשר Tanya 42: the essence of da'as is not information but a bond as strong as the bond to what one sees. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 42:16 (line 619): על ידי ראיית עיני בשר בשמים וארץ וכל צבאם, אשר אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא מלובש בהם להחיותם Tanya 42: da'as sees, through the physical, the light of Ein Sof clothed in heaven and earth to give them life.

## S067 · The emotions of the heart · מדות שבלב

*Face F09 The unity of opposites; also touches F06, F12, F11. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Love and awe pull opposite ways in a person, like fire and water. They meet in Him, because what joins them is the 'nothing' that He is.

**Hold.** 'There must be one more thing that joins them, and that is the attribute of ayin.' (the Maggid, Maggid Devarav LeYaakov 3)

**The move.** Open it: love and fear are opposites, like fire and water, and cannot sit together unless something joins them. The Maggid adds that in every single thing are God's own attributes, so every love and every fear is first in His thought. And Tanya says where the heart's love is meant to point: it blazes 'for the greatness of Ein Sof', born from contemplating Him. Bring it down to one person you both love and hold in awe, a parent or a teacher, here, now: the love and the awe in you are not two rivals; they meet in the 'nothing' that joins them, which is Him.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'one more thing that joins them', and in the pauses think: He is the joiner. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A heart wide enough for both. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the first line of the Shema slowly today, love and awe together in the one word 'One'; show that person one act of love with respect in it; and return during the day to 'He joins them'.

**Where the sources differ.** Where the emotions come from is disputed: Tanya 3 says from the mind, their 'mothers'; the Tzemach Tzedek, from a will and delight above the mind; the Mitteler Rebbe, from an essential trait in the soul that makes a will and mind of its own kind; the Rashab, from the soul's simple rootedness. The later Rebbeim partly soften it themselves.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Maggid Devarav leYaakov 3:1 (line 14): רק צריך להיות עוד דבר א' המחברם והוא מדת אי"ן The Maggid: opposites (fire and water, love and fear) need a joiner, the attribute of ayin. - Maggid Devarav leYaakov 25:2 (line 78): דבכל דבר ודבר יש מדותיו של הש"י The Maggid: in every single thing are God's attributes; every story of love or fear in Torah is His clothing (after 25:1: He created the worlds so the attributes would be revealed). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 3:9 (line 86): ושוב יתלהב לבו באהבה עזה כרשפי אש, בחשיקה וחפיצה ותשוקה ונפש שוקקה, לגדולת איןסוף ברוךהוא Tanya 3: the heart's love is born from contemplating His greatness, and its object is the greatness of Ein Sof.

## S068 · Expanded and constricted mind · מוחין דגדלות ודקטנות

*Face F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; also touches F12, F11, F05. Tie: stated (derivation steps 3, 4). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** The small, narrow mind is not being away from Him. Even when all you can hold is 'the whole earth is full of His glory, He is near', that too is His contraction, from Him and in Him.

**Hold.** 'At times a person serves only in smallness: he does not enter the upper worlds at all, and only thinks that the whole earth is full of His glory and He is near to him.' (Tzava'at HaRivash 67)

**The move.** Open it: even in that smallness there can be great devekus. Keter Shem Tov calls the coming and going of small mind and great mind the secret of the tzimtzum, which is 'from Him and in Him'. And Me'or Einayim says the small comes first so that the great mind, when it comes, can join even the smallness to itself. Bring it down to a small, dull day, when nothing big comes to you in prayer or in learning: here, now, 'the whole earth is full of His glory, and He is near' is enough to hold, and it too is from Him and in Him.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the whole earth is full of His glory, and He is near', and in the pauses think: small, and still with Him. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The relief of finding He was not gone when the mind went small. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'the whole earth is full of His glory' with your lips today, at the Kedushah or on its own; learn one short line of Torah even on the dull day, in your measure; and return during the day to 'He is near'.

**Kept modest.** The tie is stated where small and great mind are defined by what they hold or by the tzimtzum; the technical anatomy of mochin states none, so this meditation keeps to those lines.

**Where the sources differ.** Whether smallness is a fall or a way of cleaving is disputed: the Besht's circle honours it as devekus in its own right; Tanya and Derekh Mitzvotekha treat it as the great mind of Ein Sof withdrawn, or faith alone, to be broadened into knowing; Me'or Einayim has it come first in order to be included in the great.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tzava'at HaRivash 67:1 (line 122): פעמים אין אדם עובד רק בקטנות דהיינו שאינו נכנס בעולמות עליונים כלל רק שמחשב שמלא כל הארץ כבודו והוא קרוב אצלו The Besht's circle: katnus is thinking only that the whole earth is full of His glory, and even so it can be 'great devekus'. - Keter Shem Tov 1:77:4 (line 125): בסוד והחיות רצוא ושוב ונקרא קטנות וגדלות כמ"ש הבעש"ט סוד הצמצום שהוא מיני' ובי' Keter Shem Tov: the rhythm of katnus and gadlus is the tzimtzum 'from Him and in Him' (F03). - Me'or Einayim, Bereshit 6 (line 28): והאדם תחלה בחינת קטנות ואחר כך כשבא אל השכל הגדול מדבק הכל גם הקטנות לגדלות Me'or Einayim: man is created small first so that the great mind may join even the smallness to itself, as darkness longs to be included in light.

## S069 · The mind rules the heart · מוח שליט על הלב

*Face F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'; also touches F08, F09, F05. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** What rules the heart matters more than the ruling. When the mind holds 'all is as nothing before Him', the heart can follow, and even its love stops saying 'I'.

**Hold.** 'The main thing is to rule over the nature in the left chamber by the light of God that shines to the divine soul in the mind.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 16)

**The move.** Open it: the mind rules the heart by nature, from birth, so by the will in his mind a person can hold back what his heart desires. But the great rule is what the mind rules with, and the Tzemach Tzedek names it: the mind grasps that all is as nothing before Him, and the heart answers amen. Likkutei Torah says where it leads: to a love that is not 'I love, I exist' but 'to You, God, I lift my soul'. Bring it down to one reaction you can see coming today, at work or at home: before it, here, now, the mind holds 'all is as nothing before Him', and the heart can follow, even if it follows slowly.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'by the light of God that shines in the mind', and in the pauses think: all is as nothing before Him. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A heart that follows without being forced, and a love with less 'I' in it. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'to You, God, I lift my soul' with your lips once today; before the reaction you expect, hold back one word, as your mitzvah of the day; and return during the day to 'all is as nothing before Him'.

**Kept modest.** The principle alone is a law of the soul and does not state the unity; the tie is stated only in what the mind rules with and what for, so the meditation holds that content, not the ruling itself.

**Where the sources differ.** Whether the mind always rules is disputed: Tanya says by nature, in everyone but the truly wicked; the Rashab, only by inner light and only when the feelings are weakened, for strong feelings do not yield; the Tzemach Tzedek calls such rule the outer, small-mind mode, against inner change; Likkutei Torah says mind and heart are opposites joined only through Torah.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 16:1 (line 230): העיקר, הוא למשול ולשלוט על הטבע שבחלל השמאלי על ידי אור ה׳ המאיר לנפש האלהית שבמוח Tanya 16: the great rule for beinonim is to rule the heart by the light of God in the mind, contemplating Ein Sof. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, Not to eat the sciatic nerve 3 (line 34): שמחמת שמשיג בשכלו בגדולת ה' איך דכולא קמי' כלא חשיבי' וכי לו יאתה לדבוק בו ולעזוב כל אשר זולתו מנגד והמוח שליט על הלב בטבעו ותולדתו The Tzemach Tzedek: what the mind grasps and imposes is 'all is as nothing before Him'; this is iskafya. - Likkutei Torah, Matot 6:16 (line 2658): ולכן עי״ז נמשך להיות מוח שליט על הלב שלא יהיה התפעלות הלב בענין אהבה בבחי׳ יש אני אוהב Likkutei Torah: the mind's rule means the heart's love is not selfhood ('I love'), but bitul.

## S070 · Will · רצון

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F04, F05, F07. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** His will is not behind the mitzvah or behind the halachah; it is the halachah itself. When you hold what He wants, you are holding Him.

**Hold.** 'The supernal will is itself the very matter of the halachah that he thinks and speaks.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 23)

**The move.** Open it: in Him will is not a separate attribute, for His will and wisdom are simply one with His Essence, and the Rebbe calls the mitzvot, His will, 'the revelation of the Essence'. The Tzemach Tzedek says it is the will, through the delight within it, that brings every power out of hiding. The Rebbe's picture is your own will: not a thing of its own, only the soul leaning toward something, so it is not clothed in any limb but surrounds them all at once; it is only a picture, since in you will is one power among several and in Him it is simply Himself. Bring it down to one halachah you will keep today, here, now: in keeping it you are not near His will, you are holding it.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the will is itself the halachah', and in the pauses think of the one law you will keep. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** An embrace: holding what He wants is holding Him. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over one mitzvah today with your lips, slowly; keep that mitzvah because He wants it, and for no other reason; and return during the day to 'His will, itself'.

**Where the sources differ.** Will's place is put differently: for the Tzemach Tzedek and the Rebbe will is the outer side and delight the inner; Breslov calls Ein Sof 'the will of all wills'; Izhbitz makes His will the explanation of everything that happens, even human acts.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 23:8 (line 309): כי רצון העליון הוא הוא הדבר הלכה עצמה שמהרהר ומדבר בה Tanya 23: the supernal will is the halacha itself, so the learner is united with it, not only its chariot. - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ד / בס"ד. ש"פ עקב, כ"ף מנחם-אב (מאמר ב') ה'תשכ"ז:8 (line 2905): והרצון, אינו מציאות לעצמו וכל ענינו הוא הטיית והמשכת הנפש, ולכן, גם כשנמשך באברי הגוף אינו מתלבש בהם ורק מקיף עליהם The Rebbe: will surrounds the limbs as sovev surrounds worlds; the mitzvot, His will, are 'the revelation of the Essence'. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of confession of sin 2 (line 84): נמצא שהרצון הוא הגורם גילוי הכחות מההעלם והיינו ע"י העונג שמלובש ברצון The Tzemach Tzedek: will reveals the powers from concealment, by the delight clothed in it; so above, Keter mediates between Ein Sof and the sefirot.

## S071 · Delight · תענוג

*Face F07 The home below; also touches F05, F06, F11. Tie: stated (derivation steps 3, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** He made this world out of desire, not out of a reason. And a delight that deep reaches all the way down, to the bones of the feet and to this plain place.

**Hold.** 'In the words of the Alter Rebbe: on a desire there is no question.' (the Rebbe, Beshalach 5731)

**The move.** Open it: He desired a dwelling below, and reasons like 'that they know Me' would be met by the upper worlds, so only a desire above reason explains this lowest one. The same shape is in you: delight, above every power and even above will, is for that very reason drawn into every limb, 'even the bones of the foot'; and Me'or Einayim says He is 'the delight of all delights', so one who knows it does not tear a pleasure from its root. The Maggid's picture is a father delighting in his small son riding a stick; it is only a picture, since He is not a father with a body. Bring it down to this plain place and this plain day, here, now: it is here because He wanted it, and wanting needs no reason.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'on a desire there is no question', and in the pauses think: this plain place is what He desired. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The joy of the plain man whose king lives with him. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say a blessing with your lips over one plain pleasure today, food or drink, slowly; take it without tearing it from its root; and return during the day to 'on a desire there is no question'.

**Where the sources differ.** Breslov calls Ein Sof 'the will of all wills' and Izhbitz makes His will the center of all; the Chabad sources here set delight above will.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד. ש"ק ומוצאי ש"ק פ' בשלח, י"א שבט, ה'תשל"א:8 (line 1382): ובלשון אדמו"ר הזקן אויף א תאוה איז קיין קשיא The Rebbe quoting the Alter Rebbe: 'on a desire there is no question'; the dwelling below is from a desire above reason. - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד. ש"ק ומוצאי ש"ק פ' בשלח, י"א שבט, ה'תשל"א:9 (line 1383): וע"ד התענוג שבאדם (דנתאוה שייך לתענוג), דהגם שהתענוג הוא למעלה מכל הכחות, למעלה גם מרצון The Rebbe: human delight, above all powers and will, is for that reason drawn into every power and even the bones of the foot; so 'nisaveh' is drawn into the lowest world. - Me'or Einayim, Vaera 1 (line 326): והוא תענוג כל התענוגים ברוך הוא וברוך שמו חי החיים Me'or Einayim: He is the delight of all delights; one who knows this cleaves to the true delight and does not separate a pleasure from its root.

## S072 · Thought, speech and action: the garments · מחשבה דבור ומעשה

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F03, F07, F05. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The thoughts, words and deeds you wear usually hide who you are. When they are the thoughts, words and deeds of His mitzvot, they are His own will and wisdom, higher than the soul inside them.

**Hold.** 'Their worth is infinitely higher than that of nefesh, ruach and neshamah themselves, for the Torah and the Holy One are all one.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 4)

**The move.** Open it: thought, speech and action are the soul's three garments, and the Rashab says that on their own they are separate from the soul, like a thing in itself, and so they hide it. But when they are the thought, speech and action of Torah and mitzvot they are His own will and wisdom, which He contracted into the 613; and the Rebbe says action, the lowest of the three, is rooted in the very essence of the soul, which is why action is the main thing. The picture is a garment, and here it turns over: an ordinary garment is lower than the one who wears it, and these are higher than the soul inside them. Bring it down to one mitzvah you will do today, here, now: the thought of it, the words of it and the doing of it are His will and wisdom in your hands.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'infinitely higher than the soul itself', and in the pauses think of the one mitzvah. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being dressed in what is higher than you. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over one mitzvah today with your lips; do it in all three garments, thinking what it is, saying its words, doing it with your hands; and return during the day to 'the Torah and the Holy One are one'.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 4:5 (line 97): גבהה וגדלה מעלתם לאין קץ וסוף על מעלת נפש רוח ונשמה עצמן, כמו שכתוב בזהר, דאורייתא וקודשאבריךהוא כולא חד Tanya 4: these garments are infinitely higher than the soul itself, because Torah and God are one. - Hemshech Ayin Beis, חלק ראשון / בס"ד. ש"פ ויצא, תער"ג / רב:3 (line 815): משא"כ לבושי' דמחדו"מ הם לבושי' נפרדים שהרי אינן מסוג הנפש וממהותו וה"ה כמו דבר בפ"ע מהנפש The Rashab: thought, speech and action are separate garments, like a thing in itself, and so they conceal the soul. - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, חלק ב / בס"ד. מוצש"ק פ' מקץ, ליל זאת חנוכה ה'תשמ"ו:6 (line 1077): והשרש דכח המעשה הוא בעצם הנפש The Rebbe: the root of action, the lowest power, is in the soul's essence (G05).

## S073 · Speech · דבור

*Face F01 The world said into being now; also touches F02, F08, F10. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The world is His word, and a word is nothing next to the one who speaks it. But His word never leaves Him, so the world has never for a moment been outside Him.

**Hold.** 'But the Holy One, His speech is not separate from Him, for there is nothing outside Him.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 21)

**The move.** Open it: one word you speak is as nothing before your power of speech, which could say endless words, and before your thought and your whole soul, and so is the world before Him. The Maggid adds that every word people speak is hewn from that speech above, and the Rebbe says that speaking is itself a going out of oneself for another. Tanya's picture is your own speech, and it breaks exactly at the unity: your word, once spoken, is felt as a thing apart from you, and His is not. Bring it down to the next sentence you say, here, now: it leaves you, and the world He speaks has never for a moment left Him.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'there is nothing outside Him', and in the pauses think of the room as spoken and still in the Speaker. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being held: His word does not leave Him the way yours leaves you. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say one line of Torah aloud today, the Shema or a verse you know, not only in thought, since Iggeret HaKodesh says speaking words of Torah stirs the speech above; and return during the day to 'nothing outside Him'.

**Where the sources differ.** In Chabad human speech is chiefly the picture that fails; in the Besht, the Maggid and Breslov it is a force that joins or harms the worlds above.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 20:5 (line 281): כשמדבר דבור אחד; שדבור זה לבדו כלא ממש, אפילו לגבי כללות נפשו המדברת Tanya 20: one word is as nothing before the soul's power of speech, thought and ten powers; so the world before Him. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 21:1 (line 285): הרי הבל הדבור שבפיו הוא מורגש ונראה דבר בפני עצמו מובדל משרשו Tanya 21: in man, speech once spoken is a thing separate from its root. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 21:2 (line 286): אבל הקדושברוךהוא אין דבורו מובדל ממנו יתברך חס ושלום, כי אין דבר חוץ ממנו Tanya 21: His speech is not separate from Him, 'for there is nothing outside Him'. The analogy fails exactly at the unity.

## S074 · Imagination · כח המדמה

*Face F09 The unity of opposites; also touches F03, F12, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 3, 4). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** A dream puts together two things that cannot go together, and in the dream they are one. Torah Ohr says that joining is true in its root above, where what is divided here is one.

**Hold.** 'A dream joins two opposites in one subject as if they were truly one, because in truth, in their root in the upper world, they are joined and united.' (Torah Ohr, Vayeshev 3)

**The move.** Open it: in sleep only the imagination remains, which is why a dream can join opposites; in exile the soul is asleep like that, so a person prays with love of God in the morning and spends the day in business, two opposites held together. Above, where all things divided below are joined, 'these and these are the words of the living God'. And Torah Ohr draws the comfort itself: let a person's heart not fall, saying that the arousal of his prayer was empty imagination because it passed. Bring it down to your own day, here, now: whatever you held in prayer this morning and the hours of work after it are not a lie against each other; they are joined in their root.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'in their root they are joined', and in the pauses think of the prayer and the work side by side. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A heart wide enough for both. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say one line of this morning's prayer with your lips in the middle of work today; learn one short piece of Torah in a break, in your measure; and return during the day to 'joined in their root'.

**Kept modest.** Most Chabad passages treat imagination as a counterfeit to be ruled by the mind; the positive tie is stated in one Alter Rebbe discourse (Torah Ohr, Vayeshev 3) and in Homel's analogy, and this meditation rests on the first.

**Where the sources differ.** Chabad's first word on imagination is a warning, false imaginings to be ruled by intellect; Breslov holds that 'the essence of faith depends on the imaginative power', where intellect stops. Both place faith where intellect stops; Tanya seats it in Chochmah, Breslov in the imagination.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Torah Ohr, Vayeshev 3:1 (line 671): הנה החלום הוא מחבר שני הפכים בנושא א' ומרכיב שני ענינים הפכיים כאלו היו לאחדים Torah Ohr: a dream joins opposites in one subject, because only imagination remains in sleep. - Torah Ohr, Vayeshev 3:9 (line 679): בבחי' חלום שהוא כח המדמה לחבר שני הפכים בנושא אחד כאלו הם אחדים ממש לפי שבאמת בשרשם בעולם העליון הם מחוברים ומיוחדים Torah Ohr: the dream's joining of opposites is true in their root above, where they are united (F09). - Torah Ohr, Vayeshev 3:6 (line 676): לא יפול לב האדם עליו לומר כי ההתעוררות שבתפלה והתבוננותו הן דמיונות שוא Torah Ohr: the fear that prayer's arousal is only imagination (since it passes) is answered by the dream's root above.

## S075 · The heart and its two chambers · חלל הימני והשמאלי

*Face F11 Darkness turned to light; also touches F02, F07, F09. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Your heart is not meant to be a truce between two sides. The love that comes from knowing Him is meant to fill even the side that wants other things, and turn its wanting into love of Him.

**Hold.** 'To change it and turn it from the pleasures of this world to love of God, as it is written: with all your heart, with both your inclinations.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 9)

**The move.** Open it: the animal soul's seat is the left chamber of the heart, and the divine soul's love, born from contemplating His greatness, spreads to the right; in prayer, when the mind is bound to Ein Sof, the evil in the left is bent and nullified to the good of the right. But the aim is not a truce: the love is to spread into the left chamber too. And the Tzemach Tzedek says the evil inclination itself comes back to love through contemplating the higher and lower unity. Bring it down to an ordinary evening, here, now: the wish to be close to Him and the wish for comfort are in the same heart, and the part that wants comfort is not to be shut out but won.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'with both your inclinations', and in the pauses think of the part that wants comfort. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** One heart instead of two sides; a want turned into love. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'and you shall love Havayah your God with all your heart' with your lips today, thinking 'with both inclinations'; take one plain pleasure, a meal or a rest, with a blessing, for the sake of that love; and return during the day to 'both your inclinations'.

**Where the sources differ.** Breslov differs about everyone but the tzaddik: for anyone with a little da'as the coarse inclination of the left chamber is folly and no test at all; Tanya holds that in the beinoni it stays at full strength all his life. On David both agree: he killed it.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K06.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 9:5 (line 163): לשנותה ולהפכה מתענוגי עולם הזה לאהבת ה׳, כמו שכתוב: ״בכל לבבך״ – ״בשני יצריך״ Tanya 9: the aim is that love spread into the left chamber and transform its desire: 'with both your inclinations'. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 12:3 (line 185): הרע שבחלל השמאלי כפוף ובטל לטוב המתפשט בחלל הימני מחכמהבינהדעת שבמוח המקושרים בגדולת איןסוף ברוךהוא Tanya 12: in prayer the left chamber's evil is nullified to the right chamber's good, which flows from a mind bound to Ein Sof. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 42:1 (line 258): בכל לבבך בשני יצריך שגם היצה"ר והנפש השכלי' ישוב לאהבת ה' מחמת ההתבוננות הנ"ל The Tzemach Tzedek: the evil inclination too returns to love through contemplation of the two unities (ch. 41).
