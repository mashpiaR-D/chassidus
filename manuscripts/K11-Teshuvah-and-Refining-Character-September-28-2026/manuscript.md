## What this cluster shows

These thirteen subjects are the moral life seen from the one idea. If there is nothing besides Him, then sin cannot be a private debt between two parties. Tanya defines it as separation from His unity, like idolatry, because it opposes 'the will that gives life to all' (Tanya 24; IT 5). Teshuvah is then the soul's return to where it was one with Him before it was breathed out (IT 8). It reaches His very Essence (IH 1:4), above the chain of worlds and past every limit (the Rebbe, 5723; R. Tzadok, Takanat HaShavin 3:2). Its two grades are the two letters heh of the Name, the lower returned from the exile of the Shechinah and the upper where the soul cleaves spirit to spirit (IT 4-11). Confession and regret uproot a will that went out from Him and restore it from 'the will of all wills' (Derekh Mitzvotekha; Sha'arei Teshuvah). The traits follow the same pattern. Pride is 'I, and none besides me', idolatry itself (Tanya 22; Likutei Moharan 10). Anger is faith in providence lost for a moment (IH 25). Desire is His love fallen into exile (Tzava'at HaRivash 90, 120). Baseless hatred splits souls that have one Father and exiles the Shechinah (Tanya 32; IH 31). Slander tears 'one man' (IH 22). Forbearance rises above one's measures to the place of His infinite forgiveness (IT 11; Me'or Einayim; Toras Shmuel). Toil in Torah follows from the Essence being in it (the Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos 35). The tie is stated, not read in, for most of the cluster: teshuvah, its two grades, sin, confession, pride, anger, desire, baseless hatred, slander and forbearance. Three corrections to SUBJECTS follow from the sources. Forbearance, marked 'thin', is stated by three voices (the Chernobyler, the Maharash, R. Nachman); the Alter Rebbe states its two halves without joining them. Desire and confession, marked 'reading', are stated by the Besht, the Maggid, the Rashab, the Mitteler Rebbe and the Tzemach Tzedek. The tie is thin, or runs only through teshuvah and bitul, for jealousy as a trait of its own, for idle speech, for the techniques of trait-refinement and for the 'merchant's account' of Elul. The explicit Essence-tie of human toil is late and single (the Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos 35).

## S125 · Teshuvah · תשובה

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F05 The Essence beyond both · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F07 The home below **Search terms:** תשובה · בעל תשובה · בעלי תשובה · תשובה שלימה

> Teshuvah is the unity of God as the soul's return to where it was one with Him before it was breathed out, a return that goes beyond every limit to His very Essence.

## Derivation

1. The divine soul is 'a part of Havayah', breathed 'from within Him'; it hangs from its source like a rope, one end above and one below (IT 4:4-8, 5:1-2) (F06). (*stated*)
2. Nothing physical or spiritual separates before Him, since He fills all and 'ein od' (IT 5:4). Only sins separate, because they oppose the supreme will that gives life to all (IT 5:5). The distance is one of will, not of place. (*stated*)
3. So teshuvah is not fasting but return: 'to return to God with all one's heart' (IT 1:8-9). Complete teshuvah is the soul cleaving to Him 'as it was united with Him in utter unity' before it came down (IT 8:4). (*stated*)
4. It reaches the Essence, not a radiance: teshuvah and good deeds bring Israel close 'to His very Essence', beyond the World to Come (IH 1:4). The Rebbe places teshuvah in the light of Ein Sof above the chain of worlds, as a person going out of his own limits (Shabbat Shuvah 5723). R. Tzadok says it breaks every boundary of Torah and answers to Havayah, which has no beginning or end (Takanat HaShavin 3:2). R. Tzadok speaks of the unbounded Name Havayah, not of the Essence; filing him under F05 is our reading (F05). (*stated*)
5. The Maggid, via the Alter Rebbe and the Rayatz: teshuvah must go on until Havayah above the worlds becomes 'your Elokim' (G14), so that the Name beyond nature is found inside one's own nature (F02). (*stated*)
6. The Rashab, via the Rayatz: Reb Zusya's five letters of teshuvah are general paths of service, brought into action through prayer, that complete the dwelling below (F07). (*stated*)
7. Putting the Alter Rebbe's soul-return, the Maggid's Havayah-as-Elokim and the Rebbe's Essence-teshuvah together as one chain is our arrangement. Each link is stated by its own author. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Iggeret HaTeshuvah (Tanya part III) is the systematic text: it strips teshuvah of the fasting tradition (ch. 1-3), grounds it in the soul as 'a part of Havayah' and the Name's letters (ch. 4-6), and ends in higher teshuvah as union (ch. 8-11). Iggeret HaKodesh 1: teshuvah reaches His very Essence. He also carried the Maggid's 'Havayah above the worlds your Elokim' (via the Rayatz).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Sha'arei Teshuvah: teshuvah preceded the world and stands above Torah, because Torah only separates pure from impure while teshuvah turns evil itself into good (3a:2). Its introduction says the light of Ein Sof rests only on a broken heart, so contemplative joy without teshuvah 'falls at once'.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Mitzvat Vidui: forgiveness comes from 'the will of all wills' (S128). Hayom Yom 10 Tevet (his yechidus answer): the man 'strong in his own opinion' must leave his thoughts, as the wicked must leave his way (S131).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627, Mi El Kamocha: forgiveness from Arich Anpin above the measuring line (S136). A general teshuvah discourse was not read in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah: lower teshuvah breaks the animal soul's selfhood, and higher teshuvah transforms it (S126). The five ways of teshuvah, as he explained them to the Rayatz (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus p. 24; Hayom Yom 3-8 Tishrei), culminate in the dwelling below.
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus pp. 22-24 (OCR) records the Maggid's teaching, Reb Zusya's five letters and his father's commentary, and sets teshuvah inside prayer ('all of them come into action through the work of prayer').
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, Shabbat Shuvah 5723: teshuvah is in Or Ein Sof above the chain of worlds, and it is going out of one's limits. Shabbat Shuvah 5737: Yom Kippur reveals that inwardly there was no blemish (S127).
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (via the Rayatz) as above. R. Tzadok (Takanat HaShavin 3:2): teshuvah breaks all the Torah's bounds, is the place where bounded tzaddikim cannot stand, and corresponds to limitless Havayah. This is the closest non-Chabad statement of the F05 tie. Breslov (Likutei Moharan 6): teshuvah is Keter, 'Ehyeh', and one must repent of one's earlier repentance (S128, S136). Pillar 15 lists Ruzhin (the penitent goes straight to the root beneath the Throne) and Ropshitz (forgiveness opens the next repentance); these were not re-verified in this pass.

## Thin or forced?

No. The tie is stated by the Alter Rebbe (IT 5, 8; IH 1), the Maggid via the Rayatz, R. Tzadok and the Rebbe. Teshuvah is one of the most direct expressions of the one idea.

## S126 · Higher and lower teshuvah · תשובה עילאה ותשובה תתאה

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F11 Darkness turned to light · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** תשובה עילאה · תשובה תתאה · תשובה מאהבה · תשובה מיראה

> Higher and lower teshuvah are the unity of God as the two letters heh of His Name returned to their place: the lower heh brought back from exile among the husks, and the upper heh where the soul cleaves to Him spirit to spirit.

## Derivation

1. The Zohar reads 'teshuvah' as 'tashuv heh', 'return the heh': the lower heh is lower teshuvah, the upper heh is higher teshuvah (IT 4:2). The two kinds are two letters of the one Name. (*stated*)
2. The lower heh is the soul's descent from the inner source of speech. Sin draws its flow down into the husks, which is the exile of the Shechinah. Lower teshuvah takes the flow away from them and 'returns the lower heh from exile' (IT 6:8-11) (F03). (*stated*)
3. Its way is bitul: arousing mercy for the fallen Godliness, and crushing the husk, 'whose whole vitality is coarseness and haughtiness', through a broken heart and the hour of accounting (IT 7:1-9) (F08). (*stated*)
4. Higher teshuvah comes after it: the soul rises and cleaves 'as it was united with Him in utter unity' (IT 8:4-5). Mind, traits, thought, speech and deed each cleave to His (IT 9:3-7). Prayer is higher teshuvah and so must be preceded by lower teshuvah (IT 10:5). One should be in higher teshuvah all one's days, in great joy (IT 11:12). (*stated*)
5. The Rashab sharpens the difference. Lower teshuvah breaks the selfhood of the animal soul. Only higher teshuvah changes its essence so that it rises and is included in holiness (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan p. 28) (F11). (*stated*)
6. The Mitteler Rebbe: lower teshuvah, gathering the scattered sparks of the soul, precedes higher. Teshuvah preceded the world and transforms evil itself into good, which Torah's clarifying cannot do (Sha'arei Teshuvah 3a:2). (*stated*)
7. R. Aharon II of Karlin: everything must be bound to higher teshuvah, uniting the two worlds (the two hehs) until each thing is united with its first source (Beit Aharon, Eikev 13). R. Tzadok: in love-teshuvah the distance itself turns out to have been closeness, revealed only in the future (Takanat HaShavin 5:8-9). (*stated*)
8. Reading the pair as the lower and higher unity (Baruch Shem and Shema) is ours. IT 10:3 comes close when it grounds 'who sanctified us' in the surrounding light, for which 'all is as nothing before Him', but it does not name the two unities. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Iggeret HaTeshuvah 4-11 is the locus: the two hehs, the exile of the lower heh, tikkun chatzot and the broken heart for the lower, Torah, acts of kindness and prayer for the higher. Shabbat is higher teshuvah ('Shabbat = tashev enosh', IT 10:6).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Sha'arei Teshuvah 3a:2: lower teshuvah as the ingathering of the soul's scattered sparks, preceding higher teshuvah. 116d-117a: higher teshuvah uproots every foreign will through the yechidah (S128, S133).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located as a separate treatment in this pass (his Derekh Mitzvotekha on confession treats forgiveness from 'the will of all wills', S128).
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 4 (OCR): lower teshuvah's bitterness breaks selfhood but does not raise the animal soul; higher teshuvah and fiery love do.
- **Rayatz.** Not located as a separate treatment in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Shabbat Shuvah 5737 distinguishes 'until Havayah your God' (higher) grades inside Atik; not quoted here.
- **Other schools.** Karlin (Beit Aharon, Eikev 13): higher teshuvah as uniting both worlds to the source. R. Tzadok (Takanat HaShavin 5): love-teshuvah turns sins into merits because the distance was closeness. Pillar 15 records a real disagreement not re-verified here: Karlin and R. Tzadok say the higher teshuvah is God's ('I will return to you'), where Tanya makes it the person's cleaving through Torah and prayer.

## Thin or forced?

No for the pair as such: it is built out of the Name Havayah in the Zohar and Tanya, and IT 8 states higher teshuvah as the soul's union with Him. But the face listed first, F02 (the higher and lower unity), is our reading (step 8): no text read here calls the two hehs Shema and Baruch Shem.

## S127 · Sin · חטא

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F09 The unity of opposites · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** חטא · עבירה · עון · פגם

> Sin is the unity of God seen from its denial: a creature holding itself apart from the will that gives it life. The separation is real toward the person and nothing before Him.

## Derivation

1. Holiness rests only on what is nullified to Him. The husk is called 'other gods' because it regards itself as separate, and that is 'denial of His true unity' (Tanya 22:6-8). (*stated*)
2. Every prohibition is against His will and wisdom and so is 'separated from His unity in utter separation', like the husk called idolatry. The sinner is worse than the husk, which knows its Master and never rebels (Tanya 24:1-6). (*stated*)
3. Nothing separates before Him, but sins do, 'because they oppose the supreme will that gives life to all' (IT 5:4-5). Karet is the cutting of the soul's rope from the Name; lesser sins nick its strands (IT 5:6-8). (*stated*)
4. IT 11:7 states that His forgiveness makes no difference between once and a thousand times, since before Ein Sof small and great numbers are the same: 'all is as nothing before Him'. It does not say that the sin's separation holds only from below; drawing that out as G02, the two knowledges, is our reading. (**partly stated**)
5. The power in evil is holiness fallen in the breaking, so confession raises the letters of the sin (Maggid 66:1). R. Tzadok goes further, but for one level only: Shlomo, whose teshuvah reached the Throne, recognized 'You are with me', that His glory fills the earth, and for him 'evil has no existence at all'. R. Tzadok uses this to explain Shlomo's dangerous conduct (the foreign wives); it is not a general statement about sin (Takanat HaShavin 2:6) (F09, F02). (*stated*)
6. The Rebbe: the atonement from 'the essence of the day' of Yom Kippur reveals that inwardly there was no blemish from the start (Shabbat Shuvah 5737). He keeps the other side too: in nefesh, ruach and neshamah blemishes do apply and are atoned, and the chief novelty is atonement for real sins. That this inner level is the yechidah of F06 is our gloss; the text speaks of 'the essence of the soul' bound with the Essence. (**partly stated**)
7. Holding both claims, a real separation (Tanya 24; IT 5) and its absence at a higher level (R. Tzadok for Shlomo's level; the Rebbe for the soul's inwardness), as the two knowledges of IH 20 is our application of G02. Neither R. Tzadok nor the Rebbe says evil has no existence for everyone. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not quoted on sin as such; see S130 on unrefined traits.
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Shabbat Shuvah 5737: inwardly there was no blemish, while the outer soul-levels are really blemished and really atoned. Shabbat Shuvah 5723: sin is 'against the intent of creation', so wisdom says the sinning soul must die, while God answers 'let him repent' from above the chain.

## S128 · Confession and regret · וידוי וחרטה

**Faces:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F05 The Essence beyond both · F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity **Search terms:** וידוי · חרטה · עזיבת החטא · וידוי דברים

> Confession and regret are the unity of God as a will that went out from Him being uprooted and returned to the Source of all wills, spoken aloud so that even the matter of the sin is undone.

## Derivation

1. The frame is halachic. The Torah's commandment of teshuvah is abandoning the sin (IT 1:7), and the Rambam and the Smag require confession and a request for forgiveness, not fasting (IT 1:10). No unity is stated here; this step is frame, and any tie of it to the unity is ours. (**reading**)
2. The Tzemach Tzedek gives the root. Mitzvot draw Ein Sof into the letters of Havayah, and sin lessens that drawing and feeds the husks. Forgiveness is 'the return of the will that departed'. It is not the same will but one drawn anew from 'the will of all wills', higher than before, which is why sins become merits (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Mitzvat Vidui 1, 3) (F10). 'Ra'ava de-ra'avin' is the source of wills in Keter, not 'atzmus' by name; filing it under F05 is our reading (F05). (*stated*)
3. The Mitteler Rebbe: regret uproots the will. True teshuvah is 'true regret in the essence' of the will's attachment, more than abandoning the sin. Desires scatter the soul, and only the simple will in the soul's essence (yechidah) gathers them back (Sha'arei Teshuvah 116d-117a) (F06). (*stated*)
4. He also explains confession in words. Heart-teshuvah removes the vitality of the destructive angel made by the sin, but its 'matter' is destroyed 'only through confession, in speech with the mouth' (Sha'arei Teshuvah 6c-d). (*stated*)
5. The Maggid: evil's power is holiness fallen in the breaking, and confession in love and awe raises the letters of the sin (66:1) (F09). (*stated*)
6. The Alter Rebbe: after contemplation one can truly ask 'wipe away my transgressions', because compassion for the Godliness in one's soul and above is now fixed in the heart (IT 8:1). The compassion is for the Godliness in the soul and above; the contrast 'not over oneself' is our gloss (F08). (*stated*)
7. R. Nachman: even 'I have sinned' is not said free of self-interest, and after a higher grasp one must repent of the earlier repentance for having 'corporealized His exaltedness' (Likutei Moharan 6:3). Confession is refined by knowledge of Him (F12). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** IT 1 (abandoning, confession, no fasting required), IT 3 (charity in place of fasts in 'our generations'), IT 8:1 (the true plea after contemplation). Tanya 29:14: if the soul's light does not rise, his teshuvah may not have been accepted, or he is being raised to a higher teshuvah.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Sha'arei Teshuvah: spoken confession destroys the 'matter' of the sin's angel (6c-d). Regret as uprooting of will in the soul's essence (116d-117a).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Mitzvat Vidui: forgiveness is the will restored from 'ra'ava de-ra'avin'. Confession is a commandment with its own root.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not quoted on confession as such.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (66:1): confession raises the sin's letters. R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 6:3): repentance on repentance. Pillar 15's Ropshitz ('forgiveness opens the eyes to what we broke') was not re-verified.

## S129 · Self-accounting · חשבון הנפש

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F06 The soul as part of the One **Search terms:** חשבון הנפש · חשבון צדק · חשבון נפש · שעל המטה

> Self-accounting is the unity of God as a daily audit of what in one's thought, speech and deed was 'not for Him', which is all that 'sitra achra' means.

## Derivation

1. Tanya defines 'sitra achra' as whatever is 'not for God, His will and His service' (ch. 6). The masters of accounting reckon every thought, word and deed by that line (Tanya 29:15). The account measures one thing: what stood apart from Him (F08). (*stated*)
2. Every holy thought makes a person a chariot for the chambers of holiness at that moment, and every evil one a chariot for impurity (Tanya 29:16). The account records where one's life was drawn from. (*stated*)
3. For lower teshuvah, the heart is broken by an hour of deep contemplation, before tikkun chatzot, on how one's sins caused the exile of the Shechinah and uprooted one's soul from the Life of life (IT 7:9). The account is made in terms of the soul's place in God (F06, F03). (*stated*)
4. Hayom Yom 27 Av: Elul is the month of accounting, like a merchant's periodic review of his business. This entry does not state a tie to the unity; linking it is our reading. (**reading**)
5. The Besht: what one sees in another is a trace in oneself. Repair yourself, and include the other 'through unity, for all are one man' (Keter Shem Tov 1:59). The account reaches past oneself because souls are one (G12). (*stated*)
6. R. Nachman: whoever judges himself removes the judgment that is otherwise clothed in all things, and the fear in all things rises to its root until only fear of God remains (Likutei Moharan 15:2). In hitbodedut one judges oneself 'before God who does him good at every moment' (Likkutei Etzot, Hitbodedut 1) (F03). The text states that fear clothed in all things is His judgment and rises to its root when one judges oneself; calling this F03's concealment for revelation is our reading. (**partly stated**)
7. That the practice as such, apart from these frames, expresses the unity is our reading. Its tie runs through the definition of sitra achra, teshuvah and bitul. (**reading**)

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 29:15-17 (masters of accounting; even dreams as an index). IT 7:9 (the hour of accounting before tikkun chatzot). The bedtime Shema's acceptance of the four death penalties (IT 7:15).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah: one must know which particular trait is one's portion to repair (S130). This is an accounting of the traits rather than of deeds.
- **Rayatz.** Hayom Yom 27 Av (Elul as the month of accounting) and 11 Nisan (on one's birthday, seclude oneself and review one's memories; read in grep, not in context, so not quoted).
- **The Rebbe.** Compiled Hayom Yom. No separate treatment located.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:59): self-accounting prompted by what one sees, grounded in the unity of souls. R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 15; Likkutei Etzot): daily self-judgment in hitbodedut, which frees fear from its garments. Here Breslov makes the tie more explicitly than the Hayom Yom entry does.

## S130 · Refining the traits · תיקון המדות

**Faces:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F06 The soul as part of the One · F07 The home below · F09 The unity of opposites **Search terms:** תיקון המדות · בירור המדות · זיכוך המדות · עבודת המדות

> Refining the traits is the unity of God as natural powers becoming Godly powers: the traits of the animal soul, one's own portion of the world, turned and joined to His traits.

## Derivation

1. Love of God is 'cleaving of spirit to spirit' with every part of the soul. The traits cleave to His traits, 'as He is merciful' (IT 9:3-4). A refined trait is one joined to its source (F06). (*stated*)
2. The Rebbe cites 'the virtue of Chassidus is that natural powers become Godly powers', and the Alter Rebbe's 'the whole of Chassidus is to change the nature of one's traits', not only the traits but their nature (Inyanah 1:7). (*stated*)
3. The Rebbe then demotes this to a branch of the one point (Inyanah 2:1), yet says the essence shows most when it reaches down to transforming the nature of the animal soul (19:4) (F11, G05). This folding-in is his reading, and it is recorded as such. (*stated*)
4. The Rashab: the soul came down to refine the natural traits of its own portion. That completes the supreme intent (F07). General contemplation leaves the dominant trait untouched, so one must know the particular trait and work on it (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan pp. 45-46). (*stated*)
5. The Alter Rebbe, via the Rayatz: in each of the 49 gates of the traits there must be a judge over its quality and an officer over its quantity, and each gate has its kind of prayer (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus p. 23). This is method, not unity. (**reading**)
6. The Maggid: a bad thought is a trait fallen from the seven days of building. Bound to God in love and awe, it returns to its root (Maggid 12:13). Hayom Yom 3 Iyar: even bad traits can serve God. The Maggid states the return to the root; the Hayom Yom entry states no tie to the unity, and filing it under F09 is our reading. (**partly stated**)
7. Pillar 21's fault lines are recorded but not re-verified: override the trait or uproot it (Vitebsk, Peri HaAretz), heart first or mind first (Strashelye), and whether the repair can be finished (R. Nosson). For the trait-manuals (Sefer HaMiddot) the tie to the unity is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 12-13 (the beinoni's mind ruling the heart's traits); IT 9 (traits cleaving to His); 'the whole of Chassidus is to change the nature of the traits' (via Inyanah 1:7); Shoftim 5564 on judges and officers for each trait (via the Rayatz).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Pillar 21 ranks The Gate of Unity high for trait-vocabulary; not quoted here. Sha'arei Teshuvah on scattered wills (S133).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Pillar 21: a trait is one in the heart and many in speech (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Tefillah 20); not re-verified here.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627: engaging in Torah refines the traits into forbearance (S136).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah is the manual. The purpose of the soul's descent is refining one's own natural traits. Only specific contemplation and toil change the dominant trait, and the test is whether it has changed (hisbonenus study).
- **Rayatz.** Recorded the Alter Rebbe's Shoftim teaching and the five ways; Hayom Yom 8 Nisan (unattributed entry, read in grep only): every soul has its particular work in mind and traits.
- **The Rebbe.** Inyanah 1:7, 2:1, 19:4: changing the nature of traits is a branch of Chassidus's point, and the point shows most in it.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (12:13): fallen traits returned to their root. Pillar 21: Chernobyl (the thirteen attributes implanted in man; cleave to His traits in deed), Ropshitz (a trait is a measure), Radzin (a trait gives room to its opposite), Vitebsk (uproot, not only override). These state or imply the tie differently and were not re-verified in this pass.

## S131 · Pride · גאוה

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** גאוה · גסות הרוח · יוהרא · מתגאה

> Pride is the unity of God denied from within: the self that says 'I, and none besides me', which is why it is idolatry itself and not only a bad trait.

## Derivation

1. The husk raises itself like an eagle and says 'I, and none besides me', the exact inverse of 'ein od milvado' (Tanya 22:7). (*stated*)
2. So haughtiness 'equals idolatry literally'. The root of idolatry is not denying God but regarding oneself as a separate thing. That is denial of the true unity, in which all is as nothing before Him, and holiness rests only on what is nullified (Tanya 22:8) (F08, F02). (*stated*)
3. The husk's whole vitality is coarseness and haughtiness, and its death is being crushed to dust through a broken heart, which is lower teshuvah (IT 7:5-6). The memory of sin keeps a person lowly before everyone, without sadness (IT 11:12). (*stated*)
4. The Tzemach Tzedek: every 'I' and sense of self is the source of evil and causes separation of hearts (Hayom Yom 10 Tevet). The Alter Rebbe to a gifted newcomer: take out the chametz of selfhood and haughtiness and bring in the matzah of bitul (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus p. 21). (*stated*)
5. The Maggid: holding oneself as 'ayin' keeps one from sin and is complete teshuvah (27:3). (*stated*)
6. R. Nachman agrees that pride is idolatry but gives a different remedy: closeness to tzaddikim, whose spirit subdues the haughty spirit, so that 'acher' becomes 'echad' (Likutei Moharan 10:5). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 22 (pride as idolatry, the husk's 'I and none else'); Tanya 27:3 (sadness itself comes from haughtiness; grep only); IT 7 and 11 (crushing the husk, lowly before all); the chametz and matzah answer (via the Rayatz).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim, Mishpatim (per 00_BITUL_PATH stage 2): two lowlinesses, the crushed pauper and the lowliness of the soul's essence. Earlier work; not re-verified here.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Hayom Yom 10 Tevet: every 'I' is the source of evil and separates hearts.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627: 'making oneself like leftovers' as the sign of forbearance (S136).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (spine F08h): Chassidus without prayer turns into more selfhood. Kuntres HaAvodah: selfhood can strengthen anger (S132).
- **Rayatz.** Transmitted the chametz and matzah answer (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus p. 21) and the fifth way of teshuvah, 'walk humbly', letting nothing stand out (Hayom Yom 8 Tishrei).
- **The Rebbe.** Hayom Yom (19 Iyar, spine F08g): Chassidus shows a person 'how small he is and how high he can rise'. Bitul is not self-crushing.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (27:3): hold oneself as ayin. The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:41, spine): praise God and fault oneself; whoever disparages others separates from the unity. R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 10): pride is idolatry, removed through the tzaddik. Tanya and the Maggid undo pride by contemplation and bitul, R. Nachman by attachment to the tzaddik. The difference is real.

## S132 · Anger · כעס

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F12 Knowing the unity · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** כעס · כועס · רוגז · כאילו עובד

> Anger is the unity of God forgotten in the moment: faith in providence leaves, and a person sees the other as the source of his hurt instead of the word of God that sustains the one who hurts him.

## Derivation

1. 'Whoever is angry is as if he worshipped idols', because in his anger faith has left him. 'Had he believed it was from God, he would not be angry at all' (IH 25:2). (*stated*)
2. The one who strikes or curses has free choice and is liable. Yet the harm to the victim was decreed, and 'there are many messengers' (IH 25:3). The text keeps choice beside decree. Calling this G02 is our reading and a loose one: G02 sets 'nothing before Him' beside 'a real thing below', which is not what IH 25:3 discusses. (**partly stated**)
3. Even at that very moment, the power of God and the breath of His mouth are clothed in the other and sustain him, as with Shimi: 'God told him, curse' (IH 25:4) (F01). (*stated*)
4. The ground is the Besht's reading of 'Your word stands in the heavens', continuous creation from nothing, against the philosophers who deny particular providence (IH 25:6-8). Anger is a lapse of faith (the text says emunah) in the continuous creation that is providence (F12). (*stated*)
5. In practice: remembering one's sin makes one accept everything with joy, 'a good counsel to be saved from anger' (IT 11:13). The beinoni does not let anger into his mind and repays with kindness (Tanya 12:12-13) (F08). (*stated*)
6. The Rashab: general contemplation leaves the angry and cruel as they were, and selfhood can even strengthen it. Only specific toil in prayer on that trait refines it (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan p. 45). (*stated*)
7. Counter-voice, Izhbitz: Cain pleaded that everything, even fear of Heaven, is in Heaven's hands, so his anger overcame him without choice (Beit Yaakov, Bereshit 69:1). The answer in the note there is 'the voice of your brother's blood': the blood cries out, so it was not its time and he had choice. God's 'if you do well' (69:2) is read as Cain, mixed of good and evil, needing to bear constrictions. And 70:1 grants that 'on the face of it' Cain's plea was correct for the first generations, who saw their creation from nothing. So Izhbitz does not simply reject the plea; it holds that the plea did not free Cain. 'Everything is from God' dissolves anger at the other (IH 25) but does not excuse one's own anger, though Izhbitz gives the plea more weight than that summary suggests. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** IH 25 (on a Tzava'at HaRivash saying): anger as loss of faith in providence. IT 7:14 lists anger with slander and haughtiness as idolatry. IT 11:13 gives joy in whatever comes as the counsel against anger. Tanya 12:12-13 describes the beinoni's mastery.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Toras Chaim (line read in grep only): the one 'hard as a cedar' cannot forgive. Not quoted.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627: 'long-suffering is better than a mighty man', subduing the inclination (S136).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah ch. 5 (OCR): anger and cruelty are unchanged by general contemplation and may grow through selfhood; they need specific toil.
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** Tzava'at HaRivash is the source IH 25 explains. R. Nachman (Sefer HaMiddot): anger brings thoughts of idolatry, a parallel without Tanya's reason. Izhbitz (Beit Yaakov): Cain's anger and the misuse of 'all is in Heaven's hands'.

## S133 · Desire · תאוה

**Faces:** F11 Darkness turned to light · F09 The unity of opposites · F01 The world said into being now · F06 The soul as part of the One **Search terms:** תאוה · תאות · תאוות · תאוות עוה"ז

> Desire is the unity of God as love fallen into exile: the pull toward beauty and pleasure is His own love clothed in a physical thing, to be traced back to its root or burned up in longing for Him.

## Derivation

1. The Besht: beauty is the divine power spread in the woman, and without it the face would be as the dead. 'Why be drawn after the part? Better to cleave to the root of all worlds' (Tzava'at HaRivash 90:5). The sweetness of food is its divine vitality (90:7) (F01). (*stated*)
2. The Besht: everything spreads from Him through His traits of love and awe. Love is in exile, clothed in a woman or in food. 'If I love this fallen love, how much more must I love Him' (Tzava'at HaRivash 120:2) (F09, F11). (*stated*)
3. The Maggid: a thought of desire fell from the world of love. Bound to love of the Creator, it returns to its root (Maggid 12:13). (*stated*)
4. The Alter Rebbe draws a boundary. Desire for permitted pleasure is kelipat nogah and can return to holiness. Forbidden desire belongs to the wholly impure husks (Tanya 8:2-3). Holding back even briefly from the permitted subdues the other side and raises God's glory above (Tanya 27:11-12) (F11, as itkafya). (*stated*)
5. The Rashab: contemplating Ein Sof, filling and surrounding all, before whom 'all is as nothing', produces fiery love that burns up the animal soul's desire. In prayer, as an offering, that desire turns into flames of longing 'to cleave to the living God' (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan pp. 26-27). (*stated*)
6. The Mitteler Rebbe: many worldly desires scatter the soul itself into parts ('pizur hanefesh'). Only the simple source-point of all wills, in the soul's essence (yechidah), can uproot and gather them all (Sha'arei Teshuvah 116d-117a) (F06). (*stated*)
7. Contrasting the Besht (trace the desire to its root) with Tanya (first subdue, itkafya) is our characterization. Both are stated, but neither text sets itself against the other. (**reading**)

## S134 · Jealousy and baseless hatred · קנאה ושנאת חנם

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F09 The unity of opposites · F07 The home below **Search terms:** קנאה · שנאת חנם · שנאה · תחרות

> Baseless hatred is the unity of God broken among souls: bodies divided and each 'I' made the root, where the souls, with 'one Father to all', are meant to be bound into one Havayah.

## Derivation

1. All souls are matched and have one Father, so Israel are 'literally brothers' from their root in the one God; only the bodies are divided (Tanya 32:2) (F06). (*stated*)
2. When all souls are joined, the flow circulates 'to bind and join them all to Havayah echad'. The destruction of the Second Temple and the Shechinah's exile came 'from baseless hatred and separation of hearts' (IH 31:8-9). The unity of souls and the dwelling below rise and fall together (F07). (*stated*)
3. The root of hatred is the self: every 'I' and sense of self is the source of evil 'and causes separation of hearts' (the Tzemach Tzedek, Hayom Yom 10 Tevet) (F08). (*stated*)
4. The beinoni lets no grudge, hatred, jealousy or anger into his mind, and bears with the other to the utmost (Tanya 12:12-13). This practical defense does not itself state the unity; linking it is our reading. (**reading**)
5. Even hatred commanded toward an unrepentant sinner coexists with love for 'the hidden good, the spark of Godliness'. 'Both are true', and compassion cancels the hatred (Tanya 32:6). The text holds both toward the same person; F09 speaks of opposites joined in Him, so filing this there is our reading (F09). (**partly stated**)
6. Polonoye: baseless hatred cannot be repaired by any reprover. Only God Himself makes the twelve stones one (Toldot, Vayetzei 77). The Alter Rebbe's saying that love of one's fellow is a vessel for love of God, quoted in the Rashab's explanation of the five ways of teshuvah (Hayom Yom 6 Tishrei). (*stated*)
7. Jealousy as such: no passage found in this pass that ties it to the unity. It appears only listed with hatred and anger (Tanya 12:12). Tying it to the 'I' of Hayom Yom 10 Tevet is our reading. (**reading**)

## S135 · Speech: slander and idle words · לשון הרע ודברים בטלים

**Faces:** F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F11 Darkness turned to light · F12 Knowing the unity **Search terms:** לשון הרע · דברים בטלים · רכילות · שיחה בטלה

> Guarded speech is the unity of God kept in the mouth: slander tears the one body of Israel, and idle words spend on the world the letters that belong to Him.

## Derivation

1. All Israel are 'like one man' joined of many limbs. So an evil thought about a fellow must be pushed out 'like a thought of idolatry', and slander is as grave as idolatry, incest and murder (IH 22:19-21) (F06). (*stated*)
2. Forbidden speech (mockery, slander) belongs to the three wholly impure husks. Idle permitted words belong to nogah, and wasting words where one could learn is the sin of neglecting Torah (Tanya 8:5-8). This classifies speech by the husks; it does not name the unity. The tie runs through Tanya 22's definition of the husk as what is not nullified, which is our joining. (**reading**)
3. Holding one's mouth back from worldly talk the heart craves, even a little, subdues the other side below and raises God's glory above in all the worlds (Tanya 27:12) (F11). (*stated*)
4. The Mitteler Rebbe: excess cleverness in thought, speech and deed in business, and the many forces made by thoughts of idle matters, though not wholly evil, drive away the soul's surrounding levels, chayah and yechidah (Sha'arei Teshuvah 6d:2). The text speaks of thoughts and business more than of speech; applying it to idle talk is a short step of ours (F06). (*stated*)
5. The Besht: seeing another's sin, 'guard your tongue', then include him with you 'through unity, for all are one man' (Keter Shem Tov 1:59). Tzava'at HaRivash 133: silence is better even than Torah talk with people, so as to think of His greatness and bind oneself to Him (F12). (*stated*)
6. Idle speech as such is framed by the husks and by devekut rather than by the unity itself. Calling it 'spending His letters' draws on G01 and is our reading. (**reading**)

## S136 · Forbearance · מעביר על מדותיו

**Faces:** F05 The Essence beyond both · F02 The higher and the lower unity · F09 The unity of opposites · F01 The world said into being now **Search terms:** מעביר על מדותיו · ארך אפים · סבלנות · מאריך אף

> Forbearance is the unity of God as passing beyond one's own measures to the place above measure, where His forgiveness keeps no count.

## Derivation

1. His forgiveness makes no difference between once and a thousand times, because His traits are infinite and before Ein Sof small and great are the same (IT 11:7) (F02). (*stated*)
2. The human side: remembering one's sin, one accepts with joy whatever comes, from Heaven or from people, like 'those insulted who do not insult'. 'Whoever passes over his traits, all his sins are passed over' (IT 11:13). IT 11:13 grounds this in the memory of sin and joy, not in 11:7's infinity; the 'hence' joining them is ours. (**partly stated**)
3. Chernobyl: teshuvah preceded the world and is above the traits, where there is no judgment, only will and kindness. Whoever passes over his traits for one who angered him 'goes above the traits to the place of teshuvah', and so all is forgiven (Me'or Einayim, Ha'Azinu) (F05). (*stated*)
4. The Maharash: forgiveness comes from Arich Anpin, above the measuring line. 'With all your might', beyond one's limit, awakens the true limitlessness of Ein Sof; that is passing over one's traits, and it makes the soul a vessel for that forgiveness (Toras Shmuel 5627, Mi El Kamocha). (*stated*)
5. R. Nachman: the main teshuvah is to hear one's shame and be silent, which is Keter, 'Ehyeh'. The repair is to be among those who hear insult and do not answer (Likutei Moharan 6:2). (*stated*)
6. The beinoni bears with his fellow to the utmost and repays with kindness (Tanya 12:13). Joining this to IH 25's providence (the other is sustained by God's word even as he insults, S132) is ours; Tanya 12 itself gives no reason from the unity (F01). (**partly stated**)

## S137 · Toil · יגיעה

**Faces:** F05 The Essence beyond both · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F06 The soul as part of the One · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** יגיעה · ביגיעה · יגעת ומצאת · יגיעת נפש

> Toil is the unity of God as the only way to draw out what is hidden 'not as an existing thing': the Essence rooted in the soul and in Torah, struck out like fire from flint.

## Derivation

1. 'Avodah' applies only to what a person does 'with immense toil against his nature', nullifying his nature and will before the supreme will (IH 12:15). Toil is bitul in action (F08). (*stated*)
2. One must dig 'with immense toil' to uncover the treasure of awe hidden in the understanding of every Jewish heart: 'I toiled and found, believe it' (Tanya 42:8) (F06). (*stated*)
3. The Rashab: a dominant trait not yet refined cannot be refined by easy general work, only by 'toil of flesh and toil of soul' (Kuntres HaAvodah, scan p. 45). (*stated*)
4. The Rebbe (Rosh Hashanah 5741), crediting the Rashab's discourses for the flint and coal: the soul's root is in the Essence, a concealment 'that is not in existence', and drawing from such concealment 'requires toil specifically', as fire is struck from flint, while fire hidden in a coal needs only blowing. He says this of God's 'breathing with force' (the Alter Rebbe on 'He blew into his nostrils'), that is, of the soul's drawing-down, not of human toil. Applying it to the person's toil is our reading (F05). (**reading**)
5. The Rebbe: because 'the Essence is found' in Torah, toil in it without limit follows, and 'you toiled and found' is promised (Likkutei Sichos 35, Chanukah). (*stated*)
6. The Rayatz: first toil the mind to understand as far as one can, then believe the rest in simple faith (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus p. 14) (F12). Sfas Emes: Torah is hidden in every place, and by toil one finds everywhere its light and the inner vitality from the Life of life (Toldot 5650) (F03). (*stated*)
7. The explicit tie of human toil to the Essence is late and single: the Rebbe's Likkutei Sichos 35 (the Essence is in Torah, hence toil in it). The 5741 maamar ties 'toil' to the Essence-root only for the soul's drawing-down by God. Earlier texts tie toil to bitul and to uncovering hidden awe, not to the Essence. Reading the earlier texts through the later is ours. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** IH 12:15 (avodah as toil against nature); Tanya 42 (toil of soul to find hidden awe, 'yagati u-matzati').
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaAvodah: 'toil of flesh and toil of soul' for the unrefined trait. The Rebbe credits his discourses with the flint and coal analysis (not read here).
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus p. 14: toil the mind, then believe.
- **The Rebbe.** Rosh Hashanah 5741: drawing from the Essence-root 'not in existence' needs toil, said of God's breathing the soul 'with force', not of human work. Likkutei Sichos 35: toil in Torah because the Essence is in it. Shavuot 5728 (grep only): through toil in the details of the Oral Torah one reaches above the general.
- **Other schools.** Sfas Emes (Toldot 5633, 5650): the wells of the patriarchs, and finding Torah's hidden vitality in every place by toil; nullifying one's own will as the weekday 'well'. Toil elsewhere (Beit Yaakov: 'the toil of your hands') is framed by choice, not by the unity.
