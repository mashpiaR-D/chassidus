## The shape of the range

Range 6 showed that man is the purpose of creation. This range asks what that means in practice.

1. **Why the angels opposed man** (1116-1144). Because man can do what they cannot: *unite* G-d, not only praise Him. Israel's Shema is dearer than the angels' "Holy".
2. **Only G-d can make us righteous** (1145-1160). We cannot know His will by our own reason. He gives it to us, and doing it makes us like Him.
3. **Man rules nature when he keeps his image** (1161-1183). Sin is what made the gap between man and his Maker.
4. **Miracles** (1184-1276). A long debate with the philosophers (Ralbag, Crescas, Bibago, Abravanel, Arama) on whether the sun stood still for Joshua. Inside it are some of the book's deepest lines on prayer: prayer that reaches **the source of the Will** (makor haratzon), and the daily prayer for bread as a way to bless G-d each day.

## The unity of G-d

**Praise and unity are not the same.** When the angels asked "what is man?", G-d answered:

> מה שאתם מקלסין אותי בעליונים הוא מיחד אותי בתחתונים (Avodat HaKodesh 3:7:2; line 1117)
> What you praise Me with above, he unites Me with below.

Praise tells of the One praised "because of a lack of knowing" His qualities (line 1122). Uniting does more. So Moses brought the angels' line "Blessed be the Name of His glorious kingdom" down to Israel, "to move it from light praise to weighty unity" (להעתיקו משבח קל אל ייחוד חמור, line 1130).

**Unity was given only to Israel, and it depends on them.**

> והנה זה פלא כי הייחוד באלהות לא נמסר כי אם לישראל, שאלהותו יתברך מתגלה ונודע על ידם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:7:9; line 1124)
> Here is a wonder: the unity of the Godhead was given only to Israel, for His Godhead is revealed and known through them.

The Sifre: "'You are My witnesses, and I am G-d': when you are My witnesses, I am G-d; and when you are not My witnesses, as it were, I am not G-d" (line 1124). At Sinai, when they answered "the Lord our G-d, the Lord is One", they meant "that they are included in the Lord, and He is one and unique in all" (שהם נכללים ביי' והוא אחד ומיוחד בכל, line 1127).

*Ours.* "Included in the Lord": this is the Shema as self-nullifying inclusion (bittul), the heart of the Chabad reading (Tanya ch. 19-20 and Shaar HaYichud). Ibn Gabbai sees it already in the Sinai answer.

**Unity never ends, so service never ends.**

> וכמו שאין סוף לייחודו, כן אין קץ וזמן אל העבודה, כלומר שתכלית העובד במציאותו לייחד תמיד את השם באין הפסק (Avodat HaKodesh 3:7:18; line 1133)
> And as there is no end to His unity, so there is no end and no time to the service; that is, the purpose of the servant in his being is to unite the Name always, without pause.

That is why Israel may sing at any hour, and angels only once a day or once in eternity (line 1132).

**Fewer screens, more seeing.** Angels say the Name after three words ("Holy, holy, holy, the Lord"); Israel after two ("Hear, O Israel, the Lord"). Each word is a screen (mechitzah) (line 1134). The angels see G-d past three screens: He is their Maker; He keeps them in being; and He is "found with them in a bond of existence, no more", with no mixing (line 1135). That is why they cover their faces and feet and fly (line 1136). Israel sees past two: "Hear" is study, "Israel" is deed (line 1137). *Ours.* This is an unusual and usable teaching. The angels' awe is the awe of distance. Israel's unity is closer.

**Uniting below means uniting oneself.**

> ועוד כי הייחוד צריך בתחתונים להיות האדם מורכב ולהשלים עצמו וליחד חלקיו לקונו (Avodat HaKodesh 3:7:25; line 1140)
> And also, the unity is needed below, because man is composite, and must complete himself and unite his parts to his Maker.

"With all your heart: with both your inclinations", and "what is asked of him is to raise his body to the spirit in him" (line 1140). And we must unite Him "among those who cut apart", for the nations try to pull Israel from G-d, "and still they do not leave Him, and unite Him twice every day" (line 1141).

**Doing His will renews His kingship each day.** "Whenever they do His will ... it is counted as if they renew His kingship over them then and accept His Godhead" (line 1150). And "the Lord, He is His Name, for He and His Name are one thing" (הוא ושמו דבר אחד, line 1244).

**He still stretches out the heavens.** "And spreads them out" is in the present, "for He still stretches them, in that He keeps them and gives them being always" (שעדין הוא נוטה אותם במה שהוא מקיימם ונותן להם ההויה תמיד, line 1243). *Ours.* This is F01: creation is constant. The Baal Shem Tov's "Your word stands in the heavens" and Shaar HaYichud ch. 1-2 make the same point from the same kind of verse.

**Nothing outside the unity.** "All is in all, and all is within the unity and the Godhead; nothing goes outside" (כי הכל בכל והכל בכלל הייחוד והאלהות אין דבר יוצא חוצה, line 1210).

## The source of the Will: prayer that reaches the root

This is the most important passage of the range for meditation.

Miracles happen, the Zohar says, when the light of the Aleph (the head of Thought, pure mercy) reaches the Zayin (the end of Thought). "So everything depends on the unity":

> והזוכה ויודע ליחד ולקשר ראש המחשבה עם סופה וסופה עם ראשה, הנה הוא ימשול בעליונים ובתחתונים ויוציאם ממנהגם וישנם מתפקידם כי בתפלתו פותח שערי עולם הרחמים ומגיע עד מקור הרצון אשר ממנו נתחדש העולם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:11:10; line 1214)
> One who merits and knows how to unite and bind the head of the Thought with its end, and its end with its head, will rule over the upper and lower ones and move them out of their custom, for in his prayer he opens the gates of the world of mercy and reaches the source of the Will from which the world was made new.

The Will does not change, "for it is a will without limit" (line 1214). He quotes R. Yosef Gikatilla's Shaarei Orah:

> המגיע למקום הרצון הרי הכל בידו לעשות כפי רצונו וחפצו (Avodat HaKodesh 3:11:11; line 1215)
> One who reaches the place of the Will, everything is in his hand to do as he wills and desires.

"This is the secret of why we always pray 'may it be Your will' (yehi ratzon)": so that the thing we pray for will be made new "from the place of the Will and the Thought, as the world was made new from that source" (line 1215). And "he draws out favor from the Lord" (Prov. 8:35) means "he brings out and draws his wish from the source of the Will, from Ein Sof". "One who understands this will understand how great is the trust of the righteous" (line 1216). The parable: a room full of every kind of thing; whoever has the key takes what he needs (line 1216).

*Ours.* Chassidus builds on exactly this. The Will (ratzon) is Keter, above wisdom; prayer reaches "the inner will" (pnimiyut haratzon), where a new will can come about. Chabad maamarim often teach that prayer "draws a new will" from the Essence, and that is why prayer can change what is decreed. Ibn Gabbai's line is almost the same: prayer reaches "the source of the Will from which the world was made new". The difference: ibn Gabbai calls it "no change of will", since the source is above change, and Chassidus says it is a *new* will from above the will. The picture for the guide is the same: **reaching the source of the Will**.

**Why the Will allows miracles.** The simple Will "wants always the good and completeness of His creatures, for He made them for this: that they be always 'for a will' before Him, that is, fearing and uniting His Name always without pause" (line 1217). Nature running alone would make people forget; so miracles renew the purpose (line 1217). A miracle "is a new creation" like the first, so that "this one will come and bear witness to that one" (line 1220, 1228).

**The world stays as long as the partnership holds.** "Conditions" G-d made with creation are like terms between two partners: "as long as they do His will, they are fit to exist" (line 1221). When people broke the terms, Abraham "filled in" for all creation, "and he was a vessel of atonement and a place of existence for all creation" (line 1222). *Ours.* This picture matters: I am a partner, and my service keeps the partnership, and so the world, standing.

**Sing, and His throne is set.** "Before You made Your world You were ... but only when we stood at the sea and said song before You was Your kingship settled and Your throne set" (line 1228).

## Daily bread: blessing G-d each day

A small teaching with a large reach. The Zohar says each person of faith should ask G-d for his food every day, even if he has food ready:

> בגין דכל מאן דמצלי צלותיה לגבי קב"ה על מזוניה גרים דיתברך כל יומא (Avodat HaKodesh 3:12:16; line 1254)
> For whoever prays his prayer to the Holy One for his food causes Him to be blessed every day.

Ibn Gabbai explains: prayer with intention opens the upper source, "the secret of the Will and the blessing", and each of the upper days is blessed from it (line 1255). So one should not cook today for tomorrow, "so that his eyes will be lifted up and he will pray for his food, and the blessing will be found above" (כדי שיהיו עיניו נשואות למעלה ויתפלל על מזונו, line 1255). Like the manna, gathered day by day.

Then Shammai and Hillel. Shammai prepared for Shabbat all week. "But Hillel had another trait, for all his deeds were for the sake of Heaven, as it says 'Blessed is the Lord day by day'." Ibn Gabbai:

> וכל מעשיו היה מכוין בהם צורך גבוה לא צרכו בלבד (Avodat HaKodesh 3:12:18; line 1256)
> And in all his deeds he intended the need on high, not only his own need.

*Ours.* This is a real practice for the guide: **the daily ask**. Ask G-d each day for today's needs, even when they are covered, to open the day's blessing above. The Talmud says the opposite fear is small faith: "whoever has bread in his basket and says 'what will I eat tomorrow' is of little faith" (Sotah 48b). The guide's version has no worry in it: the ask is for His sake, so He is blessed today.

## G-d's closeness: providence without a middleman

**His humility is His care.** "Wherever you find the might of the Holy One, you find His humility" (line 1247). Ibn Gabbai: the sages said this against the philosophers who hold that He does not know or watch the low things. "With all the height of His level, His humility is with Him, and His eyes and heart are to do kindness, justice and righteousness on earth" (line 1248).

**Like a judge who hears with his own ears.** A judge may not hear the case through an interpreter; so G-d's providence "is not through a middleman, but He Himself in His glory is the Judge and the Watcher" (line 1249). When Israel do His will "He leads them Himself in His glory"; even in exile, "the Shechinah is with them" (line 1251).

**He is in trouble with us.** "When a person suffers, what does the Shechinah say? 'I am lighter than my head, I am lighter than my arm'" (line 1253). The Shechinah is "the redeeming angel" who goes into exile with Israel, "and when He comes to redeem Himself He redeems Israel" (line 1253). *Ours.* The guide can hold this for a person in pain: He is in it with you (S-level "not alone in the pain"; ties to "your pain set aside for His", range 3).

## Man's image and sin

> שקודם שחטא כביכול לא היה הפרש בינו לבין קונו, והעון הבדיל ביניהם (Avodat HaKodesh 3:9:3; line 1163)
> For before he sinned, as it were, there was no difference between him and his Maker, and the sin separated them.

Solomon first ruled over the upper ones, then the lower ones, then Israel, then Jerusalem, then his bed, and at last only his staff (line 1163). "If the righteous wished, they could create a world" (line 1164).

**Be like Him: this is why you were made.** "Walk in His ways": "for this he was created, that the form be like its Former" (כי לזה נברא שידמה הצורה ליוצרה, line 1151). "As He is called merciful, you be merciful" (line 1152). The soul came from Binah, where mercy and kindness are, "so he must be like the place he came from, and was made in its likeness, and by this he bears witness to his source" (line 1153).

## Wonder, awe, and song

> ולפי שהפלאים והבחינה בהם פתח אל היראה, והבחינה הזאת תביא הדבקות בעושה הנפלאות, ועל הדבקים תשרה רוח הקדש (Avodat HaKodesh 3:11:3; line 1207)
> Because wonders and the contemplation of them are a door to awe, and this contemplation brings clinging to the One who does the wonders, and on those who cling the holy spirit rests.

At the sea: "Israel saw the great hand ... and the people feared the Lord and believed", and then they sang (line 1207). *Ours.* A clear ladder for meditation: **wonder, awe, clinging, song**. It ties to the Rambam's own path to love and awe (contemplating creation, Yesodei HaTorah 2:2) and to Chabad's use of creation meditation (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 1-2). The guide can use it with any wonder, a birth, a storm, the night sky.

**The music of the spheres and the soul.** From Livnat HaSapir: the soul, "cut from the bundle of life, is used to the songs of the angels and the song of the spheres"; so when it hears music in the body it finds rest, "and from the great pleasure it is fit for the spirit of G-d to rest on it" (line 1188). "A person must wake himself in Torah and prayer for the holy spirit to rest on him" (line 1188). *Ours.* This is why a niggun (wordless melody) opens the heart. Chabad uses the niggun before prayer and meditation (Sefer HaNiggunim; the saying, often told in Chabad, that melody is "the pen of the soul").

**The sun's song.** Every moment the sun moves it praises G-d, "and every hour that it praises it has strength to stand" (line 1185); its song is "Give thanks to the Lord, call on His Name" (line 1195). Abraham's pearl, which healed every sick one who saw it, was "his tongue, with which he proclaimed His Godhead and unity"; after his death G-d hung it on the sun, so that the sun would announce the unity (line 1196).

## States of consciousness

- **Included in the One** (line 1127). At the Shema, "they are included in the Lord, and He is one and unique in all". Ties to S-level bittul states (Tanya ch. 19-20). New as a named state here, proposed below.
- **Reaching the source of the Will** (lines 1214-1216). A state of prayer: the sense of having gone past words to the place where the world is made new. Proposed below. Ties to Chabad's pnimiyut haratzon.
- **The trust of the righteous** (line 1216). Calm certainty that comes from knowing the Will has no limit. Ties to bitachon states (Shaar HaBitachon; Tzemach Tzedek "think good and it will be good").
- **Wonder into song** (line 1207). Proposed below as a technique.
- **He is in it with me** (line 1253). Ties to "your pain set aside for His" (range 3).

## Meditation and practice

- **The daily ask** (lines 1254-1256). Proposed below.
- **Wonder, awe, clinging, song** (line 1207). Proposed below.
- **Yehi ratzon with the whole heart** (line 1215). When saying "may it be Your will", aim at the source, above all limits.
- **A melody first** (line 1188). Begin prayer or meditation with a niggun, to let the soul remember its song.
- **Be like Him today** (lines 1151-1153). Choose one of His ways (mercy, kindness, patience) and walk in it for a day.

## The big human questions

**What is man for?** To unite what the angels can only praise (line 1117).

**Can prayer change things?** Yes. Prayer that reaches the source of the Will draws from where the world is made new (line 1214).

**Does G-d care about small things?** His humility is His care, and He does it Himself, not through a messenger (lines 1248-1249).

**Why do I need to ask for what I already have?** So He is blessed today (line 1255).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 1145-1149 and 1157-1160 (the reading of Eliphaz's speech in Job), 1166-1179 (the three dominions and the survey of philosophers' views of Joshua's sun), 1180-1183 and 1187-1200 (whether angels change, and whether the spheres make sound), 1209-1212 and 1239-1242 (against the "active intellect" of the Arab philosophers), and 1257-1276 (the debate with Abravanel and Arama on Moses' miracles and when prophets needed prayer) were read in full. They serve the argument above.
