## What this cluster shows

These eleven subjects are the service of the heart. Here the one idea stops being described and is enacted. If there is nothing besides Him, prayer cannot be one self petitioning another; the sources rebuild it as the essence of the soul joining the Essence of Ein Sof ('to Him and not to His attributes', Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei). It draws 'the light itself', which alone is omnipotent, into the created world (Kuntres Acharon 4), and joins the filling light to the surrounding light (the Rebbe, 10 Shevat 5730). Intention is union with the Will that is one with Him (Tanya 38). Contemplation is the unity known in detail and carried into the heart, down to the lectern (the Alter Rebbe's answer, via the Rayatz). The Shema is the higher and the lower unity in the liturgy's own words (SH 1, 7). The Amidah is bitul before the Essence 'found in every place', speaking with lips He opens (Kuntres HaTefillah; Likkutei Torah). The order of prayer is a ladder whose rungs, as the Rashab lays them out, contemplate creation, then 'Havayah is One' and Baruch Shem, and end in bitul (Hemshech Ayin Beis 382; Toras Shmuel 5627); that these rungs are our faces F01, F02 and F08 is our mapping. Blessing is 'baruch', drawing Havayah down until it is 'You' (Torah Ohr), and the Besht has it wake the creative word inside the food. Tikkun chatzot is grief over the hiding, for the sake of light out of darkness (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7; Tanya 26). The tie is stated, at least in part, for every subject except dance, and unevenly. It is strong and stated for prayer, intention, contemplation, the Shema, the Amidah, the ladder and blessings. It is stated in part for melody (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 30; Likutei Moharan 64), midnight lament and Psalms, where the customs themselves are framed by merit and power, not by the unity. For hitbodedut the tie is stated strongly by R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 52: hitbodedut, then bitul, then inclusion in the Necessary Existent), which corrects the spine's §5, while the Rebbe subordinates withdrawal to the dwelling below. For dance none was found.

## S101 · Prayer · תפילה

**Faces:** F05 The Essence beyond both · F06 The soul as part of the One · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** תפילה · תפלה · עבודה שבלב · בשעת התפלה

> Prayer is the unity of God as the essence of the soul joining the Essence of Ein Sof, so that the light that 'alone is omnipotent' is drawn down to change the created world.

## Derivation

1. If there is nothing besides Him, then to whom does one pray, and for what? The Alter Rebbe answers from 'to Him and not to His attributes': the 'Him' is the Essence of Ein Sof, known to every simple Jew by faith, and prayer in simple faith 'joins the essence of the soul with the Essence of Ein Sof' (Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei). (*stated*)
2. What prayer does differs from Torah and mitzvot. They add light by clothing it; prayer draws 'the light itself' of Ein Sof into Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah to change creatures from what they are, since only He 'alone is omnipotent' (Kuntres Acharon 4:4-5). The request is a request for the Essence's power in the finite (F05). (*stated*)
3. That drawing needs an ascent from below: 'mayin nukvin' in the mind and heart, the unbounded love called 'with all your might' (Kuntres Acharon 4:6-8). (*stated*)
4. For the person, prayer is self-offering. The thought is clothed in the letters of prayer, which are God's word, 'and they become literally one' (Tanya 41:23). The Besht puts it as not feeling one's own existence in prayer (Tzava'at HaRivash 62); regarding oneself as a thing is the opening for foreign thoughts (F08). (*stated*)
5. The one who prays is 'a part of God above'. So one does not pray for one's own lack but for the lack in the whole, the Shechinah (Tzava'at HaRivash 73; Maggid 6:3, 67:3) (F06). (*stated*)
6. The Rebbe, from the Tzemach Tzedek, reads the word itself: tefillah is 'joining'. It joins Malchus, the filling light, with the surrounding light, so that 'Havayah becomes your Elokim, your strength and life'. The joining is done by the Essence, which is above both, and reveals the yechidah (maamar of 10 Shevat 5730) (F04, G14). (*stated*)
7. Setting these side by side as one chain, from the Besht's bitul to the Rebbe's joining of sovev and memale, is our arrangement, not a sequence any text lays out. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Kuntres Acharon 4: prayer draws Or Ein Sof itself into the lower worlds and changes creatures. Tanya 41: prayer as self-offering, thought one with the letters. Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei (his Motzaei Yom Kippur 5556 teaching): prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul with the Essence. Tension within his own writing: Tanya 23:12 ranks Torah study above prayer ('the unification of the upper worlds'), while Kuntres Acharon 8:3 makes prayer the main service in the footsteps of Messiah (S106).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut and Shaar HaYichud make prayer the setting of contemplation (S103). His own Rosh Hashanah prayer contemplated the bitul of Adam Kadmon (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus, OCR; S103).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah: a treatise on the root of prayer. Prayer is 'to Him', the Essence, even where Names are intended (7); the Shema draws from Ein Sof itself, and the Amidah draws that light into the lower worlds (16-17; S104, S105). The Rebbe cites his maamar on 'You shall serve Havayah your God' for tefillah as joining sovev and memale.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627: prayer is the ladder joining the ray of the soul in the body to its root in 'ayin' (S106).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR): the refining of the natural traits happens only through the work of prayer, not through study alone (scan p. 20). Without prayer, learned Chassidus becomes more yeshus (spine F08h). The Amidah is inner bitul before the Essence 'found in every place' (S105).
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus scan p. 7 (OCR) and Hayom Yom 21 Cheshvan: 'the work of prayer brings the mind's comprehension into the heart's feeling', and both together into action. He preserved the Alter Rebbe's 'I prayed with the lectern' (S103).
- **The Rebbe.** Maamar of 10 Shevat 5730 (Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat): tefillah as joining Malchus and sovev, done by the Essence and revealing the yechidah. He compiled Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei. On action and spreading outward as against the Rashab's emphasis on prayer, see the spine's disagreements.
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov (Tzava'at HaRivash 62, 73): stripped of physicality, pray for the Shechinah's lack, since man is a part of God. The Maggid (6:3): pray always for the Shechinah's redemption, since Torah and the Holy One are one; a self-interested mitzvah makes a kelipah of separation. The Polish and Galician schools (pillar 11: Kozhnitz, Sfas Emes, Radomsk, Izhbitz, Radzin) build prayer's aim and limits largely without stating the tie to the Essence; for them the tie is our reading, and it was not re-verified in this pass.

## Thin or forced?

No. The tie is stated in so many words by the Alter Rebbe (Kuntres Acharon 4; Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei), the Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 73) and the Rebbe (10 Shevat 5730). What is open is prayer's rank against Torah study, which is recorded, not resolved.

## S102 · Intention · כוונה

**Faces:** F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual · F05 The Essence beyond both · F12 Knowing the unity **Search terms:** כוונה · כונה · כוונות · כוונת הלב

> Intention is the unity of God as the Will He is one with, clothed in the letters one says, so that every Jew, with or without the kabbalists' kavanot, calls 'to Him and not to His attributes'.

## Derivation

1. A mitzvah or a prayer without intention is 'a body without a soul' (Tanya 38:3). The Alter Rebbe draws the analogy from creation itself: God gives life to stone and soul alike, yet the two lights do not compare (G06). (*stated*)
2. What intention adds is union with the Will: one intends 'to cleave to Him by fulfilling His will, for He and His will are one' (Tanya 38:8). Intention is where the unity of Torah and mitzvah (F10) becomes the unity of the person with it. (*stated*)
3. Intention clothes itself in the letters it causes and lifts them to its own world, where Or Ein Sof, the supernal Will, shines (Tanya 40:7-8). The Besht puts it more simply: God is clothed in the letters, and by speaking them 'a oneness is made with Him' (Tzava'at HaRivash 108). (*stated*)
4. Its object is the Essence, not an attribute. 'Every Jew calls to the very Essence', and intending a Name is not praying to a sefirah, since the Names signify the Essence clothed in the attributes (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh 7-8) (F05). (*stated*)
5. Its core, distilled from any contemplation of filling and surrounding light, is 'all before Him is as nothing' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 14:3) (F12). (*stated*)
6. So the detailed Lurianic kavanot are one route, not the substance. General intention that one's prayer be heard 'with all the kavanot' suffices for one who cannot hold them (Hayom Yom 11 Adar I). That this follows from the Essence being the single object of every kavanah is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 38-41: intention as the soul of the mitzvah, union with the Will that is one with Him; the true intention, present in every Jew, is to give Him 'nachat ruach' by returning the spark to its source (41:27-28). Kuntres Acharon 3: prayer without intention is 'pushed down', yet is easily repaired by a single prayer said with intention, and even a disqualified prayer ranks above Torah learned for self-aggrandizement, which stays 'under the sun'.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 14: 'the intention in the heart' is a stage above the heart's arousal, and its root is 'all before Him is as nothing'.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 7-8: 'to Him, not to His attributes'. He quotes the Rivash's objection to kabbalists who direct blessings to sefirot, then defends them: the flow is from the Creator alone even when it passes through a sefirah, and the king-and-minister parable 'does not match the thing'.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR) warns against inventing new interpretations of the prayer words during prayer, especially for the young (scan p. 13).
- **Rayatz.** Hayom Yom 11 Adar I and 2 Adar I (compiled from him): general intention suffices; the Alter Rebbe's service needs no overturning of mountains, since any act with true intention is enough, 'a blessing with intention, a word of prayer as it should be, knowing before Whom you stand'.
- **The Rebbe.** Compiled the Hayom Yom entries. Inyanah 11:6: the soul's essential thanks (Modeh Ani) needs no Name at all (S108).
- **Other schools.** Baal Shem Tov: God is clothed in the letters, and so speaking makes a unity with Him (Tzava'at HaRivash 108). If one cannot pray with intention, know that the guard is heavy because the King is there (72) (F03). The Maggid: praying 'according to Kabbalah', every word is a Name; praying plainly, only the Name gives life to the words (6:3). Setting this against the Rivash's plain prayer and Hayom Yom's general intention as a different ideal is our comparison. The Yismach Moshe on Chanah (pillar 11): the laws of voice exist to arouse intention. Not re-verified here.

## S103 · Contemplation · התבוננות

**Faces:** F12 Knowing the unity · F04 Filling and surrounding · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** התבוננות · יתבונן · להתבונן · ההתבוננות

> Contemplation is the unity of God as known in detail and carried from the mind into the heart, until the lowest thing, a lectern, is seen coming from the Essence.

## Derivation

1. The unity must be known, not only believed, and 'returned to the heart' (SH 1; spine F12). The spine keeps the disagreement on this: the Alter Rebbe leaves what is beyond comprehension to faith (SH 7, 9), and the Maharash puts the higher knowledge in faith, while the Rayatz allows no exemption from knowing. Contemplation is how that happens: all study is preparation for the main thing, contemplation in prayer, and it must be detailed, since general contemplation only touches the mind from outside (Kuntres HaTefillah scan p. 11, OCR). (*stated*)
2. It moves by stages: contemplation in study, before prayer, and in prayer, 'the rungs of the ladder of feeling'. In prayer its content is to feel the Godliness in what one learned (Hayom Yom 20 Tammuz). (*stated*)
3. Its material is the order of worlds, particular by particular, joined to the whole: contemplating every detail from Malchus of Ein Sof to Malchus of Asiyah draws everything to 'the Essence of the light before the tzimtzum' (Shaar HaYichud 7:29) (G04). (*stated*)
4. Its object can be the highest or the lowest. The Mitteler Rebbe prayed with the bitul of Adam Kadmon; the Alter Rebbe prayed 'with the lectern', since the physical comes from the Essence (Kuntres Toras HaChassidus, OCR) (G05, F05). (*stated*)
5. It must become habit: seeing heaven and earth as the King's outer garments (Tanya 42:19). Its core is 'all before Him is as nothing' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 14:3) (F04). (*stated*)
6. It ends in bitul, where 'all the senses are nullified and one does not feel oneself' (Kuntres HaTefillah scan p. 10). Then it ends in action: prayer brings comprehension into feeling and both into deed (Hayom Yom 21 Cheshvan) (F08, G11). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 41-44: fixed times to contemplate God's greatness; 42:19 habituation, 'emunah' as training. Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah is itself the content to be contemplated (spine F12). Prayed 'with the lectern'.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** The systematizer. Shaar HaYichud 7: join every detail to the whole and to the Essence; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut classifies the states contemplation produces. He contemplated Adam Kadmon's bitul (the Rayatz's report).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh 39: 'every thinking person can deepen his mind in the Creator's greatness', that all the life of the worlds is a mere ray of His Essence (read, not quoted).
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass beyond the spine's two knowledges (F02).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR): detailed, lengthy contemplation in prayer is the main thing; general contemplation produces 'false imagining'. He warns those who deal only with the deepest matters (spine limits). Hemshech Ayin Beis 382: the ordered ascent (S106).
- **Rayatz.** Kuntres Toras HaChassidus (OCR): the lectern story. Hayom Yom 20 Tammuz: three contemplations as rungs, and Godliness sometimes felt without work 'because of the Essence in the soul'.
- **The Rebbe.** Maamar of Kedoshim 5714: a person at the lowest point must also contemplate his own low state, since the ascent is from 'earthward' (S106).
- **Other schools.** R. Aharon of Strashelye (Sha'arei Avodah 34:1-2): contemplating in depth that there is nothing besides Him and that He is in all worlds without change gives birth to love and awe; felt arousal strengthens understanding rather than weakening it. The earlier hisbonenus study records that his emphasis runs close to the reverse of the received summary (Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md). Outside the Chabad sphere, systematic contemplation of the unity in prayer is rare. Breslov's counterpart is hitbodedut and simple faith (S107).

## S104 · The Shema · קריאת שמע

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F12 Knowing the unity **Search terms:** קריאת שמע · שמע ישראל · ברוך שם כבוד · קבלת עול מלכות שמים

> The Shema is the unity of God declared in both its forms, the higher ('Havayah is One') and the lower ('Blessed be the Name…'), and the soul's inclusion in that unity by accepting His kingship.

## Derivation

1. The Zohar's pair opens Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah: the verse 'Shema Yisrael' is the higher unity, and 'Baruch Shem' the lower unity (SH 1:1, 7:1). F02 takes its two names from these two lines of the liturgy (that the whole face, including the Maharash's two knowledges, is a commentary on them would be our reading). (*stated*)
2. The first verse: after contemplating every detail, everything is drawn to the Essence before the tzimtzum, the simple unity of 'before One, what do you count' (Shaar HaYichud 7:29-31). Contemplating how He is one 'as before the world was created, without any change' is the first self-offering, 'to give one's soul in Echad' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh 41). (*stated*)
3. Baruch Shem: Malchus clothed in Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah, where the created yesh is 'as nothing before Him' from above and truly separate from below (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh 23, quoting the Alter Rebbe) (G02). (*stated*)
4. For the soul, the Shema is the daily exodus: accepting His kingship 'draws His unity upon oneself explicitly', and the moment one wills it and says 'Havayah our God, Havayah is One', the soul is automatically included in His unity (Tanya 47:1-4) (F08). (*stated*)
5. What is accepted is kingship, 'equal for every Jew': a Master who unifies His kingship upon each one (Kuntres HaTefillah scan p. 28, OCR). That passage speaks of kabbalat ol in general, as the ground of self-scrutiny, and does not name the Shema; applying it here is ours. The Amidah's bowing repeats in deed what the Shema accepted in speech (Tanya 42:20; S105). (*stated*)
6. Outside Chabad: Israel's testimony in the Shema is that even nature is one, 'Havayah is One', the One who brings all into being (Sefat Emet, Vaetchanan 16:2) (F01). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** SH 1 and 7: Shema is the higher unity, Baruch Shem the lower. Tanya 47: kabbalat ol in the Shema draws His unity upon one and includes the soul in it. Tanya 49: the two blessings before it are the rungs (S106). Kuntres Acharon 8: pesukei deZimra and the blessings bring the hidden love to revelation in the Shema.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 7:29-33: the first verse is the simple unity before the first tzimtzum; Baruch Shem is Malchus of Ein Sof after it, down to Malchus of Asiyah.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 16-41: through the Shema one draws from 'the source of all', Or Ein Sof itself ('to Him and not to His attributes'); Baruch Shem is the lower unity; there are two self-offerings, in 'Echad' and in 'with all your soul'. Its letters: alef (the Essence), chet (Chochmah), dalet (speech, Malchus) (19).
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass for the Shema itself. His two knowledges (spine F02) are the same pair.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR): contemplating the first and second verses of the Shema is among the standard contemplations. Kabbalat ol is equal for every Jew. Hemshech Ayin Beis 382: in the Shema and its blessings one contemplates 'Havayah is One' and Baruch Shem (S106).
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Not quoted here. Inyanah on Modeh Ani (S108) is his parallel demonstration from the liturgy.
- **Other schools.** Sfas Emes (Sefat Emet, Vaetchanan 16:2-3): the Shema testifies that even nature is one; the testimony is credible only through self-offering ('with all your soul'), which removes any interest of one's own. The Maggid and the Besht: not quoted on the Shema in this pass. R. Aharon of Strashelye (Sha'arei Avodah 33:7): the Shema's self-offering 'through contemplation' still keeps a hold of feeling the self, so a self-offering without contemplation (nefilat apayim) is needed after the Amidah (S105).

## S105 · The Amidah · שמונה עשרה

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F05 The Essence beyond both · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** שמונה עשרה · תפלת שמונה עשרה · תפלת העמידה · שפתי תפתח

> The Amidah is the unity of God as standing, nullified, before the Essence 'found in every single place', letting His speech open one's lips, and drawing His light into the lower worlds as healing and blessing.

## Derivation

1. After the Shema's contemplation comes standing: its specific preparation is to consider that one stands before the King of kings and speaks to Him face to face, 'Blessed are You' (Kuntres HaTefillah scan p. 24, OCR). (*stated*)
2. Before whom? Before the Essence of the Luminary, 'found in every single place', in whom concealment and revelation do not apply. So the main point of the Amidah is inner bitul, in contrast to the Shema's contemplation (Kuntres HaTefillah scan p. 25, OCR) (F05, F08). (*stated*)
3. Even the speech is not one's own: 'Adonai, open my lips'. The Name Adnut, Malchus, opens the lips of the one praying; the praise is His own, and the mouth only draws it (Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2:7). The Maggid: the world of speech speaks in you, as the shofar sounds only while blown (Maggid 68:1). (*stated*)
4. It is said standing because it is 'in Atzilus itself', while the Shema, said sitting, is still in Beriah. Yet the Shema's union (Abba and Imma) is higher (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh 2). In the Amidah the union of voice and speech draws the revelation of Or Ein Sof into the lower worlds, and the middle requests are the vessels for it (Shoresh 17; Likkutei Torah, Balak 3:10-11) (F02, F07). (*stated*)
5. The bowings repeat in deed the acceptance of kingship that the Shema made in speech (Tanya 42:20) (G11). (*stated*)
6. R. Aharon of Strashelye adds a turn: because the Amidah draws Godliness into the yesh itself, nefilat apayim follows at once, as a self-offering without contemplation that nullifies the yesh completely (Sha'arei Avodah 33:7). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 42:20: the bowings are kabbalat ol in deed. Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2 and 32: 'Adonai, open my lips', Malchus speaks through the one praying, 'and there is nothing besides Him at all'. Likkutei Torah, Balak 3: 'Baruch atah' asks that the light above the chain of worlds be drawn down; the requests are vessels (S108). Kuntres Acharon 4: prayer changes creatures, the sick are healed (S101).
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not quoted on the Amidah in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 1-2, 17: a guide to each blessing (1); the Shema is higher, but standing in the Amidah is 'Atzilus itself' (2); the Amidah is the union of Z'A and Nukva that draws Or Ein Sof into the lower worlds (17).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627: the Amidah is the fourth rung of the ladder (S106).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR): standing before the Essence found in every place, speaking face to face; the Amidah's essence is bitul. Hemshech Ayin Beis 382: the middle blessings are the drawing down that follows the ascent (read, not quoted).
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Maamar of 10 Shevat 5730: the fulfilment of the requests (healing and so on) comes by joining sovev and memale through the Essence (S101).
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (68:1): 'Adonai, open my lips', the world of speech speaks in him. Berditchev (Kedushat Levi, Vaetchanan 2, via pillar 11): 'open my lips' is the prayer to be able to pray. Not re-verified here. R. Aharon of Strashelye: after the Amidah, nefilat apayim nullifies the yesh the Amidah drew into.

## S106 · The order of prayer as a ladder · סדר התפילה / סולם מוצב ארצה

**Faces:** F02 The higher and the lower unity · F06 The soul as part of the One · F11 Darkness turned to light · F04 Filling and surrounding **Search terms:** סולם מוצב ארצה · פסוקי דזמרה · סדר התפלה · ברוך שאמר

> The order of prayer is the unity of God as a ladder set on the earth: the soul climbs from acknowledgment through creation and the two unities to bitul, and the light comes down the same rungs.

## Derivation

1. 'A ladder set on the earth, its head reaching heaven': prayer is the ladder of the soul's bond with Godliness. It begins with mere acknowledgment and reaches essential bitul, through comprehension in Pesukei deZimra and the Shema with its blessings (Hayom Yom 5 Kislev). (*stated*)
2. Each rung has a content. Pesukei deZimra contemplates creation of yesh from ayin (F01); the blessings and the Shema contemplate 'Havayah is One' and Baruch Shem (F02); then comes the Amidah's bitul and drawing down (Hemshech Ayin Beis 382). The blessings before the Shema show the angels nullified and His contraction 'into a boundary called world' out of love, 'to include them in His unity' (Tanya 49:8-10). (*stated*)
3. The ladder is the soul's own shape. Only a ray of the soul is in the body, and its root stays in 'ayin'; the four rungs match the four descents 'pure, You created, You formed, You breathed', and prayer joins ray and root (Toras Shmuel 5627) (F06). (*stated*)
4. It must be climbed in order: in prayer, unlike Torah study, skipping a rung achieves nothing and misleads (Hemshech Ayin Beis 382). (*stated*)
5. Why this is the main service now: in the footsteps of Messiah prayer is the chief service, because it refines the sparks and subdues or transforms the animal soul (Kuntres Acharon 8:3-7) (F11). (*stated*)
6. The ladder reaches from the very lowest: 'even when a person is at the lowest rung (earthward), by the ladder of prayer he reaches heaven' (the Rebbe, Kedoshim 5714). The joining of soul-in-body and soul-above parallels the joining of memale and sovev (10 Shevat 5730) (G05, F04). (*stated*)
7. The Maggid's ladder runs inward rather than through the liturgy: letters of speech, letters of thought, then ayin (Maggid 27:4). That both are one ladder is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Tanya 49: the blessings before the Shema as preparation for self-offering. Kuntres Acharon 8: pesukei deZimra and the blessings arouse the hidden love for the Shema; prayer is the main service in the footsteps of Messiah, for refining the sparks.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Sha'arei Teshuvah: prayer is called a ladder because its root is raising 'mayin nukvin' from below (located by search, not quoted).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Cited by the Rebbe (10 Shevat 5730): prayer is the ladder joining the soul in the body with the soul above, as memale with sovev.
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel 5627 (Lech Lecha): four rungs of prayer match the soul's four descents; prayer joins the ray of the soul to its root.
- **Rashab.** Hemshech Ayin Beis 382: the ordered ascent, acknowledgment to Pesukei deZimra (creation) to the Shema (the two unities); no skipping rungs in prayer.
- **Rayatz.** Hayom Yom 5 Kislev (compiled from him): prayer is the ladder of the soul's bond, from acknowledgment to essential bitul.
- **The Rebbe.** Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat (Kedoshim 5714; 10 Shevat 5730): the ladder reaches from the lowest; prayer joins memale and sovev; torah and prayer contain each other.
- **Other schools.** The Maggid (27:4): the inward ladder, speech to thought to ayin; (86:2) the ladder's head is the supernal Heh. The phrase appears widely: Me'or Einayim, Toldot Yaakov Yosef, Degel, Kozhnitz, Ropshitz, R. Tzadok (found by search, not read in this pass).

## S107 · Solitary prayer · התבודדות

**Faces:** F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F07 The home below · F02 The higher and the lower unity **Search terms:** התבודדות · מתבודד · בינו לבין קונו · שפיכת הנפש

> Solitary prayer is the unity of God as bitul reached alone, speaking to Him in one's own words until one is included, with the world, in the Necessary Existent (Breslov). Chabad sets withdrawal from the world beneath the dwelling made in it.

## Derivation

1. R. Nachman states the tie outright. Creation's purpose is that Israel return and be included in their root, His unity, the Necessary Existent; that is reached only by complete bitul, and bitul only by hitbodedut, 'pouring out one's words between oneself and one's Maker' (Likutei Moharan 52:2-3). (*stated*)
2. It works by stages, one trait at a time, until even the residue of self-regard is nullified. Then 'the whole world is included with his soul in its root' (Likutei Moharan 52:3:5-7). Hitbodedut is 'higher than everything' (Likutei Moharan II 25) (F08, F02). (*stated*)
3. The Besht knew seclusion as inward: with a companion in the room, each alone with the Creator. One who is cleaving can be secluded even in a house full of people (Tzava'at HaRivash 63). (*stated*)
4. The nearest Chabad instruction found in this pass is the night-time reckoning at tikkun chatzot, done alone, 'not before others', as the hidden foundation of the day's service. The text does not call it hitbodedut; treating it as Chabad's counterpart is our reading (the Rashab, Kuntres HaTefillah scan p. 24, OCR) (S110). (*stated*)
5. The Rebbe sets withdrawal from the world against the purpose: 'to engage with the world and make it a dwelling for Him'. Seclusion is for a minority (after mesirat nefesh), or for a small part of each person's time, and Chassidus's way is 'not separation and seclusion but raising the world's matters' (Toras Menachem 5719, 5724) (F07). (*stated*)
6. The Rebbe speaks of withdrawal from worldly affairs, not of Breslov's hour of speech to God. That the two positions meet head-on is our reading. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Not located in this pass under this name. The nearest Tanya practice is the self-reckoning ('marei d'chushbana', Tanya 29, 31; Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7:9), tied to chatzot in Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7 (S110). Calling it the equivalent of hitbodedut is our reading, not his.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Not located in this pass.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR): the reckoning at chatzot is to be done alone, in secret, as a foundation.
- **Rayatz.** Likkutei Dibburim has about 30 hits for the word (OCR; not read in this pass).
- **The Rebbe.** Toras Menachem 5719 (Mattos-Masei) and 5724 (Shabbos HaGadol): the purpose is the dwelling in the world; seclusion only for a few or briefly; Chassidus's way is raising the world, not withdrawing from it.
- **Other schools.** R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 52; II 25): hitbodedut is the only road to bitul and inclusion in the Necessary Existent. Tikkun chatzot and Tehillim can serve as hitbodedut if one finds oneself in them (II 101; S110, S111). The Besht (Tzava'at HaRivash 63, 82): seclusion with a companion, and inward seclusion among people. Noam Elimelekh and Maor VaShemesh carry the word often (not read here).

## S108 · Blessings · ברכות

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F07 The home below · F05 The Essence beyond both **Search terms:** ברכות · ברכה · ברכת הנהנין · ברוך אתה

> A blessing is the unity of God as 'baruch', a drawing down: the Havayah hidden in each thing's creative word is woken and becomes 'You', present and addressed, in Elokim itself.

## Derivation

1. Every creature lives by the word that created it: 'Forever, O God, Your word stands in the heavens'. When one blesses food with intention and says 'Baruch atah Hashem', the life by which that fruit was created is woken, and it feeds the soul (the Besht, Keter Shem Tov 1:159) (F01, G01). (*stated*)
2. 'Baruch' means drawing down. Havayah, which contracts and extends, should become 'Atah', 'You', present and revealed (Torah Ohr, Chayei Sara 1:25). It should be revealed precisely in Elokim, as a child hidden in the womb is born (Torah Ohr, Shemot supplements) (G14, F03). (*stated*)
3. What is drawn is the light above the chain of worlds: 'Baruch atah' asks that Or Ein Sof above hishtalshelus be blessed, drawn down, and healing and blessing of the years are the vessels in which Godliness is revealed below, in physicality (Likkutei Torah, Balak 3:10-11) (F05). (*stated*)
4. Its purpose is the dwelling below: the same Torah Ohr passage ends in 'the ultimate intent is to be a dwelling for Him in the lowest' (F07). (*stated*)
5. Its form is address: the soul is bound to Him 'especially in speaking to God face to face, as in "Blessed are You"' (Tanya 41:24). For now the body hides it, so bitul is shown outwardly by bowing at 'Baruch' (Torah Ohr, Chayei Sara 1:25). (*stated*)
6. A limit case: Modeh Ani, the first thanks of the day, has no Name at all, because the essence of the soul thanks the Essence, which is not captured by a Name (Inyanah 11:6). The formal blessing needs the Name; the essential thanks goes past it. Setting this against the Besht's 'when he mentions the Name' is our juxtaposition. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Torah Ohr and Likkutei Torah (several places): 'baruch' as drawing down, that Havayah become 'You'. Tanya 41:24: 'Blessed are You' as speaking face to face. His halachic Seder Birkat HaNehenin was not searched for the tie; the spine (§5) expects none on the halachic page.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Derekh Mitzvotekha, Shoresh Mitzvat HaTefillah 9: why praise Him at all if all the earth's inhabitants are as nothing (read, not quoted; S111).
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR): the Amidah's preparation, speaking face to face, 'Blessed are You' (S105).
- **Rayatz.** Hayom Yom 2 Adar I: 'a blessing with intention' suffices as true service (S102).
- **The Rebbe.** Inyanah 10-17: Modeh Ani read at all four levels and then given life from the yechidah. It carries no Name because it is the essence thanking the Essence.
- **Other schools.** The Besht (Keter Shem Tov 1:159-160): the blessing wakes the creative word in the food; creation was so that Israel would refine the sparks from permitted food. Me'or Einayim (Additions 38): seeing the Creator's glory in a thing unites one with it and repairs it, and the blessings on seeing are its model. The Maggid (6:3): 'baruch' and 'atah' are themselves Names when one prays according to Kabbalah.

## S109 · Melody and dance · ניגון

**Faces:** F04 Filling and surrounding · F12 Knowing the unity · F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation **Search terms:** ניגון · נגון · ריקוד · נגינה

> Melody is the unity of God as a voice above words: contemplation set to music (Chabad), and the song of faith in the Ein Sof that surrounds all, which lifts souls out of the void (Breslov).

## Derivation

1. The Mitteler Rebbe ties niggun to contemplation: the Levites' ordered song follows the unfolding of the idea, its movements set out like the steps of the explanation (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 30:11) (F12). (*stated*)
2. Above that is 'the voice of Moshe', a single sudden movement from the core of the idea, 'a voice not heard' that never stops, 'so that glory may sing to You and not be silent' (30:12-16). Likkutei Torah applies the same verse to Malchus singing without pause, which is why the heart is sometimes drawn of itself 'as soon as God's unity comes to mind' (Shir HaShirim 2:3-4). (*stated*)
3. R. Nachman: every wisdom has its melody, and the highest is the melody of faith in the Ein Sof itself, which surrounds all worlds; all melodies come from it (Likutei Moharan 64:5) (F04). (*stated*)
4. That melody is the answer to the empty space. Through the tzaddik's niggun, which is Moshe's and is silence, souls fallen into the heresy of the chalal hapanui rise out (Likutei Moharan 64:5:10) (F03). Both the Mitteler Rebbe and R. Nachman place the highest melody with Moshe; the parallel is our observation. (*stated*)
5. In Chabad practice niggun is voice waking intention ('kol me'orer hakavanah'), a tune for each stage of avodah (the Rashab via the Rayatz), and 'leaving where one is to arrive where one must be' (the Alter Rebbe from Mezritch, via the Rayatz). That this leaving is bitul is our reading. (**reading**)
6. Dance (rikud): no passage tying it to the unity was found in this pass. It is left unforced. (**reading**)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Hayom Yom 25 Tishrei: ten niggunim attributed to him. Hayom Yom 8 Adar I: he spoke 'with a niggun, as was his way'. Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2: Malchus sings without pause.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 30: ordered niggun follows contemplation; above it the unheard voice of Moshe. His 'kapelye' of singers and players (Hayom Yom 25 Tishrei).
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Hayom Yom 13 Sivan: composed niggunim, studied with a tune, and paused writing to sing; the Maharash knew from the tune what he was working on.
- **Maharash.** Via Hayom Yom 13 Sivan and Sefer HaSichos 5706 (Tzemach Tzedek to Maharash): Chabad niggunim either come from ChaBaD or reveal it (read, not quoted).
- **Rashab.** 'Kol me'orer hakavanah' (via the Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos 5706).
- **Rayatz.** Sefer HaSichos 5706: niggun as taken from Mezritch, 'leaving where one is'; a niggun for each stage of avodah, before and after yechidus.
- **The Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Other schools.** R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 64): the melody of faith in Ein Sof answers the void, and only the tzaddik who is 'Moshe' has it. (54:10) David's harp at midnight wakes the holy niggun (S110). Breslov and Chabad differ here: for R. Nachman the highest melody is faith above all wisdom, while for the Mitteler Rebbe it grows out of contemplation's core point.

> כי הוא הזמר השיך להאמונה בהאור אין סוף עצמו, שהוא למעלה מן הכל
> — R. Nachman (Likutei Moharan 64): every wisdom and faith has its melody; the highest is the melody of faith in the light of Ein Sof itself, which surrounds all worlds, and all melodies come from it.

> **Likutei Moharan 64:5:10** (R. Nachman of Breslov, Likutei Moharan; line 3443)
> עולים ויוצאים כל הנשמות שנפלו בתוך האפיקורסות הזאת של חלל הפנוי
> — Through the tzaddik's niggun, which is Moshe's and is silence, souls fallen into the heresy of the empty space rise out. The melody of faith answers the question of the void (F03).

> **Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2:3** (Alter Rebbe, Likkutei Torah; line 4352)
> מיד שתעלה במחשבתו אחדות ה׳ וגדולתו תמשך הלב מאליה בהתפעלות
> — Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 2: sometimes, as soon as God's unity comes to mind, the heart is drawn of itself. That is Malchus singing without pause above ('so that glory may sing to You and not be silent').

> **Hayom Yom, סיון / יג סיון:1** (Hayom Yom (compiled by the Rebbe from the Rayatz, 1942); line 318)
> הצ"צ חבר כמה נגונים, למודו הי' בקול ונגון
> — Hayom Yom 13 Sivan: the Tzemach Tzedek composed niggunim, studied with a tune, and would stop writing Chassidus or responsa to sing. The Maharash could tell from the tune what he was working on. Practice, not doctrine.

> **Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz), תש"ו-ה'שי"ת / ספר השיחות — תש"ו / פורים תש"ו / 23:3** (Rebbe Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos; line 6270)
> וואס א ניגון איז אויף ארויסגיין פון וואנען מען איז, און צוקומען דארט וואו מען דארף זיין
> — The Rayatz: from Mezritch the Alter Rebbe took the niggun, which is for 'leaving where one is and arriving where one must be'. That this leaving is bitul is our reading. (**reading**)

> **Sefer HaSichos (Rayatz), תש"ו-ה'שי"ת / ספר השיחות — תש"ו / יום שבת קודש חול המועד סוכות תש"ו / 7:4** (Rebbe Rayatz, Sefer HaSichos; line 6138)
> קול מעורר הכוונה
> — The Rashab via the Rayatz: 'the voice arouses the intention'. The early chassidim had a niggun for every stage of avodah, before and after yechidus.

## Thin or forced?

Partly. Niggun's tie is stated in Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 30 and Likutei Moharan 64. The Chabad practice passages (Hayom Yom, the Rayatz) describe custom and function without stating the tie. For dance none was found, and none is forced.

## S110 · Midnight lament · תיקון חצות

**Faces:** F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation · F11 Darkness turned to light · F09 The unity of opposites **Search terms:** תיקון חצות · חצות לילה · בחצות · קינות

> Tikkun chatzot is the unity of God as grief over its own hiding: mourning that the flow of Havayah went down into exile, so that the joy after it is light that comes out of darkness.

## Derivation

1. The lower teshuvah is to arouse mercy on one's soul, fallen 'from a high roof to a deep pit', and on the flow of Havayah that one's deeds sent down into the chambers of impurity to give them life. That is the exile of the Shechinah, and its fitting time is tikkun chatzot (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7:2-3) (F03). (*stated*)
2. The grief grows out of the unity: one who contemplates the greatness of Ein Sof, filling and surrounding all worlds, is embittered that the King is humiliated (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7:4) (F04). (*stated*)
3. The bitterness is for the sake of joy: after tikkun chatzot one says a joyful psalm so as to study in a joy that has 'the advantage of light that comes from darkness' (Tanya 26:3) (F11). (*stated*)
4. The Mitteler Rebbe gives the reason: now the joy of grasping the One breaks through only by its opposite, weeping. So chatzot is done in weeping, so that in prayer the essential delight is revealed (Shaar HaYichud 6:58-63) (F09). (*stated*)
5. It makes the person a dwelling: the reckoning that makes one a vessel and 'the King's dwelling' belongs, as a rule, to tikkun chatzot, and only occasionally, not every night (the Rashab, Kuntres HaTefillah scan p. 23, OCR) (F07). (*stated*)
6. R. Nachman ties it to melody and providence: at midnight David's harp played by itself and the holy niggun wakes (Likutei Moharan 54:10); rising at midnight one sees all that God brings about each day as a hint to draw near (Likkutei Etzot, Midnight 1) (G12). He also calls it a sweetening of judgments (Likutei Moharan 149), with no stated tie to the unity. (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7: tikkun chatzot as the time for the lower teshuvah, over the exile of the Shechinah. Tanya 26: joy after it, light from darkness. The Siddur note it cites was not read in this pass.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Shaar HaYichud 6:62-74: weeping at chatzot is 'the main preparation' for prayer, since the joy of the One now breaks out only through its opposite.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Or HaTorah carries the term often (OCR; not read in this pass).
- **Maharash.** Toras Shmuel carries the term (not read in this pass).
- **Rashab.** Kuntres HaTefillah (OCR): bitterness at chatzot only occasionally for most people, alone and in secret, followed by joy, as the foundation of the day's service.
- **Rayatz.** Not located in this pass.
- **The Rebbe.** Toras Menachem has about 30 mentions (not read in this pass).
- **Other schools.** R. Nachman: chatzot sweetens judgments (Likutei Moharan 149); mourn the Temple so as not to delay its building (II 67); saying chatzot can be hitbodedut if it is about what is happening to one now (II 101); David's harp (54:10). The Besht: 'be careful with chatzot, and join day and night' (Tzava'at HaRivash 83).

> — Likkutei Etzot, Midnight 1: through rising at midnight one sees that all God brings about each day is a hint to draw near (G12).

## Thin or forced?

Partly. The tie is stated through the exile of the Shechinah and through light from darkness (Iggeret HaTeshuvah 7; Tanya 26; Shaar HaYichud 6). The timing and the order of Tikkun Rachel and Tikkun Leah are not tied to the unity in any passage read.

## S111 · Psalms · תהלים

**Faces:** F01 The world said into being now · F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity · F06 The soul as part of the One **Search terms:** תהלים · ספר תהלים · מזמור · דוד המלך

> Psalms is the unity of God as the words of a king whose mouth was only the vessel of His speech. Its verse 'Your word stands in the heavens' grounds continuous creation, and simple Jews' recital of it breaks every partition.

## Derivation

1. The foundational face of continuous creation (F01) is the Besht's reading of a psalm verse, 'Forever, O God, Your word stands in the heavens' (Psalm 119:89), which the Alter Rebbe cites in his name and which is already in Midrash Tehillim (Hayom Yom 26 Tishrei). That the book thereby grounds the face is our reading. (**reading**)
2. David's speech was His speech: 'the spirit of God spoke in me', 'Adonai, open my lips'. The mouth is only the vessel, 'for truly there is no place empty of Him… and there is nothing besides Him at all' (Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 32:25). (*stated*)
3. The Tzemach Tzedek reads particular psalms through the unity. In 'Halleli nafshi' (Psalm 146), David's soul is Malchus, which cannot receive from Ein Sof, 'before whom all is as nothing and there is no other', except through Havayah; so it asks for the higher unity to be drawn down (Yahel Or 146) (F02). (*stated*)
4. Its power is breaking through: psalm verses 'break every partition' and rise without hindrance (the Tzemach Tzedek, Hayom Yom 24 Shevat), and the simple people's Tehillim will be revealed in Messiah's time (Hayom Yom 24 Iyar). That this power comes from the unity is our reading. (**reading**)
5. R. Nachman: David's whole occupation was breaking his heart before God, which is the essence of the book (Likutei Moharan II 100). One must find oneself in every psalm; the same teaching sets Tehillim beside hitbodedut among the chief counsels (II 101) (F08, S107). (*stated*)
6. A counter-voice on weighting: do not say many Tehillim before prayer, lest the obligation of the day lose its devekut (Tzava'at HaRivash 38). (*stated*)

## The Rebbeim and the schools

- **Alter Rebbe.** Likkutei Torah, Shir HaShirim 32: David's 'the spirit of God spoke in me', and there is nothing besides Him. SH 1:3: the Besht's reading of Psalm 119:89.
- **Mitteler Rebbe.** Not located in this pass.
- **Tzemach Tzedek.** Yahel Or, a commentary on Tehillim: Psalm 146 through Malchus and the higher unity; Psalm 119:89 at length (read by search). Hayom Yom 24 Shevat: verses of Tehillim break all partitions.
- **Maharash.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rashab.** Not located in this pass.
- **Rayatz.** Hayom Yom (compiled from him): daily Tehillim, the whole book on Shabbos Mevarchim, the simple people's Tehillim (24 Iyar).
- **The Rebbe.** Hayom Yom 29 Cheshvan (from a letter): communal recital of Tehillim (read, not quoted).
- **Other schools.** R. Nachman: breaking the heart, finding oneself in every psalm (Likutei Moharan II 100-101). The Besht: the psalm verse behind continuous creation; also do not say too many Tehillim before prayer (Tzava'at HaRivash 38).
