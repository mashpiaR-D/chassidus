# The Tanya as a book of consciousness · Part 2: Likkutei Amarim, chapters 19 to 35

**Only One research** · October 5, 2026 · early draft 0.1 · Result [030](../../CONTENTS.md#030) · manuscript 2 of 7 · [Cite](CITATION.bib) · [Quote check](../../verification/catalogue.md#the-tanya-as-a-book-of-consciousness-part-2-likkutei-amarim-chapters-19-to-35-october-5-2026)

---
This part of the Tanya goes under the thinking mind and then comes back up into the working day. Chapters 19 to 22 find the deepest layer a person has: a pull toward G-d that is older than any reason, a self that wants to go quiet in its source, and the plain truth that makes that pull make sense, that before the One everything is as nothing and the only real distance is feeling yourself a thing of your own. Chapters 23 to 25 show what that means in the hand, the mouth and the moment of choice: while you do His will you are one with Him, and the soul was faithful even when you fell. Chapters 26 to 32 are the inner life of the beinoni, the person awake inside the struggle: joy against heaviness, stray thoughts pushed off without a fight, a heart too dull to feel, a proud self brought low and then lifted into the joy of a prince let out of prison, and love for other people because there is one Father. Chapter 33 is the summit: He alone is here, in this very place, and He chose to live here. Chapters 34 and 35 bring it into the hours of the day: a room made for Him in fixed learning and giving, weeping and joy in one heart, and the body as a wick whose light burns above. Each chapter was read in the Hebrew before it was written, and each source line was checked word for word against the Hebrew text at the line given.

## Chapter 19 · The flame that wants to go home

**The state.** The hidden love (ahavah mesuteres, the love that is covered over): a pull in the soul toward its source that is below all reasons, like a flame straining upward. It lives in chochmah (the soul's first flash, the point before thought), and it can sleep for years and wake in one moment.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "The soul of man is the lamp of G-d." Look at a candle flame. It keeps leaning up and flickering, all the time. Fire, by its nature, wants to tear itself off the wick and go back to where fire comes from. If it got its wish it would go out. It would give no light down here, and up there it would dissolve and be nothing. Still, that is what it wants.

The soul is like that. Deep down it wants to leave the body and hold on to G-d, the Life of all life, even though there it would be nothing at all and nothing of its old self would be left. This is not a wish you arrive at by thinking. The Tanya uses the word "nature" for exactly this: whatever is not a matter of reasons. It sits above understanding, in chochmah, where the light of the Infinite lives in the soul.

So here is the first state: a part of you that already wants to be nothing in Him. Everything holy works like this. Holiness is only what comes from chochmah, which is given over to the light inside it and is not a thing on its own. That is why chochmah is read as koach mah, "the power of what?", a self that asks what it is and finds nothing to hold up.

The opposite state is the other side (the sitra achra, the pull that serves only itself). Its cry is "give, give," "feed me." It wants to be a something, a self for itself. The Tanya calls that state dead, because "wisdom gives life" and a self cut off from chochmah has no life of its own. It says this of the nations' souls and also of Jews who sin.

Then how can a sinner have this love? Because it is hidden. It lies under a sackcloth, and a spirit of foolishness gets in and makes a person sin. But only the part of chochmah that spreads out to give life to the whole soul is in exile in the body. Its root stays in the mind. It is not in exile there. It is asleep. It does nothing while the person's knowing and understanding are busy with what the world wants.

Then a test comes about faith itself: bow to an idol, deny the One. That touches the soul where chochmah is. The sleeper wakes up, "like one who wakes from sleep." Now a person who all his life could not stand up to his desires stands up without any reason at all. He can despise all of it and choose G-d, even give up his life for His name. Before this point, all the other side's strength melts "like wax before fire."

The light in chochmah is so strong that it will not let the other side touch even its clothes: thought, speech and action about the One. So a person will die rather than bow to an idol even with no belief in it, or say one false word about G-d's oneness while his heart stays whole. The Tanya names this "awe held inside love": because the soul loves and wants to cling, it shrinks by its own nature from any touch of what denies the One.

Where the picture breaks: a flame that reaches its source goes out. The soul in its source is not lost the way a flame is lost. And the flame never chooses; the soul does.

**The movement.** From the soul asleep, busy with the world's wants, to the soul awake at the point of chochmah. What carries a person across in this chapter is a test that touches the root: the moment you are asked to cut yourself off from the One. Later chapters will teach how to wake it without waiting for a test.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "My soul leans home, like a flame." 2. Picture a real candle. It leans up. It does not argue its way up. Think of the one time in your life you would not have given up G-d, even when nothing made sense. 3. The picture breaks here: the flame goes out in its source; you would become more yourself, not less. 4. Stop at the edge. There is a want in you under all your wants. You cannot see it. You can only lean with it. 5. Say to Him: "I don't know why, but I want You." 6. Today, one thing you would not do even if no one would know, because it would cut you off from Him. Keep it.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a plain "no" to something false rises in you with no argument behind it, and it is calm. The fake is a dramatic longing to "dissolve" that is really tiredness with life, or a stubborn "no" that is only pride.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 19 (line 286): כְּאוֹר הַנֵּר שֶׁמִּתְנַעְנֵעַ תָּמִיד לְמַעְלָה בְּטִבְעוֹ*

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this reading applies it to every person.*

## Chapter 20 · Before Him everything is as nothing

**The state.** Seeing the world as a word still being spoken: the unity of the One (achdus, oneness) seen so that all things, you included, are kelo chashiv, "as nothing," before Him. This is the ground of all the Torah and the root of the want in chapter 19.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The first two of the Ten Commandments, "I am" and "You shall have no other gods," hold the whole Torah. "I am" holds all the things to do; "no other gods" holds all the things not to do. That is why the people heard those two straight from His mouth. So to understand the whole Torah you need to understand what His oneness is.

He is called "One and alone." He is exactly the same after the world was made as before. "You were the same before the world was created, You are the same since." The worlds, high and low, change nothing in Him when they come from nothing into being. How can that be? Because everything, before Him, counts as nothing at all.

Here is why. What brings every world out of nothing, and what keeps it from falling back into nothing, is only "the word of G-d and the breath of His mouth" dressed inside it. That is all a thing is, from the inside: a word being said.

Now the Tanya gives the picture. When you speak one word, that word is nothing next to your power to speak, which could speak words without end. It is even less next to your thought, where the word came from. It is nothing at all next to your soul itself, your wisdom, understanding and knowing, which have no letters yet. Think of a want before it becomes words: a love falls into your heart, and before it rises to the mind to be thought through, it is a plain wanting with no words in it. Before that, it was only in your wisdom, knowing that a thing is good. Only after the heart wants and it climbs back to the mind to plan "how do I get it" are letters born, in whatever language you think in.

So, from inside: the whole world, with its weight and its hardness and its "me," is one word. Next to the One it is less than your one spoken word is next to your whole soul. This is not a feeling of being small. It is seeing that there is no second thing standing next to Him.

The picture breaks, and the Tanya says so in the next chapter: your word leaves you and hangs in the air. His does not.

**The movement.** From seeing things as simply there, to seeing them as said. What carries a person across is understanding in detail: going down the steps of the picture (a word, the power to speak, thought, the soul before letters) until the mind is sure, and then turning it on the world.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Before Him, everything is as nothing." 2. Say one word out loud. Weigh it against all the words you could still say, then against the thought behind it, then against the you who is thinking. 3. Now look at one real thing in the room, a table or a hand. It is a word He is saying right now. 4. The picture breaks: your word leaves your mouth; His word stays inside Him. Stop there. You cannot see how. 5. Say to Him: "You are the same now as before anything was." 6. Today, when a thing looks huge and final to you, say its name slowly once, and remember whose word it is.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when your worries lose their hardness without losing their reality, and you feel held, not erased. The fake is a floating "nothing is real, nothing matters" that lets you stop caring about people and duties.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 20 (line 302): אֵינוֹ אֶלָּא דְּבַר ה׳ וְרוּחַ פִּיו יִתְבָּרֵךְ הַמְלוּבָּשׁ בָּהֶם*

## Chapter 21 · The word that never leaves Him

**The state.** Tzimtzum seen from both sides: from our side, hester panim (hiding of the face), the state in which the world seems to stand on its own and His light seems like something that went out from Him; from His side, no hiding at all, "darkness is like light."

**The chapter as consciousness.** His way is not like ours. When a person speaks, the breath of the word is felt as a thing by itself, cut off from the soul it came from. With Him nothing is cut off, because there is nothing outside Him and "no place empty of Him." So His speech is not like ours. It is called "speech" only as a picture: our speech brings out what was hidden in our thought, and His light and life coming out of hiding into being, to make worlds and keep them alive, is called His "speech." These are the ten sayings that made the world, and all the Torah the prophets saw.

His speech and thought are one with Him completely. The picture: think of your own words while they are still in your wisdom, or still in the wanting of your heart before they climb to the mind as letters. There they are one with their root. That is how His speech stays one with Him even after it has gone out and made worlds, the same as before. Nothing changes for Him at all.

The change is only on our side. The life reaches creatures through a long chain, cause to effect, step after step, shrunk down again and again, so that they can take it in and not be swallowed up. All those shrinkings are called "hiding of the face": they cover the light so it does not shine so strongly that the lower beings cannot hold it. And so the light inside them seems to them to be something apart from Him that only came from Him, the way a person's word comes out of his soul.

That is the state most of us live in, most of the time: a world that looks like it stands on its own, and a G-d who, at best, sent it out. The chapter does not call this state a lie about us; it is how the light had to be covered so we could exist.

But before Him there is no hiding. "Even darkness is not dark to You; darkness is like light." The coverings are not something separate from Him. They are like the snail whose shell is part of its body. "The Lord is G-d": the name of hiding and the name of being are one. So before Him everything counts as nothing at all.

Where the picture breaks: even your words "in the heart" are still something; His speech is not a something next to Him in any way.

**The movement.** From "the world stands on its own and G-d is somewhere behind it" to "the hiding itself is His, and nothing is apart." What carries a person across is knowing that the hiding is a kindness made for your sake, and that the covering and the One behind it are one.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Even darkness is not dark to You." 2. Think of your own want before it had words, how one it was with you. His word is always like that with Him. 3. Now think of one thing in your life that feels far from G-d, a job, a street, a bill. It is covered so you can stand in it. The cover is His. 4. The picture breaks: the snail's shell is still a shell. Here there is no second thing. Stop. 5. Say to Him: "You are not hidden from Yourself. Let me not forget that." 6. Today, in the place that feels most empty of Him, do one kind thing on purpose.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a plain, dull moment feels full without anything changing on the outside. The fake is dismissing the hiding as "illusion" so that your real struggles and other people's pain stop counting.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 21 (line 313): וְכָל הַצִּמְצוּמִים הֵם בְּחִינַת ״הֶסְתֵּר פָּנִים״*

## Chapter 22 · The self that stands apart

**The state.** Yeshus (being a something of your own): the state of feeling yourself a thing in itself, separate from the Holy. The Tanya names it the true root of idolatry and of arrogance, and its opposite is bittul, the self gone quiet before Him.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Torah uses the word "speech" for G-d's word the way people talk, because the way life comes down truly is through great shrinkings, one kind after another. The hiding grows so strong that even unclean things and the other side can come to be and get their life from His word.

That is why they are called "other gods." They get their life not from His "face" but from His "back." The Tanya's picture: a man who has to give something to someone he hates. He does not hand it over face to face. He throws it over his shoulder with his face turned away. His "face" above is His inner will, what He truly wants: to pour life into everything near Him in holiness. The other side is what He hates. He gives it life only from the "back," against His will, so that the wicked can be judged and the good can be rewarded for holding it down. Even that "back" is not inside it. It only hovers over it. So the other side is a place of death and impurity, and the little light it holds is in exile inside it.

From inside, what is this state? It is a self that is not given over. It lifts itself "like an eagle" and says, "I am, and there is no other." "My river is mine, I made myself." It does not deny G-d. It may even call Him "the G-d of gods." It simply feels itself as a thing standing by itself.

So the sages said arrogance is like idolatry itself. The root of idolatry is not a total denial of G-d. It is feeling yourself a something on your own, and so cutting yourself off from His holiness, because holiness rests only on what is given over to Him. The Zohar calls such things "mountains of separation." That is a denial of His true oneness, where everything is as nothing before Him and given over to His will, which keeps making it from nothing, always.

This chapter is honest and not gentle about the other side. Read it gently about yourself: the "I made myself" voice is in every person. The chapter tells you what it is, so you can know it when it speaks.

Where the picture breaks: a man who throws a gift over his shoulder is still forced by something outside him. Nothing forces the One. He chose to allow a "back" so that choice could exist.

**The movement.** From yeshus, the self standing apart, to bittul, the self that knows it is being made right now. What carries a person across is seeing that pride is not a big feeling about yourself but a small untruth about what you are.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "I am not a thing of my own." 2. Think of one thing you are proud of. Trace it back step by step: the skill, the body, the strength, the life, all being given right now. 3. Bring it down to one thing: the hand you did it with. Its life is His will this second. 4. The picture breaks: you are not thrown over a shoulder. You are held face to face when you are given over. Stop. 5. Say to Him: "What I have is Yours. Rest on me." 6. Today, give the credit for one good thing away, out loud or in your heart.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you can do something well and feel no need to be seen. The fake is putting yourself down in words while inside you still want to be the center.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 22 (line 323): כִּי עִיקַּר וְשֹׁרֶשׁ עֲבוֹדָה זָרָה, הוּא מַה שֶּׁנֶּחְשָׁב לְדָבָר בִּפְנֵי עַצְמוֹ*

## Chapter 23 · Being His will while you do it

**The state.** Unity through the act (yichud, oneness): the state in which, while you do a mitzvah (a commandment) or learn Torah, your hand, mouth and mind are given over to His will the way a limb is given over to a person, and in learning you are one with Him outright.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Zohar says "the Torah and the Holy One are all one," and the Tikkunim say "the 248 commands are the 248 limbs of the King." Here is what that means from inside.

The commands are His inner will, what He truly wants, and that will is dressed in all the worlds and gives them life. So doing a command is the inner clothing of His inner will. Through the act, His light and life come down into the worlds.

That is why they are called "limbs of the King." Think of your own arm. The moment you want to stretch it out, it moves. You don't tell it. You don't wait. It is simply given over to you, like a body to its soul. The life of a mitzvah is given over like that to the will of G-d inside it. And the part of your soul that does the act is dressed in that deed and is given over too. So your limbs, while you do it, become a merkavah (a chariot, a thing with no will of its own but its rider's): the hand that gives charity to the poor, the feet that walk to do a mitzvah, the mouth that speaks Torah, the mind that thinks about Torah and about His greatness. The sages said "the fathers are the chariot": every limb they had was only for His will, all their days.

Learning goes further. When you think Torah and speak Torah, those are the soul's inner clothes, and they are not only a chariot. They are one with His will outright. His will is the very law you are thinking. Each law is a piece of His inner will, that He wanted this to be allowed and that not. The letters of the Torah are His will and wisdom, one with Him. So "the Torah and the Holy One are all one," and not only "limbs of the King."

So while you learn, His will is in the open in your soul, with no hiding of the face at all. And nothing is apart from Him except through hiding. That means your soul and its thought and speech are, at that hour, one with the Infinite, as one as His own speech is with Him. It is even a closer union than He has with the higher worlds. The worlds get their life from Torah, His wisdom, which is above them all and "surrounds all worlds" and cannot fit inside them. And that very wisdom is what dresses itself in your soul when you learn. You don't see it. That is why you can bear it.

So learning is greater than every other mitzvah, even prayer. And a thinking person can draw a great awe from it: to know that his mind and mouth, right now, are one with the Infinite before whom all worlds are nothing. Not every mind can hold such awe. The Tanya says so plainly, and says later that a person who cannot feel it at all is not held back from the practice.

Where the picture breaks: your arm has a life of its own that obeys you. In learning there is no second life that obeys. There is one will.

**The movement.** From doing a good deed as something you do, to doing it as a limb of His will, and from learning as information to learning as union. What carries a person across is contemplation during the act itself: stopping to know what your hand and mouth are at this moment.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "The Torah and the Holy One are all one." 2. Think of your hand. You wanted to lift it and it lifted. It did not argue. 3. Bring it down to one real act today: a coin given, a line of Torah said aloud. While you do it, His will is in that hand. 4. The picture breaks: your hand obeys you; here there is no "you" beside Him. Stop at the edge. You will not feel it. That is His mercy. 5. Say to Him: "Let this hand be only Yours for this minute." 6. Learn one law or one verse today, slowly, knowing it is His will itself.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a small act feels weighty and quiet, and you stop needing to feel anything for it to count. The fake is a thrill of being "holy" while you do it, which is really the self watching itself.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 23 (line 327): כְּמוֹ שֶׁאֵבְרֵי גּוּף הָאָדָם הֵם לְבוּשׁ לְנַפְשׁוֹ וּבְטֵלִים לְגַמְרֵי אֵלֶיהָ*

## Chapter 24 · Faithful even then

**The state.** The state of the act of sin seen truthfully: at the moment of choosing against His will, a person is cut off from His oneness more than anything else in creation, and still the soul inside him stays faithful. Its name is galus (exile): the soul held captive, not gone.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Against the 248 things to do stand the 365 things not to do, and every rabbinic prohibition. They are against His will, its exact opposite, so they are cut off from His oneness completely, like the other side itself, which is called "idolatry" and "other gods" because His will is hidden there. The clothing of the soul that does such an act, its thought, speech and action, and the soul dressed in them, are joined at that moment to that side.

Now the chapter says something hard. The other side, for all it is, knows its Master. It never goes against Him. Like Bilaam: "I cannot go past the word of G-d." It calls Him "G-d of gods." It knows its life comes from Him, only it holds that life in exile, feeling itself a power of its own. It does not break His will. A person who does break His will is, in that act, lower than the other side and further from His oneness. Even a small sin. Even a gnat, "which takes in and gives nothing back," the lowest of creatures, comes before a sinning person in the order of creation. Wild animals do not change their task. The Tanya adds: an animal rules a man only when he looks to it like an animal; around the righteous, whose image of G-d never leaves their face, even lions bow.

Why, then, does a person sin at all? "No one sins unless a spirit of foolishness enters him." Even someone with a light and easy mind could master her desire, if not for that foolishness that covers the hidden love. The proof: the same person would give up her life rather than bow to an idol even for a moment and even without belief. Resisting a desire is far easier than dying. The only difference she feels between the two is the foolishness itself, which covers the soul up to chochmah but not chochmah itself.

(The Tanya adds that when life is in danger, most commands give way, not because those sins are light, but because the Torah says to break one Shabbos so as to keep many. It is a decree, not a scale of weight.)

Then the turn. After a sin that does not cut the soul off completely, the body and the animal soul climb back up out of that place and come near the holy soul again. And even at the time of the sin the holy soul was faithful to Him. It was only in exile, held inside the animal soul that dragged the body down. There is no greater exile. The Tanya's picture is meant to shock: like taking a king by the head and pushing his face down into a pit of filth, even for a moment. That is what it is to drag the part of G-d in you into what He does not want.

Read this gently and exactly. The picture is not about how dirty you are. It is about how high the one inside you is, and that He never stopped being yours. The one pushed down in that picture is the King, not a stranger.

Where the picture breaks: a king would be ruined by that shame. The soul is not ruined. It is waiting.

**The movement.** From the blur that lets a person say "it's small, it doesn't matter," to seeing clearly what the act is, and from there to knowing the soul stayed faithful and can come home. What carries a person across is the truth told plainly: a small sin is not small, and the soul inside is not lost.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Even then, the soul was faithful to Him." 2. Think of one thing you do that you tell yourself is small. Look at it plainly: at that moment, your hand is against His will. 3. Now look at the other thing, which is also true: the part of you that is His did not leave. It was held down. 4. The picture of the king breaks: He is not ruined. He is waiting for you. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "I dragged You down. I am sorry. Lift me back." 6. Today, choose one moment to say no to the "small" thing, as a way of letting the King up.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you feel a clean sorrow that makes you want to come closer, not run. The fake is a heavy self-hatred that leaves you too flat to do anything good.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 24 (line 353): וְגַם בִּשְׁעַת הַחֵטְא הָיְתָה בְאָמְנָה אִתּוֹ יִתְבָּרֵךְ*

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this reading applies it to every person.*

## Chapter 25 · Very near

**The state.** The awakened hidden love, called on at will: the state in which a person, at any moment, pushes off the spirit of foolishness and forgetting and remembers the love for the One that is surely hidden in his heart. It carries awe inside it (dechilu, awe that will not be cut off from Him).

**The chapter as consciousness.** "The thing is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, to do it." At every moment it is in a person's hand to push out the spirit of foolishness and forgetting and to remember and wake the love for the One that is hidden in his heart, without any doubt. That love holds awe inside it: not to be cut off from His oneness in any way, even at the cost of life, with no reason, by a G-dly nature.

So here is the state this chapter opens: you are not waiting for a test from outside to wake the sleeper of chapter 19. You can wake it now, by remembering. And if you would give up your life before bowing to an idol, then breaking a desire, which is easier than death, is closer still. Not doing even a small rabbinic wrong, because in that moment it would cut you off as idolatry does. It is true that after idolatry one can also do teshuvah (return). The Tanya brings the saying "whoever says I will sin and then return is not given the chance," and explains that this only means Heaven does not help him to the moment of return; if he pushes through and returns, nothing stands before teshuvah. And still every Jew is ready to give up his life rather than bow even for a moment and repent afterward. Why? Because the light of G-d in the soul is above time. It does not think "for a moment." It rules time.

The same holds for "do good": to rise like a lion against the heaviness that the body's earth puts on you. The laziness that won't let you get up to learn, to pray with all your strength, to give money. The cold voice that tells you to be careful with your money and your health. To stand against all that is very near to a person once he takes to heart that it is far easier than dying, and he would take death with love rather than be cut off from Him even for a moment.

And all the more he should take this with love, to cling to Him forever. When you do His will in this work, His inner will is revealed in it face to face, with no hiding. Where there is no hiding, nothing is a separate something. So both souls, and all their clothes, are one with His will. That union above is eternal, because He and His will are above time. Below, it holds only for the time you are learning or doing the mitzvah. When you turn to empty things, you are cut off from it. And when you come back to Torah and prayer and ask forgiveness for the time you could have learned and didn't, He forgives. That is why "Forgive us" is said three times a day. This is not "I will sin and return"; that is only when a person sins relying on the return.

So Moshe told the people entering the land to say the Shema twice a day and take on His kingship with their very lives, even though they were promised no enemy would stand before them. Because keeping the Torah hangs on remembering this always: that you would give your life for His oneness. With that set in your heart day and night, you can stand against your desire at every moment.

Where the picture breaks: death is not really on the table in most of your choices. The chapter uses the extreme to show you what is already in you, not to make life a scene of martyrdom.

**The movement.** From the soul asleep by default and forgetting, to the soul awake by choice. What carries a person across is remembering, a simple act of mind, and speech: the Shema said twice a day with your life in it.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "It is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart." 2. Ask yourself, honestly: would I deny G-d to save my life? Feel the "no" come up with no reason behind it. 3. Now bring it down to one desire today. This is far smaller than dying. The same "no" is right there. 4. Where it breaks: you cannot keep this feeling all day. You can remember it. Stop at that. 5. Say the Shema slowly once today, and mean: my life is Yours. 6. Do one hard good thing today that the heavy part of you wants to put off.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you find a steady "no" or "yes" in you that does not depend on your mood. The fake is feeling brave in theory while nothing changes in the next hour.

## Chapter 26 · Light, not heavy

**The state.** Simchah (joy) against atzvus (heaviness, a sad and stone-dull heart): joy here is an open, quick, light heart that can move, and heaviness is the state in which even a strong person loses. Joy can also be the real joy that comes after a true broken heart.

**The chapter as consciousness.** A great rule. Two men are wrestling, each trying to throw the other. If one is slow and heavy, he will lose easily, even if he is the stronger. The struggle with the inner pull is exactly the same. You cannot win it from heaviness and slowness, which come from sadness and a heart gone dull like a stone. You win it by quickness, which comes from joy, an open heart, clean of every trace of worry and sadness in the world.

This tells you something about your inner weather. Heaviness is not humility. It is a state in which you lose even when you are strong.

But the verse says "In all sadness there is gain." Read it closely: the sadness itself has nothing good in it. The gain is what comes from it. That gain is the true joy in G-d that comes after a true sadness over your wrongs, felt at fixed times, with a bitter soul and a broken heart. That breaks the spirit of impurity and the iron wall between you and your Father in heaven. Then "Let me hear joy and gladness; give me back the joy of Your help." The Ari set that psalm after the midnight lament, before learning, so one learns with the joy that comes after sorrow. That joy has an edge the way light has an edge when it comes out of darkness. The verse even says the curses come "because you did not serve G-d with joy."

Now, worldly worry: livelihood, children, health. The counsel is the sages' saying, "Just as one blesses for the good, so one blesses for the bad," which the Talmud explains as "accept it with joy." This too is for the good, only it is not seen by eyes of flesh. It comes from the "hidden world," which is above the "revealed world." (The Tanya ties these to the letters of G-d's name: yud and heh for the hidden, vav and heh for the revealed. We compress that here.) "Happy is the man whom Yah chastens," with the hidden name.

That is why the sages said that those who rejoice in suffering are "like the sun going out in its might." The joy comes from loving closeness to G-d more than all of life. "Your kindness is better than life." And that closeness is infinitely greater in the hidden world, "where His strength is hidden." In the time to come, the sun will come out of its sheath and the hidden world will shine openly for all who sheltered in its shade here, the shade of wisdom, which looks like shadow and not like light and visible good.

Where the picture breaks: a wrestler wins by weight and strength too. Here strength matters less than lightness.

**The movement.** From heaviness, which feels deep but loses, to joy, which is quick and wins. What carries a person across: sorrow kept to its fixed time, and faith that the hidden is also good, accepted with joy.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "This too is for the good, only I can't see it." 2. Think of one real worry you carry today, by name. 3. Think it through: it comes from a place above what can be seen. The good in it is hidden, not missing. 4. The picture breaks: you cannot make it look good. Stop there. You don't have to. 5. Say to Him: "I bless You for this too, as I bless You for what I can see." 6. Do the next task in front of you quickly and lightly, as a wrestler would.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the worry is still there but your step is light. The fake is a forced cheerfulness laid over a sadness you won't face.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 26 (line 371): כִּי אִם, בִּזְרִיזוּת – הַנִּמְשֶׁכֶת מִשִּׂמְחָה וּפְתִיחַת הַלֵּב*

## Chapter 27 · Perhaps for this you were made

**The state.** The joy of the beinoni (the in-between person, who never acts on the bad but still feels it): the state of being glad at every stray thought you push away, instead of being crushed that you have it. Its false twin is sadness about spiritual things, which the Tanya says is really pride.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Sadness about spiritual things needs its own counsel. During prayer and learning you must serve with joy. And if a heaviness about heavenly things falls on you in the middle of your business day, know that it is a trick of the pull, meant to throw you into desire afterward. Otherwise, where would a true sorrow born of love and awe come from, right in the middle of your work? Whenever it comes, say to yourself: this is not the time. Even for real worry over serious wrongs, you need fixed times, with a settled mind, to think about the greatness of the One you wronged, so the heart truly breaks. Right after that, drop the sadness entirely and believe with full faith that He has taken away your sin and "forgives abundantly." That is the true joy after sorrow.

And if the sadness is from bad thoughts and desires that fall into your mind? If they come outside prayer and learning, in the middle of work, the opposite: be glad. They fell into your mind and you turned away from them. That keeps the command "do not stray after your heart and your eyes." The verse is not speaking of the righteous; it is speaking of beinonim, to whom such thoughts come, even about things that are allowed. When you turn away, you have kept that command. The sages said, "One who sat and did not sin is rewarded as if he did a mitzvah." So be glad as over a mitzvah.

Here is the honest turn: the sadness is pride. You do not know your place. You feel bad that you are not a tzaddik (a righteous person, in whom the bad is gone), and to a tzaddik such foolish thoughts surely do not come. If you knew your place, far from a tzaddik, and if only you were a beinoni and not a wicked person even for an hour, you would see that this is exactly the beinoni's task: to push down the thought that rises from heart to mind, to turn from it completely, to push it off with both hands.

And every time you push it away, the other side is bent down below. What is woken below wakes above, and the other side above, which lifts itself like an eagle, is brought down: "If you soar like an eagle, from there I will bring you down." The Zohar says there is no joy before Him like this, when the other side is bent below and His glory rises above everything.

So do not lose heart, even if it is like this all your life. Perhaps for this you were made. This is your work: to bend the other side, always. Job said "You created the wicked," meaning people to whom the thoughts of the wicked come, only as thoughts, who fight them all their lives and never wipe them out, since that is the work of tzaddikim.

There are two kinds of pleasure before Him. One, when the other side is wiped out and bitter turns sweet, darkness to light, by the tzaddikim. The second, when the other side is bent while still strong and soaring, by the beinonim. "Make me tasty foods, as I love": foods in the plural, two kinds, as in real food there are sweet dishes, and sharp or sour ones that are seasoned so well they revive the soul.

Even in things fully allowed, whenever you hold back a desire even a little, to bend the other side: you want to eat and put off the meal for an hour and learn instead (the sages' students waited two hours for this reason). You hold back from saying the worldly words your heart very much wants to say. Even a small thought held back. His glory rises greatly above, and from that holiness comes down on you to help you, much. "A person sanctifies himself a little below, and he is sanctified a lot from above." "Make yourselves holy," though you are not, since the pull is still strong in you, "and you will be holy": in the end, with that help, you will drive it out of your heart little by little.

Where the picture breaks: two dishes are for a meal. This is not food for Him. It is His will being done in a place that fights it.

**The movement.** From shame and sadness at your own bad thoughts, to the quiet joy of a beinoni who is doing his exact job. What carries a person across: knowing your place truthfully, and the act of pushing the thought away, over and over, and holding back a little in what is allowed.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Perhaps for this I was made." 2. Recall the last stray thought you pushed off. Think it through: that was not failure. That was the task, done. 3. Bring it down to one allowed thing today: wait a little before a meal, or keep one sentence unsaid. 4. The picture breaks: you will not feel His joy. You are told it is there. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "If this is my work all my life, I take it gladly." 6. Hold back once today, and learn one line in that time.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a bad thought no longer ruins your day, and you turn from it almost gladly. The fake is being fine with the thoughts because "it's just who I am," and no longer pushing them off.

## Chapter 28 · Don't answer the voice

**The state.** Prayer while a foreign thought (machshavah zarah, a stray thought that doesn't belong) keeps falling in: the state of standing in prayer or learning while another voice keeps talking. The chapter teaches it is not a sign the prayer is worthless; it is a sign the prayer is strong.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Even if thoughts of desire and other strange thoughts fall into your mind while you learn or pray with intent, don't give them your heart. Turn your mind from them at once.

Don't be foolish and try to "raise up" the stray thought, as some teachings describe. That was said only for tzaddikim, to whom stray thoughts come from others and not from themselves. A thought that comes from your own heart, from the bad in you, how will you lift it, when you yourself are tied down below?

And still, don't let your heart fall and become sad during prayer, which needs great joy. The opposite: gather more strength and pray with more intent and more gladness. Take to heart that the stray thought comes from the pull in you that is at war with your G-dly soul. Wrestlers are like this: when one grows stronger, the other strains with all his power. So when your G-dly soul makes an effort to pray, the other one strains against it to confuse it and throw it into its own thoughts.

People make a mistake here. They say, "If stray thoughts come, my prayer is nothing; if I prayed right, they wouldn't come." That would be true if you had one soul, the same one praying and the same one thinking the stray thoughts. But in truth there are two souls fighting in your mind. Each wants to rule and fill the mind alone. Every thought of Torah and awe of heaven is from the G-dly soul. Every worldly thought is from the animal soul, only the G-dly soul is dressed in it. From inside, then, the state is: I am praying, and someone else is talking in my head.

The picture: you are praying with intent and a hostile, coarse man (the Tanya says "an uncircumcised wicked man") stands in front of you talking, joking, trying to throw you off. The plan is clear. Don't answer him, good or bad. Act as if you are deaf. "Don't answer a fool in his folly, lest you become like him." So with the stray thought: give no answer, no argument. One who wrestles with a filthy man gets filthy too. Act as if you don't know and don't hear it, put it out of your mind, and add strength to your intent.

And if it is hard, because the thoughts press hard, then bring yourself low before G-d and plead with Him in your thought to have mercy, as a father has mercy on children born from his own mind. So G-d will have mercy on your soul, which comes from Him, and save it from "the wild waters." He will do it for His own sake, because "His people is a very part of G-d."

Where the picture breaks: the man in front of you is outside. The voice is inside your own head, and that is exactly why it feels like yours. It isn't the one praying.

**The movement.** From "these thoughts prove my prayer is worthless," to "two souls are at war and mine is the one praying." What carries a person across: not answering, turning away, adding strength to intent, and when that fails, words to the Father.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Father, have mercy on what came from You." 2. Think back to the last prayer that was full of other thoughts. The one who was praying was you. The talker was the other. 3. Bring it down: next time, one word of prayer said with more strength right when a stray thought comes, not less. 4. The picture breaks: you cannot throw the talker out by force. Stop fighting him. Turn your face. 5. Say to Him in your thought: "I come from You. Save me from the wild waters." 6. Pray one short prayer today slowly, and let no stray thought get an answer.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a stray thought passes through and your prayer gets stronger instead of stopping. The fake is arguing with the thought for ten minutes and calling that holy struggle.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 28 (line 402): כְּרַחֵם אָב עַל בָּנִים הַנִּמְשָׁכִים מִמּוֹחוֹ*

## Chapter 29 · The log that won't catch fire

**The state.** Timtum halev (the blocked heart): the state in which the heart is like stone, you cannot open it to prayer, you understand G-d's greatness and feel nothing, and the mind cannot rule the heart. The way out is shiflus (lowliness): bringing the swollen coarse self down, and then seeing, as plainly as with your eyes, that He fills this very place.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Beinonim often have this: the heart goes stone-dull and will not open for prayer, the service of the heart. Sometimes the heaviness is so great they cannot even fight to hold back a little in what is allowed.

The Zohar gives the counsel. "A log that the flame will not catch, you split it up. A body that the light of the soul will not catch, you break it." The light of the soul means the soul's light and understanding do not shine strongly enough to rule the coarseness of the body. You understand His greatness in your mind, but it does not grip your mind firmly enough to rule the coarse heart. The cause is the coarseness of the husk (kelipah, the shell over the light), which lifts itself up over the light of your G-dly soul and darkens it.

So you have to break it and bring it down to the dust. Set times to lower yourself, "despised in his own eyes," with a broken heart. The "broken spirit" is the other side, and in a beinoni the other side is the person himself, because the animal soul that gives life to the body is still at full strength in his heart. Of the G-dly soul it says "the soul You gave in me is pure": gave in me, so I myself am not that pure soul. In a tzaddik it is the other way around. The G-dly soul is the person, and the body is "the flesh of the person." Hillel, going to eat, told his students he was going to do a kindness to "the poor, lowly one," his body, which to him was like a stranger. In a beinoni the animal soul has not been turned to good, so it is the person himself.

Here the Tanya is hard and plain. Such a person is far from G-d. His animal soul can still want forbidden things, even if he would never do them, because they are not truly disgusting to him. In this he is lower than unclean creatures: "I am a worm and not a man." Even the love he wakes in prayer is not fully true, since it passes after prayer. All the more when he remembers the wrongs of his youth and what they did above, where time does not pass, as if he did them today. Even after real teshuvah, teshuvah is in the heart, and the heart has many depths. If today the light of the soul will not catch, perhaps today his teshuvah was not accepted, or perhaps he is being lifted to a deeper teshuvah. "My sin is before me always." Even one clean of serious wrongs should do a reckoning of every thought, word and deed from his first day: were they for G-d or not? Every thought is a chariot for holiness or for its opposite. Most of his dreams are empty, because his soul does not rise.

Read this gently and truly. The target is not your worth. It is the swollen, coarse "I" that sits over your light like a wet log over a flame. The longer you dwell on these things, in thought and in books, until your heart breaks and your own swollen life looks worthless to you, the more you bring down the other side from its height. Then speak to it with anger in your mind, as the sages said, "Let the good pull rage at the bad." Say: "You are bad and wicked. How long will you hide the light of the Infinite that fills all worlds, that was, is and will be the same, even in this very place where I stand, just as before the world was made? You deny what is plain to the eyes: that before Him everything is truly nothing."

That helps the G-dly soul open its eyes to the oneness of the Infinite as if seeing it, not only hearing and understanding. That is the root of all the service.

Why does it work? Because the other side has no real substance. It is like darkness, which has nothing in it and is simply pushed away by light. All its life comes from holiness, so before holiness it is nothing, as darkness before light. Only against the G-dly soul in a person has G-d given it leave to lift itself, so that the person will wake up and bring it down by lowering himself. And when you move below, He moves above: "from there I will bring you down." He takes away its leave, and it falls away on its own like darkness before light.

The spies show this. First they said "he is stronger than us," meaning, than Him; they did not believe. Then they said "we will go up." Moshe showed them no sign in between. He only told them G-d was angry and they would die in the desert. Their hearts broke, "the people mourned greatly," and the other side fell from its height. The faith had been there all along. So anyone who has doubts about faith can learn: the doubts are the other side's voice lifting itself over your soul. You yourself believe. The other side itself has no doubts; it only has leave to confuse, like a harlot sent by the king to test his son.

Where the picture breaks: a log is split with an axe. Here what breaks is not you. It is the stone over you, and the one splitting it is the soul.

**The movement.** From a heart like stone that understands and feels nothing, to a soul whose eyes are open to "He is here." What carries a person across: fixed times of lowering yourself, reckoning, speaking hard truth to the coarse self, and then the sight of the One in this place.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "He fills this very place where I stand." 2. Think through, slowly, one day of your thoughts, words and acts. Which were for Him and which were only for you? 3. Let the swollen "I" look small. Then speak to it in your mind: "How long will you hide Him?" 4. Bring it down to the room you are in. He is here exactly as before anything was made. The picture breaks: you still may not feel it. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "I believe. The doubt is not me." 6. Pray one prayer today right after this, while the log is split.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when after the lowering a quiet, open lightness comes, and the room feels full. The fake is a crushing self-disgust that stays crushed and never looks up to the light.

## Chapter 30 · Lower than every person

**The state.** Humility before every person (shiflus ruach, a low spirit, in truth and not for show): the state in which you truly feel yourself lower than the lightest of people, because you measure your own battle against theirs and find yours smaller.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "Be low in spirit before every person." Truly, and before every person, even the lightest of the light. How? By the sages' saying: "Do not judge your fellow until you reach his place."

His place makes him sin. He earns his bread in the market all day, one of those who sit on the corners. His eyes see every desire, "the eye sees and the heart wants," and his pull burns "like an oven heated by the baker." Someone who is in the market a little and mostly at home is different. And even one in the market all day may not be so hot by nature. Pulls are not equal in every soul.

This is not an excuse. The hot one has no defense for his sins, and is called fully wicked, because he should have held back for fear of G-d who sees all he does. The mind rules the heart by nature. Still, to break a pull that burns like fire, for fear of G-d, is a great and terrible war, like a real test.

So each person, in his own place and level, has to weigh himself honestly. Do I serve G-d with a war that big, a test like that? In prayer: pouring out the soul before Him with all my strength, till the soul is spent, fighting my body and the animal soul that stop my intent, breaking them before every morning and evening prayer, and working hard in soul and body in the prayer itself. Anyone who has not reached that has not reached the size of the war that the burning man in the market fights. The same for blessings, for mitzvos with intent, for learning much more than your nature wants. Learning a little more than you like is a small war. It is nothing next to that man's war. "Turn from bad" or "do good": both are the command of the one King.

Even in "turn from bad," a thinking person can find places where he does not turn away completely, where it would take a great war, or even less: stopping in the middle of a good story, or a story that puts someone down, even a small, true slight, even to clear yourself. (Rebbe's son said "Yehudah the tailor wrote it, not I," and was told "Keep away from slander.") So many things like this come up. Holding back in what is allowed is a command of the Torah: "Be holy." These become "sins a person tramples with his heels," and after you repeat them they feel allowed.

But if you know Torah and hold on to it and want to be close to G-d, your guilt for not fighting a war that size is far greater than the guilt of the lightest man on the corner. He is far from G-d and Torah. For him "willful sins are like mistakes." For you, who are near, it is like the sage who was told, "he knew My honor."

So, from inside, the state is: you look at the roughest person you know and see a man in a terrible war. You look at yourself and see small wars you often skip. And you feel, truly, lower than him.

Where the picture breaks: you cannot really stand in his place. You can only know that you have not, and stop judging.

**The movement.** From quiet pride over people who sin more openly, to true lowliness before every person. What carries a person across: weighing your own wars honestly against theirs, detail by detail.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Not until I reach his place." 2. Picture one person you look down on. Put yourself in his day: his street, his eyes, his heat. 3. Now weigh your own day: what war did you fight that was as big as his? 4. The picture breaks: you cannot know his heart fully. Stop there, and stop judging. 5. Say to Him: "Forgive me for the small wars I skipped." 6. Today, stop one sentence halfway that would have put someone down.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you look at a rough person and feel respect and even a little awe. The fake is saying "I'm the worst" while inside you are still keeping score.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 30 (line 429): אַל תָּדִין אֶת חֲבֵירָךְ – עַד שֶׁתַּגִּיעַ לִמְקוֹמוֹ*

## Chapter 31 · The prince let out of prison

**The state.** Bitter heart turning into joy: merirus (bitterness, a heart that is alive and aching) as opposed to atzvus (sadness, a heart that is dead like stone), and the joy that comes after it, the joy of the soul going out of exile and home to its Father's house.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Even if you spend an hour or two going deep into the things of chapter 29, lowering yourself with a broken heart, and it brings a great sadness, don't worry. It's true that sadness is not from holiness. Holiness is "strength and gladness in His place." The Presence rests only where there is joy. (The Ari wrote that even worry over sins belongs only to the time of confession, not to prayer or learning, which need joy from the holy side.) Sadness over heavenly things comes from the good side of the mixed shell. Still, this is how you bend the other side: with its own kind. "From the forest itself comes the axe handle that cuts it." "In all sadness there is gain," and the gain is the joy that comes after it.

Then the chapter makes a fine distinction. In truth, a broken heart and a bitter soul over being far from the light of G-d's face are not "sadness" at all in the holy tongue. Sadness is a heart gone dull like a stone with no life in it. Bitterness and a broken heart have life in them, life that is moved and aches. It is the life of holy severity (gevuros), while joy is the life of kindness (chasadim), and the heart holds both. Sometimes you need to wake the holy severity to sweeten the harsh forces of the animal soul when it rules you, because harshness is sweetened only at its root. That is why the sages said, "let a person always rouse the good pull," whenever you see you need it.

The best time for most people: when you are sad anyway, about worldly things or for no reason at all. That is the moment to turn the sadness into a reckoning, and so you are also rid of the worldly sadness.

After that comes true joy. Say to your heart, doubly comforting yourself: "Yes, it is true without doubt, I am very far from G-d, and all of that. But all that is only me, the body and the life-soul in it. Inside me there is a real part of G-d, which is there even in the lightest of the light, a G-dly soul with a spark of G-d in it, only it is in exile. So the further I am, the greater its exile and the greater the pity on it. That will be my whole aim: to take it out of this exile and bring it back to its Father's house as in its youth, before it came into my body, when it was held in His light and one with Him. It will be one with Him now too, when I put all of myself into Torah and mitzvos, and above all prayer, to cry out to G-d for it in its trouble, to bring it out of prison and let it cling to Him."

That is "teshuvah and good deeds": good deeds done to bring the part of G-d back to its source. This is your work all your days, with great joy: the joy of the soul going out of the body and "returning to her Father's house as in her youth" during learning and prayer. There is no joy like going out of exile and captivity. The Tanya's picture: a king's son held captive, grinding at the mill in prison, filthy in the dung heap, and then set free and brought home to his father the king.

The body stays what it is. The Zohar calls it "a snake's skin," because the animal soul has not been changed to good. But let your soul be dearer to you than your low body, so that the body's grief does not spoil the soul's joy.

This is the going out of Egypt, about which it says "the people fled." Why flee? If they had asked Pharaoh to let them go forever, he would have had to. Because the bad in them was still at full strength. It did not stop until Sinai. Their only aim was for the G-dly soul to get out and cling to G-d. "G-d is my strength and my refuge," "my escape." So in the time to come, when impurity is gone from the earth, "you will not go in haste or in flight, for G-d goes before you."

And for this teshuvah to go deeper, and the joy to be brighter, tell your heart: "True, all that. But I did not make myself. Why did G-d do this, lower a part of His light that fills and surrounds all worlds and dress it in a snake's skin and a foul drop? It must be that this going down is for the sake of going up: to lift up the animal soul and its thought, speech and action, by dressing them in the thought, speech and action of Torah. Then this is what I will do all my days: tie my thought and speech to His thought and speech, which are the laws before us, and my action to the mitzvos." That is why the Torah is called "restoring the soul," back to its root. "G-d's commands are straight, making the heart glad."

Where the picture breaks: a freed prince leaves the prison behind. You don't leave your body. You lift it with you.

**The movement.** From sadness that is dead, to bitterness that is alive, to a joy that knows it is doubly true. What carries a person across: the words said to your own heart ("True, but there is a part of G-d in me," and "I did not make myself; this going down is for going up"), then Torah, prayer and mitzvos.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "There is a real part of G-d inside me." 2. Admit plainly one way you are far from Him. Don't soften it. 3. Then think: all that is the outer me. Inside is a part of Him, in prison. The further I am, the more it needs me. 4. The picture breaks: you are not leaving your body. You are lifting it. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "I did not make myself. Help me bring Your part home." 6. Learn a few lines of Torah or say a psalm today, as a way of opening the prison door.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when after a real bitter hour you feel light and eager, like someone let out. The fake is sitting in the bitterness because it feels deep, and never letting the joy come.

## Chapter 32 · One Father

**The state.** Ahavas Yisrael (love for another person as for yourself), as a state of consciousness: when your body is small in your eyes and your soul is what you are, you see other people from the soul, and all souls are twins with one Father.

**The chapter as consciousness.** When you live the last chapters, your body low in your own eyes and your joy the soul's joy alone, this is a straight and easy way to keep "love your fellow as yourself" toward every soul, great or small.

Why easy? Because the body is not your pride any more, and who can know how great the soul and spirit are, in their root in the living G-d? They are all matched, twins, and they have one Father. That is why they are called real brothers, from the root of the soul in the one G-d. Only the bodies are separate. So people who make the body the main thing and the soul second cannot have true love and brotherhood. Their love can only hang on something, and when it goes the love goes.

From inside, this means the love of others is not a separate feeling you must work up. It follows from where you stand. Stand in the body and people are other bodies, rivals and strangers. Stand in the soul and they are your own family.

So Hillel said of this command, "This is the whole Torah; the rest is commentary." The base and root of the Torah is to lift the soul above the body, up to the root of all worlds, and to draw the Infinite light into the source of all souls, "to be one in One," and not when there is division between souls, since "the Holy One does not rest in a broken place." As we pray, "Bless us, our Father, all of us as one, in the light of Your face."

What of the Talmud's saying that if you see your friend sin, it is a mitzvah to hate him? That is for a friend in Torah and mitzvos, whom you already rebuked and he did not turn back. But one who is not your friend or near you, Hillel said: "Be a student of Aaron, loving peace, loving people (beriyos, plain creatures) and bringing them close to the Torah." Even those far from Torah, called simply "creatures," you should pull close with thick ropes of love. Perhaps you will bring them near. If not, you have not lost the mitzvah of love.

And even those near you who were rebuked and did not change, whom it is a mitzvah to hate: it is a mitzvah to love them too. Both are true. Hate for the bad in them, love for the hidden good, the spark of G-d in them that gives life to their G-dly soul. And wake pity in your heart for it, since it is in exile inside the bad. Pity cancels hate and wakes love. ("Jacob who redeemed Abraham": mercy, Jacob's trait, redeems.) King David's "I hated them with total hate" was only about heretics who have no share in the G-d of Israel.

Where the picture breaks: twins know each other on sight. We don't see the soul. We have to believe it and act on it.

**The movement.** From seeing people as bodies, to seeing them as souls with one Father. What carries a person across: lowering the body in your own eyes (chapters 29 to 31), then ropes of love, and pity for the spark in exile.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "One Father to us all." 2. Picture one person you find hard. Look past the face and the habits to the part of G-d in him. 3. Think: it is in exile in him, maybe more than mine is in me. Let pity come. 4. The picture breaks: you can't see his soul. Stop there, and believe it. 5. Say to Him: "Bless us, our Father, all of us as one." 6. Do one warm thing today for someone far from you, with no hope of bringing him close.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you feel toward a stranger what you feel toward a brother, without a reason. The fake is a warm glow for "everyone" in general, and coldness to the one person in front of you.

## Chapter 33 · He lives here

**The state.** The joy of faith in the true unity: the state of seeing, deeply and at length, that He alone fills this very place as before the world was made, and that this is His dirah (dwelling) in the lowest world. The Tanya calls it real nearness to G-d, and says faith like this lets a person live as if he had come back to life.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Another true joy of the soul, above all when you see at set times that your soul needs to be cleaned and lit with gladness. Then go deep in your mind and picture with your understanding His true oneness: how He fills all worlds, high and low, and even this whole earth is full of His glory, and everything before Him is as nothing. He alone is, above and below, exactly as He was alone before the six days of creation. And in this very place where this world was made, the heavens and the earth and all in them, He alone filled this place, and so now, He alone, without any change.

Why? Because all creatures are given over to Him, as nothing, the way letters of speech and thought are nothing in their source, the soul's own wisdom, understanding and knowing, which have no letters yet (chapters 20 and 21). Or the picture of the sun: its light is surely there inside the sun's body too, brighter than out in the world, but there it is swallowed up in its source as if it did not exist. That is exactly how the world and everything in it are swallowed up next to their source, the Infinite light.

When you go deep in this for a long time, your heart will be glad and your soul will sing with all your heart and soul and strength in this faith, because it is huge. It is nearness to G-d itself. And this is the whole of man and the purpose of his creation and of all the worlds: that He should have a dwelling here below.

How great is the joy of a plain, lowly man when a flesh and blood king comes to stay and live with him in his house. How much more, without end, the nearness and dwelling of the King of kings. "Who is it whose heart dares to come near to Me?"

That is why we thank Him every morning: "How fortunate we are, how good our portion, how beautiful our inheritance." Just as a man is glad at a huge inheritance he did not work for, so and far more we should be glad at what our fathers left us: the true oneness of G-d, that even on the earth below there is nothing else besides Him, and this is His dwelling below.

The sages said, "Habakkuk stood all 613 commands on one: 'The righteous will live by his faith.'" As if there were only one command, faith. When your heart is glad with the faith in His oneness, as if this were your only command and the only purpose of your creation and of all the worlds, then by the power and life of that joy your soul rises higher and higher, above everything inside and outside you that holds you back from keeping all of them. "Will live": like the dead coming back to life, your soul will live in this great joy.

And the joy is doubled. Besides the joy of the thinking soul in being near G-d and in His living with it, there is the joy of His joy, the pleasure before Him in this faith. The other side is really bent and darkness is turned to light: the darkness of the husks in this physical world that cover His light until the end of days, when "the glory of G-d will be revealed and all flesh will see it together." Above all outside the Land of Israel, where the air is full of husks, there is no joy before Him like light that comes out of darkness. "Let Israel rejoice in its Makers": "Makers" in the plural, this physical world full of division, a "public domain" of many, turned into a "private domain" for His oneness, by this faith.

Where the picture breaks: a king who stays in your house is still a guest who came from somewhere. He did not come from anywhere. He is the place.

**The movement.** From a world that stands on its own, to the same world seen as full of Him alone, and from there to joy. What carries a person across: going deep, at set times, picturing His unity in detail with the mind, and bringing it down to this very place, until the heart is glad; then letting that joy carry you over everything that holds you back.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "Even here, there is nothing else besides Him." 2. Picture the place you are sitting before the world was made: only Him. Now, the same: only Him, with no change. 3. Go through it one thing at a time: the floor, the walls, the sky outside, your own hands. Each is swallowed in its source like sunlight inside the sun. 4. The picture breaks: sunlight inside the sun is still light. Here there is nothing beside Him at all. Stop at the edge. 5. Say to Him: "How good is my portion. You chose to live here." 6. Tomorrow morning say "How fortunate we are" slowly, and mean this place.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when an ordinary room feels like a palace with its King at home, and you want to do His will without being pushed. The fake is a lofty "all is G-d" that makes the room and the people in it matter less, not more.

## Chapter 34 · A room for Him

**The state.** Being a mishkan (a sanctuary, a dwelling place) for His oneness in your own measure: the state of a person who knows he cannot hold G-d with his mind, and so makes Him a room in fixed hours of Torah and in giving, glad to host Him; and who can hold weeping and joy in one heart at the same time.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "The fathers were the chariot." All their days they never stopped, even for an hour, binding their mind and soul to the Master of the world, given over to His oneness. After them the prophets, each by his soul's level; Moshe's above all, "the Presence spoke from Moshe's throat." At Sinai the whole people had a taste of it but could not bear it: at every word their souls flew out. That is bittul, the self given over until it is gone. So He told them at once to build Him a sanctuary with a Holy of Holies, where His Presence would rest, the showing of His oneness.

Since the Temple was destroyed, He has no sanctuary in His world but the four cubits of halachah (Jewish law), His will and wisdom dressed in the laws in front of us. So after you go as deep as you can into the bittul of the last chapters, say this to your heart: "My mind is too small, my soul's root too low, to be a true chariot and sanctuary for His oneness. My thought cannot grasp Him at all, not a trace of what the fathers and prophets grasped. So I will make Him a sanctuary and a place to live: learning Torah as much as I have time, in fixed hours by day and by night, as the law gives to each person. Even one chapter in the morning and one at night."

From inside, this state is modest and happy at once. You stop trying to be a Moshe. You take the size you are and build a room that size. And your heart is glad and thanks Him for your portion, that you get to host the Almighty twice a day, each by his time and means.

If G-d gives you more room, then "clean hands add strength." And the rest of your day, in business, can also be His place, by the charity you give from your earnings. Giving is one of His own traits: "As He is merciful, so be merciful." "Kindness is the right arm." Even if you give only a fifth, that fifth lifts the other four parts with it to G-d, to be His dwelling, as charity equals all the offerings. In the offerings, one animal lifted all animal life, one measure of flour all plant life. And in the hours of Torah and prayer, everything you ate and drank from the other four parts for your body's health rises to G-d as well.

And none of these joys stops you from being low in your own eyes, with a broken heart, at the very moment of the joy. Being low is from the side of the body and the animal soul. The joy is from the side of the G-dly soul and the spark of G-d in it (chapter 31). The Zohar says: "Weeping is lodged in my heart on this side, and joy is lodged in my heart on that side."

Where the picture breaks: a room for a guest is built once. This room is built again every day, in hours and coins.

**The movement.** From wanting to hold G-d with the mind and failing, to making Him a dwelling in the size you are. What carries a person across: fixed times for Torah, day and night, and giving from your work; and knowing that weeping and joy can live together because they come from two sides.

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "I will make Him a room in my day." 2. Admit it plainly: my mind cannot hold Him. Not a trace. 3. Bring it down to one real thing: which hour today, and which hour tonight, will be His room? How much of what I earn? 4. The picture breaks: He does not need the room. He chose it. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "I am small. Come and stay anyway." 6. Set one fixed time for learning tonight, and give one coin before it.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when your fixed learning time feels like a visit you look forward to, and you can be sorry and glad in the same moment. The fake is a big plan to learn all day that never becomes one steady hour.

## Chapter 35 · The wick, the oil and the light

**The state.** The Presence resting on the body (hashra'as haShechinah, the resting of G-d's Presence): the state of a beinoni doing a mitzvah with his hands, when for that time even the animal soul inside the act is given over and one with G-d's light, and the Presence hovers over his whole body from head to foot. It answers the beinoni's question: why was I sent down to fight a battle I cannot finish?

**The chapter as consciousness.** Here is the beinoni's hard question. His animal soul is at full strength all his life. He can keep it from acting, but he can never drive it out of the heart so that no thought of it rises to the mind. So why did his soul come down to wear this animal soul? To toil for nothing, fighting all his life a pull he can never beat?

Let this be his comfort, doubled, and let his heart be glad in G-d who lives with him inside his Torah and his service.

The Zohar's child explains "The wise man's eyes are in his head." Where else would a person's eyes be? It means: a person should not walk four cubits with his head bare, because the Presence rests on his head. A wise man's eyes are on that light over his head, to know that the light lit over his head needs oil. For "a man's body is a wick, and the light is lit above." King Solomon cried, "Let oil not be lacking on your head," for the light on his head needs oil, and the oil is good deeds.

What does the picture mean? A flame will not hold to a wick without oil. So the Presence does not rest on the body, the wick, except through good deeds. The soul alone is not enough to be the oil, even though it is a part of G-d from above. Why? Because a soul, even a perfect tzaddik serving with awe and love and delight, is still not wholly given over and lost in G-d's light, one with it. He is a someone, one who fears G-d and loves Him. The mitzvos are different. They are His will, and His will is the source of life of all worlds. That life reaches creatures only through many shrinkings and hidings, so they can exist as things. But the mitzvos are His inner will, with no hiding at all, so the life in them is not a separate thing. It is one with His will, completely.

The Presence resting on something means G-d's light showing in it, which means that thing is swallowed up in His light and given over to it completely. Only then does the One rest and show in it. Whatever is not wholly given over, His light does not rest in. Even a tzaddik who clings to Him with great love: "no thought can grasp Him," because the truth of G-d is His oneness, that He alone is and there is nothing besides Him. So the lover, who is a something and not nothing, cannot grasp Him with his thought, and G-d's light does not rest on him except by doing the mitzvos, which are His will and wisdom themselves with no hiding. (In a note: the Tanya's teacher explained why the Infinite joins even the highest world only by first dressing in chochmah; because He is truly One, alone, and that is the level of chochmah, the self that is nothing.)

So when you learn Torah, your G-dly soul with its thought and speech is taken into the Infinite and one with it. That is the Presence resting on your G-dly soul: "even one who sits and learns, the Presence is with him."

But to draw the Presence onto the body and the animal soul too, you need mitzvos done with the body itself. Then the body's real strength in that act is taken into G-d's light and one with it; it is the G-dly soul's third garment, action. And then the strength of the animal soul in your body turns from bad to good and is taken into holiness like the G-dly soul, because it is the one doing the act. Without it the G-dly soul, which is spirit, could not move the body, which is matter. The animal soul, in the blood of the heart and the whole body, is the bridge between them.

True, its core, the bad traits in the heart, is not yet taken into holiness. But since they are bent to holiness and answer "amen" against their will, agreeing to the mitzvah because the G-dly soul in the mind rules the heart, they are for that time in exile and asleep, and that does not stop the Presence from resting on the body then. The power of the animal soul dressed in the act is taken into G-d's light, and through it a light shines on the whole animal soul in the body and on the physical body, as a surrounding light from above, from head to foot. That is why it says the Presence rests on his head: "on," above. So too, "on every ten the Presence rests."

And none of this drawing down of the Presence is a change in Him or a making Him many. A heretic asked Rabban Gamliel, "If the Presence rests on every ten, how many Presences do you have?" He answered with the sun shining in through many windows.

So from inside, the beinoni's state is not a battle for nothing. Every mitzvah done with the hands is oil. For that time, the very body and the very pull that fight him become a wick with the light of G-d burning over it.

Where the picture breaks: a wick burns up. Your body is not used up by the light. It is lifted by it.

**The movement.** From "why am I here, if I can never win?" to "I am a wick, and every act is oil for His light." What carries a person across: the physical act of a mitzvah, done with the body, with the mind ruling the heart so the pull says "amen."

**Entering it.** 1. Hold one line: "My body is a wick, and the light is lit above." 2. Think of one mitzvah you will do today with your hands: giving, lighting a candle, washing hands, helping someone carry something. 3. Think it through: the hand, the strength in it, even the part of you that did not want to, is for that moment His. 4. The picture breaks: you will not see the light. It is above your head. Stop there. 5. Say to Him: "Let there be oil on my head today." 6. Do that one act slowly, with the whole body, and know the Presence is over you.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when doing a plain act feels like your whole self, body included, is quietly on the right side. The fake is thinking the good feeling you got is the light; the light is the act, and it rests above you whether you feel it or not.
