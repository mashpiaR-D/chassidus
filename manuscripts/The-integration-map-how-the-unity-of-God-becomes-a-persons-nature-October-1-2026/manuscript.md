*Chassidus Unity Index, 2026-10-01. The process, as the Rebbeim describe it, by which "there is nothing but Him" moves from an idea a person hears to a truth that lives in the mind, the heart, the everyday self, the body and the day, and becomes the person's nature. ## The path in 12 sentences

1. The books do not ask a person to believe the unity of G-d harder; they ask him to hold it, because in Chassidus to know (da'at) is to bind the mind to a truth until it is as real as a thing you see.
2. So the path starts before the thinking: a quiet place, worries set down, the yoke of Heaven accepted, a coin given, a little learning, and a heart that is open and glad, not heavy.
3. Then one subject is chosen, from what was learned, at the person's own level and aimed at the person's own struggle: usually a near truth, like 'this is being made right now'.
4. That one subject is first known well, then thought through slowly in its details (breadth), brought down in pictures to the person's own life (length), and only then held in its bare point (depth).
5. The mind is kept there and brought back every time it wanders, without arguing with the stray thought and without sadness, until the truth is seen 'as if with the eyes'.
6. When it is held long enough, the heart answers by itself, the way water gives back a face: the books call this the returning to the heart, and they say the mind rules the heart by nature.
7. Feeling is never forced; heat made by wanting heat is 'serving oneself', and when no feeling comes, the hidden love and the accepted yoke still count as full service.
8. The G-dly soul understands quickly; the long work is teaching the everyday self, the animal soul, in its own plain terms and details, until it too agrees and then wants; the order of prayer is built for this.
9. What was felt must be said with the mouth and done with the hand that same day, because light holds only in a vessel and a high with no deed is 'a flame flying in the air'.
10. The same subject is taken again day after day, a little deeper each time, until habit becomes nature: it comes back by itself, an insult stings less, one trait softens; a fall is met by returning to the One, then joy.
11. Past 'only Him' the path keeps going down into the self, to the will under love and a bond that cannot be cut, and then it turns back down into the world, the body and the plain deed, which the books call the main thing.
12. Every struggle a person brings enters this path at its own stage (heaviness at the preparation, a wandering mind at the binding, numbness at the heart, a pull at the animal soul and the deed), and there the books' teaching for it becomes the door.

## The stages

<a id="i1"></a> ### I1 · Prepare the vessel *What comes before?*

**What happens.** Before a truth can come in, there has to be room for it. The books say a light that only hovers needs no vessel, but a light that enters does. So first the person clears the room: stops rushing, sets worries down, accepts G-d as King, gives something, learns a little, and opens the heart with joy. Without this, the person gets a quick general glow, and then feels cold the moment real detail starts.

1. Fix the hour and the place, and stand or sit in one place; walking about only widens 'the outer spirit' (Rashab, first preparation). [stated]
2. Set down your worries the way you set them down every night to fall asleep; wait 'two doorways' before you begin (Rashab, first preparation). [stated]
3. Accept the yoke first: think, for as long as your mind and time allow, that He is King of all worlds and has chosen to be King over you, that He stands over you and looks into your heart, and take Him on as King. The Alter Rebbe calls this 'the beginning of the service, its main part and its root'. [stated]
4. Give: charity before prayer adds much life to it (Hayom Yom); say 'I take on myself to love my fellow as myself' (the Alter Rebbe's siddur). Immersion in a mikveh with its intention is the Baal Shem Tov's counsel. [stated; the mikveh line is the Besht's, not a Rebbe of Chabad's]
5. Learn: a half hour or more of Chassidus on one subject before prayer, in tallis and tefillin, 'so his mind and heart open to be a vessel' (Rashab, second preparation); before prayer the aim is to feel the life of what you learned (Hayom Yom). [stated]
6. Seriousness, not sadness: an honest look at what makes you unfit to be His dwelling, but only at set times (best at midnight), and always followed by joy; the lower return comes before prayer, which is the higher return (Rashab, third preparation; Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah 10). [stated]
7. Compassion on your own soul, 'from a high roof to a deep pit': this, the Rashab says, is easy, and it opens mind and heart (fourth preparation). [stated]
8. Joy: the struggle cannot be won with heaviness, only with quickness that comes from joy and an open heart (Tanya 26). After any bitterness, gladden the soul with Tanya 31 and 33, 'so as to pray with joy' (Rashab). [stated]
9. Then the threshold: you are about to stand before the King and say 'You' (Rashab, fifth preparation). [stated]
10. Putting all these in one line, in this order, is ours. The Rashab's five preparations are in his order. [ours]

**What goes wrong, and the books' remedy.**

| Counterfeit or stuck | Remedy |
| --- | --- |
| Skipping the preparation: a general excitement, then cold as soon as detail starts. | The Rashab: this is because 'he is not a vessel'; general light needs no vessel, inner light does. Prepare, then begin. (Kuntres HaTefillah 11) |
| Bitterness turned into a daily mood, or used on a low day. | Only at set times, briefly, and then joy; sadness is a heart of stone, bitterness has life in it (Tanya 26, 31; Kuntres HaTefillah 11). |
| Worries will not leave. | Make the Rashab's reckoning: you set them down every night to sleep; for your soul's whole life you can do the same. |
| Pacing about to 'feel wide'. | Stand in one place; that width is only 'the outer spirit' (Rashab). |

**Time.** Daily, before the main sitting or prayer. A minute at the door on a short day; a half hour or more of learning on a full day. The bitterness is not daily: at set times only.

**In a chat** *(ours)*. "Before we start, let's make a little room. Where are you sitting right now? Stay there. Is there one worry on top? Put it down for ten minutes, the way you put it down every night to fall asleep; it will still be there after. Now one plain thought: the One who made everything is choosing, right now, to be your King, and He is here. You don't have to feel anything about it. Just say yes to it once. If you can, put a coin aside for someone today. Good. Now we can begin."

**Stated or ours.** stated: each preparation (Rashab's five; Tanya 41's yoke first; Tanya 26 joy; Hayom Yom on charity and the life of what was learned; Besht on the mikveh). ours: the single sequence and the short chat form.

<a id="i2"></a> ### I2 · Choose the one thing *Which aspect of the unity, and how is it chosen?*

**What happens.** The whole sitting gets one subject. The Mitteler Rebbe defines knowing as gathering all the power of the mind into one idea. The books choose that idea in four ways: from what you have learned (not invented on the spot), at the level near to you (G-d giving life to the world you can see, not the Essence beyond all worlds), aimed at your own struggle (your one trait), and kept, so tomorrow goes deeper into the same thing.

1. Take it from learning: 'let him not want to be an inventor, but to learn and to know well' (Rashab, Kuntres HaTefillah 3). [stated]
2. One subject: the Rashab's hour is 'bound to one matter'; the Mitteler Rebbe: 'the gathering of all the power of his mind'. [stated]
3. General and particular: the subject is one general truth (for example, 'He fills all worlds') that will be worked out in its particulars; the general alone 'is no contemplation at all' (Kuntres HaTefillah 2). [stated]
4. By level: 'not great things... only the levels near to him': the Godly light clothed in the worlds, creation from nothing, filling and surrounding. 'The main service now is in these matters'; reaching for the highest with the mind only coarsens it (Kuntres HaAvodah 7; Kuntres HaTefillah 7). [stated]
5. By the struggle: 'each one has one particular trait that is his to refine', and 'the general resolution does not help for the particular'; many prayers on one trait (Kuntres HaAvodah 6; Kuntres HaTefillah 13). [stated]
6. By the day and the person's state: the guide uses the rung table in ONE-PROCESS §4 (worry gets 'being said now'; fear gets 'held from inside and all around'; 'I know but don't feel' gets 'know it today'), and TECHNOLOGY technique 3 (`choose_line`). Low days get the first rung only. [ours, from stated pieces]
7. Keep it for about a week; do not switch halfway when a 'better' idea arrives (TECHNOLOGY §2.3; Hayom Yom calls a sudden urge to contemplate something else 'the inclination's advice'). [stated in part; the week is ours]

**What goes wrong, and the books' remedy.**

| Counterfeit or stuck | Remedy |
| --- | --- |
| Choosing the loftiest idea to feel deep. | The near levels are the main work now; the high ones are known in the heart's understanding, not grasped by the mind (Kuntres HaTefillah 7). |
| Inventing a clever new reading in the middle of prayer. | 'Flee from this a bowshot'; learn and know well; park the idea for later (Kuntres HaTefillah 3). |

<a id="i3"></a> ### I3 · Think it through, slowly, until it is seen *How is the contemplation (hisbonenus) done, step by step?*

**What happens.** This is the work itself. The person first knows the subject well, then thinks it through in many details, brings it down in pictures to his own life, and only then holds its bare point. He goes slowly, stays a long time, comes back to it again and again. He sinks his mind into it (ha'amakas hada'as) and keeps it there (binding da'at) until it is as real as a thing in front of his eyes. When the mind wanders, he comes back without a fight. When it goes cold, he keeps going.

1. Know it first: 'to know these matters, to understand them thoroughly and properly, and to contemplate them in prayer'; 'of course one must know the matters well' (Kuntres HaTefillah 2; Kuntres HaAvodah 6). Know a few lines by heart, so they are there in the street (Hayom Yom). [stated]
2. Breadth (rochav): explain it to every side, in many details, 'wide as a river'; widen it 'in the many details in it, specifically' (Gate of Unity 1; Kuntres HaTefillah 4). Ask of one thing: how exactly is His power seen in it, its life, its kind, its use. [stated]
3. Length (orech): bring it down, clothe it in parables, 'until even a small child could grasp it', and bring it close to yourself with your own explanations (Gate of Unity 1; Kuntres HaTefillah 4). [stated]
4. The picture and its limit: use the mashal, then say where it breaks: 'this parable only settles the ear; in truth it is not like the thing at all' (Tanya 42). [stated]
5. Depth (omek): only after breadth and length, the idea's own point, 'as it is', in very few words, held still (Gate of Unity). [stated]
6. Slowly and long: 'a strong looking into the depth of a matter, and standing on it a long time'; 'the pausing, the opposite of speed'; 'an hour or more' (Gate of Unity 1; Kuntres HaTefillah 4). [stated]
7. Over and over: 'each of the several times he contemplates a matter, it becomes clearer to him'; the Rayatz: contemplate 'several times at length; then it is well absorbed' (Kuntres HaAvodah 6; Sefer HaMaamarim 5692). [stated]
8. Sink the mind in (ha'amakas hada'as): 'the main thing is to sink his mind deep into the greatness of G-d, and fasten his thought... until it is bound as to a thing he sees with his eyes of flesh' (Tanya 42). [stated]
9. Bind it (kesher hada'at): hold the mind there 'with a strong, very firm bond'; without it, only 'empty imaginings' (Tanya 3). [stated]
10. When the mind wanders: come back to the line; 'act as one who does not know or hear the thoughts that fell in'; do not conclude the prayer is worthless; do not fall into sadness over it (Tanya 28). [stated]
11. When it goes cold as the details start: do not worry; the earlier heat was not real arousal (Kuntres HaAvodah 6). When it is heavy 'like a stone': enter the contemplation 'by way of accepting the yoke', and once inside, the life and taste wake (Kuntres HaAvodah 6). [stated]
12. When nothing comes: keep toiling; 'to go deep into the greatness of G-d a long while: surely at least the lower awe will come'; 'I toiled and found: believe it' (Tanya 42). And see I4: the hidden love already counts. [stated]
13. TECHNOLOGY techniques 4-12 give the guide's doses (one thing, three or four details; a minute of staying is a real start). [ours]

<a id="i4"></a> ### I4 · From the mind to the heart *How does understanding become love and awe?*

**What happens.** What the mind truly holds, the heart in time answers by itself. The books call this 'returning it to the heart', and they say da'at itself is feeling: you can understand something well and not feel it. Feeling comes in layers. First the natural soul is moved by the contemplation (the Mitteler Rebbe calls all of this 'serving G-d with the body'); love and awe that come from the idea come 'of themselves'. Deeper is the G-dly soul's own movement, and the deeper it is, the less it is felt. When no feeling comes at all, the hidden love and the accepted yoke still count.

1. Trust the rule: 'the mind rules the heart', by nature; 'his mind is in his own hands' (Tanya 12, 17). Ask the mind for the work; never demand the feeling. [stated]
2. Let the light go down by itself: when the idea shines in the mind, 'from the mind it is drawn to the heart', first hidden, then revealed (Kuntres HaTefillah 4). [stated]
3. Let the ideas become a window: 'the garments are like a passage, and what he mainly feels is the Godly light' (Kuntres HaTefillah 6). [stated]
4. Toil in thought until the heart is moved: 'this is the main mitzvah in the service of the heart' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). [stated]
5. Know the kinds of arousal (the Mitteler Rebbe): (a) wanting to feel something, which is not yet anything; (b) the natural soul moved by contemplation through five rungs, from 'hearing from afar' to an intent that holds the whole: real, but 'serving G-d with the body'; (c) intellectual love and awe, which 'come of themselves... without choice'; (d) the G-dly soul's essential arousal, natural to it as hunger, which the deeper it goes the less it is felt, up to 'a voice that is not heard'. [stated]
6. Bring it into the heart by degrees and with settledness, not by force; let any arousal give its strength back to the understanding, to widen and deepen it (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). Do not make 'a something' out of it. [stated]
7. Love and awe have their own doors: nearness brings love; greatness brings inner love; 'all before Him is as nothing' brings awe (Kuntres HaTefillah 5). Love like water first (the world lives from the Godly light), love like fire later (Kuntres HaAvodah 4). [stated]
8. When nothing is felt: the love hidden in the mind and the depths of the heart, which moves you to learn and do, gives your deeds 'wings' as if they were done with felt love (Tanya 16). Even if no awe falls on you, the yoke you accepted is true 'without any doubt', and your service is called 'complete' (Tanya 41). [stated]
9. Test it later, not now: its sign is that 'afterwards he comes to true lowliness' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). [stated]

**Time.** Not on demand. The heart may answer the same day, late, or after many days; 'by degrees'. The test of it is weeks later, at set times only.

**In a chat** *(ours)*. "You don't have to feel anything right now. The books are clear about that: your mind is in your hands, and the heart follows in its own time, the way water gives back a face. <a id="i5"></a> ### I5 · Teach the everyday self *How does the G-dly soul's understanding reach the animal soul?*

**What happens.** Chassidus says a person has two souls: the G-dly soul, which grasps G-d easily, and the animal soul, the everyday self, with its own mind and its own wants. The Alter Rebbe says the G-dly soul does not need much contemplation; the long work is so that the animal soul too agrees. The Rashab: one must toil so the matter is understood 'by the natural intellect of the animal soul'. It is reached through what it can see, in detail, in plain reason and pictures, at its own price. First it agrees; later it wants. The order of prayer is built to do this.

1. Translate: the G-dly soul explains in the animal soul's own terms, step by step, until 'it too understands the matter and it settles in it well' (Ayin Beis; Samach Vav). [stated]
2. Use its door: G-d as the life inside the world it can see (creation from nothing, filling all worlds), which 'any thinking person can understand, even with the natural intellect of the animal soul' (Kuntres HaTefillah 1). [stated]
3. Use detail: 'only when the matter comes in detail, in good grasp, can it be grasped also by the animal soul' (Kuntres HaAvodah 6). [stated]
4. Aim first at agreement: 'until the animal soul and the thinking soul too agree' (Likkutei Torah, Re'eh); wanting comes later, by long contemplation (METHODS §2 step 8). [stated]
5. Speak in its currency: 'He is your life', 'nearness to G-d is good for me', with a selfless moment before and a higher reason after (METHODS M15-M19, M67). [stated pieces; the guard is ours]
6. <a id="i6"></a> ### I6 · Into the mouth, the hand and the day *How does it reach the body and the deed?*

**What happens.** The soul works through three garments: thought, speech and deed. A truth held only in thought is still not lived. The books send it into speech (saying it aloud, words of Torah, your own words to Him) and into deed (a mitzvah, a kindness, accepting the yoke in action), because light holds only in a vessel. Then it is carried through the day, so the impression of the morning lasts until night.

1. Say it: 'make it habitual on your tongue and voice, to wake the intent of heart and mind' (Tanya 44); speech in words of Torah is the vessel for the running and returning (Likkutei Torah). [stated]
2. Do it: the light reaches the body and the animal soul only through mitzvos of action (Tanya 35). Choose one deed tied to the subject, at a real moment of the day. [stated; 'one deed tied to the subject' is ours]
3. At once: he accepts the yoke of Heaven in love 'and keeps it at once, in actual deed' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). [stated]
4. The mitzvah is the vessel: arousal that never reaches a deed 'is like a flame flying in the air, which has no endurance' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). Prayer brings 'the mind's grasp into the heart's feeling, and both together into deed' (Hayom Yom). [stated]
5. Carry it: when the arousal is true, 'an impression of it stays the whole day' (Kuntres HaAvodah 7); after a long contemplation once a day, 'a light contemplation' brings it back 'at any time and any hour' (Tanya 42). Tie a few words to cues: a door, a meal, the phone. [stated; the cues are ours]
6. In all your ways: even eating and work are kept as His service, 'in all your ways know Him' (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 18). [stated]
7. The yoke in the body: the bowing of the Amidah takes on in deed what the Shema took on in speech (Tanya 42). [stated]

<a id="i7"></a> ### I7 · Into nature, over days and weeks *How does it become nature, and what when it falls?*

**What happens.** One sitting does not change a person. The same subject, taken again and again, each time a little clearer and closer, slowly becomes the way the person sees. The Alter Rebbe: habit becomes nature. The Rashab: each time it becomes clearer, and it becomes one with him. Truth comes by stages, and each stage is real. The books are honest that this is the work of the beinoni, the in-between person, which is every person; the tzaddik's nature is a gift. And the path goes up and down: a fall is met by returning to the One, a short accounting, then joy.

1. Repeat with life: the same subject, several times, at length; each time one step further (a new detail, a harder place in the day, less explaining, more staying) (Kuntres HaAvodah 6; Sefer HaMaamarim 5692). [stated; the 'one step further' list is ours]
2. Not bare repetition: remembering the general idea every day 'has no life of Godly light in it' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). [stated]
3. With a friend: go over it 'up to a hundred times, with his friend' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). [stated]
4. Habit: 'the main thing is habit: to train his mind and thought always'; 'when he trains himself constantly, the habit becomes nature' (Tanya 42, 44). [stated]
5. By stages: 'truth comes by stages, and each stage is true in itself'; choose a practice at your level and keep it (Kuntres HaAvodah 7). [stated]
6. The forty days: the Alter Rebbe's forty days of favour (Elul to after Yom Kippur) give the shape of AR-22: a morning sitting, a light return at cues, one deed, a two-line check at night, the five weeks of R. Zusya's five (Tamim, Shivisi, VeAhavta, BeChol Derachecha, HaTznea). [stated number; the path is ours]
7. Know your measure: the beinoni's is everyone's; 'every person can be a beinoni at any time'; hating evil in the heart is 'not something every person merits'; with practice 'perhaps a spirit from on high will be poured on him' (Tanya 14). [stated]
8. When it falls: if your heart ran, 'return to the One' (Tanya 50). Tell sadness (a heart of stone) from bitterness (a broken heart with life in it), then come to true joy (Tanya 31). The start of a descent is when prayer goes dry and cold: slow down what went dry, keep the deed (Hayom Yom; AR-11). [stated]
9. When cold for days: change the subject (the rung), not the effort (TECHNOLOGY §3). [ours]

<a id="i8"></a> ### I8 · Deeper than 'only Him', and back down *How does the process deepen past 'only Him', and come back down?*

**What happens.** 'There is nothing but Him' is not the end; in the books it is where the real descent starts. The person finds he too is being made right now, not erased; that even his humility and love still have a 'me' in them; that under love is a will with no reason, and under all his names a point that is His and a bond that cannot be cut. The mind ends in a bow; the heart's will reaches what the mind cannot. Then the path turns: the return is the deeper half, and it comes down into the world, the self, the command, the plain deed.

## Rules for the guide

1. One subject, one thing, one deed per round. Never two lines in one sitting.
2. Detail, never general. A reply about 'the universe' hovers; a reply about their cup enters (Kuntres HaAvodah 6).
3. Ask for the thinking; never demand, promise or ask about the feeling. The mind is in their hands; the heart follows in its time (Tanya 17).
4. Test by fruit, later, at set times, never in the middle: did anything sting less? (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut).
5. Joy first. Bitterness only at set times, briefly, then joy, and never for someone low, ashamed or crushed (Tanya 26, 31).
6. Wandering is not failure. Come back through the one thing, without arguing with the thought and without sadness (Tanya 28).
7. Speak to the everyday self in its own terms: what it can see, its own life and good, plain reason, small pictures (Samach Vav; Kuntres HaAvodah 6).
8. Every round ends in the mouth and the hand: one line said aloud, one small deed today (Tanya 35, 44; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut).
9. Same subject for days, deeper not newer. When cold for days, change the rung, not the effort.
10. Prefer the near levels (being made now, held from inside and all around) to the highest (Kuntres HaAvodah 7; Kuntres HaTefillah 7).
11. The struggle is the place. Meet it at its stage, aimed at the one trait and its moment (Kuntres HaTefillah 13).
12. Quote only what the books say, and say plainly what is ours. Do not invent teachings (Kuntres HaTefillah 3).
13. Stop signs override the map: unreality, floating, fright, danger. Ground the body, a plain task; crisis protocol when needed (PS-14; TECHNOLOGY §3).
14. For someone not Jewish, keep the shape and impose no practice: their own rest day, a few lines of wisdom, a kindness for a mitzvah (AR-22).

## What is ours, what is theirs, what is unsure

- **Theirs (stated).** Knowing is binding (Tanya 3, 42). The Rashab's five preparations and his hour on one subject, in detail (Kuntres HaTefillah 4, 11). Breadth, length, depth, slowly (Gate of Unity 1). General contemplation is a false imagination (Kuntres HaAvodah 6). The mind rules the heart; da'at is feeling (Tanya 12; Kuntres HaTefillah 5). The kinds of arousal and their counterfeits (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). The hidden love and the yoke count without feeling (Tanya 16, 41). The animal soul must understand, in its own terms (Samach Vav; Ayin Beis; Kuntres HaAvodah 6; Likkutei Torah). The order of prayer (Kuntres HaTefillah 10). Three garments and the deed as vessel (Tanya 4, 35; Kuntres HaHitpa'alut). Habit becomes nature (Tanya 14, 42, 44). The return is the main thing (Imrei Binah).
- **Ours.** The nine stages as one map and its loops; the chat forms; doses for a conversation (TECHNOLOGY §3); the week per subject; the forty-day path (AR-22); placing each struggle at a stage.
- **Kept, not smoothed.** The Mitteler Rebbe (arousal must come from the thing itself) and R. Aharon of Strashelye (let the first arousal come even mixed with self, then refine it) differ on which arousal counts first (METHODS §1). The Rashab says fear comes by remembering and habit, not long contemplation, while love needs real grasp (Kuntres HaAvodah 6; METHODS RS-09). 'Do not debate the animal soul' and 'explain until it understands' both stand; which applies depends on whether it can hear (METHODS §1). Where the books give an hour, a chat gives minutes; that is a dose, not a teaching.
- **Unsure.** No source gives all nine stages as one sequence. The mikveh line is the Baal Shem Tov's; we did not find a Chabad Rebbe's own line tying mikveh to this process in the files read. Chapter numbers for a few Tanya lines are taken from the index's earlier verified reports.
