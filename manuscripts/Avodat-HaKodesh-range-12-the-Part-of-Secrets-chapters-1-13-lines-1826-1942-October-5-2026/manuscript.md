## The shape of the range

Part 4 turns to the "Work of Creation" (maaseh bereishit) read as the story of the emanation above, with the lower creation as its mirror.

1. **Why write secrets at all** (1826-1845). The inner wisdom was never lost, because it is a need on high. Ibn Gabbai, who says he knows only "a drop in the great sea", writes anyway: "the thing was in me like a burning fire".
2. **Why the world needed an in-between** (1846-1848). A world made directly by the Infinite would be infinite, or could not bear His light.
3. **The six days as the emanation** (1849-1919). The hidden point, the palace, light and dark, the waters, the dry land thirsting, the diminished moon, the grass and the herb, Adam two-faced, and the teaching that only one day ever exists.
4. **The fruit of creation** (1920-1942). "These are the generations of heaven and earth": their fruit is the unity, and man was made to complete it. The tree of knowledge: all is permitted in unity; the sin was taking the tree alone.

## The unity of G-d

**Why the Infinite created through an in-between.** Before anything was, "the Ancient Existence was hidden in His existence, concealed in His being, single in His Name that is not separate from Him" (line 1846). If He had created the world directly, it would have no capacity for lack, "and would be eternal like Him of necessity, and without limit"; and if it were limited, "it could not bear the light of the Limitless". So there had to be an in-between with two sides, "a whole power and a lacking power", and that is the emanation (the sefirot), "joined" to Him and still the root of what is created (line 1846). The Idra: "If the Holy Ancient One had not been arranged in these arrangements, neither upper nor lower beings would have been found" (line 1847).

*Ours.* This is exactly the Chabad question behind the doctrine of tzimtzum and the sefirot: how does a finite world come from the Infinite? (Tanya, Shaar HaYichud ch. 4-7; Iggeret HaKodesh 20.) Ibn Gabbai's answer, the sefirot as the joint of the infinite and the finite, is what the Ari and then the Alter Rebbe would build on.

**All is in the simple will.** The first point that broke forth "is the simple will in which all was, and all need it and return to it":

> נמצא שהכל נעשה ברצון ההוא ואין דבר יוצא חוצה לו (Avodat HaKodesh 4:5:1; line 1861)
> It follows that all was made in that will, and nothing goes outside it.

"One who understands this will know in truth that the matters of creation were not by a chain of causes one from another", as the philosophers say (line 1861).

**One Name through every level.** The Zohar on "and He blew into his nostrils the breath of life": the soul of all the upper and lower beings hangs on that one soul, "to draw it from level to level to the end of all levels, so that that soul be found in all, spread in all, and that He alone be":

> ומאן דפסיק האי מעלמא כמאן דפסיק האי נשמתא ומתחזי דאית נשמתא אחרא בר מהאי (Avodat HaKodesh 4:12:3; line 1923)
> And whoever cuts this off from the world is like one who cuts this soul, and it appears as if there were another soul besides this one.

Ibn Gabbai sums up the purpose of all creation:

> ותכלית הכל לשלוף ולהביא בו הנשמה הסתומה ממדרגה למדרגה מראש ועד סוף להורות שהוא הכל ואין זולתו (Avodat HaKodesh 4:12:3; line 1923)
> And the purpose of all is to draw and bring into it the hidden soul from level to level, from head to end, to show that He is all and there is nothing besides Him.

*Ours.* This is the book's clearest statement of the goal in Chabad terms: *ein od milvado*. It is the same move as the Shema meditation of range 11 (bring the hidden root through all levels). The guide can treat line 1923 as ibn Gabbai's summary sentence.

**No gap anywhere.** Ben Zoma "gazed between the upper waters and the lower waters". The sages disagree on how far apart they are, but "all agree there is no empty place between them ... all cleaves together, one piece, like a flame bound to a coal" (line 1870). And R. Akiva's warning, "do not say 'water, water'": "all is one single domain, one, united, without any separation" (line 1871).

## Only one day ever exists

A striking teaching in chapter 11. Creation was all brought forth at once in potential, and brought out day by day. But:

> ולעולם לא נמצא כי אם יום אחד בלבד (Avodat HaKodesh 4:11:3; line 1916)
> And there is never anything but one day alone.

"There were never seven days existing together; when this one ceases, that one comes." Each day is made new from the motion of the spheres, "and by the combination and turning of the letters by which heaven and earth were created, they turn again, turn after turn" (line 1916). The first six turns brought out the roots of everything; every day since is "only the increase and spreading of the form" (line 1920). On Shabbat no new body is made: "the world of form spreads and the world of body rests" (line 1918).

*Ours.* This is very close to the Baal Shem Tov's and the Alter Rebbe's teaching that the world is made new at every moment by new combinations of the letters of the divine word (Shaar HaYichud ch. 1-2, 7; Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah). Ibn Gabbai says it of days; Chassidus says it of every instant. For the guide: **today is the only day**, and it is freshly spoken.

## The lower world waits for us

**The servant who toils to eat.** The earth "was void and empty" (tohu vavohu). The Midrash: a king bought two servants; one he fed from the treasury, the other had to toil to eat, and he sat "bewildered" (line 1857). Ibn Gabbai: the upper world is fed from the treasury; the lower (Malchut) is fed through the arousal from below, "and it is a great toil with great danger, for if no arousal rises from below by Torah and good deeds, as it were there is no food above" (line 1858).

**The dry land thirsting.** "Let the dry land appear": Malchut is dry and longs for water, "our soul is dry"; "let the dry land appear" means "that the eyes see and look at her, and she not be always in the hiding of the face" (line 1873).

**The herb for the service of man.** "Grass for the beast, and herb for the service of man" (Ps. 104:14). The grass is the angels made new each day and consumed; the "herb" is the forms of the Chariot, "set right by the service of man ... those who serve set right from below, and the blessing spreads on them from above" (line 1875). The Zohar: the herb is "the souls of the righteous ... and by their merit the whole world is fed" (line 1910).

**The moon's diminishing.** The two lights were at first equal; the moon was diminished. G-d told her: "Let the righteous be called by your name: Jacob the small, David the small, Samuel the small":

> הם יהיו כפרתך בסבלם עול חסרונך ומשאך עליהם ... ויקראו קטנים על שמך הם יבחרו הקוטן והמעוט, ולך ימשיכו הגדולה והרבוי (Avodat HaKodesh 4:8:11; line 1898)
> They will be your atonement, bearing the yoke of your lack and your burden ... and they will be called small by your name; they will choose smallness and lessening, and draw greatness and abundance to you.

*Ours.* A beautiful teaching for the guide on humility (bittul). The righteous choose to be small so that the Presence will be great. This is the Chabad ideal of the tzaddik as one who carries the Shechinah's lack (Tanya ch. 35, 37; the Rebbe's teaching on "Jacob the small"). A state: **small for her sake**.

**Why man was made.** "These are the generations of heaven and earth": their fruit is "the unity and repair of the Great Name ... for the upper ones need the lower for their existence and repair" (line 1921). "No shrub of the field ... and there was no man": the upper world was not yet face to face, and it needed "a repairer and completer from outside who will wake this completion by the fitness of service and the purity of good deeds" (line 1921).

> ולזה היתה בריאת אדם צורך גבוה גם כן (Avodat HaKodesh 4:12:1; line 1921)
> So the creation of man was also a need on high.

**Abraham, weighed against the world.** "When they were created" (behibaram) is read as "in Abraham" (be-Avraham): "Abraham was weighed against the whole world ... for all that was laid on the whole world to do, he did" (line 1922).

## The tree: all is permitted in unity

"From every tree of the garden you may eat." The Zohar: "all was permitted to him, for all is in unity; for we see that Abraham ate, Isaac and Jacob all ate and lived. But this tree is a tree of death; whoever takes it alone dies" (line 1930). Ibn Gabbai:

> והכוונה כי לא היתה המניעה כי אם שלא יאכל ממנו ולא יפנה אליו לבדו שלא יקצץ בנטיעות (Avodat HaKodesh 4:12:10; line 1930)
> The meaning is that the prohibition was only that he not eat from it and turn to it alone, so as not to cut the shoots.

The fruit was forbidden "not to cut the fruit from the tree and make it a thing by itself; had she eaten the fruit while attached, in the unity of the tree, it would have been good for her" (line 1938). And the tree would have been all good if Adam had waited for Shabbat, when the two faces turned face to face and the bad went to its place (line 1942). Instead, "in the tenth hour he sinned".

*Ours.* This is one of the most usable teachings in the book for the question of pleasure and the world. Nothing is bad in unity; the harm is taking a thing *alone*, cut from its root. Chassidus teaches the same about permitted pleasures (Tanya ch. 7: what is done for Heaven rises; what is done for itself alone falls to the neutral shell). And "wait for Shabbat" is a gentle teaching about timing: some goods are given later, whole. For the guide: **nothing alone**.

**Face to face.** "It is not good for man to be alone": while two-faced, back to back, "he is half a man, and half a thing is not a thing and is not good"; face to face, they are one body and blessing rests (lines 1931-1932). "The blessing does not rest on a lacking thing, a flawed thing, but on a whole place" (line 1932). *Ours.* Of marriage and of the soul's relation to G-d both. The Chabad reading of "face to face" (panim el panim) as the goal of all relationship above and below is the same.

## States of consciousness

- **Only this day** (line 1916). A state of freshness: the day is newly spoken, and it is the only one. Proposed below.
- **Small for her sake** (line 1898). Proposed below.
- **Nothing alone** (lines 1930, 1938). Enjoyment held in its root, never cut off. Proposed below as a technique.
- **He is all, nothing besides** (line 1923). Ties to "He is all, all is He" (range 11).
- **The thirsting land** (line 1873). The soul's dryness as the Shechinah's own thirst for His face; ties to Chabad's thirst states (Tanya ch. 50, "my soul thirsts").

## Meditation and practice

- **Drawing the hidden soul through all levels** (line 1923). The same move as the Shema meditation, done in any quiet: from the root, level by level, to this room and this body, and hold that there is nothing besides Him.
- **Waking the arousal from below** (line 1858). Any act of Torah or kindness as feeding the lower world. Ties to "the daily ask" (range 7).
- **Waiting for the whole** (line 1942). When a good is wanted too soon, wait for the time when it can come joined.

## The big human questions

**Why is there anything?** Because the Infinite, through His emanation, made a world that could both receive and lack (line 1846).

**What is my task?** To wake from below what the upper world needs, so the Presence is fed (line 1858).

**Is pleasure wrong?** No; only pleasure cut from its root (line 1930).

**Why be humble?** The righteous choose smallness so that the Presence becomes great (line 1898).

## Lines that did not bear on the four questions

Lines 1826-1845 (the defense of the Kabbalistic tradition, the "secret of intercalation", the qualifications for learning secrets), 1849-1856 (the letter-by-letter reading of "In the beginning ... void and empty", the four kingdoms), 1862-1869 (the light, the firmament and the Gehinnom of the second day), 1872 (the shells), 1876-1885 (Behemoth of the thousand hills, the angels of the fifth day), 1888-1897 and 1899 (the hidden light and the moon), 1900-1907 (the fifth day, the four angels, Leviathan and the dream-letter), 1908-1913 (the sixth day and meat for Noah), 1914-1919 (the turnings of the letters), 1924-1929 (the garden, the seven Noahide commandments) and 1933-1941 (the naming of the animals, the serpent, the sin) were read in full. They serve the argument above.
