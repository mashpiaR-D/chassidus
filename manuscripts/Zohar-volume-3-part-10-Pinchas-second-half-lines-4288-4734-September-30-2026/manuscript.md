## The shape of the range

The Torah here lists the daily, Shabbos, new-moon and festival offerings. The Zohar and the Raya Mehemna turn each into a teaching about prayer and the inner life: - the daily burnt offering as a prayer that rises to the Infinite (4674-4677); - Shabbos and the extra soul given to each person by his level (4407-4411); - the new moon and her smallness (4510-4529); - the hind of the dawn (4530-4541); - Pesach, chametz and matzah (4560-4585); - Shavuos and the holy bird drawn out by whispering (4617-4625); - Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkos and Shemini Atzeret (4646-4734).

The Faithful Shepherd also prays aloud before the Cause of all (4444-4445), and gives a long, careful account of why G-d has many names and no name (4697-4708).

## The unity of G-d

**1. He has no name of His own; the names are for us.** This is one of the clearest statements in the whole Zohar on how names relate to G-d:

> ובגין דא, כל שמהן, אינון כנויין דיליה. על שם עובדין דיליה (Zohar, Pinchas 124:2; line 4698)
> Therefore all the names are His titles, named after His deeds.

"Before He created the world, why was He called Merciful or Judge? Only because of the creatures that were to be" (line 4697). When a generation is good, He is called by the Name of mercy; when not, by the name of judgment, "for each generation and each person according to his measure. But not that He has a measure or a known name" (אבל לאו דאית ליה מדה ולא שם ידיע, line 4699).

**2. Like the soul in the body, but only in one way.** The soul too "has no known name and no known place in the whole body, but rules on every side, and no limb is empty of her" (line 4700). So it is wrong "to mark her in one place," or call her by one, two or three names (line 4701), and all the more so with the Master of the world (line 4702). But the likeness holds only "in her rule over all the limbs, not in anything else," because the soul has changes and happenings, "which is not so for the Master of all" (line 4708). *Ours:* this is exactly the Chabad caution about the soul-body parable in Tanya and Shaar Hayichud. The Zohar itself limits the parable.

**3. Before, within and after every being.**

> דאיהו הוה קדם כל הויין. ואיהו בתוך כל הויה. ואיהו לאחר כל הויה. ודא רזא, דסהדין הויין עליה, היה, הוה, ויהיה (Zohar, Pinchas 124:9; line 4705)
> He was before every being, He is within every being, and He is after every being. This is the secret that the beings testify of Him: He was, He is, He will be.

Every name bears witness: *El*, that no power is beside Him; *Elohim*, that He is G-d over all powers; *Shaddai*, that He said "enough" to the world and set its bounds (line 4706).

**4. One without a second.** The Faithful Shepherd lifts his hands to pray to the Cause of all, "who is single without a second, with whom no letter and no point shares, and no change of colors" (דאיהו יחיד בלא תניינא, ולית את ונקודה דמשתתף בהדיה, line 4445).

**5. Made in the image of the One who holds all.** Why does "let *us* make man" end with "and G-d created man in *His* image"? Because if each partner had given a share, "each would take back his share when angry with him" (line 4340). Man was made in the likeness of the Shechinah, "who includes the upper and lower without any separation," and the Cause of all "is not found less than her, in any of them" (line 4341). So "his punishment and reward hang on Him alone, not on an angel or seraph or any creature" (line 4342).

**6. The daily offering rises to the Infinite.** The burnt offering, which is the Shechinah, rises "to the place she was cut from, which is the Infinite, higher than all the sefirot... and when she rises, all the sefirot hold on to her and rise with her," and she comes down "full of atonement" (line 4675). "There is no prayer or commandment... accepted before Hashem apart from her" (line 4677). She is the gate: "this is the gate to Hashem" (line 4678).

## States of consciousness

- **Made small in love, and so able to be held.** Out of love for the song, the living one "made herself small, little by little, until she was a single point." Then:

> לגבי עילא, כל מה דהוא מלה זעירא, דא תושבחתא, ודא עלוייא ורב ברבו עלאה (Zohar, Pinchas 105:14; line 4555)
> Above, whatever is small is praise, and height, and great greatness.

"If she were large, they could not hold her at all. But because she made herself small... they hold her and raise her," and she is joined "in the love of kisses, without separation" (line 4555). Then she grows "little by little" into fullness (line 4556). This pairs with "found when small" (part 8). **Proposed state: small enough to be held.** *Ours:* this is a strong teaching against the idea that one must be big or full before one can come close. - **The hind of the dawn.** She is the most merciful of creatures. In scarcity she goes far for food, "and does not want to eat until she comes back," gives to all, "and from what she shares she is satisfied, as if she ate more than all" (line 4531). When the world needs rain she climbs a high mountain, "puts her head between her knees, and cries cry after cry, and the Holy One hears her voice and is filled with mercy" (line 4536). Her birth pangs are the seventy words of "May Hashem answer you on the day of trouble" (line 4537). Maps to S86 (the other held) and S49. **Proposed state: the hind at dawn (fed by giving).** - **The joy of Shemini Atzeret.** After the seventy bulls for the nations, "the king says to the members of his house: I and you, let us make a small meal" (line 4671). "To you and not to another, to rejoice, you in your Master and He with you" (line 4734). Maps to S53 and S60. - **Freedom on Yom Kippur.** On Yom Kippur the tree of life rules and "no Satan or evil meeting shares with it"; on it "the servants rest... and go out to freedom, out of their chains" (line 4647). Maps to S78 (higher return). - **The broken spirit that lets light through.** When sins mix darkness into light, a person must sift "like one who picks food out of waste." The broken spirit breaks the darkness that covers the spirit "like chaff over wheat, or a cloud over the sun." When a person breaks his spirit in all his limbs before Hashem, then "say to the prisoners, go out" (4497-4498). And "a heart broken" means one who "is not proud and does not indulge in the world's pleasures; He honors him" (line 4364). Maps to S77 and S05. - **Shame as the start of awe.** "Awe" is linked to "shame." "Whoever has no shame in his face, surely his fathers did not stand at Mount Sinai" (line 4682). Near S46.

## Meditation and practice

- **Whispering to the holy bird (Shavuos and every day).** How do you catch the holy bird that hides under her mother's wings? "Prepare a precious meal in joy... go into the synagogue and the study hall, and chirp to her with a whispering voice as is fitting." She lifts her head, sees the tables set, hears the chirping, "and comes out and flies to them." Once she comes, her brothers follow (4619-4622). *Ours:* this is a lovely picture of drawing the Presence by gentle, repeated, whispered words. Ties to `lips`, `niggun` and `own_words`. **Proposed technique: whispering her out.**
- **Prayer as the King's meal, then telling your trouble.** The Faithful Shepherd maps the morning service onto a feast: 1. The praises invite the four faces of the chariot. 2. "With everlasting love" is the high priest uniting the Name. 3. The Shema. 4. The Levites' song. 5. "True and firm." 6. Then no one eats "until the King eats," meaning the first and last three blessings of the Amidah (4386-4388). 7. Then a person falls on his face and gives his soul, "To You, Hashem, I lift my soul" (line 4389). 8. "From here on, let a person say his trouble before Him: May Hashem answer you on the day of trouble" (line 4390).

Hence "it is forbidden to eat anything before the King eats, and that is prayer" (line 4386). Ties to `before_whom` and `own_words`. - **The guest does the host's will.** Why does G-d "eat" in the Song of Songs when the world to come has no eating? "From His great love for the Assembly of Israel, He changed His ways." A guest does what the host asks, "even if it is not his way." Jacob took the stones she gave him as a bed "with a willing heart" (4392-4396). *Ours:* G-d accepts what we can offer, in our own house, on our terms. - **Sharing your bread and your joy.** Give with joy at the festival table and "extend the table for the poor" (line 4474). Do not throw bread (line 4483). - **Grinding words, not swallowing them.** "Whoever brings out words of Torah must grind them with the teeth, and bring out whole words" (line 4290). Do not gulp prayers (line 4475). Ties to `chazarah` and `lips`. - **Guarding against self-curse.** There are three who harm themselves. One is a person who curses himself, "for an appointed one takes that word, and seventy under him say Amen" (line 4481). Ties to `lips`. - **A cup washed inside and out.** The cup of blessing is rinsed outside and washed inside: "that his inside be like his outside." The soul too is pure inside and out only through Torah (line 4464). Ties to `act`. - **Sukkah as shade for the soul.** "Not the shade of a common hut that guards the body from the sun, but shade to guard the soul": "In His shade I delighted and sat" (line 4656). The Shechinah "spreads her wings over them like a mother over children," so we bless "who spreads the sukkah of peace over us" (line 4670). Ties to `hold`.

## The big human questions

- **What is G-d's name?** None of His own. Every name is a title from His deeds toward us (4697-4708).
- **Why do the sages' rulings bind?** The sages are like tailors who fit the cut pieces of a royal garment, "adding where short, trimming where long," so G-d says, follow "the Torah they teach you" (4631-4633).
- **Must I be great to come near?** No. "Above, whatever is small is praise" (line 4555).
- **What does G-d want from the nations at Sukkos?** Israel gives the seventy bulls "with a good heart and a joy of will" (line 4728). "If your enemy is hungry, give him bread" (line 4729).
- **What do suffering and scorn earn?** In the end "those masters of Torah who bore many pains like a woman in labor, and were despised among the ignorant, will be honored" (line 4541).

## For the guide (ours)

1. Lines 4697-4708 are a full ancient answer to "what is G-d's name." All names are titles from His deeds. He is before, within and after every being. The soul parable holds only for "rule," not for change. The guide should set this beside Shaar Hayichud chapters 7-10.
2. "Small enough to be held" (4555) should enter the guide with "found when small" (part 8) as a pair of states for the shrunken self.
3. The hind at dawn (4531-4537) is a portrait of a giver who is fed by giving, and who cries on the mountain for rain for all. It is a model for compassion that does not burn out.
4. "Whispering her out" (4619-4622) is a gentle technique for drawing the Presence: joy, a set table, a quiet repeated voice.
5. The King's meal (4386-4390) gives a clear order: praise, unity, the Amidah, giving the soul, and only then telling your own trouble.
