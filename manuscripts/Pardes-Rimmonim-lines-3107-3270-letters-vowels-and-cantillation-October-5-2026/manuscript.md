---
These three gates are about the stuff that holy speech is made of. Gate 27 covers the twenty-two letters, Gate 28 the vowel points, and Gate 29 the cantillation marks. Most of the letter-by-letter and vowel-by-vowel pages are technical. They say which letter hints at which sefirah, and what Sefer HaTemunah and Brit Menuchah say about each shape. That material and the names of the "lights" of each vowel are **not for the guide**.

But the gates open with a claim that matters for every person who prays or learns. The letters are not signs that people agreed on. They are vessels of spirit, and the breath of holy speech makes something that rises. The Ramak also says plainly that a person who reads without understanding still gains, as long as he keeps growing. And he ends with a picture of the three layers of every word: letters are the body, vowels the spirit, and the chant the soul.

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**The Torah is His garment.** The Zohar warns: "woe to the man who says the Torah came to show mere stories" (ווי לההוא בר נש דאמר דהא אורייתא אתא לאחזאה ספורין בעלמא, line 3110). The stories are the garment, the laws are the body, the inner teaching is the soul, "and in the world to come they will look into the soul of the soul of the Torah" (ולעלמא דאתי זמינין לאסתכלא בנשמתא דנשמתא דאורייתא, line 3110). "Wine sits only in a jug" (חמרא לא יתיב אלא בקנקן, line 3110). So the Ramak concludes: "the Torah itself is a garment to the essence of God" (ונמצאת התורה עצמה לבוש אל עצם האלוה, line 3111), as the sefirot are a garment to the root. Learning is touching the garment He wears.

**Letters live from Him.** The letters on the tablets held up forty measures of stone. When the letters flew off, the stone grew heavy and broke. The Jerusalem Talmud draws the rule: "so every thing from which the Holy One removes His Presence can no longer stand" (כך כל דבר שהקדוש ברוך הוא מסלק שכינתו ממנו שוב אינו מתקיים, line 3115). The Ramak's point is that the letters are not labels: "the letters of the Torah are not conventional; they are spiritual" (שאין אותיות התורה הסכמיות אמנם הם רוחניות, line 3113). The Tanya will build its Gate of Unity on this (see §6).

**Flame and coal.** After all the talk of which letter is where, he says: "all is one, united, bound with a strong bond, like a flame bound to a coal; so the effect is bound to its cause, and it is not right to separate one from the other" (ועכ"ז הכל אחד מיוחד מתקשר בקשר אמיץ וחזק כשלהבת הקשורה בגחלת, line 3197). The ladder of letters and worlds is one chain.

**With all and outside all.** The yud, the smallest letter, the point, "is with all of them and outside all of them" (והיא עם כלן וחוץ לכלן, line 3160). Its three tips are "I am first, and I am last, and besides Me there is no God". This is the Ramak's simplest image of how God relates to what He makes: in everything and not held by anything.

**Touching and not touching.** How does Keter reach Chochmah? The Zohar says it "struck and did not strike". The Ramak: "struck and did not strike, to show the fineness of emanation, that it is revealed and not revealed" (בטש ולא בטש הכונה להורות על דקות האצילות שהוא גלוי ולא גלוי, line 3227). It is revealed compared with its hiddenness, and still not grasped even through what is revealed. The best picture he finds is the light going out from the eye to what it sees.

**Ayin at the start, Ayin at the end.** Zarka, the rising mark, is the Shechinah thrown back up to her source. There the "alef" pattern appears again: "Ayin at the head and Ayin at the end" (אי"ן בראש ואי"ן בסוף, line 3236). The lowest returns to the highest, and both are Nothing to our grasp.

## 3. States of consciousness

**Reading past my understanding (RK-S24, proposed).** The Ramak lists four levels of learning: story, law, inner meaning, and the spirit of the letters themselves. The first level is open to everyone. One should learn "as words that are divine, whose inner depth is hidden from him" (במה שהם דברים אלהיים שנעלם ממנו עוצם פנימיותם, line 3111). Even one who does not understand the language at all and only reads the verses, "he too has a good reward for his toil" (גם לו יש שכר טוב בעמלו, line 3111). "It does not say 'in the Torah' but 'in *his* Torah'" (ובתורה לא נאמר אלא ובתורתו, line 3111), that is, in the measure of his knowing. There is one condition: "that he always add" (ובתנאי שיוסיף לעולם, line 3111). Inside: I am saying holy words that go far beyond me, and I do not feel shut out. The words are His, they do their work, and my part is to say them and grow a little. How to enter: before learning or praying, say "these are Your words, deeper than I know", then read slowly, and add one small thing you did not know yesterday. Near S36 (Faith above knowing) and S35 (The edge of knowing). What is new is that not understanding does not shut the door.

**The closing before the outpour (named).** Kamatz means "closing the fist". When Tiferet and Malchut unite above, they are gathered in and hidden, and for a moment there is no flow to the worlds. The Ramak: "as it were, meanwhile there is some hiding, though through that hiding afterward comes much flow and blessing" (וכביכול בעוד כך, יש קצת הסתר, עם היות ע"י ההסתר ההוא נמשך אח"כ רוב שפע וברכה, line 3219). This is S68 (The hiding seen as His) with a new reason. The hiding is sometimes the gathering before a gift.

**God groans (named).** The mark geia is "groaning": Tiferet cries "over His palace, and here it is burned; over His house, and here it is ruined; and over His Shechinah, and here she is falling into exile" (ועל שכינתו והנה היא נופלת בגלות, line 3244). The person in exile (S73) is not alone in the sorrow. The groan is His first.

**Patience for the one who fell (named).** The mark ma'arich ("lengthening") is "from the side of Chesed, which is patient even with the wicked so that they return" (המאריך הוא מצד החסד המאריך אפים אפי' לרשעים כדי שישובו, line 3257). With it goes the warning that God "is exact with the righteous to a hair's breadth" (שהקדוש ברוך הוא מדקדק עם הצדיקים כחוט השערה, line 3247). The same God is patient with the fallen and exact with the close. Near S54 (Compassion for the soul).

**Union in quiet.** The Ramak on the mark etnach ("rest"): "the matter of union is in settledness and in silence" (כי ענין הזווג בישוב ובשתיקה, line 3258). Closeness is not loud.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Breath that rises (RK-T25, proposed).** The core of Gate 27: "when a person mentions and moves one of the letters, the spirit in it is surely stirred, and from the breath of the mouth holy forms come into being, rise, and bind to their root" (והבל הפה ממנו יתהוו צורות קדושות יתעלו ויתקשרו בשרשם, line 3114). The soul strikes the letters like a hammer, "by the hammer of the soul" (על ידי פטיש הנשמה, line 3120), and "from his breath a spiritual being comes into being, like an angel" (עוד יתהווה ממנה מהבל פיו רוחניות ומציאות יהי' כמו מלאך, line 3120). "This is the secret of mentioning the Names and of intent in prayer" (וזהו סוד הזכרת השמות וכוונת התפלה, line 3120). What it is for: prayer and learning that are more than thinking. How: say the words aloud, slowly, each letter clear, and feel each breath of a holy word leave you and rise. Hold one verse this way for a few minutes. The written word is the body of the spoken word, and the spoken word is coarse compared with the thought in the heart (ואותם הנזכ' בפה הם גשמיות בערך המחשביות שהם בלב, line 3117). So let the thought ride the breath. Guard: this is prayer, not a power. Do not try to make things happen with names (below). Belongs with "lips" and "letters".

**Letters, vowels, chant: body, spirit, soul (RK-T26, proposed).** "The chant marks are souls, the vowels are spirits, and the letters are nefesh" (וטעמי אינון נשמתין ונקודי רוחין ואתוון נפשין, line 3269). The letters sit in the mouth; the vowel rides the letter "like a rider on a horse", touching it only in the sound; the chant does not touch the letter at all but rides the vowel. The Ramak adds that one must feel this oneself, "for it cannot be explained in writing, only mouth to mouth" (כי אין בכתיבה לבארו אלא מפה לפה, line 3270). What it is for: reading Torah or a psalm as a living thing with three layers. How: read a verse three times. First say only the letters clearly. Then say it with the vowels, listening to the sound between the letters. Then sing it with its tune, and feel the tune as the soul of the words. Guard: this is for love of the words, not a performance. A plain reading is fine where there is no tune.

**Keep the soul in the reading.** One who reads the written Torah without its meaning "is like one who reads a body without a soul" (הוא כמו מי שקורא בגוף בלא נשמה, line 3121). So one must learn the Oral Torah, which "reveals its spirit". This is not against RK-S24. Read even without understanding, and also study what it means. The two go together.

**Don't use the names.** The Ramak is firm: "one who chases the working of the Names will find the poison of vipers in his honey" (והרודף אחר פעולת השמות ימצא מרורות פתנים בדבשו, line 3197), because he may mix good with evil or bring disorder. "Yet the modest should seek to know them, for they are good for knowing the greatness of our Maker" (ואעפ"כ ראוי לצנועים לחפש אחר ידיעתם כי הם טובים לדעת גדולת יוצרנו, line 3197). For the guide: knowledge that brings awe, never techniques for power.

## 5. The big human questions

**What if I don't understand what I pray?** Say it anyway, as holy words, and keep adding (line 3111). Your reward is in the measure of your knowing, "his Torah", not someone else's.

**Why learn at all, if the secrets are beyond me?** "The secrets of our holy Torah no thought or idea can contain" (כי ענין סודות תורתינו הקדושה לא יכילם רעיון ומחשבה, line 3121). Even Moses stayed forty more days to learn more. The learning never ends because what it clothes has no end.

**Is the first thing we teach a child small?** "The first word of Torah that is given to children, alef-bet, is a word that people of the world cannot grasp with understanding" (מלה קדמאה דאורייתא דיהבין לינוקי אלף בית דא מלה דלא יכלין בני עלמא לאדבקא בסוכלתנו, line 3132). The simplest thing is the deepest. Teaching a child the letters is handing over what even angels cannot hold.

**What holds the world up?** Vav, the pillar letter: "mercy is the cause of the world's existence" (הרי שהרחמים הם סבת קיום העולם, line 3143). The world was first meant to stand in judgment and could not, so mercy was joined to it.

**Where is freedom?** "From Malchut's side is servitude, and from above is freedom" (כי מצדה הוא העבדות וההשתעבדות ומצד מעלה הוא החירות, line 3209). The shofar of Binah is "the great shofar for our freedom". "When merit and mitzvot shine in the world, it is a shofar that goes forth" (וכאשר הזכות והמצות מאירים בעולם ומנהרים אז היא שופר הולך, line 3238); when sins interfere, the shofar is turned over. Our deeds decide whether freedom flows.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Letters that hold the world.** The Ramak's "every thing from which the Holy One removes His Presence can no longer stand" (line 3115) and his "letters are spiritual" (line 3113) are the seed of Sha'ar HaYichud VeHa'Emunah. The Tanya says: "if the letters of the ten utterances by which the earth was created were withdrawn from it for a moment" (אילו היו מסתלקות ממנה כרגע חס ושלום האותיות מעשרה מאמרות שבהן נבראת הארץ, Tanya line 786), it would return to absolute nothing. The Ramak applies it to the tablets and to holy things. The Tanya applies it to every stone, every moment. This is the same principle, stretched from the holy object to the whole of being. It is the clearest line from the Pardes to the Gate of Unity.
- **Torah as His garment.** "The Torah itself is a garment to the essence of God" (line 3111) matches the Tanya's "Torah and the Holy One are all one, and no thought grasps Him at all" (ואורייתא וקודשאבריךהוא כולא חד, ולית מחשבה תפיסא ביה כלל, Tanya line 123). The Tanya goes one step further. Torah is His will and wisdom, and He and His wisdom are one (the Rambam's "He is the knower and the known"), so the one who learns is clothed in God, not only in His garment.
- **Breath that rises.** The Ramak's holy breath that "rises and binds to its root" (line 3114) becomes, in Chabad, the teaching that words of Torah and prayer said aloud raise the animal soul's life-force. The Ramak's picture is of an angel made of breath. The Tanya's is of the speaking soul itself being lifted. Both hold that speech matters and not only thought. The Ramak holds that thought in the heart is finer still (line 3117), which the Tanya also says of thought, speech and action.
- **Not understanding.** The Ramak's reward for the one who reads without understanding (line 3111) fits the Chassidic honor for the simple Jew's Psalms. Chabad, with its stress on understanding (chabad = wisdom, understanding, knowledge), would add: the reward is real, and the path is to understand more.
