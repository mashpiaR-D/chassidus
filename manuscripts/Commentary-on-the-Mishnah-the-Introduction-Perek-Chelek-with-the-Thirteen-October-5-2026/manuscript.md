## The shape of the range

- **The Introduction to the Mishnah (Hakdamah).** The Oral Torah given at Sinai with the Written (lines 4-30); how a prophet is tested, and that prophecy decides nothing in the law (lines 31-96); the chain from Moses to Rabbi Judah the Prince (lines 97-116); the kinds of law and the plan of the Mishnah; the worth of the Sages' aggadah, which hides divine matters in riddles (lines 265-306); and then the purpose of creation (lines 307-355).
- **Perek Chelek (Sanhedrin 10).** "All Israel have a share in the World to Come." The five views of reward; the parable of the child learning for nuts; the three ways people read the Sages; the World to Come as the soul's joy in knowing G-d; the days of the Messiah; and the Thirteen Principles.
- **Avot.** A short, practical commentary on the Mishnah of the fathers, full of the middle way, friendship, speech, humility and love.

## 1. The unity of G-d

**The first two principles (Sanhedrin lines 80-81).** The first: to believe that there is "a being complete in every way of existence, the cause of all beings"; "if His existence ceased, the existence of all beings would cease"; if all else ceased, He would not cease, "for He is sufficient in His existence and enough for Himself" (line 80). The second: He is one, not as one of a pair, a kind, a divisible body, "but one with a oneness that has no oneness like it" (line 81).

> כי בהעדר מציאותו נתבטל מציאות כל הנמצאים (line 80)

> אבל הוא הש"י אחד באחדות שאין כמותה אחדות (line 81)

**No go-between (the fifth principle, line 84).** Only He is to be served; not angels, stars or elements, "and not to serve them as intermediaries to bring near to Him, but to Him alone should thoughts be directed, and leave all besides Him".

> אלא אליו בלבד יכוונו המחשבות ויניחו כל מה שזולתו (line 84)

**The whole world for the one who knows Him (Introduction lines 316-353).** In the Introduction the Rambam says the opposite of Guide III:13. "All that exists beneath the sphere of the moon exists for the human being alone" (line 316). A person does many things, but his purpose is one act, "to picture in his soul the intelligible secrets and to know the truths" (line 325), "and the most honored of the intelligibles is to picture in his soul the unity of G-d" (line 327). "The purpose of the world and all in it is a wise and good person" (line 330). The rest of humanity exists to make that person's life possible and, the Rambam adds, "to keep him company", "to remove his sadness and the desolation of his aloneness" (line 353).

> והנכבד שבמושכלות לצייר לנפשו אחדות הקב"ה (intro line 327)

Note (ours): the Rambam holds two positions in two works. In the youthful Introduction the world is for the knower of G-d; in the mature Guide (III:13, guide line 1078) not all exists for man. Chassidus's "dwelling below" is closer to the Introduction, but its purpose is a dwelling for G-d, not a knower among men.

## 2. States of consciousness

- **The child and the nuts (Sanhedrin line 72).** The teacher says "read, and I will give you nuts or figs"; later, fine clothes; later, money; later, honor. Each stage values its reward more than learning. The goal is to learn "only to know it", and "the goal of truth is only to know that it is true" (line 72). This is the Rambam's map of motives from *shelo lishmah* (not for its own sake) to *lishmah*, and he approves the path: "from not for its sake one comes to for its sake" (line 74). *Ties to:* S60, S61, C4 (From self-enclosed to held). The guide can use the ladder as a kind, non-judging picture of mixed motives (ours).
- **Joys the body cannot know (line 79).** "As the blind cannot grasp colors... so bodies cannot grasp the pleasures of the soul"; "as fish do not know the element of fire". Angels and stars have "great pleasure in what they know and grasp of the truth of the Creator". A soul that has risen no more wants bodily pleasures than a great king wants to go back to playing ball with children (line 79). *Ties to:* S44 (Love as delight), S66 (Filled from inside).
- **Love that does not fade (Avot 5:16, avot line 113).** "When the cause of love is a divine matter, that is, true knowledge, that love can never cease, for its cause exists forever." *Ties to:* S27 (Knowing that holds), S60.

> כשתהיה סבת האהבה ענין אלהי והוא המדע האמיתי האהבה ההיא אי אפשר שתסור לעולם (avot line 113)

- **The pious man on the ship (Avot 4:4, avot line 77).** Asked his happiest day, a pious man told of lying in the lowest place on a ship when a passenger urinated on him; "I rejoiced greatly that I had reached the point where the insult of that lacking man did not pain me." The Rambam gives this as the far edge of humility, the one trait where one must go all the way. *Ties to:* S59 (True lowliness), S56 (The self quieted).
- **The one who wakes himself (Avot 1:14, avot line 19).** "If I am not the one who wakes my soul upward, who will wake it? For I have no waker from outside." *Ties to:* S41, S83.
- **Melancholy that flees people (Avot 2:11, avot line 46).** Greed, lust and "the illness of black bile" make a person hate what he sees and prefer the company of animals and desert solitude; this is not piety. *Guard:* withdrawal from people is a sign to watch, not a goal (ours; no clinical advice).

## 3. Meditation and practice

- **Three looks that keep one from sin (Avot 3:1, avot line 53).** "This contemplation brings a person to humility, remembering where he came from; looking at his end brings him to belittle worldly things; looking at the greatness of the One who commands brings him to hurry to obey." *Ties to:* techniques `cheshbon`, `before_whom`.
- **Many small deeds, not one great one (Avot 3:15, avot line 70).** Virtues come "by the number of deeds, not their size": a thousand gold coins given at once do not build generosity as a thousand given one at a time. *Ties to:* techniques `chazarah`, `act`. *Proposed new technique* **R-T6 "The thousand small gifts"**: turn one large resolve into a small act repeated every day.

> אבל לפי רוב מספר המעשים (avot line 70)

- **Know before Whom you labor (Avot 2:14, avot line 50).** Study other opinions to answer them, but guard the heart, and "know before Whom you labor, that is, direct his heart in the belief in G-d". *Ties to:* S33.
- **The kinds of speech (Avot 1:17, avot lines 23-32).** Speech is commanded, forbidden, disliked, loved or permitted. The loved kind praises virtue in stories and poems; the disliked kind is idle talk. Weigh a song by its content, not its language. *Ties to:* technique `quieting`, `lips`.
- **The friend of the good (Avot 1:6, avot line 12).** Three loves: for use, for rest (pleasure and trust), and for the good, where "the desire and aim of both is one thing, the good". "When you love, do not love by your own traits, but by the traits of your beloved." *Ties to:* S86. Chassidus's *hitva'adut* and *mashpia* have a clear partner here (ours).
- **Reading the Principles slowly (line 94).** "Go over them many times and consider them well; if your heart tempts you to think you have grasped them after once or ten times, G-d knows it has tempted you with a lie." *Ties to:* technique `chazarah`.
- **Pray for understanding (intro line 285).** With all study, "a person can only leave his affairs in the Creator's hand, and pray to Him and plead that He give him understanding". *Ties to:* S55.

## 4. The big human questions

- **What happens after death?** The World to Come is the soul, without body, joined to what it knows; "the crowns on their heads" is the knowledge they gained, and "enjoying the Shechinah's radiance" is knowing G-d (line 79). The worst punishment is that the soul is cut off (line 79). "The wicked, even in their lives, are called dead" (line 79).
- **What are the Torah's rewards for?** Its worldly blessings remove what blocks service; "the reward of a mitzvah is a mitzvah" (line 79).
- **Who belongs?** One who believes the Thirteen Principles is within Israel, and "it is a command to love him and have mercy on him", even if he sins from desire (line 94).
- **What will the Messiah bring?** A king, peace, ease of living, and freedom to know G-d; the world keeps its nature (lines 79, 93). "Though he tarry, wait for him" (line 93).
- **What is a human being for?** To know the unity of G-d, and to be both wise and good; wisdom brings to deeds (intro lines 325-337).
- **Can I be wrong?** Beit Hillel, "when they saw that the words of those who differed were better... admitted and returned to their view" (intro line 168): love of truth over winning. *Ties to:* S59.

## 5. Where Chassidus reads this range, and where it departs

- **The World to Come vs. the resurrection.** For the Rambam the final goal is the soul without body (line 79; Teshuvah 8). The Tanya holds that "the purpose of this completeness of the days of Messiah and the resurrection of the dead is the revelation of the Infinite light in this physical world" (Tanya ch. 37, line 495 of the Tanya file) and that G-d "desired a dwelling in the lower worlds" (ch. 36, line 484). This is one of the deepest disagreements: Chassidus follows the Ramban's view that the final reward is in the body, in this world (ours: the Ramban link is widely known; not checked in this pass).
- **The Thirteen Principles.** Chabad accepts them fully (they are printed in the siddur as "Ani Maamin"). The first principle's "all depends on His existence" (line 80) fits the Chabad teaching of constant dependence; the Tanya's "yesh me'ayin" goes further (see `eight-chapters.md` on will and nature).
- **Lishmah through shelo lishmah.** Chassidus accepts the ladder (line 74; Talmud Torah 3:5) and in the Tanya (ch. 39) grades service by its motive much like the Rambam's child and nuts, but grounds the path in the soul's inner love rather than the growth of the mind.
- **Humility to the end.** The pious man on the ship (avot line 77) and the Tanya's *bittul* point the same way; the Tanya roots it in the nothingness of all before G-d (S62), the Rambam in the worthlessness of honor.

## What this range adds

- The Rambam's own summary of Jewish belief (the Thirteen Principles), with the unity principles in his words. - The ladder of motives (child and nuts), and the love that lasts because its cause lasts. - The purpose-of-creation passage, which differs from the Guide and is closer to Chassidus. - Three practices: the three looks (Avot 3:1), the thousand small gifts (R-T6), and slow repeated reading.

## Where the thread is thin

- The Introduction's chapters on the kinds of law, the order of tractates and the sages (lines 117-264, 356-466) are methodology. - Sanhedrin chapters 1-9 and 11 are legal commentary (read at index level). - Parts of the Avot commentary (5:1-9: the ten sayings, trials, miracles) are brief explanations with little inner teaching.
