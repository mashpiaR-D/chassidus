Ner Mitzvah (The Candle of the Mitzvah) is a short book for Chanukah. Volume I explains why there are four kingdoms that rule the world before the end. Volume II explains the Chanukah miracle and some laws of the lights, and ends with a sharp call to keep the community's fences.

Most of Volume I compares peoples and kingdoms. For the guide only its frame is used: why a world made by the One has lack in it, and how the One relates to the "four sides".

Three teachings matter most for the guide:

- **The One and the four.** "What is one fits the middle, and what goes out from the one belongs to four" (7). The letter heh is four sides with a point inside: "G-d is One, and His power spreads to all" (9).
- **Lack is not from the Maker.** A caused world cannot be without lack, but "lack and absence are not the work of a maker at all" (5). And "nothing can come from the One and be against Him" (6).
- **Step by step.** "One does not reach this high level at first, but climbs to it step after step" (50). This is why we add a light each night.

## 2.1 The One fits the middle; the going-out fits four

"דבר שהוא אחד מסוגל לו האמצעי, והיוצא מן האחד הוא מתיחס לד'" (7, Vol. I 7): "what is one fits the middle, and what goes out from the one belongs to four." The middle has no side, so it can only be one. The four winds are split, each going its own way, so they are "the opposite of one". While the forces that pull the world to the four sides rule, His honor is hidden. The core of that honor is: "עיקר כבודו מה שהוא יתברך אחד בעולמו ואין זולתו" (8, Vol. I 8): "the core of His honor is that He is One in His world and there is none besides Him."

## 2.2 The yud and the heh: one, and His power in all

This world was made with the heh of His Name, the world to come with the yud (9). The yud is tiny and cannot be split: He is one. The heh is a dalet (four sides) with a small yud inside it: "השם יתברך הוא אחד, וכחו מתפשט אל הכל" (9, Vol. I 9): "G-d is One, and His power spreads to all." Some say one maker can make only one kind of thing. The Maharal answers the opposite: many different works point to one source. "והאחד מצטרף אל הכל והוא מקשר הכל" (9): "and the One joins all and ties all together." So this world is divided and still one world: "כי הריבוי הזה הוא לאחד" (9): "for this many is for the One." "ועולם הבא שהוא אחד לגמרי, נברא ביו"ד" (9): "and the world to come, which is fully one, was made with the yud."

## 2.3 Nothing from the One can be against Him

Why is there anything in the world that hides His honor? "אי אפשר שיהיה העולם, שהוא העלול מן השם יתברך, שיהיה העלול הזה בלא חסרון" (5, Vol. I 5): "the world, the caused from G-d, cannot be without some lack." But the lack is not His work: "אין ההעדר והחסרון מפעולת פועל כלל" (5): "lack and absence are not the work of a maker at all." And all was made for His honor, because "אי אפשר שיצא דבר מן האחד, והוא כנגדו, שאם כן יהיה הדבר כנגד עצמו" (6, Vol. I 6): "nothing can come from the One and be against Him, for then He would be against Himself." The lack comes from the side of the caused. And "וכל העדר מביא הויה חדשה" (35, Vol. I 35): "and every loss brings a new being."

## 2.4 From the Cause's side there is no change

"And I will not forget you" again (20-25, as in Be'er HaGolah 145-157). A bond like father and son can in theory be broken, because father and son are two. But the bond that comes from the Cause cannot: "מצד העילה אין שנוי, רק ההשתנות הוא מצד העלול" (25, Vol. I 25): "from the Cause's side there is no change; the changing is from the side of the caused." That is why "We will do" came before "we will hear": they did not stand on their own opinion. "ודבר שאינו מציאות לעצמו, רק הוא תולה בו יתברך, לא שייך סילוק והסרה מאתו" (25): "and a thing that has no being of its own, but hangs on Him, cannot be removed or taken from Him."

## 2.5 The world takes only some of His glory

"נר ה' נשמת אדם" (61, Vol. II 18): "the soul of man is the candle of G-d." A candle lights only at the wick; the oil is drawn up to it. So the soul is not spread through all the body; one power in the body receives it. In the same way "אין העולם מקבל רק דבר מה מכבודו, ואין יכול לקבל כל כבודו" (61): "the world receives only something of His glory, and cannot receive all His glory." So we say "ברוך כבוד ה' ממקומו" (61): "blessed is the glory of G-d from His place," which the Targum reads as "the whole earth is full of the shine of His glory".

## 4.1 One light more each night (`act`; new: `mh_one_more_light`)

Beit Hillel adds a light each night because "one goes up in holiness and not down" (50). Practice (all year, not only Chanukah): choose one practice (a minute of stillness before prayer, one line learned, one kind word). Each day add a little. If you miss a day, start again from where you were, not from zero.

## 4.2 The heh: the point inside the four (`bittul`; new: `mh_point_in_the_heh`)

Picture the letter heh: an open frame of four sides with a small point inside. The sides are the pulls of your day: work, people, body, worry. The point is Him. Say: "G-d is One, and His power spreads to all" (9). Then: "this many is for the One" (9). Rest at the point.

## 4.3 The night for what is above (`before_whom`; with `mh_night_joining`)

"והלילה הוא מיוחד אל הדבר שהוא אחר הטבע" (71, Vol. II 28): "and the night is set for what is beyond nature." Light a small light at nightfall and give the first minutes of the night to learning or quiet before Him.

## 4.4 Stay with the whole (`act`; new: `mh_with_the_whole`)

"כי הוא יתברך הוא עם הכלל, שהם הצבור" (82, Vol. II 39): "for He is with the whole, which is the community." One who breaks away from the ways of the community breaks away, as it were, from Him. Practice: keep one shared practice (a minyan, a class, a communal custom) even when alone it seems not to matter. Adding such fences on yourself shows love: "חדשים גם ישנים דודי צפנתי לך" (84, Vol. II 41): "new ones and old ones, my beloved, I have stored up for You."

## 4.5 Today (`own_words`)

The Mashiach has no fixed time: "אם בקולו תשמעו היום" (89, Vol. II 46): "today, if you will listen to His voice." Pray in your own words: "יסיר השם יתברך לב האבן מקרבינו" (89): "may G-d remove the heart of stone from within us."

## 5. The big human questions

**If G-d is One and good, why is the world broken?** A made thing lacks something; the lack is not His doing (5), and nothing that comes from Him can truly be against Him (6). The lack is in the caused, and every loss brings a new being (35).

**Why is life so divided?** Because this world was made with the heh: four sides and a point. "This many is for the One" (9).

**Can I lose G-d?** What is from His side does not change (25). What hangs on Him cannot be taken from Him.

**Why do I get only glimpses?** The world receives "only something of His glory" (61), like a candle that lights at one point. It is enough to see by.

**What does my life add?** "כי נר מצוה ותורה אור" (76, Vol. II 33): "for a mitzvah is a candle and Torah is light." A candle goes out, light does not.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **"G-d is One, and His power spreads to all"** (9) and **"the One joins all"** are close to the Tanya's teaching that His power is in every thing and keeps it in being (Gate of Unity ch. 2). The Maharal speaks of power spreading; the Tanya speaks of the divine word that is the thing's whole being. Keep the difference.
- **The world receives only some of His glory** (61), with the Targum "the whole earth is full of the shine of His glory", is near the Tanya's "light that fills" (memale) and the hiding that lets the world be. The Maharal does not use tzimtzum. His reason is the receiver's limit, the same "receiver's side" key as in Be'er HaGolah 308.
- **The Rambam's negative attributes.** "Lack is not the work of a maker" (5) is the Rambam's view that absence is not a thing that is made. The Maharal uses it to clear G-d of the world's lack.
- **The Ramak's formula.** Not stated. The heh (9) comes near: the point is inside the four sides (He is in all), and it does not touch them (He is not the sides).
- **Step by step** (50) matches the Chabad teaching on steady, gradual service (avodah by stages), and the Rebbe's teaching on adding in holiness.
- **The soul as candle** (61) is the same verse as Tanya ch. 19 ("the soul of man is the candle of G-d"). The Tanya reads the flame's upward pull as the soul's longing to be absorbed in its source. The Maharal reads it as the soul lit at one point of the body. Both are useful; they are not the same teaching.
