## The short answer

This is not a book about the unity. It is a book about how knowing the unity is made real in a person, and prayer is where that happens. Study, the Rashab says, is only the preparation (line 49). The main thing is the verse "know today and return it to your heart", and the main word in it is the heart (line 62). **Contemplate in detail what the mind can hold (memale and sovev, creation from nothing, the Shema) until the light is felt in the heart; stand before the Essence, which is found in every place, in bitul; and know that Chassidus learned without prayer turns into selfhood.**

## How the book builds it

**What one contemplates, and where it ends (lines 41-47: the opening and §1).** A list, starting from memale, "which any thinking person can understand, even with the natural mind of the animal soul" (line 44), and going up to sovev, what is above it, and the Shema. The highest contemplation, "gazing at the King's glory", ends where the inner heart "sees and feels the essence of Godliness as it is, truly above comprehension" (line 44), and he becomes "like a silent stone" (line 45). Then the peak: "the perfection of His Essence bears all opposites, and all is truly yachid" (line 46).

**Detailed and inward (lines 48-59: §2-4).** "All the learning and knowledge before is only preparation for the main thing" (line 49). General contemplation "is no contemplation at all": the light stays surrounding, over the mind (line 49). True contemplation takes an hour or more, until "the light shines revealed in his mind, and from the mind it is drawn to the heart, engraved and felt in his heart" (line 57). Do not invent new readings of the prayer words while praying (lines 52-55).

**Da'at is feeling (lines 60-69: §5-6).** Da'at is not only binding but feeling (line 61). Two contemplations give awe: "all before Him is as nothing", and "the Infinite is found below as above" (line 62). And the verse: "the main thing is the returning to the heart" (line 62). In the lesser arousal the heart is moved by the leftover conclusion. In the true one "the garments are like a passage, and the main thing he feels is the Godly light" (line 66). This is "near to anyone who has a mind and a heart" (line 67).

**Against "deep matters only" (lines 70-73: §7).** Some deal only with what is above Atzilut and call creation from nothing, memale and sovev "low matters". The Rashab answers that "the main service now is in these matters most of all" (line 71). The highest matters are above comprehension; those who reach for them with the mind coarsen them, "bringing them into the selfhood of grasp and garments of corporealization" (line 72).

**Without prayer, knowledge turns to selfhood (lines 74-83: §8-9).** Prayer is the life of Torah and mitzvot (line 75). The soul came down into the body to refine the animal soul (line 77), and when the contemplation is of things the natural mind can hold, the animal soul itself comes to feel and love (line 77). Then the sharpest claim: "one may learn much Chassidus and be far from Godliness himself, and even the opposite" (line 80), and without prayer what he learned "becomes a deadly poison in him, making him a 'something' and very coarse in spirit" (line 81).

**The order of prayer, and the Amidah (lines 84-93: §10-11).** Pesukei deZimra, the blessings of the Shema, the Shema and Baruch Shem form a ladder. The Shema teaches "how He is one in the seven heavens, the earth and the four directions, truly yachid" (line 85). Each person must be "a vessel and dwelling for God's light and holiness" (line 89); unrefined, he exiles the Torah he learns and the Shechinah above; broken-hearted, "he becomes a vessel for God's light to dwell within him" (line 89). Before the Amidah he contemplates that "the Essence of the Luminary is revealed, found in every single place" (line 91), where hiding and revealing do not apply. He stands in inner bitul, "the main point of the Amidah".

**Diagnoses and the close (lines 94-123: §12-18).** The student who is whole in himself, for whom the teaching stays outside; "the true vessel for inner Godliness is the broken heart" (line 100). True feeling of Godliness makes one feel one's own evil as its opposite (line 104). The yoke of heaven is knowing "a Master, Havayah our God, who makes His kingship one over each person in particular" (line 111). The close: learn to know God, in order to do (line 122).

## What it comes to, for a person

- **Study is the preparation; prayer is the thing.** What you learned becomes yours only when it is felt in prayer (lines 49, 75, 77).
- **Stay with it until it lands.** An hour or more on one idea, in detail, until the light moves from the mind into the heart (line 57).
- **Let the words be a passage.** The ideas are garments. The aim is to feel the Godliness through them, not to be pleased with the conclusion (line 66).
- **Work where you can see.** Memale and creation from nothing are the working matters, and they are the main service now. Do not reach for the Essence with the mind (lines 71-72).
- **Broken is a vessel.** The one who is whole in himself cannot take in what he learns. The broken heart can (lines 89, 100).
- **It is near.** "Near to anyone who has a mind and a heart" (line 67).
- **Watch for knowledge that feeds the self.** Learning without prayer can leave a man "far from Godliness, and even the opposite" (line 80).

## What it adds to the Tanya and the other sources

- **Prayer, not study, is where the unity is known.** The Tanya asks for contemplation until the thought is bound to God "with a strong and firm bond" (Tanya line 629). The Rashab makes prayer the one place where knowing becomes real, and study of Chassidus without prayer a poison (lines 80-81). The SPINE keeps this as the dispute "prayer, or action and spreading outward" (the Rashab against the Rebbe); this book gives the Rashab's side in full.
- **Da'at is feeling.** The Tanya defines Da'at as binding the mind "with a strong and firm bond" (Tanya lines 112 and 629, chapters 3 and 42). The Rashab adds that it is feeling, and spells out how the mind draws the heart and how the garments become "a passage" (lines 61-66).
- **The animal soul turned, not only subdued.** Contemplating memale, which the natural mind can hold, lets the animal soul itself feel and love (line 77). The Tanya's beinoni regards his evil as still in its strength (Tanya line 222). Kuntres HaAvodah develops the same turn at length (Kuntres HaAvodah lines 142, 149).
- **"Yachid" said twice.** Of the Essence that bears all opposites (line 46), and of His oneness in the heavens, earth and four directions (line 85). The Rashab uses the index's precise word for the lower unity too.
- **"The Essence is found in every place."** Hiding and revealing do not apply to it (line 91). The Gate of Unity says the roots of the highest world and ours are exactly equal before the Essence (Gate line 2653). Here that is what one stands before in the Amidah.
- **A warning the index must heed.** "The main service now is in these matters most of all" (line 71): memale, sovev and creation from nothing, not the Essence before the tzimtzum. The SPINE already keeps the Rayatz's summary of this passage as anchor G21.

## Where the thread is thin (kept honest)

- **Most of the file is not the tract.** 40 lines of front matter (some of them facsimiles of handwriting turned to noise) and 130 lines of subject index are marked no tie.
- **Most of the tract is the mechanics of prayer:** distractions, standing still, the siddur, halacha study, businessmen's schedules. The tie is carried by the frame (F12), not by each line.
- **Ten ties are ours:** two line entries and eight sentence entries, for example "the essence brings its own revelation" heard as the Essence (57.2), and "near to Godliness in all his affairs" as the home below (85.6).
- **The home below in its full sense is never stated.** It appears only in the person, as "vessel and dwelling" (line 89). The purpose of the soul's descent is stated as refinement (line 77).
- **Heavy OCR** in places (lines 14-15, 26-39, 76). No tie was read into garbled text.

## Where it differs (kept as disagreements)

- **Knowledge or faith.** The Tanya ends Shaar HaYichud 7 with belief "above intellect and grasp" (Tanya line 834). The Rashab too puts the Essence above comprehension (line 72). But his remedy is not faith. It is deeper, inward knowing felt in the heart (lines 44, 72), and he warns against reaching for the Essence with the mind at all (line 71).
- **Who can do it.** "Near to anyone with a mind and a heart" (line 67). Yet most fail, and the young should not attempt it before their time (line 50, §12-14). Both stand.
- **Study against prayer.** The SPINE records that the Rebbe puts action and spreading outward first. This tract puts prayer first and study below it (lines 75, 80-81). Only the Rashab's side is here.
- **Whose words?** The summaries are the Rayatz's, and they sometimes sharpen the Rashab. The SPINE's G21 anchor (line 73) is the Rayatz's summary of lines 71-72, not the Rashab's own words; the Rashab's own sentence is line 72.
