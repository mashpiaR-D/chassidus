## The short answer

The Rambam is the one medieval master whom every Chabad Rebbe quotes by name, and argues with by name. His unity has three parts, and each one is a practice as well as a doctrine.

1. **He alone truly is.** "The foundation of foundations and the pillar of the sciences is to know that there is a First Existent" (`mt-01` line 1), and "He alone is the truth, and nothing else has truth like His truth" (`mt-01` line 4). All exists "only from the truth of His existence".
2. **He is known only by what He is not.** No attribute adds to Him. "He exists, not by existence"; "one, not by oneness" (`guide` lines 373, 375). Every true negation brings the mind nearer (the negation ladder, I:58-60), and at the top, "silence before You is praise" (`guide` line 393).
3. **In Him the knower, the known and the knowing are one.** "He is the knower, He is the known and He is the knowing itself, all one; and this the mouth cannot say" (`mt-01` line 22). "By knowing Himself He knows all, for all leans on Him for its being."

Then the path. Love is born of knowing, and is as large as the knowing (`mt-01` line 457). After knowing comes the "service of the heart": to put all one's thought on Him and be alone with it (`guide` line 1433), trained step by step from the first verse of the Shema to the whole waking day (`guide` lines 1437-1438). The highest are those who talk, trade and farm "with their limbs only", while their hearts do not move from before G-d (`guide` line 1439). And the book ends with deeds: to know His ways and "do kindness, right-giving and judgment in the earth", becoming like His actions (`guide` line 1469).

Chassidus takes all three parts, and limits each one. It takes the knower-and-known and says it holds only for the light clothed in the vessels of Chochmah, Binah and Daat, not for the Essence (Tanya line 832). It takes the negative attributes and makes them the door to *ahavah rabbah*, the great love (Hemshech Ayin Beis line 802). It takes the love by knowing and adds a love that needs no knowing, hidden in every Jew (Tanya line 1608). Where the Rambam's world is one that G-d made and lets run by nature, the Chassidic world is re-made at every moment and exists for a dwelling below.

> הוא לבדו האמת ואין לאחר אמת כאמתתו (`mt-01` line 4)

> נמצאת אתה אומר הוא היודע והוא הידוע והוא הדעה עצמה הכל אחד (`mt-01` line 22)

## His core teaching on the unity

**One, not as other ones.** "Not one like a kind that holds many ones, and not one like a body that divides, but a oneness that has no other oneness like it" (`mt-01` line 7; the second of the Thirteen Principles, `mishnah-sanhedrin` line 81). The Tanya's "a wondrous unity that has no unity like it" (Tanya line 112) has the same shape.

**Unity is pictured, not said.** "Belief is not what is said with the mouth but what is pictured in the soul"; the goal is to be "one who pictures the unity of the Name, not one who says it with his mouth" (`guide` lines 317-320). "There is no oneness at all unless one believes one simple essence with no composition in it" (`guide` line 324).

**Only negative attributes are true.** He is known in His existence, not His essence: "we grasp only that He is, not what He is" (`guide` line 384). The ladder of I:58: "He exists" means His absence is impossible; "He lives" means He is not dead; "one" means no multiplicity (`guide` line 385). Each proven negation brings one nearer (`guide` lines 389, 400); "the way is here; walk it if you wish" (`guide` line 402).

**Knower, known, knowing.** In the Guide (I:68): "He is the intellect, the one who intellects and the intellected, and these three are one thing in Him, with no multiplicity" (`guide` line 443); and in us too, "when we have intellect in act" (`guide` line 450). In III:21: G-d knows as a maker knows his work: "by knowing the truth of His own self, which does not change, He knows all that follows from His acts" (`guide` line 1128).

**No go-between.** The whole of idolatry began from honoring His servants; the law is not to serve anything "as a go-between between you and the Creator" (`mt-01` line 244).

**The world is His act, through causes.** Scripture attributes every event to G-d, whether it came by nature, by a person's choice, or by chance: "it was not you who sent me here, but G-d" (`guide` line 1012). Yet the will was set in the six days; things run by their natures (`eight-chapters` line 56).

**Our cloud, His light.** "There is no darkness with Him, only the great, strong, constant light"; the cloud is our matter (`guide` line 1055). The hiding of the face is "a screen we make between us and Him" (`guide` line 1442).

## The states and meditations he adds

The Rambam does not map feelings as Chassidus does, but he names real places a person stands, and he gives two full training programs.

**States (six proposed; JSON below).** - *R-S1 The flash that comes and goes.* Truth shines "until we think it is day", then hides; "like one on whom lightning flashes time after time in a very dark night" (`guide` line 33). - *R-S2 Silent praise.* "Silence before You is praise" (`guide` line 393): the mind that has understood stops speaking because words would lessen what it sees. - *R-S3 The overflow that must be given.* "Like a burning fire shut up in my bones" (`guide` line 936): the one with more than he needs must teach. - *R-S4 Moved to rescue.* The first degree of prophecy: divine help "moves and urges him to a great good deed" (`guide` line 980). - *R-S5 The cloudy day.* The one who knows and turns to business "is like one on a cloudy day": the sun is there, behind the cloud; he is "like a quick scribe when he is not writing" (`guide` line 1440). - *R-S6 Heart awake while the hands work.* The Fathers and Moses did worldly deeds "with their limbs only", while "their hearts and minds did not move from before G-d" (`guide` line 1439).

**States he describes that the map already has.** Love and awe from one contemplation (`mt-01` line 14: S39, S45, S46); love-sickness (`mt-01` line 454; *chesek*, `guide` line 1443: S43, S44); knowing that holds in suffering (Job, `guide` lines 1139, 1146: S27, S84); glad all one's days (`mt-01` line 92: S53); sadness blocks, joy opens (`mt-01` line 72; `eight-chapters` line 46); the returner who is "another man" (`mt-01` line 396: S31, S77); the inner will to be good (Gerushin 2:20, `mt-04` line 583: S41); always before the King (`guide` line 1447: S33, S46); nothing is fixed (`eight-chapters` line 57, the answer to S11).

**Techniques (six proposed; JSON below).** - *R-T1 The negation ladder* (Guide I:58-60). - *R-T2 The widening circle of attention* (Guide III:51): one verse held fully, then Torah, then all blessings; worldly thought given its own times; the alone and waking hours kept for Him. - *R-T3 The doorpost return* (Mezuzah 6:13, `mt-02` line 397): each passing of a door, meet the unity, remember His love, wake from sleep. - *R-T4 Immersion in knowing* (Mikvaot 11:12, `mt-10` line 1808): resolve once to leave a thought, then bring the soul "into the waters of knowledge". - *R-T5 The opposite for a season* (Eight Chapters 4, Deot 2): lean past the middle against a trait gone to an extreme, then settle and watch daily. - *R-T6 The thousand small gifts* (Avot 3:15, `mishnah-avot` line 70): virtue comes by the number of deeds, not their size.

**Practices he gives that the technology already has.** Sit before and after prayer and see yourself before the Shechinah (`mt-02` line 113: `arrive`, `before_whom`); contemplate creation until love comes (`mt-01` lines 14, 47: `breadth`, `depth`); daily weighing of traits (`eight-chapters` line 28: `cheshbon`); repeat the deed until it is easy (`mt-01` line 95: `chazarah`, `act`); everything for His sake, even sleep (`mt-01` line 105: `carry`); count the hours with Him (`guide` line 1446: `cheshbon`); stand at the doubt and do not force (`guide` line 210: `edge`).

## Where he agrees with Chabad, and where he differs

**1. The knower and the known: taken, then limited.** The Tanya quotes it three times as the model of the unity: "He and His wisdom are one, as the Rambam wrote" (Tanya line 65, ch. 2); "He is the knower and He is the knowing, as explained above in the name of the Rambam" (Tanya line 97, ch. 4); "by knowing Himself, as it were, He knows all creatures", adding that the Ramak in Pardes agreed (Tanya line 614, ch. 42); and word for word in Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah 7 (Tanya line 804). Then the gloss to ch. 9 draws the line:

> אחר שנתלבש אוראיןסוף בבחינות כלים דחכמהבינהדעת, אז שייך לומר מה שכתב הרמב״ם (Tanya line 832)

"But without the contraction and clothing, it does not apply at all to say 'He is the knower...', for He is not in the category of knowledge at all." The Mitteler Rebbe uses the Rambam's phrase to explain the one glance that sees all generations (Gate of Unity line 904), and elsewhere writes "not as the Rambam's phrase, 'by knowing Himself He knows all'" (Gate of Unity line 2285). The Tzemach Tzedek is the most exact: the kabbalists "differ from the Rambam" (Derech Mitzvotecha line 101), because the Essence is above knowing; yet "in this the Rambam's words are right" for the sefirot, which are divinity itself and not a thing apart (line 104); "we cannot say what the Rambam said, that by knowing Himself He knows all creatures", and the Rambam was forced to it by his system, to avoid multiplicity (line 107). He maps the dispute onto the kabbalists: the Rambam's view is like R. David's, that the sefirot are the Essence; the Maharal's like Recanati's, that they are vessels (line 103). (See also `GATE-OF-UNITY.md`, where this dispute is followed.) *Ours:* the guide should teach the Rambam's formula as true of the light that fills the worlds, and keep the Essence beyond it, as the Tanya does.

**2. The negative attributes: taken as the door to the great love.** The Gate of Unity does not use the Rambam's negative attributes (no "שלילה" in the whole book). Where Chabad takes them is Likkutei Torah and the Rashab. Likkutei Torah explains the difference between negative and positive attributes (Pekudei, line 116 of that file) and cites the Guide I:57-60 by chapter: "there are many knowings by way of negation" (line 117). The Rayatz repeats the Rambam's definition (Sefer HaMaamarim 5692, line 196) and adds that "negation is also a great and wide knowing" (line 197). The Rashab builds a whole stage of avodah on it: "hasagas hashlilah", knowing by negation, is the only knowing that reaches the Infinite light above the worlds (Samach Vav line 876), and it is the source of the higher love: "the great love comes through knowing by negation and knowing the wonder of the Infinite light" (Ayin Beis line 802).

> ואה"ר באה ע"י השגת השלילה וידיעת ההפלאה דאוא"ס (Hemshech Ayin Beis, line 802)

*Agreement:* the ladder is real. *Difference:* for the Rambam the ladder ends in silent knowing; for the Rashab it ends in *ahavah rabbah*, a love beyond reason, and in the negative commandments as its vessel below (Samach Vav line 1413). The guide's technique `edge` is the Chassidic heir of R-T1.

**3. Love by knowing, and the love that needs none.** The Rambam: "one loves G-d only by the knowledge with which he knows Him; if little, little; if much, much" (`mt-01` line 457). Love is commanded because contemplation can be commanded (`sefer-hamitzvot` line 76). The Tzemach Tzedek agrees on the command: it is "for deepening the mind" (Derech Mitzvotekha line 99). But Kuntres Acharon 8 says the commandment of love, "counted first... as the Rambam wrote", is the love of contemplation, "for on the love hidden in the heart of every Jew by birth and nature, no command applies at all" (Tanya line 1608). *Difference:* Chassidus has a love below and before knowing, which the Rambam does not.

**4. The world for man, and the dwelling below.** In the Introduction to the Mishnah "all beneath the sphere of the moon exists for man alone" and "the purpose of the world and all in it is a wise and good person" (`intro-mishnah` lines 316, 330). In the Guide he corrects this: the other beings "are also intended for themselves" (`guide` line 1078). Chassidus holds "that G-d desired to have a dwelling in the lower worlds" (Tanya line 484). *Difference:* the Rambam's purpose is a knower of G-d; the Chassidic purpose is G-d's own dwelling, made through the deed below.

**5. The final good: soul or body.** For the Rambam the World to Come is souls without bodies who "know and grasp of the truth of G-d" (`mt-01` line 443; `mishnah-sanhedrin` line 79); resurrection is real but not the last goal (`iggerot` line 212). The Tanya: "the purpose of this completeness, of the days of Messiah and the resurrection of the dead, is the revelation of the Infinite light in this physical world" (Tanya line 495). A real disagreement.

**6. Providence.** The Rambam: individual providence follows the intellect; a falling leaf is "pure chance, as Aristotle thinks" (`guide` line 1106); evil reaches the pious only when their mind turns away (`guide` line 1441). The Baal Shem Tov's providence over every leaf is exactly the view the Rambam rejects. Yet in the laws of fasts the Rambam calls "this happened by chance" "the way of cruelty" (`mt-03` line 1557), and in II:48 every event is G-d's sending (`guide` line 1012). Chassidus builds on these, not on III:17.

**7. Will in the six days, or renewal every moment.** "We do not believe so; the will was in the six days of creation" (`eight-chapters` line 56). The Tanya opens Shaar HaYichud 2 against those who "compare the work of G-d to the work of a man", the smith whose vessel no longer needs him (Tanya line 764): the Creator's power must be in the creature always (Tanya line 767). The Rambam also says all being depends on His (`mishnah-sanhedrin` line 80), so the point of difference is whether each moment is a new act of will. *Ours:* keep both; the Rambam's dependence is the floor, the Tanya's renewal the fuller view.

**8. The imagination.** The Rambam: whoever remembers G-d "following imagination only" is "outside the palace" and "not truly remembering G-d" (`guide` line 1433). Chassidus uses picturing and parables as tools (`picturing`, `mashal`), always knowing the picture is not Him. The Rambam himself commands one act of imagination: "to see oneself as if he himself went out of Egypt" (`mt-03` line 1121).

**9. Teshuvah without fasts.** The Tanya relies on him: "the Rambam and the Semag did not mention any fast at all in the commandment of return, only confession and asking forgiveness" (Tanya line 865). Agreement.

**10. Tzimtzum and the sefirot.** The Rambam has neither. His chain is G-d, the separate intellects, the spheres, the Active Intellect. Chassidus reads his "overflow" (*shefa*) and "light" as the root of its own language (`guide` lines 703-704), but its unity is a unity of Infinite light and vessels, not of a first cause and its effects (Derech Mitzvotekha line 109 contrasts the two chains explicitly: tzimtzum is "the opposite of the chain of cause and effect").

**11. The prayer kavanah.** The Gate of Unity notes the dispute of "the Rambam and the Tur" on whether to intend the words in detail or in general, "before Whom you stand" (Gate of Unity line 151). The Rambam's minimum, the first verse and the first blessing, is the floor the Chassidic long prayer stands on.

## What the guide should take (ours)

1. **The negation ladder as a sitting practice** (R-T1), taught as the Rambam's floor and the Rashab's door: negate, then rest at the edge in silent praise (R-S2), then let the great love come if it comes.
2. **The widening circle of attention** (R-T2) as the guide's plan for beginners: one verse fully held; widen only when it holds; give worldly thought its own times; keep waking and alone moments for Him. This is the most usable single passage in the Guide.
3. **The cloudy day** (R-S5) as the name for the busy knower's state, with its comfort: the sun did not go out.
4. **Love and awe from one look** (YT 2:2) as a short contemplation for anyone, universal in its language (D28).
5. **The two healing methods for traits** (R-T5 and R-T6): the opposite for a season, and many small acts, with the daily weighing.
6. **The doorpost return** (R-T3) and **immersion in knowing** (R-T4) as small bells for the day.
7. **The knower-and-known** taught with the Tanya's limit, and **the hidden love** added where the Rambam has only love by knowing.
8. **Guards:** do not strain past the mind's limit (`guide` lines 208-211); no self-torment (`eight-chapters` line 24); do not tell a sufferer that their suffering came from a wandering mind (`guide` line 1441); do not force the end (`iggerot` line 209); treat the Deot health regimen as history, not advice.

## Where the thread is thin (kept honest)

- The Mishneh Torah beyond Madda was read at index level only: its inner halachot were found and verified, but long stretches of law were not read line by line. - Guide I:69-76 (Kalam), II:3-11 and II:19-24 (cosmology), and III:37-50 (reasons for particular laws) are largely technical. - The Introduction to the Mishnah's middle and end (the kinds of law, the order of tractates) and Sanhedrin chapters 1-9 were read at index level. The rest of the Commentary on the Mishnah was not imported. - Of the Iggerot, the Chiddushim, Teshuvot, the calendar, the treatise against Galen, the letter attributed to the Rambam and the controversy letters were not read in full. Pirkei HaHatzlachah is of doubtful attribution. Sefer HaMitzvot beyond positive 1-10 and negative 1-4 was scanned. - Sefaria has no separate titles "Iggeret HaShmad" or "Treatise on Resurrection"; they are "Maamar Kiddush HaShem" and "Maamar Tekhiyat HaMetim" inside Iggerot HaRambam. The Responsa (Blau) and the medical works are not on Sefaria in Hebrew. - The Chabad citations above were found by search in the Chabad library and verified at their lines; they are not a complete list. The Rebbe's many sichos on the Rambam (Likkutei Sichos alone has an index of Rambam sources) were sampled, not read.
