## The shape of the range

Vayikra is the book of offerings, and the Zohar reads every offering as one thing: bringing near. A korban (offering) is a drawing close, and what is drawn close is not only a person to G-d but the powers above to each other, until they are one. That idea runs the whole range. Around it the companions walk the roads, spend a night in a cave with two donkey drivers who turn out to be sages, rise at midnight to learn, and bring back teachings on the soul, on sin and return, on the exile of the Shechinah (the Divine Presence) and on prayer.

1. **The letters and the call** (1-25). The letters of the Name sparkle over the camps of angels around the Tabernacle and combine into ויקרא, "and He called".
2. **The offering as union** (26-133). The Tabernacle stands, the King and the Matrona (the Shechinah) meet, and the offering binds the crowns. The broken heart is the vessel He uses.
3. **The Name, the ten names, the ten sayings** (134-206). Peace as the Name of G-d; the yud that holds all; the ten names that "are His name, and He is them"; midnight Torah and the hind that rises with it.
4. **Sin, return and the exile** (207-394). The soul's descent under oath, the two shadows that leave before death, sin that separates above, the tzaddik who holds the world, teshuvah as the Hidden returning into the revealed, the night in the cave.
5. **Knowing one's sin, and the three levels of soul** (395-449). Torah tells a person his sin "like a mother to her son", holiness from below and from above, nefesh, ruach and neshamah, and prayer from the depths.

## The unity of G-d

**The One is made whole, or left unwhole, by what happens below.** This is the sharpest thing the range says, and it is said many times. The Zohar calls G-d "One" in a double sense. There is the One who is simply above all ("above all of them is the Holy One, blessed be He, who carries all in His strength", line 136). And there is the One that is a union, the joining of the Holy One and the Shechinah, and this union depends on Israel:

> דאמר רבי שמעון, זווגא דדכר ונוקבא אקרי אחד (Zohar, Vayikra 16:2; line 101)
> Rabbi Shimon said: the joining of male and female is called One.

> והשתא קודשא בריך הוא לא אקרי אחד. ורזא דמלה, כנסת ישראל בגלותא (Vayikra 16:3; line 102)
> And now the Holy One is not called One. The secret of it: the Assembly of Israel is in exile.

So the verse "on that day the Lord will be One" (Zechariah 14:9) is read literally: now He is not called One, and on that day He will be. The same point comes from the other side. When Israel act well, the Name is complete; when they do not, "as it were, the holy Name is not complete above" (כביכול שמא קדישא לא שלים לעילא, line 48). Sin divides: "the one who whispers separates the Chief" (ונרגן מפריד אלוף), that is, separates the Matrona from the King, "and so He is not called One" (line 270). And the offering undoes the split: "the offering brings the upper world close to the lower world, and all becomes one" (קרבן קריב עלמא עלאה בעלמא תתאה ואתעביד כלא חד, line 108).

> קרבן מאינון כתרין קדישין, דמתקרבי כלהו כחדא, ומתקשרן דא בדא, עד דאתעבידו כלהו חד, ביחודא שלים (Vayikra 6:1; line 51)
> "Korban" is from the holy crowns, which all draw near together and bind one to another until they all become one, in a complete unity.

**When the faces look straight, all the worlds are one.** The deepest version is given at line 246. When the deeds of people below are straight, the Ancient Holy One (Atika Kadisha, the hidden innermost face of G-d) is revealed in the Small Face (Zeir Anpin, G-d as He relates and responds), the faces look into each other without turning aside, and all are blessed:

> ומשתכחי כלהו עלמין כחד, וכדין אתקרי … יי' אחד ושמו אחד (Vayikra 32:6; line 246)
> And all the worlds are found as one, and then He is called "the Lord is One and His Name One".

When sins grow, "the Ancient Holy One is hidden and the faces do not look into each other" (line 247). Teshuvah is then read as the return of the Hidden into the revealed: "He will return: the Ancient Holy One will return to be revealed in the Small One, He will return to reveal what was hidden before, and all of it is called teshuvah" (line 260). Teshuvah is also each crown going back to its place, "and all return one to one, and each is blessed" (וכלהו תבין חד לחד, line 257).

**The names are Him.** The Zohar lists the ten names of G-d, from Ehyeh, "the hidden above all", to Adonai, the kingship below, and ends:

> ואלין אינון כתרין קדישין דמלכא, דהוא אשתמודע בהו, ואינון שמיה, והוא אינון (Vayikra 24:23; line 177)
> These are the King's holy crowns, by which He is known. They are His name, and He is them.

Ehyeh itself holds the move from hidden to revealed: the first Ehyeh is "I am who I am, and it is not known who He is"; "asher Ehyeh" is "I am ready to be revealed" (line 166). The Name is also Peace: "the Holy One is called Peace, He is Peace, His Name is Peace, and all is bound in peace" (line 146). The upper Name "is all peace, and all is one" (כלא הוא שלום, וכלא חד, line 146). And the Name holds opposites together: only His Name "brings water near fire and neither puts out the other" (line 280).

**Above all of it, Ein Sof.** At the end of this range (line 461, read with part 2) Rabbi Shimon says that the cleaving of the will in the offering rises "to Ein Sof", where no light, no will and no lamp stands. That passage is reported in part 2.

*Ours.* This is the Zohar's great difference from the Tanya's first framing, and the guide should say it plainly. The Tanya (Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah) starts from G-d's side, where nothing is apart and nothing changes. The Zohar here starts from the relation, where the One is a union that can be whole or broken, and the person's deed decides which. Chassidus holds both: it calls the first "the higher unity" and the second the unity drawn below (F02, F07, F10). The Zohar gives the second its full weight: your act completes or divides the Name. It never says G-d Himself is less; it says "as it were" (כביכול) every time.

## States of consciousness

**Constant union and longing at times (rei'im and dodim).** Rabbi Shimon reads "eat, friends; drink and be drunk, beloved" as two kinds of love. The upper ones are "friends": they are always together, "in will, in oneness, in joy, never apart". The lower ones are "beloved": "those who long for one another and are not always found together" (line 39). Both are needed: "this is the wholeness of all". From inside, the first is a steady nearness that does not come and go; the second is a love that grows by absence. This fits S42 (love like water) and S43 (love like fire), and it adds that the fire-love of longing is the lower one's own form of completeness, not a lack.

> אלין ברעותא באחדותא תדיר, ואלין בתיאובתא לזמנין. ודא הוא שלימותא דכלא (Vayikra 3:8; line 39)
> These are always in will and oneness, and these in longing at times. And this is the wholeness of all.

**The broken heart as the King's own vessel.** "The vessels the King uses are a broken and crushed heart" (אלין אינון מאני דמלכא, line 120). The poor man in famine who weeps is nearest of all. The poor man's small offering is worth more: "his pain is enough for him" (דהא די ליה בצעריה, line 122). This is S05 (bitter with purpose) seen from G-d's side: not a place to escape but the place He is nearest. The one who offers "with the will of his heart", the Holy One comes to meet him (line 128).

**Joy for the one who comes in sorrow.** A person who sinned and comes to return stands "in a broken spirit, a sad spirit, and if he weeps, best of all" (line 110). Where then is the joy that service needs? In the Temple the priest and the Levites supplied it for him. Now "the praises of his Master and the joy of Torah" are the joy, "joy in the heart and song in the mouth" (שמחה בלב, ורננה בפה, line 115). This is S49 (weeping and joy in one heart), with a clear teaching on how the two are held: the sorrow is his, the joy is lent to him by others and by the Torah until it is his.

**A face and heart that match.** Rabbi Yehudah came to Rabbi Shimon troubled; Rabbi Shimon said "from the look of your face, what is in your heart is known", and he answered "my face and my heart are equal" (דאנפי ולבי שוין, line 76). A small line, but a clean picture of an honest state: nothing hidden between inner and outer.

**Far away, and still seeking Him.** David, driven out of the land, says "I seek You" (Psalm 63). How can he seek from far off? "Though he was driven from there, he did not leave his part, to seek the Holy One." It means "I would go and be seen before You, only I cannot"; "my soul and my body long for You and I cannot, for I am in a dry and weary land" (line 365). This is a precise state: thirst in the far place, when one cannot reach the place of the Presence. It sits near S13 (the unnamed longing) and S43, but its object is named and the distance is outward. *Proposed state: the seeker in the dry land.*

**Being lifted by the King Himself.** The exile passage (79-83) gives one of the strongest pictures of being held in the whole Zohar. The King has sent the Matrona out before, and each time she came back on her own. This last time she cannot. So the King goes Himself with all His household, finds her lying in the dust, and "the King took her by His hands, and raised her, and brought her to His palace, and swore never to part from her" (line 80). "Behold your King comes to you: not you to Him" (line 83). This belongs with S84 (trust) and the being-held work: when a person cannot rise, the rising is His.

> כד מטא לגבה, חמא לה דהוה שכיבת לעפרא ... עד דאחיד לה מלכא בידוי, ואוקים לה (Vayikra 10:6; line 80)
> When He reached her He saw her lying in the dust ... until the King took hold of her by His hands and raised her.

**The watchman question.** A striking inside view (376-378): the exiles lying in the dust call "Watchman, what of the night?" They leave their own pain and ask after the Shechinah: "My beloved sons, who are pressed in exile, leave their own pain and ask Me about My Matrona" (ושבקין צערא דלהון, ותבעין לי על מטרוניתא, line 377). His answer: I am ready to meet her; it waits on you; return. *Ours:* this is a real state, the pain that turns from "what about me" to "what about Him". It is S54 (compassion for the soul) turned outward to the Shechinah, and it is the root of the Chassidic "prayer for the Shechinah" (tzar haShechinah). *Proposed state: grief for the Presence.*

**The three levels of the soul as three places.** Lines 430-438: a person who has only nefesh (the life-soul) is near the unclean side; his dreams mix truth and lies and small spirits "laugh at him". When he gains ruach (spirit), it "breaks mountains and rocks", rises among angels, learns and returns. When he gains neshamah (the higher soul), it rises and "sees the delight of the King, and enjoys the upper radiance" (line 434). And for any Jew, however low: "a higher level stands over him, and if he wants to acquire ruach, if he wants to acquire neshamah, he acquires it" (line 438). This is C1 (outer to inner) as a ladder that is always open.

## Meditation and practice

**Stand before the altar in a broken spirit, and be ashamed of your deeds.** "A person must stand on the altar in a broken spirit and be ashamed of his deeds, so that the hard spirit be broken" (line 53). This is the inner act the offering asks for. *Ours:* in the guide it goes with `cheshbon` (the evening look-back), but its end is not the look-back itself: it is that the hardness in judgment be sweetened.

**The self as the offering.** "Happy are the righteous who offer every day this offering before the Holy One: their bodies and their souls, which they offer before Him" (line 131). The poor man's offering is "his fat and his blood", burnt in the effort; so "the root of the offering is the will of his heart, his spirit and his soul" (line 130). *Proposed technique: offering oneself daily* (see the overview), a morning act of giving over the day's strength.

**Prayer: tie it all in one knot.** The most exact instruction in the range:

> מאן דיתבע בעותיה למלכא, בעי ליחדא שמא קדישא ברעותיה, מתתא לעילא ומעילא לתתא, ולקשרא כלא בחד קשרא, ובההוא קשורא אשתכח בעותיה (Vayikra 49:5; line 342)
> One who asks his request of the King must unify the holy Name in his will, from below upward and from above downward, and tie all in one knot, and in that knot his request is found.

The request is not set beside the unity; it is placed inside it. Prayer should be "with will and with the intent of the heart, to draw from that deep river", and "from the depths I called You" means from the depth of all, where the upper Father and Mother join (line 448).

**Before prayer, enter two doorways and take counsel with the fathers.** "Let a person always enter the length of two doorways" (line 113), and aim his prayer toward the Holy of Holies, which is the holy Name. Before entering the synagogue he should "take counsel with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob", include himself in them, and then pray (line 116). *Ours:* this is `before_whom` given a body: a pause at the door, a joining to the ones who first prayed.

**Confess, do not cover.** "Whoever covers his sins and does not lay them out before the holy King and ask mercy for them, they do not give him the door of teshuvah. And if he lays them out, the Holy One pities him" (line 338). If he weeps, "he opens all the closed doors" (line 339). Laying out one's sins is itself "the honor of the King".

**Midnight.** At midnight the north wind strikes David's harp, the hind rises to praise, and the Holy One delights with the righteous in Eden. "Whoever wakes at that time and toils in Torah is called a friend of the Holy One and the Assembly of Israel" (line 370). His words rise "into the bosom of the King", and by day a thread of kindness is drawn over him, "and he is not judged" (371-372). Such a person the Holy One and the Shechinah "make every day a new creation" (עבדי ליה בכל יומא בריה חדשה, line 399). *Proposed technique: the midnight rising (tikkun chatzot as the Zohar gives it).*

**The Torah that tells you your sin gently.** When a person sins and forgets it, the soul itself goes up and testifies (line 402), and he is reminded by judgment. But one who rises at night to learn is told his sin by the Torah, "not by way of judgment, but like a mother who tells her son, with a soft word, and he does not forget it, and returns" (כאימא דאודעא לברה, במלה רכיך, line 405). *Ours:* this is a rule for the guide's `discern` and `cheshbon`: the right knowing of one's fault comes softly, from inside learning, and the hard knowing comes when it is ignored.

**Guards.** The pure intention matters. A man who could not bring the rich man's ox and brought doves was told the offering was not his, and he vowed "never to raise a thought of sin on my heart" (line 124). Each brings by his means: "the Holy One does not burden a person more than he can" (line 117). A leader of prayer must be clean and know the King's ways (298, 312); Rabbi Shimon would not hear a man's prayer on Rosh Hashanah unless he had purified him for three days (line 302). For the shofar: "those who know the teruah", not "those who blow it" (line 311); the voice rises "from his spirit and his soul".

## The big human questions

**Why the soul comes down.** Before descent the soul is sworn to keep the commands, shown "a thousand and eighteen worlds", stands before the King in a garment of honor and looks on His glory every day (208-209). Then she enters a body and sins, and "the Torah wonders at her: with all this honor, she sins?" (line 209). The soul was sent to complete the holy Name below: a person "who does not want to complete the holy Name below, it is better for him not to have been created" (line 95). The soul that returns "goes back to her Father's house, as in her youth, to delight in the King's delight" (line 96).

**Death and the chance to return.** Before death the two shadows (צולמין) of a person leave him (210-211). Return while you stand in strength; return when you are "caught in the collar" is return, "but not as good" (line 212).

**Suffering and nearness.** The poor, the broken, the weeping are the King's vessels (120). In famine He hears their cry twice: once to attend, once to call to account those who caused it (121).

**Providence and the tzaddik.** When a righteous person is in the world, judgments do not rule, "for he is a sign in the world" (line 233). The mark of the true tzaddik is that he "leaves his own and takes up the whole world's" (שביק דידיה, ונסיב לדכולי עלמא, line 234), as Moses offered himself for Israel; Noah did not pray for the world, so the Flood is called "the waters of Noah" (236). And: "the Holy One decrees, and the tzaddik annuls" (240).

**Trust.** "I will save you from far off": He went far up, and from that far place He saves (351). When He works a miracle, "He does it in everything" (352, 355).

**Joy.** Service must be in joy (109). Peace-offerings are dearest because they bring peace to upper and lower (164), and the incense "does not come for sin or guilt but for joy" (165).

## For the guide (ours)

1. Name the Zohar's second "One": the union that the deed completes, and teach it next to the higher unity, never against it.
2. Take the broken heart as the King's vessel into S05 and the being-held material.
3. Add the prayer-knot (line 342) to the prayer method: the request placed inside the unity.
4. Add the soft knowing of one's fault (line 405) to `discern` and `cheshbon`.
5. Propose two states: *the seeker in the dry land* (365) and *grief for the Presence* (377); and two techniques: *offering oneself daily* (131) and *the midnight rising* (369-372, 399).
