## Before you begin: how to use this book

**What this book is.** A map of one road: from where you are right now, to knowing, and not only believing, that there is nothing but Him, and back down into an ordinary day. Chassidus teaches that knowing this truth and meditating on it are one and the same thing. To know, in its language, is to hold a truth until it is real to you. So this book is a book of practice. Each chapter is a stage. Each stage tells you:

- **The goal**, in one line.
- **What it is like from inside**: what people often notice. Described, never demanded. If you feel nothing, you have not failed.
- **The practice**: how long to sit, the steps, the thought to hold, and why it works.
- **The skill** you are building.
- **The obstacles**, with their Chassidic names and their remedies.
- **Signs you can move on**: small, checkable changes in your day, never a special feeling.
- **Warnings**: what can go wrong, honestly.
- **In daily life**: how it shows outside the chair.
- **The Chassidus underneath**: the sources, in Hebrew and in English.

**Who it is for.** Anyone. If you are Jewish and observant, the full way is yours: the sitting moves into the time before prayer. If you are Jewish and not observant, it is yours too, at your own pace: the Shema, a coin for charity, a line of Torah. If you are of another faith, use the name for God you use, a verse from your own tradition for the lips, and a kindness for the act; you are never asked to leave your faith. If you are not sure God exists, treat each line as something to think through, not something to sign. Follow the reasoning as far as it goes. Where thought ends, the sources say, faith begins, and your answer is your own.

**How to read it.** Read the introduction and the basic sitting first. Then take the self-check at the back to find your stage. Read that chapter, and the one before it. Practise it for days or weeks. Move on when the signs are there, not when you are bored or when you have had a big moment.

**Four things to know before you start.**

1. **Stages and states are different.** Stages 1 to 7 are skills, built in order, like learning to walk before running. Stages 8 to 12 are rungs that are *glimpsed*. The sources say the deepest of them are given from above, not earned. You may taste Stage 11 in your first week. That does not mean you are "at" Stage 11. Keep practising where you are.
2. **It goes up and down.** The Piaseczner says a person must always be going up and coming down. A flat week is not a failure; it is the path. Going back a stage is part of the design.
3. **Signs are fruit, not feelings.** The question is never "what did I feel?" It is "am I a little more my own master than before? Does an insult sting a little less? Did a deed come from it?"
4. **The top is a turn.** The book does not end at the highest experience. It ends in your hand: a mitzvah, a kindness, an honest word. Then it starts again, one rung deeper.

**How long it takes.** No one can say, and the sources do not count. A rough picture: the first three stages, a few weeks; Part II, a few months; Stage 7, as long as it takes, often a long time; the rest, a lifetime, coming round again and again. The minutes given in each chapter are this book's suggestions, not the sources' rules.

## The basic sitting

Every stage uses the same basic sitting. Only the line, the length and the emphasis change. Learn this shape once.

| Part | What you do | 3 min | 10 min | 20 min |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before | A coin, a few words of Torah, step out of the day |  | 1 | 2 |
| 1. Arrive | Name what is moving in you, in one sentence | 10 sec | 30 sec | 1 |
| 2. Quiet | Watch the thoughts calmly; don't push |  | 1 | 2 |
| 3. The line | Say the one line softly | 10 sec | 20 sec | 30 sec |
| 4. Think it through | Breadth, length, depth, and down into your life | 1 | 4 | 8 |
| 5. The picture | One picture, and where it breaks |  | 1 | 2 |
| 6. Binding | Come back to the line whenever you drift | all through | all through | all through |
| 7. From inside | Let the heart answer, or not; don't check | all through | all through | all through |
| 8. Your own words | Talk to Him about it | 30 sec | 1 | 2 |
| 9. The lips | A verse, or the Shema with "Echad" drawn out | 30 sec | 1 | 1 |
| 10. A tune | Only if one comes |  |  | 1 |
| After | One act; one sentence to carry; a look back later | 20 sec | 30 sec | 1 |

**Body.** Sit upright but at ease. Eyes closed or resting on the thing you are thinking about. Say the line in a low voice; the mouth helps the heart. No special breathing. The object of this meditation is a line of teaching, not the breath or the body.

**The one line.** Each stage gives a line. Keep the same line for several days. A short sitting that truly stays is better than a long one that only recites.

**After.** The sitting is not over until something is done with it. One small act within the hour. One sentence carried into the day ("being said", "held", "only He"). In the evening, a short look back that ends in joy.

## The three skills, and the one rule

Modern meditation books name the few skills everything rests on. Chassidus has its own three, and every stage trains one of them more than the others.

- **Binding (kesher hada'as).** Holding the mind on one truth and bringing it back without a fight. This is what makes knowing into knowing.
- **Detail (hisbonenus pratis).** Taking a truth out of the general and into particular things: this cup, this worry, this hour. General thoughts, the Rashab says, only hover around you.
- **The deed (ma'aseh).** Putting every good stirring into an act. Light holds only in a vessel.

**The one rule: describe, never demand.** No feeling is required at any stage, and no feeling proves anything. The Mitteler Rebbe's great rule is that the deeper something is, the less it is felt. So this book tells you what people often notice, and then asks you to look at the fruit.

## Part I · The Door

Making a place to sit, waking up, and finding the self that is looking. Nothing lofty yet. Most of the work of a lifetime is done here, one ordinary day at a time.

### Stage 1 · A Set Time *Establishing the practice · Before the rungs (ONE-PROCESS step 1, the door)*

**The goal.** Sit every day at the same time, even for three minutes, and never miss two days in a row.

**What it is like from inside.** At first it feels like one more chore. Part of you will bargain: 'tomorrow, when I have more time.' Some days the sitting will feel empty. That is fine. The point of this stage is not a feeling. It is a place in your day that belongs to Him and to your soul, the way a meal has its place.

**The skill.** Showing up. Keeping a fixed time (keviut) and a fixed place.

**The practice.** *3 minutes a day for the first week; then 10 minutes. Same time, same place.*

1. Pick the time. Best: before prayer in the morning, or right after. If not, any fixed time you can keep: before the phone, before the car, before bed.
2. Pick the place. A chair you can sit in quietly. The same chair each day.
3. Before: a coin in a charity box, if you can; a few words of Torah; then 'I'm here.'
4. Say one line softly: 'The word that made this is saying it right now.'
5. Look at one thing in front of you, your cup, and think: this is being made right now. If the word stopped, it would not fall. It would simply not be.
6. Say the Shema, eyes covered, drawing out 'Echad'. (Or: 'There is nothing besides Him.')
7. One act: do one small good thing within the hour.

*The thought to hold.* The line of the first rung: 'The word that made this is saying it right now.' Keep it this simple for now.

*Why it works.* The Tanya sets the length of a contemplation by two things: how far your mind can go, and how much free time you have. So the short sitting is not second best. It is your measure today. And the Piaseczner says: if you cannot be aflame all day, at least set times when God is revealed in you. A set time is the vessel; everything later pours into it.

- **'I don't have time.'** *(tirdas haparnasah, the press of making a living)*. Three minutes is enough. The Rashab passes on the Mitteler Rebbe's advice to busy men: every extra moment is like buying life for your soul for the whole day.
- **Heaviness, laziness** *(atzlus and kevedus, from the earth element in the animal soul)*. Do not wait to feel like it. The Tanya: heaviness is not beaten by heaviness, only by quickness, which comes from joy. Get up, go to the chair, start. Speed first, feeling later.
- **'I'm not a spiritual person.'** *(false humility, a trick of the inclination)*. This path is for everyone. Giving your whole self to Him in general, as in Modeh Ani, is 'very near to every Jew' (Kuntres HaAvodah), and the line about God's oneness is the truth about every person.

- You have sat at your time on at least 25 of the last 30 days. - When you miss a day, you come back the next day without guilt or a speech to yourself. - You have done the 'one act' after most sittings.

- Do not start with long sittings. A long sitting that only recites is worth less than a short one that truly stays. - Do not judge the sitting by how it felt. In this stage it is supposed to feel ordinary.

**In daily life.** A small anchor in the day. People often notice that the rest of the day has a little more shape, simply because one part of it is fixed.

**The Chassidus underneath.** The practice starts with time, not with ideas. Chassidus asks for 'a long, unbroken time' eventually, but it says the right length is different for every soul and depends on the free time you have.

### Stage 2 · Waking the Sleeper *Arriving from what is already moving · ONE-PROCESS step 1 · sitting L01 opens*

**The goal.** Begin each sitting from what is really moving in you, and let the flood of thoughts slow down without fighting it.

**What it is like from inside.** A head like a train station. Worries, lists, a song you heard. Underneath, sometimes, an ache you can't name. The Mitteler Rebbe calls the daily rush 'sleep': life is not gone from a sleeper, only hidden. After a minute of watching, the head empties a little, and what is left is not empty.

**The skill.** Noticing, and coming back without arguing. Watching a thought instead of being inside it.

**The practice.** *10 minutes: 1 to arrive and quiet, 6 for the line, 3 for words, Shema and the act.*

1. Arrive (30 seconds): name in one sentence what is moving in you now. 'I'm worried about money.' 'I'm tired.' 'I feel a small joy.' That sentence is the door.
2. Quiet (1 to 2 minutes): look at your thoughts and ask gently, 'What am I thinking?' Watch them the way you watch cars pass. Don't push them away. Slowly the head empties.
3. Say the line softly: 'The word that made this is saying it right now.'
4. Take the thing you named at the start (the money, the tiredness) and say: this too is being said, right now.
5. Each time you drift, come back to the line. No scolding, no argument. Coming back is the meditation.
6. A few words to Him in your own language. The Shema. One act.

*The thought to hold.* Even the scatter is inside the unity. The restlessness itself is the soul reaching out: the Piaseczner says it is not you who feels this ache, but the spark in you.

*Why it works.* You cannot make the heart feel, and forcing it kills it. So start not from an idea but from where you actually are. The Piaseczner: every stirring, even a bodily one, is a key to the soul. Then quiet by watching, because the more you push thoughts away, the stronger they get.

- **Stray thoughts** *(machshavos zaros, foreign thoughts)*. Two ways, both without a fight. For passing thoughts, the Tanya: give them no answer and no argument; act as if you did not hear them, and put more strength into the line. For one stubborn thought, the Piaseczner: look straight at it for twenty seconds, 'so this is what I'm thinking'. A thought you look at melts.
- **Getting upset about the stray thoughts** *(atzvus during the service)*. Do not let your heart fall over it. The Tanya says this sadness during the work is itself the inclination's trick. Come back gladly; each return is a small victory.
- **The sleeper** *(sheinah, sleep: the rush of the day that hides the soul)*. Don't take the dullness for death. A sleeper is alive; he only needs waking. One honest sentence about where you are is the fly that wakes him.
- **Chasing a feeling, or taking quiet as the goal** *(wanting arousal 'so as to be a something')*. The quiet is only the table cleared for the line. Don't stay in it. Move on to the line.

- You can name what is moving in you in one honest sentence, most days. - Coming back from a drift takes seconds, not minutes, and you no longer argue with yourself about drifting. - The quiet minute usually works: the head empties a little.

- This is not mindfulness for its own sake. The watching is a door, not the room. - If the whole stream is wild every day, check the plain things first: sleep, food, a hurt someone gave you.

**In daily life.** Small: you catch yourself mid-worry and say what it is. A worry that is named is already a little smaller.

**The Chassidus underneath.** The Mitteler Rebbe calls distraction the exile of the Shechinah and sleep. The Piaseczner teaches the 'quieting' (hashkatah): watch your thoughts for a few moments, 'what am I thinking?', and slowly the head empties.

### Stage 3 · The Floor Under the Floor *The true self · ONE-PROCESS step 2 · sitting L01*

**The goal.** Find, under all the layers you carry, the part of you that is 'truly a part of God above', and say yes to it.

**What it is like from inside.** A floor under the floor. The voice that says 'this is who you are' (your mistakes, your role, your mood) goes quiet without being argued away. Sometimes a homesickness that turns out to be love: like a candle flame, which always leans upward.

**The skill.** Looking at a layer of yourself instead of from inside it. Acknowledgment (hoda'ah): a simple yes.

1. Arrive and quiet, as in Stage
2. 2. Line: 'Under everything I carry, the deepest me is a part of Him.'
3. Walk the layers one at a time, slowly: what I do; what I feel; what I believe about myself. For each one, notice: it changes. So it is not the bottom.
4. Look at each layer from the side, not from inside it. A thought you look at melts.
5. Ask quietly: who is looking out through my eyes right now?
6. Say Modeh Ani slowly, as handing your whole self to Him. Or a thank-you in your own words.
7. One act: one good deed done as if the good in you were the real you.

*The thought to hold.* The search for yourself is already the search for Him. The Tanya: the second soul is truly a part of God above. The Piaseczner: the knowledge of God is in the knowledge of oneself.

*Why it works.* People are not first looking for God; they are looking for themselves. Chassidus agrees that the road starts there. But the self you usually know is 'only the shadow of the world'. So the true self is not found by looking inward alone; it is found by looking toward Him. That is why this stage leads straight into the rungs.

- **Inflation** *(yeshus, selfhood: 'I'm a divine spark, so anything goes')*. The spark is His, not a trophy. A real glimpse leaves you humbler, not grander.
- **The verdict: 'I am my mistakes'** *(being wicked in your own eyes)*. The Tanya: do not be wicked in your own eyes. The layers change; the spark does not. Say Modeh Ani anyway.
- **Looking only inward** *(the shadow-self (tzel ha'olam))*. Turn toward Him. The true self shows up in the reaching, not in endless self-study.

- You can watch a mood or a self-judgment from the side, at least for a moment, during the day. - Modeh Ani (or your thank-you) is said every morning and sometimes means something. - A bad mistake no longer feels like the whole truth about you.

- This is not therapy, and it does not dig into old pain. If old pain comes, be kind to yourself and go to Stage 2's plain door. - Never use 'the true self' to excuse a wrong. The spark wants good deeds.

**In daily life.** You answer a harsh inner voice with: 'that is a layer, not me.' And you act once a day from the better self.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Kuntres HaAvodah gives this level of the soul (the nefesh) its service: acknowledgment, the simple general giving of yourself to Him, and says it is 'very near to every Jew'.

> **Milestone One: the sitting holds.** You sit at your set time almost every day, even for three minutes. When the mind wanders, you notice sooner and come back without a fight. You can name, in one sentence, what is moving in you.

## Part II · The World as His Word

The lower unity: everything you see is being said into being right now, filled and held by Him, and the hiding itself is His. Here you learn the three moves of the mind: breadth, length and depth.

### Stage 4 · Being Said, Right Now *Rung one: the lower unity · ONE-PROCESS step 3 · sitting L02*

**The goal.** See, in one particular thing at a time, that it exists only because His word is saying it now.

**What it is like from inside.** For a second it is not an idea but simply so: this cup is being said. Then it often goes flat, right when the details begin. That flatness is where the work starts. Sometimes a quiet love comes, 'like water': seeing how the world lives from the Godly light.

**The skill.** Breadth (rochav): taking one idea to many particular things, 'wide as a river'. Detail instead of generality.

**The practice.** *10 to 15 minutes; the thinking-through is 4 to 8 of them.*

1. Arrive, quiet, and say the line: 'The word that made this is saying it right now.'
2. Breadth: go to one thing at a time. The table. The window. The street outside. Your own hand. For each, slowly: this is being said into being this second. Not 'was made'. Is being made.
3. Bring in the thing you named at the start. The bills, the worry, you sitting here worrying: all being said right now.
4. It may go flat. Stay. Add one more detail. Don't explain more; look more.
5. A picture, then break it: His word never leaves Him the way my words leave me, so I am never outside Him. Only a picture; His speech is nothing like ours.
6. Your own words: 'You are saying me now. You are saying the bills too.' The Shema. One act.

*The thought to hold.* If the letters of His word withdrew for an instant, the heavens would be truly nothing. Each thing is real, and it is not a thing on its own.

*Why it works.* The Rashab warns that contemplating 'the world' in general is a false imagination: it only hovers around you. The power of the Infinite is seen in particulars. So you train breadth: one thing, then another, then another, until the truth has touched your actual life.

- **Staying general** *(hisbonenus klalis, which the Rashab calls a dimyon kozev, a false imagination)*. Always move toward detail. If you hear yourself say 'everything' or 'always', name this cup and this hour instead.
- **Going flat** *(kerirus, coldness: going cold when the real work starts)*. Expected. The Rashab: when real contemplation begins, a person cools, because the work is heavy and new. Stay with one more detail. Don't change the line yet.
- **'Nothing is real, so nothing matters.'** *(a counterfeit of the higher unity)*. The world is real and really given. This rung makes things more His, not less real. Pay the bill; hug the child.

- You can take the line through five or six particular things without going general. - At least once during the day, at a cue, the words 'being said' come back by themselves. - When it goes flat, you stay instead of quitting.

- How something comes from nothing 'is not truly grasped by the human mind' (Kuntres HaAvodah). Expect the edge. It is not failure. - This rung alone can slide back into self. It needs a taste of the next rungs.

**In daily life.** At a moment of worry, look at one plain object and say: 'Being said.' People often notice that worries lose some of their weight, because they too are being said.

**The Chassidus underneath.** This is the lower unity (yichuda tataah): the world is real, and continuously given. Kuntres HaAvodah gives this contemplation to the ruach, the heart level, and says this is what 'know today and return it to your heart' means.

### Stage 5 · Held *Filling and surrounding: this particular thing, from inside and all around · ONE-PROCESS step 4 · sitting L04*

**The goal.** Know that He fills each thing from inside and surrounds all things the same, and bring that picture all the way down into your own life, then let the picture break.

**What it is like from inside.** The world begins to look like a garment with someone in it. You feel held, not holding everything up. Sometimes, before something you fear, a steadiness: 'You surround me and fill my body and my spirit.'

**The skill.** Length (orech): bringing an idea down, through pictures, 'until a small child could grasp it'. And the picture's edge: knowing where it breaks.

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'He fills this from inside and holds it from all around.'
2. Length: bring it down into the thing you named at the start. Your job. Your child. Your body. Say how He is inside it, giving it life to its own measure.
3. Then the surrounding: the same light holds it all the same, from head to foot, from outside. Not more in the big things, not less in the small.
4. Picture: everything your eyes see is the King's outer clothing, and someone is inside it.
5. Break the picture: He is not in the world like a soul in a body. Every picture fails here. Say so out loud.
6. Say the Piaseczner's line slowly: 'You surround me and fill my body and my spirit.' Your own words, the Shema, one act.

*The thought to hold.* Filling (memaleh) is the life clothed inside each creature; surrounding (sovev) cannot clothe itself in a bounded soul but holds it from above.

*Why it works.* Breadth spreads an idea out; length brings it down to where you live. The Tanya's picture of the King's garments is a ladder for the mind, and the Rashab adds that one cannot grasp a divine matter with no garment at all. But every garment must finally be seen as a garment. That breaking is part of the practice.

- **The cozy glow** *(a general 'everything is surrounded by light', kept far away)*. Bring it down into one hard thing in your life. If it stays pleasant and vague, it hasn't landed.
- **Picturing God as a soul in a body** *(a picture that fails at the root (the Tanya))*. Use the picture, then break it on purpose. Say: 'only a picture.'
- **Head without heart** *(knowing that does not reach the heart)*. Length is the cure: closer, smaller, more yours. Kuntres HaTefillah: bring the matter close to yourself.

- You can take one idea from its general form down to a specific moment in your day, and say where its picture breaks. - Before something hard, the sense of being held is sometimes there.

- Don't use 'He fills everything' to explain away someone's pain. Never.

**In daily life.** Say 'I have set God before me always' right before the thing you fear today. People often find that the fear gets a little room around it.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Kuntres HaAvodah: through the filling light, the neshamah comes to the surrounding, and there to the knowledge of His wonder. The Gate of Unity defines length as bringing the idea down until a small child could grasp it.

> Chovat HaTalmidim (the Piaseczner), line 129: «ואתה מקיף אותי וממלא את גופי ורוחי» — *You surround me and fill my body and my spirit*<br>
> Gate of Unity 1:11, line 18: «ואורך הוא עוצם ירידות ההשכלה להלביש את המושכל במשלים שונים עד להביאו בהשגות התינוק קטן» — *length: bringing the idea down, clothing it in parables, until even a small child could grasp it*</small>

### Stage 6 · Two Knowings *And the hiding is His · ONE-PROCESS step 5 · sittings L03 and L05*

**The goal.** Hold two true views at once without collapsing them: from His side the world is nothing; from ours it is really a separate thing. And see the hiding as His own power.

**What it is like from inside.** The cover is seen as a cover. The world becomes more solid and more His at the same time. The question 'then why can't I see Him?' gets quieter, because the not-seeing is itself where He is.

**The skill.** Holding two truths at once. 'Heart above, eyes below.'

**The practice.** *15 minutes. Can be split over two sittings, one knowing each, then one sitting with both.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'From His side, nothing. From mine, really here. Both true.'
2. First knowing: from above, 'before Him', all is as nothing. Stay a minute.
3. Second knowing: from below, the created thing really is a separate thing. Your table is a table. Stay a minute.
4. Don't merge them. Keep both open, like looking up and looking down at once.
5. Then the hiding: the sense that the world runs by itself is His strongest power, the power to hide. If you know He is hiding there, it is no longer a hiding.
6. Pick the thing today that seemed emptiest of Him. Say a blessing over it slowly, or a thank-you. The Shema. One act.

*The thought to hold.* The Tanya (Iggeret HaKodesh 20): 'before Him' is His knowledge from above downward; in the knowledge from below upward, the created thing is completely separate. Both are true.

*Why it works.* The mind wants one answer. Here it learns to carry two without forcing them together. That is a real skill, and it guards against two errors: a cold 'nothing matters' and a flat 'there's only the world'. Where the two knowings meet is above the mind (Stage 12).

- **'It's all one, so the lines melt.'** *(Korach's error)*. The unity does not cancel the distinctions God made between one thing and another (Sefer HaArachim). Right is still right.
- **Using 'He is hiding' on someone's suffering** *(misusing the teaching)*. Never. This line is for your own heart, not for explaining another person's pain.
- **Collapsing into one view** *(losing the 'heart above, eyes below')*. When you notice you're holding only one, say the other aloud.

- You can hold both knowings for a minute without one swallowing the other. - A moment that felt empty of Him can be met with a blessing or a thank-you, sometimes.

- This rung is close to philosophy. If it turns into debate in your head, go back to one particular thing and one blessing.

**In daily life.** The bedtime Shema with the two thoughts. A blessing said slowly over something ordinary.

**The Chassidus underneath.** The Baal Shem Tov: if a person knows the Holy One is hiding there, it is no hiding. The Rashab adds (Ayin Beis) that the aim is not that the hiding be removed but that it stop hiding.

> Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 20, line 1289: «היינו קמיה דוקא, שהיא ידיעתו יתברך מלמעלה למטה» — *that is 'before Him' precisely: His knowledge from above downward*<br>
> Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 20, line 1290: «אבל בידיעה שממטה למעלה – היש הנברא הוא דבר נפרד לגמרי» — *but in the knowledge from below upward, the created 'something' is a completely separate thing*<br>
> Keter Shem Tov 1:55 (the Baal Shem Tov), line 92: «שאם ידע האדם שהקב"ה מסתתר שם אין זה הסתרה» — *if a person knows that the Holy One is hiding there, it is no hiding*</small>

> **Milestone Two: the line comes back by itself.** During the day, at a cue (a door, a cup, a worry), the line returns on its own: 'being said, right now'. You can take one idea through breadth, length and depth. The world has started, now and then, to look like a garment with someone inside it.

## Part III · The Hinge and the Heights

Knowing becomes 'it is so'. Then the higher unity, the meeting of opposites, the place where the mind stops, and the heart's simple will. These rungs are glimpsed more than climbed; the sources say the deepest of them are given.

### Stage 7 · It Is So *Binding the mind until the heart answers · ONE-PROCESS step 6 · sitting L07 · the hinge*

**The goal.** Hold one truth so firmly, for so long, that it stops being an idea and becomes 'so', and let the heart answer when it does.

**What it is like from inside.** 'I knew this before too, but I only knew it.' Then, sometimes, it is simply so. The heart answers the way water gives back a face: often only a little, like the tip of a feather trembling. Often, nothing for a while. That is normal here.

**The skill.** Binding (kesher hada'as): fastening the mind, coming back, repeating only while the line widens, staying long. Depth (omek): the idea's single point, in the fewest words.

**The practice.** *15 to 20 minutes, the longest sittings so far. Stay on the same line for many days.*

1. Before: the coin, a few words of Torah, 'I'm here.' Arrive, quiet.
2. Choose one line from Stages 4 to 6 that has already touched you. Keep it the same for a week or more.
3. Breadth and length, briefly, as you learned.
4. Depth: say the line's single point in the fewest words. 'Being said.' 'Held.' Then stop explaining and stay.
5. Bind: when the mind drifts, come back without arguing. Repeat the short sentence only while it widens; when it goes stale, add one new detail instead.
6. If the heart answers, don't stop there. Go back into one more detail of the line before you finish. Cutting it short at the first warmth is what the Mitteler Rebbe calls 'the bad sickness that loses everything'.
7. Say one resolution aloud. The Shema. One act today that comes from the line.

*The thought to hold.* 'Know today and return it to your heart.' Knowing is not having an opinion. It is binding the mind to a truth 'with a strong, very firm bond', as firmly as to a thing you see with your eyes.

*Why it works.* This is where knowing the unity and meditating on it become one act. The Rashab: feeling is what knowing is; a person can understand a thing well and still not feel it. Strashelye puts the whole path in two sentences: first the unity made through knowing, then the main bitul, above knowing. You are at the first.

- **Head, not heart** *('I only knew it' (Tzav VeZeruz); the owner of the idea)*. Not more ideas. Three things: time (the same line, longer, for more days); length (bring it down closer); the mouth (say it aloud: 'put yourself into the words, and the fervour will come by itself').
- **The stone heart** *(timtum halev, a heart dulled like stone (Tanya 29))*. The Tanya gives the cause: the coarseness of the husk lifts itself over the soul's light. The thickness is self, not absence. Remedies: the deed, your own words, a tune, compassion for your own soul. The Piaseczner: it only seems sealed; in truth it is full of holiness even now. (Not to be used on someone who is truly low: see the warnings.)
- **Arousal that serves itself** *(hispa'alus as 'a something'; 'serving oneself' (oved es atzmo))*. Ask not 'am I moved?' but 'what moved me?'. If you were after the feeling, go back to the content. The real kind comes 'by itself', and you hardly notice it.
- **A flame in the air** *(arousal with no vessel (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut))*. 'Aha, I'm warm, I've seen light' and then nothing changes. Light only holds in a vessel: one resolution, one deed, today.
- **Collecting insights** *(knowledge kept as a possession)*. Bind one line instead of gathering ten. The Rashab: without the service of prayer, even what one learned can turn to poison.

- You have held one line for a week or more and it has gone deeper, not stale. - At least once, the heart answered without being pushed, and a deed came from it the same day. - You can tell, afterward, whether you were chasing a feeling or following the line.

- Expect to go cold, again and again. The Rashab says it happens precisely when real work begins. - Never force a feeling. Without binding, the Tanya says, you get only empty imaginings; forcing is the counterfeit. - The stone-heart remedy of lowering yourself is only for a steady person on an ordinary day. If you are low, hopeless or numb in all of life, that is not timtum: take the plainest, gladdest door, and get human help alongside.

**In daily life.** Set Him before you at one fixed moment each day: just the line, once. A 'light contemplation' then brings back the long one at any hour (Tanya 42).

**The Chassidus underneath.** Da'at is the hinge of the whole path. Tanya 3: binding the mind 'with a strong, very firm bond'. Kuntres HaTefillah: the feeling is what da'at is. The Rebbe: recognize, not only understand.

> Kuntres HaTefillah 5, line 61 (OCR): «וההרגשה זהו"ע הדעת, כי יכול להיות שמבין את הדבר היטב ומ"מ אינו במורגש אצלו» — *the feeling is what da'at is: a person can understand a thing well and still not feel it*<br>

### Stage 8 · Not a Thing on My Own *Rung two: before Him, nothing · ONE-PROCESS step 7 · sitting L06*

**The goal.** Taste, a little, that before Him all counts as nothing, you included: like a ray still inside the sun.

**What it is like from inside.** Less full of yourself. Not crushed, only less in the way. 'Not delight and not joy, only the self nullified.' Still, like a silent stone. It is quiet: the Rashab says this kind of bitul makes no noise at all.

**The skill.** Seeing (Chochmah) rather than reasoning: the point of the idea, held in stillness. Bitul: giving way.

**The practice.** *15 to 20 minutes. Not on a low day.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'Before Him, everything counts as nothing. Like a ray still inside the sun.'
2. Breadth briefly: a ray inside the sun is fully light, and has no existence of its own. So the sky. So the city. So me.
3. Depth: the fewest words. 'Only He.' Then stillness. Don't add.
4. The Piaseczner's daily line, said slowly: everything is God except me; only I, by my private will, made myself a thing apart.
5. Let that private will soften, a little. Don't push. If nothing happens, that is fine: Sefer HaArachim says the true bitul of this rung is given from above.
6. Your own words. The Shema. One act: one mitzvah or kindness no one will ever know about.

*The thought to hold.* Tanya 20: before Him all counts as nothing. Shaar HaYichud 10: the ray is truly one with the sun, with no existence of its own at all.

*Why it works.* At rung one, things are real and depend on Him. Here, from His side, they are not things at all. You can understand that in a minute; that it settle, be felt and actually make you less, is 'not in created beings by their own power' (Ayin Beis). So the practice makes the vessel: stillness, a hidden kindness, a little less 'me'. What fills it is given.

- **Pride in humility** *(bitul sought as a prize 'turns into its opposite')*. If you feel fuller of yourself afterward 'from that very experience' (Kuntres HaHitpa'alut), it was the counterfeit. Stop checking your humility; look at Him.
- **Self-hatred dressed as humility** *(atzvus, not bitul)*. Bitul is light, not heavy. If it feels like being crushed or worthless, stop and go back to Stage 3: you are a part of Him.
- **It fades when the thinking stops** *(bitul of the mind returns to selfhood)*. Expected (Samach Vav). The real kind is quiet and comes of itself. Keep making the vessel; the deed holds what the mind cannot.

- After sitting, a quiet lowliness sometimes stays, and you don't need to tell anyone. - An insult stings a little less, 'because he has nothing of his own'. - You can do a hidden good deed and not think about it afterward.

- Never on a low day. A sad or heavy person should not contemplate their own nothingness. Use Stage 2 or Stage 12. - This is not 'I don't exist' or 'nothing matters'. You are given a place; Stage 13 gives the self back.

**In daily life.** One kindness that no one will ever know about. People often notice they need less credit.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Kuntres HaAvodah: at this level the neshamah grasps the matter stripped of its garments, and the natural senses fall away. Kuntres HaHitpa'alut calls it kavanah: love and awe 'not separate from the contemplation at all'.

### Stage 9 · Weeping and Joy *The honest valley: opposites in one heart · ONE-PROCESS step 8 · sitting L08*

**The goal.** Let the cry over your distance and the joy at His nearness both be true, at the same time, without numbing either one.

**What it is like from inside.** Weeping lodged on one side of the heart, joy on the other. A bitter ache that you are so far, and a gladness that He is so near, and they turn out to be one movement seen from two sides. This is the hardest-feeling stage, and also, many find, the most alive.

**The skill.** Holding both awake. Telling a broken heart (merirus) from sadness (atzvus).

**The practice.** *15 to 20 minutes, at a set time. Bitterness is given a short, fixed place, never the whole day.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'What can't meet in me meets in Him.'
2. The far side: honestly, where am I far from Him? One or two plain things. Let it ache. Not long.
3. The near side: He is right here, saying me now, holding me. Let it gladden.
4. Hold both. Don't let one cancel the other. The test the Mitteler Rebbe gives: as much joy, exactly that much bitul.
5. Pour out both to Him in your own words, in any language you speak.
6. End in joy, always. The Shema. One act.

*The thought to hold.* The Zohar in Tanya 34: 'weeping is lodged in my heart on one side, and joy on the other.' Kuntres HaTefillah: the perfection of His Essence bears all opposites.

*Why it works.* This is the Chassidic answer to the modern 'dark night', and it is different. Pain here is not produced by the practice. It is a chosen, short bitterness at a set hour, with life in it, and it opens into joy. The Tanya draws the line sharply: sadness is a heart like stone with no life; a broken heart has life in it.

- **Sadness** *(atzvus: 'a heart dulled like stone, with no life in it')*. Lift first: joy, a tune, the deed. Don't do the bitter half today. The Tanya calls sadness over one's failings a kind of pride; never say that to someone heavy, but know it for yourself.
- **Bitterness spreading into the day** *(merirus without its set hour)*. Give it its fixed time and keep it brief. When it comes for no reason, that is the fit hour to turn it into an honest accounting, then joy (Tanya 31).
- **Numbing both** *(calling flatness peace)*. Name one real ache and one real gladness, out loud.
- **'It's all one, so nothing is really wrong.'** *(Korach again)*. The joining is His, not an excuse. Wrong is still wrong.

- You can tell, in yourself, whether today's heaviness is sadness or a broken heart. - The bitter part stays in its place and ends in joy. - Talking to Him in your own words has become natural.

- If the pain grows instead of opening, stop. Comfort yourself, do something plain with your hands, and go back to Stage 2 tomorrow. - Hopelessness, lasting numbness, or thoughts of not wanting to be here are not this stage. They need a person now (see Appendix C).

**In daily life.** A few minutes a day of talking to Him in your own words, both halves. People often find a strange lightness after an honest cry.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut: the bitter cry and the joy are 'one' arousal, only this one comes from the far side; 'two opposites in one subject'. Rebbe Nachman: a broken heart and sadness are not one thing at all.

> Gate of Unity 5, line 199: «ולפי ערך השמחה כך ערך הביטול ממש» — *as much joy, exactly that much bitul*<br>

> **Milestone Three: the heart has answered, and a deed came from it.** At least once, the heart answered by itself, without being pushed, and you put it into a deed the same day. You can tell sadness from a broken heart in yourself. A small humility stays after a sitting, and an insult stings a little less.

### Stage 10 · Alone *Rung three: where the mind stops · ONE-PROCESS step 9 · sitting L09*

**The goal.** Let the mind go as far as it can toward Him as He is (not this, not that, wonder) and bow at the edge.

**What it is like from inside.** A hush. Nothing to hold, and no one holding. The Piaseczner wrote of a man who did not know what his soul saw, or even whether it saw. Afterward: a thirst, not an answer.

**The skill.** Negation and wonder: knowing by 'not this', and stopping at the edge without grabbing.

**The practice.** *15 to 20 minutes, followed at once by something plain with your hands.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'He is alone. Not one of many; the One that is not a number.'
2. Negation, slowly: not a body. Not a light. Not even 'the Infinite' as I picture it. Not this. Not that.
3. Wonder: stay with the 'not'. Let the mind feel how far it cannot reach.
4. The Shema, with 'Echad' held long, as a leap rather than a thought.
5. Stop. Do not try to see. Say a few plain words to Him.
6. Right after: wash one dish slowly, or another plain task with your hands.

*The thought to hold.* 'Yachid' means truly alone, above 'echad', which still means separate things joined. Even 'yachid' is said of Him only from our side.

*Why it works.* The Rashab lays out the mind's last moves: grasp by negation, then knowing the wonder, then recognition, then, if it comes, seeing. Then he says no grasp reaches the Essence, not even negation, 'the highest grasp there is'. So the use of this stage is the thirst it leaves. The main service, he adds, is lower down.

- **Thinking you've arrived** *(seeing 'from behind' taken for seeing)*. All contemplation sees Him only from behind (Samach Vav). If you feel you've 'got it', you've got a picture.
- **Cold detachment** *('the world doesn't count')*. Go straight to Stage 12: the world held, one with Him. Then do something kind.
- **Spacey, unreal, floating** *(leaving the body behind)*. Stop. Stand up, drink water, do something plain with your hands. Next time, start from Stage 4 or 13.

- You can come to the edge of thought, stop there quietly, and come back to a plain task without a jolt. - What is left afterward is a longing, not a claim.

- Do not do this stage if you are prone to feeling unreal or detached. It is optional for many people; the heart's road (Stage 11) does not need it. - Never long sittings here. Short, then the hands.

**In daily life.** Wonder at ordinary things. People sometimes notice that they argue less about God and pray more simply.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut's chayah: the point itself is aroused 'in the very Godly light itself, not from grasping it'. The Rashab's 'nothing apart from You' stands above 'none besides You'.

### Stage 11 · The Simple Will *Where you and He meet · ONE-PROCESS step 10 · sitting L11 · the summit*

**The goal.** Rest in the plainest fact under all your feelings: you do not want to be separated from Him. That will is already one with Him.

**What it is like from inside.** Like a song that sings itself: it comes of itself, without your knowing. The deeper it is, the less you feel it as yours. Sometimes a sudden lifting. More often, simply: 'I will not be separated from You,' said like a child saying 'Father'.

**The skill.** Letting the mind stop and the heart's simple will (re'uta deliba) hold. Not watching yourself.

**The practice.** *10 to 20 minutes. Simpler than the stages before it.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'Under everything I feel, I want You. That want is already with You.'
2. Ask yourself honestly, without drama: whatever I fail at, would I agree to be cut off from Him? Notice the answer under the answer.
3. Stop thinking about it. Say a plain prayer in plain words, like a child to a father.
4. A niggun, if one comes. Let it sing itself.
5. The Shema as a leap. Do not examine what happened.
6. One act of simple faith: a short prayer in plain words, or a kindness done without explaining it to yourself.

*The thought to hold.* The yechidah, the single one at the bottom of the soul, is always one with His Essence. Samach Vav: by the heart's will the Essence is grasped.

*Why it works.* Here the mind's job is done; its part was to make the thirst. The Mitteler Rebbe says that at this level the unlearned and the scholars are exactly equal: it is one will in which all wills are included. That is why the summit is open to everyone, and why it is reached by simplicity, not by effort.

- **Watching yourself for signs** *(examining the elevation)*. The Piaseczner: one may not examine his elevation at that moment; investigating with the mind spoils it. Don't check. Do the act.
- **Grandness** *('I am one with God' turning into a mission)*. The real kind makes you plainer and kinder. A sense of special mission, with racing thoughts and little sleep, is the danger line (Appendix C), not a spiritual level.
- **Making the arousal the main thing** *('serving oneself'; fervour that flares into anger when its honour is touched)*. Look at the fruit: if your prayer is fiery and your temper short, the fire was yours. Go back to the deed.

- You stop needing the sitting to feel like anything. - In a hard moment, 'I won't be separated from You' is there without effort. - Plain prayer in your own words is easy and frequent.

- This is not the end of the road. The rising was for the sake of coming back down. - No one can tell you that you are 'at' this stage, including yourself. It is glimpsed, not owned.

**In daily life.** Simplicity: doing the right thing without needing a reason that day. People often notice fewer inner speeches.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Kuntres HaHitpa'alut: 'the great rule: the deeper the arousal of the essence, the less he feels it.' The martyr's plain sign: he may fail in much, but will not be separated from the One.

> Inyanah 11:5, line 64: «מצד היחידה שבנשמה שמיוחדת תמיד בעצמותו ית'» — *from the yechidah in the soul, which is always united with His Essence*<br>
> Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 29:19, line 556: «וזהו הכלל גדול דכל שההתפעלות העצמות עמוקה יותר כמו באהבה ורצון ותענוג היותר פנימית, יותר לא ירגיש בה» — *the great rule: the deeper the arousal of the essence, as in the most inward love, will and delight, the less he feels it*<br>

> **Milestone Four: you stop checking.** You no longer watch yourself for signs. What matters is simple: you will not be separated from Him, whatever you feel. Prayer in plain words comes easily.

## Part IV · Coming Down

The top turns out to be a turn. The world comes back as real and His, the dark is turned, the ordinary day becomes His home, and the whole thing ends in the hand. Then round again, one rung deeper.

### Stage 12 · The Plainest Thing *The turn: the world given back, one with Him · ONE-PROCESS step 11 · sitting L10*

**The goal.** See the plain thing as plain and as His: things really exist, and their existence is Godliness.

**What it is like from inside.** Plain. The bread is bread, and it is His. Nothing glows. The Rashab warns this is harder than rapture: it is much easier to long to rise out of the world than to live in it keeping the Torah.

**The skill.** Nothing to add. Coming back from the top without losing it.

**The practice.** *10 minutes. Often best right after a meal or before one.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'It is really here, and its being here is Him.'
2. Take one ordinary thing: a slice of bread. Look at it. It is bread. Don't make it holy-looking.
3. Now: its being bread, right now, is His. Not hidden behind it; its very existence.
4. Hold the two together, plain. No need for a feeling.
5. Say the blessing over it, slowly, or your thank-you. Eat it slowly.

*The thought to hold.* Samach Vav: they exist, only their existence is Godliness. Sefer HaArachim: even the world's existence is one with Him, which only the Essence, bearing opposites, can do.

*Why it works.* The top of the ladder is not the world erased. The Shema says 'echad', not 'yachid', on purpose. Stage 6's two knowings meet here, above the mind, in the plain thing itself. This is where the ascent turns into the descent.

- **'The bread is glowing.'** *(a new high taken for the Essence)*. If it glows, you've made a state. Let it be bread again.
- **'Everything is holy, so I can indulge.'** *(the animal soul speaking in the unity's words)*. The plain thing is His, so it is handled His way: with a blessing, with measure.

- A blessing or thank-you before eating is a real moment most of the time. - You no longer need the world to feel spiritual in order to see it as His.

- Don't skip to this stage to avoid the work of the earlier ones. Plainness that comes too early is just ordinariness.

**In daily life.** Eat one thing a day slowly, with its blessing. People often notice that ordinary things feel more given.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Strashelye: the intent is that bitul show precisely in the 'something'. The Mitteler Rebbe: a simple man's fleshly joy reaches the essential delight in the yechidah.

### Stage 13 · Coming Down *The dark place turned, and the home below · ONE-PROCESS steps 12 and 13 · sittings L12 and L13*

**The goal.** Bring the unity into the two places it is hardest to see: your own pulls and your ordinary day. Say one small 'no', and treat this Tuesday as His home.

**What it is like from inside.** Sharp, then lighter. A pull you didn't follow, and afterward an odd freedom. And the plain man's joy when the king comes to his little house: the kitchen, the commute, the hard conversation, as His home.

**The skill.** Holding back (iskafya), then turning (is'hapcha). Seeing the ordinary as the dwelling place.

**The practice.** *10 minutes, plus one small 'no' during the day.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'Even here, below, there is nothing besides Him, and this is His home.'
2. Name one pull that tugs at you lately. Don't argue with it in your head; you'll lose that argument.
3. Picture the next time it comes. See yourself not answering it, as if you didn't hear it, and putting more strength into what you are doing.
4. Tell Him the small 'no' in your own words.
5. Then the home: picture your kitchen, your street, your Tuesday. A king comes to live in a plain man's house. This is that house.
6. One act: a coin in the charity box before breakfast, as the first thing done in His house. Or one small 'no' kept today.

*The thought to hold.* Tanya 33: even on the earth below there is nothing besides Him, and this is His dwelling in the lowest realm. Tanya 33 again: the other side subdued, and darkness turned to light.

*Why it works.* The unity is shown most fully not by escaping the dark but by turning it. Kuntres HaAvodah says the soul was bound to the body 'to make everything one'. So the descent is not a fall from the top; it is the top's purpose.

- **'Since darkness turns to light, let me go into the dark.'** *(the pull talking in holy words)*. Recognize the voice. The turning happens by holding back first, never by going in.
- **Pride in the win** *(yeshus returning through victory)*. Thank Him for it and forget it. Tomorrow is a new pull.
- **A crushing self-inspection on a low day** *(cheshbon hanefesh at the wrong hour)*. On a low day: no accounting. A coin, a thank-you, one small good act.
- **Escaping upward** *(neglecting the people in front of you)*. The home is made of people. One kind word to someone in your house.

- One small 'no' a day is kept, more days than not, without drama. - Ordinary places (the kitchen, the office) sometimes feel like His home.

- This is not willpower against yourself all day. One small 'no', told to Him. If you are caught in something too strong for reasons, use the short road (the yoke, a deed, a person) and come back to the long road later.

**In daily life.** The house and the day become the practice. People often notice that they are kinder at home.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Imrei Binah: true teshuvah makes good out of the evil itself. The Rashab (Ayin Beis): overpowering weakens the hard thing; sweetening it at its root completes the dwelling below.

> Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 33, line 477: «אשר אפילו בארץ מתחת – אין עוד מלבדו, וזו היא דירתו בתחתונים» — *even on the earth below there is nothing besides Him, and this is His home in the lowest realm*<br>

### Stage 14 · The Deed in the Hand *And begin again · ONE-PROCESS step 14 · sitting L14*

**The goal.** Do the next mitzvah or kindness slowly, with your hand, as the place where the unity becomes real; then start the circle again, one rung deeper.

**What it is like from inside.** Not much during. Afterward: a little quieter, a little less full of yourself. An insult that doesn't sting, 'because he has nothing of his own'. Sometimes the Piaseczner's sentence: 'I find myself another man.'

**The skill.** The servant's simple doing. Clothing every good stirring in a deed at once.

**The practice.** *10 minutes of sitting, and the deed itself, done slowly.*

1. Arrive, quiet. Line: 'The deed in my hand is where He wants to be.'
2. Pick the next mitzvah or kindness you will do today: tefillin, candles, a blessing, a coin, an honest word, a visit.
3. Picture doing it slowly, with the hand, with nothing to prove.
4. Why the deed: His will in it has no reason, so it holds no limit, and it brings Him into this place and this time.
5. Say the Shema. Then go and do it, slowly.
6. Tonight, a short look back: one thing that went well, one to do better, and end in joy. Then tomorrow: back to Stage 2 with your line, one rung deeper.

*The thought to hold.* Samach Vav: the Essence is drawn down precisely through the practical mitzvot, which are outside the category of a vessel. Imrei Binah: the return is the main thing.

*Why it works.* Every stage of this book ends in an act, and this stage explains why. The highest thing, the will with no reason, lives in the lowest power: the hand. 'When you grasp part of the essence, you grasp all of it.' So the climb was for the sake of this.

- **Rote doing** *(the body doing, the mind elsewhere)*. Slow down one deed a day. Just one, slowly.
- **Doing it to be seen** *(serving oneself through the deed)*. Add one hidden good deed for each visible one.
- **Arousal with no deed** *('a flame flying in the air')*. The Piaseczner: when a good thought comes, do a mitzvah or learn with it at once, and so clothe it in holiness.

- Most good stirrings now go into a deed the same day. - You've gone around the circle at least once and found the first lines deeper the second time. - The fruit the Piaseczner asks about: you are a little more your own master than before.

- There is no last stage. Thinking you have finished is a sign you have not begun (the Piaseczner: whoever thinks he never falls never rose).

**In daily life.** The whole day: one mitzvah done slowly, one kindness, one honest word. The practice now lives in the hand.

**The Chassidus underneath.** Tanya 37: in doing a mitzvah a person draws the Infinite Light down into the physical world. Imrei Binah: bowing the body is higher than the inner bitul of mind and heart.

> <small>**Sources.**
> Imrei Binah, the Shema 50, line 283: «והשוב הוא העיקר וכל ההעלא' דרצוא לא הי' רק בשביל הירידה דשוב» — *the return is the main thing; all the rising was only for the sake of coming back down*<br>
> Samach Vav, Ki Ka'asher HaShamayim 5666, line 2005 (OCR): «ולכן המשכת בחי' העצמות הוא במצות מעשיות דוקא, שאינו בגדר כלי» — *so the Essence is drawn down precisely through the practical mitzvot, which are outside the category of a vessel*<br>
> Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim (the Piaseczner), line 270: «שכשבא איזה הרהור טוב באיש יעשה בו מצוה או ילמוד בו, ובזה ילבישו בקדושה» — *when a good thought comes, let him do a mitzvah with it or learn with it, and so clothe it in holiness*</small>

> **Milestone Five: the circle closes.** The plain day is where the practice lives. A good stirring goes straight into a mitzvah or a kindness. You start again at Stage 2 with the same lines, and they go deeper.

**The honest dips.** Modern insight maps warn of a "dark night" that the practice itself produces. This path has no such stage, and this book will not invent one. It has three honest dips instead: going cold when the real work begins (Stage 4 and Stage 7), the up-and-down that is built into every life (all stages), and the chosen, brief bitterness at a set hour that opens into joy (Stage 9). If something darker than these comes, it is not a stage. See Appendix C.

## Appendix C · The stop line

If what you are carrying is real danger, stop the practice. This is not a meditation problem. Real danger means: thoughts of harming yourself or of not wanting to be here; someone hurting or controlling you; fear of hurting someone; not eating or not functioning at all; or racing, sleepless certainty about a special mission. Reach a person now: someone you trust, a doctor, or a local crisis line. The guide will help you find one and will do nothing else until you have.

Also stop, and do not deepen, if the practice leaves you feeling unreal, detached from your body, or hopeless for days. Go back to the plainest door (Stage 2 or Stage 12, three minutes, a tune, a kindness), and let a person who cares for you know what is happening. Lasting heaviness that colours all of life is not the stone heart of Stage 7; it deserves real help alongside the gentlest practice.

## Appendix D · Using this book with the guide

Only One, the guide, walks the same fourteen stages in conversation. The book is the map; the guide is a companion on the road.

- **Tell it where you are.** "I'm at Stage 7 and my heart is like stone." It will offer the remedy for that obstacle from the sources, one move at a time.
- **Or just tell it what is moving.** You don't need to name a stage. The guide starts, like Stage 2, from what is already moving in you, and offers the rung that fits.
- **One round per visit.** The guide takes you through one sitting's shape: meet, turn toward what holds, one true line, try it on something real, stay, from inside, say it to Him, into the day.
- **It keeps your line.** The same line comes back next time, one step deeper, the way the book asks you to stay with one line for days.
- **It will not grade you.** It never praises a sitting, ranks you, or asks how intense it was. It asks about fruit: did the line come back by itself? Did a deed come from it?
- **On a low day it changes the door.** It will take you to the gentlest stage, whatever chapter you are on.
- **For danger, it stops.** It follows its safety rule and helps you reach a person. Nothing else.
- **The forty-day course.** If you want structure, ask the guide for the forty-day course: the lower stages in the first week; detail and the picture in the second; the hinge (Stage 7) in the third; the heights in the fourth; coming down in the last twelve days.

## Appendix E · What is the sources', what is ours

- **The sources'**: the rungs of the unity (being said now; filling and surrounding; the two knowings; before Him, nothing; alone; the yechidah; the world's existence one with Him; the home below; the deed), the moves of the mind (breadth, length, depth), binding, the levels of soul and of bitul and many of their pairings, the named obstacles and their remedies, the warnings about counterfeit arousal, and the rule that the return is the main thing.
- **Ours**: laying all of it on one line of fourteen stages; adding Stage 1 (a set time) as its own chapter, following the modern format and the Piaseczner's "at least set times"; the four parts and five milestones; the minutes; the self-check; the split between stages built by practice (1 to 7) and rungs glimpsed (8 to 12); placing Stages 6 and 9 between soul levels; and putting "the plainest thing" after "the simple will" (the manual has them the other way round).
- **Where the sources differ, and we kept it.** The Mitteler Rebbe keeps the natural soul's arousals and the Godly soul's levels as two ladders; this book lays them on one line, and says so. Sefer HaArachim says the higher bitul is given, not reached by contemplation; Stage 8 says so. The Tanya comforts the dry heart (the deed still counts); Kuntres HaHitpa'alut is sterner about praying dry. Stage 7 gives both: comfort for a dry day or two, a change of door for dry weeks.

*The format is borrowed from modern stage books (see MODERN.md): named stages, one skill each, obstacles with fixes, signs before moving on, honest warnings, a way to know where you are. The content is Chassidus, and the measure is the deed.*
