The Sefaria file "Drashot Maharal" holds four sermons. Lines 1-58 are the introduction and the sermon on the Torah. They are the same text as Derush al HaTorah, with small spelling changes. I read two chunks of them in full and compared the rest by machine against `derush-al-hatorah.txt`. That report covers them, so they are not repeated here. Line 141 is only a note that the Shabbat HaGadol sermon has moved to a new edition. That sermon has its own report (derashat-shabbat-hagadol-1-271.md). So the new text is lines 59-140: the sermon on the mitzvot (commandments) and the sermon for Shabbat Shuva (the Shabbat of return, before Yom Kippur).

Four teachings matter most for the guide:

- **Learning must become deed.** "כי אם אין מעשים אין תורה" (59): "if there are no deeds, there is no Torah." And "one who busies himself with Torah alone is like one who has no G-d" (61).
- **The four letters of the Name are four truths about Him** (129-130). He is not a body, He must exist, He is eternal, and all being comes from Him. Four kinds of people cannot see His face, because each one lives the opposite of one letter.
- **Man is one, so his tongue is one** (125). "If a person had two tongues, the person, who is one, would be two."
- **Cleaving with no barrier cleans.** "As a mikveh purifies the impure, so G-d purifies" (139). Sin has no place with Him, so it falls away from one who cleaves to Him fully.

## 2.1 The four letters: four truths about the One

The Maharal gives four descriptions "that point to His truth and to knowing Him" (129, Shuva 37):

- "התאר הראשון שהוא יתעלה אינו גוף" (129): "the first: He is not a body." - "התאר השני שהוא יתעלה מחויב המציאות" (129): "the second: He must exist" (He cannot not be). - "התאר השלישי שהוא יתעלה נצחי באין התחלה ובאין סוף" (129): "the third: He is eternal, with no start and no end." - "התאר הרביעי שמאתו וממציאותו נמצא הכל" (129): "the fourth: from Him and from His being, all exists."

Then each letter of the Name (Havayah, the four-letter Name) holds one of them (130, Shuva 38):

- **Yud.** "והיו"ד אין אות קטן מאות זה" (130): "no letter is smaller than the yud." It has no length or width to split. So it shows He is not a body, since a body is what can be split.
- **First heh.** In the Talmud "ha" means "here it is". "שמורה מלת הא על הישות ועל המציאות" (130): "the word 'ha' points to being and existence." He must exist.
- **Vav.** It is a straight, even line. "כי השווי והיושר הוא הנצחי" (130): "the even and the straight is what is eternal." What has no edge has no end.
- **Second heh.** "והה"א השניה שהוא נותן המציאות אל הכל" (130): "the second heh: He gives being to all." The first heh is that He is. The second heh is that all things are, from Him.

This is a whole teaching on unity in one line. The Name says that He is one without parts, that He is, that He does not end, and that everything that is comes from His being.

## 2.2 The one tongue

Eyes, ears and hands come in pairs. They match His two kinds of acts, to give good and to hold back (125, Shuva 33). But "רק הלשון היא יחידת באדם" (125): "only the tongue is single in a person." Speech is what a person is: a living thing that speaks. "ואילו היו שתי לשונות באדם היה האדם שהוא אחד שנים" (125): "if a person had two tongues, the person, who is one, would be two." So "החוטא בלשון הרע הוא מקלקל ומתעב את כל האדם" (128, Shuva 36): "one who sins with evil speech spoils and defiles the whole person." The person is one the way his speech is one. Evil speech breaks the whole.

## 2.3 Four who cannot see His face

"Being like G-d in His deeds brings a person nearness and cleaving to Him" (129): "כי ההדמות אל השם יתעלה במעשיו מביא אל האדם הקירוב והדיבוק בו יתעלה שמו." The reason: "מפני כי הדברים המתדמים יחד יש להם חבור ביחד" (129): "things that are alike have joining together." So four kinds of people cannot see the face of the Shechina (His Presence), because each one is the opposite of one letter (130):

| Letter | What it says of Him | Who is its opposite | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yud | Not a body; no parts | The flatterer | "הרי הוא מתחלק לחלקים מחולקים אחד בפנים ואחד בחוץ" (130): "he is split into parts, one inside and one outside." |
| First heh | He must exist | The scoffer | He chases things that have no being. |
| Vav | Eternal, straight | The liar | Truth stands; a lie has no legs. |
| Second heh | Gives being to all | The one who speaks evil | "שמוליד ממנו דברי מיתה והעדר" (130): "he brings out of himself words of death and absence." |

The unity of G-d is here a rule for how to live. Be whole inside and out, deal with what is real, stay straight, and let your words give life.

## 2.4 "He contracted His Presence among us"

In rhymed praise of the calendar, the Maharal says of G-d "whose glory fills the heavens": "צמצם שכינתו ביניים" (68, Mitzvot 10): "He contracted His Presence among us," "להיות אלקים בסוד אנשים" (68): "to be G-d in the council of men." This is the only place in the range with the word tzimtzum (contraction). It is used for His coming close to a human court, not for the making of the world. Of the menorah whose six branches face the middle: "כי יסוד הכל אחד" (69, Mitzvot 11): "for the foundation of all is one."

## 2.5 Three links in the bond

"האדם יש לו דביקות אל השם יתעלה ע"י שלשה דברים" (102, Shuva 10): "a person has cleaving to G-d through three things": G-d Himself, the person, "והשלישי הוא האמצעי" (102): "and the third is the middle," which is the Torah. Three sins cut one link each. Casting off the yoke cuts Him off. Twisting the Torah cuts the middle. Breaking the covenant in the flesh cuts the one who receives. These are the sins that Yom Kippur alone does not clear.

## 2.6 Cleaving with no barrier

Rabbi Akiva said: "מה מקוה מטהר את הטמאים אף הקב"ה מטהר את ישראל" (139, Shuva 47): "as a mikveh purifies the impure, so G-d purifies." The Maharal explains: "כי המקוה מטהר הטמא כאשר יתדבק בו לגמרי מבלי שום חציצה בעולם" (139): "the mikveh purifies when one cleaves to it fully, with no barrier at all." And "מאחר שהוא יתעלה לא שייך אצלו חטא מסלק החטא מן הדבקים בו" (139): "since sin has no place with Him, He removes sin from those who cleave to Him." The cleaving itself is the cleaning: "דבר זה עצמו הוא הסרת והסתלקות החטא" (139): "this itself is the removal of the sin." That is why Yom Kippur clears sins between a person and G-d. It does not clear sins against another person, because those are not a distance from Him.

- **MH-S31 "In the mikveh" (139).** Inside: nothing stands between me and Him, and in that closeness what was stuck to me falls off by itself. I am not scrubbing the sin; I am held where it cannot stay. How to enter: after the reckoning, stop working on the sins. Turn to Him with the whole self, the way one goes fully under water, and let His being clean. It differs from S60 The essential bond, which is a bond that was always there. This state is the act of going under.

## 4.1 Learn in order to do (`mh_learn_to_do`, new)

"הלומד על מנת לעשות מספיקין בידו" (64, Mitzvot 6): "one who learns in order to do is given the means." Learning is greater than deed only because "התלמוד מביא לידי מעשה" (63): "learning brings to the deed," as a parent is greater than the child. Practice: before you learn, name one thing you will do from it today. After you learn, do it. Say the words out loud too: "as long as the Torah has not come out into act from the mouth, the lazy body holds it back" (66).

## 4.2 The ledger (`mh_reckoning`, extended)

"וכל מעשיך בספר נכתבין" (106, Shuva 14): "all your deeds are written in a book." The Maharal reads the unborn child's folded shape as a folded ledger: the person is himself the book. "האדם נברא שיחשוב תמיד על דרכיו" (110, Shuva 18): "man was made to think always about his ways." And "מחשב הפסד מצוה כנגד שכרה" (110): "weigh the loss of a mitzvah against its reward." Practice: hands on the temples, as the child's hands lie. Ask: what did this cost, and what did it bring? Add harm done without meaning to, since those are written too (111).

## 4.3 Answer the blessing (`mh_answer_the_blessing`, new)

When the reader says "bless G-d", the listeners answer "ברוך ה' המבורך לעולם ועד" (104, Shuva 12): "blessed is G-d who is blessed forever and ever." One who hears His Name adds praise. "הבו גודל לאלקינו" (105, Shuva 13): "give greatness to our G-d." We cannot tell the greatness of His own self, but we can as "our G-d". Practice: when you hear a blessing, answer with the whole heart and add your own word of praise.

## 4.4 Four guards for the four letters (`mh_four_letter_guard`, new)

Take the four letters of the Name (129-130) as four checks on the day:

1. Yud: was I one inside and out, or did I flatter?
2. First heh: did I deal with what is real, or scoff at it?
3. Vav: did I stay straight, or lie?
4. Second heh: did my words give life, or did I speak ill of someone?

Because "things that are alike have joining together" (129), each check is also a way to cleave.

## 4.5 Flee honor, be glad in your portion (`mh_glad_in_portion`, new)

"מי שבורח מן הכבוד הכבוד רודף אחריו" (115, Shuva 23): "one who flees honor, honor chases after him." "אין אדם נוגע במוכן לחברו" (116, Shuva 24): "no one touches what is set aside for another." That cures envy. "גדול הנהנה מיגיע כפיו יותר מירא שמים" (118, Shuva 26): "greater is one who enjoys the work of his hands than one who fears Heaven," because he is glad and lacks nothing. Practice: when envy comes, say: what is his was set for him, and what is mine was set for me. Then do your own work with joy.

## 4.6 Go under (`mh_go_under`, new)

For the end of Yom Kippur, or any time of return. After you have named your sins, stop. Picture a mikveh: you do not clean yourself, you go all the way in. Say: "He removes sin from those who cleave to Him" (139). Stay with Him and not with the sins. This is MH-S31.

## 4.7 Incense as binding (`mh_bind_the_bundle`, extended)

"הקטורת שהוא הקישור בו יתעלה" (133, Shuva 41): "the incense, which is the binding to Him." It clears evil speech, which cuts a person off. After careless speech, do not only say sorry. Bind yourself again: say a word of praise for Him and a good word about the person.

## 5. The big human questions

**Is knowing enough?** No. "One who busies himself with Torah alone is like one who has no G-d" (61). Knowing that is not lived is not yet true, because truth is the mind matching the thing (61).

**Why does speech matter so much?** Because a person is a speaking being, and the tongue is one as he is one (125). Evil speech breaks the whole person (128), and it is the opposite of the One who gives being (130).

**How can I be near Him if I cannot see Him?** By being like Him in what you do (129). What is alike is joined.

**What is the way out of envy and the hunger for honor?** Lack nothing: be glad in the work of your hands (118), and know that what is set for another was never yours (116).

**Can my sins be washed away?** Yes, by going fully to Him. Sin has no place with Him, so it cannot stay on one who cleaves to Him with no barrier (139). Sins against another person must first be set right with that person.

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **Tzimtzum.** The one use here (68) is about His Presence coming to rest "among us" in a court of people. It is not the Tanya's or Gate of Unity's tzimtzum, which is about how a finite world can be at all. The Maharal's frame stays "the receiver's side" (Be'er HaGolah 308).
- **The four letters.** Tanya and Gate of Unity (ch. 4 and on) read the four letters as stages in how light flows down: yud is the point of wisdom, heh is its spread, vav draws down, the last heh is speech that makes the worlds. The Maharal reads them as four truths about Him. They meet at the last heh: for both, it is where all being comes from Him (130).
- **The Rambam's attributes.** The four descriptions (129) are the Rambam's: not a body, must exist, eternal, and cause of all. The Maharal gives them as positive marks, but the first is a negation (not a body), and he reads the yud as smallness with no parts. This is close to the Rambam's way of knowing Him by what He is not.
- **The Ramak's formula** ("He is all, and all is not He"): not stated. The two hehs come near. The first heh is that He is. The second is that all is from His being. The Maharal does not say all is Him.
- **Speech is the person.** The Tanya (ch. 4) counts thought, speech and deed as the soul's garments. The Maharal goes further: speech is what a person is, the "living speaker" (125, 128). Both give speech great weight.
- **Cleaving and the mikveh** (139) is close to Tanya ch. 29-31 on the bond that sin cannot fully cut, and to the Chabad teaching that return reaches the essence of the soul, which was never touched. The Maharal says the sin came "from outside"; Chabad says the essence of the soul stays whole. The guide can hold both.
- **Glad in your portion** (118) matches Tanya ch. 26 and 31: joy as the ground of service, and sadness as a lack.
