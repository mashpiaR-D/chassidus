# Pardes Rimmonim, lines 1226-1455: fifty gates, returning light, and the King in weekday clothes

*Range:* lines 1226-1455: Gates 13 (Fifty Gates), 14 (Levels of Being), 15 (From Below Upward), 16 (ABYA, the four worlds)

---
Four gates that turn the Ramak's map toward time and toward the world we live in. Gate 13 counts the fifty gates of Binah (understanding) through the fifty times the Torah says "I brought you out of Egypt", and from there reads time itself: hours, days, weeks, years, each a channel from the one above; Egypt as a furnace for gathering lost sparks; the counting of the Omer as a daily practice. Gate 14 says the spiritual never loses its earlier form when it unfolds. Gate 15 is the Ramak's great teaching on returning light (or chozer): light goes down, strikes a mirror, and comes back up, so the end is fixed in the beginning and the lowest is made equal to the highest. Gate 16 lays out the four worlds, Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, Asiyah, and answers a hard question: on weekdays and in exile, when the Shechinah (the indwelling Presence) is hidden in an angel's garment, has God stopped watching? No.

## 2. The unity of God in this range

**There is no place without Him, not even in the shells.** The strongest line of the range. Why must holiness be clothed even in the kelipot (shells, the forces that hide God)? "For if the shells existed without holiness within them, there would have to be a place of which one could say it is not within the being of God, and this is impossible, for He gives life to them all, and nothing in all the world has being or standing apart from Him" (אין זה במציאות האלהות ח"ו וזה א"א מפני שהוא מחייה את כולם ואין לכל המציאות שבעולם מציאות וקיום זולתו, line 1439). And the way they are finally undone is not by being fought from outside but "by their being brought within holiness" (שבירת הקליפות והשבתתם הוא בהכנסתם בתוך הקדושה, line 1439), as Yitro, a priest of idols, came close and sanctified God.

**He and His life are one, in Atzilut.** The Tikkunim: in the ten sefirot of Atzilut "He and His self are one in them, He and His life are one in them" (איהו וגרמיה חד בהון, איהו וחייוי חד בהון, line 1403); in Beriah it is not so. The Ramak reads it in three steps: "He" is Ein Sof, "His self" is the essence that spreads into the sefirot, and "in them" is the sefirot. And he draws the practical line from it: all our service, prayer, blessing, Torah and mitzvot go "to Him, surely, and not to His attributes, but to the attributes joined to His essence" (אליו ודאי ולא למדותיו אלא בהצטרפות עצמותו, line 1403). In the lower worlds, "called by My name, but not truly My name" (נקרא בשמי אבל אינו שמי ממש, line 1410).

**Separation that is not separation.** "Beriah" means cutting off, being outside. The things of Beriah are "masters of parting and joining, change and limit", so they must be set apart from the root, "though there is no separation, God forbid" (ואע"פ שלא יהיה הבדל ח"ו, line 1396). Atzilut means nearness, from "etzel", beside; and emanation is lighting a candle from a candle: "in Ein Sof there is no lack after emanation and no more before it" (כן בהא"ס אין חסרון בו אחר אצילות ואין יתרון בו קודם האצילות, line 1394). The First Cause "shines in all of them and does not change in any place" (ולא אשתני בכל אתר, line 1449).

**The end is fixed in the beginning.** Gate 15. Light spreads down "like a ray of the sun that goes out from its source and strikes a polished mirror", and then turns and goes back (כדמיון נצוץ השמש המתפשט ממקורו ומכה במראה הלטושה, line 1363). So "Keter rests in Malchut and Malchut in Keter" (כתר שורה במלכות ומלכות בכתר, line 1363), the highest in the lowest and the lowest in the highest, "until it becomes light for light and light for light without end" (line 1363). "Every sefirah is a mirror in which the light above strikes and the light returns" (line 1367). In Hebrew this is ayin (nothing, Keter) and ani (I, Malchut): the same letters, one at the top and one at the bottom (line 1365).

**The lowest is not far.** The Bahir's parable: a king had seven sons and seated them one above the other. "The lowest said: I am below; do not be far from me. He said: I surround you and see you all" (אמר התחתון אני למטה לא אתרחק ממך... הריני מסבב ורואה אתכם, line 1377). "The Shechinah is below just as the Shechinah is above" (line 1377). The Ramak: the lower ones complained "until He had to make them equal through the returning light" (עד שהוצרך מפני זה להשוותם בסוד האור החוזר, line 1380). The returning light exists so that the bottom is not the edge of God's world but a second beginning.

**Unfolding without loss.** When a sheep is sheared, it is left bare; each stage of making cloth erases the last (lines 1330-1331). Not so above: when the spiritual spreads out, "its first being is not undone, God forbid, but rather they multiply" (לא מפני זה יתבטלו מציאותם הראשונים ח"ו אלא אדרבה יתרבו, line 1332). The Mother, Binah, gives birth and stays full: after she bears the sefirot "their fine being remains there as before the birth" (line 1319).

## 3. States of consciousness

**Sought by the Presence (RK-S12, proposed).** The Zohar: "when a tzaddik is in the world, at once the Shechinah does not leave him, and her longing is for him" (כד צדיקא איהו בעלמא מיד שכינתא לא אתעדיעת מיניה ותיאובתא דילה ביה, line 1431). On weekdays "the holy dove wanders from place to place, and if she finds a tzaddik in the world she clings to him" (ואם אשכחת צדיק בעלמא אתדבקת ביה, line 1423). The Ramak: "the Shechinah runs after the doing of mitzvot" (line 1432); such a person becomes "a base and a seat for the Shechinah" (יהיה מכון ובסיס לשכינה, line 1432). Inside: I am not only looking for Him; something of Him is looking for a place to rest, and my small good act is a place. How to enter: do one mitzvah as a place offered, not a coin paid. Guard: he says plainly this is not only for the great; he did not mean "there are no good deeds at all" (line 1432). Near S41 (The hidden love wakes) and S82 (The home below); the map lacks being wanted.

**The weekday watched (RK-S13, proposed).** The hardest question of the gate: on weekdays the "gate is closed" and the world is fed through an angel's hands. Has providence been handed over? The Zohar: "before they rise to rule, He looks always at the world" (עד לא יקומון לשלטאה איהו אסתכל תדיר בעלמא, line 1425). The Ramak's parable: a king feeds his house some days from the inner treasury and some days from the small purse before him, "and always He is the judge, He is the watcher and the feeder" (ולעולם הוא הדיין הוא המשגיח והזן, line 1430). "All the days cleave to the Shabbat and are fed from there" (line 1425). Inside: a plain gray day, no light in it, and still I know I am watched and fed; only the purse is smaller. How to enter: on a dull day, say, "the watching has not changed, only the garment". Near S84 (Trust) and S68 (The hiding seen as His).

**Climbing slowly, by another road (RK-S14, proposed).** The Bahir's students: "the one going down goes down running, and the one going up does not" (שהיורד יורד במרוצה והעולה אינו כן, line 1377), and "the one going up can go up by a different road than he came down" (line 1377). The Ramak: blessing comes down fast and in order, right before left; the rising comes from the left, from judgment, slowly (line 1378). Inside: my climb back is slow and it does not retrace my fall, and that is how it is meant to be. How to enter: stop measuring the way back by the way down. Tied to S77 (The lower return). New: the permission to be slow, and to find a different path up.

**Mirror.** Each person, like each sefirah, is a mirror: light that reaches you can go back up through you, and "the light returning from a dim glass is not like light returning from a bright mirror" (line 1368). Inside: what I receive is not finished until it goes back. This is S58 (A ray in the sun) turned around: not only a ray, a mirror. No new id.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Count and join (RK-T11, proposed).** The Omer. "Omer" is the Shechinah; "sefirah", counting, is from sapir, sapphire: we adorn her. "Each day we repair and adorn one gate", and we must say "today is day such and such, and join it to the days before, so that their light shines all together" (ולצרף אותו בימים הקודמים כדי שיהיה הארתם כלם יחד, line 1307). And the Zohar: one who does not count these weeks "is not called pure" (line 1303). What it is for: turning time into a ladder, each day a gate, none lost. How (the guide's form, ours): once a day, name what this day was for, and name it as the next step after the days before, not as a new start. Ties to `chazarah` and `cheshbon`.

**Step in a little first (RK-T12, proposed).** Why circumcision before counting, and before immersion for a convert? Because "if they had not entered a little into the levels of holiness through the mitzvah of milah, they could never have immersed in the higher waters" (ואם לא היו נכנסין במצות המילה קצת במדרגות הקדושה, line 1305). Purification needs a foothold inside first. What it is for: a person who waits to be clean before coming near will never come. How: take one small step inside (a blessing, a mitzvah, one prayer) before trying to fix yourself; then do the cleaning from inside. Guard: the step is small and real, not a show. Ties to `act`.

**Return each day to its root before sleep.** Time runs in channels: "the hours are channels of light flowing from the days" (השעות הם צנורות של אור נשפעים מהימים, line 1299), days from weeks, up to the jubilee. Branches return to their root, so the Zohar says a person should "confess his sins before lying down" (שיתודה האדם על עונותיו קודם שוכבו, line 1300); Shabbat atones for the week, Rosh Chodesh for the month, the ten days of teshuvah for the year, "so a person can repair the days he bent" (line 1295). And "what is this day is not tomorrow" (מה שהוא היום הזה אינו מחרתו, line 1300): each day has its own light. This is `cheshbon`, given its reason; no new id.

**Pray to Him, not to the attributes.** "To Him, surely, and not to His attributes" (line 1403). The sefirot are how we can name Him at all; the address is always Him. This guards `before_whom` against praying to a map.

**Prayer rises by the measure of the soul.** "The breath of a person's mouth is a power drawn from his soul, and by the measure of his soul is the measure of his prayer" (כפי שעור נשמתו תהיה שיעור תפילתו, line 1429). Weekday prayers that cannot rise "wait until Shabbat" and go up with the Shabbat prayers (הם מתעכבים עד יום השבת, line 1430). Comfort for a weak prayer: it is kept, not lost. Ties to `lips`, `own_words`.

**Giving and receiving take turns.** "While the sefirot give, they do not receive" (line 1373). Hence the old rule not to hold one's hands raised in prayer more than three hours, and Moses' hands that grew heavy. Practice (ours): do not try to pour out and take in at once; know which you are doing.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why exile? Why Egypt?** The Ramak's answer is gathering. After Adam's sin holy sparks were mixed among the nations; God wanted "to purify and to gather the scattered sparks, for His will is that none cast away be cast away from Him" (לטהר ולקבץ ניצוצות הנדחות כי רצונו לבלתי ידח ממנו נדח, line 1302). Egypt was the iron furnace where the silver was refined. This is the pre-Lurianic root of the whole teaching of sparks that Chassidus lives on.

**Falling to the forty-ninth gate.** "Just as there are forty-nine faces of purity, so there are forty-nine faces of impurity" (line 1230). Israel sank to the forty-ninth; only all fifty gates of Binah could bring them out. Nobody is too deep for the fifty gates. And the turn can be sudden: "until now slaves of Pharaoh, from here on slaves of God" (עד השתא עבדי פרעה, מכאן ולהלאה עבדי ה', line 1305).

**Providence on ordinary days.** See RK-S13. God does not withdraw on weekdays; He feeds from a smaller purse.

**The soul, and humility in learning.** No one goes down into the depth of even one secret of the Torah "except by a little of this light coming out between the cracks of the gate, the width of a needle" (אם לא מעט האור הזה היוצא מבין בדקי וסדקי השער כעובי המחט, line 1332). Each world is to the one above it "like a mustard seed in the great sea" (כגרגיר חרדל בתוך הים הגדול, line 1453).

**Nourishing even the source.** The Zohar's child: when the moon rises to her father's house she gives back even to the Mother, like a nursing child whose mouth fills and "who pours the milk back into his mother's mouth" (אתמלי פומיה ואתרבי ביה אריק חלבא לפומא דאימיה, line 1343). The receiver can feed the giver. And inside all the rings of Jerusalem "there is one point: all the glory of the king's daughter is within" (line 1343).

**Joy and rest.** On Shabbat "rest is found for all, above and below" (line 1425), and the King wears "Shabbat and festival clothes, clothes of freedom" (line 1400); on weekdays, clothes of exile. The difference is the garment, not the King.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **"He and His life are one."** Both read the same Tikkunim line. The Ramak (line 1403): "He" = Ein Sof, "His self" = the essence spreading, "in them" = the sefirot as vessels, joined by the essence. The Alter Rebbe (Iggeret HaKodesh 20, Tanya line 1285): "His life" are the lights and "His self" the vessels, "all of them Godliness", and he then argues the vessels are the part of Godliness that can create something from nothing. The Ramak keeps the vessels one step away from Ein Sof; the Alter Rebbe makes them its most surprising expression. This is the same disagreement noted in earlier ranges (Tanya line 1287 on "cause and effect").
- **No place without Him.** Line 1439 is the Ramak's version of "there is no place empty of Him", which the Alter Rebbe calls "the root of the faith for which man was created" (שבשבילה נברא האדם, להאמין דלית אתר פנוי מיניה, Tanya line 1145). The Ramak argues it for the shells; the Tanya adds that He is still "the hidden God" (ונקרא אל מסתתר, Tanya line 588) in them. Same floor.
- **Sparks.** Line 1302 is the gathering of sparks through exile, before the Ari. Chassidus makes it the meaning of every meal and every errand. The Ramak keeps it historical (Egypt, the generations); the guide can keep both.
- **Returning light and the lowest.** Chabad's "the purpose is a dwelling in the lowest world" finds a root here: the returning light makes the bottom equal to the top (line 1380), Keter in Malchut. But the Ramak's world is still a ladder; for Chabad the lowest is not only equal, it is the goal. Ours: the Ramak gives the physics, Chabad the will.
- **Weekday and Shabbat.** The Ramak's "weekday clothes" and the "gate closed on weekdays" become, in Chabad, the difference between birurim (sorting) on weekdays and ascent on Shabbat (S75, S76). Close agreement.
- **Tzimtzum.** When the Ramak says the Shechinah gives on weekdays "only in constriction" (כ"א בצמצום, line 1421), the word means rationing, not the primordial withdrawal of the Ari and the Tanya. Worth saying so the words are not confused.
