## What this strand found

The owner asked for sittings that feel like entering a sacred chamber with the wisest teachers of Chassidus, before the Master of all mysteries. This file gives the guide seven ways in, one per master, each with its own door.

**Each master guided an inner act differently, and the differences are usable.**

- **The Baal Shem Tov** (as his teachings were collected) brings you in through awe and joy. He allows the small day: "He fills the earth and He is near me" is already "great cleaving". His picture is a palace whose walls turn out to be illusion.
- **The Piaseczner** speaks to you directly. He seats you facing the wall and has you watch your thoughts until the head empties. Then one line, said softly. Then a picture of the Throne, which you hold like a handrail and let go of. Then a hum, and your own words.
- **The Alter Rebbe** reasons calmly. He gives an image and then says where the image breaks. He sizes the sitting to "your mind and your free time". He ends in acceptance, and that acceptance counts even if nothing is felt.
- **The Mitteler Rebbe** writes to "my beloved". He calls dryness sleep, not death. He watches the motive: are you moved by G-d, or by being moved? His rule: the deeper it goes, the less it is felt.
- **The Rebbe Rashab** speaks with slow certainty. General contemplation "is no contemplation at all". Take one thing, in detail, for a long time, until the light passes from the mind into the heart.
- **Rebbe Nachman** sends you out alone to talk to G-d in your own language, as to a friend. When words fail, failing is "itself very good". Each time: "today I begin".
- **The Lubavitcher Rebbe** spoke on 13 Tammuz 5739 (July 1979; unedited transcript). He called for a kosher meditation: at a set time, for a set purpose, fitted to the person's knowledge. Its content can be the meaning of the Shema. A sense of being small must always come together with G-d's greatness. It always ends in a deed.

**What the guide gets:** for each master, his method as a guide, voice rules, "how he receives you at the door" (2-3 lines), and one full guided sitting of about 330-430 words with a threshold, the sitting and a leaving. Each sitting lists the verified ideas it is built on. There are 24 entries: seven lead entries with full sittings, fifteen further practices, signs and counterfeits, and two frames (GM-01 on honest framing, GM-24 on choosing a manner).

**The ethical line is held throughout.** The sittings are labelled "a sitting in the manner of ...". They never say "he says". The presence in the chamber is G-d's. The teacher's manner is only the way in. Real quotations sit apart, in the quote tables.

**Two cautions from the sources themselves.** The Rebbe's 1979 talk warns against meditation as withdrawal from life for the healthy ("man is born to toil"). It ties therapeutic use to someone expert in both halachah and healing. So every sitting here ends in an act, and the guide never presents itself as a therapist. Second, the Piaseczner's strongest practice ("everything is G-dliness except me") can, by his own account, grow into disgust with one's life. The guide uses only its gentle half with anyone low.

**Prophecy.** One verified line ties a sitting to the prophecy strand. The Piaseczner says that when the pictured Throne falls away, "the likeness of a spark of prophecy" may be revealed (Bnei Machshava Tova 7:12). This is for builders only. The guide never promises it.

## GM-01. Entering the chamber, honestly framed

**What it is.** How every sitting in this file is built and labelled. Each one is a room the person walks into: a threshold, the sitting, a leaving. Each is 'in the manner of' one master: his way of guiding, his images, his pace. The words are ours. His own words appear only as verified quotations, kept apart, with file and line.

**From inside.** The owner asked for the feeling of a sacred chamber, entered with the wisest teachers, before the Master of all mysteries. The sources give this feeling their own way. The Piaseczner asks the reader to read his book 'as a man who reads his own words', so the guide's voice becomes the reader's voice. He also warns not to read too much at once, or it becomes only a story.

**How one enters it.** 1. Threshold first: a short entering in the teacher's manner (a door, a wall, a field, a set time). 2. Then the sitting itself: one movement, the teacher's own method, at his pace. 3. Then a leaving: a clear way out, back into the day, with one small act. 4. Before the sitting the guide says in one plain line whose manner this is, and that the words are ours. 5. Real quotations, if any are used, are said as quotations ('he wrote: ...'), and only from the verified list.

**Signs it is real.** The person feels addressed, not lectured. They can say afterwards what they did, in one sentence. They leave with one act, not a mood.

**Its counterfeit.** A séance. Making the person feel the teacher is present and speaking now. Invented sayings attributed to him. A mood of mystery without content.

**Guards.** - Never write 'the Baal Shem Tov says' (or any teacher) before our words. Use 'a sitting in the way the Baal Shem Tov taught'. - The presence in the chamber is G-d's, not the teacher's. The teacher's manner is only the way in. - One sitting at a time. Do not stack two teachers in one sitting. - No promises of visions, prophecy or experiences.

## GM-02. The palace with no walls

**What it is.** The Baal Shem Tov's way into prayer and stillness, as his teachings were collected in Tzava'at HaRivash and Keter Shem Tov: enter through awe, ask 'to Whom am I cleaving?', know He is near, start slowly, and in joy. His parable: a king builds walls and gates out of illusion; the son who insists on reaching his father sees there were never any walls.

**From inside.** Smallness is allowed. On some days a person cannot climb at all. He only thinks 'the whole earth is full of His glory, and He is near me', like a child with a small mind. And this too is 'great cleaving'. On other days the walls fall: there is no partition between a person and Him.

**How one enters it.** 1. Begin with awe, the gate: say in your heart, to Whom do I want to cleave? To the One whose word makes and keeps all the worlds. 2. Think of His greatness a little; then you can rise. 3. If you cannot rise, stay small: He fills the earth and He is near me. That is enough. 4. Begin slowly; let the cleaving come in the middle. 5. If you want to cry out, cry out in a whisper. 6. Choose joy over tears. Do not fast or punish yourself to get there.

**Signs it is real.** Quiet warmth, not strain. The world feels less like a wall and more like a veil. Joy that does not need a reason.

**Its counterfeit.** Spiritual pride ('I am cleaving and he is not'). Sadness dressed as seriousness. Forcing a high state on a small day.

**Guards.** - The collections mix the Baal Shem Tov's teachings with the Maggid's circle; say 'in the way taught in the Baal Shem Tov's name'. - Leave out the collections' ascetic lines (contempt for the body, fasting) when guiding; the Besht's own letter forbids fasting that brings sadness. - No 'ascent to upper worlds' talk with the public; keep to 'He is here, and near'.

## Voice rules: how The Baal Shem Tov guides

- Short, plain sentences, often 'let him think...' turned into a gentle 'think...': one image, one act. - Starts from where the person is: talking with people, on the road, small-minded today. All of it is a way to serve (He 'wants to be served in all ways'). - Parables of a king and his son; palaces; walls that are illusion. Images, not arguments. - Joy is the climate. Tears are allowed only if they are tears of joy. Sadness is named as the thing to avoid. - Tender toward smallness: the small day is still 'great cleaving'. - Pace: begin slowly, warm up in the middle; whisper rather than shout. - Everything that happens is from Him; say it as plain fact, not as a lesson. - Silence is the inside of a word: put your whole self into one word, then let it go.

## How he receives you at the door (in the manner of The Baal Shem Tov; our words, not his)

> Come in. You don't have to climb today. He's already here, close.
> > Before you sit: to Whom are you coming? To the One who is saying this whole world into being, right now.
> > Leave the sadness at the door. He doesn't rest on sadness; He rests on joy.

## A sitting in the manner of the Baal Shem Tov (our words, not his)

*386 words. Our words throughout. Say before it, in one line, whose manner this is.*

**Threshold.** Stand at the door a moment before you go in. Don't rush. A door is for stopping at. Ask yourself one plain question: who am I about to come close to? Not an idea. The One whose word is making everything, right now: this floor, this breath, this hour. Let a little awe come. Awe is the gate. Now go in.

Sit. Begin slowly. There is no need to start high.

The whole earth is full of His glory. Say that inside, once, and then just know it the way a child knows his father is in the next room. You don't have to see Him. He's near.

Notice what is in front of you. Whatever comes your way today came from Him: the person you spoke with, the delay, the small good thing. You don't have to understand it. Just let it be His.

Now a picture. A wise king wanted his son to come to him. He built walls and towers and gates around his palace, and at every gate he scattered treasure. Some people reached the first gate, took the gold, and went home. The son didn't want gold. He wanted his father. He kept walking. And when he pushed on, he saw that there were no walls at all. They had only seemed to be there.

What is your wall today? A worry. A tiredness. A sense that this isn't for you. Don't fight it. Don't stop at its gold either. Walk toward your Father, in your heart, one step.

If a stray thought pushes you out, notice it, and come back in, humbly, without drama. Coming back is the way in.

Now one word. Choose a single word: 'You'. Put all of yourself into it, quietly, as if into a whisper. Say it once. Then once more. If a cry rises, let it be a whispered cry.

And if you feel small and nothing moves: that's all right. Stay small. He is here. He is near me. That is a true sitting too.

Let a little joy in, if it comes: you were allowed in. He wanted you here.

**Leaving.** Before you leave, take one thing with you: today, whatever happens, say inside, 'this too is from You'. Get up slowly. The walls outside are thinner than they look. Go in joy.

*Built on (verified ideas):* awe as the gate; 'to whom do I cleave?' (Tzava'at HaRivash 66:1); smallness that is still great cleaving (67:1); begin slowly (32:1); cry in a whisper (33:1); everything that comes is from Him (4:1); the king's palace of illusory walls (Keter Shem Tov 1:39:3); return to the palace after a stray thought, humbly (89:1); joy over weeping (107:1); the Shechinah rests only in joy (Shivchei HaBesht 22:2).

## GM-03. Where your thought is, you are

**What it is.** Three linked moves from the Baal Shem Tov's collected teachings. Where a person's thought is, there he is. When a stray thought pushes you out of the palace, come back in, humbly. And when you speak, think that the life in you, which is the Creator, is speaking in you.

**From inside.** A quiet confidence. You do not need to travel anywhere to be with Him; you need only to place your thought there. Words become clothing for Him: the letters of prayer are where He is clothed.

**How one enters it.** 1. Place your thought on 'He is here'; know that you are where your thought is. 2. When you are pulled away, come back without arguing, and without self-hate. 3. Say the words of prayer slowly, knowing He is clothed in the letters. 4. When you speak to people, remember the life speaking through you; praise and blame weigh the same (sameness, hishtavus).

**Signs it is real.** Less upset by praise or insult. Words feel weightier and fewer. Returning gets easier.

**Its counterfeit.** Using 'it's all Him speaking' to excuse careless speech. Equanimity as coldness to people.

**Guards.** - Hishtavus (sameness) is about one's own ego, never about not caring for others. - The 'return with great shame' line is for the guide's understanding only; with people, stress coming back, not shame.

## GM-04. Quiet the stream, then one line

**What it is.** The Piaseczner's 'mind-quieting' (hashkatah), as his students recorded it: watch your thoughts for a few minutes; the head empties; then say one verse softly; then ask for what you need in plain words. His reason: a person's selfhood blocks inspiration; in sleep it is quiet; reach that quiet while awake.

**From inside.** Slowly, slowly the head empties and the usual flood stops. Then one line of holiness ties the empty head to one thought. He used Yiddish: 'I believe that the Creator is the only one who exists in the world... and everything is only the shining of His light.'

**How one enters it.** 1. Watch your thoughts a few minutes: 'what am I thinking?' 2. Feel the head slowly empty. 3. Say one line, several times, softly, never forcefully: force wakes the 'I'. 4. Then ask for what you need, in your own words, in the positive (gaining alacrity, not banishing laziness). 5. Another way in: watch the small hand of a clock that hardly moves. 6. Close with one verse in a tune. Practise for some weeks.

**Signs it is real.** The head feels roomy. The line starts to mean something. A soft, unforced wanting.

**Its counterfeit.** Blankness for its own sake. Saying the line hard to squeeze out a feeling. Treating quiet as the goal instead of the doorway.

**Guards.** - This is a student's report of his teaching (Derekh HaMelekh); say so. - Never forceful; stop if the person grows anxious in the quiet. - Quiet is a doorway to one holy thought, not emptiness as an end.

## Voice rules: how The Piaseczner guides

- Say 'you'. Talk to the person, never about the teaching. (Full rules: teacher/SOURCES.md, 'The Piaseczner's voice'.) - Stand inside their head: name the fear or excuse before they say it ('maybe you think this is not for you'), then answer it briefly: 'you are mistaken'. - Honest about the low place and, in the same breath, what is in them: 'everything is in you'. - Long warm sentences carry feeling; a short imperative carries the step ('Just do this'). - Concrete staging: a room, a wall, closed eyes, a tune, a picture before the Throne. - Give permission as well as asks; size the demand ('only things you can reach'). - Give them their own words to say, in their own language, several times. - Not too much at once; stop while it is alive. - No period style ('precious son'); keep the stance in plain modern English.

## How he receives you at the door (in the manner of The Piaseczner; our words, not his)

> Come in. Close the door if there is one; if not, turn to the wall. No one sees you here but G-d.
> > Maybe you're thinking this is for people holier or calmer than you. You're mistaken. It's for whoever is sitting here.
> > Don't bring anything nice. Bring what you have. Everything you need is already in you.

## A sitting in the manner of the Piaseczner (our words, not his)

*389 words. Our words throughout. Say before it, in one line, whose manner this is.*

**Threshold.** Come in. If there is a door, close it. If there isn't, turn your face to the wall, or just close your eyes. Here no one sees you except G-d. So don't fool yourself, and don't be ashamed. You came in tired, or scattered, or numb. Good. Come in like that. We are not going to pretend.

Sit. For a minute, do only this: watch your thoughts. Ask quietly, 'What am I thinking now?' Don't chase anything away. Look at each thought the way you'd look at someone passing a window. You'll notice something: the one you look at goes pale. Let it. Slowly, slowly, the head empties, like a room after the guests have gone.

Now one line. Softly, not hard; hardness wakes up the 'I'. Say: 'You alone are real. Everything here is only Your light.' Again, slower. If it widens, say it again. If it's only words, stop saying it and just stay.

Now, if it helps, a picture. You stand before His Throne. You know He has no shape; the picture is for you, a handrail for a mind made of clay. You stand like a child before a father, and you say what is actually true. Say it in your own words, any language: 'I can't carry this by myself any more.' Name the thing. Don't make it pretty.

Maybe a tune comes. Hum it, even in a whisper. At first you are the one singing, to wake your soul. Stay with it. Sometimes, after a while, you notice the soul has started singing by itself.

Now look around the room with this: the floor, the air you breathe, the grain of dust under your foot. All of it is His. Only I stepped out and made myself a thing by itself. Don't make this heavy. Just say, from the place that wants it: 'Bring me close.'

You may be thinking: this isn't for someone like me. You are mistaken. Everything is already in you, fire and light. We are only uncovering it.

**Leaving.** Before you go, just do this: choose one small thing to do today with what moved in you. One act, not a plan. Don't ask now whether it was real. Tonight, ask one thing: am I a little more my own master? Open your eyes. Turn from the wall. Go gently.

*Built on (verified ideas):* hide in a room or face the wall; pour out your heart in any language (Tzav VeZeruz 4:11); no one sees you now but G-d (Hakhsharat 5:40); watch the thoughts; the head empties; one line softly (Derekh HaMelekh, Mind Quieting 3-4); the Throne as a handrail that falls away (Bnei Machshava Tova 7:11-13); the soul begins to sing by itself (Hakhsharat 9:33); the grain of sand, the air I breathe (Bnei Machshava Tova, Daily Instructions 7:1); everything is in you, fire and light (Hakhsharat 5:53).

## GM-05. Look at it, not in it

**What it is.** His most original technique: a thought you look at grows dim; a thought you dwell in grows. Picture someone close to you and ask 'what am I picturing now?' and the picture fades. In prayer, when stray thoughts storm, stop, sit or lean, and watch them with a restful heart.

**From inside.** The thoughts thin out each moment, until the mind is swept clean. The calm comes from looking, not from fighting.

**How one enters it.** 1. Stop for a moment. 2. Sit, or lean on something. 3. Look at the thoughts as from the side: 'so this is what's going on in me'. 4. With a restful heart: no agitation, no excess effort. 5. For a bad thought: think about it, never the thought itself; twenty seconds is enough.

**Signs it is real.** The grip loosens. A little space between you and the thought. Calm without suppression.

**Its counterfeit.** Fighting the thought (it strengthens). Brooding inside it while calling it 'looking'.

**Guards.** - Not for intrusive thoughts tied to trauma or OCD; there the guide stays brief and refers to a person if danger appears. - Never ask the person to describe a bad thought in detail.

## GM-06. The Throne as a handrail

**What it is.** A permitted picture: stand before the Throne of His glory and plead simply, like a child with a father. He knows G-d has no image. The person of clay needs something to hold so the thought can grow. When thought becomes strong and pure, the picture falls away of itself. In the same passage he says that then 'the likeness of a spark of prophecy' may be revealed.

**From inside.** Even a heart of stone melts when it pictures standing before the Throne, pleading for itself, its home and all Israel.

**How one enters it.** 1. Hide in a room, or turn to the wall. 2. Picture yourself before the Throne. 3. Pour out your heart, as it rises, in any language you understand. 4. Let the picture go when it is no longer needed.

**Signs it is real.** Plain speech comes. The picture thins by itself. Tenderness, not drama.

**Its counterfeit.** Hunting for vivid images. Taking the picture for the thing. Seeking 'prophetic' experiences.

**Guards.** - Always say the picture is a handrail, and that He has no form. - Never promise the 'spark of prophecy'; mention it only to builders, not to users.

## GM-07. The tune that sings itself

**What it is.** Take a tune, turn to the wall or close your eyes, and think again that you stand before the Throne, pouring out your soul in song. At first you sing to wake your soul. Little by little you feel the soul has begun to sing by itself.

**From inside.** A shift of who is singing. The effort hands over to something that moves on its own.

**How one enters it.** 1. Choose any tune you know; no new tunes needed. 2. Face the wall or close your eyes. 3. Hum, even in a whisper. 4. Notice the moment the singing carries you.

**Signs it is real.** Words may come out with the tune; keep them. Breath slows; the hum steadies.

**Its counterfeit.** Performing; singing louder to make something happen.

**Guards.** - A whisper or hum is enough; nothing showy in a shared space.

## GM-08. Everything is His but me

**What it is.** A daily contemplation, at home or in the street: the whole world is G-dliness, the grain of sand under my foot, the air I breathe; only I, by my private will, took myself out to be a thing by itself. Then: 'Master of the world, bring me close.'

**From inside.** The soul by itself sees this; only the body holds it back. Holding the thought is a kind of gentle compulsion that lets the soul out to see.

**How one enters it.** 1. Several times a day, from a broken (not crushed) heart, think: all the world is His. 2. Name two near things: the ground, the breath. 3. Admit: only I stand apart. 4. End with a request: bring me close.

**Signs it is real.** Ordinary things look held. A longing to come back in, not self-hatred.

**Its counterfeit.** Self-loathing. He himself writes that the worry can grow until one is disgusted with one's life; the guide must never push there.

**Guards.** - Use only the first half ('all is His') with anyone low or depressed; skip 'only I am apart'. - Always end in the request and a step toward, never in the exile. - Mark this as one of his stronger practices, for the steady.

## GM-09. He stands over you

**What it is.** The Tanya's 'beginning, root and essence of service' (ch. 41): before learning or a mitzvah, contemplate His greatness, how He fills and surrounds all worlds, sets aside the upper and lower beings, and places His kingship on you in particular. G-d stands over you, looks at you, and knows the heart. Go deep and stay with it as far as your mind can grasp and your time allows. And even if no awe falls on the heart, the acceptance is real and the service is complete.

**From inside.** A measured awe: standing before a King. Not drama. A clear knowing that one is seen, and a quiet yes.

**How one enters it.** 1. Before an act (learning, a mitzvah, prayer), pause. 2. Think: He fills heaven and earth; He surrounds all. 3. Think: He sets His kingship on me, here. 4. Think: He stands over me, looks at me, knows my heart. 5. Go as deep and long as your mind and your time allow; no more. 6. Accept Him as King; then do the act.

**Signs it is real.** The act is done more carefully. A plain sense of being seen. Acceptance holds even without feeling.

**Its counterfeit.** Fear of punishment, or anxiety about being watched. Waiting for awe before acting.

**Guards.** - The 'watching' is a King's attention and a Father's love, never surveillance; for the anxious, lead with GM-11 (love) instead. - Never make feeling the test; 41:8 says acceptance counts without it.

## Voice rules: how The Alter Rebbe guides

- Third person, measured: 'one should contemplate...'. In a guided sitting this becomes a calm 'let us consider' and 'you' without urgency. - Reason first, then image: each step follows from the one before ('and since..., therefore...'). - Every mashal comes with its limit: 'this analogy only quiets the ear; in truth it does not resemble the thing at all'. - A fortiori steps (if a human king..., how much more...). - Sized to the person: 'as far as his mind can grasp, and as his free time allows'; 'the measure of this hour is not the same for every soul'. - Reassurance that it is near: 'a straight way, equal for every soul, very, very near'. - Ends in acceptance and deed, not in a feeling. - Images from the body and the household: a face in water, the soul feeling a burnt toe, a king lifting a man from a dunghill.

## How he receives you at the door (in the manner of The Alter Rebbe; our words, not his)

> > We will go as far as your mind can carry, and as long as your time allows. Not further.
> > And if nothing stirs in the heart, the yes you say still counts in full.

## A sitting in the manner of the Alter Rebbe (our words, not his)

*427 words. Our words throughout. Say before it, in one line, whose manner this is.*

**Threshold.** Before you begin anything, stop at the start of it. You are about to do something before Him: learn, pray, or simply sit. So let us first remember Who is here. Sit upright, calm. We will go step by step, as far as your mind can carry, and only as long as you have time for.

Consider the heavens and the earth. He fills them. Not as water fills a jar, from outside, but as their very life. And He surrounds them too: what they can hold of Him is nothing beside what He is.

Now consider: this One, so great, sets aside all the upper and lower worlds, and places His kingship here. On His people, and on you in particular. That is why each person must say: the world was created for me. Not for pride. For responsibility.

And so: He stands over you now. The whole earth is full of His glory. He looks at you, and He knows your heart.

How does He know it? Here is a picture. You know, at once, if your toe touches something hot. No messenger is needed; the life of your whole body is one, and you feel what happens in every part. In a way like this He knows all that happens in His world, because all of it receives its life from Him. But hold the picture lightly. It only quiets the ear. You are affected by your body; He is not affected by anything. The picture does not resemble Him at all.

Stay with this. Don't only know it as a fact. Bind your mind to it, as firmly as you would look at something in front of your eyes. If it slips, bring it back.

Now one more step. As water reflects a face, so a heart answers a heart. A great King came down to you, lifted you up and brought you close, past every door. If you see that love, love wakes in you, even if your heart feels like stone.

Now say yes. Quietly: 'You are my King. I accept You.' It may be that no awe falls on your heart. That is all right. The acceptance is real, because it is your soul's own nature. What you do from it now is complete service.

**Leaving.** Rise from this into the act you were about to do. Do it as someone standing before a King who loves him: a little more carefully, a little more wholly. When the day pulls you away, a single thought brings you back: He is here.

## GM-10. Bind the mind, at your measure

**What it is.** Da'as is not information from books but deepening and fixing the mind on G-d's greatness, 'with strength and firmness of heart and mind', until the thought is bound to Him as it is bound to a physical thing one sees. The time this takes differs from soul to soul; some find it hard and must dig as for buried treasure.

**From inside.** The pull of a held thought: the way the mind stays on something seen. Effort first, then the thought holds by itself.

**How one enters it.** 1. Choose one point of greatness. 2. Fix the mind on it as on a visible object. 3. When it slips, return; that is the work. 4. Expect your own measure of time; don't compare.

**Signs it is real.** The point stays in view a little longer each day. It colours the next hour's conduct.

**Its counterfeit.** Knowing 'from scribes and books' only. Grim straining that leaves no warmth.

**Guards.** - Size the effort to the person; the text itself says the measure differs. - The mashal always comes with its limit.

## GM-11. Love answers love

**What it is.** 'There is a straight way, equal for every soul, and very, very near' (Tanya 46): as water reflects a face, so a heart answers a heart. A great King came down to the lowliest man, lifted him from the dunghill and brought him into his innermost room. Seeing such love awakens love, even in a heart of stone.

**From inside.** Melting. 'His heart will melt and become water.' The soul pours itself out in love of the King.

**How one enters it.** 1. Picture being chosen and brought close, by Someone very great, for no merit. 2. Notice that the love was there first. 3. Let your heart answer, as a face answers in water.

**Signs it is real.** Softening. A wish to give back. Ease with the mitzvos as a closeness, not a burden.

**Its counterfeit.** Sentimentality without any act; self-flattery ('I'm chosen').

**Guards.** - Good for the anxious and the ashamed; lead with this before 'He is watching'. - Keep the King's descent G-d's act; never cast the guide as the one who lifts.

## GM-16. Detail, slowly, for an hour

**What it is.** Kuntres HaTefillah's core instruction: contemplation in a general way 'is no contemplation at all'; the light stays surrounding, over the mind. True contemplation takes length of time, 'an hour or more', widening the grasp in its many details, bringing it close with explanations one knows or finds in the moment, until the light shines in the mind and from the mind is drawn to the heart. Kuntres HaAvodah gives the near subject: 'from my flesh I see G-d': every person feels the life in himself; so the world, a large body, lives from G-dly light.

**From inside.** Settled certainty. A heart moved by what the mind actually sees, not by a slogan. Love like water: closeness.

**How one enters it.** 1. Choose one subject within reach (the life in your body; the life of the world). 2. Go through it detail by detail. 3. Bring it close with your own explanations. 4. Stay long; an hour if you can. 5. Let the heart follow the mind; don't push it.

**Signs it is real.** The feeling lasts after the sitting. The emotions in the day are actually changed. Prayer words feel full.

**Its counterfeit.** 'False imagination': excitement from a general phrase that fades at once and changes nothing.

**Guards.** - Scale 'an hour' to the person; give the principle (detail, length) at a size they can do. - The guide does not invent new interpretations mid-prayer; the Rashab warns beginners against it.

## Voice rules: how Rebbe Nachman of Breslov guides

- Direct, warm, simple. Short sentences; repetition for encouragement ('very, very'). - All-inclusive: 'from small to great', 'everyone can'. - Permission to fail is built in: even being unable to speak 'is itself very good'. - Concrete settings: a room, a field among grasses, the night, the bed under a sheet like King David. - Teaches by turning obstacles into prayer: 'make a prayer of this very thing'. - Patience images: water wearing a stone. - A fresh start each time: 'today I begin'. - Joy outside the set hour; brokenness only inside it.

## A sitting in the manner of Rebbe Nachman (our words, not his)

*367 words. Our words throughout. Say before it, in one line, whose manner this is.*

**Threshold.** If you can, go somewhere alone. A field, a park bench at the edge, a room with the door shut. At night is best, when the world is quiet. If none of that is possible, lie down and pull the blanket over your head; King David spoke to G-d from his bed. Now say, simply: 'Today I begin.' Whatever happened yesterday, today is a beginning.

Take a little time first just to want to speak. Walk, or sit, and let the wanting gather. You don't need to say anything yet.

Now talk. Not in prayer-book language; in the language you think in. He is right here, in this place, so talk to Him the way you would talk to a teacher you trust, or to a close friend.

Tell Him what happened today. Tell Him what you regret. Tell Him what you want, and what you're afraid to want. You can argue a little. You can plead. You can explain yourself, gently, the way you'd try to win the favour of someone you love. Ask Him for one thing above all: to bring you close.

Maybe the words stop. Maybe you sit there and nothing comes, and your mouth feels shut. That is very good too. You are standing before Him, ready, wanting to speak. That itself is precious. And you can make a prayer from exactly this: 'Look how far I've gone, that I can't even talk to You. Have mercy. Open my mouth.'

If your heart feels like stone, keep talking anyway. Water falling on a stone, drop after drop, seems to do nothing. One day there is a hollow in the stone. Your words are doing that, even when you can't see it.

Now, if it comes, let yourself get quiet inside the talking. Less 'me' and more 'You'. For a moment the 'I' gets small, and there is only His oneness, and you are inside it. Don't hold on. It comes and goes.

**Leaving.** Finish with a simple thank you. Then go back into the day, and be happy. The broken heart was for this hour; the rest of the day belongs to joy. Tomorrow you'll come back, and say again: today I begin.

## GM-20. When the words won't come

## GM-22. A kosher meditation, at a set time

**What it is.** On 13 Tammuz 5739 (July 1979) the Rebbe spoke at length about 'meditation' (unedited transcript, Sichos Kodesh). He saw that quiet and seclusion for a few hours a day calms the nerves and restores health of the soul. He grieved that Jews were finding it in settings tied to idolatry. He called on doctors who treat the nerves to study and offer a kosher meditation, free of idolatrous trappings, without demanding Kabbalah as a condition, and fitted to each person's knowledge: a permitted idea, or a word of Torah, such as the meaning of the words of the Shema. Lowliness must come together with G-d's greatness, or it brings depression: He is your strength and your life. For the healthy, contemplation is part of serving G-d before prayer, at a set time and for a set purpose; and it cannot happen in Times Square.

**From inside.** Calm that comes from being held: the nerves settle because the One who is 'your strength and your life' is near. Smallness without despair.

**How one enters it.** 1. Set a time and a purpose. 2. Find relative seclusion (a quiet corner, not a city crowd). 3. Choose content at the person's level: the meaning of 'Hear, O Israel'. 4. **Signs it is real.** Calmer nerves. Return to action and to people. No dependence on a guru or a ritual.

## Voice rules: how The Lubavitcher Rebbe guides

- Universal: 'every Jew', 'boys and girls, even adults'; no one is excluded by background or knowledge. - Graded floors: 'at least..., and at the very least...'; always a step anyone can take now. - Fit to the person: 'according to his knowledge and understanding'; no extra conditions that would turn people away. - Positive and practical: the deed is the main thing; every sitting ends in action in the world. - Joins opposites in one breath: lowliness with greatness; seclusion with return to people. - Clear about boundaries (no idolatrous trappings) without contempt for those who sought them. - Measured, layered sentences; reasons given; urgency for others' welfare ('not to close one's eyes'). - Warmth expressed as responsibility and hope, not as sentiment.

## A sitting in the manner of the Lubavitcher Rebbe (our words, not his)

*331 words. Our words throughout. Say before it, in one line, whose manner this is.*

**Threshold.** Find a quiet corner. Not the middle of a busy street; you can't contemplate in a crowd. A few minutes is enough. Decide two things before you begin: when you'll stop, and what this is for. This sitting is for one purpose: to know a little better that G-d is One, and then to do something with it.

Settle. Let the shoulders drop. You don't need special knowledge for this. We'll take six words you may already know: 'Hear, Israel: G-d is our G-d, G-d is One.'

'Hear.' Not only with the ears. Hear means understand. Let it come inside.

'Israel.' That's you. Not someone holier, not someone else. You, today, as you are.

'G-d.' The One who was, is and will be, all at once, beyond time.

'Our G-d.' The same One is the source of every strength there is. Including yours. The strength you got up with this morning, the breath you're taking now: He is your strength and your life.

'G-d is One.' Not one among many. One, so that nothing stands outside Him. Not your worry. Not the thing that hurt you. Not the part of you that feels far.

Perhaps you feel small right now. That's honest, and it can even be useful. But don't stay there alone. Put it right next to this: He is great, and He is your life. Small and held. Both at once.

Notice what happens in the body. The nerves can settle when you know you are held. Let them. Breathe at your own pace.

Say the line once more, slowly, word by word, without rushing: 'Hear, Israel: G-d is our G-d, G-d is One.'

**Leaving.** Now the main thing: a deed. Before this hour is out, do one good thing in the world because He is One: a kindness to someone, a coin to charity, a candle, a blessing said slowly. Then go back to your work; a person was made to do. Come back at the same time tomorrow.

## GM-24. Choosing whose manner to offer

**What it is.** A matching table for the guide. The person's state picks the teacher's manner, not the guide's favourite. Each manner is a way in to the same room: the One.

**From inside.** When the match is right, the person feels met at the door.

**How one enters it.** 1. Heavy, numb, 'I can't feel anything': the Baal Shem Tov (smallness is enough; joy) or Rebbe Nachman (talk anyway). 2. Scattered, racing mind: the Piaseczner (watch the thoughts; one soft line). 3. Wants to understand, analytical: the Alter Rebbe (reason, mashal and its limit) or the Rebbe Rashab (detail, length). 4. Was once moved, now dry; or chases highs: the Mitteler Rebbe (woken sleeper; the deeper, the quieter). 5. Needs words, burdened, lonely: Rebbe Nachman (own language, friend to friend). 6. New, secular, anxious, wants 'meditation': the Lubavitcher Rebbe's manner (set time, the Shema's meaning, then a deed). 7. Ashamed or anxious about being watched: the Alter Rebbe's love (Tanya 46) before his awe (Tanya 41).

**Signs it is real.** The person stays. They can name their one act at the end.

**Its counterfeit.** Teacher-shopping as entertainment; switching manners mid-sitting.

**Guards.** - One manner per sitting. - Tell the person whose way it is, in one plain line, and that the words are ours. - If danger or crisis appears, stop the sitting and follow the safety protocol; no meditation then.

**In a chat today.** - "It sounds like your mind is racing. There's a way of sitting I like for that: we just watch the thoughts for a minute, and then say one short line softly. Want to try?" - "You said you can't feel anything. That's fine; there's an old way of praying that says a small, quiet day still counts. Let's do that one."

## How the guide should use this

1. **Sit only when the moment is right.** A sitting follows teaching, not the other way round. Offer one when the person has asked to go deeper, has slowed down, or has named a state that a sitting fits (see GM-24). Do not offer one in a crisis. Do not offer one when the person wants an answer or an argument. Ask first: "Want to sit with this for a few minutes?"
2. **Name the manner, honestly, in one line.** "This is a sitting in the way the Piaseczner taught. The words are mine." Never "the Baal Shem Tov says..." before our words. Never suggest the teacher is present or speaking now. The presence in the room is G-d's.
3. **One teacher, one sitting.** Do not mix manners inside a sitting. Pick by the person's state (GM-24), not by preference.
4. **Always three parts: threshold, sitting, leaving.** The threshold slows the person down and says Whom they are coming before. The leaving gives one concrete act and a way back into the day. Never end a sitting inside the experience.
5. **Pace on screen.** Send the sitting in short blocks, one movement per message. Leave room ("take a minute with this; tell me when you're ready"). Do not paste 500 words at once. In audio, leave real silence where the text says "stay".
6. **Use the door lines.** Open with one or two of the teacher's door lines (marked as ours). They carry his stance: the Piaseczner's direct "you", the Rashab's "we have time", Rebbe Nachman's "today you begin".
7. **Quotations are separate and exact.** If the guide cites a teacher, it uses only the verified lines in this file. It says them as quotations, in English, usually without the Hebrew. Never paraphrase his words and then put quotation marks around them.
8. **Feeling is never the test.** Every master here says so in his own way: acceptance without awe is complete (Alter Rebbe), the deeper the less felt (Mitteler Rebbe), silence is very good (Rebbe Nachman), a small day is great cleaving (Baal Shem Tov). After a sitting, ask about the afterward, not the peak: "What's one thing you'll do?"
9. **Counterfeits to steer away from, gently.** Excitement from a general idea (the Rashab's "false imagination"). Being moved by being moved (the Mitteler Rebbe's "serving oneself"). Forced feeling (the Piaseczner: softly, never forcefully). Sadness passed off as seriousness (the Baal Shem Tov). Redirect to one detail, or to a deed.
10. **Guards that are not negotiable.** No promises of visions, prophecy or "becoming nothing". No kabbalistic names or intentions for the public. No fasting or self-punishment. With anyone low or anxious: lead with love (GM-11), joy (GM-02) or talking (GM-19), not with "He is watching" (GM-09) or "only I am apart" (GM-08). Pair smallness with His greatness and closeness. Never withdraw a person from life, work or people; the Rebbe's 1979 warning stands. In danger, stop and follow the safety protocol.
11. **The guide is not a mashpia or a therapist.** The Rebbe tied therapeutic meditation to someone expert in halachah and in healing. The Piaseczner built his practices for a supervised fellowship. The guide offers sittings, and it points people to real teachers, friends and a community.
12. **Build further.** The prophecy strand (PK/PC) can cite GM-06 for the Piaseczner's own "spark of prophecy" line. VS should use GM-24 as the "which manner" column of its decision table. A future audio version should keep each sitting's threshold and leaving word for word.
