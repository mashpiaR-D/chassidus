The Iggeret HaKodesh is not a book the Alter Rebbe sat down to write. It is a bundle of real letters, sent over many years to his Chassidim in their towns: letters of thanks, letters of warning, letters asking for money for the poor and for the Holy Land, one written just after he came home from prison in Petersburg. So they sound different from the chapters before them. They are shorter, warmer, more urgent, and they almost always end in the hand: pray longer, answer softly, give. But inside each practical request sits the same unity the whole Tanya teaches. He is found below exactly as above. Every world is "poor" before Him and has nothing of its own. A coin given in love reaches His very Essence. The first sixteen letters, retold here, keep coming back to one deed, charity, and each time they open it from a new side: armor, a seed, a spoken word, a freed prisoner, wine settling on its lees. Each section retells the letter in plain words, names the unity in it, and turns it into a short sitting. Where a letter speaks specifically of Jews or Israel, a one-line note says so, and the adaptation applies it to every person, as the owner has ruled.

## Letter 1 · The loins that hold up the head

It opens with good news. Across many towns, groups of the Rebbe's people have finished learning the whole Talmud. "My soul came alive," he writes. He thanks G-d for what is done and asks for more next year.

Then he gives a picture from a verse about the woman of valor: "She girds her loins with strength." The loins, the hips and lower back, are what hold the body up. The head rests on them. They carry a person where he wants to go.

The soul has loins too. They are faith. Real faith in the One, the Infinite, who fills every world and surrounds every world, with no place empty of Him, above without end and below without end, in every direction, in every moment and in every soul.

That faith holds up the head. The head is the mind that thinks deeply about how great He is, and how kind He has been to us, to make us a people close to Him, able to cling to Him truly. The sages said: "One hour of return and good deeds in this world is better than all the life of the World to Come." Why? The World to Come is only a glow, a radiance of His presence. But return and good deeds bring a person near to His very Being and Essence, as it were, to the Infinite itself.

From that thinking come the arms: love and awe. Sometimes a fiery rush toward Him. Sometimes a stepping back in awe, like the people at Sinai who "saw and trembled and stood far off."

So who gives the loins their strength? Learning halacha, the practical laws of the Oral Torah. The law is His revealed will, and His will is higher than even His wisdom. "There is no strength but Torah."

And when is the time for strengthening the arms and the head? Morning prayer, the hour of mercy. So he repeats what he asked last year: pray from the depths of the heart, pour your soul out like water before G-d.

Then the letter gets practical, and stern. On weekdays, busy working people should not lead the prayers. The leader should be someone with time, a teacher or a young man still supported at his father's table, who can pray for at least an hour and a half. On Shabbat and holidays, when everyone has time, the working people should pray long too, and take turns leading. He plans to send quiet observers to every prayer group. Whoever has the time to pray at length and is lazy about it will be kept at a distance when he comes to hear the Rebbe teach. "And to those who listen, it will be sweet."

*The Tanya speaks here of Israel and its particular nearness; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

The One who fills and surrounds everything is not only thought about from far away. A plain good deed reaches Him Himself, further than any heaven. Faith is the ground the mind stands on, and the deed is where the Infinite is actually touched.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Whatever is holding you up today, notice it. You didn't build most of it. - **The line, softly:** "A good deed brings me near to Him Himself." - **Think it through.** The loins hold up the head without thinking. Faith holds up your thinking the same way: no place is empty of Him, above, below, on every side, now. Then think of paradise, all of it. It is a glow. One kind act today reaches past it, to Him. - **Where the picture breaks.** Loins tire and give out. This faith isn't a muscle you made. It's the soul's own ground. - **From inside.** Firm footing under your thoughts. The mind stands on something it didn't put there. - **The edge.** "His very Being and Essence." The letter says a deed reaches there. You can't picture it. Just say it again. - **Into the day.** Learn one practical law of Jewish life today, and do one good deed. Anyone can do the deed, a kindness, a coin, a true word to G-d in your own words. Know that it reaches further than heaven.

## Letter 2 · The nearer, the smaller

This letter was written after the Rebbe came home from Petersburg, freed from prison. His followers were overjoyed. He writes to tell them how to hold that joy.

He starts with Jacob's words after a lifetime of blessing: "I am too small for all the kindnesses." Every kindness G-d does for a person should make him lower in spirit, not higher. Why? Kindness is G-d's right hand, His embrace: "His right hand holds me." It brings a person closer to Him than before. And the closer you are, the lower you must be. "From far away G-d appeared to me." Everything before Him counts as nothing. So whoever is more "before Him" is more nothing, more naught, more zero.

This is the holy right side, the kindness of Abraham, who said "I am dust and ashes." It is also Jacob's way. Jacob was afraid of Esau even after G-d promised, "I am with you." Why wasn't the promise enough? Because all those kindnesses made him very, very small in his own eyes. He felt he didn't deserve to be saved. Maybe some sin had spoiled it.

The opposite is the kindness of the "other side," which the letter pictures as Ishmael. For that kind of person, the bigger the gift, the bigger he gets. His heart swells and his spirit grows proud.

So the Rebbe gives a firm instruction to all his people, about the great kindness G-d has just shown them. Hold on to Jacob's way. Make yourself like the leftovers, the part no one needs. Don't let your heart rise over your brothers, the ones who opposed us. Don't open your mouth against them. Don't mock them, not even a hiss. He calls that a terrible warning. Instead, lower your spirit before every person, in Jacob's trait of truth. "A soft answer turns away anger." And maybe, if you do this, G-d will put the same softness in their hearts, "as water reflects a face to a face."

*The Tanya speaks here of Jacob's people and the "remnant of Israel"; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

Before Him, all is nothing. So nearness to Him doesn't show up as feeling bigger. It shows up as being less full of yourself. The humility is not a pose. It's what closeness feels like from inside.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Think of something good that happened to you lately. Let it be there, good as it is. - **The line, softly:** "The nearer to Him, the more I am nothing." - **Think it through.** A kindness is His arm around you. Being held by Him means standing where everything counts as nothing. So the gift brings you closer, and closer means smaller. Jacob, blessed and still afraid. Abraham, a friend of G-d, saying "dust and ashes." - **Where the picture breaks.** A person hugged by a king might puff up with pride. This embrace works the other way: the stronger it is, the less of you there is. - **From inside.** A thankfulness that makes you quieter, not louder. Less need to be right. - **The edge.** "Before Him," where all is nothing. The mind can't stand there. It only tastes it, as smallness. - **Into the day.** After something good happens today, speak more softly to someone who didn't get it, or to someone who opposed you.

## Letter 3 · Armor made of coins

"He put on charity like armor, and a helmet of salvation on his head." The sages explained: in a coat of armor, each small scale joins the others to make one great armor. So with charity: each small coin joins the others into one great sum.

Think about how armor works. It is made of scales laid over holes. The scales cover the holes so no arrow gets in. Charity works like that.

Here is why. Every mitzvah makes a garment for the soul. The garment is made of the light that surrounds all worlds, the Infinite light that doesn't fit inside anything. It comes down when a person does what G-d asked. That surrounding light wraps around the ten sefirot, the ten powers through which He makes and runs the worlds, and it joins with the inner light that fills them. A small glow of it, made very small, becomes the garment a soul wears in the Garden of Eden, so it can enjoy some sense of Him there.

But the sages also said: "There is no reward for a mitzvah in this world." This coarse, limited world can only receive through the ten sefirot, which the Zohar calls "the body." And even those ten, next to the Infinite, are like a body next to a soul. Even the highest wisdom is like plain physical action compared to Him.

Charity is different. The sages said a person "eats its fruits in this world." So charity makes holes, so to speak, in that high garment. Through the holes, blessing flows down into this physical world: long life from the right arm, wealth and honor from the left, and beauty, glory, joy.

But the flow must not feed the wrong places on its way down. And the person below must be guarded from every harm, in body and in spirit. So the surrounding light shines again and seals the hole. It can, because it is Infinite. That's why the sages said the coins join into a "great" sum: "For G-d is great," without end.

The chapter then traces this through the Kabbalah of the sefirot and the Zohar's "370 lights"; in short: "helmet of salvation" means His face turning toward us and shining, and "with us" happens through the deed of charity. "One who sows charity grows salvation."

The light that fills from inside and the light that surrounds from every side meet in one small coin. You are held both ways at once: blessing comes through, and the same Infinite closes around you.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Notice how small your giving feels. A few coins. Let that be there. - **The line, softly:** "Each coin joins a great sum." - **Think it through.** Armor: many small scales, one coat. Each coin you give is one scale. It opens a way for blessing into this world, and the surrounding light closes behind it so nothing is lost. - **Where the picture breaks.** Armor is iron, made by a smith. This armor is His own light around you, and the "great sum" has no end. - **From inside.** Held, not holding. Lightness about small amounts, because they add up where you can't see. - **The edge.** The light that seals the hole is Infinite. The letter names it and uses it. You don't have to understand it to be inside it. - **Into the day.** Give one small coin today, to a charity box or a person in need, and think: one scale.

## Letter 4 · The point in the depth of the heart

"Israel will be redeemed only through charity." And a verse says charity "will walk before him." The Rebbe asks: why "walk before him"? Why not just "go"?

He answers with another verse: "My heart says to You: seek My face." Seek the inside of the heart.

The heart has an outside and an inside. The outside is the fire that comes from thinking. You think about how great G-d is, and a strong love flares up, like sparks of fire.

The inside is a point, deep in the heart, higher than thinking. "From the depths I called You": from the depth of the heart. The letter gives a picture from ordinary life. Sometimes something so huge happens that your whole life hangs on it. It touches that inmost point. And then a person may do things and say things without thinking at all.

Serving G-d from that point is the same. That point is above understanding. A light of the highest wisdom rests there, and G-d's own light is hidden in it. It is the spark of G-d in every soul.

Then why doesn't everyone pray from there? Because that point is in exile, a captive. It has been dressed in the dirty clothes of this world's wants. The letter calls this the foreskin of the heart, a covering over the inner point. And there are two layers: a thick covering and a thin one. The thick one, the coarse desires, you are told to remove yourself: "Circumcise the foreskin of your heart." The thin one is too hard for a person. About that one it says G-d will do it, in the days of Moshiach: "G-d will circumcise your heart, to love G-d with all your heart and soul, for the sake of your life." Meaning: because G-d alone is your whole life, truly.

That's why Moshiach comes "when the mind isn't looking." It's the inner point of the whole people coming out of exile. And each person's own spark comes out for a while in prayer, the service of the heart. That, too, comes when the mind isn't looking. It's a gift from above, from His shining face.

But a gift from above answers a move from below. So you begin the circumcision yourself. How? By giving charity from your money, which is your life. Especially if you barely have enough. Especially if you earned it with your own hands, because you surely worked for it with that very point of your heart. Now, when you give it gladly, you ransom that point from prison. You pay off a debt you owed to the outside forces. "Charity walks before him": it leads the inside of the heart to G-d. Then every mitzvah and all your learning rise through that inside, far higher than through the outside alone. That's why charity is called "peace": it makes peace between people and their Father in heaven. The letter adds that charity for the Land of Israel is G-d's own charity, and it thanks that charity for saving them from those who tried to push them down.

*The Tanya speaks here of the spark in every Jewish soul and of Israel's redemption; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

At the bottom of the heart, under every reason, is a point where He is your whole life, truly. You don't make that love by thinking. You uncover it, and He finishes uncovering it.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Notice what you want most today. Don't fight it. That wanting is the inner point, dressed in the wrong clothes. - **The line, softly:** "G-d alone is my whole life, truly." - **Think it through.** The heart's outside: the warmth that comes after thinking. The heart's inside: the point that acts before thinking, the way a person runs into a fire for his child. That point is His spark, held captive by wants. Money earned is life spent. Giving it frees the point. - **Where the picture breaks.** A prisoner is ransomed once and walks free. This point slips back into its clothes. That's why you give again, and why the thin layer is left for Him. - **From inside.** A love that wasn't argued into being. It was just there, under the argument. - **The edge.** It comes "when the mind isn't looking," a gift. You can't reach for it. You can only clear a little space. - **Into the day.** Give something that costs you, from money you worked for, and say: "For the sake of my life, which is You."

## Letter 5 · Who makes the holy Name

"David made a name." The Zohar says Rabbi Shimon wept and said: "Who makes the holy Name every day? One who gives charity to the poor."

To see why, the letter starts with a saying of the sages. G-d's name has letters. With the small letter yud He made the World to Come. With the letter hei He made this world.

The World to Come is a world of understanding. Souls there delight in grasping something of His light. That grasping flows from wisdom, the first flash of the mind before it opens into understanding. That hidden point is the yud.

Now the hei. Think about how you speak. When you say a letter with your lips, you don't plan how your lips move. You want to say "b" or "m," and your lips just move that way. When you want to say an "ah" sound, your mouth opens by itself. Speech comes from a hidden mind, higher than understanding. That's why a baby can understand everything and still can't talk.

A letter has two sides. Its form, the particular shape of the sound, comes from that hidden mind and will. Its body is plain breath, coming up from the heart before it splits into sounds. Breath, the sound "h," is the letter hei. It is the stuff speech is made of.

There are countless worlds and angels and souls, all made from endless combinations of G-d's letters, and they all know their Creator, because they live from the inside of the letters. But this lowly world is too small to take that inner light openly. It gets only the breath, the outside. Inside the breath, hidden, are the forms: His traits, His will, His wisdom, fully one with the Infinite. The letter adds a line of Kabbalah about how the outer vessels of the lowest sefirah became the soul of this world.

Here is a picture. A great sage squeezes his whole wonderful mind into one letter of speech. That's a huge squeezing down. The making of this world was like that, and infinitely more.

At creation, no one below asked for it. "There was no person to work the ground." It was pure kindness: "the world is built on kindness." But once people were made, kindness from above answers kindness from below. That's why the sages said: one who says he has only Torah, without acts of kindness, doesn't even have Torah. Torah draws wisdom down into the letters of the world. But to bring it all the way down, like water running from high to low, you need kindness: giving life to the broken and the low. "Let the wise man not boast of his wisdom... but I am G-d who does kindness." Without kindness, wisdom stays "his wisdom," alone, and gives no life.

So there are two kinds of souls: scholars who learn all day, and people of deeds who spend most of their days in charity and kindness. The scholars' learning reaches the higher worlds. The givers bring the light all the way down into this one. They are "the supporters of Torah." That's why charity is called a "deed," and why the Zohar says "makes" the holy Name. By giving, you draw the yud into the hei, wisdom into speech, for this world.

One last picture, from far below. A person doesn't really speak to himself. He speaks to others. And then he squeezes his mind into words for them. "The wise will understand."

*The Tanya speaks here of the charity Israel does and of two kinds of souls in Israel; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

This whole ordinary world is His breath, with His wisdom hidden inside it. When you give, wisdom comes all the way down into the breath. You are helping Him say Himself here.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Say one word out loud, any word. Notice you didn't plan how your lips moved. - **The line, softly:** "Who makes the holy Name? One who gives." - **Think it through.** Speech comes from a mind deeper than your thinking. The world is His breath, and inside the breath is His wisdom. A great sage squeezing his mind into one letter. Then your hand giving bread to someone. That's where the wisdom reaches the ground. - **Where the picture breaks.** Your words leave you and fade. His breath never leaves Him, and the forms inside it are "one with the Infinite, completely." - **From inside.** Your giving feels like speech. Something in you is being said. - **The edge.** The forms hidden in the breath are fully one with Him. The mind stops there. - **Into the day.** Give to someone today and think: "I am making the Name."

## Letter 6 · Equally here, and the truth that is given

"One who sows charity gets a true reward." The reward is truth itself. And the prophet says to G-d: "You give truth to Jacob." But why does G-d need to give it? Didn't Jacob have truth already?

Jacob's trait is mercy. To serve G-d with mercy means this: you feel great pity for the spark of G-d in your own soul, which is far from His light while it walks in the darkness of this world's empty things. And that pity comes from thinking about how great G-d is. Even the highest worlds, endlessly high, count as nothing before Him. All their life is only a glow from one letter of His name.

Inside that glow there are differences. High worlds and low worlds. This world made with one letter, the next world with another. Every detail in every world comes from a different combination of letters. So do the changes of time, past, present and future, and everything that happens as time goes on.

But He Himself doesn't change. "I am G-d, I have not changed." Not from high to low: as He is found in the higher worlds, so He is, truly and equally, in the lower ones. And not over time: as He was alone before the six days of creation, so He is now. Why? Because everything is as nothing to Him. Like one letter you say, or even think, next to your whole soul. The letter adds right away that this picture is only "to soothe the ear." In truth, "nothing compares to You." That's what we mean when we say in prayer: "the King who alone was exalted from then." As He was alone before creation, so He is now, above all the days of the world.

So the pity is very great for the spark that lives in the dark body, "the serpent's skin," which could easily be pulled into every craving, if G-d didn't shield it and give it strength to fight and win.

There are two kinds of love and awe. The first kind is born from your own thinking about His greatness. The second kind comes afterward, from above, as a gift. There is no comparison between them. The first is made by a created mind. The second comes from the Creator. That's why only the second is called "truth." "G-d's seal is truth," the real truth, and all the truth of created things is nothing next to it.

How does a person come to G-d's truth? By waking great mercy before G-d for the spark in his soul. That's Jacob's trait, which runs from top to bottom, bringing G-d's truth down into this dark, low world. "Though I sit in darkness, G-d is my light."

But here is a problem. The mercy you wake has to be true. And even if it's true, it's your truth. How can your truth call down His truth?

The answer is charity. Charity is mercy for someone who has nothing of his own. And a move from below wakes a move from above. He "wakes the sleeping": His hidden mercy comes out into the open. That's why charity is called sowing. You plant it and truth sprouts. The letter ends with charity for the Holy Land: "Truth will sprout from the earth."

He is the same everywhere and always, as present in your darkest hour as in the highest heaven. Knowing that doesn't make you cold. It makes you tender toward your own soul, lost in the dark, and that tenderness, turned into giving, brings down a truth you could never make.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Find the low place in your day, the part that feels far from anything holy. Let it be there. - **The line, softly:** "As He is above, so He is here, equally." - **Think it through.** Worlds differ. Times differ. He doesn't. Next to Him, all of it is like one letter next to your whole soul. Then look at your own soul, walking in the dark. Feel sorry for it, the way you'd feel for a child lost at night. - **Where the picture breaks.** One letter next to the soul is still something. The letter itself says: "nothing compares to You." - **From inside.** The low place isn't a place of less. And tenderness for your own soul, not blame. - **The edge.** "G-d's truth" is given, not produced. Your truth can't reach it. So give, and let it sprout. - **Into the day.** In a low, dark hour today, say: "He is here equally." Then give something to someone who has nothing of their own.

## Letter 7 · Your portion and your lot

"Happy are we, how good is our portion, how pleasant our lot." "G-d is my portion and my cup." To understand "portion" and "lot," the letter looks at a common phrase: "he has no portion in the G-d of Israel." But G-d has no parts. How can anyone have a "portion" in Him?

Here's the answer. G-d fills all worlds, from the highest to under this physical earth. "Do I not fill heaven and earth?" says G-d. "I," truly: His very Being and Essence, as it were, not just His glory. And yet He is holy and apart. He isn't caught inside the worlds the way your soul is caught inside your body.

So creatures can't take their life from His Essence directly. What gives them life is like a glow shining from His Name. He and His Name are one. "His Name alone is exalted"; only His glow is on earth and heaven. That glow does dress itself in every creature to give it life, through many steps and many narrowings.

Up high, the glow shines without limit, giving life to endless hidden worlds. But as it comes down to give life to creatures, it splits into 613 kinds, matching the 613 commandments of the Torah. And those match the soul of a person, which has 248 "limbs" and 365 "sinews." The whole purpose of the glow coming down to all creatures is the person.

That's the general count. In detail, each mitzvah splits into countless details, the endless laws of each commandment. The same with souls. All souls were once included in Adam's soul, which split into 613 in general, and into countless sparks in detail: every soul from the forefathers until Moshiach. Jacob's beauty was like Adam's. He fixed Adam's sin, and his soul too held all the souls. He was a chariot for the Torah above, which is called "man."

That's why G-d called Jacob "E-l, G-d of Israel." "E-l" means the light of the Infinite coming out of hiding into the open, shining in his soul. And after him, it shines in everyone who is upright in heart and busy with Torah and mitzvot. Most of all it shines in their minds and hearts at the time of prayer.

In general, this light is the same for everyone. One Torah for all of us. But in detail, souls are not the same. It depends on the time they came into the world. The sages asked a man: "In what was your father most careful?" He said: "In tzitzit." Generations differ too. Like the limbs of a body, the eye to see and the ear to hear, each mitzvah shines a particular light of its own.

Every soul must, over its lifetimes, keep all 613. But it needs only one to hold with extra care, doubled and redoubled, above the rest. That's the word "most": "most careful."

And why that one? Not for a reason you can understand. It's above reason. It rose in His thought that way. Down here, the closest thing to it is a lot drawn from a hat.

*The Tanya speaks here of "the G-d of Israel," of Jewish souls rooted in Adam and Jacob, and of the 613 mitzvot; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

The One who fills everything with His Essence is also utterly apart from it. So He gives each of us a particular light, chosen above reason. You have a "portion" in what has no parts, because He picked it for you.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Think of the one good thing you are drawn to more than others. Don't judge whether it's big enough. - **The line, softly:** "He fills heaven and earth, I, truly." - **Think it through.** His Essence fills everything, yet nothing contains Him. Creatures live from a glow of His Name. The glow splits into many kinds of light, like the limbs of one body: eye to see, ear to hear. One of those lights is yours, the way a lot falls to you. - **Where the picture breaks.** A lot is chance. This is His thought, above reason, not luck. - **From inside.** Feeling chosen without pride. Your particular thing matters because it was given. - **The edge.** "His Essence, not only His glory," next to "a glow of His Name," in the same letter. Hold both. Don't try to join them. - **Into the day.** Name the mitzvah or good deed you feel most drawn to, and do it today with extra care. For a Jew, that may be tzitzit, Shabbat, hospitality, charity; for anyone, the one kindness that is most "yours."

## Letter 8 · The seed that disappears

"One who sows charity grows salvation." Why does Scripture call charity "sowing"? The sages told of Rabbi Elazar. He would give a coin to a poor man, and then pray. He said: "Through charity I will see Your face."

Here's what that means. When G-d shows Himself in a person's mind and heart during prayer, each one to his own measure, it comes as charity. As a gift. His light shines hugely in the higher worlds, so strongly that they are wiped out in it and count as nothing before Him. From there it shines down into this low world, onto those who want to serve Him in prayer. "G-d lights up my darkness." That coming-down is called "G-d's kindness," like water running from a high place to a low one.

Above there is also a power of holding back, which hides His light from those below. Everything depends on what people do down here. If a person acts with kindness and gives life, that is what he wakes above. "In the measure a person measures, so he is measured."

But wait, the letter says. That doesn't quite add up. If you give someone the life of this world, measure for measure you should get the life of the World to Come. Not the light of G-d Himself shining in your prayer. That light is higher than the whole World to Come. "One hour of return and good deeds is better than all the life of the World to Come."

The answer is a picture: a seed. You plant a seed or a pit. The stalk that grows, the tree and its fruit, are not the seed. The seed's own being rots away in the earth. It is the growing power in the earth that brings up the stalk and the tree. But that power only comes out through the seed. The seed rots, all its strength is spent into the earth's power, they become one, and then the earth brings up a stalk with many grains, and a tree with many fruits. And the fruit is far better than the pit that was planted.

So with charity. Every act of charity rises up to the root of souls, which the Zohar calls the Shechinah, the Presence that holds all His traits, beginning with kindness. That rising wakes "G-d's kindness" itself, His light, to come down and shine openly in souls, at least at the time of prayer. For though His greatness has no end and everything is nothing before Him, "where you find His greatness, there you find His humility," like water running down.

"Light shines in darkness for the upright: gracious, merciful and just." When a person is gracious and merciful and gives, he makes G-d's light shine into his soul, which stands in the body's darkness. That is "salvation": darkness turned into light. It grows from the charity sown in the higher earth, the Presence that dresses itself in all things to give them life. And most of all when you sow in the Holy Land, which lies right under the higher land. There, nothing blocks. "This is the gate of heaven."

*The Tanya speaks here of the charity Israel does rising to the root of Jewish souls; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

The seed has to stop being itself to become one with the power of the earth. What you give becomes one with His power, and what grows back is His own light, far more than you planted. Losing yourself into Him is how the dark turns to light.

## Letter 9 · The Shema with your eyes open

"My beloved brothers and friends, dear as my own soul." The Rebbe writes to wake the sleepers from the sleep of empty things, and to open the eyes of the blind. He wants all their hope and all their aim, all their days, from soul down to flesh, to be in the Source of living waters, the Life of all life.

What does that mean in practice? That all your business in the world, all your work for a living, should not be "for yourself." He contrasts this with the nations, who feed and support and honor their wives and children out of love. "Who is like Your people Israel, one nation on earth": one, meaning that even in earthly matters they should not separate from the One who is true.

Picture it. Morning and night you say the Shema with your eyes covered. "G-d is One," in all four directions, in the heavens above and on the earth below. Then you open your eyes, and it's as if "you flick your eyes at Him and He is not there." The letter calls that giving false testimony.

So what fits us is this. Let all our dealings in the world be not for ourselves, but to give life to souls, which are parts of G-d, and to fill what they lack, with free kindness. That's how we become like the One who formed us. "G-d's kindness is all day long," a kindness of truth, giving life to the whole world at every moment. (He adds the order of the law: a man's wife and children come first. The righteous of the generation come even before his children, and the righteous of the Land of Israel before those outside it.)

So put your hearts to these words, he says. He is writing them very briefly, and hopes to explain them at length face to face. In these times, the "heels of Moshiach," the main service of G-d is charity. "Israel will be redeemed only through charity." The sages said Torah study equals all acts of kindness, but that was in their days, when learning was the main service, and so they became great sages. Now it's different. The "fallen booth of David," the Presence, has sunk down to the level of feet and heels, the level of action. The only way to cling to it truly, and to turn its darkness into light, is also through action: the deed of charity. In G-d, "action" means sending life down to the very lowest, to one who has nothing of his own. Whoever overcomes himself here and opens his hand and his heart, pushes down the other side and turns darkness into His light. And he will see, "eye to eye, G-d's return to Zion."

*The Tanya speaks here of Israel and contrasts it with the nations; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

The One you name with closed eyes is the same One you meet with open eyes, at the desk and in the shop. Working to give life, not just to keep, is how the day stops contradicting the Shema.

## Letter 10 · Kindness without measure

After greeting them, the Rebbe opens with a verse a prophet spoke after the destruction: "G-d's kindnesses, for they are not ended." But the word in the verse is odd. It doesn't say "are not ended" the usual way. The Zohar helps: "There is kindness and there is kindness." There is the kindness of the world, and there is the higher kindness, called "great kindness."

The Torah is called "strength." It came "from the mouth of Power." Its root is His kindness, His right hand: His light drawn down into all the worlds by a person who keeps the 248 positive commandments. Those are like 248 limbs of the King, vessels for His light, and from that light a person gets love and awe in each mitzvah.

But on the way down, that light was dressed first in His Power, His holding back. Why? So it could fit into mitzvot done with physical things: tzitzit, tefillin, sacrifices, charity. Even love and awe, which are inside a person, have limits. Endless love for G-d, with no limit at all, no one could hold in his heart and stay alive for a moment. At Sinai, when He was revealed, "their souls flew out."

So most mitzvot have a set size. Tzitzit twelve thumbs long. Tefillin square. A lulav four handbreadths, a sukkah seven, a shofar one. A mikvah forty measures. Sacrifices of a certain age.

Charity too, though the world stands on it, has a measure: a fifth is best, a tenth is average. That's "the kindness of the world," the kindness that dresses itself in the worlds through the charity people do for each other. The world has a size, so the mitzvah has a size.

But that's only for someone who kept the Torah and never swerved. Someone who went off the path has taken away from the holy above. He drew down less light than he could have. That damage can't be fixed except by the light that is above all worlds and doesn't dress in them. It surrounds them from above, from the top of all levels to the end. It is called "higher kindness," "great kindness," because it has no measure. When a person draws it down by his deeds, it shines into the worlds, fixes every crookedness, and renews their light and good more than before, a truly new light. That's why "where those who return stand, even the perfectly righteous cannot stand."

The heart of return is in the heart: regret from the depths of the heart wakes that deep light. But to bring it into the worlds, you need an actual deed: charity and kindness without limit. "Great kindness" hints at "dal," the poor one, who has nothing of his own. When a person gives to the poor without limit, G-d gives His light and good without limit. For all the worlds are "poor" before Him: they have nothing of their own. Everything before Him is as nothing. And so every flaw is fixed.

"Doing charity is chosen by G-d more than sacrifice." Sacrifices have a measure. Charity can be spread without limit. The rule "don't give more than a fifth" is only for one who never sinned, or who already repaired his sins through fasting. One who still needs to heal his soul: surely the soul's healing is worth no less than the body's, where money counts for nothing. "All a man has he will give for his life." G-d's kindness has no end, but a person's mercy has limits. So the prophet said, after the exile: "G-d's kindnesses, for we are not whole." We are not perfect, so we need to act with G-d's kind of kindness, without limit, to wake His mercy without limit. And that's why "Israel is redeemed only through charity," giving even when the law doesn't require it.

*The Tanya speaks here of Israel and the 613 mitzvot, and of Israel's redemption through charity; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

Every world is poor before Him. Nothing has anything of its own. When you give to someone with nothing, without counting, you are acting the way He acts toward everything, and the light that surrounds all worlds comes in to heal.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Bring to mind something you did that you know was wrong. Don't argue it away. Let it be there. - **The line, softly:** "Before Him, all of them are poor, with nothing of their own." - **Think it through.** A sukkah has a size. A tenth is a measure. Measured kindness fits a measured world. But what's broken needs light that has no measure. It comes when you give that way to a poor person, who has nothing of his own. Then see: you are that poor one too. So is every world. - **Where the picture breaks.** A poor man hopes to own something someday. Before Him, the worlds never will. And that isn't sad; it's what makes room for Him. - **From inside.** Humility and freedom together. You have nothing of your own, so you can give without counting. - **The edge.** The light that "surrounds from the top of all levels to the end." It can't be held inside anything. It can only come in. - **Into the day.** Give once today without working out the amount first. If you've been carrying a wrong, let this gift be part of your return, alongside your words to G-d.

## Letter 11 · Being made, right now, from the nothing

This letter is about pain: about money, children, health, food, and every kind of trouble. It begins hard. G-d's light doesn't rest on someone who clings to wanting the things of the body for their own sake: children, life, food. That's why the sages said, "Nullify your will." Have no will of your own at all for the things of this world. "Against your will you live."

How can anyone do that? Only through real faith in the One who makes all things, and in how He makes them. Creation is something from nothing, and the "nothing" is His wisdom, which no created mind can grasp. And this creating happens at every single moment. All things are coming into being from nothing, from His wisdom, which gives life to all.

So when a person thinks deeply and pictures in his mind his own coming into being from nothing, at every single moment, how could it enter his mind that something is bad for him? That he is suffering, with children, with health, with money, or any other pain in the world? The "nothing," His wisdom, is the source of life and good and delight. It is the Eden that is higher than the World to Come. Only, since it can't be grasped, it looks like bad or like suffering. But in truth, no evil comes down from above. All is good. It just can't be grasped, because the good is so great.

This, the letter says, is the heart of the faith for which a person was created: to believe that no place is empty of Him, and "in the light of the King's face is life." So "strength and joy are in His place," since He is only good all day.

So first of all, before anything else, a person should be glad at every moment, and truly live by his faith in G-d, who gives him life and does him good every moment. Here the letter is severe. One who is sad and complains shows that he feels something bad in his life, something missing, and that, it says, is like denying G-d. That's why the true sages pushed sadness far away. But the believer is not shaken by suffering. In all the world's matters, "yes" and "no" are truly equal to him.

It goes further. One for whom they are not equal shows that he works for himself and loves himself. He wants to step out from under G-d's hand and live a life of his own, for love of himself, and that's why he wants the life of the body, children and food, because it's good for him. The letter even says it would be better for such a person not to have been born. For the main reason a person is put in this world is to be tested in exactly these things: will his heart turn after "other gods," the body's cravings, or does he want to live true life, flowing from the living G-d?

And he should believe that he truly does live in that life, and that all his needs and matters, down to the smallest detail, truly come from there and not from the other side. "A man's steps are set by G-d." So all is utterly good, only not grasped.

And the last line is the turn. By this faith, held truly, everything becomes good even openly. When you believe that the bad you see draws all its life from the hidden higher good, His wisdom, the Eden above the World to Come, then by that very faith, the seeming bad is truly taken up and raised into the hidden good.

*The Tanya speaks here in terms of Israel and "the life of the nations"; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

You are not a finished thing that trouble happens to. You are being made, right now, out of His wisdom, which is only good. The hurt is real as a feeling, but underneath it is a good too large to be seen, and faith lets it be lifted into that good.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Let the thing that hurts be here. The letter's demand is high; don't use it against yourself. Just let the hurt be here, in front of Him. - **The line, softly:** "Made from nothing, this moment." - **Think it through.** Not "I was made." I am being made. Now, and now. Out of His wisdom, the source of life and good and delight. What comes from there can only be good. If it looks bad, it's because the good is too big for my eyes. - **Where the picture breaks.** When we say "made from nothing," we picture an empty space. This nothing isn't empty. It is His wisdom, fuller than anything. - **From inside.** Not numbness. The pain is held inside something larger, and a quiet gladness starts under it. The letter describes this; it doesn't ask you to fake it. - **The edge.** The hidden good is "the Eden above the World to Come," not grasped. The letter calls it deep thought and faith in the same breath. Past that, you can only trust. - **Into the day.** When something hurts today, say: "From the nothing, now. Too good to grasp." Then say to G-d, in your own words, what you need.

## Letter 12 · Fire and water, and wine on its lees

"The deed of charity will be peace, and the service of charity, quiet and safety forever." What's the difference between "deed" and "service"? Between "peace" and "quiet and safety"?

The sages said about "He makes peace in His heights": Michael is the angel of water, Gabriel the angel of fire, and they don't put each other out. Michael is kindness. Water runs down, from high to low: giving, spreading life downward. Fire goes up. Gabriel is power: pulling back, holding back the flow of life. They are opposites, as long as they are only traits.

But G-d makes peace between them. How? A great light of the Infinite shows itself in them. And the Infinite is not a trait at all. It is endlessly above, even above the mind that is the source of the traits. When it shines in them, the two opposites are taken back into their source. They become truly one, nullified in His light. Then power is sweetened by kindness through a middle trait called mercy, or beauty, which blends white and red. That's why G-d's four-letter Name in the Torah usually means this middle trait: the Infinite shows there most.

What wakes that great light from above? A move from below: the deed of charity, giving life and grace and mercy to one who has nothing of his own.

Learning Torah for its own sake also makes peace, above and below. "Below" includes this world, which since Adam's sin is mixed good and bad, where the bad often rules. You can see it in a person, "a small world": sometimes good wins, sometimes the other way. There won't be full peace until the end of days, when the good is separated from the bad and the spirit of impurity passes from the earth, through His light revealed: "The earth will be full of knowing G-d."

But in a person, in every "time of favor," prayer, or any time he sets aside to be alone with his Maker, he gets a taste of that separation, each by his own deeds. Through Torah for its own sake, and through charity: Rabbi Elazar gave a coin, then prayed. His mind opens to think about G-d's greatness, and love and awe are born. And the good is refined like silver in a crucible, "each man by his praise": by how deeply he praises G-d.

Now: people are by nature merciful and kind, because their souls come from His traits, where kindness wins. Charity that comes from that nature is called a "deed." A "deed" is something already done, or that happens by itself, all the time. The kindness was put in the soul when G-d breathed it in, and He renews it every day.

"Service" is something a person does with great effort, against his own nature, setting aside his own will for G-d's will. Like wearing yourself out in Torah and prayer. Or in charity: giving much more than your natural mercy wants to. "Give, give": "even a hundred times."

Even charity as a "deed" wakes great light and makes peace, above and below. But in this low world the peace won't show fully until the end. In a person it shows in prayer. But, the letter says soberly, after prayer, the bad can easily stir up again and mix in with the good, as he walks through the darkness of this world.

Charity as "service" is different. When you set aside your nature and your body's will for His will, the other side is pushed down, and His glory rises, "like the advantage of light out of darkness." Then the bad can't easily stir up again by itself, unless a person stirs it himself.

That's "quiet," as in "wine settled on its lees." The lees separate from the wine and sink all the way down. The wine above is clear and pure. So in the service of charity: the lees, the bad mixed in the soul, are separated little by little until they fall back to their own root. "You will cast all their sins into the depths of the sea."

*The Tanya speaks here of Israel as merciful by nature; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

Opposites that can't meet in any trait meet in Him, because He is not a trait at all. When you give past your own nature, some of that peace comes into you and stays, the way the lees sink and the wine clears.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Notice two things in you that pull opposite ways today: to give and to hold back, to soften and to be firm. Let both be there. - **The line, softly:** "They become truly one in His light." - **Think it through.** Water runs down. Fire rises. In Him they don't put each other out, because He is above both. Then think of wine left to stand. The cloudy part sinks. What's above clears. Giving more than you naturally would is what lets it stand. - **Where the picture breaks.** Wine clears by itself if you leave it. The soul clears when you give against your nature. And it can be stirred again, if you stir it. - **From inside.** A settling. Not a flash of feeling. Clearer wine. - **The edge.** The Infinite is "not a trait at all." Opposites join there, and only there. You can't feel that place. You can only see its peace. - **Into the day.** Give a little more than you naturally would today, past where it's comfortable, and notice what settles.

## Letter 13 · The quiet good

"How great is Your goodness that You have hidden for those who fear You."

Among people who serve G-d there are two kinds, by the root of their souls: right and left.

The left is the way of holding back and hiding. "Walk humbly." "My soul will weep in secret." "Whoever learns Torah in secret." From that side also comes a sense of limits. In charity, give according to your means; don't give more than a fifth. In learning and other mitzvot, it's enough to do what the Torah clearly asks, to set times for study. And from that side comes the sages' advice to "throw fear into your students."

The right is the way of kindness and spreading out, wide open, with no hiding. "I will walk in wide spaces." No limits, nothing holding back the generous spirit, in charity, in learning, in every mitzvah. Not satisfied just to do what's required, but without end.

Every person needs both. "Nothing is without its place." That's why we find some cases where the school of Shammai was lenient, and the school of Hillel strict. Shammai's school came from the higher left, so they usually ruled strictly. Hillel's school came from the higher right, so they looked for ways to permit what Shammai forbade, and to lift it up. But Shammai's root also held some right, and Hillel's also held some left. Because above, "there is no cutting and no separation." All the traits include each other, and so they are one. Abraham was love and kindness itself, yet G-d said to him "now I know you fear G-d," when he put on the trait of power and bound Isaac.

Scripture says "Abraham who loved Me" and "the fear of Isaac." The difference is only in what shows and what hides. In Isaac, fear shows and love is hidden. In Abraham, the other way around.

That's what David meant. "How great is Your goodness which You hid for those who fear You": the goodness and kindness hidden in people of the left, like Shammai's school. It is hidden and stored away, but it is very great, just like the open kindness of the right. Both are without limit. "Which You hid for those who fear You, and which You did for those who trust in You, before all people": the open generosity of the right.

A small note in the letter: it says "for" those who fear, not "in" them. Because whatever is hidden in a soul isn't dressed inside the body, in the mind and heart. It surrounds from above. From there it shines into the mind and heart at the times it's needed, so it will come out in a real deed.

So since the good of every person, hidden and open, is without limit as far as a soul in a body can be, G-d, please act with them with Your great kindness without limit, "great kindness." "There is kindness and there is kindness." The kindness of the world has judgment set against it, to hold it back. But the higher kindness has no judgment against it. It comes from what surrounds all worlds, "the most hidden of all hidden." "You hide them in the shelter of Your face; You store them in a booth."

*The Tanya speaks here of every Jew and "the house of Israel"; this adaptation applies it to every person.*

Right and left, open and hidden, are cut apart only down here. Above, each holds the other and they are one. The quiet person's hidden good is as great as the generous person's open good, because both come from a kindness nothing stands against.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Are you more of a quiet one or an open one? Let whichever you are be there, without wishing you were the other. - **The line, softly:** "Stored and hidden, and still very great." - **Think it through.** Shammai's school, strict, yet lenient sometimes. Hillel's school, lenient, yet strict sometimes. Abraham, all love, lifting the knife in awe. Then someone quiet you know, who never makes a show. The good in them surrounds them, and shines in when it's needed. - **Where the picture breaks.** A treasure is hidden so no one takes it. This good is hidden so it stays whole, waiting for the moment of the deed. - **From inside.** Respect for your own quiet side, and for other people's. - **The edge.** "Great kindness" has nothing set against it, because it comes from "the most hidden of all hidden." Nothing to picture. Just rest in being sheltered there. - **Into the day.** Notice one quiet person today and credit the good you can't see.

## Letter 14 · A light that never shone before

This letter asks for help for the Holy Land. The Rebbe wants to wake the old love for it, burning like sparks from deep in the heart, as if G-d had put a generous spirit on us today, to give with a full, wide hand, more and more each year. Like the holiness that shines on that land, which is always renewed and growing. "The eyes of G-d your G-d are always on it, from the beginning of the year to the end of the year."

He asks a question about that verse. "To the end of the year"? At the end of one year the next year begins. Why not say "forever"?

His answer. "G-d founded the earth with wisdom." There is a higher earth, the life that fills all the worlds, and a lower earth, the Holy Land, lying right under it, called "the land of life." Its life is drawn from His highest wisdom, the source of all life.

And that drawing is renewed, with a truly new light, every year. Because He and His wisdom are perfectly one, and He is called the Infinite light. There is no end and no limit to how high and great the light that comes from Him and His wisdom can be. Higher and higher, without end.

So every year, a new and renewed light comes down from the highest wisdom, a light that has never yet shone. Each year's light goes back up to its root on the eve of Rosh Hashanah, when "the moon is covered." Then, through the shofar and the prayers, a new, higher light comes, from a higher place in wisdom, to give life to all the worlds. "With You is the source of life; in Your light we see light." The letter adds a line of Kabbalah about what renews each Rosh Hashanah.

The same happens in small, every day. Every morning prayer draws down a higher light. Not the same light that left after yesterday's prayer. A higher one. And in large, across the whole six thousand years of the world, every Rosh Hashanah is like that.

That's "G-d's eyes are always on it." Eyes stand for the light of wisdom. That's why the sages are called "the eyes of the community," and why "the air of the Land of Israel makes one wise." The light is constant, but it isn't the same light from the beginning of the world. Each year it is new and higher, because this year's light goes back to its root at the end of the year. So: "from the beginning of the year to the end of the year," and no further. Even the spelling of "beginning" in the verse is missing a letter, hinting at the light going away on Rosh Hashanah night until after the shofar, when a higher light comes down that has never shone before, hidden in "the land of life" above and below, to give life to all the worlds all year.

But whether it comes out of hiding depends on what people below do: their merit and their return, in the Ten Days of Return. "Enough for the one who understands."

Because He and His wisdom are one and He has no end, what comes from Him is never a repeat. Today's light has never shone before. And its coming into the open waits on us.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Notice the sense that today is just like yesterday. Let it be there. - **The line, softly:** "A new light, which has never yet shone." - **Think it through.** The year's light goes back to its root. A higher one comes. Every morning prayer, higher than yesterday's. Not because you earned more, but because He has no ceiling. So this morning, this hour, has never been lit this way before. - **Where the picture breaks.** A new day on earth is the same sun, one more time around. This light is not the same light again. It's higher. - **From inside.** Freshness. The day is new, not recycled. - **The edge.** The Infinite has no end of height. The letter says it and leaves the new light "hidden," waiting on what we do. - **Into the day.** In the morning, say: "This light never shone before." Then do one act of return or kindness to help it come out, a gift for the Holy Land if you can, or any gift to anyone in need.

## Letter 15 · From my own flesh, and dust and ashes

The sages spoke about the sefirot, His ten traits and powers, in parables and riddles. This letter brings some of it a little closer to the mind. "From my flesh I will see G-d": from the soul dressed in a person's flesh, you can understand a little about Him. "As G-d fills the world, so the soul fills the body." "He breathed into him": whoever breathes, breathes from inside himself. The letter traces how even the lowest level of soul comes down from the sefirot, which are G-dly, with the Infinite light hidden in them. So the Infinite light shines in a person's soul too, hidden in its wisdom, and gives him life. And from the soul, which holds all ten sefirot, a person can understand a little of the sefirot above.

But first, a warning the Rebbe heard from his teacher, the Maggid. Abraham said: "I am dust and ashes." Abraham loved G-d so greatly that he became a "chariot" for Him. You might think that the love and kindness up in the sefirot are the same kind of thing as Abraham's love, just endlessly bigger. So Abraham said: I am ashes.

Think of a tree burned in a fire. It was made of four elements: fire, air, water, earth. Fire, air and water leave in the smoke. What's left is the earth in it, which the fire can't touch. That's the ash. Everything you saw of the tree, its length and width and thickness, came mostly from its earth. And yet the ash is nothing like the tree. Not in size, not in kind. Though it is the tree's own stuff.

So Abraham said of his love. It is the higher love, shining in his soul. But coming down through all the worlds, through so many narrowings, into a body, it is no more like the love above than ash is like "a tree lovely to see and good to eat." And even less, by thousands of differences. The Torah just speaks in human language.

Now the traits of the soul. There are seven in general. Kindness: to give without limit. Power: to hold back, or not give at all. Mercy, or beauty: the middle, which gives to everyone, even to one who isn't lacking anything. It's called "beauty," like a garment dyed in many colors blended well.

Then, when it's time actually to give, you have to plan how to give so the other can take it in. Like a father teaching his son. If he says the idea the way it is in his own mind, the son won't understand. He has to arrange it, step by step, little by little. That planning is called Netzach and Hod, victory and splendor. The letter compares them to the "advising kidneys," and, openly, to the body's organs that prepare the seed drawn from the brain, so it can take form. They are also called "millstones": like grinding wheat into fine flour, the father breaks the idea into small pieces. Netzach is also to win against anything that blocks the teaching, from outside or inside, including the father's own impatience that says "he isn't ready yet." (Here the manuscript breaks off.)

Yesod, the foundation, is the bond: the father ties his mind to his son's, with love, wanting him to understand. Without that, even if the son heard the same words, he wouldn't understand as well. The greater the desire and delight, the more the father gives and the son takes in. The letter says this is why the sages of Kabbalah compared it to the union of a man and a woman, where desire draws more from the brain.

These are the outer traits. Inside them are inner ones: love and awe. The father gives because he loves; he holds back because he's afraid for the child. And the source of all the traits is the mind: wisdom, understanding and knowing. A small child's mind is small, so he cares about small things. "A man is praised by his mind." Above all, it depends on da'at, knowing.

But all this is only a parable. It describes the lower, thinking soul, which comes from the mixed shell. In the higher, G-dly soul, "truly a part of G-d above," all the traits, inner and outer, are for G-d alone. Kindness, to cling to His ways. Power, to judge by the Torah's law, to overcome your urge, to make fences out of awe. Beauty, to glorify Him and His Torah with mind and speech. Victory, to stand firm against whatever stops you from serving Him, as David fought G-d's battles. Splendor: to bow and to acknowledge G-d, who gives life to all. Everything is nothing before Him. And even though we can't grasp how all is truly nothing before Him, we acknowledge it with a true acknowledgment: it is so, in utter truth. That includes thanking Him for every good, not being ungrateful. Foundation, to be bound to Him with wonderful desire and delight. Kingship, to accept His rule like a servant before his master.

And the source: wisdom grasps something of His greatness. Understanding spreads it out, long and wide and deep, and gives birth to love and awe. Different sides of His greatness bring different feelings: lower awe, higher awe, great love, small love. And through all of them, knowing has to stay tied to what you grasped. If you look away from the idea even for a moment, the feeling it bore goes back into hiding. That's why union is called "knowing": it means binding. There is a lower knowing, spread into the feelings to keep them alive, and a higher knowing, like a flash of lightning in the mind, that opens into understanding, "the wide streets of the river."

You can learn His ways from your own soul, and at the same moment, you learn that your soul is ash next to them. What's left when understanding stops is acknowledgment: it is so, even though I can't grasp it.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Think of someone you love, and how you love them. Let that be there. - **The line, softly:** "I don't grasp it, and I acknowledge that it is so." - **Think it through.** A father teaching his son: breaking the idea small, grinding it like wheat, pushing past his own impatience, tying his mind to his son's with love. That's how kindness above comes down to you. Then the burned tree. The ash is the tree's own stuff, and nothing like the tree. Your love is like that, next to His. - **Where the picture breaks.** Ash at least came from the tree and can be weighed against it. The letter says the real gap is thousands of times wider. - **From inside.** The honest relief of saying, "I don't understand, and it's true." And a patience like a father's. - **The edge.** Acknowledgment where grasping stops. Keep your mind tied there. Look away and the feeling hides again. - **Into the day.** Once today, about something you can't understand, say: "I acknowledge that it is so." And thank Him for one good thing, by name.

## Letter 16 · If we don't, who will?

This letter went to one town in a hard season. "My beloved brothers and friends, dear as my own soul. I know how hard the times are. Livelihoods have dried up. Some of you have fallen with nothing to lean on, and are borrowing just to eat. May G-d have mercy on them and give them room soon."

And yet, he writes, from what he hears, some are not doing well by their own souls. Hands that were always open, until now, have closed. Hands that used to give fully and with a good eye to what the poor truly need. The poor are looking to us. "And if we do not have mercy on them, who will have mercy on them?" "Let your brother live with you."

He answers the obvious reply. The sages did say "your life comes first." But only when two people have one jug of water in the desert, and both need it just the same, to stay alive. If the poor man needs bread for his children, wood, a coat in the cold, those come before your fine clothes, your family feast, meat, fish, and every treat for you and your house. "Your life comes first" doesn't apply there. Your treats are not life, the way his bread is life.

That is the strict law. But really, he says, even where the law might let you off, no one should stand on the law. Squeeze your own living and go beyond the letter of the law. And worry a little for yourself: the sages said that whoever is exacting about this ends up in need himself, G-d forbid.

And also, all of us need mercy from heaven every moment. And mercy above is woken by mercy below, every hour. Whoever hardens his heart and holds back his mercy, for any reason at all, causes mercy to be held back above too.

And also, "there is no one on earth so righteous that he only does good and never sins." Charity atones and protects from harm. It is healing for body and soul. "A man will give everything he has for his life."

And also, we are believers, children of believers: charity is only a loan to G-d. "One who is kind to the poor lends to G-d, and He will repay him," double, in this world. There is no reward for mitzvot in this world, except charity, because it is good for people.

And also, the sages warn about the one who stands aside when his friends are counted in for a mitzvah.

"And to those who listen, it will be sweet. May a good blessing come on them, in every good thing."

This letter stays close to the ground. Its one thread to the unity is this: the mercy you open below is the mercy that opens above. The same mercy runs through both.

**Sit with it** *(5 minutes)* - **Arrive.** Notice where your hand has closed lately, and the good reasons you have. Don't argue with them. Let them be there. - **The line, softly:** "If we do not have mercy on them, who will?" - **Think it through.** One jug of water, two people dying of thirst. That's when your life comes first. Now the real picture: your extra, and his bread. Your treat, and his child's coat. Then remember that you too need mercy from above, every hour. - **Where the picture breaks.** A loan gets paid back and the account closes. Mercy given and mercy received never close. They keep opening each other. - **From inside.** The heart's cry turned outward: who else will? - **The edge.** This letter doesn't reach for the edge. It stays with the deed. Let this sitting stay there too. - **Into the day.** Before your next purchase for yourself this week, give first, to someone who needs bread, warmth or a coat.
