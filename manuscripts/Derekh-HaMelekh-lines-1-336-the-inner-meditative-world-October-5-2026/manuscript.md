---
Derekh HaMelekh ("The King's Way") collects the Shabbos and festival talks the Piaseczner Rebbe gave in Piaseczno and Warsaw between about 1928 and 1933. The file is labelled "Warsaw, 1931", but this range already includes talks dated תרצ"א and תרצ"ג, so the file follows a later, expanded edition. Unlike Bnei Machshava Tova, this is not a manual. These are sermons on the weekly portion. Still, almost every one of them turns into a close description of what goes on inside a person at prayer, at Torah study, in repentance and in longing, and a few of them give real instructions.

- **Lines 1-72 are not by the Piaseczner.** They are *Kuntres Tiferet Banim*, teachings of his father, R. Elimelech of Grodzisk (author of Imrei Elimelech), copied by a chassid and printed alongside Derekh HaMelekh. They are quoted below as "the father", never as the Rebbe.
- **There is very little about melody in this range.** The only melody image is in line 151. Nothing here tells anyone how to use a niggun. For that, see bnei-machshava-tova.md.

Every Hebrew line below was checked with `find_he.py verify` against the source file and came back FOUND at the line given (145 phrases checked, 145 found).

## 2. The method: concrete instructions for inner practice

Listed in the order in which a person would meet them.

**a. Before prayer, separate from the world and know whom you are approaching (83).** "וצריך להפריש עצמו קודם התפלה מן העולם ולכוון שהוא מתקרב עתה לדבר עם ד'" (83). He promises that when you separate from the world and its noise, the garments over the divine part fall away: "אז גם הלבושים שחלק אלקות מלובש בהם בכחו מחשבותיו רצוניו וכל מעשיו חולפים" (83).

**b. Stop the flow of thought: "waiting an hour" (89-90).** This is the nearest thing in the range to *hashkatah* (quieting). The earliest chassidim "waited an hour before prayer, to be aroused above speech altogether": "וחסידים הראשונים היו שוהין שעה אחת קודם התפלה, להתעורר למעלה מן הדבור בכלל" (89). How it works: "ובאם היה האדם מעמיד ומפסיק על שעה את כל שטף מחשבותיו ורצוניו, היה חלק אלקות נראה לו בלא לבוש" (90). The mechanism is not a concept to think about. It is the *halting* of the stream: what will I do tomorrow, how will I earn, how will I be honoured. His tool for halting it is "return one day before your death", meaning think that you have no tomorrow and so have nothing to plan. The word "wait" is read literally as standing still: "שוהין והוא נשאר עומד בדעתו ומחשבותיו" (90). What follows is the pure love and fear "of Atzilus" (90). *How long:* an hour (the classical figure). *Order:* before the words of prayer, so that the words afterwards bring that light down into them (89).

**c. Say "Baruch Atah" as if a door has opened (98, 100).** Think hard that you stand before the One who holds all life, and that your family's and all Israel's needs hang on this moment (98). A more developed person is moved by "Blessed are *You*" alone, addressing God in the second person: "ונדמה לו שדרך כל העולמות מסכים ומחיצות המפסיקות נפרץ לו פתח ישר אל ד'" (98). When he is low he should think: "וצריך איש הישראל לחשוב איך שאני נמצא מ"מ אני ישראל" (100), and I have been given the right to say "Baruch Atah".

**d. Pray aloud, hear the letters, and let the body move (102-105).** Thought alone is not enough. Speech reveals letters in the body, and swaying is the letters combining (104: "ואפשר זה הוא ענין התנועות שמתנענע האיש ישראל"). Even without the Arizal's intentions, pray aloud and feel each letter as a piece of the upper world: "אז יתרגש בדבור כל אות ותיבה ישמח ויתפעל מן חלקי המרכבה שעוברים עתה דרך פיו" (105).

**e. Join every thought of God to yourself (193).** This is the central exercise of the range: "צריך האיש ישראל בכל עת שזוכר את ד' לחברה עם עצמו". Do not stop at thinking about God. "רק יחשוב תיכף ומה אני עושה לפניו", and how can I be in my present state before Him, until the soul is moved. The aim is "ותהי' ידיעה זו עכ"פ לא פחותה מידיעת עצמו" (193). Before learning, think that you are now bringing God's thought and will into your soul and body (193).

**f. Leave a free hour after prayer and after hearing Torah (223-224).** After receiving (a teacher, a book, prayer), time must be left for the "drops from below" to rise by themselves. "וחסידים הראשונים שעה של אחר התפלה כ"כ היתה נחוצה להם" (223). Pausing is not idleness: "גם הזמן הזה של החדלה עבודה היא, עבודה של עליית הטפיים מעצמם" (224).

**g. Schedule times to stand "as a naked golem", but never during prayer (213-216).** During prayer or study do not doubt whether your fire is real (214: "בשעה שעובד לא טוב שיסתפק האדם אם עבודה זו טובה"). Work in confidence: "בשעת עבודה צריך הוא לעבוד בבטחון ובהתחזקות שד' יקבלה" (215). Afterwards, at set times, "צריך האיש לעשות לו זמנים להתפשט מכל התלבשות עבודתו ויעמוד כגולם ערום בלא לבוש" (215), and ask whether perhaps all of it has been a mistake. The fruit: "ואם יש לו זמנים כאלה אדרבה מסתכל בלא נגיעה על עצמו ועל דרכו" (216). The same stripping is how new insight comes in study: "לא את עצמו ולא את חידושיו מכיר, מן הכל התפשט, ובשכל ערום בלא התלבשות מסתכל" (213).

**h. Fear before, confidence during (327-329).** Before beginning, tremble and feel ashamed: "האיש הישראלי צריך תמיד ובפרט קודם עבודה לירא ולרעוד" (327). Once begun, stop: "משא"כ כשפתח בעבודה אם גם אז יפחד ויתבייש לא יוכל לעבוד מאומה" (328). Say to yourself that God craves my prayer and will take the good in it even if it is not perfectly clean (328).

**i. Catch every faint good thought (160, 314).** Even a passing wish, "if only I were like that tzaddik", should be seized and drawn inward: "יתפוש בקרבו כל מחשבה אף כל ברכה שמתברך בעצמו, אילו היה כך וכך אילו היה כצדיק הזה" (160), "כי גם לו חלקי נשמה אשר ממעל לו שצריך להמשיכם אל קרבו" (160). The common failure is that sparks are lost before they register: "מפני שנכבו טרם נתודעו בגופו" (314). The father's version: when a thought of repentance comes, go at once and learn or do a mitzvah, so that it has a vessel: "שילך מיד ללמוד או לעשות איזה מצוה שיהי' כלי להתעוררות" (71, the father; repeated as the father's teaching in 319).

**j. Give a stirring a "thought-sketch" (reshimah) (270-275).** When the soul rises and you do not know what it is, it will be lost unless the soul "writes" a picture into your thinking. "לפעמים מרגיש האיש ישראל מין התעלות הנפש ואינו יודע מה היא" (271), "ואז הנפש רושמת במחשבתו רשימה וציור" (271). His worked example is lighting Chanukah candles. Sketch for yourself how you and all Israel chase a living all year, until it seems everything depends on your own effort and God slips out of view. Then see God break through the screens and show "it all depends on Me" (272-273). Then the candles are no longer a reminder but light itself, and the Arizal's intentions stop being dry (274). He refers to the Seudah Shlishis exercise "שרשמתי בקונטרס של בני מחשבה טובה" (274).

**k. When you fall, drop into simplicity (137).** If thought-work fails you, "ויאמר הנני עתה עבד פשוט של ד' ואומר אדון עולם וכו' כבן כפר" (137), with the plain meaning of the words.

**l. At a mitzvah, disown the ulterior motive to God (264).** If you feel self-interest while doing a mitzvah, grieve over it and pray: "הלא אין הפניה מעצמותי כי הלא מצטער אני עליה" (264). For the length of the mitzvah, set aside every doing that is not for God.

**m. Treat even eating as a mitzvah, and so notice every craving (228, 309).** When you eat, "יחשוב כמה מאושר הי' אם בראו ד' שלא יצטרך לאכול לשתות" (228). Know that eating is a commanded act that the inclination is soiling, and worry about that. This is not a pretence (see §5). The benefit is that you start to feel what you did not feel before, just as one notices every stray thought in prayer but not in the market (309).

**n. Plain contemplation (the father).** The father teaches classical *hisbonenus*, for example "בהתבונן האדם במעוז שכלו איך שאין לשער ואין לספר" how great God's love is for each Jew (19), or that all the vitality of limbs, thoughts and senses is from Him: "וכל חיות רמ"ח אברי' ושס"ה גידי' והחיות מחשבותיו והרהוריו וחושיו" (62). From this come fear and trust. And "איש הנלבב צריך להתיישב עצמו ולשום אל לבו" that he has only this one day (43).

## 3. The inner experience, in his own images

**The coal under ash.** Holy traits are present in everything and in the person, but covered: "למשל גחלת עוממת, כיון שמכוסה באפר אין האדם יודע שיש אש לפניו" (78). Throw water on it (come close to sin) and it hisses. That hiss is the sudden fear of being seen. Look into that fear and you feel "יראה טהורה לד' בלא לבוש" (80).

**Calling God up out of my own depths.** Prayer starts by uncovering the Shechinah that is exiled inside: "כן קורא אני את ד' שיעלה מן המעמקים שלי" (82), which he says again in Yiddish, "פין מיין טיפעניש ריף איך דיך ארוים" ("from my depth I call You out", 82).

**Pure love, without reason.** Beyond loving God for favours or for help against the inclination there is a love "בשעה שמתבונן בד' נפשו משתוקקת באהבה רבה אליו בלא שום טעם רק סתם נפשי חולת אהבתך" (88). It is felt "sometimes, at times of expanded mind".

**The father seeing his son.** Being moved in prayer is like a father whose son comes home after a long time: "מתפעל כולו מפניו" (98), forgetting food and sleep, every faculty cancelled into the moment.

**The light written on the body.** In a real prayer, "האור מקיף את כל גופו וחודר בכל פנימיותו" (97). It stays afterwards as a *reshimah*, like the trace of an erased letter on paper (96). The light takes form in brain, heart and face the way an architect's idea takes form on paper, until "וכל הדיוקן שלו נתהפכה לצורה של קדושה" (101).

**Speech that knows more than thought.** While speaking or writing an insight, new insights are added to it: "בשעה שמדברו או כותבו נתוסף לו חידושים על חדושו" (102).

**The soul weeping in the next room.** Perhaps the strongest image in the range: a person lives with his soul "הוא דר עם הנשמה כמו בין שכנים בבית אחד" (116). He does not feel her pain. "יכול להיות שהנשמה יושבת ובוכה על פגמיה" (116) while he and his animal soul are out celebrating. If he opened his eyes he would see her sitting with her head covered.

**Inner calm against inner restlessness.** The pleasure-seeker is not full of joy. He is running from a hidden unease: "בנפשם פנימה יש מין דאגה של עמל ותלאה" (130). He is "בכלל בפנימיותו אינו שבע־רצון ואין לו נחת מן העולם" (128). The servant, even under troubles, is "בבחי' שאנן ונחת שלוה והשקט" (128). The troubles are chaff to be ground off the grain (129-130).

**Two singers, one tune.** "אע"פ שניגון אחד שניהם מזמרים מ"מ כ"א שמחתו לעצמו מגלה" (151). Each person's deeds show his own soul the same way. This is the only melody image in the range.

**Oil and wick become one flame.** The goal is not a soul and a body living side by side but a single light: "משל לנר הדולק, מה יש באש הזה שמן ופתילה" (169). Once lit, it is no longer oil and wick.

**Knowledge that follows you into sleep.** Self-knowledge fills a person and colours every other thought: "ומכש"כ ידיעת עצמו שבאדם שמלאה את כל האדם ופועלת על כל שאר הידיעות" (191), and it is there "ואפי' כשישן ג"כ נמצאת עמו" (191). Knowledge that has entered the soul stays before a person even after the thing is gone from his eyes, "עודו בדמיונו לנגדו מגודל התרגשות נפשו" (190).

**The golem, the seed rotting.** To change, a thing must lose its particular form: "וחילופיהן בגולם, החילוף יכול להיות רק בגולם" (207). A seed becomes a tree only after it rots. Even the sefiros pass through a "slumber" (דורמיטא) before they rise (208). In a tzaddik this can come as *charadah*, a nameless terror (217-219).

**Drops rising from below.** A person dried up or covered by "a heart of stone" feels nothing, "ודומה לו שהוא איש ריק" (222). Hearing holy words is a drop from above. What comes up from inside afterwards, even about other matters, is the answering "drops from below" (222).

**Longing as the heart's fine veins.** Real longing is felt "עד שכל עצמו מתבטל" (233). Once felt, it stays as a will even when the feeling has gone, "כמו הלב ששולח חיות לכל האברים ע"י גידים וצנורות דקים" (235), like light from one room reaching the next (233).

**Longing breaking out like a captive being ransomed.** When a person finally sees that he has no real will, "אז א"א שנפשו לא תתפרץ בתשוקה והשתוקקות רבש"ע מתי אזכה לחזות בנועם ד'" (239), and "והתשוקה הזו מין פדיון שבוים היא" (239): the essence of the soul is redeemed from foreign hands.

**Suffering as unrecognised longing.** The inner longing for the light hidden in worldly things, if a person does not know it as longing, "תשוקה בלתי נודעת לו נעשית רק יסורים" (249). When he knows it as a lack of higher light and prays, he feels closeness already, and pleasure (249).

**Bold as a leopard inside.** Gentle and humble to people's faces, "אבל בעצמותך ובקרבך פנימה תהי' עז כנמר" (250), a force stronger than body and world, one that could walk into fire.

**Revelation dressed in your own thoughts.** Inner revelation arrives through one's own ideas, like a dream: "נמצא שההתגלות הבאה בפנימיות האדם נראה לו ברעיונותיו" (318). A beginner sees it as a sudden sight of his own pettiness. That sight *is* the revelation.

**God holding you by the garment.** Worry over one's low state is God taking hold of a person: "ולא ידע שגם דאגה זו ושבירת הלב זה מין התרוממות הוא" (315). The reply: "אם ד' אצלי אחזתיו ולא ארפנו" (315).

**Leaping out of one's skin.** The chassid "יכול לפעמים לקפוץ מכל בשרו ועורו ולהשליך מע"ע את כל דאגותיו" (329) and stand outside himself for a while. At such times, rising "באמת בלא רמיה" is possible (330). At the Shema this becomes a leap: "ראה שאין עצה אחרת רק המסי"נ וההשלכה על שעה, ואז זוכים לדילוג" (336).

**Strength that is not one's own.** When faith becomes force, the person "מרגיש בכל עת ורגע שכחו נתמך על ידי ד'" (293).

**Joy as life.** "השמחה היא החיים ועצבות היא מסטרא דמותא" (332). A child is joyful because he is full of life.

## 4. The self and God

The owner's thesis is that people seek themselves, and in finding their true self they find God. This range supports it strongly, but with a sharp division inside the word "self". There is a **true self** (עצמות, the soul, the "true Jew", the divine part) that must be found, entered and joined. And there is a **particular ego-self** (אנכיות, the "particular form") that must be stripped off. Finding the first requires losing the second. Both halves are his.

- *Knowing God must become like knowing yourself.* This is the most direct statement. Knowledge of God must "רק שתכנם אל נפשו ותתאחד בעצמותו כמו ידיעת עצמו" (192). The method is to join every thought of God to yourself (193). Jacob's "I did not know" means "הידיעה לא חדרה אל פנימיותי ואל עצמי" (202): "כי אילו אנכי ידעתי לא ישנתי" (202). Again, "אבל ואנכי מה אני בעצמי עוד לא ידעתי" (226). God was revealed to Jacob, but he did not yet know what *he himself* was, and so he vowed "if God will be with *me*".
- *The mitzvos exist to bring out the real person.* "העיקר הוא להוציא את איש הישראל האמיתי" (168). The garden exists for the rose: "ותכלית כל מעשיו היא להוציא את השושנה" (168). Preparing for a mitzvah means "שלא את העשיה לבד מכין רק גם את עצמו" (181): parts of you come out.
- *The soul is present but estranged, and must be mixed back in.* The soul weeps next door (116). "לכן האיש הישראלי צריך להיות עם הנשמה מעורב" (117), "אך התקרבות העצם שתהיה נשמתו נמשכת ומעורבת בבשרו ודמו" (117). What pushed the soul out of the limbs is "the soul of the world" entering through the senses (117).
- *The divine part in you already longs; you cannot feel it because you are far from yourself.* "ובאמת התשוקה של ישראל. לא לבד רצון להתקרב היא רק התשוקה בעצמה התקרבות היא" (232). Why don't we feel it? "היינו כשהאיש רחוק מנפשו ונשמתו מפני שטמונה היא תחת תל האשפה" (232), and "וכשמגביר את נפשו ומסיר את המחצלת של קנים מרגיש הוא אז את תשוקתו" (232). To come near to one's own soul is to feel the longing, and that longing *is* nearness to God. This is the thesis almost word for word.
- *Will comes from the essence of the soul.* "כי הרצון בא מעצמיות הנפש" (239). Recovering real will is ransoming the soul's essence.
- *God inside prays to God.* "כי ד' מתפלל "ד' שבנו" כי תמיד הוא עמנו" (322). The uncovered depth that I call up (82) is the God who then prays in my voice.
- *What is really mine.* Holy thoughts may be God's light passing through. Faith is one's own essence: "במחשבה עלה חלק נשמתנו ובאמונה עלה חלק עצמותינו" (289).
- *Being, not doing.* God made man in His image "not to do righteous deeds only, but to *be* righteous", so that righteousness comes from the self "כמו ששואף אויר בריאותיו וכמו שהלב דופק" (303).
- *Your faults are not you.* Of an ulterior motive: "הלא אין הפניה מעצמותי כי הלא מצטער אני עליה" (264). The fact that he grieves over it shows the true self is elsewhere.
- *To overcome yourself you need a self greater than you.* Repentance cancels one's own past, which is cancelling oneself, since deeds are one's essence (151-152: "וכשבא לבטל את מעשיו את עצמו שהיה בא לבטל"). So "מוכרח הוא להמשיך בקרבו חלק נפש הגדול מעצמו" (153). The deeper self is what lets you step past the shallower one.
- *The father* puts the same idea as an article of faith: in the innermost point of his heart every Jew's chief desire is to do God's will: "בפנמיות נקודת לבבו בוודאי עיקר חפצו וחשוקתו" (64).

- "האנכיות של האדם זה עומד ומבדיל בין ד' וביניכם" (209). To the degree a person makes his particular self stand out, he is separated from holiness. To the degree he cancels it, he comes near. - The golem state, the loss of form, comes before every rise (207-216). - The chassid can "להשליך מע"ע את כל האנכי שלו ואת כל עצמותו" (329), at least for the hour of prayer. Only then is the service for God and not for self (331).

**How the two fit.** The "self" to be found is the soul, the divine part, the essence. The "self" to be dropped is the garment, the particular form, the ego's knowledge of itself that colours every thought toward "what's in it for me" (191). Self-knowledge is the *model* for God-knowledge (192): as present, as total, as automatic. It is not the content. You find yourself by passing through your self-image into the soul beneath it, and there God is already calling (82, 232, 322).

## 5. Cautions and counterfeits

**False humility is emptiness, not humility.** "אין זאת הכנעה ועניוות אמיתית רק בערות וריקות היא" (96). A person with no will or spirit is low the way a sick man is low. True submission comes "לא מפני שהוא ריק, רק מפני האור ששוכן עליו" (96). Humility through self-talk ("I am clay, full of bad traits") is good at the start, but it presses a person down so that he no longer longs for greatness: "וחוץ מזה שאין האיש משתוקק אחריה לגדלות" (99). Think such thoughts only sometimes (100).

**Humility can be pride.** "ואפשר עוד מין גסות היא שמתגאה לאמר עניו אני" (210). *Test:* good humility brings you closer to Torah and service. If you stay where you were, it is false (210). And at a time of greatness a person does not need to belittle himself at all: "רק העצמות שלו בטל ממילא לפני הקדושה, והחושך נדחה מפני האור" (211).

**Self-examination at the wrong moment kills the fire.** Do not ask during prayer "אפשר אין ההתלהבות שלו שבתפלה זו אמת" (214). Examine afterwards, at set times (215).

**Suspecting that a true elevation is self-deception.** Sometimes a person feels raised up and says "ומשתאה על עצמו איך באתי אני איש המגושם למצב עליון כזה, ואומר בטח רק מרמה אני א"ע" (330). For someone still tied to himself the suspicion may be right. The chassid who can set himself aside "יכול הוא על שעה לעלות מהם באמת בלא רמיה" (330). So the test is whether you could actually step out of yourself, not how high the state feels.

**The mirror problem.** "כי כל הנגעים אדם רואה חוץ מנגעי עצמו" (330). Only one who can stand outside himself can see his own faults truly. So the leap out of self is the condition of honest self-knowledge, not an escape from it. Chassidus begins with "עיקר וראשית החסידות היא להיות מכיר א"ע ולא לרמות ע"ע" (329).

**Pious self-deception about motives.** The father's story: a man grabbed food on Shabbos "for the honour of Shabbos", and the father told him that until food disgusts you, "יוכל להתדמות לו שאוכל לכבוד שבת, ובאמת כבוד עצמו הוא דורש" (46). The Rebbe quotes a similar teaching from his great-grandfather and draws the line exactly: "אין כוונתנו בזה שירמה האדם א"ע לאמר שאוכל הוא לשם ד' בעוד לבו מלא תאוות" (309). Instead, know it is a mitzvah and *worry* that the inclination is soiling it.

**Understanding sin without feeling it.** A thorn in the foot gets pulled out because it hurts, not because of a theory about infection: "רק פשוט בגלל ההרגשה שמרגיש בהרגשה לבדה בלי שכל שרגלו כואבת לו" (113). If a person only *understands* his sin, the intellect, which serves the will, "ממציא לו השכל שכליות שקר שאין הוא חייב בדבר" (114). Excuses are "fine" thoughts that sneak into the heart (112).

**"A wish to wish."** Everyone says he wants to be close to God. Without some felt longing, "ורצון בלא תשוקה גם רצון אינו רק רצון לרצון" (235), like a poor man who has never known joy wishing to rejoice like his rich friend (234). The inclination may even allow a few good days, just so that a person can go back to justifying himself (238).

**Blazing up and falling.** "שיש מי שמתלהב לפעמים ונופל אח"כ לנמיכיותו" (313), and then ends up lower than before he rose. The danger is not that the fire was fake. It is that nothing held it.

**Real arousal and passing arousal.** *Test:* "וזה הסימן יכול האיש לדעת בעצמו אם אחר ששמע או ראה בספר מתעורר" (222), and above all if he is moved in matters he did not hear about. If he was moved only while listening, "אין ראי' שהוא נתפעל מזה" (222).

**A faint thought of repentance.** If the wish to change comes lightly, "המחשבה קלה כאור הבא מרחוק ודרך מחיצה" (158), it is a sign the work will be hard, and the thought will pass unless bitterness fixes it: "וכשמתמרמר ובוכה מעורר למעלה בחי' מסתרים" (159).

**Real stirrings misread as depression (the father, and the Rebbe after him).** The daily call to return reaches people who take it for gloom, "נדמה להם שהוא בחי' מ"ש ועצבות והכבדת הלב" (37), and so they push it away. The Rebbe goes further: even the bare sight of one's own lowness, which can lead to despair, is from above (315, 319).

**Too much lowliness leads to sadness (the father).** "ואך לפעמים ע''י שפלוח יתירה יוכל לבא לבחי' עצבות" (28). Lean one way and then the other: "פעם למדת הכנעה ושפלות, ופעם למדת התחזקות" (28). Do not look back at the past while fixing your traits: "העיקר שלא להשגיח כלל במה שעשה מקודם" (24).

**Do not grab a level that is above you (the father).** "שאין רשות לאדם לתפוס דרך העבודה הגבוה אשר היא למעלה ממדרגתו" (39). Test yourself carefully first. And for beginners who feel nothing: "כי באמת גם זה הוא רצונו ית' ובהשגחתו שיעבוד אותו באופן זה" (41).

**Imagination: sketch, not fantasy.** The reshimah is explicitly not a fantasy picture: "אבל לא רשימה של דמיון וציורי דמיונות רושמת, רק ג"כ רשימה של מחשבה" (271). Without it, even kabbalistic intentions stay "הכל ביבשות ורקות" (275). And the one sunk wholly in bodily matters has nothing to write on: "ומי ששקוע רק בעניני הגוף וכל מחשבותיו בהם. אין לנפשו נייר על מה לרשום" (275).

**Separating straw from prophecy in one's own inner pictures.** Because inner revelation comes dressed in one's own ideas, "וצריכים לפתרו להסיר את התבן ואת הציורים שבאים מרעיונותיו של עצמו" (316). The content is one's own. The *arrangement*, why these thoughts and in this form, may be the message.

**Stray thoughts are often not sin.** "רק מפני חסרון טבעי" (265): "כי טבעה של המחשבה היא שאינה יכולה לנוח" (265). They jump from one idea to the next because thought is not bound to anything. The cure is not suppression but new life in the prayer: "וכל מה שיש בו יותר אהבה יראה והתפעלות בתפלה ומצוה יותר נשמר מן בלבול המחשבות" (266). It works the way a new Torah insight holds the mind without effort (265).

## 6. Comparison with the Chabad method (hisbonenus)

The Chabad ontology (00_HISBONENUS.md) describes contemplation as taking one truth, unfolding it in Binah, holding it in Daas until it reaches the heart, and then bringing it down into action. The controlling claim is that the mind rules the heart.

- *Knowledge has to reach the self, not stay in the head.* The Rebbe's "outer knowledge in the vessels of the brain" as against knowledge that "enters the soul and unites with its essence" (190-192) is very close to the Chabad account of Daas as the depth that carries understanding into the heart. Both say the test is whether it changes everything else you know and feel.
- *Duration.* "Waiting an hour" before prayer (89-90) matches the instruction the Chabad sources repeat most.
- *Bringing it down.* The Rebbe insists that thought be dressed in action (136) and that the fervour of prayer leave a *reshimah* that works on the rest of the day (95-98). This parallels the Chabad fifth stage, "drawing down".
- *Classical contemplation exists here too.* The father's teachings (19, 31, 55, 60, 62, 71) are straightforward *hisbonenus*: contemplate God's love, His giving life to every limb and thought, the bitterness of distance. They produce fear, shame and trust in the way the Chabad model predicts.
- *Stray thoughts are handled without fighting.* Neither side prescribes suppression.

- *The intellect is not trusted to lead.* Chabad holds that the mind rules the heart. The Rebbe says the intellect is the will's servant and will invent "lying reasons" to acquit a person (114). What moves a person is *feeling*: the thorn hurts (113). Feeling a sin comes before seeing it. This is a real disagreement about which faculty leads.
- *Quieting against filling.* The Chabad process fills the mind with a single concept and expands it. The Rebbe's "waiting an hour" *empties*: it halts the flood of plans so that the divine part appears "without a garment" (90). The object is not a concept but the uncovering of what is already there.
- *The object is often oneself.* For Chabad the practitioner's own soul is one object among several. For the Rebbe, joining God to "what am I doing before Him" is *the* method (193), and self-knowledge is the model for God-knowledge (192).
- *Picturing.* Chabad hisbonenus works through worked analogy, not visualisation. In this range the Rebbe teaches the *reshimah*, a thought-sketch or scene (the whole year's chase after a living, then God breaking through) that the soul writes in order to hold and look at its own rising (271-275). He explicitly separates it from fantasy (271). This is more pictorial than Chabad, and more cautious than the concession to mental images in Bnei Machshava Tova.
- *Speech, letters and the body.* The Rebbe gives the spoken letters and bodily movement their own revelatory weight (102-105): speech carries more than the thought behind it. Chabad hisbonenus is mainly silent and intellectual before prayer.
- *Longing against intellectual resolve.* The Rebbe says a will with no felt longing behind it is "only a wish to wish" (235). The Chabad tradition (Tanya ch. 16, cited here from memory and not verified in this file) accepts intellect-born resolve as real service even when the heart feels nothing. His answer to the cold heart is also different. Chabad prescribes "duration, not new material". He prescribes the shock of *admitting* that you have no real will, which makes the longing break out (238-239).
- *Self-awareness and self-doubt.* The Chabad map lists being aware of yourself in the state as the subtlest obstacle, and records it as disputed. The Rebbe's answer is timing. Never doubt during service (214). Doubt thoroughly at scheduled times afterwards (215-216). And be confident *during* prayer, having been afraid *before* it (327-329). He also holds that only one who can leap out of himself can see himself truly (330). That is not the same as Chabad self-nullification: it is a temporary self-abandonment, gained by a "leap" (336) rather than by gradual extension.
- *Humility.* Chabad bittul grows out of contemplating God's greatness, which the Rebbe accepts (96, 211). But he warns that humility produced by telling oneself how low one is can press a person down and kill ambition (99). Real lowness comes from being filled with light, not from being empty (96).
- *Melody.* Nothing in this range. Do not import it from here.
- *Joy.* He makes joy structural: joy is life, sadness is death (332). He is closer here to the Chabad warning against sadness than to the Strashelye defence of bitterness, while still giving bitterness and weeping a place in repentance (159).

## 7. For the guide

1. "Before you begin, stop. For a few minutes, don't plan tomorrow. Think that you have only today, and let the stream of thoughts stand still. What's left when it stops is not empty." (89-90)
2. "Each time God comes to mind, don't leave it as a thought about Him. Ask straight away: and what am I doing, standing before Him like this?" (193)
3. "That unease you carry, that the world never quite satisfies, isn't a defect. It's your soul's longing that you haven't recognised yet. Name it, and it starts to become prayer." (128-130, 249)
4. "If you find you don't really want closeness, and only wish you wanted it, say so honestly. That admission is often the moment the real longing breaks through." (235-239)
5. "While you're praying, don't stop to ask whether it's real. Be afraid before, be confident during, and do your checking afterwards, at a set time, standing honestly before yourself." (214-216, 327-328)
6. "When some small good feeling passes through you, even a wish to be like someone better, catch it. Give it a simple picture in your mind, or do something with it right away, before it fades." (160, 271-272, 314)
7. "Seeing how far you've fallen isn't the enemy. It's God holding you by the sleeve. Answer Him: 'I've got hold of You and I won't let go.'" (315, 318)
8. "You are not your worst motives. If it hurts you to catch yourself in one, the part of you that hurts is the real you, and that part is already near Him." (264, 116-117, 232)

Summary (five lines): 1. Lines 1-72 are the father's Tiferet Banim (classical contemplation of God's love, humility, and the order of repair). Lines 73-336 are the Rebbe's Derekh HaMelekh talks, Bereshit to Vayigash, 1928-1933. 2. Method: separate from the world before prayer; "wait an hour" to stop the stream of thought so that the divine part shows unclothed (89-90); join every thought of God to "what am I doing before Him" (193); pray aloud; leave an hour after prayer; schedule "naked golem" self-scrutiny but never during prayer (213-216). 3. Experience: the coal under ash, the soul weeping next door, knowledge as present as self-knowledge even in sleep, longing felt as the heart's fine veins, inner revelation dressed in one's own thoughts, leaping out of one's skin. 4. Self and God: the thesis holds almost verbatim (232: we don't feel the longing because we are far from our own soul), with a twist: the true self (essence, soul) must be found by shedding the particular "anochi" (209, 329). 5. Against Chabad: feeling leads, not intellect (113-114); quieting instead of filling; thought-sketches (reshimah) that are not fantasy (271); a will without felt longing is "a wish to wish" (235). There is no melody instruction in this range.
