## Why a meditation

Every concept in Chassidus points to the unity of God, and that unity cannot be understood by definition. It can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (*hisbonenus*). So behind each subject below is a sitting, and the sitting, not the idea, is what matters.

## How to sit with a section

This is *hisbonenus*, the Chabad way of contemplation, not mindfulness. There is nothing here to watch in the body and nothing to empty out.

1. **Hold** the one line of teaching. It is the object for the whole sitting.
2. **The move.** Think it through slowly: open it detail by detail, bring it down into your own day, take the source's own picture and remember that it is only a picture, and make it particular: you, here, now.
3. **Stay.** Bind the mind to it, the Tanya's *da'at* (Tanya 3): say the line again slowly. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought. The pauses are time to think, not silence to watch anything.
4. **From inside** describes the glimpse. It is never asked for. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts: what you held in your mind carries the act that follows.
5. **Back into the day.** Say it with your lips in words of Torah or prayer, do the one mitzvah or act of Torah in your own measure, and carry the return line through the day.

**Tie** says whether the move rests on derivation steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our own *reading*; the steps are listed. **Thin** marks subjects whose tie to the unity the entry calls partly thin (7 of 11 here); their sittings are kept modest and say why. **Kept** records a disagreement the entry keeps between the sources; it is left standing, not smoothed. **Before you start** is a safety line where a subject touches health, fasting or sleep.

## S163 · The tzaddik · צדיק

*Face F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity. Also touches F11 Darkness turned to light, F07 The home below, F02 The higher and the lower unity. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4). Thin: partly.*

**The glimpse.** Creation made something out of nothing; the tzaddik runs it back, making nothing out of something, so that through a person the world is joined to its Source.

**Hold.** 'The making of heaven and earth was yesh from ayin, something from nothing, and tzaddikim make ayin from yesh.' (the Maggid)

**The move.** Open it. The tzaddik makes 'nothing from something' even in eating, raising the sparks to their source; the Alter Rebbe defines the complete tzaddik by turning, not by holding back: his evil is turned to good, and his service is not to quench his own thirst but 'to unite the Holy One and His Shechinah in the lower realms'; and in the Besht's circle he is the one who binds all the worlds to God and draws divine life into them, a channel of blessing only as one who 'walks always in the true unity'. Bring it down: one ordinary act of yours today, a meal, a sale, a conversation, taken up toward its Source instead of kept for yourself. The Tanya's picture is the ray inside the sun, which has no existence of its own there; only a picture, since the sun is one thing among others and He is not. You, here, in that one act: the something can become nothing, held for Him.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'ayin from yesh.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** Lightness, like a ray inside the sun: a thing held not for itself but for its Source. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips: 'I have set Havayah before me always.' Do one act of service today not to quench your own thirst but for the need above, and tell no one. Through the day, return to: 'ayin from yesh'.

**Thin.** The entry marks this partly thin: the tzaddik's own work is stated as the unity, but the tzaddik as wonder-worker and provider of 'children, life and sustenance' is not derived from it. This sitting keeps to his work, not his powers.

**Kept.** Intercessor or rank? Lizhensk, the Degel and Chernobyl build an intercessor whose power rests on his unity with God; Tanya keeps the tzaddik a rank of the soul, defined by turning evil to good. The Rashab's ranking of the chassid above the tzaddik uses 'tzaddik' in a narrower sense, so the difference rests on Tanya, not on it.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Maggid Devarav leYaakov 6:2 (line 18): כי מעשה שמים וארץ הי' יש מאין וצדיקים עושים מיש אין The spine's own anchor for the tzaddik (F08d): creation is yesh from ayin, the tzaddik reverses it, ayin from yesh, even in eating. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 10:6 (line 172): שמהפכין הרע ומעלים אותו לקדושה The complete tzaddik is defined by transforming darkness into light (F11): the evil itself is raised, not only suppressed. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 10:7 (line 173): לייחדא קודשא בריך הוא ושכינתיה בתחתונים The tzaddik's service is for 'the need on high': to unite the Holy One and His Shechinah in the lower realms, not to quench his own thirst (F07).

## S164 · The bond with the Rebbe · התקשרות

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One. Also touches F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual, F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5). Thin: partly.*

**The glimpse.** Your soul draws its life through the soul of a sage, and cleaving to him joins you to your root in His wisdom, which is one with Him.

**Hold.** 'By cleaving to the sages, the souls are bound and united with their first essence and root in supernal wisdom, for He and His wisdom are one.' (Tanya 2)

**The move.** Open it. The divine soul is literally a part of God above, drawn from His wisdom; the souls of the simple draw their life from the souls of the sages of their generation; so cleaving to the sage joins the soul to its root and is 'as cleaving to the Shechinah'; and after a tzaddik's passing his faith, love and awe spread into each disciple according to his bond and closeness, in life and after death, with great love. Bring it down: the maamar of your Rebbe on your shelf, the teacher through whom your learning came to you. The Tanya's own picture is the nails drawing their life from the brain; only a picture, since souls are not limbs of one body of flesh. You, now, opening that page: the line runs from you, through him, to the root.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'He and His wisdom are one.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** Like a branch still on the tree: what reaches you comes from the root, through the whole tree. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips: 'He and His wisdom are one.' Learn one paragraph of your Rebbe's teaching today; the Rayatz says true bonding is through learning his Torah. Through the day, return to: 'bound to the root'.

**Thin.** The entry marks this partly thin: Tanya 2 and Iggeret HaKodesh 27 state the bond as unity, but its institutional side (the maamad, reporting to the Rebbe) is not derived from the unity. This sitting keeps to the bond of souls.

**Kept.** What carries the bond? Tanya 2: a shared root. The Degel: the channel works only if the world is bound to the tzaddik. The Rayatz: learning his Torah, farbrenging, keeping his requests, not seeing his face. Breslov: one root, sealed at the tzaddik's passing.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 2:11 (line 74): כי על ידי דביקה בתלמידי חכמים, קשורות נפש רוח ונשמה של עמי הארץ ומיוחדות במהותן הראשון ושרשם שבחכמה עילאה, שהוא יתברך וחכמתו אחד The Alter Rebbe's ground for the bond: the souls of the simple draw their life from the sages' souls, so cleaving to the sage re-unites them with their root in Chochmah, which is one with God. Cleaving to the sage 'is as cleaving to the Shechinah'. - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 27:16 (line 1432): היא המתפשטת בתלמידיו, כל אחד כפי בחינת התקשרותו וקרבתו אליו בחייו ובמותו באהבה רבה After the tzaddik's passing his faith, love and awe spread into his disciples, each according to his hitkashrut; the bond is the channel, and it works through great love and toil. - Hayom Yom, 24 Sivan:2 (line 337): ההתקשרות האמיתית היא ע"י לימוד התורה, כשהוא לומד המאמרי חסידות שלי The Rayatz defines hitkashrut not by seeing the Rebbe's face but by learning his Torah, farbrenging with friends and keeping his requests: the bond runs through Torah, which is one with God (F10).

## S165 · Moshe and the leader of the generation · נשיא הדור / אתפשטותא דמשה

*Face F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'. Also touches F05 The Essence beyond both, F02 The higher and the lower unity, F06 The soul as part of the One. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 6). Thin: partly.*

**The glimpse.** Knowing God is given to a whole people through one soul. Moshe and his extension in every generation bind Israel to the infinite light.

**Hold.** 'Moshe and his extension in every generation bind and join Israel with Or Ein Sof.' (the Rebbe)

**The move.** Open it. Knowing, da'at, is binding the mind to God as it is bound to a thing seen with the eyes; Moshe, the faithful shepherd, draws this knowing to all Israel, 'to know God', from a soul rooted in supernal knowing, which is one with its Source; and in every generation sparks of his soul clothe themselves in the sages of the generation to teach the people knowledge. Bring it down: the teacher who first made God real to you, the sage whose words you still carry. The Iggeret HaKodesh's picture: after his passing his light shines in every generation, hidden, like the sun shining from under the earth on the stars; only a picture, since a soul's light is not sunlight. You, now: the knowing you have came to you through him.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'to know God.' When the mind drifts, come back to the words without arguing.

**From inside.** Like starlight at night: the sun is under the earth, and its light is still what shines. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the Shema's first line with your lips, drawing out 'One'. Learn one line today from a sage of this generation. Through the day, return to: 'to know God'.

**Thin.** The entry marks this partly thin: the tie is stated for Moshe and his extension, but 'nasi hador' as a title was not found, and reading the institution of the leader as the unity is a later framing.

**Kept.** Does the leader draw knowing down or go down to the fallen? Tanya 42 and the Rebbe: Moshe and his extension draw knowledge and faith down to the people. Breslov: the tzaddik who is Moshe enters the confusions of the emptied space to raise the souls fallen there.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 42:2 (line 605): שממשיך בחינת הדעת לכללות ישראל, לידע את ה׳ Moshe, 'the faithful shepherd', is the sum of the seven shepherds who draw vitality and Godliness to all souls; he draws da'at, rooted in the supernal da'at 'united with its Emanator, He and His knowledge are one'. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 42:3 (line 606): בכל דור ודור יורדין ניצוצין מנשמת משה רבינו עליו השלום ומתלבשין בגוף ונפש של חכמי הדור The 'extension of Moshe' in every generation: sparks of his soul in the sages of the generation, to teach the people knowledge of God. - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, vol. 3, Tetzaveh 10 Adar I 5741:12 (line 1732): שמשה ואתפשטותא דילי' שבכל דור מקשרים ומחברים את ישראל עם אוא"ס The Rebbe states the function of the leader in so many words: Moshe and his extension in each generation bind and join Israel with Or Ein Sof.

## S166 · The Patriarchs as the chariot · האבות הן הן המרכבה

*Face F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity. Also touches F07 The home below, F02 The higher and the lower unity, F06 The soul as part of the One. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** A body can carry nothing but His will, as a chariot has no will of its own. The fathers were that all their days, and whoever cannot be a chariot can still make Him a dwelling.

**Hold.** 'All their limbs were holy and set apart, and became a chariot only for the Supreme Will alone, all their days.' (Tanya 23)

**The move.** Open it. A chariot has no will of its own, and the limbs that do a mitzvah become a chariot for the Supreme Will; the fathers never stopped, even for one hour, binding mind and soul to the Master of the worlds 'in the bitul to His unity'; and whoever cannot be a chariot like them makes Him a dwelling through set times of Torah. Bring it down: your hand at the moment it gives a coin, your mouth when it says a blessing; for that moment they carry nothing but His will. The chariot is the Sages' own picture; only a picture, since a chariot is pulled without choosing, and your hand chooses. You, now, in the next mitzvah: a chariot for that moment.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'only for the Supreme Will.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** Lightness: like a chariot, carrying, with no will of its own to push. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips: 'I will make Him a dwelling.' Set one fixed time of Torah today, even ten minutes. Through the day, return to: 'a chariot for this moment'.

**Kept.** Are the fathers the summit? Tanya 34 and 39: the chariot is the model few can reach. The Maharash: they remained creatures and knew Havayah only by knowing; union waited for Sinai. The Sfas Emes: their covenant is at the root of the unity, and Israel's cleaving 'above the chariot' comes in their merit.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 23:7 (line 308): שכל אבריהם כולם היו קדושים ומובדלים מענייני עולם הזה, ולא נעשו מרכבה, רק לרצון העליון לבדו כל ימיהם Tanya defines 'chariot': a body whose limbs carry nothing but the Supreme Will. The Avos were this all their days. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 34:1 (line 462): לא הפסיקו אפילו שעה אחת מלקשר דעתם ונשמתם לרבון העולמים – בביטול הנזכר לעיל ליחודו יתברך Chapter 34 follows the unity of chapter 33: being a chariot is unbroken bitul to His unity (F08). - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 34:4 (line 465): אעשה לו משכן ומכון לשבתו, הוא העסק בתלמוד תורה Whoever cannot be a chariot like the Avos makes a dwelling for His unity through set times of Torah: the chariot is the model of the home below (F07).

## S167 · Love of Israel · אהבת ישראל

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One. Also touches F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity, F09 The unity of opposites, F04 Filling and surrounding. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** You and the person across from you have one Father. At the root you are literally brothers; only the bodies divide.

**Hold.** 'One Father to all of them; so all Israel are called brothers, literally, from the root of their souls in the One God.' (Tanya 32)

**The move.** Open it. All souls are matched and have one Father, and only the bodies are divided; so whoever makes the body the main thing cannot truly love, and whoever makes the soul the main thing can; and toward one whose deeds are wrong, love and hate are both true, hate for the wrong and love for the spark of Godliness hidden in him, and compassion for that spark, in exile within him, cancels the hate. Bring it down: one particular Jew, the neighbour who irritates you, the relative you avoid, the stranger you passed today. The Tanya's picture is brothers of one father; only a picture, since these brothers share a root in the One God, not a father of flesh. You, now, facing that one person: the same Father under both of you.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'one Father to all of them.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** A homesickness that is really love, like meeting a brother you had not recognized. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Before morning prayer, say with your lips: 'I take on myself the mitzvah of "love your fellow as yourself".' Do one kindness today for that particular person. Through the day, return to: 'one Father'.

**Kept.** On what is the love grounded? Tanya 32: the soul's root in the One God, one Father. The Tzemach Tzedek: one body, the soul of Adam, joined through Ein Sof. Izhbitz: one Creator and the divine breath in each person.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 32:2 (line 444): ואב אחד לכולנה, ולכן נקראו כל ישראל ״אחים״ ממש, מצד שורש נפשם בה׳ אחד The spine's anchor (F06h): souls are brothers literally, from their root in the One God; only bodies are divided. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 32:6 (line 448): ושתיהן הן אמת: שנאה – מצד הרע שבהם, ואהבה – מצד בחינת הטוב הגנוז שבהם, שהוא ניצוץ אלקות שבתוכם The hard case: love and hate toward the same sinner are both true, the love being for the spark of Godliness in him (F09 kept distinct in conduct). - Hayom Yom, 28 Nissan:1 (line 249): אהבת ה' ואהבת ישראל שתיהן כאחת חקוקות בנשמתו רוחו ונפשו של כל אחד מישראל The Alter Rebbe, asked which is higher: both are engraved as one in the soul, and love of Israel is greater, for one loves what the Beloved loves.

## S168 · Knesses Yisrael: one body · כנסת ישראל / כלל ישראל

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One. Also touches F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity, F07 The home below, F04 Filling and surrounding. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** All Israel is one stature, head to feet. When each is lowly before the other, they become one place for His unity to rest.

**Hold.** 'By this bitul they become one, for the indwelling of His unity.' (Likkutei Torah, Nitzavim)

**The move.** Open it. All Israel are one complete stature, from 'your heads' to 'your water-drawers', and the whole of their souls is called Knesset Yisrael; the head is not complete without the feet, so each is lowly before the other, and whoever thinks himself separate falls into the world of division; and the root of the whole Torah is to draw the infinite light into Knesset Yisrael, 'one in One', and not where souls are divided. Bring it down: your shul on Shabbos, the rabbi, the children, the one who sets out the chairs, each with something the others lack. One body is the sources' own picture; only a picture, since limbs do not choose, and here the joining is made by each one's bitul. You, now, in that room: one limb, needed, and not alone.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'one in One.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** Like one limb in a whole body: small, needed, and not alone. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the Shema with your lips, with a minyan today if you can, drawing out 'One'. Thank one person whose part is 'lower' than yours, the one who sets out the chairs or cleans up. Through the day, return to: 'one in One'.

**Kept.** Chabad reads the one body as the place of His indwelling, made by bitul. Sochatchov reads it as the individual's only access to God, whose separateness is Amalek; the bitul link there is the entry's reading.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 32:3 (line 445): להמשיך אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא בכנסת ישראל … למהוי אחד באחד דוקא, ולא כשיש פירוד חס ושלום בנשמות The root of the whole Torah is to draw Or Ein Sof into Knesset Yisrael, the source of all souls, 'one in One'; God does not rest where there is division among souls. - Likkutei Torah, Nitzavim 1:1 (line 3511): כך הנה כל ישראל הם קומה אחת שלימה וכללות נשמתותיהן נקרא כנסת ישראל All Israel is one complete stature of ten levels, from the heads to the water-drawers, and its totality is Knesset Yisrael. - Likkutei Torah, Nitzavim 1:3 (line 3513): וע״י ביטול זה יתאחדו להשראת יחודו ית׳ The head is incomplete without the feet; so each is batel to the other, and by this bitul they become one for the indwelling of His unity. Whoever thinks himself separate falls into the world of division.

## S169 · Man and woman; marriage · איש ואשה / נישואין

*Face F04 Filling and surrounding. Also touches F09 The unity of opposites, F05 The Essence beyond both, F07 The home below. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 5). Thin: partly.*

**The glimpse.** The groom and bride above are the light that surrounds and the life that fills. When the two are joined, something higher than both is drawn down.

**Hold.** 'The groom is the Holy One, surrounding all worlds, called Havayah; the bride is the power of the Maker in the made, filling all worlds.' (Torah Ohr)

**The move.** Open it. When the two are included in each other a level higher than both is drawn, as the chuppah brings revelation from the surrounding lights; first the bride receives from the groom, then through the bride he is gladdened, and she adds light from 'opposite him'; and the Rebbe: man and woman below derive from the Holy One as groom and Knesset Yisrael as bride, and when a home is built on Torah, 'the Shechinah between them' makes the marriage of God and Israel below. Bring it down: your own home, the table you share, the small peace kept there tonight. The chuppah is Torah Ohr's own image; only a picture, since He has no body and the picture only settles the ear. You, tonight, in that home: peace between you is where the Shechinah rests.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'the Shechinah between them.' When the mind drifts, come back to the words without arguing.

**From inside.** Held in a way that has no edge, from above and from within at once, as under a canopy. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips the wedding blessing: 'Blessed are You, who gladdens the groom with the bride.' Tonight, yield one point at home for the sake of peace. Through the day, return to: 'the Shechinah between them'.

**Thin.** The entry marks this partly thin: the metaphysics of marriage is stated at length, but the ordinary conduct of married life (beyond the Rebbe's 5727 talk on peace at home) and kiddushin as a legal act are not traced to the unity. This sitting stays modest.

**Kept.** Analogy or derivation? The Besht uses the joy of groom and bride as an analogy for unification; the Rebbe derives the couple below from the supernal groom and bride; Torah Ohr maps the supernal couple and uses the human wedding as its image. The Tzemach Tzedek marks the limit: unions above that are forbidden below.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Torah Ohr, Supplements, Shemot 3 (line 2741): דחתן הוא הקב"ה סוכ"ע הנק' בשם הוי'. וכלה כל ה' הוא שם אדני כח הפועל בנפעל בחי' ממכ"ע The Alter Rebbe maps the supernal groom and bride ('chatan vekallah ha'elyonim') on the two modes of presence (F04): the groom is sovev, Havayah; the bride is memale, Adnut, 'the power of the maker in the made'. 'Chatan' is from 'descent'. - Torah Ohr, Vayechi 5:9 (line 1239): וזהו ענין יחוד הוי' אלהים שהם דו"נ Divine male and female are the union of Havayah and Elokim (G14); when the two are included in each other a level higher than both is drawn, 'as with the chuppah, which brings revelation from the makifim'. The human chuppah is only the image. - Toras Menachem 5727, Pinchas 12 Tammuz 422 (line 99673): שמאחדת ומייחדת את האיש והאשה כפשוטם – אזי נעשה כן גם בנוגע לקוב"ה ושכינתי' Peace between husband and wife, 'the Shechinah between them', effects the revealed marriage of God and Israel below, even in 'the tanners' market'.

## S170 · Children and education · חינוך

*Face F12 Knowing the unity: 'know today and return it to your heart'. Also touches F06 The soul as part of the One, F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity, F11 Darkness turned to light. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Thin: partly.*

**The glimpse.** The first thing planted in a child is faith in His unity, and the love grown on it is the love that holds when one is grown and when one falls.

**Hold.** 'The beginning and foundation of what rouses love and awe is the pure and faithful belief in His unity and oneness.' (Tanya, Chinuch Katan)

**The move.** Open it. The Alter Rebbe named the book of the unity 'Chinuch Katan', the training of a child; the love every person can reach is to think that 'He is our very life', and as a person loves his own life, so he will love God; and even a tzaddik falls between levels, and what remains is the love he was trained in from youth. Bring it down: a child at your table, the question he asked about God, the plain words you answer with. Piaseczno's picture is the teacher bending down into the child's smallness to reach the spirit of God hidden in him; only a picture, since the soul is not small, only hidden. You, now: the plain faith you were taught as a child is still the foundation.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'He is our very life.' When the mind drifts, come back to the words without arguing.

**From inside.** Like something learned as a child that is still there years later: simple, and it holds. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips: 'Hear, Israel, Havayah is our God, Havayah is One.' Tell one child one plain thing about God today, or learn one line of the Shaar HaYichud as a beginner would. Through the day, return to: 'He is our very life'.

**Thin.** The entry marks this partly thin: Tanya names the book of the unity 'Chinuch Katan', but the practical pedagogy (curriculum, age, method) is not tied to the unity in anything read.

**Kept.** A difference of emphasis: Tanya starts from what is taught, faith in the unity; Piaseczno starts from the child's hidden soul. Both are stated.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, Chinukh Katan 12 (line 754): ראשית הדברים המעוררים האהבה והיראה ויסודן – היא האמונה הטהורה ונאמנה ביחודו ואחדותו יתברך The Alter Rebbe titles the Shaar HaYichud 'Chinuch Katan' and derives 'train a child in his way' from it: love trained from youth remains when the tzaddik 'falls' between levels, and the foundation of that love is faith in His unity. - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, Chinukh Katan 8 (line 750): כי הוא חיינו ממש, וכאשר האדם אוהב את נפשו וחייו – כן יאהב את ה׳ The love that every person can reach, and that is taught: 'He is our very life' (F01). - Chovat HaTalmidim, Introduction 5 (line 5): וכיון שהאיש הישראלי עוד בילדותו רוח ד' נשמת שדי טמונה וגנוזה בו Piaseczno: chinuch means drawing out what is hidden, the breath of God already in the child; only then 'when he is old he will not depart' (F06, F03).

## S171 · Peace and dispute · שלום ומחלוקת

*Face F09 The unity of opposites. Also touches F04 Filling and surrounding, F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation, F01 The world said into being now. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: partly.*

**The glimpse.** Fire and water stay what they are, and still become one when the light that is no trait at all shines in them. Peace is that light.

**Hold.** 'The Holy One makes peace between them, by revealing in them the infinite light, which is not an attribute at all.' (Iggeret HaKodesh 12)

**The move.** Open it. Michael is water, kindness flowing down, and Gavriel is fire, strength drawing back up; they are opposed while they are only attributes, and when the infinite light is revealed in them they are included in their root 'and become literally one'; below, this is woken by charity, giving life to one who has nothing of his own; and the Besht reads 'seek peace and pursue it' as making peace between the physical thing and the divine life in it. Bring it down: two people you know who are at odds, or two pulls in your own day, to give and to hold back. Fire and water are the Iggeret HaKodesh's own picture; only a picture, since He is neither fire nor water. You, now, between the two: not choosing a side, but asking for the light that is neither.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'He makes peace in His heights.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** A heart wide enough for both. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips: 'He who makes peace in His heights, may He make peace for us.' Give one coin today to someone who has nothing of his own. Through the day, return to: 'fire and water, one'.

**Thin.** The entry marks this partly thin: peace is stated (Iggeret HaKodesh 12), but ordinary quarrels between neighbours are not; the texts treat dispute through the cosmos or through Korach. So this sitting does not claim to settle a real quarrel.

**Kept.** Is dispute a fall or the space of creation? Likkutei Torah: Korach's dispute collapses the surrounding light into the inward one. Breslov: dispute among the sages is the emptied space in which a world is made by their words. The Degel: a dispute for Heaven is fire and water made heaven. The Breslov reading rests on an emptied space the Alter Rebbe does not take literally.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Tanya, Part IV; Iggeret HaKodesh 12:3 (line 1120): אך הקדושברוךהוא – עושה שלום ביניהם, דהיינו, עלידי גילוי שמתגלה בהן הארה רבה והשפעה עצומה מאד מאוראיןסוף ברוךהוא Peace defined: fire and water (chesed and gevurah) are opposites while they are only attributes; revealed Ein Sof, which is no attribute, includes them in their root 'and they become literally one' (F09). - Hayom Yom, 5 Av:1 (line 395): באדארף זוכען און נאכלויפען צו מאכען שלום צווישען דעם גשמי און חיות אלקי וואס איז איהם מחי' The Besht: 'seek peace and pursue it' means making peace between the physical thing and the divine vitality that gives it life (F01). - Toras Menachem 5727, Pinchas 12 Tammuz 421 (line 99672): ואין שלום אלא המיוסד על תורתנו The Rebbe: 'the whole Torah was given to make peace in the world', and there is no peace except on Torah (F10).

## S172 · The gathering of friends · התוועדות / אהבת חברים

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One. Also touches F08 Bitul: the person's side of the unity, F01 The world said into being now. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 4, 5). Thin: partly.*

**The glimpse.** When friends sit together and wake one another, each one hears God, and in each one God is heard.

**Hold.** 'Then each one hears God, and in each one God is heard.' (the Rayatz)

**The move.** Open it. The farbrengen is 'one of the foundations of the ways of the chassidim, and the entrance to the foundational mitzvah of love of Israel', whose ground is that all souls have one Father; the Rayatz reads the gathering where friends wake one another as 'then those who fear God spoke to one another, and God listened', and says the very gathering 'is a great and essential matter'; and rebuke there is only 'with love and great affection', never with shaming. Bring it down: a table with two or three friends, a cup, one thought of Torah, a word from one that wakes another. The verse's own picture is God listening to those who speak together; only a picture, since He needs no ears. You, at that table: in the friend across from you, God is heard.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'in each one, God is heard.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** Like a table where one friend's word wakes another, and the word is His. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips: 'Then those who fear God spoke to one another, and God listened.' This week, sit with one friend over one thought of Torah. Through the day, return to: 'in each one, God is heard'.

**Thin.** The entry marks this partly thin: the tie runs through love of Israel and one line of the Rayatz; the customs of the farbrengen (the drink, the tune, the stories) carry no stated tie.

**Kept.** Chabad makes the gathering itself an institution and a gateway; Sochatchov prescribes cleaving to friends as a remedy when closeness to God fails.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Sefer HaMaamarim (Rayatz), 5689, Asher Bara 86:1 (line 1271): יעמאלט איז יעדערער דערהערט דעם אייבערשטין, און אין יעדערין, ווערט דער אייבערשטער דער הערט The Rayatz, on the wedding-feast gathering and gatherings generally, where friends arouse one another ('then those who fear God spoke to one another'): then each one hears God, and in each one God is heard (F06). - Hayom Yom, 24 Tishrei:1 (line 530): היא אחת היסודות בדרכי החסידים והחסידות, ופתח ומבוא למצוה היסודית דאהבת ישראל The farbrengen is one of the foundations of Chassidus and the entrance to the foundational mitzvah of love of Israel: its tie to the unity runs through S167. - Hayom Yom, 24 Tishrei:2 (line 531): אשר איש את רעהו הוכיחו באהבה ובחיבת גדולה Rebuke at a farbrengen is with love, without shaming: correction inside the bond.

## S173 · Spreading the wellsprings · הפצת המעיינות

*Face F11 Darkness turned to light. Also touches F05 The Essence beyond both, F07 The home below, F06 The soul as part of the One. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 3, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The aim is not to carry water out to the street but to find that the street itself is wellsprings. Like oil, the inner teaching mixes with nothing and so reaches everything.

**Hold.** 'Until they turn the "outside" into wellsprings.' (the Rebbe, Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus)

**The move.** Open it. The Besht was told that Moshiach comes when his teaching is revealed and his wellsprings spread outward, so that others too can make unifications like him; the Rebbe: the essence of the wellsprings is expressed only when they spread outward; and spreading reveals that 'the true existence of the outside itself is the wellsprings'. Bring it down: the most 'outside' place in your week, the office, the train, a person who has never heard a word of Chassidus. The Rebbe's picture is oil, which mixes with no liquid and yet soaks through everything; only a picture, since oil is one substance among others and He is not. You, tomorrow, in that outside place: wellsprings waiting to be found.

**Stay.** Say it again, slowly: 'turn the outside into wellsprings.' When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing.

**From inside.** The sweetness of light that came out of darkness: the outside found to be wellsprings. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say with your lips: 'Your wellsprings shall spread outward.' Share one sentence of Chassidus today with someone 'outside', plainly, going to him rather than waiting for him. Through the day, return to: 'the outside is wellsprings'.

**Kept.** Is spreading the goal? The Tzemach Tzedek held that printing Likkutei Torah fulfilled the promised date; the Maharash answered 'we need Mashiach below ten handbreadths'; the Rebbe: spreading is the vessel for Mashiach. The spine keeps the Rashab's warning that Chassidus without the work of prayer becomes selfhood.

**Anchors** (the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly): - Ben Porat Yosef, Baal Shem Tov's Letter 8 (line 3204): בעת שיתפרסם למודך ויתגלה בעולם ויפוצו מעינותיך חוצה מה שלמדתי אותך והשגת, ויוכלו גם המה לעשות יחודים ועליות כמוך The Besht's letter: Mashiach comes when the Besht's teaching spreads outward so that others too can make unifications and ascents like him. The spreading is of the power to unify. - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 23:1 (line 124): ועד שיהפכו את ה"חוצה" למעינות The aim is not to bring water outside but to turn 'outside' itself into wellsprings (F11); this is the vessel for Mashiach. - Sefer HaMaamarim Melukat, vol. 2, Vayeshev 5726:17 (line 975): כי שמן, עם היותו מובדל מכל דבר (שלכן אינו מתערב בשוםמשקין), מ"מ הוא מפעפע בכל דבר The spine's oil image applied to spreading: because the inner Atik is separate from everything, after 19 Kislev it spreads into everything, the wellsprings themselves outside.
