Here the book turns to the weekly Torah portions. For each portion the Shelah gives the mitzvah it holds (*Ner Mitzvah*), its reasons and secrets (*Torah Ohr*), and the traits and practices it teaches (*Derech Chayim*). This range covers the introduction and the first seven portions of Genesis. It is full of Kabbalah on the Names and the sefirot, but it also holds some of the book's clearest lines on the unity: **the world is G-d's guest at every instant**, **He is the place of the world and the world is not His place**, and **if He did not exist nothing could, but He would not cease if all else ceased**. The practical core is the **awakening from below**: Abraham woke himself first, and help came.

## 1.1 The world is His guest at every instant

The strongest line of the range comes in a law about hospitality. Hospitality is part of "walk in His ways" because G-d Himself takes in guests always (line 6566):

> כי הקב"ה מכניס אורחים בכל זמן ועידן בכל שעה ובכל עת ובכל רגע
> (for G-d takes in guests at every time and season, every hour, every moment, every instant; line 6566)

> רצוני לומר כי כל העולם אורחים הם לגבי הש"י
> (I mean that the whole world is guests before G-d; line 6566)

> וכהרף עין אם לא השפיע עליהם והכניסם לקיום אז הכל אבד
> (and if for the blink of an eye He did not pour on them and take them into existence, all would be lost; line 6566)

*Ours:* this is *Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah* ch. 1-2 (the word of G-d renews all being each moment) in the Shelah's own image. We are guests in His house, now.

## 1.2 He is the Being on which everything depends

Quoting *Ginat Egoz*, the Shelah explains the Name *Havayah* as "the true being of His existence" (lines 6369-6370):

> כי אם יעלה על הדעת שהוא אינו נמצא, אין לכל הנמצאים אפשרות מציאות והויה
> (for if one imagined that He does not exist, none of the existing things could exist or be; line 6369)

> אמנם הוא לא יבטל לבטול הנמצאים כולם זולתו
> (but He would not cease if all things other than Him ceased; line 6369)

> ומכח מציאותו הם נמצאים
> (and by the power of His existence they exist; line 6370)

This is almost word for word the Rambam, *Yesodei HaTorah* 1:1-3. And the world did not change Him: "the making of the world was no newness before Him, no new will, only newness from the side of the creatures" (line 6368); "before the world was created there were only He and His Name" (line 6368). Because creation was from absolute nothing, "from G-d's side the world has no substance" (נמצא מצד הקב"ה העולם אין בו ממש, line 6393).

## 1.3 He is the place of the world; the world is not His place

On Jacob's ladder (lines 6921-6925). "He is the place of the world and the world is not His place" (שהוא מקומו של עולם ואין עולמו מקומו, line 6921). An angel can be clothed in a form and then "the world is its place"; not so G-d: "He does not clothe Himself to be in a place, not even by way of clothing" (כי אין מתלבש להיות במקום אפילו בדרך התלבשות, line 6923). Yet the service of the lower ones is a need above, and their good deeds "make peace in the house above" (line 6925). How can both be true? The parable of the rider: the horse serves the rider, but "the horse is secondary to the rider" (הסוס טפיל לרוכב, line 6925). *Ours:* this is the Ramak's "He is all that exists, but not all that exists is He" in another key. He fills all and holds all, and is not held by any of it.

## 1.4 He knows every possibility equally

On "He built worlds and destroyed them": G-d had no change of mind. "He is the knowledge, the knower and the known" (הוא יתברך הדעת והיודע והידוע, line 6312), the Rambam's formula (*Yesodei HaTorah* 2:10) that Tanya ch. 2 and 48 also use. What exists and what could exist are known to Him alike; "building" is His knowing all sides, and "destroying" is choosing one (line 6312).

## 1.5 Why He made the world

"So that His G-dliness be known, and to acknowledge and testify to the existence and unity of the single Master, root of roots" (line 6282). The sefirot are to the Infinite "like a flame bound to a coal, united in complete oneness in the Soul of all souls, who is the Infinite" (line 6282). "The world was created in generosity, and His complete goodness commanded that His G-dliness be known to His creatures" (line 6679). He has no need of it: "G-d has no need of the world; He created it only out of good generosity" (line 6401). And the means: "the Torah is the imprint of G-dliness, and man is the imprint of the Torah" (line 6289; also 6183, 6329).

## 1.6 Evil is joined to the good

"Evil is joined to the good, and evil was created for the sake of the good" (line 6328), so that light is known from darkness. Its root still holds on to holiness; it spreads into impurity only below, and in the end "even the outer ones will be purified and become inner" (line 6698). *Ours:* the same line as 4474 and 5671-5673 in earlier ranges: all is from Him, but evil is a stage, not a rival.

## 1.7 He is beyond our grasp; Keter is known only by estimate

Keter and Chokhmah are both called "thought", "but with no grasp at all from its side"; by thinking in Chokhmah one comes to think of Keter "by estimate" (line 6752). Keter is called *Ayin*, nothing, only relative to what is below it (line 6364). The sefirot are not His Essence: the Shelah rejects the view of R. Yehudah Chayat that they are (line 6909).

## 3.1 Start first (proposed: `wake_first`)

"This is the wonderful rank, that a person begins with awakening" (line 6432). The Zohar: "whoever wakes to purify himself is helped" (line 6512); "the matter above does not wake until there is waking below first" (line 6512). A gift that comes without one's own awakening is only "a small gift" of grace (line 6432). Noah was awakened from above and "found favor"; Abraham woke himself, and "You found his heart faithful" (lines 6436-6443). "The main awakening is from below to above, and then he is whole" (line 6456). *Tie:* `act`, `heart_after`. *Ours:* this is the Shelah's answer to passivity. The first step is ours; help follows.

## 3.2 Readiness counts (proposed: `ready_heart`)

No one can keep all 613 (lines 6185-6187). The patriarchs "clung with the utmost clinging to the Creator and were glad and joyful to do their Maker's will", "and this readiness is like the actual deed" (line 6189). "What he cannot keep is as if he kept it, since he is ready to keep it and the block is not from his side" (line 6191). Readiness means: do what you can; learn the rest; help another who can do it (line 6195). *Ours:* for the guide, a strong answer to "I can't do it all".

## 3.3 Want it, and hold back for Him

Negative mitzvot are also acts: "let him say, I want it, but what can I do, my Father in heaven commanded me" (line 6198). Restraint done with awareness is like the pulsing arteries, not the resting veins (line 6199). And "he does not kill his evil inclination; he subdues it beneath him and purifies it", taking its traits "and uses them in the service of G-d": envy becomes the scholars' envy, longing becomes longing for the hidden treasure (line 6798). *Tie:* `discern`, `hold`. *Ours:* the inclination is redirected, not crushed.

## 3.4 The test question (proposed: `is_this_a_test`)

"When a sin or a mitzvah comes to his hand, let him think: perhaps G-d is testing me now" (line 6665). "Happy is the person whose G-d is always in his thought" (line 6665). *Tie:* `before_whom`, `edge`. *Ours:* a short question for any moment of choice, with no fear attached.

## 3.5 Every moment is a branch of the Name

We are day workers and night workers of G-d; "let him not lose any time sitting idle, for each and every time is a thing of its own, in the secret of the spreading of a branch of the Name" (line 6883). *Tie:* `details`. *Ours:* keep the reverence for time; do not turn it into guilt over rest.

## 3.6 The inner generosity (proposed: `give_like_Him`)

"Let him learn from his Maker to be generous of heart, to do good to another from the generosity of his heart", not for return, "for that is not called generous but a merchant" (line 6401). "One who does good to people should show them that he receives a kindness from them when they accept from him" (line 6650). Abraham fed guests and then said "you ate from the One who spoke and the world came to be" (line 6583). *Tie:* S86, `act`.

## 3.7 Peace practices

Stop a quarrel at once: "enough that the quarrel began; let it be like a male that does not give birth" (line 6559). Do not hate in the heart: tell the other, accept the apology, "and let there be love and peace between them" (line 6663). Isaac left the wells rather than fight, then made a feast for those who had wronged him: "seek peace, pass over his traits, forgive everyone, and add love to them" (line 6879). Pray for the wicked to return, never that they die (line 6658). Speak gently: Jacob called strangers "my brothers" (line 6994). At home, "let him try to win them over... so they will wake to it by themselves" rather than force (line 6999). *Tie:* S86, S15 (anger), `lips`.

## 3.8 Good eye, low spirit, humble soul

Envy, desire and honor took Adam out of the world (line 6399). Abraham's cure: "a good eye, the opposite of envy... a low spirit, the opposite of honor; a humble soul, the opposite of desire, content with little" (line 6709). "A person should not rush after wealth; the necessary should suffice him" (line 6993). *Tie:* S83, `cheshbon`.

## 3.9 Eat before the King

"That even while eating he not move from clinging to G-d" (line 6877). Love that depends on a thing ends when the thing ends; love of Torah and G-d does not (lines 6875-6876). *Tie:* `garment_look`, S82.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Why am I here, if the soul already knows G-d?** "Why did it come to a murky body, if not to be clothed in doing the mitzvah" (line 6206). "The goal of all is the actual deed" (line 6183).
- **What is my reward?** "The reward of the mitzvah he does is the mitzvah itself, that is, the spirit of this mitzvah, for there he clings" (line 6791).
- **Am I forced to do wrong?** No. The Torah begins with *bet* of blessing "to remove from the hearts of those who err, that they not say they are forced to do evil"; "one who comes to purify is helped" (line 6825).
- **Is G-d harsh?** "The trait of judgment does not mean distress", only precision (line 6774); and judgment itself turns to great mercy in the end (line 6610).
- **Can I change now?** "At every time the matter is in our hands": "today, if you listen to His voice" (line 6766).
- **Do I need to be great to count?** "One who does much and one who does little, as long as he aims his heart to heaven" (line 6211).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **Renewal of being.** Line 6566 (the world as guests, "for the blink of an eye") matches *Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah* ch. 1-2. Line 6393 ("from G-d's side the world has no substance") matches ch. 3 and 6.
- **The Rambam.** A key agreement. Lines 6369-6370 give the Rambam's *Yesodei HaTorah* 1:1-3 on necessary existence, and line 6312 his "knowledge, knower and known", which Tanya ch. 2 and 48 also adopt. In law, the Shelah follows the Rambam's count of the mitzvot and defends it at length: "Rabbeinu Moshe ben Maimon is true and his Torah is true" (line 6215). Where he differs, he differs on creation: against "the philosophers" who posit a pre-existing matter (line 6391); the Rambam himself agreed, so this is a disagreement with Aristotle, not the Rambam. He also notes the Rambam's surprise at the *Pirkei deRabbi Eliezer* midrash (line 6381) and answers it by Kabbalah.
- **The Ramak.** "He does not clothe Himself to be in a place" (line 6923) with "the horse is secondary to the rider" (line 6925) is the Ramak's balance. Line 6909 rejects the view that the sefirot are the Essence, as the Ramak (*Pardes*) does.
- **Tzimtzum.** Not stated here in the Ari's terms. The Zohar's "Ein Sof split its air and revealed a point" (line 6366), "touches and does not touch", is the closest.
- **Awakening from below.** Lines 6432-6456 and 6512 are the *itaruta diltata* that Tanya ch. 46 and *Likkutei Torah* build on.
- **Hidden love and readiness.** Line 6189 (the patriarchs' readiness counted as deed) is near Tanya ch. 18-19 (the hidden love that makes a Jew ready to give his life) and ch. 16 (intent counts).
- **Not carried.** The halachic count of the mitzvot (6212-6275), including interest from a gentile as a positive mitzvah and "the nations have no share in our Torah" (6216-6227, 6222); particularist lines on the land and circumcision (6484-6488), on the nations' death (6538), "all the nations are uncircumcised of heart" (6605), and "Israel abroad serve idols in purity" (6958); the anti-Esau polemic (6819-6834); martyrdom framing (6632-6634, 6914); suffering accepted "in love" as purifying (6404, 6504, 6737, 6813), which the guide does not push; death called "very good" (6671); niddah separation (7000-7001); the Names and sefirot of time (6884-6892) and of the Patriarchs' letters.
