## The shape of the range

These parashot hold the holiness code, the priests, the calendar of festivals, the Sabbatical year and the blessings and curses. The Zohar reads them as the year of a soul: a person made "one" by holiness, a week that ends in the King's wedding, festivals that are invitations to a guest, Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur as a trial where the King's love for His children overcomes His love of justice, Sukkot as sitting "in the shade of faith", and the curses of Bechukotai read as the King who goes into exile with His son.

1. **"Let the one come and engage with the One"** (1444-1449). A person becomes "one" in holiness, and the One dwells only in one.
2. **The deed below wakes the deed above**, spoken sharply: "whoever says no deed is needed ... may his spirit expire" (1851). A day's kindness crowns the day and the day guards you (1632).
3. **The Raya Mehemna's prayer to the Cause of Causes** (1934-1935), the most exact statement of Ein Sof in this volume so far: inside all, outside all, "in the thickness of each sefirah".
4. **The pledge and the King at the window** (2013-2034). He left His Presence with Israel as a pledge, comes every Shabbat and new moon to look at it through the cracks of the wall, and goes into exile with them.

## The unity of G-d

**He is outside all and inside all.** Moses in the Raya Mehemna prays to "the Cause of Causes":

> דאנת הוא נשמה לנשמות, ולית נשמה עלך, ולא אלהא עלך, אנת לבר מכלא, ולגאו מכלא, ולכל סטרא, ולעילא מכלא, ולתתא מכלא (Zohar, Behar 7:6; line 1934)
> For You are the soul of souls; there is no soul above You and no god above You. You are outside all and inside all, and on every side, above all and below all.

And more: "You are in each sefirah, in its length and breadth, above and below, and between each sefirah and the next, and in the thickness of each sefirah" (1934). "You have no body and no limbs, and no female, but One without a second" (אלא אחד בלא שני, line 1935).

*Ours.* This is the Zohar text the Chabad tradition most leans on for "filling and surrounding" (F04) and for the Essence (F05). The Tanya's Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7 and many maamarim cite exactly this pair, "He fills all worlds and surrounds all worlds", and the Zohar here adds the image that the Tanya takes over: He is in the sefirot as a soul is in a body, and yet He is not their soul in the way a soul needs a body. An earlier prayer of Moses in the same voice (1461) calls Him "the Cause of Causes, exalted from height to height until there is no height, for You are above every height", "one and not two, without partner".

**His holiness needs nothing; ours needs Him.** Carried from part 2: the ten crowns "are His name, and He and His name are one word" (אינון שמיה, ואיהו ושמיה, מלה חדא הוא, line 1658). "The world, and the next world, hang on the letters: He and His Name are one" (הוא ושמיה חד, line 1881).

**Opposites held in one.** When He created the world, "to reveal the deep out of the hidden and light out of darkness, they were included in each other"; "out of good came bad, out of mercy came judgment, and all is included one in another ... all hangs one on one" (וכלא חד בחד תליא, line 1431). The world is judged only with judgment included in mercy, "otherwise it could not stand one moment" (1432). This is F09.

**Not One now, One when she returns.** The theme of parts 1 and 3 comes again, in Emor: "night without day is not, and day without night is not, and it is not called One except in one union"; "because the Assembly of Israel is now in exile, as it were He is not called One" (כביכול לא אקרי אחד, line 1654). And in the Raya Mehemna: when the Holy One is not with the Shechinah in the offering, "it is as if the Holy One were not One" (כאלו לא הוה קודשא בריך הוא חד, line 1930).

**Everything is His servant.** Rabbi Shimon rebukes Rabbi Yosi for a small act of contempt toward water: it is "a servant of the world". "And G-d saw all He had made and behold it was very good: even snakes, scorpions and gnats, and even those that look like destroyers of the world, of all of them it is written 'very good'; they are all servants of the world, and people do not know" (1886). "In everything the Holy One does His errand, and we must not treat with contempt anything He made" (1887). *Ours:* F01 as reverence for every creature, and S63 (the world held) from a new side.

## States of consciousness

**Made one.** The central teaching of Kedoshim:

> ובגיני כך, ליתי אחד, וישתדל באחד. קודשא בריך הוא דאיהו אחד, ישתדל באחד (Kedoshim 3:13; line 1446)
> Therefore let "one" come and engage with the One. The Holy One who is One engages with "one".

When is a person called one? When he is "crowned" with tefillin and wrapped in the tallit (1445), and when he is joined with his wife "and they intend to be sanctified" (1447): "then all is one, in soul and in body; in soul, to cleave one to the other in one will ... and then the Holy One dwells in 'one' and places a holy spirit in that one" (1448). "He does not dwell except in one: in the one who is prepared in upper holiness to be one" (1446). *Ours:* this is a state the guide lacks as such: not "no self" (S57) but a self made single, whole, gathered, with nothing split off. *Proposed state: made one.*

**The single heart and the turning sword.** A deep account of why the inner life keeps changing. The Holy One wanted Adam "to cleave to Him, so that he would be single, with a single heart, in a single place of cleaving that does not change or turn" (1892). He left the one tree for "the place that changes and turns from color to color, from good to bad and from bad to good" (1893), and so:

> כדין אשתני אדם לכמה גוונין, זמנין לטב, זמנין לביש. זמנין רוגזא, זמנין נייחא ... ולא קאים בקיומא תדיר בחד מנייהו (Emor 51:5; line 1896)
> Then man changed into many colors: sometimes good, sometimes bad; sometimes anger, sometimes rest ... and he does not stand steady in any one of them.

*Ours:* this names the ordinary turning heart (S10 torn in two, S26 it goes flat) and gives its cure in the same image: cleaving to the one tree, the single place. It fits S60 (the essential bond) as the place that does not turn.

**Watched through the cracks.** Of all the images of being held in this volume, this may be the tenderest:

> בשעתא דסליק רעותא דמלכא קדישא, על מטרוניתא ועל ישראל. סליק אגרין ... לאסתכלא ולאשגחא בין קוסטי כותלא עלייהו. כיון דחמי לון, שארי ובכי (Bechukotai 10:3; line 2024)
> When the will of the holy King rises toward the Matrona and toward Israel, He climbs the roofs ... to look and watch over them through the cracks of the wall. When He sees them, He begins to weep.

"Behold He stands behind our wall": in the synagogues and study houses, "watching from the windows, peering through the lattices". "And so Israel must rejoice on that day, because they know this" (2024). He comes on every new moon, Shabbat and festival "to look at His pledge" (2022). *Proposed state: seen from behind the wall*, the knowledge that one is being looked at with longing, not judgment, in the ordinary place of prayer. It belongs with S33 (before Whom) and S84.

**He goes into exile with you.** The King's only son keeps sinning. "If I drive you out of the land alone, perhaps bears and wolves will rise against you. What shall I do? You and I will go out of the land" (2032). "'I too' (אף אני): I and you will go into exile together" (2033). And the Shechinah is His "pledge" (משכונא): as a friend leaves his most precious thing, or his own bed, in the house of the one he loves, so "the Holy One left His most precious thing with Israel, so as never to part from them" (2013-2015).

**The tanners' market.** "I did not despise them nor loathe them" because of the bride among them: "a man who loves a woman who lives in the tanners' market; if she were not there he would never enter; since she is there, it seems to him like the perfumers' market" (2041). *Ours:* the most direct image in the Zohar for S12 and S11 (the verdict: I'm my mistakes): the place is foul, and it is loved for the Presence in it.

**Shabbat: all pain forgotten.** "On this day all pain, all anger and all pressure in the world are forgotten, for it is the day of the King's wedding and souls are added, like the world to come" (1684). Even the wicked in Gehinnom rest (1673). Rabbi Shimon, at the third meal, would set his table and learn the Chariot and say: "this is the meal of the King, who comes to eat with me" (1683). This is S76 (Shabbos: the soul returned).

**The day as guardian.** "There is no day below over which an upper day does not rule" (1631). "If a person does kindness in the world, kindness wakes above and rests on that day and is crowned by it for his sake ... and then that day stands over him as a guardian when he needs it" (1632). Cruelty does the reverse (1633). *Ours:* a felt truth for the guide: today's kindness is not gone at sunset; the day itself keeps it.

## Meditation and practice

**Kedushah as an inner act (Raya Mehemna).** "This commandment: to sanctify Him every day, to raise His holiness from below upward" (1644). Where to include oneself: "when a person reaches 'the whole earth is full of His glory', then let him include himself in that holiness, to be sanctified below" (1650). Whoever can set his will on the three levels, "the three fathers in one", to unify them in this holiness, does well (1649). And:

> אנן צריכין בקדושתא דא, לאשתכחא תמן קדושה וברכה ומלכות, לאשתכחא כלא כחדא ... ועל דא יכוון בר נש, וישוי רעותיה בכל יומא (Emor 21:8; line 1651)
> In this Kedushah we must find holiness, blessing and kingship, all found together ... and so a person should intend, and set his will on it, every day.

*Proposed technique: the Kedushah inclusion.* At "the whole earth is full of His glory", include yourself in the holiness below; at "blessed be the glory", the blessing; at "the Lord shall reign", the kingship.

**Naming each festival as an invitation.** The festivals are "invited from Kodesh": a King invites an honored guest and tells His household, "all other days you each do your work; on my day ... prepare to receive this guest with shining faces, with joy, with praise, and set him a precious meal" (1665-1666). Shabbat is not "invited": it is the son's own home, where he eats "whenever he wants" (1669). *Ours:* this is an entry the guide can use for any holy day: receive it as a guest.

**The ushpizin and the poor.** At the sukkah door Rav Hamnuna Sava would say: "Sit, supernal guests, sit; sit, guests of faith, sit", lift his hands in joy, and sit (1835). "One must gladden the poor, for the portion of those guests belongs to the poor"; if he invites them and gives the poor nothing, "they all rise from his table" (1836). "A person should not say: I will eat and drink first and give the rest to the poor. The first of all is the guests' share" (1839).

**Tzedakah makes the Name.** "Rabbi Shimon wept and said: woe to people who do not know ... who makes the holy Name every day? Say: the one who gives tzedakah to the poor" (2003). The poor person "has nothing of his own", like the moon; giving joins tzedakah to tzedek, "and the holy Name is found whole" (2004). "Do good": don't say "if I give this now, what will I do tomorrow?" (1954). A story of two travelers shows it: one gives his bread to a starving man; a snake comes down the tree to kill him in his sleep, and he is saved (1956-1960). *Ours:* this is `act` as the Zohar's highest deed: a coin that completes the Name.

**Be both servant and son in prayer.** "When a person knows the Holy One in a general way, he is called a servant ... When he knows Him in detail, he is called a son, beloved, who searches His treasures" (1975). A person needs both (1976). In prayer especially: "to serve and fix the prayer as a servant, to fix the worlds; and to cleave his will to the secrets of wisdom, to cleave to his Master" (1980). Of the one who holds both: "it is fitting for the single one to be with the Single One, one engaged with One" (1982).

**Learn aloud, learn from anyone, learn even stammering.** Torah said straight rises and "bathes in the river" (1516). "One who longs to learn Torah and finds no one to teach him, and learns from love of the Torah and stammers in it, not knowing: every word rises, and the Holy One rejoices in it and plants it by the river, and they become great trees" (1518). "One must learn words of Torah from anyone, even from one who does not know" (1520), and "from not for its sake comes for its sake" (1520). *Ours:* a strong guard against despair for beginners in the guide.

**Rebuke in love (Raya Mehemna).** "Rebuke the one who sins, to show him great love, so he will not be punished" (1524). The Holy One first rebukes in secret, then among his loved ones (illness in bed, when friends come), then openly, then leaves him to his will (1525). If the person would be ashamed, "don't rebuke him even in secret, but speak before him as if of other things, and among them mention one who did such and such" (1526). "The Holy One spares a person's honor, even the wicked's" (1527).

**Guard the mouth on Shabbat.** "Every word that comes out of a person's mouth has a voice, and it rises and wakes another word above"; weekday speech on Shabbat wakes "weekday" above. "Thought is permitted, for thought makes no voice" (1853-1854). *Ours:* a clear rule for the guide on inner speech versus spoken speech.

**Accept the yoke first.** "Like an ox that is given a yoke first, to bring good out of it ... so a person must take the yoke upon himself first, and afterward serve with it in all that is needed" (1907). This is S83 (accepting the yoke) as the beginning, and the hand-tefillin as its sign (1908).

**Stand before the Torah and the scholar.** Rav Hamnuna Sava would rise before a Torah scroll: "before the hoary head you shall rise" (1555).

## The big human questions

**Why suffering: the rod of love.** "As a father who loves his son keeps a rod in his hand always, to lead him straight ... and the one the Holy One does not love and hates, He takes the rebuke from him, takes the rod from him" (2025). Esau is left without the rod (2026).

**Death.** "On the day a person goes to leave the world ... the King wants His deposit. Happy is the one who returns his deposit as it was given" (1563). "A person does not die until he sees the Shechinah, and from great longing for the Shechinah the soul goes out to meet her" (1564).

**Youth and age.** "A person's praise is when he is in his strength and good; not at the end of his days, when he is old and cannot be bad" (1556).

**Trust.** "A person should always be careful with his Master and cleave his heart to the upper faith, so as to be whole with his Master; when he is whole with Him, none in the world can harm him" (1950). "He knows the way of the righteous ... and goes before them to guard them" (1557).

**The poor.** They stand where death is; tzedakah is the tree of life that saves (2006). The workman's wages: "whoever takes the poor man's wage, it is as if he took his soul"; Rav Hamnuna would pay each worker saying, "take your soul that you entrusted in my hand" (1509-1510).

**Joy.** "Rejoice in the Lord": "the joy is the Holy One's", "let us rejoice and be glad in Him" (1849). The priest must be seen "in beauty and a shining face, in joy more than all" (1586). Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur have no "invitation" of joy, but even there "the love of His children overcomes the love of judgment" (נצח רחימוי דבנוי לרחימו דדינא, line 1773).

## For the guide (ours)

1. Add line 1934 (outside all, inside all, in the thickness of each sefirah) to F04 and F05 as the Zohar's own source.
2. Propose the state *made one* (1444-1449) and the state *seen from behind the wall* (2022-2024); add the *tanners' market* image (2041) to the being-held work.
3. Add the *Kedushah inclusion* (1644-1651) and *receiving the day as a guest* (1665-1669) as techniques.
4. Add the teaching on the turning heart (1892-1896) to S10 and S26, with its remedy: the single place.
5. Add the stammering learner (1518) and rebuke in love (1524-1527) as guards.
