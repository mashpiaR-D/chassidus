## How to read these

Every concept in Chassidus points to the unity of God, which cannot be grasped by definition; it can only be glimpsed, in contemplation (*hisbonenus*). So each subject is given here as a meditation in the Chabad way, not as an idea. **Hold** is one line of teaching from the entry's own sources. **The move** thinks it through slowly: it opens the line detail by detail, gives the source's own picture and says where the picture breaks, and brings it down to you, here, now. It uses only what the entry's derivation and passages say. **Stay** binds the mind to the line, as Tanya 3 describes da'as: the pauses are for thinking, and a stray thought is not argued with, only left for the line. **From inside** describes the glimpse and never asks for it; if nothing is felt, the thinking still counts. **Back into the day** says it with the lips in words of Torah or prayer, adds one mitzvah or act of Torah in the person's measure, and gives a line to return to.

**Tie** says whether the move rests on derivation steps the entry marks *stated*, or on our own reading; the steps are listed. Where the entry marks a subject's tie as thin or partly thin, the meditation is kept modest and says so in one line. Where the sources differ on what the move touches, the difference is kept, not smoothed. Anchors are the entry's own verified passages, copied exactly, OCR spelling included.

## S052 · The soul, a part of God above · חלק אלוה ממעל

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One; also touches F05, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 3). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The deepest you is not a second thing standing next to Him. It is a part of the One, drawn from a wisdom that is one with Him, and the bond holds all the way down, even in the lowest person.

**Hold.** 'The second soul in Israel is a part of God above, literally.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 2)

**The move.** Open it: a 'part' of the One who has no parts is not a piece broken off, because the soul is drawn from His wisdom, and He and His wisdom are one. And the bond holds all the way down: even the soul clothed in the lowest person stays bound and united with its source. Tanya's own picture is a child whose whole body, down to the nails, grows from one drop of the father's mind; it is only a picture, for a father and a child are two, and here there is no second thing. Now bring it to yourself, here, on an ordinary day, at your worst as at your best: this is said of you, now, still bound and still one.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'a part of God above, literally', and in the pauses think: not a second thing beside Him. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A core no one has ever touched, and a bond you did not make. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say Modeh Ani tomorrow on waking with this line in mind; today add one sentence to your prayer, in your own words, asking for what the part of God in you lacks, as the Maggid teaches; and return during the day to 'a part of God above'.

**Where the sources differ.** Whose soul this is, and a part of what, is put differently: the Keter Shem Tov speaks of the tzaddik, Tanya of every Jew, R. Nachman of 'parts of the Shechinah', and the Rashab of 'a part of the Essence', without measure.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 2:1 (line 64): ונפש השנית בישראל, היא חלק אלוה ממעל ממש Tanya 2: the second soul in every Jew is literally a part of God above, breathed 'from within'. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 2:2 (line 65): נמשכה ממחשבתו וחכמתו יתברך, דאיהו חכים ולא בחכמה ידיעא, אלא הוא וחכמתו אחד The soul is drawn from His wisdom, and He and His wisdom are one: this is what makes the 'part' a part of the One and not a second thing. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 2:8 (line 71): ועם כל זה, עודנה קשורה ומיוחדת ביחוד נפלא ועצום במהותה ועצמותה הראשון Even in the lowest Jew the soul stays bound and united with its first essence, as the nail with the brain.

## S053 · The divine soul · נפש האלקית

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One; also touches F08, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** There is a life in you that has no self to protect. Its whole nature is to be drawn into its source, the way a flame keeps reaching up.

**Hold.** 'By its nature it desires and longs to cleave to its root and source, in God, the Life of life.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 19)

**The move.** Open it: this longing is not an effort the soul makes but its nature, even to be wholly nullified in its source. Holiness is simply what is nullified to Ein Sof and is not a thing in itself; and R. Yitzchak Eizik of Homel says from inside that the soul's life is nothing but the living God, with no self-feeling at all. Tanya's picture is a flame that keeps leaping up from the wick toward its source; it is only a picture, for a flame that leaves its wick goes out, while the soul is drawn to the Life of life. Bring it down to your own wants today, at the desk or in the kitchen: under them is a life in you, here, now, that wants Him more than it wants itself.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'to cleave to its source, the Life of life', and in the pauses think: this is its nature, not my effort. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A life that is not busy with itself; a homesickness that is really love. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the first line of the Shema slowly today, as the soul's own word; learn a few lines of Tanya chapter 19; and return during the day to 'to its source, the Life of life'.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 19:4 (line 266): חפצה וחשקה בטבעה ליפרד ולצאת מן הגוף ולידבק בשרשה ומקורה – בה׳ חיי החיים ברוךהוא; הגם שתהיה אין ואפס ותתבטל שם במציאות לגמרי The divine soul's nature is to be nullified in its source, like a flame leaping to leave the wick: bitul is its nature, not an effort. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 19:7 (line 269): וזהו כלל בכל סטרא דקדושה, שאינו אלא מה שנמשך מחכמה, שנקרא ״קודש העליון״, הבטל במציאות באוראיןסוף ברוךהוא המלובש בו, ואינו דבר בפני עצמו Holiness is defined as what is nullified to Ein Sof and not a thing in itself; the divine soul's Chochmah is the place of Ein Sof in the soul. - Chanah Ariel, Yitro 2:10 (line 107): והרגשת עצמה אין לה כלל כי אין בה עדיין רק האור האלקי שהוא החיים שלה לבדו Homel: the soul has no self-feeling at all, because it holds only the divine light that is its life.

## S054 · The animal soul · נפש הבהמית

*Face F11 Darkness turned to light; also touches F07, F05. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The part of you that wants, grabs and feels most like a 'something' is not outside the unity. It is the very place where He is to be known, and turning it is where His Essence shows most.

**Hold.** 'The soul is revealed in the body to teach the animal soul that Havayah is Elokim, and to refine it.' (Derekh Mitzvotekha, Chametz and Matzah 6)

**The move.** Open it: every Jew has this second soul, from a husk that 'also has good in it', so it can be refined and not only fought. The Tzemach Tzedek calls it a 'something', and the divine soul was lowered into it to turn it; the Rebbe says the further Chassidus reaches down, to changing its very nature, the more its own essence shows. The Alter Rebbe's picture is the earth in which the seed grows; it is only a picture, for this earth has to be taught, and it can learn. Bring it down to the want that rises in you today, for food, for comfort, for the last word: that is the earth, and the lesson is for it, here, now: Havayah is Elokim.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'Havayah is Elokim', and in the pauses think: this is said to the part of me that wants. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The dignity of being the ground where it happens: nothing in you is left out of the work. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'Havayah is Elokim' with your lips before one meal today; eat that meal with a blessing before and after; and return during the day to 'Havayah is Elokim'.

**Where the sources differ.** Where this soul is rooted is disputed: the Alter Rebbe says its root is higher than the divine soul's, which is why it is the earth that grows the seed; the Rashab says its root is Tohu and the divine soul's is higher, which is why the divine soul can prevail.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 1:17 (line 62): כי בישראל נפש זו דקליפה, היא מקליפת נוגה, שיש בה גם כן טוב The animal soul is from kelipat nogah, which has good in it: it can be refined, not only fought. - Derekh Mitzvotekha, The commandment of disposing of Chamets; The commandment of eating Matzah 6 (line 54): שהנשמה נתגלית בגוף לגלות דעה את נפש הבהמית דנוגה כי הוי' הוא האלקים ולזככה The soul is revealed in the body to teach the animal soul that Havayah is Elokim: the animal soul is the place where the unity is to be known. - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 19:4 (line 106): עד להפיכת טבע נפש הבהמית ["חוצה" שבאדם The Rebbe: the lower Chassidus reaches, down to changing the animal soul's nature, the more its essence shows.

## S055 · The good and evil inclinations · יצר הטוב ויצר הרע

*Face F03 The hiding for the sake of revelation; also touches F11, F09. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 4, 7). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Even the voice in you that pulls against Him lives from Him and was set there by Him, so that it can be overcome. When it is held down below, His glory rises.

**Hold.** 'The animal soul wants the very opposite, for the man's benefit, that he overcome it.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 9)

**The move.** Open it: the opposing will is set up for your good; the Besht adds that the wise sense it is doing the King's will, His Name clothed even there, and the Ruzhiner that its very life is only a holy spark. And when it is held down below, 'the glory of the Holy One rises above all'. The Zohar's picture, in Tanya, is a king who sends a harlot to tempt his son so that the son will stand; it is only a picture, for the harlot only plays a part while the pull in you really wants the opposite, which is why the Rashab calls it your greatest enemy and the war in what you do is real. Bring it down to the pull you know best, late at night or at the sharp word: it is here, now, sent, and holding it down is where His glory rises.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'for the man's benefit, that he overcome it', and in the pauses think: sent to me, so that I stand. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The dignity of the struggle, and the relief that even what opposes you is not outside Him. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'I have set God before me always' when the pull comes today, which the Ruzhiner reads as including what stands against you; refuse the pull once, in one small thing; and return during the day to 'that I overcome it'.

**Where the sources differ.** Agent or enemy: the Besht and the Ruzhiner stress that it does His will; the Rashab that it wants your spiritual death, and the Maharash calls it a clever fox. Both are said: the union is in Him, the war is in conduct.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 9:8 (line 166): אך נפש הבהמית שמהקליפה, רצונה להפך ממש – לטובת האדם שיתגבר עליה וינצחנה, כמשל הזונה שבזהר הקדוש The opposing will is itself for the person's good: the Zohar's harlot sent by the king to test the prince. - Keter Shem Tov 1:82:1 (line 131): ויש אלא שהרגישו שהוא עושה רצון הבורא ושם מלובש שמו הקדוש The Besht: the yetzer acts like a rebel servant; the wise sense that it does the King's will and His holy Name is clothed there. - Kuntres Umaayan, מַאֲמָר טו. / פֶּרֶק ג:2 (line 313): והאדם צריך לדעת כי הנפש הבהמית והיצר הרע הם שונאיו היותר גדולים וחפצים במיתתו הרוחנית Counter-voice: the Rashab says the yetzer is one's greatest enemy, seeking his spiritual death; the warfare is real.

## S056 · The five levels of the soul · נפש רוח נשמה חיה יחידה

*Face F02 The higher and the lower unity; also touches F04, F06. Tie: stated (derivation steps 3). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** Both ways of seeing are in one soul. Most of you lives in the world and simply feels it is real; at the top is a point that simply feels it is nothing before Him.

**Hold.** 'Only the yechidah, which is above the worlds, feels simply that all the worlds are absolutely nothing.' (Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus 15)

**The move.** Open it: the four lower levels each live in a world, nefesh in Asiyah, ruach in Yetzirah, neshamah in Beriah and chayah in Atzilus. So what they feel, simply, is that the worlds exist, and that the worlds are being made from nothing is news to them; only the fifth, the yechidah, above all worlds, feels them as nothing. Bring it down to your own room today, the chair, the walls, the work ahead: they are real to you, and that is where you live and work. And at the top of the same you, here and now, is a point for which all of it is nothing before Him.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'the yechidah feels the worlds as nothing', and in the pauses think: this point is in me, now. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The room stays real; at the top of you, a point rests in the One. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'there is nothing besides Him' with your lips once today; give one coin to charity; and return during the day to 'real to me, nothing before Him'.

**Kept modest.** The anatomy of the levels carries no tie to the unity; the tie is stated only where the Rebbe relates them to feeling the worlds as real or as nothing, and calling that the lower and higher unity is our reading, so this meditation keeps to the Rebbe's words.

**Where the sources differ.** Other schools read the ladder differently: Krakow has the levels earned one by one, stopping at chayah; the Izhbitz anthology puts the never-blemished point at the neshamah, not the yechidah.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 14:5 (line 79): ד' הבחי' נרנ"ח, מכיון שהם עצמם נמצאים ב"עולם" [נפש – בעשי', רוח – ביצירה, נשמה – בבריאה וחי' – באצילות], הרי מה שנרגש אצלם בפשיטות הוא – מציאות העולמות The Rebbe: the four lower levels live inside the four worlds, so what they feel simply is the existence of the worlds. - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 15:1 (line 80): ורק בחי' היחידה שלמעלה מעולמות, נרגש אצלה בפשיטות שכל העולמות הם אין ואפס המוחלט Only the yechidah, above worlds, feels simply that the worlds are absolutely nothing. (Calling this the lower and higher unity inside the soul is our reading.)

## S057 · Yechidah and the essence of the soul · יחידה / עצם הנשמה

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One; also touches F05, F11. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** At the very center of you is a point that is always one with Him. Nothing you did and nothing done to you has reached it, and it leaves no room for anything besides Him.

**Hold.** 'From the side of the yechidah, which is always united with His Essence, no blemish or impurity applies.' (Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus 11)

**The move.** Open it: this is why Modeh Ani is said before washing the hands, and why it names none of His Names, since the essence of the soul is thanking the Essence, which no Name holds. The Mitteler Rebbe says this point is in every Jew: when even a person of small understanding is stirred on hearing of Him, that stirring is the yechidah. And the Alter Rebbe's way to it is plain: prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul with the Essence of Ein Sof. Bring it down to tomorrow morning, before the hands are washed and before the phone: the one who says 'I thank You' then is this point, in you, now, untouched by anything you did or anything done to you.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'always united, no blemish reaches it', and in the pauses think: this is said of me, now. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A core no one has ever touched; nothing to earn there and nothing to repair. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say Modeh Ani tomorrow on waking, before anything else, with this line in mind; pray one prayer today in simple faith, without calculation; and return during the day to 'always united'.

**Where the sources differ.** Where the untouched point lies is put differently: the Rebbe and the Mitteler Rebbe say the yechidah, one with the Essence, in every Jew; the Izhbitz anthology says the neshamah; the Sfas Emes speaks of an inner point that no stranger touches, but that is not always findable.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 11:5 (line 64): מצד היחידה שבנשמה שמיוחדת תמיד בעצמותו ית', אין שייך כל פגם וטומאה ח"ו The yechidah is always united with the Essence; no blemish reaches it (Modeh Ani before washing). - Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus, 14:1 (line 75): משא"כ בחי' היחידה, שכל ענינה הוא מה שהיא מיוחדת בעצמותו ית' – אין בה שום נתינת מקום לשום ענין זולתו ית' The yechidah is the soul's ein od milvado: it gives no room to anything besides Him. - Hayom Yom, תשרי / יא תשרי:1 (line 501): תפלה באמונה פשוטה איז מחבר עצמות הנשמה מיט עצמות א"ס The Alter Rebbe (5556): prayer in simple faith joins the essence of the soul with the Essence of Ein Sof.

## S058 · The descent of the soul into the body · ירידת הנשמה לגוף

*Face F07 The home below; also touches F11, F06. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Your soul was not dropped here by mistake. A part of Him was sent down into this body and this ordinary day on purpose, because this lowest place is where He wanted to live.

**Hold.** 'This descent is for the sake of an ascent: to raise to God the whole animal soul.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 31)

**The move.** Open it: Tanya first asks why He would lower a part of His light, before which all is as nothing, into what it calls a snake's skin, and answers that raising this life is the purpose of creation. The Rashab adds that the soul clothed in a body has something it lacked in the Garden of Eden, where it stood in total unity, because He desired a dwelling below. And Likkutei Torah says the ascent happens now, while the soul is in the body, not only later. Bring it down to an ordinary evening at the kitchen sink, tired: this is where the part of His light was sent, and you, here, now, are the errand, not a mistake.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'a descent for the sake of an ascent', and in the pauses think: sent here, for this. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The steadiness of someone on an errand: sent, not lost. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'this descent is for the sake of an ascent' with your lips once today; do one Jew one favor, in something material or of the spirit, since the Besht said a soul may come down for seventy or eighty years for that alone; and return during the day to 'descent for the sake of ascent'.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 31:17 (line 441): ולמה עשה ה׳ כזאת, להוריד חלק מאורו יתברך, הממלא וסובב כל עלמין וכולא קמיה כלא חשיב, והלבישו ב״משכא דחויא״ Why would He lower a part of His light, which fills and surrounds all and before which all is nothing, into a snake's skin? - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 31:17 (line 441): אין זה, כי אם ירידה זו – היא צורך עליה, להעלות לה׳ כל נפש החיונית הבהמית The answer: the descent is for an ascent, raising the animal soul, which is 'the purpose of creation'. - Kuntres Umaayan, מַאֲמָר טו. / פֶּרֶק ג:1 (line 312): ומעלת ירידת הנשמה בהתלבשותה בגוף גשמי ונפש הטבעי לגבי מעלת עמידתה בגן עדן בתכלית היחוד והביטול לאלקות The Rashab: the soul's descent into a body surpasses its standing in Eden in total unity and bitul, because the purpose is the dwelling below (Tanya 36).

## S059 · The root of the soul · שורש הנשמה

*Face F06 The soul as part of the One; also touches F02, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 4, 5). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** Above, where souls are rooted, you and the person you find hardest are not two. Only the bodies are divided.

**Hold.** 'All Israel are called brothers, literally, from the root of their souls in the One God; only the bodies are divided.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 32)

**The move.** Open it: all souls are matched and have one Father, and Ein Sof rests on the source of souls only when they are one in One. Nothing physical or spiritual separates before Him; only going against His will cuts a soul's rope, and the Mitteler Rebbe says its highest part, the mazal, stays attached above always. Tanya's picture is a rope, one end tied above and the other down here; it is only a picture, for in truth nothing separates before Him and there is no distance for a rope to cross. Bring it down to one ordinary person who irritates you, not someone who has harmed you: here, now, that person's root and yours are in the same One.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'brothers, literally, from the root in the One God', and in the pauses think of that one person. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Kinship that does not wait for liking. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'Hear, O Israel, the Lord is One' today with that person in mind; do them one small kindness, or, as the Besht teaches, include them with yourself in your prayer; and return during the day to 'one Father'.

## S060 · Tzaddik, beinoni and rasha · צדיק בינוני ורשע

*Face F11 Darkness turned to light; also touches F08, F03. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 3, 4). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** You do not have to be finished to give Him joy. There are two kinds of delight before Him: from those who have turned the dark to light, and from those who hold it down while it is still strong.

**Hold.** 'There are two kinds of delight before Him: when the other side is wholly turned, by the tzaddikim, and when it is subdued while it is still strong.' (Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 27)

**The move.** Open it: Tanya measures people not by counting deeds but by the two souls and which one rules. The tzaddik has turned his dark to light; the beinoni still feels the pull and does not let it into deed, speech or thought, and that is 'the measure of every man', open to anyone at any time. And when the other side is held down below, 'the glory of the Holy One rises above all'. Bring it down to one thing you did not do today that you wanted to, the word you swallowed or the thing you put back: that holding back, here, now, is one of the two delights before Him.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'subdued while it is still strong', and in the pauses think: this too is a delight before Him. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** The dignity of a war that is itself the gift. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'His glory rises above all' with your lips when you hold back one thing today; make that holding back, in one word, one look or one helping, your mitzvah of the day; and return during the day to 'while it is still strong'.

**Kept modest.** The tie is stated only where Tanya speaks of the two delights and of turning dark to light (chapters 10 and 27); chapters 12 to 14 are mostly a portrait of the beinoni, so this meditation stays with the delight.

**Where the sources differ.** Tanya 27 gives the two delights without ranking them; the Rebbe says the delight from one who returns in teshuvah is greater than the tzaddik's, being something new from below.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 14:1 (line 210): והנה , מדת הבינוני, היא מדת כל אדם ואחריה כל אדם ימשוך, שכל אדם יכול להיות בינוני בכל עת ובכל שעה The beinoni is the measure everyone can reach at any time. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 27:8 (line 365): ושני מיני נחת רוח לפניו יתברך למעלה: אחד – מביטול הסטרא אחרא לגמרי, ואתהפכא ממרירו למתקא ומחשוכא לנהורא, על ידי הצדיקים. והשנית – כד אתכפיא הסטרא אחרא בעודה בתקפה Two kinds of delight above: the tzaddik's transformation and the beinoni's subduing. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 27:5 (line 362): כד אתכפיא סטרא אחרא לתתא, דאסתלק יקרא דקודשאבריךהוא לעילא על כולא When the other side is subdued below, His glory rises above all: the beinoni's war reveals the unity.

## S061 · Free choice · בחירה

*Face F05 The Essence beyond both; also touches F04, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 4, 7). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** The one thing in you that nothing forces is a trace of Him. Only He is truly free, and He placed that freedom in the person made in His image.

**Hold.** 'Truly free choice belongs only to the Creator Himself, whom nothing compels.' (the Rebbe, Likkutei Sichos 31)

**The move.** Open it: every creature is pushed by something, its nature, its mind or its desire, and since free choice belongs only to the Essence, below it has a place only in the person made 'in Our image'. Tanya marks where it lives: in deed, speech and thought, even against what the heart wants, while loving the good and loathing the evil in the heart is not fully in one's hands. And the Rebbe says every Jew has the free choice to do teshuvah. Bring it down to one small choice you will face today that could go either way, with nothing forcing your hand: when you make it, you, here, now, are using something that is His alone.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'only the Creator, whom nothing compels', and in the pauses think: and He placed it in me. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** A moment where nothing pushes; a freedom that is His, placed in you. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'let us make man in Our image' with your lips once today; use the choice for one act of teshuvah, setting one small thing right; and return during the day to 'in His image'.

**Where the sources differ.** The Izhbitzer pulls the other way: choice is 'only a small thing, like a garlic peel', all is as He wills, and even Adam's sin was by His will, above Adam's knowledge. The Chabad sources root choice in the Essence; Izhbitz nearly dissolves it into His will.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Likkutei Sichos, כרך לא / הוספות / יתרו / 256:3 (line 6056): כי בחירה חפשית לגמרי אינה אלא בבורא עצמו, שהוא אין דבר מכריחו The Rebbe: truly free choice belongs only to the Creator Himself, whom nothing compels. - Likkutei Sichos, כרך לא / הוספות / יתרו / 256:3 (line 6056): וכיון שאין בחירה חפשית שייכת אלא במהות ועצמות א"ס ב"ה שהוא תכלית השלימות, הרי גם למטה אין לזה מקום אלא באדם הנעשה בצלמנו כדמותנו Since free choice belongs only to the Essence, below it exists only in the man made in His image. - Tanya, Part I; Likkutei Amarim 14:1 (line 210): שבהם הבחירה והיכולת והרשות נתונה לכל אדם לעשות ולדבר ולחשוב גם מה שהוא נגד תאות לבו והפכה ממש Choice is given in deed, speech and thought, even against the heart's desire.

## S062 · Reincarnation · גלגול

*Face F10 Torah and mitzvah: the unity made actual; also touches F11, F06. Tie: stated (derivation steps 1, 2, 4). Thin: yes.*

**The glimpse.** A created soul cannot hold Him bare; it holds Him through the garments of His mitzvot. Each mitzvah is a thread of the garment your soul came here, perhaps not for the first time, to finish.

**Hold.** 'Every Jew must come in many incarnations until he fulfils all 613 mitzvot in thought, speech and deed, to complete the garments of his soul.' (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 29)

**The move.** Open it: the soul is a created thing, and no creature can grasp the Creator, so only clothed in the garments of Torah and mitzvot can it bear His light; the garments are woven of thought, speech and deed. The Rebbe writes that souls now come to complete what they lacked before. Tanya's picture is a garment; it is only a picture, for an ordinary garment comes between, and these are what let the soul hold Him at all. Bring it down to the small mitzvah you do so often you hardly see it, the blessing over bread or the coin for charity: here, now, it is a thread of the garment in which your soul holds Him.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'to complete the garments of his soul', and in the pauses think: this mitzvah is a thread. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being clothed, and so able to hold what could not be held bare. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say the blessing over one mitzvah today slowly, with your lips; do one mitzvah that you rarely do, in thought, speech and deed; and return during the day to 'a thread of the garment'.

**Kept modest.** Gilgul is received from the Ari, not derived from the unity; the tie is stated only through the garments of the mitzvot (Iggeret HaKodesh 29) and the refining of sparks (Kuntres Acharon 5), so this meditation stays with the garments.

**Where the sources differ.** What gilgul is for is put differently: the Ari via Tanya, to complete the garments and refine the sparks; the Besht, a tzaddik's spirit in one's enemies, sweetened by prayer; R. Nachman, souls born of longing and speech that roll on. The Rebbe notes that some medieval thinkers rejected the doctrine.

## S063 · Man as a small world · אדם עולם קטן

*Face F01 The world said into being now; also touches F07, F02. Tie: stated (derivation steps 2, 3, 4). Thin: no.*

**The glimpse.** You know a body is alive without ever seeing its soul; the world is alive the same way, with a life you never see. And the world is more one with its Source than a body is with its soul.

**Hold.** 'Man is a small world: all that is in the world is in man.' (the Maharash, Toras Shmuel 5631, after Avot deRabbi Natan)

**The move.** Open it: as we know a soul gives life to a body because the body is alive, so we know a hidden life gives life to the world; and of both we know only that it is, never what it is. Shaar HaYichud breaks the picture exactly at the unity: soul and body are separate in their root, the body coming from the parents and from food, while heaven and earth come wholly from His word and stay nullified in it. So the world is more one with its Source than a body is with its soul. Bring it down to the street outside your window, here, now: it is alive with a life you do not see, and held by it more closely than you are held by your own soul.

**Stay.** Say it again slowly, 'man is a small world', and in the pauses think: the world is more one with Him than a body with its soul. When the mind drifts, come back to the line without arguing with the stray thought.

**From inside.** Being held from inside, more closely than a body by its soul. If nothing is felt, the thinking still counts.

**Back into the day.** Say 'from my flesh I see God' with your lips once today; set one small thing in yourself right, a habit or a word, since Likkutei Torah says that in fixing his four elements a person fixes the whole world; and return during the day to 'a small world'.

**Anchors** (verified, from `subjects/K05.json`): - Toras Shmuel, תרל"א ח"א / מאמרים / [ואתה הרם את מטך] / רצג:2 (line 3438): כי האדם הוא עולם קטן וכמ"ש באדר"נ שכל מה שיש בעולם יש באדם, ולכן כמו באדם אנו מכירים שיש בו נפש המחי' אותו The Maharash: as we know a soul enlivens the body, we know a spiritual life enlivens the world. - Toras Shmuel, תרל"א ח"א / מאמרים / [ואתה הרם את מטך] / רצג:2 (line 3438): כן בחיות המחי' את העולם אנו יודעים רק בבחי' ידיעת המציאות לבד ולא השגת המהות כלל The limit: we know that it is, never what it is, of the soul and of the world's life. - Tanya, Part II; Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah 6:7 (line 793): אך באמת, אין המשל דומה לנמשל כלל, כי הנשמה והגוף הם באמת נפרדים זה מזה בשרשם The analogy fails: soul and body are separate in root; the world and its Source are not.
