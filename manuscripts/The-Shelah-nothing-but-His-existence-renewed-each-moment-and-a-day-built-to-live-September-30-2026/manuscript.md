## The short answer

The Shelah is the great bridge between Safed and the Chassidic masters. He gathers the Zohar, the Ramak, the early Ari circle, the *Reshit Chochmah* and the *Chovot HaLevavot* into one book of practice, and the Chassidic masters read him daily. His unity has three parts.

1. **There is no existence but His.** "There is no other reality in the world but His reality, for if He hid it, all would be lost" (line 3938). "Nothing exists except His existence, for He brings all into being and gives all life" (line 4477).
2. **He renews it at every moment.** "If He held it back one moment, all would be as if it had never been" (lines 878, 3938, 7506, 10858). The whole world is His guest at every instant (line 6566).
3. **Therefore any power of its own is a second god.** Whoever "gives anything power of its own makes two domains" (line 607). The same logic runs through his ethics. Anger comes "from an alien god" (line 2561). A lie denies that He is One, because "truth is His seal, to make known that He is one" (line 10016). The only thing that separates us from Him is sin (line 4481).

Then the path. The book is less a theory than a daily order: the intention said aloud before each act, the prayer before work, eating "before the King", the nightly search and release, a small Shabbat each day, weekly seclusion with G-d, and return that is always open and never harsh. Its goal is the Midrash the Tanya builds on: "He longed to have a dwelling in the lower worlds" (line 8779).

> אלא רצה לומר, שאין עוד מציאות בעולם זולת מציאותו יתברך, כי בהסתרו יאבד הכל (line 3938)

> ומי שאינו מאמין כן, ומפריד ונותן לאיזה דבר כח לעצמו, ח"ו עושה שתי רשויות (line 607)

> כי הרצון מורה על אחדותו יתברך, והכעס הוא נמשך מאל זר (line 2561)

## His core teaching on the unity

**No existence apart from His.** The Shelah reads "there is none else" as a fact of being, not only of power. "What is in the heavens above and on the earth below has no other existence apart from Him" (ומה שיש בשמים ממעל ועל הארץ מתחת אין לו עוד מציאות זולתו יתברך, line 7626). "There is no existence at all that is apart from Him, for all is from Him" (כלומר אין בנמצא שום מציאות שיהיה מזולתו כי הכל מאתו יתברך, line 8709). "He is all; He alone is all; He is one without division" (הוא כלא הוא לבדו כלא הוא חד בלי פרודא, line 10242, from the Zohar).

**Clinging, not sameness.** Things differ only in how close they cling. "Only that this existence cleaves to Him more, and from this existence another existence chains down" (רק שזה המציאות דבק בו יותר, וממציאות זה משתלשל מציאות אחר, line 4477). So:

> ואין הבדל בינו ית' לבינינו, כי אנחנו תכלית המציאות והכל ממציאותו, והעונות הם מבדילים (line 4481)

"There is no separation between Him and us, for we are the goal of existence and all is from His existence, and sins are what separate." And "the higher a thing is, the more it is one" (כי כל הגבוה ביותר יותר מתאחד, line 7763).

**Renewed every moment.** This is his most repeated line, and it is the first chapters of *Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah* in outline:

> הש"י מחדש בטובו בכל יום תמיד מעשה בראשית בכוונה מכוונת שופע שפעו, ואלו היה מונע רגע אחד היה הכל כלא היה בטל המציאות (line 878)

The last range puts it in the Chabad words: "He brings all of them into being, gives them all life, and pours into them always" (והוא מהוה את כולם ומחיה את כולם ומשפיע בהם תמיד, line 10861). And in the *Tomer Devorah* form: no one ever sinned against Him without Him giving the sinner life "in that very moment" (שלא יחייהו הוא יתברך באותה רגע ממש, line 10828).

**Limit made by the Unlimited, as it were.** His one full statement on tzimtzum (line 8095) is non-literal and has all of the Chabad points: "by His great power without limit He brought limit into being" (ובכחו הגדול מבלי גבול המציא גבול); "as it were, He contracted Himself to bring limit into being, for the whole earth is full of His glory" (וכביכול צמצם בעצמו להמציא גבול כי מלא כל הארץ כבודו); and He is still "within them and surrounds them without end or limit", while "the showing of His glory in all beings He contracted to the measure of the creatures" (הוא בתוכם ומקיף אותם בלי סוף ומבלי תכלית, וגילוי כבודו בכל הנמצאים הוא צמצם בערך הנבראים).

**The place of the world.** "He is the place of the world and the world is not His place" (שהוא מקומו של עולם ואין עולמו מקומו, line 6921). "He does not clothe Himself to be in a place, not even by way of clothing" (כי אין מתלבש להיות במקום אפילו בדרך התלבשות, line 6923). He is "set apart from all three worlds; and still the whole earth is full of His glory" (שהוא נבדל מכל שלשה העולמות. מכל מקום מלא כל הארץ כבודו, line 9374).

**Known by His acts, never in Himself.** Before the Will "we cannot speak any letter or hint, only 'necessary being', and even this by negation and not by affirmation" (line 7758). "The Name of the Essence ... is not grasped" (line 8119). "Whoever grasps most grasps most that He cannot be grasped" (על כן המשיג ביותר הוא ביותר משיג שאין להשיגו, line 9700).

**Our words are borrowed from His.** The Shelah reverses the Rambam's borrowed names. "The limbs of the chariot carry their names in Him essentially; the names of our limbs are borrowed" (line 263). And at the end: "the plain sense of things, that is, their essence, is above, and what we take as the plain sense is a garment" (נמצא פשוטן של דברים כלומר עצמיותיהן הוא למעלה ומה שאנחנו סוברין שהוא פשוטו הוא התלבשות, line 10699). "The whole Torah is His Names" (line 10697).

**Truth and oneness are one thing.** "So truth and oneness are one thing" (נמצא האמת והאחדות הוא ענין אחד, line 1550). A lie has many forms; the truth "is only one thing, what it is" (line 10016). His son makes this a proof: G-d created all "except falsehood and wrong" (line 10918), so a liar implies another creator, "and then there would be two powers" (ואיכא שני רשויות, line 10918).

**Why a world.** From "the fullness of His goodness, to do good to another" (בנדבה בלי הכרח, רק משלימות מדת טובו להטיב לזולתו, line 2561). He longed for a dwelling below (line 8779), "and more: what is below is dearer than what is above" (line 8370). "More than the calf wants to suck, the cow wants to nurse" (line 8782). The soul is "a part of G-d above" (line 8138), and He is "ready at every time and every moment" for our return (line 8139).

## The states and meditations he adds

The range reports propose many states and practices, and many repeat across ranges. The merged list, 32 states and 43 techniques, is in the JSON below. The strongest, by kind:

**States of being held.** - *SH-S01 A guest this instant.* The whole world is His guest; if He did not take it in for the blink of an eye, all would be lost (line 6566). - *SH-S02 Given life while I fall.* He gives the one who sins against Him life at that very moment (line 10828). - *SH-S03 All things inside His oneness.* "A single Master with no second is one in them, and they are in Your oneness" (line 10803). - *SH-S04 The word in the bread.* The food lives by the word of G-d still speaking in it (line 1728). - *SH-S05 Each breath a letter.* Every breath is a letter of the Name, given now (lines 1458-1462).

**States of the heart before G-d.** - *SH-S06 Eyes below, heart above.* "What am I", then gladness to speak with Him (line 9129). - *SH-S07 Wanted more than wanting.* He wants to give more than I want to receive (line 8782). - *SH-S08 Sitting in the light.* Weekly seclusion, the light of the Presence around the head (lines 5542-5545). - *SH-S09 Mouth and heart equal.* No gap between what I say and what I mean (line 1576). - *SH-S10 Good will as the unity felt.* Anger is a second power; good will is oneness felt (line 2561). - *SH-S11 Humility as height.* "Lowliness is one with the height of the soul" (line 10830). - *SH-S12 Grasping that He cannot be grasped* (line 9700). - *SH-S13 Trust as cleaving.* "One who sets his affairs on trust in G-d truly cleaves to G-d" (line 1602). - *SH-S14 Nothing between us but sin; return always open* (lines 4481, 5287, 8139).

**Practices of the mind.** - *SH-T01 Up the worlds and back down.* Climb in thought to awe, come down to see Him spreading through all (lines 1048, 1142, 10861). - *SH-T02 Know it in the heart.* Faith from tradition "is not enough, only knowledge in the heart" (line 7491). - *SH-T03 Gazing at the flame.* The white and the dark light of one flame (lines 526-529). - *SH-T04 Read the tempter's inner aim.* "The inside of the bad is the best good" (lines 1546-1548). - *SH-T05 Perhaps G-d is testing me now* (line 6665); *meant for good* (lines 7155-7156).

**Practices of the day.** - *SH-T10 Name the aim before the act*, and "with G-d's help" over every plan (lines 1363, 3352, 9259). - *SH-T11 The prayer before work*, and loss is for good (lines 2771-2772). - *SH-T12 Eat before the King*; stop while still wanting (lines 1958, 1968, 2056). - *SH-T13 A small Shabbat every day*, an hour set apart (lines 3349, 8407, 10858). - *SH-T14 Cling in every act*: name the act, the Giver, the purpose (line 9682). - *SH-T15 Hidden with Him, then out to do good* (lines 10012, 10213). - *SH-T16 Weekly seclusion*: speak softly to G-d as a son to a father (lines 5542-5545).

## Where he meets Chabad, and where he parts

**Where they agree.** - *Renewal every moment.* Lines 878, 3938, 7506, 10858 and 10861 are *Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah* ch. 1 to 2 in outline, down to "brings them into being and gives them life". - *Tzimtzum.* Line 8095 is non-literal and holds all four Chabad points: "as it were"; for the sake of limit; to the measure of the creatures; and He is still within and around all. This is *Shaar HaYichud* ch. 7 and Tanya ch. 48 before the fact. Line 2863 ("hidden within, like the king in his palace") assumes the same reading: hiding, not removal. - *The dwelling below.* Lines 8779, 8370 and 778 are the Midrash Tanya ch. 36 builds on, and the Shelah reads it the same way. - *The soul.* "A part of G-d above" (line 8138) is Tanya ch. 2 word for word. - *Language.* Our words borrowed from His (lines 263, 3827, 10699) is the root of *Shaar HaYichud* ch. 1 and 10 to 11: the letters of the Ten Utterances stand above and the thing is their garment. - *"All is as nothing before Him."* Line 9699 (the Zohar's "all before Him counts as nothing") and line 10861 ("as nothing before the Throne") are the source of Tanya's *bittul*. - *Joy, not sadness.* Joy in the unity (line 5991), with the poor (line 6137), at each mitzvah as a gift (lines 2645, 5689). - *Insult as atonement.* Line 9760 is *Iggeret HaKodesh* 25, with the same verse about Shimi.

**Where they part, or where Chabad goes further.** - *"Not other" versus "nothing".* The Shelah's *ein od* is that nothing is apart from Him and nothing has force of its own (lines 607, 7626). The Tanya adds that from His side the world is truly nothing, like the sun's light inside the sun (*Shaar HaYichud* ch. 3, 6). The Shelah stands with the Ramak here; Chabad goes one step further. - *Is G-d touched by our deeds?* The Shelah records the argument among the kabbalists and divides it: the upper three are untouched, the "building" below really is affected (lines 672-731, 726). Chabad keeps the Essence untouched and puts the effect in the drawing down of light. His later ranges (line 10243, "the damage is not in Him") come closer to Chabad. - *Fasting and self-affliction.* The Shelah passes on the fasting programs of the penitential books (lines 4961-4968, 10842, 10843, 10847, 10857), even while warning "it is not right to afflict one's soul and mortify one's body without need" (כי אין נכון לענות נפשו ולסגף גופו שלא לצורך, line 5302). Chabad (*Iggeret HaTeshuvah* ch. 2 to 3) replaces fasts with charity and joy. The guide follows Chabad and his own guard, and never prescribes self-affliction. - *Particular passages.* In *Toldot Adam* he follows the Zohar in reading "adam" as Israel alone (lines 177, 193). The guide (D28) does not carry this. It takes his own claim that "adam" means the inner form (line 177) and applies it to every person. - *Drink.* He defends sages who drank at mitzvah meals and warns not to teach it (lines 1987-1993, 6138). The guide never uses drink.

**The Rambam.** The Shelah keeps the Rambam's negative attributes for the Essence (lines 6062, 7758, 8119, 9700) and puts the positive ones in the sefirot, which is exactly the Chabad settlement (*Shaar HaYichud* ch. 9 to 10). He takes the Rambam's "knowledge, knower and known" (line 6312), as Tanya ch. 2 and 48 do, and defends his count of the commandments (line 6215). He parts from him, with the Kabbalists and with Chabad, on three points: the world to come is body and soul (lines 469-519, 4438, 10114, 10206); Israel heard the words at Sinai, not only a created sound (lines 4530-4541); and evil is not mere absence (line 4480). And he reverses the Rambam on the body words: they are His names first and ours by borrowing (line 263).

**The Ramak's "He is all that exists, and not all that exists is He."** The Shelah holds both halves in almost every range. First half: "all that exists, bodily or spiritual, all is from His reality" (וכל מה שיש בנמצא, הן גשמי הן רוחני, הכל הוא ממציאותו, line 3268); "He is all; He alone is all" (line 10242); He is "within them" (line 8095). Second half: "He does not clothe Himself to be in a place" and "the horse is secondary to the rider" (lines 6923, 6925); "He is the place of the world and the world is not His place" (line 6921); the lower things are His "as they cling", and "sins are what separate" (line 4481); "set apart from all three worlds" (line 9374). Chabad keeps the first half and sharpens it (nothing apart from Him), and keeps the second as a guard (the world as it looks, many and limited, is not He).

## What the guide should take (ours)

1. **The sentence.** "There is no existence but His; He renews it every moment; if He held back one moment all would be as if it never was" (lines 878, 3938, 4477). In the Shelah's plain words it can be said to anyone, before any Chassidic terms.
2. **The unity as ethics.** Anger is a second power (line 2561); a lie denies the One (lines 10016, 10918); giving anything power of its own is two domains (line 607). This lets the guide teach the unity through daily conduct: good will, truth, trust.
3. **Held while falling.** He gives life even in the moment of sin (line 10828); the gate of return is always open, and He is "ready at every moment" (lines 5287, 8139). Return is grace and never harsh (line 5302).
4. **The day as the practice.** The Shelah gives the fullest daily order in the library: morning intention, say it before you do it, the prayer before work, eat before the King, cling in every act, a small Shabbat each day, the evening return, and the nightly release. The guide should offer it as a menu, not a burden, with his own minimum: even one who cannot learn or give can hold "return and prayer" (line 812).
5. **Weekly seclusion, spoken softly.** One of his most valuable gifts: once a week, alone, speak to G-d "as a son to his father", sitting in the light (lines 5542-5545).
6. **Up the worlds and back down.** His own contemplation: up to awe, down to presence (lines 1048, 1142, 10861).
7. **Eyes below, heart above.** The simplest form of prayer's two movements (line 9129); with the son's "heaven, then the Temple" (line 10895).
8. **Our words are borrowed from above.** A doorway into *Shaar HaYichud*'s letters (lines 263, 10699).
9. **Humility as the root and the height** (lines 781-787, 9700, 10830), and **praise and blame equal** (line 9716).
10. **Leave aside.** The fasting schedules and lashes; the sefirot tables and kavanot of the Ari's circle as practice; the Kabbalah of sin's damage above; the particularist passages; everything to do with drink.
