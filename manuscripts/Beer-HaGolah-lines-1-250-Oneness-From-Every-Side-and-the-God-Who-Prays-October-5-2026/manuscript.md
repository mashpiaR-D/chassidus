Be'er HaGolah (The Well of the Exile) defends the words of the sages against seven complaints. Each "well" answers one complaint. This first half covers the Introduction, Wells 1-3 (rabbinic laws, laws that look strange, and readings of verses that look forced), and the first part of Well 4 (aggadot that seem to say strange things about G-d).

Four teachings in this half matter most for the guide:

- **Knowing your lack is a level.** "Living flesh feels the scalpel, and dead flesh does not" (11). To feel what you lack is already a sign of life.
- **Oneness cancels harm.** Harmful forces rule only where there is twoness. "When a person cleaves to His oneness, His oneness cancels the power of the harmful ones" (91). G-d alone is "fully one from every side".
- **He is the true good, so He seeks to give.** "G-d prays" means His wish to do good. "Whatever is good seeks to do good" (207). He acts by will, not by blind necessity.
- **He lifts the small.** "This is G-d's way: He gives a rise in place of a lowering" (214). "He wants smallness" (217). And the change is always on the side of the receiver (221, 226).

## 2.1 Fully one from every side

The bedtime Shema is "a two-edged sword" against harmful forces. Harmful forces are "secondary" things, not the core of creation. They have power only where there is twoness. So: "וכאשר האדם מתדבק באחדותו יתברך, האחדות שלו מבטל כח המזיקים, שאין בהם רק השניות" (91, Well 2 4:8): "when a person cleaves to His oneness, His oneness cancels the power of the harmful ones, which have only twoness." Other things have some oneness too, but only in one respect. A king is one as king, not as a man. "אבל השם יתברך אחד לגמרי מכל צד" (91): "but G-d is fully one from every side."

The same rule comes back with the doubtful plague. A plague is an accident, not the core of a thing. "השם יתברך אשר הוא מחויב המציאות, רחוק ממנו דבר שהוא במקרה" (232, Well 4 5:6): "G-d, who is the Necessary Being, is far from anything that is by accident." And "השם יתברך, שהוא אחד לגמרי ואין זולתו, הוא מחויב המציאות לגמרי" (233, Well 4 5:7): "G-d, who is fully one and there is none besides Him, is fully the Necessary Being." Whatever is one in some way is near Him: "ומי שהוא אחד בדבר מה" (233): "and whoever is one in some way" has nearness to the Necessary Being. So "יש לקרוא קריאת שמע לסלוק הפגעים" (234, Well 4 5:8): "one should read the Shema to remove harms."

## 2.2 None besides Him, so a lie has no legs

"כל המחליף בדיבורו כאלו עובד עבודה זרה" (198, Well 4 1:20): "whoever changes his word is as if he worships idols." Why? Because He is One, "אפס זולתו, והוא משקר לעשות אלהים שהוא שקר" (198): "there is none besides Him, and he lies to make a god that is a lie." "השקר אין לו רגלים" (198): "a lie has no legs." A lie has no being at all, so it is the opposite of the One who alone is. For the same reason liars do not receive the Shechina (199): two opposites do not join.

## 2.3 He is the true good, and He prays

The Talmud says G-d prays: "יהי רצון מלפני שיכבשו רחמיי את כעסי" (202, Well 4 2:1): "may it be My will that My mercy conquer My anger." The Maharal explains: "מפני שהוא יתברך הטוב האמיתי" (204, Well 4 2:3): "because He is the true good," He seeks good. "כל אשר הוא טוב מבקש לפעול הטוב" (207, Well 4 2:6): "whatever is good seeks to do good." So His "prayer" is His own wish to give. Against the philosophers who say He acts by blind necessity, the Maharal says He acts by will and aim (210). That is why there is kindness beyond strict law.

## 2.4 He evens things out, and He wants the small

On the new-moon offering, "an atonement for Me for making the moon small": "זה מדת השם יתברך שנותן מעלה תחת שפלות שהגיע לו, עד שהכל שוה" (214, Well 4 3:2): "this is G-d's way: He gives a rise in place of a lowering that came to a thing, until all is even." He is the One "אשר מקרב השפלים והקטנים" (215, Well 4 3:3): "who brings the low and the small near." Sinai was the smallest mountain. "שהוא יתברך חפץ בקטנות" (217, Well 4 3:5): "that He wants smallness." And "הקטנות הוא מדת הצדק" (217): "smallness is the trait of the righteous."

## 2.5 The change is on the receiver's side

"It is not in heaven": the sages ruled against a heavenly voice, and G-d said "My children have won Me." This is not a change in Him. "המקבל גם כן הוא שלו" (221, Well 4 4:3): "the receiver too is His." He is "כמו האב שבניו מנצחים אותו" (226, Well 4 4:8): "like the father whose children win over him," and the father does their will. The same key opens "I will not forget you": "דבר שהוא מצד השם יתברך אין הסרה וסלוק" (157, Well 3 2:13): "what is from G-d's side has no removing and no taking away." Only what is from the receiver's side can in theory be lost.

## 2.6 One world, with no opposition

"Let us make man": G-d consulted the heavenly court "עד שלפי זה יהיה העולם אחד, ואין בו התנגדות" (229, Well 4 5:3): "so that by this the world will be one, with no opposition in it." And His Name makes everything: "כל הדברים שמו יתברך פועל אותם" (87, Well 2 4:4): "His Name makes all things."

## 2.7 Why He made the world

G-d "wears tefillin": the whole world is His garment, "שהכל ברא לכבודו" (239, Well 4 6:2): "that He made all for His honor." "ברוב עם הדרת מלך" (245, Well 4 6:8): "in the many people is the King's majesty": when many praise Him, they clothe Him in glory. Why create at all? Not to show power. "העולם נברא לעצמו" (249, Well 4 6:12): "the world was made for its own sake," so that the caused could add praise to the Cause from the other's side. "כל פעל ה' למענהו" (250, Well 4 6:13): "G-d made all for His sake."

**Proposed MH-S23 "Living flesh feels".** Inside: a clear, quiet pain at how far you are, felt as a sign of life and not as despair. The first generations had hearts wide as the Temple hall; ours are like the eye of a needle. Even so: "המכיר והמרגיש בחסרון אשר לו, הרי הוא מרגיש ומכיר המעלה" (11, Introduction 11): "one who knows and feels his own lack, feels and knows the higher level." "בשר החי הוא מרגיש באיזמל, ובשר המת אינו מרגיש באיזמל" (11): "living flesh feels the scalpel, and dead flesh does not feel the scalpel." "הכרת חסרוננו הוא המדריגה אשר יש לנו" (11): "knowing our lack is the level we have." How to enter: when you feel small or far, do not argue with the feeling. Say: "I feel it, so I am alive to it." Then turn toward Him. Source: Introduction 11. Map: near S13 The unnamed longing, S59 True lowliness and MH-S13 Back to the beginning; the opposite of S03 Numb, the stone heart; guard: this is not self-blame.

**Proposed MH-S24 "Covered by the One".** Inside: at night, or when afraid, the sense that what harms has no real hold, because it lives only on twoness, and you are held in what is one from every side. Source: 91, 232-234. How to enter: the bedtime Shema said slowly, resting on the word "Echad" (One), with the thought "He is fully one from every side; I am inside that." Map: near S62 Only Him, S67 Surrounded, S84 Trust and MH-S3 Silence of trust; the answer to S21 Danger. Guard: this is not a cure for illness or danger; take normal care too.

## 4.1 The Shema against twoness (`bittul`; new: `mh_shema_against_twoness`)

At bedtime, or when fear comes, read the Shema and lean on "Echad". The ground is 91: "His oneness cancels the power of the harmful ones, which have only twoness", and 234: "one should read the Shema to remove harms." What to hold in mind: He is "fully one from every side" (91). Fear feeds on "two": me here, the threat there. Put both inside the One. This is not magic and does not replace care or a doctor.

## 4.2 Holy in what is allowed (`act`; new: `mh_holy_in_the_permitted`)

"קדש עצמך במותר לך" (47, Well 1 4:3): "make yourself holy in what is permitted to you." Pick one allowed thing each day (a second helping, a word you could say, a scroll on the phone) and hold back a little, for Him. The Maharal ties adding on yourself to love (45): it "shows that he serves from love."

## 4.3 Start with merit (`heart_after`; new: `mh_start_with_merit`)

A court "opens with merit". The reason is about G-d's goodness: "מן הצדיק והישר אינו בא ממנו בעצם רק הטוב" (81, Well 2 3:3): "from the righteous and upright, of itself only good comes." Guilt comes only by accident. Practice: when you judge someone (or yourself), say the case for merit first, out loud or in writing, before any charge. The warning at 75: "כל החושד בכשרים לוקה בגופו": "whoever suspects the upright is struck in his body": a bad thought not received comes back on the thinker.

## 4.4 Forgive when asked (`heart_after`; with `mh_forgive_before_sleep`)

The scholar holds justice in the heart, but "אם בקש שימחול, אין זה וויתור, ומחויב למחול לו" (99, Well 2 5:3): "if he asks to be forgiven, this is not letting things slide, and he must forgive him." "כל המעביר על מדותיו מעבירין לו על כל פשעיו" (97, Well 2 5:1): "whoever passes over his traits, all his sins are passed over."

## 4.5 Give from what He gave (`act`; `mh_todah_all_to_him`)

"שיתן האדם אל השם יתברך ממה שנתן לו" (108, Well 2 7:2): "that a person give to G-d from what He gave him." Before giving charity, say: "This came from You; I give a part of it back." Money is not the self: "תורה ומעשים טובים מלוין אותו" (105, Well 2 6:4): "Torah and good deeds go with him."

## 4.6 Bless the fire (`before_whom`)

At havdalah we bless on fire because man brought it out from two stones: "כי הברכה הוא אל השם יתברך שהוסיף שלימות העולם" (128, Well 2 10:3): "for the blessing is to G-d, who added wholeness to the world." Practice: when you finish some work that brings a new good into being, bless Him for adding wholeness through you.

## 4.7 Draw near the near ones (`act`)

The ladder at 132-135: love your neighbors, bring your relatives close, lend to the poor in his need even when you yourself lack. "אז תקרא וה' יענה" (135, Well 2 11:4): "then you will call and G-d will answer." One who draws the near ones near, G-d draws near.

## 4.8 A finger in the ear (`quieting`; new: `mh_finger_in_the_ear`)

"If you hear an improper word, put your finger in your ear" (138-140). The ear is made to receive, so it has no lid of its own: "האוזן שהוא כלי הקיבול, ראוי שאין בו סתימה בעצמו" (141, Well 3 1:6): "the ear, the vessel that receives, should have no closing in itself." The closing must come from outside, by choice. Practice: when gossip or cruel talk starts, leave, change the subject, or take out the earbuds. Guard what enters.

## 4.9 The reckoning of the world (`own_words`; new: `mh_reckoning`)

"בואו ונחשוב חשבונו של עולם" (163, Well 3 3:3): "come, let us make the reckoning of the world": the loss from a mitzvah against its reward, and the gain from a sin against its loss. Practice: once a day, pick one choice and reckon it slowly, in your own words: what does it really cost, what does it really give.

## 4.10 Silent when wronged (`accept`; new: `mh_silent_when_wronged`)

"כל שיש לו קנאה על חבירו, ודומם, שוכן עדי עד עושה לו דין" (167, Well 3 3:7): "whoever has a grievance against his fellow and stays silent, He who dwells for ever judges for him." Practice: when hurt, hold back the sharp answer and hand the case to Him. Guard: this is not staying silent about abuse or danger; tell someone who can help.

## 5. The big human questions

**Where do I start?** With yourself: "היה יותר ראשונה לדעת ולהשיג מהות עצמו" (1): "it should come first to know and grasp one's own essence." It is easy because it is near, and hard because people misjudge themselves.

**I feel so far. Is that bad?** It is a sign of life. "Living flesh feels the scalpel" (11).

**Why do wise people disagree?** Because "אי אפשר שלא יהיה בחינה יותר מאחת לדבר אחד" (53, Well 1 5:6): "it cannot be that one thing has no more than one aspect." When both sides are equal, "אלו ואלו דברי אלקים חיים" (56, Well 1 6:1): "these and these are the words of the living G-d." The law follows the gentle, because the gentle are simple and do not push to a side (57).

**Can one text mean many things?** Yes. "כל דבר ודבר שנמצא בעולם, הוא אחד בעצמו כאשר הוא נגלה לעין כל" (158, Well 3 2:14): "each thing in the world is one in itself as it shows to every eye," and when you search it, you find many true things in it. But "אין מקרא יוצא מידי פשוטו" (176, Well 3 4:1): "a verse does not leave its plain meaning": the plain meaning is the root and the rest are branches.

**Does prayer change a decree?** Yes. Like the holy names, "הוא כמו שאר תפילה, בשביל שהוא קורא השם יתברך, מבטל הגזירה" (85, Well 2 4:2): "it is like any prayer: because he calls on G-d, it cancels the decree."

**Who set up the rules of the sages?** "הכל הוא מן השם יתברך, אשר סידר את החכמים" (34, Well 1 2:8): "all is from G-d, who set up the sages." Like nature building the body after G-d breathed in the soul.

**Why is there a world?** For His honor, and so that there be an "other" who can praise (239-250).

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **"Fully one from every side"** (91) and **"fully one and there is none besides Him"** (233) come close to the Tanya's "ein od milvado" (Gate of Unity ch. 6). The Maharal's reason is different. He argues from oneness to necessary being: only what is fully one is fully necessary. The Tanya argues from speech renewed every moment. Keep the two routes apart.
- **Tzimtzum.** Not present in this half. The nearest idea is 221-226 and 157: the "change" in how He is found belongs to the receiver. The Tanya (Gate of Unity ch. 7) says the same of tzimtzum: it is a hiding from the receivers, not a change in Him.
- **The Rambam's negative attributes.** The Maharal uses the Rambam's language ("Necessary Being", 232-233), but at 210 he openly rejects the philosophers' idea of blind necessity: He acts by will and aim. Chabad also holds that His will is free and above reasons.
- **The Ramak's formula** ("He is all, and all is not He"). Not stated here. The nearest is 239: the world is His garment (joined to Him) and yet He made it for its own sake (apart from Him).
- **"He is the true good, so He seeks to give"** (204-207) is the same idea as "the nature of the Good is to do good" that Chabad uses to explain why He created.
- **Harmful forces live on twoness** (91) matches the Tanya's teaching that the other side lives on separation, and that self-nullification before the One removes its hold.
- **Smallness brings nearness** (214-217) matches Chabad's teaching on bittul: the lowly and self-effacing receive the most light.
