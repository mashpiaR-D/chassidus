This range closes the Shelah's commentary on the Written Torah. It has the book's plainest statements of the unity. The Zohar's "He is all; He alone is all; He is one without division" (line 10242). "There is none but Him" (line 9695). Idolatry defined as a thought "that there is any divinity or power in the world besides Him" (line 9593). And the rule for how He is known: "whoever grasps most grasps most that He cannot be grasped" (line 9700). It is also the book's richest range on the inner life. Eikev holds a long treatise on humility. It includes praise and blame made equal (line 9716), the insult as atonement (line 9760), and "the secret of humility is the secret of love" (line 9749). Vaetchanan holds the rule for clinging in every act, "even when busy with needed bodily things" (line 9682). Shoftim and Ki Tavo hold a teaching on seclusion. A person should copy G-d, who is hidden in His essence and revealed in His acts. So a person should be hidden with his Maker, go out to do good, and come back (lines 10012, 10213, 10222).

## 1.1 He is all, one without division

In Ha'azinu the Shelah copies the Zohar on "For I call the Name of G-d" (Deut. 32:3). The seven attributes are each named. Then "the end of the verse ties the knot of faith":

> הוא כלא הוא לבדו כלא הוא חד בלי פרודא
> "He is all; He alone is all; He is one without division." (line 10242, Torah Shebikhtav, Nitzavim, Veyeilech, Ha'Azinu, Torah Ohr 5)

"And if you say, these are many," the Zohar answers:

> הוא כלא סלקין ומתקשרים בחד
> "He is all; they rise and are tied into one." (line 10242)

The line before it (10239) turns the unity into a statement about people. The nations are divided among seventy stars and princes. Israel has none over it, because "the part is like the whole":

> והחלק מתדמה להכלל, דכמו שהוא יתברך אחד, כך ישראל אחד
> "The part is like the whole: as He is one, so Israel is one." (line 10239)

The Shelah's own gloss on "He has corrupted, not His" (Deut. 32:5) keeps the unity from being touched by sin. Sin "damages above, as it were". But the damage is "not" in Him. He wants to give, "and the holding back is from the side of their receiving":

> והמניעה מצד קבולם
> "And the holding back is from the side of their receiving." (line 10243)

## 1.2 There is none but Him

Balak wanted to curse Israel by sorcery. The Talmud's R. Chanina said sorcery has no power over him, "for it is written 'there is none else besides Him'". The Shelah widens it:

> שקולי אין עוד מלבדו כתיב
> "For it is written, 'there is none else besides Him.'" (line 9515)

> גם ענין הכישוף אין לירא לדביקים בו יתברך
> "Also sorcery, those who cling to Him need not fear." (line 9515)

In Devarim he defines idolatry by the thought, not the act:

> שלא להעלות במחשבה שיש שום אלהות ויכולת בעולם זולתו יתברך
> "Not to let a thought rise that there is any divinity or power in the world besides Him." (line 9593)

> וביראת השם כלול אחדות השם
> "In the fear of G-d is included the oneness of G-d." (line 9694)

> כי ה' אלהינו ה' אחד ואין זולתו
> "For the L-rd our G-d is one and there is none but Him." (line 9695)

And from the Zohar, on why He is feared:

> וכלא קמיה כלא חשיב
> "And all before Him counts as nothing." (line 9699)

In Shoftim the Shelah reads "be whole with the L-rd your G-d" as seclusion, and defines seclusion by the unity:

> היחוד עם יי' אלהיו לבדו ואין זולתו אתו זהו התבודדות
> "Being one with the L-rd his G-d alone, with none but Him: that is seclusion." (line 10012)

## 1.3 Truth is one

The Shelah gives a simple proof of why a lie denies the unity. It is one of the most usable lines in the book:

> אמת חותמו להודיע שהוא אחד
> "Truth is His seal, to make known that He is one." (line 10016)

> אבל כשתרצה לומר האמת אז אינו אלא דבר אחד דהוא מה שהוא
> "But when you want to tell the truth, it is only one thing, what it is." (line 10016)

A lie can take a thousand forms. The truth has one. So in line 10022 one who changes his word "is as if he denied the unity". The Shelah allows a change only for peace, as the fathers did.

## 1.4 Known only through His acts

This is the Shelah's rule of knowledge, and it comes inside the treatise on humility (Eikev, Torah Ohr):

> ומי שמבין ומשיג ביותר מגדולת הש"י, אז הוא יותר ירא ה'
> "Whoever understands and grasps more of G-d's greatness is more in awe of G-d." (line 9700)

> על כן המשיג ביותר הוא ביותר משיג שאין להשיגו
> "So whoever grasps most grasps most that He cannot be grasped." (line 9700)

The reason, given in the same line and again in line 9723:

> ואין דרך להשיגו רק אנו משיגים מצד פעולותיו
> "There is no way to grasp Him; we grasp only from His acts." (line 9723)

He sets a limit in Chukat. A person must reach "as far as his strength allows", and that purifies the body. But past that:

> האדם מחויב לילך בהשגת האלהית כפי כחו
> "A person must go in grasping the divine as far as his strength allows." (line 9467)

> אז הוא כרואה פני חמה בתקפו ועיניו חשוכות
> "Then he is like one who looks at the full sun, and his eyes go dark." (line 9467)

And in Ki Tavo he names the ceiling: "from there up there is no grasp" (line 10221, וממנה למעלה אין השגה).

## 1.5 Hidden first, revealed after; for us the reverse

The last Torah Ohr of the book (V'Zot HaBerachah) gives the order of descent and ascent:

> אצל הש"י מתחלה הנסתר, ואח"כ נמשך ברצונו ההתפשטות וההתגלות
> "With G-d, the hidden comes first, and then by His will the spreading and revealing follow." (line 10281)

> ואצל השגת האדם הוא להיפך, מתחלה צריך להבין הנגלה
> "With a person's grasp it is the reverse: first he must understand the revealed." (line 10281)

He ties it to "I set G-d before me always": one who stands facing another sees left as right, like a mirror. In Ki Tavo:

> והנגלה שלו יתברך הוא הנעלם שלנו
> "What is revealed of Him is what is hidden for us." (line 10222)

## 1.6 One root, divided only by bodies

On Re'eh ("See, I set before you"), singular then plural:

> כי אנחנו למעלה שורש אחד חלק ה', ולמטה מתפרדים מצד גופתם
> "For above we are one root, a part of G-d, and below we split apart because of our bodies." (line 9859)

In the same line he says blessing and curse also come "from one root". Above "they are all equal for good". The bad comes only in the chain downward, "from the readiness of the receiver". Shoftim says it flatly:

> דע כי אין רע יורד מלמעלה, והרע הוא בשביל הטוב
> "Know that no evil comes down from above, and the bad is for the sake of the good." (line 9979)

In Balak the two opposite paths "join together in the upper root" (line 9490, מכל מקום למעלה מצטרפים יחד בשורש העליון).

## 1.7 The soul a part of Him; the mitzvot cut from Him

Chukat opens by turning "decree" (*gezerah*) around. A decree is not a rule without reason. It is a piece "cut" from Him:

> כי כמו שהנשמות חלק אלוה ממעל לקוחה ממנו יתברך
> "For just as souls are a part of G-d above, taken from Him," (line 9424)

> כי הקב"ה מלמד לאדם דעת כמוהו ומלבישו במדותיו יתברך
> "For G-d teaches a person knowledge like His own and dresses him in His own traits." (line 9424)

The soul has two sides. Its "upper aspect is united with His truth" (line 9428, בחינה עליונה מתאחדת באמתתו יתברך). This is why the Torah's traits differ from the philosophers' (line 9436). They "reached good traits from the side of man as man". Moses received traits that are G-d's own, "for our souls are a part of G-d" (כי נשמותינו חלק מהקב"ה). The Shelah still blesses the gentile sages "who gave of His wisdom to flesh and blood".

## 3.1 Cling in every act (proposed: `intent_in_every_act`)

The key text of the range, from Vaetchanan's Derech Chayim. First the frame: "all Torah and all deeds should be for His sake, and he should always remember G-d", with *shiviti* and "know this day... there is none else":

> וכל עסק התורה והמעשה יהיה הכל לשמו יתברך, ויזכור תמיד בהש"י
> "All Torah and all deeds should be for His sake, and he should always remember G-d." (line 9681)

> ואפילו כשעוסק בצרכי גופניים ההכרחיים, לא יזוז מהדביקות
> "Even when busy with needed bodily things, he should not move from clinging." (line 9682)

> כשעוסק במשא ומתן, יחשוב הנני עוסק בעסק זה
> "When doing business, he should think: here I am busy with this business," (line 9682)

and I hope to G-d for profit, "for He is the one who gives". He eats and sleeps to have strength to serve. So:

> נמצא כל ימי חיי האדם הוא דבוק תמיד בהש"י
> "So all the days of a person's life he is always clinging to G-d." (line 9682)

*Tie:* `own_words`, `act`, `garment_look`; the R14 `with_his_help`. *Ours:* at the start of each task, say one sentence in your own words: "I am doing this, and He is the one who gives." The Shelah's form has three parts: name the act, name the Giver, name the purpose.

## 3.2 Hidden with Him, revealed in deeds (proposed: `hidden_and_revealed`)

Ki Tavo gives the reason for seclusion. We must be like Him in what we can. He is hidden in His essence and revealed in His acts:

> כן צריך האדם להיות נסתר מצד מהותו
> "So a person must be hidden in his essence," (line 10213)

that is, alone, apart, "one with his Maker".

> ויהיה נגלה מצד הפעולות לפעול טוב באחריני
> "And revealed in his acts, doing good for others," (line 10213)

> וכאשר נגמר המצוה, אזי ישוב להתבודדות שלו
> "And when the mitzvah is done, he goes back to his seclusion." (line 10213)

Line 10222 reads "blessed in your coming and blessed in your going" this way: go out for a mitzvah, come right back. Shoftim counts seclusion among the four supports of the throne:

> מעלת התבודדות שהיא מעלה גדולה לפרישות וליראת שמים
> "The quality of being alone with G-d, a great quality for setting oneself apart and for fear of heaven." (line 10008)

It also brings peace, "for when one separates from people there is no envy and no hatred" (line 10017, והתבודדות גרם גדול לשלום). In prayer, "he sets himself apart from all bodily thoughts until his thought clings above" (line 10014). *Tie:* `quieting`, `arrive`, `depth`. *Ours:* a short fixed time alone each day, and a rule that every going out has a purpose and a way back. The Shelah's balance matters. Line 9552 says piety kept at home "among themselves" is not enough. One must also go out and sanctify the Name in public.

## 3.3 Grasp through the acts, then fear (proposed: `know_by_acts`)

From 1.4. Contemplate what He does, not what He is. The more one sees, the more one fears and the smaller one feels (line 9700). Then comes *ratzo* and *shov*:

> ולהיות רצוא ושוב לחוס על כבוד קונו בסוד (שמות ג, ו) ויסתר משה (את) פניו
> "And to run and return, guarding his Maker's honor, in the secret of 'Moses hid his face'." (line 9708)

The line opens with the rule that one cannot "come into the holy within" without fear first. Then "he merits and clings". *Tie:* `breadth`, `details`, `edge`; the R14 `keep_your_place`. *Ours:* this is the same shape as Tanya's order of contemplation. The Shelah's addition is the brake: at the edge, hide your face.

## 3.4 Praise and blame equal (proposed: `equanimity`)

From the *Chovot HaLevavot*, the story of the sage asked by a pious man:

> נשתוה בעיניך השבח וגנות
> "Are praise and blame equal in your eyes?" (line 9716)

If not, you have not reached humility, "the highest of the levels of the pious". Line 9717 reads "to those who curse me, let my soul be silent" as the goal:

> שיהיה השפלות מוטבע בנפש
> "That lowliness be stamped into the soul." (line 9717)

He adds a note of hope in line 9718. It is said "so that people won't say it is impossible to be like that, and give up hope" (שלא יאמרו העולם שכבר אי אפשר להיותן כן ויתייאשו). Line 9766: be patient and humble, "even if they walk on him the way people walk on dust" (ואפילו אם דורסין עליו כמו שדורסים על גבי עפר). *Tie:* S15, `cheshbon`. *Ours:* after any praise or insult, check whether it moved you. Repeat until it moves you less.

## 3.5 The insult as atonement (proposed: `insult_as_atonement`)

From the Ramak's *Tomer Devorah*, quoted in line 9760, as "a trick of the mind" against anger:

> כי הבזיון הוא לכפרת עון
> "For the insult is an atonement for sin." (line 9760)

> כי לפעמים הקב"ה שולח עליו את האדם הרע הזה לביישו
> "For sometimes G-d sends this bad man against him to shame him," (line 9760)

as David said of Shimi, "for G-d told him: curse". Line 9758 (from the *Chovot*): the one who speaks ill of you hands you his merits. *Tie:* S15 cure; `discern`. *Ours:* this is the same verse and the same move as *Iggeret HaKodesh* 25. Chabad and the Shelah give one tool here.

## 3.6 The day worker, glad of the work (proposed: `day_worker`)

A king told his servant to fill a pit with no bottom. The fool looked in and asked, when will I fill it? The wise one said:

> מה איכפת לי שכיר יום אני שמח אני שמצאתי לי מלאכה
> "What do I care? I am a day worker; I am glad I found work." (line 9861)

With it comes the rule that thought counts. "One who thought to do a mitzvah and could not, it is counted as if he did it" (lines 9855, 9657). And one learns *lishmah* "to do all he can"; then "even what he cannot do is counted for him as if he did it" (line 9657). Line 9891 adds joy at the command even for "do nots". And Ki Tavo gives Rashi's rule, every day new:

> בכל יום יהיו בעיניך חדשים כאלו בו ביום נצטוית עליהם
> "Every day let them be new in your eyes, as if you were commanded that very day." (line 10229)

> ביותר מזה צריך לשמוח במצוה הבאה לידו שיקיימנה
> "Even more than that, he should be glad at a mitzvah that comes to his hand to do." (line 10229)

That is, more than one is glad at "all the plenty" G-d sends. *Tie:* S53, `heart_after`. *Ours:* a cure for despair at the size of the task. You are paid by the day. The pit is not yours to fill.

## 3.7 Give yourself in the Shema (proposed: `give_self_in_shema`)

Line 9901, from the Ari: when accepting the oneness, "he thinks to give his soul and body for G-d", and it is counted "as if it were done":

> וחושב כן בקבלת היחוד למסור נפשו וגופו בשביל ה'
> "And he thinks this when accepting the oneness, to give his soul and body for G-d." (line 9901)

*Tie:* `lips`, `before_whom`; Tanya ch. 25 and 41 teach the same intention at the Shema. *Ours:* at "the L-rd is one", one sentence of full giving.

## 3.8 Guard the seven gates (proposed: `guard_the_gates`)

On "set judges and officers in all your gates". The *Sefer Yetzirah* lists seven gates in the head. The Shelah adds two more below. Guard sight, hearing, speech, the anger that goes out of the nose, the covenant, and the belly:

> ועל אלו השערים ישים האדם לעצמו שופטים ושוטרים, כלומר שישפוט את עצמו תמיד
> "On these gates a person should set judges and officers for himself, meaning he should always judge himself." (line 10025)

Line 9565: the fingers are shaped like pegs "so he can put them in his ears and not hear a bad word" (כדי לתחבם באזניו שלא ישמע דבר רע). *Tie:* `cheshbon`, `discern`. *Ours:* an evening check, gate by gate.

## 3.9 Give what was entrusted; thank for each success (proposed: `steward`, `thank_each_success`)

> שאינו נותן את שלו, רק הופקד בידו מהקדוש ברוך הוא
> "That he is not giving what is his; it was only placed in his hand by G-d." (line 9907)

The same line adds that blessing from above waits for the awakening from below. On "my strength and the might of my hand" (Eikev):

> בכל מה שהאדם עושה ומצליח, הן דבר גדול הן דבר קטן, יתן הודיה ושבח להש"י
> "In everything a person does and succeeds in, big or small, he should give thanks and praise to G-d." (line 9773)

Line 10228 repeats it for Ki Tavo. *Tie:* S84; `with_his_help`. *Ours:* thank at once, for the small success too.

## 3.10 The table and the joy (proposed: `table_before_him`)

From the Zohar on "eat before the L-rd your G-d... and rejoice". Now, without a Temple:

> וכיון שמברך, באה השכינה לפניו
> "Once he blesses, the Presence comes before him." (line 9903)

He should give the poor what he himself eats. He should speak words of Torah. At grace after meals he should show joy:

> ולא עצבות כלל, ואז השכינה שורה למעלה על ראשו
> "And no sadness at all, and then the Presence rests above his head." (line 9905)

Line 9775: eat to break hunger, and "whatever you eat, be satisfied with it" (כל מה שאתה אוכל תהיה שבע בו). Line 10212: "then eating stands in place of an offering". *Tie:* S53, `act`; the R14 `eat_as_offering`. *Ours:* the "cup of blessing" here is read only as a sign of joy. The guide never uses drink (see 5).

## 3.11 Inner speech (proposed: `inner_speech`)

Ki Teitzei calls the holiness of the mouth the best part of the body's holiness:

> כי דיבור החיצוני נמשך מדיבור הפנימי
> "For the outer speech is drawn from the inner speech." (line 10176)

> שיהיה בנו האמירה הפנימיית מצד נשמותינו שהוא חלק אלוה ממעל
> "That inner speech be in us from our souls, which are a part of G-d above." (line 10176)

So keep your word; guard the tongue even in idle talk; "open it in praise". Line 9585: "the mouth that G-d made in man, He made only to tell His praise" (והפה שברא הקב"ה באדם, לא בראו רק לספר שבחו וגדלו). Line 9604: be faithful in your word "even when it was not a vow". *Tie:* `lips`. *Ours:* before speaking, ask whether it comes from the inner speech.

## 3.12 Search each day

> שיפשפש אדם דבר יום ביומו במעשיו, כי כל יום בחינה בפני עצמו
> "A person should search his deeds day by day, for each day is a thing of its own." (line 10170)

Line 10224 (from the Ari): each day repaired becomes a spiritual "day" that stays. *Tie:* `cheshbon`; the R12 `lamp_search`.

## 3.13 Conquer desire by the decree

> אלא יאמר אפשי ואבי שבשמים גזר עלי
> "Rather he should say: I do want it, but my Father in heaven decreed against it." (line 10184)

The same line says the gates of tears are never locked, "and a person can see to it that his tears are ready" (ויכול אדם לראות שיהיו דמעותיו מצויות). Line 10177: "if a person begins to fight, then G-d helps him". *Tie:* `discern`, S83. *Ours:* Tanya ch. 27 praises the same *itkafya*.

## 3.14 Rebuke and love

> רק אדרבה תאמר לו חכם אתה
> "Rather, on the contrary, tell him: you are wise," (line 9615)

and so it is a shame for a man like you to do this. Line 9614: "so a person should love Israel with body, soul and means". Line 10189 (R. Menachem HaBavli): returning a lost object teaches that "you must stand guard and work and toil at how your brother may return" (שצריך אתה לעמוד על משמרת לטרוח וליגע איך אחיך ישוב בתשובה). Line 10248 (Zohar): bless others "with a good eye", for "in everything G-d wants love of the heart" (ובכלא בעי קב"ה רחימותא דלבא). *Tie:* S86.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Can I know G-d?** Only through His acts. The more you grasp, the more you grasp that He cannot be grasped (lines 9700, 9723). Go as far as your strength allows, and no further (line 9467).
- **Where is evil from?** Not from above. The bad is for the sake of the good and comes from the receiver's side (lines 9859, 9979, 10243).
- **What is the body for?** It is "the case of the soul, and it too was made in G-d's image" (line 9588, הגוף הוא נרתק הנשמה, ונעשה ג"כ בצלם אלהים). "The essence of a person is the inside, and the body is the garment" (line 10112). Body and soul are both meant to cling (line 9536). The final reward is for both (lines 10114, 10206).
- **I can't do it all.** You are a day worker (line 9861). What you meant to do and could not is counted (lines 9657, 9855).
- **Why am I insulted?** It may be sent to atone (line 9760). The one who shames you gives you his merits (line 9758).
- **What makes a leader?** Grasp, not power (lines 9652, 9654). Whoever leads must add holiness (line 10027).
- **Am I responsible for what I didn't know?** For what you should have understood, yes, "for this is why G-d gave him a mind" (line 9525).
- **Does anyone outside Israel count?** The Shelah holds that exile spreads holiness "through the whole world" (line 9597) and brings converts (line 9600). G-d's love for converts is "equal" to love for Him (line 9695). But see 5 for a ranking the guide does not carry.

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **"He is all that exists."** Line 10242 (the Zohar's "He is all, one without division"), 9515 and 9695 ("there is none but Him") and 9699 ("all before Him counts as nothing") are the first half of the Ramak's rule and Tanya's *bittul*. Line 9699 is the Zohar source of Tanya's "כולא קמיה כלא חשיב".
- **"Not all that exists is He."** Line 10221 ("from there up there is no grasp"), 9700 and 9723 (known only from His acts), and 10243 (the damage is not in Him) keep the second half.
- **The Rambam's negative attributes.** Line 9700 ("whoever grasps most grasps most that He cannot be grasped", with "the end of what we know is that we do not know You") is the Rambam's *Guide* 1:59 in the Shelah's words. Chabad keeps this for the Essence and lets the lights be spoken of. The Shelah does the same: essence hidden, acts revealed (10213, 10222).
- **Tzimtzum.** No new statement here. Line 10281 (hidden first, then revealed "by His will") fits R13's non-literal reading (line 8095).
- **The soul a part of G-d.** Lines 9424, 9436, 10012 and 10176 repeat Tanya ch. 2.
- **Insult as atonement.** Line 9760 is *Iggeret HaKodesh* 25, with the same verse about Shimi.
- **The Shema as giving oneself.** Line 9901 is Tanya ch. 25 and 41.
- **Where he differs from the Rambam, with Chabad.** Lines 10114 and 10206 reject the Rambam's view that eternal life is for the soul alone. They follow the Ramban: in the end the body is fed "from the radiance of the Presence". Chabad follows the Ramban here too.
- **Where the guide follows Chabad against the Shelah.** Line 10035 allows "siege by fasting" when the inclination is not conquered by nature. *Iggeret HaTeshuvah* 3 limits fasting in our generation. The guide keeps the Shelah's other means, "steady Torah study" and "more yielding".
- **Not carried.** The legal counts (9782-9834, 9910-9950, 10038-10110, 10199-10201, 10235-10236); the seven nations and "no mercy" (9634-9636); zealot violence (9564); suffering as refinement (9476 in part, 9772, 10231 on the curses); Balaam as Laban; Edom polemics (9449-9457); reincarnation, levirate and soul-mate lore (9589, 9659-9667, 10115-10154, 10128-10138); Lilith and Machalat (10126, 10182-10185); the ranking of converts and righteous gentiles "at the feet" (10265); the war command on Amalek (10196). The "cup of blessing" (9905) and "wine kept in its grapes" (10034, 10241) are read only as images for joy and the world to come. The guide never uses drink.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **He is all, one without division; there is none but Him** (lines 10242, 9695, 9515).
2. **The more you grasp, the more you grasp that He cannot be grasped. Know Him by His acts** (lines 9700, 9723).
3. **Truth is one, as He is one; a lie denies the unity** (line 10016).
4. **Cling in every act: name the act, name the Giver, name the purpose** (line 9682).
5. **Be hidden with Him, go out to do good, and come back** (lines 10012, 10213, 10222).
6. **Praise and blame equal; an insult may be atonement** (lines 9716-9717, 9760).
7. **Humility is the root of all traits and the secret of love** (lines 9437, 9705, 9749).
8. **You are a day worker; be glad of the work; every day is new** (lines 9861, 10229).
9. **What you give was entrusted to you; thank Him for each success** (lines 9907, 9773).
10. **Guard the gates, search each day, and let speech come from the inner speech** (lines 10025, 10170, 10176).
