Rosh HaShanah is the pillar of judgment (*din*); Yoma is the pillar of return (*teshuvah*). Together this is the book's manual of return. Much of it is quoted: from the Shelah's father's *Emek Beracha* and *Brit Avraham*, from Rabbeinu Yonah, the Rambam, *Reishit Chochmah*, *Sefer Charedim*, the Ramak and the Ari's circle. The Shelah weaves them and adds his own rulings. Two findings matter most for the guide. First, the range argues at length **against needless self-affliction**: it weakens the mind, spoils prayer, scares others away from return, and shows little trust in G-d's forgiveness. Second, it teaches **seclusion with G-d** (*hitbodedut*) as a weekly practice that "helps the soul sevenfold more than study".

## 1. The unity of G-d

This range speaks less of G-d's being than the Pesach and Shevuot ranges. Its unity teaching is about **nearness and separation**.

## 1.1 One heart below, one Name above

Why does Yom Kippur not atone for wrongs between people until the other is appeased? The Ramak answers (quoted twice, lines 5249 and 5622): souls stand before G-d on this day in their root. A grudge "separates and divides the upper root" (הרי הוא מפריד ומחלק השורש העליון, line 5249). The holy Name rests only in unity, "so one must make peace, hold on to it, and become one in one heart" (לכן צריך לתווך השלום ולהחזיק בו ולהתאחד בלב אחד, line 5249). The same idea closes the range, on the Shema: "He rests only when Israel is in unity of one heart, without envy" (והוא יתברך אינו שורה כי אם כשישראל ביחוד לב אחד בלי קנאה, line 5793). So at *Shema Yisrael* each person joins his heart to everyone "in love and one bond of heart" before saying "the L-rd is One".

## 1.2 Return is going back to where the soul was hewn from

The Zohar: the one who returns "returns his soul to the place it was hewn from" (דא איהו דאתיב נשמתיה לאתר דאיתגזרת מיניה מתמן, line 5645). *Teshuvah* read as *tashuv heh*: the last letter of the Name, the Presence in exile, returns to its place (line 5640). Every day's return sends "the branches back to their root" (line 5641).

## 1.3 Good is helped from Above; evil is only allowed

"When a person begins to sanctify himself, then, as it were, he cleaves to His holiness and opens the source" (line 5671). But G-d does not add impurity: "added impurity in a man does not come into being from Him, who brings all into being; the way is only opened for him" (line 5673), because "evil does not dwell with You" and "there is no evil above at all" (line 5671). This refines lines 4474 and 4480 (good and bad both from Him): the root of all is from Him, but His active help flows only toward holiness.

## 1.4 The heart is His house

"The main dwelling of the Presence is in the heart" (עיקר מכון השכינה בלב ישראל, line 5736), the heart in the middle of the 248 limbs. Host the King: sweep the heart of the dust of bad and idle thoughts (line 5737). "Happy is one who lets Him dwell in every limb, making a place for Him to dwell there" (line 5688).

## 1.5 Return is grace beyond reason

Wisdom says the sinner is pursued by evil; prophecy says the sinning soul dies; G-d says, let him return (line 5547, from the Jerusalem Talmud). "In truth return has no place by strict law and reason; it is a way of kindness and free gift drawn from G-d" (line 5547). This is the unity of judgment and mercy from line 4557 made practical: the world was planned in judgment and could not stand, so mercy was joined to it (line 5548).

## 3.1 Weekly seclusion with G-d (proposed: `seclusion`)

From the Ari's circle (via *Sefer Charedim*), for anyone who learns: instead of harsh penances, "one day a week let him withdraw from people and be alone between himself and his Maker, and let his thought be bound to Him as if he were already standing before Him on the day of judgment, and let him speak softly to G-d as a servant speaks to his master and a son to his father" (line 5542). The early pious did this: "when they were alone they set the world's affairs apart from their mind and bound their thoughts to the Master of all... this helps the soul sevenfold more than study"; at least once a week, or once a fortnight, or once a month, "and not less" (line 5544). The Ramban's form: an hour before prayer, picturing "the light of the Presence above their heads as if spreading around them, and they sitting inside the light" (line 5545). And among the cheap remedies for a weak soul: "set his face toward his G-d... and they cleave together" (line 5751). *Tie:* `quieting`, `own_words`, `before_whom`, `picturing`. *Ours:* this is one of the Shelah's most valuable practices for the guide. Keep the soft, spoken voice of servant and son; skip "as if on the day of judgment" for anyone fragile.

## 3.2 The daily return (proposed: `three_checks`)

From *Be'er Mayim Chayim* (lines 5603-5605): on the first day, "make yourself as if born today". Then in the morning think over your ways, and guard each step until the meal; if you slipped, name it at once; if not, "thank and praise the Creator who helped him against his enemy and let him be in return for one hour in the world" (line 5605). Do the same from the meal to the evening meal, and from then to bed: three short checks a day, for a month or a year, until it is fixed. And nightly: "before he sleeps, let him make an account of the deeds he did that day, and sit alone, be still, and search his deeds" (line 5503). *Tie:* `cheshbon`, S77, S78. *Ours:* the thanks when nothing is found is the key; it keeps the check from becoming anxious.

## 3.3 Return by the same road

The parable (lines 5472-5473): a man left the beautiful city where G-d is and went out by a crooked road into a desert of snakes. Even if he reaches a good road, he is still far away. Fasting, prayer and charity are good roads, but "still they are far from the city where G-d is" (line 5473). He must go back the way he came and kill the snakes on it: repair the wrong itself. "Not like the error of those who think the sinner's repair is in fasts" (line 5473). So: speech repaired by true and kind speech, the eye by lowered eyes, theft by repayment, hurt by appeasing (lines 5476-5481). *Tie:* `act`, `cheshbon`. *Ours:* a strong corrective to "penance" as a substitute for repair.

## 3.4 Appease first, and go yourself

Wrongs between people are not forgiven "until he appeases his fellow" (lines 5326-5330, 5433-5437). Go yourself, not by a go-between; if he refuses, go again with others. "Even if the one who wronged him is stiff-necked, let him put himself in his path so as to bring things to reconciliation" (line 5437); Rav went to the butcher who had wronged him. The Shelah mocks the custom where friends who love each other ask forgiveness on the eve of Yom Kippur while the real enemies stay apart (line 5330). And the forgiver "must not be cruel" (line 5435). *Tie:* S86. *Ours:* pairs with the bedtime release (R08, line 4806).

## 3.5 The Name before the eyes (tie: `picturing`, `letters`)

A pious elder in Safed, who never sinned, revealed before he died "that all his days he pictured before his eyes the letters of the Name as if they were written before his eyes", even while eating and at business (line 5682). *Kol Bochim*: the four letters rest over a person's head; "I set G-d before me always" means "that Name which is over my head is before me always" (line 5680). *Ours:* for the guide, the universal form is to keep a felt sense of His presence before you; the Hebrew letters are for those who read them.

## 3.6 The Shema of love

The Shema guards against honor, desire and envy (lines 5791-5793). Honor: "blessed is our G-d who created us for His honor", not ours. Desire: "as soon as you are full, draw back your hand". Envy: join your heart to everyone before you declare Him One (line 5793). And at meals: "let him draw his hand back from it for the honor of his Creator... this will remind him of the love of G-d more than one fast a week" (line 5606). *Tie:* `choose_line`, S62, S86. *Ours:* the small held-back bite replaces the fast.

## 3.7 Passing over one's traits (proposed: `pass_over`)

"Whoever passes over his traits, all his wrongs are passed over for him" (line 5750). Three rungs in hearing insult: answer without insulting; do not answer though the heart is bitter; be glad (line 5750). The Ari called bearing insults the best of all returns, "higher than all the lashes and fasting penances in the world", because "you eat, drink and work and the wrongs are atoned" (line 5794). For anger: Rabbi Akiva's daily prayer "that I not be angry today and not anger You" (line 5506); "in your anger you drive out your precious soul" (line 5738). *Tie:* S15, `hold`. *Ours:* for someone being mistreated, this is about one's own reactivity, never about accepting abuse.

## 3.8 The double address

"Always a person, in all he does or speaks or thinks in his heart, toward G-d" (line 5754). Esther said "if it please the king" and meant the King of kings; so should anyone who stands before rulers (line 5754). *Tie:* `before_whom`. *Ours:* proposed id `double_address`: in any hard conversation, let an inner sentence be addressed to Him.

## 3.9 The mitzvah said, meant and done

Every good act is speech, thought and deed: say it ("I am ready to do this mitzvah"), know its meaning, do it "with joy, speed and full service" (lines 5723-5729); "let his intention always be that he does this mitzvah to give pleasure to our Maker" (line 5727); ready the limb: "I do this mitzvah with this limb" (line 5727). "Let no good you do for His sake seem small in your eyes, even a word or a look, for the little from you is much with Him" (line 5684). *Tie:* `lips`, `act`, R08 threefold cord.

## 3.10 The shofar as a soul check

The four sounds (lines 5041-5043): the plain *tekiah* is the person made upright; the broken *shevarim* are the groans of a sick soul; the wailing *teruah* is a soul that has died in sin; the last *tekiah* is uprightness restored, because "G-d is good and upright, so He teaches sinners the way"; "upright" is plural: "He made him upright, and when he sinned and returned He makes him upright again" (line 5043). *Tie:* S77, S78.

## 4. The big human questions

- **Can I come back after everything?** "Nothing stands before return" (line 5279). Menasheh, Ahab, the people of Nineveh; "even a denier". And the boldest line in the range: if G-d Himself seems to say "leave My house", do not listen, "come in by return, for this is the Master's desire" (line 5287).
- **What if I despair?** "Let him not think, I am a sinner... what use are mitzvot to me; rather, if he did many wrongs, let him do many mitzvot" (line 5367). "Let him think in his heart that He is great in forgiveness" (line 5601).
- **Should I punish myself?** No, beyond what is needed. "It is not right to afflict one's soul and mortify one's body without need" (line 5302); "there is no Torah except from joy; and his prayer too will not be accepted when he prays it tormented" (line 5305); "especially in these generations, when people are very weak in body" (line 5306). Once done, "let his heart be firm and sure" (line 5313).
- **Why must I die?** The Shelah's father, via *Maaseh Hashem*: "you shall surely die" was said "in kindness and mercy", because the body after the sin is a screen, and death is the way to the goal (lines 5238-5243). On Rosh HaShanah we ask for the life of the soul (line 5230), and G-d "knows whether it is for his good to live longer" (line 5233).
- **What is a person?** "A person is not called so because of the body"; the body is his garment, and deeds weave the soul's next garment (lines 5730-5733). "The main wholeness of created beings is to grasp the Creator" (line 5237).
- **Why does it feel as if He tests me?** "Perhaps the Holy One is testing him this time" (line 5653).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **Against self-affliction.** Lines 5302-5316 and 5539-5542, 5794 match the Alter Rebbe's *Iggeret HaTeshuvah* ch. 3: in our weak generations, fasting that harms health and study is not the way; charity and Torah replace fasts; and the heart's regret is the essence. The Shelah even quotes the Ari that the harsh penances are "only for one whose toil is not in Torah" (line 5542). This is a strong agreement and a key support for the guide's rule of no fasting.
- **Return from love.** "Let him return out of love for the Creator" (line 5560), for the Presence in exile (lines 5563-5564), matches Tanya ch. 7 and *Iggeret HaTeshuvah* ch. 8-10 (higher return, *tashuv heh*); line 5640 reads *teshuvah* as *tashuv heh* just as *Iggeret HaTeshuvah* ch. 4 does.
- **The dwelling below.** Line 5482 quotes the midrash "the Holy One desired a dwelling in the lower worlds" (Tanchuma) with the seven righteous who brought the Presence down: the very text Tanya ch. 36 builds on.
- **The Rambam.** No new disagreement here; the Shelah uses the Rambam's laws of return throughout and his rule that G-d's justice is beyond us (line 5781).
- **The Ramak.** "Added impurity... does not come into being from Him... the way is only opened" (line 5673) keeps "not all that exists is He": evil is allowed, not emanated.
- **Not carried.** The fasting schedules and Elul fasts (lines 4961-4968, 5538, 5646-5649); the lashes on the eve (line 4970); the Hebron "four deaths" ritual (lines 5410-5415); "teshuvat ha-mishkal", suffering measured against pleasure (lines 5292-5301); praise of dying from penance (line 5534); the harsh self-descriptions in the confession text (lines 5497, 5515); the Ari's crying test (line 4991); the shofar and Name kavanot (lines 5059-5228, 5796-5801); kaparot (line 5615); the azazel goat as a gift to Esau's angel (lines 5631-5633). The Shelah's own balancing lines (5302-5316) are the reason these stay out.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **Weekly seclusion: speak softly to G-d, alone** (lines 5542-5545, 5751).
2. **Return is grace, and nothing stands before it** (lines 5279, 5287, 5547).
3. **No needless self-affliction; once returned, trust** (lines 5302-5313).
4. **Return by the same road: repair, not substitute** (line 5473).
5. **Three short checks a day, with thanks when clean** (line 5605).
6. **Appease first, go yourself** (lines 5433-5437).
7. **Every mitzvah a gift; do it with joy** (line 5689).
8. **One heart with all before "He is One"** (lines 5249, 5793).
