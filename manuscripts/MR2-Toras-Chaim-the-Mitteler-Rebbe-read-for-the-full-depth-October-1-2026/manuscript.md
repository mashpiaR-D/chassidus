## What this slice adds (one page)

The Mitteler Rebbe's Toras Chaim (on Bereishis and Shemos) climbs slowly and at great length: each maamar lays foundation upon foundation until the reader stands inside the idea. What it adds to 'Go deeper': (1) two bittuls, not one: setting yourself aside versus finding you were never apart (the one who stands before the King always, 'like a silent stone'), and each bittul is a 'something' before the next. (2) The soul's core is under even its strongest will and pleasure (the miser who gives freely at his only son's wedding), and the yechida is already embraced with G-d, so that self-sacrifice still means 'someone is there'. (3) The yechida shows in the body before the mind: sudden clapping, a plain scream, a simple tune, the mother's hug; and it runs backward: 'the voice wakes the intention' even when you feel nothing. (4) Every ascent is for descent: the mute sheaf, the baby hidden to be born with its own eyes, 'thirst that reaches water and does not drink is not thirst', 'against your will you live'. (5) The highest lives only in the lowest: the seal shows in dark wax, His simple will rests in parchment, His essential delight is reached only by the finished deed ('very good'), and the plain still yes of the yoke rises above love, like dust from which everything grows. (6) Teshuva and joy: joy from opposite to opposite, 'nishkeit' lowliness in the marketplace, the sudden leap of Pesach that must become the step-by-step counting.

**How to use it with DESCENT (ours).** MR2-D1 sharpens D2: the deeper stillness is not losing a self but finding you were never apart (and every bittul is a 'something' before the next). MR2-D2 and MR2-D3 go under D8: under even the deepest will is the simple self, and the bond there is already an embrace, needing no sacrifice. MR2-D4 extends D5 (knowing by what cannot be borne). MR2-D5 is a new body route to the yechida (hands, scream, song, voice), open to anyone. MR2-D7 gives D9 its reason (thirst that does not drink is not thirst). MR2-D8 and MR2-D12 extend D10-D13 (the highest only in the lowest; the many as the One's strength). MR2-D9 and MR2-D11 serve the person who fell (joy from opposite to opposite; nishkeit, not self-hatred). MR2-D10 is new: changing a will by raising it to its root.

**Guards (ours, from the text's own balances).** The text never sets self-loss as a goal: every ascent is 'for the sake of descent', and a sudden awakening must become step-by-step counting (MR2-C08). Lowliness is 'nishkeit', not self-hatred; despair 'leads to falls' (MR2-C16). Mesirus nefesh is spoken of as the hidden bond's revealing, never as a thing to seek.

## MR2-C01 · Vayeshev, 'Vehineh anachnu me'almim', sections 10-11 (lines 1567-1572)

**Start.** You have learned to let yourself go quiet before G-d. The text asks: is that the deepest giving-way there is?

1. **There are two kinds of giving yourself up. In one, you are something, and you set yourself aside. In the other, you never stood apart as something in the first place.** (M2) *How:* The brothers' sheaves bow by 'nullifying something into nothing'. Joseph's sheaf stands up straight, and the text says his is a higher bittul: not becoming nothing, but never having been a separate thing. «ביטול העצמי דבחי' אין ומה עצמו שנק' ביטול בעצם ולא שנעשה בטל מן הישות כי לא היה בבחי' יש כלל מעולם» — *The essential self-nullification of 'nothing' and 'what' itself, called bittul in essence; not that he became nullified from being something, for he was never in the state of a something at all.* · line 1570

2. **Picture two people and a king. One walks in and bows; he stops being important for that moment. The other stands before the king all the time; he does not feel himself at all, and stands there like a quiet stone.** (M9) *How:* The mashal of the one who comes before the king versus the one who stands before him always. «כמו העומד לפני המלך תמיד שביטולו בעצם עד שאינו מרגיש א"ע כלל וכלל ועומד כאבן דומם ממש משא"כ הבא לפני המלך שמתבטל מישותו בלבד» — *Like one who stands before the king always, whose nullification is in essence, until he does not feel himself at all, and he stands like a silent stone; unlike one who comes before the king, who only sets aside his own self-importance.* · line 1570

3. **So the deeper bittul does not look dramatic. Joseph sat over account books and was not separated for a moment. The quiet is in the person, not in a special hour.** (M15) *How:* Proof the text gives: Joseph was busy with his work and accounts, and 'the screen between Atzilus and Beriah did not block him at all'. «א"א לאדם להגיע לשום דבר מה כ"א ע"י בחי' ההתקשרות העצמיות שיקשר א"ע בזה הדבר בעומק הלב מנקודת הרצון» — *A person cannot reach anything except through essential attachment: that he ties himself to the thing in the depth of the heart, from the point of the will.* · line 1570

4. **How does one get there? Not by thinking more, but by holding on. The text uses the Yiddish word for sticking (klepn): the deep learner sticks to the subject with his whole will, and that is how Rabbi Akiva, who started unlearned, became a vessel for all of Torah.** (M7) *How:* It moves from the content of the bittul to its engine: hiskashrus, binding the point of the will. «א"א לאדם להגיע לשום דבר מה כ"א ע"י בחי' ההתקשרות העצמיות שיקשר א"ע בזה הדבר בעומק הלב מנקודת הרצון» — *(same line) It is only through essential attachment, binding oneself in the depth of the heart from the point of the will.* · line 1570

5. **Real depth of mind works the same way. The deep thinker climbs until he goes blank and empty; it feels as if his mind has stopped. Right then a new, deeper idea comes.** (M9) *How:* A mashal from the experience of study: the mind's own self-emptying brings the new insight. «עד שישתומם ויתרוקן דוקא בכח שכלו להיותו עולה למעלה בהסתלקות כאילו נתבטל מכל מציאות כח חכמתו לגמרי אז יומשך לו כח חדש» — *Until he becomes stunned and empty in the very power of his mind, because it rises up and withdraws, as if he were nullified from every trace of his wisdom; then a new power is drawn to him.* · line 1571

6. **And the lower you go, the higher you are sent. A broken heart that comes from plain, essential lowliness, without drama, is like a bow drawn back: the further back toward yourself, the higher the arrow flies.** (M10) *How:* The bow-and-arrow mashal for lev nishbar from essential lowliness. «במושך הקשת שכל שמושכו למטה אל עצמו בנמיכות יותר כך יהי' יותר עליית החץ» — *Like one who draws a bow: the more he pulls it down toward himself, the higher the arrow rises.* · line 1571

7. **Even this has no end. A man who bows to an officer must forget that bow when he stands before the general; that first bow is now 'something' compared to the second. Before the king he forgets both.** (M5) *How:* The nehar dinur image: souls bathe in the river of fire to forget the first bittul, which counts as yesh next to the higher one. «כמו מי שמתבטל לפני שר בהגיעו לפני השר צבא יתבטל גם מביטול הראשון שנק' יש ממש» — *Like one who bows before an officer: when he reaches the general he is nullified even from the first nullification, which is called a real 'something' compared to it.* · line 1572

**Bottom.** Bittul that was never a 'something': not losing a self, but finding you never stood apart. **Return.** Back to the account books: the person standing before the King always is the one who can sit at his work and not be separated. *Whose:* stated: two bittuls, the king mashal, Joseph and the accounts, the bow, the officer-general-king. ours: ordering, the plain-English framing.

## MR2-C02 · Vayeshev, sections 7-9 (lines 1558-1566)

**Start.** Why are the brothers' acts of gathering G-d's sparks called 'binding sheaves'?

1. **In a field each stalk of wheat drew its food from the ground on its own. At harvest you tie them into one sheaf, and now they are one body. That is what a life of mitzvos and prayer does: it makes oneness out of scattering.** (M13) *How:* The plain meaning of alumim: the sheaf. «שעושים מן הפירוד אחדות גמורה» — *They make, out of separateness, complete oneness.* · line 1558

2. **When Jews pray with their whole selves, the many become one, like at Sinai 'as one man', and like the words 'bless us, our Father, all of us as one'.** (M13) *How:* The text applies the sheaf to prayer with mesirus nefesh. «ברכנו אבינו כולנו כאחד ממש» — *Bless us, our Father, all of us as one, truly.* · line 1559

3. **But the word for sheaf also means 'mute'. Why? Think of a teacher working out a hard idea for himself. His lips move silently; for that moment he cannot speak at all. His speech is held inside his thinking.** (M9) *How:* Second meaning of alumim (illem, mute); the mashal of the absorbed thinker. «מרחשין שפתיו בחשאי מאד עד שהוא כמו אלם ממש שאינו יכול לדבר כלל» — *His lips move silently, very quietly, until he is like a real mute who cannot speak at all.* · line 1561

4. **That is what rising into your source feels like. You don't talk; you are held, like a baby inside its mother. That is why the Amidah opens, 'G-d, open my lips': on our own we are mute.** (M8) *How:* Every effect that rises into its cause is 'mute', included like a fetus. «וכמו בש"ע שאומר אד' שפתי תפתח כו' שא"י לדבר מעצמו כאלם» — *As in Shemoneh Esrei, when one says 'G-d, open my lips', for he cannot speak by himself, like a mute.* · line 1562

5. **But the silence is not the goal. A baby is hidden nine months so it can be born with eyes and ears of its own. Every going-up is for a coming-down with more light.** (M15) *How:* The rule the text states, then the mashal of pregnancy and birth. «כל עליה והסתלקות למעלה הוא בשביל ירידה והמשכה למטה» — *Every ascent and withdrawal upward is for the sake of a descent and drawing down below.* · line 1564

6. **Even exile is this kind of hiddenness, a pregnancy. And there is no last stage: even in the World to Come the righteous 'have no rest', going from strength to strength.** (M5) *How:* Galus as ibbur; birth as redemption; then 'second pregnancy' for greater mind, without end. «היה בשביל שיומשך לו מוחין בפ"ע בלידתו» — *It was so that he would have a mind of his own when he is born.* · line 1564

**Bottom.** Silent inclusion in the Source, like a child in the womb. **Return.** Birth: the ascent exists to come back down with eyes of one's own. *Whose:* stated: every image and step. ours: the plain wording.

## MR2-C03 · Vayeshev, sections 14-16 (lines 1580-1585)

**Start.** In the dream, the sheaves bow to Joseph. What is a bow?

1. **There are two bows. A servant falls at his master's feet. And a king bends his head down toward the one asking him for something.** (M2) *How:* Two meanings of hishtachavaya: from below up, and from above down. «כמלך שמרכין ומנענע בראשו למבקש נפשו בשאלתו» — *Like a king who bends and nods his head to the one asking him for his life's request.* · line 1580

2. **In the Amidah we bow at 'Blessed'. That is our bow: we let go of our own existence before the living G-d.** (M9) *How:* The text applies it to the bows of Shemoneh Esrei. «א"א להיות בחי' ירידה והמשכה מגבוה לנמוך מלמעלה למטה מן המשפיע אל המקבל אם לא בהקדם תחלה הביטול» — *There cannot be a descent and drawing from high to low, from giver to receiver, without the nullification coming first.* · line 1580

3. **And that very bow makes the King bend down to us. They look like opposites, but they are one act: His bending only comes through our bowing.** (M12) *How:* Both bows 'come as one; they are really one matter, not two'. «א"א להיות בחי' ירידה והמשכה מגבוה לנמוך מלמעלה למטה מן המשפיע אל המקבל אם לא בהקדם תחלה הביטול» — *(same line) There is no drawing down from above unless the bow from below comes first.* · line 1580

4. **A seed of wheat must rot in the ground before it can grow many seeds. The giving-way comes first; the gift grows from it.** (M10) *How:* The wheat seed mashal for bittul before blessing. «כחטה שנקלטה בארץ שיכול כח הצומח להתלבש בה להצמיח בה חטים רבים» — *Like a seed of wheat taken into the earth, so that the power of growth can clothe itself in it and grow many seeds.* · line 1585

**Bottom.** Our bow and His bending are one act. **Return.** Bow at 'Baruch', straighten at the Name: you stand up again carrying what He gave. *Whose:* stated: all. ours: phrasing.

## MR2-C04 · Vayigash, sections 2-5 (lines 1686-1695)

**Start.** Which came first, heaven or earth? The sages argue. The text uses the argument to ask: what is the point of all G-d's giving?

1. **A kindness that stays in your heart and your words, and never becomes an act, is not called kindness at all.** (M15) *How:* The mashal of a man doing a kindness: the whole intent is the deed. «ולא יקיים בפו"מ לא נק' חסד כלל וכלל» — *If it is not carried out in deed, it is not called kindness at all.* · line 1686

2. **Will and deed need each other. A will with no deed is nothing; a deed with no will is nothing. The foot carries out what the head wants, and the head's wish is only complete through the foot.** (M12) *How:* Neither has an advantage; 'there is no head and no end'. «רצון בלא מעשה ומעשה בלא רצון אינו כלום» — *Will without deed and deed without will are nothing.* · line 1687

3. **A giver's joy depends on the gift being received. A kind man gives to a poor man and is happy only when he sees it really helps him. The cow wants to nurse more than the calf wants to drink.** (M9) *How:* From daily life: the giver's pleasure is woken by the receiver. «יותר משהעגל רוצה לינק» — *More than the calf wants to suck (the cow wants to nurse).* · line 1690

4. **But the receiver does not create the goodness. A truly good person (a 'guter') gives his whole self because that is his nature; the receiver only brings it out.** (M4) *How:* 'Both are true': the pleasure was already in the giver's nature. «וזהו רק מצד עצם טבע הטוב (בל"א גוטער) שיתן כל עצמותו» — *This is only from the very nature of goodness (in Yiddish, a guter), that he gives his whole self.* · line 1691

5. **Now the deepest point: G-d's own delight was not reached until the work was finished and He saw it was 'very good'. Even though it was already in His hidden will, it did not reach His very self until the end.** (M5) *How:* From the verse 'He saw all that He made, and behold it was very good' after the sixth day. «קודם גמר המעשה בטוב מאד הגם שכבר היה ברצונו הנעלם בעצמו' בתענוג העצמי להיטיב לא הגיע זה לבחי' תענוג שבעצמותו ממש» — *Before the finished deed was 'very good', even though it was already in His hidden will, in His essential pleasure to do good, it had not reached the pleasure that is in His very self.* · line 1695

6. **Like a builder who finishes a house, and his whole soul delights in it. The finished, plain, physical thing is what touches His essence.** (M15) *How:* The mashal of the finished building; 'the end of the deed rose first in thought'. «משעשועים שבגמר שלימות טוב הבנין שכל נפשו משתעשע בזה» — *Like the delight at the completion of a fine building, in which his whole soul delights.* · line 1695

**Bottom.** His essential delight is reached only through the finished deed below. **Return.** So finish the kindness: the act in the hand is what He was waiting for. *Whose:* stated: all steps. ours: 'the finished thing touches His essence' as summary.

## MR2-C05 · Vayigash, sections 6-11 (lines 1698-1716)

**Start.** Why was Solomon's Temple built of stone, and why will the stone the builders rejected become the cornerstone?

1. **Only earth makes something truly new. Water, air and fire don't; out of earth grows everything. The lowest thing has the most power.** (M1) *How:* Kasha: how can the inanimate feed the plant above it? 'Everything was from the dust.' «ביסוד המים והרוח ואש לא יוליד חדש ממש רק בעפר יוליד התהוות חדשה לאין שיעור» — *The elements of water, air and fire do not bring forth anything truly new; only earth brings forth new things without limit.* · line 1698

2. **Why? Because it is simple. When wood burns, three parts go and ash is left: the simple element. Because it is simple, everything can come from it.** (M2) *How:* The domem is 'simple, uncompounded matter', the hyle. «משום שהוא עצם פשוט ע"כ ממנו יסתעף הכל» — *Because it is a simple essence, therefore everything branches out from it.* · line 1699

3. **Our service has the same layers. Love grows and spreads like a plant. Plain fear and acceptance of the yoke is like a stone: it doesn't spread, it just stays.** (M7) *How:* Love is 'tzomeach' (growing), lower fear is 'domem' (still). «ביטול רצונו וכל עצמו להיות נכנע לכל אשר יגזור אומר עליו» — *The setting aside of his will and his whole self, to submit to all that is decreed on him.* · line 1715

4. **And yet in the end the plain acceptance rises above the love. Why? Because it is rooted in the highest fear: the one who suddenly comes before the king and is struck still like a stone, unable to speak.** (M4) *How:* 'Adarabah hi hanosenes': the outer bittul is planted in the inner one; 'the end is wedged in the beginning'. «כמו שמתבטל פתאום בבואו לפני המלך עד שנעשה כאבן דומם ממש שלא יכול לדבר» — *Like one who is suddenly nullified when he comes before the king, until he becomes like a silent stone, unable to speak.* · line 1715

5. **That is why at Sinai saying 'we will do' was enough to bring the highest bittul. The plainest yes reaches the highest place.** (M15) *How:* Proof from Sinai: from naaseh alone came the higher fear. «ביטול רצונו וכל עצמו להיות נכנע לכל אשר יגזור אומר עליו» — *(same line) The setting aside of his will and his whole self, to submit.* · line 1715

**Bottom.** The plain, still yes of the yoke, rooted in the stillness before the King. **Return.** Back to stone: build with what is plain. *Whose:* stated: all. ours: phrasing.

## MR2-C06 · Toldos, 'Vayiten lecha' (lines 1252, 1301-1302)

**Start.** Torah is called truth. Why then do the sages say 'the end of wisdom is teshuva'?

1. **Teshuva is where light is tested and proven, by pulling it out of darkness. 'No one is as wise as the one who has been tested.'** (M2) *How:* Teshuva as birur: the place where every light is made clear. «תכלית חכמה תשובה. שהתשובה הוא בירור האור מתוך החושך. שזהו מקום המתברר כל אור לאמיתתו» — *The end of wisdom is teshuva, for teshuva is drawing light out of darkness; this is the place where every light is made clear in its truth.* · line 1252

2. **There are two kinds of return. A lower one, from below, reaches only a garment of His hiding. A higher one, of people who also keep Torah, comes from above and turns the evil itself into good.** (M5) *How:* Two lights and two darknesses; two teshuvos. «ותשובה הב' נקרא תשובה עילאה שהיא מלמעלה למטה כיו"כ» — *The second teshuva is called the higher teshuva, which comes from above to below, like Yom Kippur.* · line 1301

3. **Rebbi, the greatest Torah scholar of his age, wept when a man earned his world in one hour of return. He envied him.** (M10) *How:* The story of Rebbi weeping (as the text reads it). «לא היה בו תורה ומצות כלל אלא היה מתקנא בתשובה זו בהיפוך הרע עצמו לטוב עד שיצא נשמתו בבכיה» — *(same line) Rebbi envied this teshuva, the turning of evil itself into good.* · line 1302

4. **This return reaches the place where darkness and light are the same to Him. That is why Yom Kippur can turn even sins into merits.** (M5) *How:* 'Before Him darkness is as light'. «קדושת אור העצמות דא"ס ממש שלפניו שוים חושך ואור» — *The holiness of the light of the very Essence of the Infinite, before whom darkness and light are equal.* · line 1302

**Bottom.** Before the Essence, darkness and light are equal; so even the dark can become light. **Return.** Vidui: the plain words 'we have sinned' wake the root. *Whose:* stated: all. ours: phrasing.

## MR2-C07 · Bo, 'Lehavin shoresh inyan Pesach u'matza', sections 13-16 (lines 2580-2589)

**Start.** What happens when something touches the very essence of the soul, the yechida? The text studies it in the body.

1. **When someone hears wonderful news that touches the core of who they are, their hands clap by themselves, at once, before any decision.** (M9) *How:* Observation: Sarah hearing of Isaac, Jacob hearing Joseph lives. «כשמבשרים אותו בשורה טובה ונפלאה ביותר שמגעת בעצמיות נפשו ממש» — *When they bring him wonderful good news that reaches the very essence of his soul.* · line 2580

2. **No thought or will moves the hands then. The joy is too big for mind and heart to hold, so it jumps straight to the hands, or to dancing feet. Grief does the same: the mourner wrings his hands.** (M7) *How:* Normal movements go from will to brain to limb, in order; this one skips the order. The hands carry a light from the yechida, above the head. «כי הרי מיד שיבא השמחה בלבו פתאום תומ"י יתנענעו הידים בסיפוק ממילא ומאליהן או בריקוד ברגל» — *For as soon as the joy comes into his heart suddenly, at once the hands move clapping by themselves, or in dancing with the feet.* · line 2581

3. **Prayer has the same two levels. One goes step by step, mind then heart. The other is a stirring of the whole self that comes all at once, past even the longing to melt into G-d. In it even light-headed people break their hearts truly, without knowing where it came from.** (M5) *How:* Two levels in avodah; even klos hanefesh has a limit; yechida's arousal has none. «גם הקלים ישברו לבבם באמת מבלי נודע מאין בא לו זאת» — *Even the light-headed break their hearts in truth, without knowing where it came from.* · line 2583

4. **This point is never damaged by what a person did wrong. 'You are all beautiful, My beloved', even if she limps or is blind.** (M11) *How:* No evil of the lower soul-levels touches the yechida. «כולך יפה רעייתי כו' אע"פ שהיא חיגרת או סומא» — *'You are all beautiful, My beloved,' even though she is lame or blind.* · line 2584

5. **The voice is the same. When something touches a person's very life, he screams a plain scream, suddenly, without choosing. And when joy reaches the essence, a simple tune comes out by itself.** (M9) *How:* The cry in Egypt; simple song vs. composed song. «כשנוגע לחייו העצמיי' ביותר אז יצעק בצעקת קול פשוט היוצא מן הגרון פתאום בלא בחירה» — *When it touches his very life, he cries out with a plain scream from the throat, suddenly, without choosing.* · line 2587

6. **And it works the other way too. 'The voice wakes the intention.' **Hisbonenus.** 1. Recall a time you climbed out of darkness. 2. That joy was bigger than ordinary joy. 3. Now do a mitzvah and think: G-d comes close to my lowly soul and shines even in my dark. 4. Be happy in the mitzvah, not in yourself.

**Inside.** From death to life; the burnt scrapings; the princess. **Lands.** A hard mitzvah done with joy. **Extends.** D9, D14 (for one who fell)

## MR2-D10 · Changing a will at its root

**Teaching.** To change a desire, don't fight it on its level. Let it rise into the core of the self, and it comes back down in new 'letters'.

**Hisbonenus.** 1. Name a will you want to change. 2. Don't argue with it. 3. Raise it: bring it into your deepest self before G-d. 4. Then act; stretch the hand in a new way.

**Inside.** Raising the staff before stretching it over the sea. **Lands.** A new act. **Extends.** new

**Quotes.** - «צריך שיתבטל ויעלה הרצון או השכל הקדום למעלה לבחי' יחידה שבנפש ואז יורד הרצון ויבא בגילוי באופן אחר» — *The old will must rise to the yechida, then comes down revealed another way.* · line 2831

## MR2-D11 · Nishkeit: not being by yourself

**Teaching.** A lowliness deeper than guilt: simply not being anyone in your own eyes. Not from bitterness. It breaks pride in the marketplace.

**Hisbonenus.** 1. Think of Avraham: 'I am dust and ashes'. 2. This lowliness is not self-hatred. 3. It is not being 'by yourself' at all. 4. Walk the street with it.

**Inside.** The ground everyone walks on; Pharaoh falling before Moshe. **Lands.** In the market, at work. **Extends.** D2 (guard: lowliness is not self-hatred)

**Quotes.** - «בל"א נישקייט בעצם ער איז ג ר נישט באיי זיך בתכלית» — *'Nishkeit' in essence: he is not by himself at all.* · line 4459 - «כל היום בלכתו בשוק שלא בשעת הכושר בתפלה» — *All day as he walks in the market.* · line 4459

## MR2-D12 · The many are the One's strength

**Teaching.** Many things do not mean separation. The greater the oneness, the more it branches.

**Hisbonenus.** 1. Look at many things around you. 2. Think of Solomon's 3000 parables from one wisdom. 3. Every part is fed each moment from one river. 4. See the many as the One's strength.

**Inside.** River from Eden; a lump cut into pieces. **Lands.** Looking at the world. **Extends.** D10

**Quotes.** - «לפי עוצם כח האח"פ כך יוגדל כח התחלקות שלו» — *According to the strength of the oneness, so great its power to divide.* · line 1551 - «ואם יופסק הנביעה שלהם מנהר הזה יחרבו ויבשו כל הד' נהרות» — *If the flow stopped, all four rivers would dry up.* · line 1551
