This is the Ramak's treatise on angels, in seven "inquiries": how many ranks there are, what they are, why they exist, their order, how a person receives from them, how a person "rides" them, and how a person can bind them to his will. Most of it is angelology: ranks, names, thrones, the Merkavah (the chariot vision). That part is not for the guide.

But the book keeps turning back to the human being, and there it says things the guide needs. Why did God make a world at all? What is a person worth next to an angel? What is the right way to reach the upper worlds, by force or by love? How can a spiritual experience deceive?

## 2. The unity of God in this book

**Nothing is made by any other power.** "There is no doubt that no power among the powers can create even one small creature" (שאין ספק שאין לשום כח שבכוחות לברוא אפילו בריה קטנה, line 89). Even the gnats on a carcass are made in the upper Wisdom. The angels, the stars and the shells have only what flows through them. An angel "has no power of knowing except as much as the divine care placed in it" (line 105), and when that power leaves, it goes back to its place.

**Why there is a world.** "The Creator, who does good, delights in doing good to another" (היות הבורא המטיב משתעשע בהטיבו לזולת, line 48). He made creatures to know His kindness, and "the delight a creature reaches when the Creator draws His light to it has no limit and no likeness" (והעדן המושג לנברא בהיות הבורא מושך אורו אליו אין לו גבול ולא דמיון, line 46). The goodness went on spreading to lower and lower worlds, for the further it reaches, the more it is goodness.

**Nothing is wasted.** "There is nothing empty that does not have some use" (ואין דבר ריק שלא יהיה בו צורך מה, line 56). This is the unity seen from below: every creature, even the pull to wrong, serves the one purpose.

## 3. States of consciousness

- **Rising and falling, and that is the gift** (new, RK-S33). The angel is fixed; a human "is not fixed but rises and falls" (והוא אינו עומד קבוע אלא עולה ויורד, line 105), and so stands higher than the angel beyond measure. The souls in Eden still choose to learn and "add to it day by day" (ויעסקו בה ויספרו בה ויוסיפו בה מידי יום ביום, line 105). The state: I notice my ups and downs and, instead of shame, I feel that this movement is what makes me human and able to grow. Links S34 (two voices), S48 (returning), and a guard on S11 (the verdict: I'm my mistakes).
- **A false light** (a warning, not a goal). A person with an unrepented sin who tries to cling to holiness may see "things, and imagine he is a prophet, and it will mislead him all it can" (ויראה אל אדם עניינים ידמה בעצמו שהוא נביא וכיוצא ויטעהו כל מה שאפשר, line 95). The remedy: "he has no fix but to return in full teshuvah and pray to his Maker" (אין לו תקנה אלא לשוב בתשובה שלימה ולהתפלל לקונו, line 95). Links S18 (chasing the high) and S20 (unreal, spacey or frightened). For the guide: a state that feels grand and special, and comes with pride, is suspect.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**RK-T43. For the sake of Heaven, three ways** (new). The whole of the sixth inquiry rests on this: "when a person puts the Name of Heaven first in all his deeds, at once the shell leaves" (ובהיות האדם מקדים שם שמים בכל מעשיו מיד הקליפה מסתלקת משם, line 99). Do it in three layers. In thought: aim the act "for the unity of his Maker" (במחשבה שיישיר מחשבתו בפועל ההוא לשם יחוד קונו, line 99). In speech: say out loud that it is for Him. In deed: do the act well, "for thought and speech do not help when the vessel is ugly" (כי לא תועיל המחשבה והדבור אחר שהכלי מכוער, line 99). The Ramak's example is building a house. Links act, lips, and CATALOGUE A2.

**RK-T44. Reach by love, not by force** (new). The seventh inquiry ranks the ways a person moves the upper world. The best is through "his piety and goodness". "What comes by will and love comes easily and without sin" (והענין שיהיה ברצון ואהבה יבא בקלות ובלי עון, line 115). "The way that comes by force, by saying Names and the like, is a great flaw to the soul" (והדרך שיבוא בהכרח על ידי הזכרת השמות וכיוצא פגם גדול לנפש, line 115). And: "the way that comes by will is true, and there is no deception in it at all" (הדרך שיבא ברצון היא אמתית ואין בה הטעאה כלל, line 115). The sages of the Talmud, like R. Chanina ben Dosa, worked by goodness, not by Names. For the guide this is a rule, not a technique: never force a state; become good, and the door opens.

**Not for the guide.** Angel names and their verses (lines 9-17), Names to bind angels (lines 112-125), the size of the Throne (line 64). The Ramak himself warns: this knowledge is out of our reach "and dealing in it is dangerous" (כי זה יעדר ידיעתנו בו ואף אם נרצה להכריח לא יספיקו ימים ושנים והעסק בו מסוכן, line 9). Using the wrong Name is "like one who switched a flask of balm for a flask of deadly poison" (ויהיה המשל למי שנתחלף לו צלוחית של צרי בצלוחית של סם המות, line 119). Divination by stars, birds or palms: "there are many ways, and all of them are idolatry" (ויש דרכים רבים וכולם עבודה זרה, line 128).

## 5. The big human questions

**Why was I made?** Because the Good delights in giving (line 48), and the joy a creature finds in His light has no limit (line 46). The world was made to be given to.

**Am I lower than the angels?** No. The angel only carries what it is given. A human can choose, grow, fall and rise, and learn. "A single human is a chariot, while the angels only all together are a chariot" (שהאדם יחידי מרכבה, והמלאך כולם יחד הם מרכבה, line 107). One person alone carries the whole shape of the ten sefirot. An angel is one part.

**How high or low can I go?** Anywhere. "A person has power by his deeds to ride on all the levels and cleave to his Maker, or to go down below the demon" (כי יש באדם כח במעשיו לרכוב על כל המדרגות ולהדבק בקונו או לרדת למטה מהשד, line 95). This is freedom, and it is also weight.

**What do I do with my bad traits?** Use them. "All the ugly traits are beautiful in their time" (ולזה ימצאו כל המדות המגונות יפות בעתם, line 56). Desire is for "desiring to store up mitzvot and good deeds and wisdom" (ויצר חמדה לחמד לאצור מצות ומעשים טובים וחכמה, line 56). Jealousy is for zeal for the good. Even laziness has a place: to be slow to sin.

**Is there a part of the Torah that is mine?** Yes. "If a person knew the secret of how his soul hangs on the Torah, on which letter, which verse, which portion" (ואם היה אדם בקי לדעת סוד היותו תלוי בנשמתו בתורה, line 13), he would reach a wonderful level. The 22 letters were placed in our mouth "out of great love for the human being created in the image of God" (לרוב חבה עם האדם הנברא בצלם אלהים היה קובעם בפיו, line 9). The Ramak says "the human", not only Israel. D28.

**Can a spiritual experience lie to me?** Yes (line 95). Test it by its fruit: true closeness comes "by will and love", with no deception (line 115). A vision that feeds pride, or that comes while a wrong is left unmended, is not to be trusted. Mend the wrong first.

## 6. Against Chabad (ours)

- **Why the world.** The Ramak: the Good delights in giving to another (line 48). The Tanya: "the purpose of the creation of this world is that the Holy One desired to have a dwelling in the lower worlds" (שתכלית בריאת עולם הזה הוא שנתאוה הקדוש ברוך הוא להיות לו דירה בתחתונים, Tanya line 506). They are two sides of one thing. The Ramak says why God gives (goodness); the Tanya says where He wants to be received (here, below). The guide needs both: you are given to, and you are His home.
- **For the sake of Heaven.** Line 99 is the root of the Tanya's line on permitted things done not for Heaven: they draw from kelipat nogah (the mixed shell) when done "not for the sake of Heaven, but for the body's will and desire" (רק שאינן לשם שמים אלא רצון הגוף וחפצו ותאותו, Tanya line 154). Same teaching. The Ramak gives it as a three-layer practice (thought, speech, deed); the Tanya gives the reason (what the act is fed from).
- **Human above angel.** Chabad teaches the same: the soul that goes down into a body and chooses reaches higher than the angel who stands still (a common theme in Chabad maamarim). The Ramak's line 107 (one human is a whole chariot) is a clear, early form.
- **Force versus love.** Chabad avoids practical kabbalah entirely. The Ramak is already there: Names are a flaw to the soul; goodness is the true way (line 115). Full agreement.
- **Tzimtzum.** Not in this book.
