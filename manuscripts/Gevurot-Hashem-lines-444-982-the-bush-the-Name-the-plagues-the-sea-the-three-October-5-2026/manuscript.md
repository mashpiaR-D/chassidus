This range carries the Exodus story from Moshe at the well of Midian to the splitting of the sea, then turns to the mitzvot that came out of it (tefillin, the first-born, the festivals). It is the richest part of the book for the unity of G-d. Here the Maharal explains the burning bush, the Names Ehyeh and Havayah (the four-letter Name), why the One can bring both a wound and its healing, why harmful forces have no hold on oneness, and why awe comes from His separateness while love comes from joining. It also gives the most practical teaching in the book on trust: the four camps at the sea and the command "and you shall be silent".

## 2.1 The bush: no place empty of Him

The Midrash asks why G-d appeared in a thorn bush. The Maharal answers:

- "אין מקום פנוי בלא שכינה, אפילו סנה" (540, 23:22): "There is no place empty of the Shechina (the divine Presence), not even a thorn bush."

The bush is low and common. That is the point. The lowest place is not outside Him. This is the Maharal's form of the teaching the Tanya quotes from the Tikkunei Zohar, "no place is empty of Him". He also says the bush was chosen because He is "with him in trouble" (529-533). When the Midrash says G-d is saddened with Israel, the Maharal explains that this is "לפי המקבל" (535): "according to the receiver". From His own side all is glory; from our side His Presence is felt as sharing our pain. This is a careful balance. He does not deny the closeness, and he does not make G-d change.

## 2.2 The Names: "there is nothing outside Him"

Why does G-d have no proper name? Because a name marks off one thing from others. He is "simple being" (הויה פשוטה, 579), and

- "כי אין חוץ ממנו" (578, 25:13): "for there is nothing outside Him".

So He is named only by His acts. Ehyeh ("I will be") means He is the being from whom all beings stand, present to them: "אהיה עמהם בצרה זו" (568): "I will be with them in this trouble". Havayah (the four-letter Name) is His essence, the form by which all things stand.

## 2.3 The One who wounds and heals

The philosophers said "from One comes only one". The Maharal says the reverse. Because He is One, and One means all, opposites come from Him:

- "הוא המוחץ והוא הרופא" (734, 36:2): "He wounds and He heals."

That is why the Pesach lamb is eaten with both matza (freedom) and maror (bitterness) at once. (He says it most fully at 1446, in the next range.)

## 2.4 Dusk: all beings pass, He remains

Why is the Pesach offering slaughtered at dusk? Because at the end of the day

- "כל הנמצאים נפסדים, והוא יתברך אחד בלבד קיים" (743, 36:11): "all beings pass away, and He alone, One, remains."

This is why the Shabbat afternoon prayer says "You are One and Your Name is One". The evening is a daily picture of unity: things fading back into their Maker. This is one of the most usable images in the book (see 4.3).

## 2.5 Singular, so He singles out

- "הקב"ה מיוחד מכל הנמצאים ונבדל מהם, ולכך מייחד ומבדיל" (676, 32:12): "The Holy One is singular and separate from all beings, and so He singles out and separates."

He set an exact hour for a plague and kept Goshen apart. Precise, particular care is itself a sign of the One. A world run by many powers would be blurry. Particular providence (S85) is read here as a proof of unity.

## 2.6 A ladder of seeing, up to "there is nothing else"

In chapter 34 the plagues climb. First a "finger" (a particular power). Then the sea (a general power). Then the heavens. At last at Sinai:

- "אתה הראת לדעת כי ה' הוא האלקים אין עוד" (708, 34:16): "you were shown to know that G-d is G-d, there is nothing else."

This is a teaching path from the particular to the whole, the guide's C2 (general and detail) run upward.

## 2.7 Oneness is divine, division is physical

- "החילוק הוא דבר גשמי, והאחדות הוא ענין אלקי" (891, 43:9): "Division is a physical thing, and oneness is a divine thing."

When a man and woman are truly one, their joining is divine. The same goes for any real union.

- "במה שהוא יתברך אחד, הוא מאחד ומסדר ומקשר החלקים זה בזה עד שהם אחד" (793, 39:6): "Because He is One, He unites, orders and binds the parts to each other until they are one."

His oneness is not only a fact about Him. It is a force that makes things one.

## 2.8 Before Him no being has existence

- "אין אצל מדריגתו מציאות לשום נמצא" (825, 40:18): "at His level no being has existence".

And "החמרי אין מציאות לו בפעל" (855): "the physical has no existence in act". It is only possibility. This is the Maharal's form of bittul hayesh (the self and world as nothing before Him, S62 Only Him).

## 2.9 The world bound to Him three ways, renewed each day

The festivals teach that the world has no standing of its own: "שאין עמידה לעולם בעצמו" (959, 46:5). It is bound to Him by flowing out from Him, by returning to Him, and by its own completion. Then:

- "השם יתברך בכל יום מחדש מעשה בראשית" (961, 46:7): "G-d renews the work of creation every day."

This is F01 in the Maharal's words (from the prayer book, as in Tanya's Gate of Unity). But he ties it to chesed: the world stands on kindness, and He renews it from kindness.

## 4.1 Feeding others draws near to the Root

- "מי שהוא מפרנס ומשפיע הפרנסה לאחר, מתקרב אל העיקר" (484, 20:9): "One who feeds and gives livelihood to another draws near to the Root."

G-d is the Root that feeds all. One who feeds others does His work and so draws near. For the guide (ours): giving is a way into closeness, not only a duty. It fits `act` and `carry`.

## 4.2 The silence at the sea

The four camps at the sea (818): some wanted to throw themselves in, some to go back, some to fight, some to shout in prayer. To each came an answer. To the shouters: "be silent". The practice (ours, **proposed technique `mh_silent_trust`**): when you have done what is yours to do, stop adding noise. Sit, hands open, and let the words "He will fight for you, and you shall be silent" settle. This is not the guide's `quieting` of the mind. It is quieting of *effort*.

## 4.3 The dusk meditation

From 743 (ours, **proposed technique `mh_dusk`**): at sunset, watch the light go. Say to yourself: "Everything fades back to its Maker. He alone remains, One." At Shabbat mincha this is built into the words "You are One". It pairs well with S63 The world held.

## 4.4 Four ways of knowing Him (a ladder)

In chapter 21 His bond with Israel grows in four steps: He hears, He remembers, He sees, He knows. "אין דבר חבור יותר מן הידיעה" (494): "nothing joins more than knowing". This is the Maharal's form of da'at as bond (the Tanya's da'at as attachment, Tanya ch. 3).

## 4.5 The road, the desert, the sea

"לנסות כח שכלי, אם הוא יאמין" (809): the sea tested "the power of the mind, whether it will trust". The road wears down the body's habits, the desert trains the soul in awe, the sea tests the mind in trust. For the guide (ours): hard stretches of life can be read as one of these three.

## 4.6 Perfection that flows outward

"כל שלימות אשר יוצא לזולתו" (916) is the highest perfection. So a person should see "שיהיה שלימות שלו יוצא לזולתו" (918): "that his perfection should go out to others". The one with divine level cares for the "יתום ואלמנה" (938): "orphan and widow". This is his ground for S86.

## 5. The big human questions

**Purpose.** The world was made to be joined to Him and to return to Him (959-963). Man's perfection is to flow out to others (916-918).

**Suffering.** He wounds and heals (734). His "sadness" with us is how His Presence feels to a receiver in pain (535). Harmful forces "אין מזיקים שולטים רק בשניות" (780): "rule only where there is twoness". Where a person is at one with Him, they have no hold. (Ours: this is a teaching about inner oneness, not a promise of safety from all harm.)

**The soul.** "כל דבר שהוא בהסתר" (448): what is holy is hidden. The inner self is not on show. The core that stays unchanged (902) is what heals.

**Providence.** Particular care proves the One (676). Even Pharaoh's hardened heart is placed inside His plan, yet a sin done from the changeable body can be undone "ונחשב המעשה כלא היה מעולם" (662): "and the deed counts as if it never was".

**Trust.** The fathers' trust without questions (644-645); faith as clinging, "האמונה היא הדבקות בו יתברך" (832); the silence at the sea (818).

**Joy and sadness.** Every coming-into-being is joy (971). The festivals are joy because they are the world's bond to Him.

**Love and awe.** Love from joining, awe from separateness (789). Both in the tefillin, on arm and head.

**Righteousness shown.** "כאשר צדקתו בפעל" (502): when goodness is in act, it can be raised further. That is why trials come: to bring out what is there (see also 1399 in the next range).

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **Agree:** "No place empty" (540); creation renewed daily (961); before Him no being has existence (825); Yom Kippur returning us to the Only King (981).
- **Different key:** The Maharal names awe as coming from *separateness* (789, 1053) and love from *joining*. Chabad's Tanya ties lower awe to seeing His filling presence and higher awe to bittul. The two fit (ours): awe is the sense that He is not another thing among things.
- **Opposites:** "He wounds and He heals" (734) agrees with the Tanya's "all is good" (Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 11) but is argued differently: not "hidden good" but "the One is all, and all includes opposites".
- **Tzimtzum absent:** Nowhere here does he use contraction. His whole account runs on simple / separate / including.
