## 1. The book as one meditative journey

Likkutei Amarim walks a person from the inside of their own chest to the purpose of the whole world, and back into their hands. It starts where people actually are: split. A good impulse and a selfish one in the same hour, a prayer that moves you and fades by lunch. It names two souls, one of them "truly a part of God above" (line 86), and it refuses to let the struggle mean failure. The whole first stretch (chapters 1-17) builds a floor a person can stand on: the beinoni, the one in the middle, whose bad never wins in thought, speech or deed, because "the mind rules over the heart" (line 209).

Then the road goes down, under the mind. Chapters 18-19 find a love and a faith in every Jew that are above reason, a candle flame that "always flickers upward by its nature" (line 286). Chapters 20-22 give the unity itself: before Him everything counts "truly as zero" (line 301), and the root of every separation is feeling oneself "a thing in itself" (line 323). Chapters 23-32 turn that into daily inner work: Torah and the hand as His limbs, the faithfulness of the soul even in sin, joy over sadness, how to meet stray thoughts, when to lower the swollen self, and how to rise from that into the joy of a prince let out of prison, and into love of others, since "there is one Father to them all" (line 466).

Chapter 33 is the summit. It asks the reader to picture, slowly, that He alone fills this very place as before creation, and to rejoice in it as an inheritance: "even on the earth below there is nothing else besides Him, and this is His dwelling in the lower realms" (line 477). Everything after it comes back down. Why beinonim exist (35), why this dark world is the purpose (36), how deeds and charity lift the world (37), why intention is the soul of the deed (38-40), and then a series of practical contemplations for before prayer and study: awe before the King who looks at you, love as a son, mercy on the exiled spark, the mirror of water, the Shema as a daily exodus (41-49). It ends by teaching the return: "if your heart runs, return to the One" (line 730), and on the image of a wick whose burning is the light (line 762).

So the book goes from "I am at war with myself" to "my struggle is the wick, and He is the fire". The contemplation it asks for is mostly thinking through: taking a truth about God slowly, holding it with da'at until it warms (ch. 3, 41-43, 48). It also asks for picturing in the mind (line 472, line 668), for the Shema and prayer as the hour this happens, for self-giving (bitul: the flame, the soul handed back), for the heart's cry (plead like a child, ch. 28; cry to God from the soul's exile, ch. 31; weep like Jacob, ch. 45), and once, for song (the Levites' running and returning, line 728). Above all it asks that every height end in a deed. The book speaks to Jewish souls in its own terms; the meditation manual says how its sittings are fitted to someone of another faith or someone unsure.

## Title page (lines 14-18)

**The movement.** It sets the promise of the whole book before a single argument: what is asked of you is not far away. It lowers the mountain before you start to climb.

> כי קרוב אליך הדבר מאוד, בפיך ובלבבך לעשותו
> *For the thing is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, to do it.*

**Contemplating it.** Take it wide first. "The thing" is the whole of serving God, and the verse says all of it is near. Then step by step. It is near in your mouth, in words you can say. It is near in your heart, in a love that is already there. It is near in doing, in your hands. Then to the root. Why near? Because, as the book will show, what you are looking for is already inside you: a part of God above (ch. 2) and a love hidden in every Jewish heart (ch. 18). Now bring it home. Your mouth is here at this table. Your hands are here. The book is not asking you to become someone else first. The picture of "near" breaks in a good way. Near usually means a short distance. Here the book will show there is no distance at all, only forgetting.

**From inside.** A heaviness lifts: the old sense that holiness belongs to other people. In its place comes a small, plain confidence: this is for me, and it is for today.

**At the edge.** Not yet. This is a doorway. It points ahead to a love the book will place above reason (ch. 18-19).

**Into the day.** Say one sentence of prayer or Torah out loud today, slowly, as the "mouth" part of "very near".

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Compiler's Foreword (lines 48-64)

**The movement.** It turns a book into a letter. The Alter Rebbe writes as someone who has heard people pour out their hearts, and it warms the reader into a relationship before any teaching begins.

> כולהו מתקשראן באורייתא, ואורייתא – היא המקשרת אותן להקדוש ברוך הוא
> *All of them are bound up with the Torah, and the Torah is what binds them to the Holy One, blessed be He.*

**Contemplating it.** He begins with an honest worry: hearing a teacher is not like reading a book, because a reader reads through his own mind, and a confused mind can hardly see the light hidden in books (line 52). Then he says every soul, down to "the lightest spark" of the people, is tied into the Torah, and the Torah ties it to God (line 54). He wrote these pages as answers to the questions people brought him in private, so each reader could keep them "between his eyes" (lines 58-60). And if you can't understand, bring it to the wise people of your town, and let them not hide behind false humility (line 61). Bring it home. The question you carry about your inner life is one of the questions this book was written to answer. Picture a rope. You are one thread, the rope is the Torah, the rope is tied to the King. The picture breaks, because a rope is outside the one who holds it, and the book will say the Torah is one with Him (ch. 4-5).

**From inside.** Being spoken to. The sense that someone thought about your question before you asked it, and that you are one thread in a whole people, not alone.

**At the edge.** Not yet. It names the Torah as the bond, and chapters 4-5 carry that bond to "a union like no other".

**Into the day.** Bring one real question about your inner life to someone wiser this week.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 1 (lines 66-85)

**The movement.** It empties out a false picture: that a good person has no war inside. It opens the question the whole book answers, and names two souls in every Jew.

> דלכל איש ישראל, אחד צדיק ואחד רשע, יש שתי נשמות
> *Every Jew, righteous or wicked, has two souls.*

**Contemplating it.** The chapter opens with an oath given before birth: "Be righteous... and even if the whole world tells you that you are righteous, be in your own eyes like one who is wicked" (line 68). It asks at once: won't that make a person sad, so he can't serve God with joy? (line 70). The book takes the danger of sadness seriously from its first page. Then five kinds of people, and the beinoni, the one in the middle, whom the book will spend chapters defining. Then the root of the answer: two souls. One comes from the shell (kelipah, what hides God) and lives in the blood. From it come anger, pride, lust, empty talk, laziness and sadness (line 82), and also a person's natural kindness and mercy (line 83). Bring it home. The impatience you felt this morning and the kindness you felt an hour later are not proof that you are a fraud. They are two voices with two sources. The picture of "two souls" breaks if you imagine two people: it is one person, one body, "a small city" that both want to rule (ch. 9).

**From inside.** Relief: the pull inside is not the whole of you. A quiet honesty follows. You can look at your anger without denying it and without being it.

**At the edge.** It does not reach the edge. This chapter maps the ground. (Line 85 says the souls of the nations come from shells with no good in them. The book does not reconcile this here, and neither do we.)

**Into the day.** When a harsh feeling rises today, name it silently, "that's the animal soul talking", and don't argue with it.

## Chapter 2 (lines 86-99)

**The movement.** It goes under everything you have decided about yourself to the floor: the second soul is truly a part of God. Then it widens from you to every Jew, down to the simplest.

> ונפש השנית בישראל, היא חלק אלוה ממעל ממש
> *The second soul in a Jew is truly a part of God above.*

**Contemplating it.** Wide first: this is said of every Jew, the lightest and least learned too (line 91). Step by step: "He blew into his nostrils". One who blows, blows from inside himself (line 86). Your soul came out of His inwardness, like breath from deep in the chest. To the root: the soul comes from His wisdom, and He and His wisdom are one (line 87). "A person cannot understand this fully" (line 89). The picture: a child comes from the father's mind. Even the toenails grew from that first drop, and they are still fed from the head (lines 92-93). So the lowest soul stays bound "in a wonderful and mighty union" with its source (line 95). The picture breaks: a child becomes a separate person, and the soul never leaves the unity. Bring it home. Look at your hands on the table. The one who moves them has a root in God's own wisdom.

**From inside.** A floor under the floor. What you thought about yourself stays true on its own level, but there is something under it that nothing has touched. The breath picture can leave a person feeling carried, as if breathed out from close by.

**At the edge.** The text says it outright: the oneness of the knower and the known cannot be grasped by a human mind (line 89). What is left is to hold it, not grasp it. Later Chassidus calls this deepest point of the soul yechidah.

**Into the day.** Before you get up from the table, say quietly: "Under all of this, a part of God above."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Chapter 3 (lines 100-114)

**The movement.** It teaches how contemplation actually works. Wisdom and understanding give birth to awe and love, and the hinge is da'at, the mind that holds on. It turns thinking into a practice.

> ויתקע מחשבתו בחוזק בגדולת אין סוף ברוך הוא, ואינו מסיח דעתו
> *He fastens his thought firmly on the greatness of the Infinite, blessed be He, and does not let his mind drift from it.*

**Contemplating it.** Chochmah is the first flash, read as "koach mah", the power of "what is it?" Binah unfolds it "to its truth and its depth" (lines 104-105). The first thing the book gives the mind to hold: how He fills all worlds, surrounds all worlds, and all before Him counts as nothing (line 107). From that comes awe, then love "like flames of fire" (line 108), and even the soul's longing to expire into Him (line 109). The warning: even a wise person who knows all this, if he does not bind his mind and hold it there, gets "false imaginings" instead of real love and awe (line 113). Bring it home. Think of one thing you truly know about God. Now hold it the way you'd hold a full cup, without putting it down, for one minute. The picture of "father and mother" giving birth (line 106) breaks here: the children are feelings, and they are born only if the parents stay together long enough.

**From inside.** What the text describes: when the mind stays, something warms. First a kind of shyness before a greatness with no end, then a pull toward it. If nothing warms, the staying still counts.

**At the edge.** The object is Ein Sof, what has no end. The mind reaches its edge by trying to hold what has no edge. Beyond it the text names love as "the soul expiring" (line 109).

**Into the day.** Pick one line of today's prayer and don't let your mind leave it until you've finished it.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Chapter 4 (lines 115-128)

**The movement.** It brings the endless God down into three garments: the thought, speech and action of the mitzvot. It turns the Infinite into something you can embrace.

> הרי זה כמחבק את המלך דרך משל … מאחר שגוף המלך בתוכם
> *It is like embracing the King... since the King's body is inside them.*

**Contemplating it.** The Torah is His will and wisdom, and He and His wisdom are one (line 119). No thought grasps Him at all (line 120). "Where you find His greatness, there you find His humility." He narrowed His will into 613 mitzvot, into letters of ink on parchment (lines 121, 124). The Torah is like water running from a high place to a low one (line 123). The picture: if you embrace a king, it makes no difference whether he wears one robe or many, since the king is inside them (line 127). The mitzvah is the robe. He is inside. One hour of return and good deeds in this world is better than all of the World to Come, because there only a glow is felt, and here you hold Him Himself (line 126). Bring it home: the strap, the coin you give, the page you read. Each is a sleeve with the King's arm in it. The picture breaks where a king's body has an end; what is inside this robe has none.

**From inside.** The sense of being held, wrapped "from head to foot" in God's light (line 125). A closeness that does not wait on feeling it.

**At the edge.** Yes: "no thought grasps Him at all" (line 120). The edge is crossed not by thinking but by doing. In the mitzvah you hold what the mind can't.

**Into the day.** When you do one mitzvah today, stop for a moment and say: "The King is inside this."

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 5 (lines 129-141)

**The movement.** It shows what happens when you truly understand one law of the Torah: you hold God's wisdom and it holds you. A union closer than any closeness of bodies.

> והוא יחוד נפלא שאין יחוד כמוהו ולא כערכו נמצא כלל בגשמיות
> *This is a wonderful union, with no union like it and nothing that compares to it anywhere in the physical world.*

**Contemplating it.** When you understand something, your mind wraps around it, and your mind is wrapped in it (lines 130-131). Take a law in the Talmud: if Reuven claims this and Shimon claims that, the ruling is this (line 132). Even if that case never happens, it is His will. Knowing it, your mind holds His will (line 133). So the union goes both ways, "from every side and angle" (line 134). The Torah is called bread. Bread becomes your own flesh, and learning becomes the life of the soul, from the Infinite who is inside it (line 138). Bring it home: the one line you learned today is now inside you like food. The picture breaks: bread is used up, and this food is God's own wisdom, which does not grow less.

**From inside.** Study becomes intimate. What felt like dry learning can be felt from the inside as being fed.

**At the edge.** His wisdom is grasped "only as it is clothed in the laws laid out before us" (line 133). The mind touches the Infinite only by holding something small and exact.

**Into the day.** Learn one law today slowly, until you could explain it to someone else.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 6 (lines 142-153)

**The movement.** It draws the line between holiness and its opposite. Holiness is what is given over to Him; the "other side" is what stands on its own. It turns the mind toward bitul, self-giving.

> ואין הקדוש ברוך הוא שורה אלא על דבר שבטל אצלו יתברך
> *The Holy One, blessed be He, rests only on a thing that is nullified before Him.*

**Contemplating it.** "The other side" (sitra achra) means everything thought, said or done that is not for God (line 145). Bitul can be actual, as in the angels, or waiting inside, as in every Jew, who can give his life for God (line 146). Even what is separate lives from Him, from His "back", in exile inside it, so it will not fall back into nothing (line 148). A note in the text adds: the Infinite fills even this lowest earth, clothed through the four worlds (line 150). Bring it home: the talk you had today that was only for your own glory. God was there too, hidden, holding it in being. The picture of His "back" breaks, since it suggests He turned away, and the note says He fills it all.

**From inside.** A clear sense of the two directions any moment can take. The part of you that wants to be something grows quieter.

**At the edge.** It touches the edge at line 148: without His hidden life everything would be "nothing and zero, as before it was created".

**Into the day.** Before one thing you're about to do today, ask: "Who is this for?"

## Chapter 7 (lines 154-169)

**The movement.** It makes the ordinary, permitted world hopeful. What you use for God rises with you, and even what you used only for yourself is "not tied down". It also names hard limits and keeps them.

> כלומר שאינו קשור ואסור בידי החיצונים שלא יוכל לחזור ולעלות לה׳
> *That is, it is not tied and bound in the hands of the outer forces, so that it could not return and rise to God.*

**Contemplating it.** The "glowing shell" (kelipat nogah) is in between. It can fall with the fully unclean shells or rise into holiness (lines 155-156). The text's examples: festive food eaten to widen the mind for God or to honour Shabbat rises "like an offering"; a joke told to open students' hearts before learning rises too (lines 157-158). "Heter", permitted, is read as "untied". Even what was taken only for pleasure can rise again when the person returns to serving God (line 159). The limit is kept: what is forbidden stays bound, and only return from great love turns sins into merits (lines 162-164). Bring it home: tonight's supper, a funny story. They are waiting to go where you go. The picture of "tied" breaks, since nothing is outside Him. "Bound" means hidden too deep to be lifted now.

**From inside.** A tenderness toward ordinary things. The day stops splitting into holy hours and wasted ones.

**At the edge.** It does not go far, but line 162 describes the thirst of one who returns from far away, "like a dry and weary land", greater than the thirst of the righteous: love that grows from distance.

**Into the day.** Before one meal today say: "I'm eating this so I'll have strength for good things."

## Chapter 8 (lines 170-180)

**The movement.** A sobering chapter. It makes you notice what you take into yourself, because it becomes you.

> להיות כי מכל מאכל ומשקה נעשה תיכף דם ובשר מבשרו
> *For everything one eats and drinks immediately becomes blood and flesh of his flesh.*

**Contemplating it.** Forbidden food eaten even by mistake, even with holy intent, does not rise (line 170). Empty talk soils the heart's traits. Foreign wisdoms soil the mind itself (lines 178-179), unless they are used "as an axe to cut with", to earn a living so as to serve God, or in the service of God and His Torah (line 180). The text speaks of the cleansing of the grave and of punishments. We don't dwell on them, but they show how seriously the book takes what enters a person. Bring it home: what did you feed your mind today, and what is it doing in you now? The picture of food becoming blood breaks: a thought needs no stomach. It enters the moment you let it stay.

**From inside.** A clean kind of care, not fear. A wish to guard the doors of the self.

**At the edge.** It does not reach the edge. It is a chapter of limits, and keeps them.

**Into the day.** Put down one thing you read or watch today that only fills time.

## Chapter 9 (lines 181-188)

**The movement.** It draws the map of the inner war: two kings, one small city. Then it shows the hope: a heart so full of love that it spills into the other side and changes it.

> שיהיה תוכו רצוף אהבה מלא וגדוש עד שתתפשט גם לחלל השמאלי
> *That his inside be paved with love, full and overflowing, until it spreads even into the left chamber.*

**Contemplating it.** The animal soul lives in the left chamber of the heart, full of blood. The divine soul lives in the brain and in the right chamber (lines 181-183). Love of God burns "like flames of fire" in those who understand and contemplate (line 184). Two kings fight over one city, the body, each wanting every limb to obey (line 185). The divine soul wants more than to win. It wants to change the other: to turn the craving for this world's pleasures into love of God, "with both your inclinations" (line 185). Higher still is "love in delights", tasting God, which turns the bad "completely good" (line 186). Line 188: the animal soul wants the opposite "for the person's good", so he will overcome it. Bring it home: the craving you fought today was sent to be won. The picture of war breaks here, because the enemy is meant to be turned, not destroyed.

**From inside.** Not hatred of yourself but hope for yourself: that the force of your wanting can become love.

**At the edge.** "Love in delights" is beyond effort. The book will call it a gift (line 233). Here it is pictured, not promised.

**Into the day.** When a strong wanting comes today, say: "This much wanting can also go to You."

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Chapter 10 (lines 189-196)

**The movement.** It describes the righteous who turn darkness to light and bitter to sweet, and a higher aim: not to quench my own thirst for God, but to join God and His Presence down here.

> די חשוכא מהפכן לנהורא וטעמין מרירו למיתקא
> *Who turn darkness into light and make the bitter taste sweet.*

**Contemplating it.** The incomplete tzaddik still has a tiny speck of bad, bent and nullified by the good (line 189). The complete tzaddik has turned it to good, by taking off its "filthy garments", the pleasures it wears (line 190). Rabbi Shimon: "I have seen the sons of ascent, and they are few" (line 194). They "ascend" because they lift the bad up into holiness. Their service is also for above: "a chasid is one who is kind to his Maker", joining the Holy One and His Presence "in the lower realms", like a son who gives himself for his father and mother (line 195). Bring it home: whose thirst does your prayer quench, yours or His? The picture of the son breaks: God lacks nothing. The "need above" is His own wish.

**From inside.** A widening. Service can be for Him, not only for my own closeness to Him.

**At the edge.** The joining of the Holy One with His Presence in the lower realms (line 195) is named as the reason. It lies beyond feeling, in His will.

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah today only because He wants it.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Chapter 11 (lines 197-204)

**The movement.** It walks through the kinds of "wicked", and at the far end finds the divine soul still there, hovering above. No one is outside.

> עד שנסתלק מקרבו ועומד בבחינת מקיף עליו מלמעלה
> *Until it has left his inside and stands as a surrounding presence (makif) above him.*

**Contemplating it.** There is one whose good bows to the bad only now and then, and one who sins more but is full of regret (lines 200-202). "The wicked are full of regrets": the good is still in them (line 202). And the one who never regrets: the good has left his inside, but it stands above him, surrounding him (line 203). Then the chapter ends: "in every gathering of ten the Presence rests" (line 204). Bring it home: the person you have given up on. Over him, too, a soul waits. The picture breaks: "above" is not a place. It is what has not yet come inside.

**From inside.** Mercy. Less contempt, for others and for your own worst self.

**At the edge.** This is the book's first use of makif, what surrounds rather than enters. What does not fit inside a person still holds him from beyond.

**Into the day.** Think of one person you've written off, and wish him well in silence.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Chapter 12 (lines 205-217)

**The movement.** It defines the beinoni: the bad never wins in thought, speech or deed. It shows the mind's rule over the heart, and it ends in kindness to others.

> כי המוח שליט על הלב
> *For the mind rules over the heart.*

**Contemplating it.** At the Shema and in prayer the beinoni binds his mind to the greatness of the Infinite, and the bad bows (line 207). After prayer it wakes up again (line 208). But it can't act, because the mind rules the heart by nature, by the way a person is made (line 209). Like light: a little light pushes away much darkness by itself (line 210). When a bad thought rises, he pushes it away "with both hands" the moment he notices (line 215). When resentment or anger toward someone rises, he does not let it into his mind; he answers with kindness, and even repays harm with good, as Yosef did (lines 216-217). Bring it home: the grudge that rose at the table today. You don't have to feel differently to act differently. The picture of light breaks: light ends darkness, and in the beinoni the dark only hides.

**From inside.** Dignity. You are more than your moods.

**At the edge.** It does not reach the edge. It is the ground floor of freedom.

**Into the day.** Answer one irritation today with a kind sentence.

## Chapter 13 (lines 218-231)

**The movement.** It lets the beinoni stop pretending. The bad in him is a sleeper who can wake, and his love in prayer passes. And yet, for his level, that love is truth.

> והריני קורא באהבתם שבתפלתם גם כן ״שפת אמת תכון לעד״
> *And I call their love in their prayer, too, "a lip of truth that stands forever".*

**Contemplating it.** The inclination is only a judge giving an opinion. The other judge is the divine soul, and God decides (lines 219-220). So be "like one who is wicked" in your own eyes: don't believe the world when it says the bad in you is gone (line 222). This is realism, not self-hatred. In the beinoni the bad sleeps during the Shema and prayer, and it can wake (line 226). Next to the righteous, a love that fades after prayer is not called "true service". But the Alter Rebbe says: I call it true, since his soul can wake it again every day (lines 228-229). Truth is Jacob's "middle bar" that runs through every level, "an inheritance without borders" (lines 230-231). Bring it home: the prayer that moved you this morning and faded by lunch was real.

**From inside.** Relief from two lies at once: that you are finished, and that you are fake.

**At the edge.** "Truth without borders" (line 231) points upward with no end: every level is as nothing next to the one above it.

**Into the day.** Tomorrow, pray one line with the attention of your best day, and trust it.

## Chapter 14 (lines 232-239)

**The movement.** It opens the beinoni's door to everyone, at every moment. The key is a refusal: I don't want to be cut off from the One.

> כי אינני רוצה להיות מובדל ונפרד חס ושלום מה׳ אחד בשום אופן
> *For I do not want, in any way, to be separated and cut off from the One God.*

**Contemplating it.** Anyone can be a beinoni at any time: not in what the heart feels but in what one does, says and thinks (line 232). When the heart wants something wrong, say to it: I don't want to be wicked even for a moment, because I don't want to be cut off from the One (line 232). Even the lightest Jew can give his life for God. "I am surely not less than him" (line 232). Truly loathing the bad, which makes a tzaddik, is like a reward and not given to everyone (line 233). Still, set times to think of God's greatness, "even if only in imaginings", and do your part (line 238). "Habit rules everything and becomes second nature" (line 239). Bring it home: say the sentence to a real pull of this week.

**From inside.** A quiet strength that does not hang on mood. The feeling of choosing to belong.

**At the edge.** Beyond the beinoni's work is "a spirit from on high" (line 239), which is given, not made.

**Into the day.** Say the sentence of line 232 once, out loud, at a moment of pull.

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Chapter 15 (lines 240-251)

## Chapter 16 (lines 252-262)

**The movement.** It tells the beinoni what to do when the heart won't catch fire: understand with the mind, let that bring you to act, and God joins the good thought to the deed.

> *It is fitting to Him that every living soul should yearn to Him, to cleave to Him and be included in His light.*

**Contemplating it.** Since everything before Him is truly nothing, it is fitting that every soul should long to melt into Him (line 256). Our own souls want to leave their sheath, the body. They are held there against their will, "like living widows" (line 256). No thought grasps Him except as He is clothed in Torah and mitzvot, like embracing the King. So it is fitting to embrace Him with all your heart, soul and might, and that means doing the mitzvot (line 256). When this understanding brings you to act, it gives the deeds "wings to fly upward" (line 257). God joins a good thought to the deed (lines 258-259). Bring it home: you didn't feel on fire today, and still you went to learn. The understanding that sent you is the wing.

**From inside.** Permission not to feel much, and a sense that what you understood matters.

**At the edge.** The soul's wish to melt into Him is the edge. The text turns it into an embrace made of deeds.

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah today because you understood it is fitting, feeling or no feeling.

## Chapter 18 (lines 271-284)

**The movement.** It goes under understanding altogether. Even one who can understand nothing of God has a love and a faith above reason, because the Infinite is clothed in the soul's chochmah.

**Contemplating it.** Even one whose mind is short can serve from the hidden love inherited from the fathers (line 272). Every Jew, even the lowest, received at least the lowest level of holiness, and inside it chochmah, where the light of the Infinite truly shines (lines 274-277). Chochmah is "koach mah", what is not yet grasped. So the Infinite, whom no thought grasps, clothes itself there (line 280). So every Jew believes. Faith is above knowing, and before God all are simple: "I was a beast with You, and I am always with You" (line 281). Even those far away give their lives rather than deny the One, without reasons (lines 282-284). Bring it home: something you know about God without ever having worked it out.

## Chapter 19 (lines 285-298)

**The movement.** The candle flame. It shows the soul's natural pull home, even to going out, and says this pull is above reason.

> *Like the light of a candle, which always flickers upward by its nature.*

**Contemplating it.** The flame wants to leave the wick and join its root above, even though it would go out (lines 286-287). So the soul wants to leave the body and cling to God, even to become nothing there (line 288). "Nature" here means what is above reason and knowing (lines 289-290). Holiness is what is not a thing in itself (line 291). Its opposite says "give, give", I want to be something (line 292). This love is "hidden", covered by a sackcloth of shell in those who sin. It sleeps, and in a test of faith it wakes, and all the shells are as if they never were (lines 293-296). Bring it home: light a candle tonight and watch it. The picture breaks: a flame that reaches its root is gone, and a soul in God is included, not destroyed.

**From inside.** Watching the flame, a person may feel the ache of their own soul upward, a homesickness without words.

**At the edge.** Bitul in being: wanting to be nothing in the source. Above reason, where the Infinite is clothed in the soul's chochmah (line 290).

**Into the day.** Light a candle, for Shabbat or just to sit by, and watch it for one minute with this line.

## Chapter 20 (lines 299-306)

**The movement.** The book's central definition of the unity. Nothing changed in Him when the world was made. Before Him all is nothing, like one word next to the whole soul.

> משום דכולא קמיה כלא חשיב, וכאין ואפס ממש
> *Because before Him everything counts as nothing, truly as zero.*

**Contemplating it.** "You were before the world was created, You are since it was created": the same, with no change (line 300). All the worlds exist only by His word clothed in them (line 302). The picture: one word you speak is nothing next to your power of speech, less next to your thought, and nothing at all next to your soul itself (line 303). Before a wish becomes words, it is only a simple wanting in the heart (line 305). Bring it home: say one word now. Next to all that you are, that word is nothing. So is everything, next to Him.

**From inside.** The world thins out, not into unreality, but into something small and held.

**At the edge.** Yes. "Before Him" is His side, not ours. The next chapter says where the picture of speech breaks.

**Into the day.** Say one word aloud slowly today and remember: all this is one word of His.

## Chapter 21 (lines 307-315)

**The movement.** It breaks the picture of chapter 20: His word never leaves Him. The hiding hides only from us. Before Him darkness is like light.

> אך לגבי הקדוש ברוך הוא, אין שום צמצום והסתר והעלם מסתיר ומעלים לפניו
> *But as for the Holy One, blessed be He, no contraction, hiding or concealment hides or conceals anything before Him.*

**Contemplating it.** Our words leave us. His don't, "for nothing is outside Him, and no place is empty of Him" (line 308). "Speech" is only a picture: going out from hidden to revealed (line 309). His speech stays one with Him after creation as before. The change is only for the creatures (line 312). The contractions are a "hiding of the face" so that we can receive (line 313). But before Him darkness is like light, because the garments are of Him, like the snail whose shell is part of it (line 314). Bring it home: the day that felt empty of God. From His side nothing was hidden at all.

**From inside.** A loosening of fear about the dark times.

**At the edge.** "For Havayah is Elokim" (line 314): the Name that reveals and the Name that hides are one. The mind cannot hold both at once; faith holds it.

**Into the day.** At one dark moment today say: "Even darkness is not dark to You."

## Chapter 22 (lines 316-323)

**The movement.** It names the root of every separation: feeling yourself a thing of your own. It empties the mind of the self as a separate thing.

> כי עיקר ושרש עבודה זרה, הוא מה שנחשב לדבר בפני עצמו
> *For the essence and root of idolatry is being regarded as a thing in itself.*

**Contemplating it.** The Torah calls it real "speech", because this is truly how life comes down to the creatures, through many contractions (line 316). The hiding is so strong that even shells can exist, fed from His "back", like something thrown over the shoulder to an enemy (lines 317-320). The shell says "I am, and there is none besides me" (line 322). So arrogance is like idolatry: not denying God, but counting yourself as something apart (line 323). Bring it home: the moment today when you needed the credit. That was the whole of idolatry in miniature.

**From inside.** A lightness, like putting down a load you didn't know you were carrying.

**At the edge.** Line 323 ends: everything is truly nullified to Him, who "brings them into being from nothing always". The self has no ground of its own.

**Into the day.** Let someone else take the credit for something today.

## Chapter 23 (lines 324-337)

**The movement.** When you do a mitzvah your hand becomes a chariot, and when you learn Torah your mind becomes one with God. It brings awe into study.

> אורייתא וקודשא בריך הוא – כולא חד
> *The Torah and the Holy One, blessed be He, are all one.*

**Contemplating it.** The mitzvot are "the King's limbs" (line 324). Limbs obey the soul at once, with no order given (line 327). So the hand giving charity and the feet walking to a mitzvah become a chariot (line 329). Torah is more than a limb: His will is the law itself that you think and speak (line 331). In learning, the surrounding light, which no world can hold, is revealed in the soul, more than in the upper worlds. You don't see it, and that is why you can bear it (line 334). From this a great awe can come in study (line 336). Not every mind can bear it, and its absence does not hold back the deed (line 337). Bring it home: the hand that gave today was a limb of the King.

**From inside.** Awe at a plain page of Talmud. The hands feel different.

**At the edge.** Sovev, what cannot be clothed in worlds, is inside the one who learns (line 334), "though he does not see".

**Into the day.** Before you learn today, stop and say: "This is one with Him."

**The movement.** The sharpest mirror in the book. Even a small sin separates more than the shells, which never disobey. And yet, even then, the soul stayed faithful.

## Chapter 29 (lines 403-428)

**Contemplating it.** A dull heart: the soul's light isn't strong enough to rule the body's coarseness, because the shell lifts itself up (lines 403-407). In the beinoni the animal soul is "the person himself". The pure soul is "given in me" (lines 408-409). Hillel fed his body as a kindness to a poor stranger (line 410). **From inside.** A sharpness, like cold water on the face, and then clarity: the spot where you stand becomes a place He fills.

**At the edge.** "Sensory sight" of the unity (line 422) is the edge of knowing: not proof, but seeing.

**Into the day.** Stand still in one ordinary place today and say: "He fills this very place."

## Chapter 31 (lines 447-464)

**The movement.** After the lowering comes the turn to joy: "I am far, but within me is a part of God, in exile." The descent is for the sake of an ascent.

But a part of God is within me, in exile. The farther I am, the greater its exile and the greater the pity on it (line 455). Its return in Torah and prayer is the joy of a prince freed from prison to his father's house (line 458). Don't mix the soul's joy with the body's sorrow (line 459). "I did not make myself. Why did God put a part of His light, which fills and surrounds all worlds, into a snake's skin? This descent is for the sake of an ascent" (line 463). Bring it home: the heaviest thing about you right now is also the measure of how far the spark came down to lift it.

**From inside.** Two things at once: honest sorrow and real joy, and a lightening of the whole chest.

**At the edge.** Line 463: the light that fills and surrounds all worlds, clothed in a snake's skin. The mind can't hold why. The text answers: descent for ascent.

**Into the day.** Learn a few lines of Torah today "to bring the part of God home".

## Chapter 36 (lines 506-516)

**The movement.** The purpose of everything. Not the high worlds but this one, the darkest, where light will shine out of darkness.

> אלא התכלית הוא עולם הזה התחתון
> *Rather, the purpose is this lowest world.*

**Contemplating it.** He desired a dwelling in the lower realms (line 506). But above and below do not apply to Him (line 507). He fills this place as He did before creation. The change is only for those who receive, through many garments (line 508). This world is the lowest in hiding, "doubled and redoubled darkness" (line 509). The purpose is here: that light shine in the place of darkness, with the advantage of light that comes from darkness (line 510). At Sinai, "you were shown": truly seen, the people "saw the voices" (line 514). Bring it home: the hardest, plainest part of your life is the purpose, not a distraction from it.

**From inside.** A reversal: the lowest places begin to glow with meaning.

**At the edge.** "There is nothing else besides Him", seen with the eyes (line 514), and the future's revelation with no garments.

**Into the day.** Do something good today in a place you think of as "not spiritual".

## Chapter 37 (lines 517-545)

**The movement.** Our deeds now build the future. The mitzvah is its own reward. Charity lifts a whole life. Learning is calling Him to come.

> וכשנותנן לצדקה – הרי כל נפשו החיונית עולה לה׳
> *And when he gives it to charity, his whole vital soul rises to God.*

**Contemplating it.** The completion depends on our deeds throughout the exile (line 517). In doing a mitzvah you draw the Infinite down into a physical thing: the parchment, the etrog, the coin (lines 518-521). Speaking aloud matters: "all my bones shall say" (lines 523-524). Each soul has its share of the world to lift, through what it uses for serving God (line 531). Charity: all the strength you spent earning it goes up (line 535), and even money that could have bought your own life (line 536). Study is "calling" Him, like a small child calling his father to be with him and not be left alone (line 544). Bring it home: the money in your pocket holds hours of your life.

**From inside.** A sense that small acts have weight, and that the world is waiting on them.

**At the edge.** The Infinite clothed in physical things (line 518), and the revelation still to come.

**Into the day.** Give some charity today and think: the hours I worked go up with this.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 38 (lines 546-560)

**The movement.** Intention is the soul of the deed. Even stones hold His light, but a prayer with intention is to a prayer without it as a living person to a stone.

> תפלה או שאר ברכה בלא כוונה – הן כגוף בלא נשמה
> *Prayer, or any blessing, without intention is like a body without a soul.*

**Contemplating it.** Speech without intention fulfils the duty; thought without speech does not. The reason: the soul needs no fixing, and the body does (lines 546-547). Even stones and dust have light and life from Him, or they would fall back into nothing (line 548). One light is in all, more or less hidden: mineral, plant, animal, speaker (lines 551-552). Two levels of intention: one who understands God's greatness, like the soul of a speaking being, and one who only wakes the natural hidden love, like the soul of an animal (lines 559-560). Bring it home: the same words at your table, said with intention, come alive.

**From inside.** The words of prayer begin to feel as though they have insides.

**At the edge.** One simple will is in both deed and intention, "with no change or multiplicity" (line 556).

**Into the day.** Choose one blessing today and say it knowing what each word means.

## Chapter 39 (lines 561-582)

**The movement.** Where service rises. Without at least a stir of love and awe it stays in the world of separation. But nothing is lost: once you learn it for His sake, the old learning joins in.

> מיד שחוזר ולומד דבר זה לשמה, הרי גם מה שלמד בסתם, מתחבר ומצטרף ללימוד זה ופרחא לעילא
> *As soon as he goes back and learns this for its own sake, what he learned plainly also joins this learning and flies upward.*

**Contemplating it.** Souls who serve with understood love and awe dwell in the world of Beriah; with natural love, in Yetzirah (lines 561-568). Great tzaddikim are a chariot, nullified into His light (line 573). Without any love or awe, even in thought, service stays below in "the world of separation" (line 576). Study for one's own honour stays in exile until return (line 579). Plain study joins as soon as you learn it once for His sake (line 581). Bring it home: years of prayer or learning done out of habit are waiting to be lifted by one moment of meaning.

**From inside.** A sense that nothing is wasted.

**At the edge.** Atzilut, above understanding (lines 570-573). The text adds: "we have no business with hidden things" (line 575).

**Into the day.** Before you learn today, say once: "For Your sake."

## Chapter 40 (lines 583-597)

**The movement.** Love and awe are the wings; Torah and mitzvot are the bird. And if you thirst for God, drink from the river you are standing in.

> הרי זה כמי שעומד בנהר וצועק: ״מים! מים לשתות!״
> *He is like one standing in a river and crying, "Water! Water to drink!"*

**Contemplating it.** He fills all worlds equally; the difference is in those who receive (line 587). Words of Torah without intention stay very contracted; with intention they rise (lines 588-589). The wings are not the bird. The head and body are; the wings let it fly (line 594). Without love and awe the bird is a chick that can't fly (line 592). Love without service is "love in delights", a taste of reward; today is for doing (line 596). If you thirst for God and don't drink from the Torah in front of you, you are standing in a river crying for water (line 597). Bring it home: the yearning you feel, and the book on your shelf.

**From inside.** Longing turns into drinking.

**At the edge.** It does not reach the edge. It is practical.

**Into the day.** Next time you long for closeness, open a book and learn a few lines.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 41 (lines 598-625)

**The movement.** The practice before any act: stand before the King who fills the earth and looks at you, take on His kingship, then hand your soul back to Him.

> לכן, מעתה אני מוסרה ומחזירה לך לייחדה באחדותך
> *Therefore, from now I hand it over and return it to You, to unite it with Your oneness.*

**Contemplating it.** First wake the natural awe: think of the Infinite's kingship over all worlds, and that He chose you, "the world was created for me" (line 599). "God stands over him, the whole earth is full of His glory, and He looks at him" (line 600). Think on this as long as you can before learning or putting on tefillin (line 601). The Infinite is clothed in these letters, in these threads. By reading or wearing them you draw His light onto the part of God inside your body (line 602). Love alone is one wing; awe alone is one wing (lines 610-611). "My God, the soul You gave me... You breathed it into me and You will take it from me": so from now I hand it back to You (line 621). Every hour a new flow of life comes down (line 623). Bring it home: tomorrow morning, before your eyes are fully open.

**From inside.** Standing up straight inside, and then a letting go, like handing something precious back to its owner.

**At the edge.** Giving the soul over in study and prayer is like the soul leaving the body at the end of life (line 620): a small dying into Him.

**Into the day.** Say "My God, the soul You gave me" tomorrow as a handing back.

## Chapter 42 (lines 626-645)

**The movement.** What knowing (da'at) really is: tying your thought to God as firmly as to something you see. Everything you see is the King's outer clothing.

> אשר כל מה שרואה בעיניו, השמים והארץ ומלואה, הכל הם לבושים החיצונים של המלך הקדוש ברוך הוא
> *That everything he sees with his eyes, the heavens and the earth and all that fills it, all are the outer garments of the King, the Holy One, blessed be He.*

**Contemplating it.** Every soul has a spark of Moshe, who brings knowledge of God to each by his measure (lines 627-628). Da'at is not only knowing from books. It is binding the mind to God with a strong bond, as bound as it is to a thing you see (line 629). That takes toil of body and of soul, staying long (lines 630-631), digging as for buried treasure (line 633). He knows all things, the way a person feels even his own toe; "only to soothe the ear", since He is not affected by anything (lines 635-638). You fear a king for the life inside him, seen through his clothes. So with heaven and earth (line 641). The main thing is habit: "emunah", faith, from "training", like a craftsman's trained hands (line 644). Bring it home: look at the window. The King's outer clothing.

**From inside.** The world begins to look like a garment with someone inside it.

**At the edge.** Line 638: "all Israel are believers... without any human inquiry". Understanding reaches its edge and faith carries the rest.

**Into the day.** Look at the sky once today and say: "His outer clothing."

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Chapter 43 (lines 646-656)

**The movement.** Two awes and two loves. Lower awe comes from the worlds; higher awe from seeing each thing being born from nothing. Love that rises by itself as a gift, and love made by understanding.

> ״איזהו חכם הרואה את הנולד״, פירוש, שרואה כל דבר איך נולד ונתהוה מאין ליש
> *"Who is wise? One who sees what is being born": he sees each thing, how it is born and comes to be from nothing into something.*

**Contemplating it.** Lower awe comes from thinking about the vastness of the worlds, five hundred years' journey from earth to sky (line 647). Higher awe, a kind of shame, comes from the Godliness inside the worlds: seeing each thing born from nothing by His word (line 648). So heaven and earth, and your own body and soul, are nullified in His word like sunlight in the sun (line 649). "Great love" is a flame that rises by itself, a gift; "love of the world" comes from understanding (lines 651-652). Bring it home: your cup, your hand, being born from nothing now.

**From inside.** A hush, a kind of blushing before Him.

**At the edge.** Chochmah, "from nothing": seeing a thing being born from nothing is seeing the edge where things begin.

**Into the day.** Hold a glass of water today and think: "This is being born from nothing, now."

## Chapter 44 (lines 657-674)

**The movement.** The love already in every Jew: God is my life, as the soul is the body's life. And a greater love, like a son who gives himself for his parents.

> מפני שאתה ה׳ ״נפשי״ וחיי האמתים, לכך – ״אויתיך״
> *Because You, God, are "my soul" and my true life, therefore "I long for You".*

**Contemplating it.** Each person loves "according to what he measures in his heart" (line 657). But one love is the same in everyone (line 659). "My soul, I longed for You at night": like one who is weak, or falling asleep, and longs for his life to come back to him, I long for the Infinite, my true life (line 660). Greater still: like a son who works for his father and mother more than for himself, "for we all have one Father" (line 662). Wake it by habit, with mouth and heart together (line 664). If it seems to be only imagination, don't worry: in itself it is true (line 665). And still toil to reach the love that comes from understanding, to make the fire greater (line 673). Bring it home: at night, as you wake.

**From inside.** A homesickness that is also a being at home.

**At the edge.** The love is hidden, above feeling, and calling it out is "very near" (line 664).

**Into the day.** Tonight before sleep, say: "You are my life."

**Ladder.** L01 (Under everything, the spark)

## Chapter 45 (lines 675-678)

**The movement.** A way through mercy: have pity on the spark in you that came so far down. Like Jacob, lift your voice and weep, and draw down mercy.

> לעורר במחשבתו תחלה רחמים רבים לפני ה׳ על ניצוץ אלהות המחיה נפשו
> *To wake in his thought, first, great mercy before God for the spark of Godliness that gives life to his soul.*

**Contemplating it.** The spark came from the Infinite, who fills and surrounds all worlds, down into "the snake's skin" (line 675). Remember your deeds that were not good. "The King is held captive in the tresses": the Presence in exile (line 676). "Jacob kissed Rachel, lifted his voice and wept": mercy lifted up to the Father of mercy, to draw mercy down on all souls (line 677). The kiss is joining your speech to His word, your thought to His thought; the embrace is deeds, above all charity and kindness (line 677). Through this one can reach great love, openly in the heart (line 678). Bring it home: feel sorry, not for yourself, but for what is holy in you that you have dragged along.

**From inside.** Tears that come from tenderness, not despair.

**At the edge.** Mercy from "the source of mercies" (line 677), beyond the mind.

**Into the day.** Give charity today as an embrace.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Chapter 46 (lines 679-692)

**The movement.** The mirror of water: love wakes love. A great King came down Himself to lift us from the dust. Even a stone heart melts.

> אלא הקדוש ברוך הוא בכבודו ובעצמו ירד לשם
> *Rather, the Holy One, blessed be He, in His glory and in Himself, came down there.*

**Contemplating it.** "As water shows face to face, so is the heart of man to man": love shown wakes love (line 680). A great king comes down to a man lying in the dust, lifts him, brings him into the innermost room and embraces him. "Even if his heart is like a stone, it will melt and become water" (line 681). God did all this and more. All worlds are nothing before Him, yet He chose Israel and came down into Egypt Himself (lines 682-683). "Who sanctified us with His commandments", like a betrothal (line 684). "I was a beast with You, and I am always with You": matter does not block the soul's union with Him (line 689). Bring it home: the moment you felt loved by someone far above you. Now let it have no end.

**From inside.** A heart melting, and a wish to give yourself back.

**At the edge.** "Himself", not an angel: the text says He came down "in His glory and in Himself".

**Into the day.** Say one blessing today, "who sanctified us with His commandments", as a betrothal.

## Chapter 47 (lines 693-696)

**The movement.** Every day a going out of Egypt: the soul leaving the body's prison into the unity, above all in the Shema. The Torah is God giving Himself.

> והרי זה כאלו נתן לנו את עצמו כביכול
> *It is as though He gave us Himself, so to speak.*

**Contemplating it.** In every generation and every day, see yourself as leaving Egypt (line 693). Avraham reached it by his deeds, step by step. For us it is an inheritance and a gift (lines 694-695). He clothed His will and wisdom, one with Him, in the Torah (line 695). Nothing blocks the soul's cleaving but the will. The moment you want it and say "the Lord is One", your soul is included in His unity (line 696). Bring it home: this morning's Shema.

**From inside.** Freedom, and receiving a gift.

**At the edge.** "Himself": the Torah as God's giving of Himself.

**Into the day.** Say "the Lord is One" once today as your leaving of Egypt.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 48 (lines 697-709)

**The movement.** It defines the surrounding light: not above in place, but what fills a thing without showing. The earth is full of Him, and He "surrounds" it while He is inside it.

> וכל אור אין סוף ברוך הוא נקרא סובב עליה, אף שהוא בתוכה ממש
> *And all the light of the Infinite, blessed be He, is called surrounding it, though He is truly inside it.*

**Contemplating it.** Without contractions the world could not be limited (line 697). Even the souls in Gan Eden have limits (line 698). The finite next to the infinite has no ratio at all (line 699). "Surrounding" is not about place. It means an influence that is not revealed (line 701). The earth: only mineral and plant life shows in it, but all the Infinite is in it (line 703). A person pictures a thing in his mind; God's knowing surrounds the whole globe, inside and through, and brings it into being (lines 704-706). Bring it home: the floor under you.

**From inside.** Being held from around and from within.

**At the edge.** Line 707: His knowing is His essence, and "a person cannot grasp it fully".

**Into the day.** Put your hand on a stone or on the ground and say: "All of Him is inside this."

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Chapter 49 (lines 710-724)

**The movement.** God set aside His great light out of love for us. Love wakes love, so a person wants to leave everything for Him. The blessings before the Shema build this.

> הניח וסילק לצד אחד, דרך משל, את אורו הגדול הבלתי תכלית
> *He set aside, as it were, His great and endless light.*

**Contemplating it.** Three great contractions made three worlds (lines 710-712). Their purpose is the physical human body, and lifting it up (line 713). As He set aside His light "all for the love of the lowly human" (line 714), so it is fitting for a person to leave everything he has in order to cleave to Him (line 714). The Shema blessings: first the angels, all nullified to His light (line 717); then "with an eternal love You loved us", the light narrowed into "world" for love of His people (line 719). With your mouth, draw the light down into the animal soul (line 723). Bring it home: the things you hold onto hardest.

**From inside.** Generosity. A readiness to give.

**At the edge.** The contraction itself as love (line 714). The mind cannot measure an endless light set aside.

**Into the day.** Say the blessing "Ahavat Olam" slowly this morning.

## Chapter 50 (lines 725-731)

**The movement.** The fiery love, gold beyond silver, the soul burning to leave the wick. Then the return: if your heart runs, come back to the One, because you live by His will, to make Him a home below.

> ואם רץ לבך – שוב לאחד
> *And if your heart runs, return to the One.*

**Contemplating it.** Love from the right side is silver. Love from the fires of Binah is gold (lines 725-726). Contemplating that all is nothing before Him, the soul flares up to leave its wick and wood: thirst, then lovesickness, then the soul's expiring (line 727). The Levites sang with voice and melody, running and returning (line 728). "It is impossible to explain this well in writing" (line 729). When the heart runs, tell it: "against your will you live" in this body, to bring life down and make a dwelling below for His oneness (line 730). Bring it home: a moment in prayer when you wanted to stay up there.

**From inside.** Burning, and then a willing return.

**At the edge.** The soul's expiring (line 727), which the text says cannot be put on paper (line 729).

**Into the day.** After a moving prayer, go and do one concrete task.

## Chapter 51 (lines 732-740)

**The movement.** Why the Presence "rests" in one place when He fills all places: like the soul in the brain. His essence is hidden equally everywhere; the difference is only how much shows.

> כמו שמצוי שם – כך נמצא בתחתונים ממש
> *As He is found there, so He is truly found in the lower realms.*

**Contemplating it.** The soul fills the whole body but lives chiefly in the brain (line 733). The soul itself is not in places at all (line 734). The Infinite fills all worlds (line 736). His essence is the same above and below, hidden in both, "hidden of all hidden" (line 737). The upper worlds receive a little more openly (line 738). This world "seems dead", but inside it is light bringing it from nothing always (line 740). Bring it home: one thing on your desk that "seems dead".

**From inside.** Seeing the ordinary as not dead.

**At the edge.** "Hidden of all hidden" (line 737): the essence is as hidden above as here.

**Into the day.** Touch one "dead" object today and say: "Found here as there."

## Chapter 52 (lines 741-756)

**The movement.** The Presence comes down through the Holy of Holies of every world into the Talmud and the Mishnah. The Torah is the garment that lets us stand near it.

**Contemplating it.** The Presence (Shechinah) is the first revealing of the Infinite, "the mother of the children" (lines 743-744). The worlds could not bear it without a garment (line 745). The garment is His will and wisdom, clothed in the Torah (line 746). The Talmud comes from Beriah, the Mishnah from Yetzirah (lines 748-750). Permitting and forbidding both come from His attributes (line 751). Bring it home: the page of Mishnah is a veil that lets you stand close.

**From inside.** Reverence for the plain text.

**At the edge.** Little here. The chapter is mostly map.

**Into the day.** Learn one Mishnah today.

**The movement.** Part One ends on the candle. Since the Temple, only the four cubits of halacha. The light rests on the soul as the wick burns and turns into fire.

The light above the head needs oil, and the oil is the mitzvot (line 762). The wick is the animal soul. In the righteous it burns and becomes light; in the beinoni at least its garments of thought, speech and deed do (line 762). "For the Lord your God is a consuming fire" (line 763). **At the edge.** "A consuming fire" (line 763): what is burned is not lost but turned to light.

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations

1. **Chapter 33, line 477:** אשר אפילו בארץ מתחת – אין עוד מלבדו, וזו היא דירתו בתחתונים. *The book's own one sentence: He alone, even here, and this is His home.* Picture this very room as the place He fills alone, as before creation, and let the joy come as an heir's joy.

2. **Chapter 20, line 301:** משום דכולא קמיה כלא חשיב, וכאין ואפס ממש. *Before Him all counts as nothing, like one spoken word next to the whole soul.* Say one word aloud and hold how small it is beside you; then turn the picture toward Him.

3. **Chapter 2, line 86:** ונפש השנית בישראל, היא חלק אלוה ממעל ממש. *Under everything, a part of God above.* The floor under your worst opinion of yourself; the door to the whole ladder.

4. **Chapter 29, line 421:** הממלא כל עלמין, היה הוה ויהיה בשוה, גם במקום זה שאני עליו. *He fills "this very place where I stand".* Seeing the unity "by sight, not only by hearing"; for a steady day, not a crushing one.

5. **Chapter 46, line 683:** אלא הקדוש ברוך הוא בכבודו ובעצמו ירד לשם. *The King came down Himself.* Love shown wakes love, like a face in water; even a heart of stone melts.

6. **Chapter 19, line 286:** כאור הנר שמתנענע תמיד למעלה בטבעו. *The candle flame flickers upward by nature.* The soul's homesickness, above reason, to be included in its source.

7. **Chapter 31, line 455:** אבל מכל מקום, יש בקרבי ״חלק ה׳״ ממש, שישנו אפילו בקל שבקלים. *Within me is truly a part of God, in exile.* The turn from honest sorrow to real joy: descent for the sake of ascent.

8. **Chapter 22, line 323:** כי עיקר ושרש עבודה זרה, הוא מה שנחשב לדבר בפני עצמו. *The root of idolatry is being a thing in itself.* Watch where you need the credit; that is where the self stands apart.

9. **Chapter 41, line 621:** לכן, מעתה אני מוסרה ומחזירה לך לייחדה באחדותך. *From now I hand it back to You.* The morning's first words as a handing over of the soul, before any act.

10. **Chapter 53, line 762:** כמו שבנר הגשמי, האור מאיר על ידי כליון ושריפת הפתילה הנהפכת לאש. *The wick burns and turns to fire.* Your ordinary nature is the fuel of the light, not its enemy.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

*Compared with the Chabad method as mapped in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`, and with the other books in this index. The differences are kept, not smoothed.*

**A floor, not only a ceiling.** The Chabad method in the hisbonenus ontology moves from contemplation to deepening to arousal to drawing down, and marks "grasp without heart" as a state to be worked through. The Tanya's beinoni is allowed to stay there. If the heart does not burn, the mind's understanding still sends him to act, and "God joins a good thought to the deed" (ch. 16, line 258-259). Natural, inherited love is a real intention, like the soul of an animal next to the soul of a speaker (line 560). Kuntres HaHitpa'alut suspects any arousal the self can feel; the Tanya says of the inherited love: if it seems to you like imagination, "don't worry" (line 665), and even contemplation "only in imaginings" is still your part (line 238). Both are kept. They are not the same instruction.

**Lowering first, at set times, then joy.** The ontology treats sadness as an obstacle. The Tanya agrees about sadness ("a heart gone dull as stone, with no life in it", line 452), but it prescribes the opposite medicine too: set times to be broken-hearted, to reckon one's days, even to speak sharply to the animal soul (ch. 29, line 421), "for an hour or two" (line 447), always followed by the joy of chapter 31. The Piaseczner starts from feeling and trust; the Tanya starts this particular road with a deliberate lowering. The index keeps the guard of the manual: not on a low day.

**Stray thoughts: don't answer, don't lift.** The Tanya says to act as though you did not hear them (line 401), and forbids the beginner the Baal Shem Tov's practice of lifting a stray thought to its root: "those things were said only of the righteous" (line 395). The Piaseczner watches thoughts calmly at the door of a sitting. The ontology marks this as disputed. It stays disputed.

**Through the self, not through the cosmos.** The Gate of Unity (Mitteler Rebbe) and Shaar HaYichud walk the unity down through the worlds, detail by detail. Likkutei Amarim walks it through the person: the two souls, the heart's chambers, the garments of thought, speech and deed. The cosmic teaching enters only as the content to be held (ch. 20-21, 33, 48), and always returns to what the person does next. This matches the manual's door, "people come looking for themselves".

**Torah above prayer.** Kuntres HaTefillah (the Rashab) makes all study a preparation for the main thing, contemplation in prayer. The Tanya ranks the union of Torah study "above all the mitzvot, even above prayer" (line 335), because in study His will is revealed in the mind with no hiding (ch. 5, 23). Prayer is where the love is woken (ch. 12-13, 49); study is where the union is greatest. The two books weigh the same parts differently, and the difference is kept.

**How long, and how often.** The Tanya asks for "a great hour every day" (line 639) and then a light reminder at every moment, and it lets each soul's time be different (line 631). Kuntres HaTefillah's contemplation is long and detailed within prayer. The Tanya's is shorter, repeated, and bound to each act (before tefillin, before learning, every hour, lines 601, 623).

**Picturing an idea, not a scene.** The Tanya does ask a person to picture ("picture in his mind the true unity", line 472; "picturing this love in the thought of his mind", line 668). But what is pictured is a truth, such as He alone in this place. The Piaseczner pictures scenes as if before one's eyes and uses melody and the body. Song appears in the Tanya only once, as the Levites' fiery love (line 728).

**It cannot be written.** At its most fiery point the Tanya stops: "it is impossible to explain this well in writing" (line 729); each one finds "according to his measure". The ontology builds a map of states; the Tanya describes a few and leaves the rest to each heart.
