**What it is.** Maamarim the Alter Rebbe gave on the weekly portions from Beshalach to Vayakhel, plus Purim maamarim on Megillat Esther and later additions. Each one opens on a verse or a teaching of the Sages, builds a Kabbalistic structure (the Name Havaya, Chochmah and Binah, sparks, kelipot), and then usually turns in a paragraph or two to avodah: prayer, Shema, bitterness and joy, humility, and love of G-d. The Esther maamarim read the Megillah as the inner story of the hidden love in every Jew and how it comes to light through weeping, prayer and mesirus nefesh while G-d's face is hidden.

**How much of it is medicine.** some - most lines are cosmology and Kabbalah, but many maamarim end with short, sharp avodah passages on dead prayer, fleeting inspiration, bitterness and joy, ego, and hidden love.

**Its model of the person.** A Divine soul (a spark) is clothed in the animal soul and body, which confine it (Mitzrayim, a narrow place). Mind can rule the heart. Understanding (Binah, hearing) is different from daas: daas binds the idea into the heart until it is felt as if seen. Love and fear are grown from above in a person who makes himself an empty vessel. Ego (yeshus, gasus) is the wall between a person and G-d. Below daas and above it sit a hidden love and the capacity for mesirus nefesh. Change goes from itkafya (forcing) to is'hapcha (transforming).

**Its philosophy.** Only G-d truly exists (kulei kamei k'lo chashiv). Worlds and self only seem separate because He hides Himself. Light that comes out of darkness is greater than light that never went down: the descent of the soul, exile and falling exist so that the one who returns can reach higher than the tzaddik did, and hiddenness (Esther) draws out a devotion that revelation cannot.

**What it adds.** Esther as a psychology of hiddenness. The love is hidden, not absent, 'not as people think'. The broken, mindless cry from distress is 'above daas'. Arrogant thoughts come from outside ('Haman') and are wept over as garments on the spark. Ego is a protective husk a beginner needs. Two diagnostics stand out: the barren soul that grasps at making its own feelings, cured by becoming an empty vessel, and the gap between knowing G-d ('hearing') and feeling Him ('seeing', daas).

## I understand it all in my head, but it doesn't touch me, and the moment I stop thinking I'm back to my cravings

*Its own term:* בחי' שמיעה / זרע בהמה (בלא דעת)

He has studied a great deal and believes firmly. But none of it is felt. It stays as thought, and as soon as his attention slips, desire takes over again.

**What is really going on.** The knowledge stays in Binah, which is 'hearing', and has not been bound into the heart by daas so that it becomes 'seeing'. Understanding without daas is like imagination, like a dream.

**Program: Contemplation deepened into daas, as if seeing.** (1) Contemplate G-d's unity and life-giving presence deeply, as far as your mind can carry it. (2) Do not stop at understanding: bind it until the heart's will, fervor and attachment are in it, with all your senses. (3) Aim for 'as if seeing', the way a wise man sees what is coming and reacts at once. (4) Receive daas from Moshe, the faithful shepherd, and by extension the leader who gives daas to souls that only 'hear'. (5) Use it to bend the heart to love and stand against every obstacle. *When:* In prayer; the depth is set by 'as far as your soul can bear'. *For:* The learned believer whose knowledge stays abstract, one of the 'hearing' souls, which the text says are nearly all souls of our generations. *Not for:* Not a demand for visions; the text says one cannot see G-d with physical eyes, and 'as if seeing' means an inward sense. `TO2·Q14` · `TO2·Q15` · `TO2·Q16` · `TO2·Q29`

## I try to make myself love G-d and nothing comes, or it comes and quickly dies

His avodah is barren. It gives birth to no real love or fear, or it gives birth to feelings that don't last. His mind feels closed.

**What is really going on.** He is grasping at love and fear as things he makes himself. He also takes up space: 'this is what I want, this is good for me, this I can't do.' His heart is tied even to permitted things, so he is not a clean, empty vessel.

**Program: Making the empty vessel so love can grow.** (1) Know that all love and fear are a gift grown from above, not produced by one's own power. (2) Stop grasping at love and fear as one's own achievements; that yields passing imaginings. (3) Notice 'I want, it's good for me, I can't': this is taking up space, and it closes the mind. (4) Humble yourself: crush and bow until you occupy no space, like a vessel hammered hollow. (5) Rinse the vessel: do not bind the heart wholeheartedly even to permitted worldly things. (6) Then pray like Hannah: weep to arouse great mercy on the Divine spark in your soul so it does not sink into this world. (7) Keep avodah fixed and settled, not on and off, with a daily reckoning of thoughts, words and deeds. *When:* Daily and steady, with a reckoning at the end of each day; weeping is part of prayer. *For:* One whose effort to feel love and fear stays barren or short-lived; one tied to his own preferences. *Not for:* The text notes that someone naturally crushed (poor, without the face to raise his head) needs rinsing, not more crushing. `TO2·Q07` · `TO2·Q08` · `TO2·Q09` · `TO2·Q10` · `TO2·Q11` · `TO2·Q12` · `TO2·Q13`

**Reframe.** From *"I have to make myself love G-d, and I'm failing."* to **Love and fear are a gift grown from above, like a seed in the earth. Your work is to plow and to become an empty vessel, not to manufacture feeling.** Grasping at emotions as self-made produces only passing imaginings. Emptying the self makes room for what is given, so the pressure to perform is replaced by making room. `TO2·Q07` · `TO2·Q08` · `TO2·Q10`

## I'm heavy and sad, or I want joy right now without doing the work

*Its own term:* עצבות ומרה שחורה / שמחה של הוללות

Either his life-force feels shrunk and closed in, or he chases a loud, free joy that falls apart at the first test.

**What is really going on.** Sadness is the life-force contracting. Joy that comes at once and not through service is levity: it hides G-d and falls at the first test. True joy comes after bitterness and after taking on the yoke.

**Program: Bitterness at a set time, then Torah in joy (the desert road).** (1) Do not jump straight to joy; joy that skips service falls at the first test. (2) Set a separate time (midnight, Tikkun Chatzos) to arouse bitterness over the Divine spark in exile and over one's own distance. (3) Keep a reckoning of thought, speech and action that were not for G-d, even for sins already repented. (4) Let the coarse spirit within break, like a seed rotting before it sprouts. (5) Then take on the yoke of Heaven through Torah and mitzvos, which lifts the yoke of the animal soul. (6) Afterwards learn Torah in joy: light that comes out of darkness. *When:* At a fixed separate time, midnight. The early chassidim needed only an hour of contemplation before prayer; others need a set time of its own. Learning in joy follows. *For:* Someone with shallow or loud joy, or who wants joy without work; someone settled enough to hold bitterness and then turn to joy. *Not for:* The text never sends a person to sadness. Anyone depressed or scrupulous should not hold constant bitterness without the joy the text makes its goal. `TO2·Q02` · `TO2·Q03` · `TO2·Q04` · `TO2·Q05`

**Reframe.** From *"Joy is either my mood or something I should grab right now."* to **Sadness is the life-force contracting and joy is its opening out. True joy comes through service and passes through bitterness; instant joy is fragile and falls at the first test.** Joy becomes the fruit of a sequence and not a mood, so the person has a road to it and reason not to trust a quick high. `TO2·Q01` · `TO2·Q04` · `TO2·Q05`

**Reframe.** From *"A broken heart means I must break myself."* to **What breaks is the heart of stone: the coarse spirit of the other side within, and the wall of gasus between him and his Father. The seed rots so it can sprout into joy.** The target is the ego-wall, not the person. The breaking is understood as making an opening, not destruction, and it ends in added light and joy. `TO2·Q03` · `TO2·Q18`

## I feel far away, as if there's no love for G-d in me at all

He sees no warmth or feeling for G-d in himself and concludes he has none.

**What is really going on.** The love is hidden, not absent. It is the inner point of the heart and every Jew has it. Worldly pursuits done with his whole heart are 'thorns' that cover it.

**Program: Esther's way: drawing the hidden love out of hiding.** (1) Know the love is certainly there, hidden in the inner point of your heart. (2) Name the thorns that hide it: worldly matters done with your whole heart and soul. (3) Treat arrogant, 'Haman' thoughts as a garment on the spark, not as your self. (4) Weep and plead to arouse great mercy on the Divine spark clothed in those coarse garments. (5) When your mind is gone, cry out from the distress itself, and hold to faith as if your eyes saw. *When:* In prayer; the Megillah's model is weeping before the King. Purim is the time it breaks open. *For:* One who feels no love at all, who is in a 'night', or who is caught in arrogant or defiant thoughts. *Not for:* Not a way to excuse the thoughts; the work still asks him to weep over them and to take on the yoke. `TO2·Q19` · `TO2·Q20` · `TO2·Q21` · `TO2·Q22` · `TO2·Q23` · `TO2·Q24`

**Reframe.** From *"I show no love for G-d, so I don't have any."* to **The love is hidden, not missing: 'not as people think'. Every Jew has it in the inner point of his heart. It is Esther, hidden, and needs to be drawn out.** The problem moves from 'I lack' to 'it is covered'. That turns despair into the task of removing thorns and revealing what is there. `TO2·Q19` · `TO2·Q20` · `TO2·Q21`

## Who am I to serve G-d? What is my service worth?

He feels too small to begin at all. His heart doesn't dare come near.

**What is really going on.** Without some raising of the heart a person cannot approach avodah. For a beginner, a sense of self as 'someone who serves' is the necessary husk that protects the seed.

**Reframe.** From *"Who am I to serve G-d? Thinking I matter is arrogance."* to **Some raising of the heart is needed to approach at all. For a beginner, a sense of self as a servant is the husk that protects the growing wheat, and it falls away once the fruit is grown.** The work gives ego a limited, protective role. That frees a person to begin without waiting to be selfless and without hating the self he has now. `TO2·Q26` · `TO2·Q27`

## My heart still wants other things; I'm only forcing myself

Contemplation lets him bend his heart toward G-d, but the pull of the world isn't gone. It's only asleep, and he feels like a fraud.

**What is really going on.** He is at the stage of itkafya (forcing), not is'hapcha (transforming). The work treats this as a real level and the path to the next one.

**Program: Itkafya: mind ruling the heart through contemplation.** (1) Contemplate the One G-d as your soul can bear. (2) Through it, force and bend your heart toward love of G-d. (3) Stand against whatever holds you back by breaking and subduing desires, the mind ruling the heart. (4) Know this stage already counts as 'only one heart'; from it you rise to is'hapcha. *When:* In daily prayer, especially Shema. *For:* The beinoni whose heart still has other desires. *Not for:* Not a warrant for crushing ordinary feelings or self-punishment. `TO2·Q28` · `TO2·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"I only force myself; my heart still wants other things, so I'm a fraud."* to **Even beinonim, who only force the heart (itkafya), have 'only one heart' in G-d's eyes. Forcing reveals the One G-d in both inclinations and leads to is'hapcha.** The inner split is recognized as a level with its own worth, so shame at being mixed gives way to steady work. `TO2·Q28`

## Strange thoughts keep falling into my prayer; I'm nothing like the angels

Foreign thoughts come up even while he prays, and he feels hopelessly below the angels.

**What is really going on.** Everyone clothed in a body has foreign thoughts, each by his own measure.

**Reframe.** From *"Strange thoughts in prayer prove I'm hopelessly low."* to **No one in a body is without foreign thoughts, the great by their measure and the small by theirs. Yet the Jew below is greater than the angels.** The thoughts are shown to be universal, which removes their power as proof of personal failure. `TO2·Q30`

## My business and livelihood swallow my heart

Commerce takes all his passion and his heart is bound up in it.

**What is really going on.** Work done with desire binds the heart to worldly things. It should be done coolly, as if compelled, to sustain himself and his household.

**Program: Business done coolly.** (1) Engage in commerce coolly, as if compelled, not with real longing. (2) Do it to sustain yourself and your household so you can serve G-d; make Torah fixed and work incidental. (3) Then, in prayer with mesirus nefesh and in Torah, lift what the work produced into holiness. *When:* Throughout the working day, with prayer and Torah around it. *For:* One whose livelihood takes his heart. *Not for:* Not advice to neglect work or providing for one's family. `TO2·Q32`

## Cautions

- Constant bitterness over sins already repented (Q05), applied to someone depressed or scrupulous, can deepen despair. The text calls sadness a contraction of life and makes joy the goal. - 'Crush yourself hollow, take up no space' can harm someone with low self-worth or an abuse history. The text itself says the naturally crushed need rinsing, not crushing, and that some raising of the heart is needed. - Midnight vigils (Tikkun Chatzos) mean lost sleep; not for someone unwell, sleep-deprived, or in a mood disorder. - Blaming intrusive thoughts on 'Haman' or kelipah can relieve shame, but it must not replace clinical care for OCD or intrusive-thought disorders. - 'Breaking desires' by the mind ruling the heart describes contemplative redirection, not suppressing grief, trauma or emotion. - Mesirus nefesh and 'casting life aside' are devotional terms; never let them be heard as permission for self-harm. - Purim's 'until he cannot tell' is not for anyone with an alcohol problem. - The hidden love is said of 'every soul of Israel'; applying it to all people is the guide's extension, not the text's claim.
