*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A systematic manual of avodah by the Alter Rebbe's student. The introduction says that the verse "know today and bring it back to your heart" contains all of service in three parts: knowing (mind), the heart (love, fear, the heart's will) and deed (Torah and mitzvot). None of them works without the others. The author writes because people each seize one part: some take knowledge and reject feeling, some take feeling and reject knowledge, some take deed alone. Five gates follow: the unity of ZuN and the person, the unity of souls, prayer (two loves), Torah and mitzvot, and teshuvah. Its polemical center is a defense of felt emotion in prayer.

**How much of it is medicine.** substantial: the Introduction and large parts of the Prayer, Torah and Teshuvah gates speak directly to the inner life of the worshipper, though much of Gates 1-2 and long stretches of 3-5 are Kabbalistic cosmology (ZuN, partzufim, Avos, soul-roots) with no therapy in them.

**Its model of the person.** The person has three faculties: mind (chochmah, binah, daas), heart (middot), which is the seat of life, and deed. The animal soul lives in its middot: it feels itself as a separate yesh and loves what is separate. The divine soul is essentially chochmah and bittul, one power rooted in God, but it is clothed in the animal soul. Change comes when contemplation gives birth to felt love and fear. That feeling, sorted by daas, transforms the animal traits rather than only subduing them. Above intellect sits the point of the heart (will), which leaves a trace in memory. Change is graded, lifelong, and "each by his own measure."

**Its philosophy.** Only God truly exists. The worlds, the body and the animal soul are His power, and they appear as separate yesh only "from our side," through concealment. God intended to reveal His unity precisely through the yesh and through physical deed. So the felt self is not something to flee but material to transform. Even a person's partial, imagined (dimyon) grasp is true, because it is His power descending into each one's measure.

**What it adds.** It is Chabad's most sustained defense of felt emotion (hitpaalut) in service against those who suppress feeling as ego. It holds that mixed, imagined devotion is still "whole" and true from its root, drawing on the Alter Rebbe's "yonati tamati." It pairs humility from outside with strength from inside. It gives a graded ladder against temptation, carries prayer through the day by memory, and offers a return in the very moment of craving by crying to what is above will. Its image of the traits melting like wax so they can be reshaped is its own.

## I understand it all in my head, but my heart feels nothing

*Its own term:* השכלה והבנה בלי התפעלות הלב

He studies and contemplates and can explain the ideas, but no love or awe stirs. He counts the understanding itself as service.

**What is really going on.** Understanding that does not give birth to love and fear is not binah at all, only 'mere thought.' The animal soul's traits live in feeling, so only felt bittul reaches and refines them.

**Program: Contemplation to feeling, then refining the 'I'.** (1) In Shema and prayer, contemplate His unity: how the worlds come from Him and are nullified to Him (ahavat olam). (2) Aim the contemplation at moving the heart to love and awe; understanding that stays in the head does not count. (3) Let daas show the distance and lack, so the heart breaks toward bittul of the yesh. (4) Once moved, do the real 'war': remove the sense of self from the love, and do not take the arousal for your own pleasure. (5) If the lower contemplation does not move you, turn to the higher one: there is nothing besides Him (ahavah rabbah). (6) Accept partial victory, each by his measure. Even imagined bittul is true from the root. (7) Carry the arousal into Torah and mitzvot in deed. *When:* Daily Shema and prayer. The 'war' lasts all one's days; its intensity varies with the hour and the soul-root. *For:* Anyone whose service has split into head-only, habit-only, or a fear of feeling. *Not for:* Someone depressed, for whom the 'lack and distance' step could deepen despair. Not a warrant to chase emotional highs. `SA·Q01` · `SA·Q02` · `SA·Q03` · `SA·Q06` · `SA·Q09` · `SA·Q11` · `SA·Q15` · `SA·Q16` · `SA·Q18`

## After a good prayer I feel I've achieved something

*Its own term:* גבה לבו / מחשיב עצמו לאיזה מהות

He is moved, then proud of being moved, and counts himself a real servant of God.

**What is really going on.** Counting oneself as a separate entity is the opposite of unity. Revelation rests only on one who is nullified, so the pride cuts off the very closeness he felt.

**Program: Lowly from outside, strong from inside.** (1) During service, do not imagine you have attained something or are 'someone.' (2) Know that from the outside view your body, soul and the worlds are no entity at all ('what are we, what is our life'). (3) Then turn to the inside view: 'fortunate are we, how good is our portion'; the portion above daas truly cleaves. (4) Lift your heart in God's ways from that inner side. Humility must not collapse into falling. *When:* Before prayer (the liturgy's 'mah anu... ashreinu'), during prayer, and kept in memory all day. *For:* One inflated by spiritual experience, or one deflated by the teaching on nullity. *Not for:* Do not give only the first half ('you are nothing') to someone with low self-worth. `SA·Q22` · `SA·Q23` · `SA·Q24`

**Reframe.** From *"I've attained something; I'm a real servant of God."* to **From yourself you are no entity. Closeness comes to the one who is nullified, not to the one who holds on.** Counting oneself as a separate entity is the opposite of the One, so pride separates. Bittul is what the revelation rests on. `SA·Q24` · `SA·Q16`

## Once prayer ends, the day swallows me and I forget

During business the awareness from the morning is gone.

**What is really going on.** The mind leaves after prayer. What remains is a trace, available only if the bittul came from the point of the heart.

**Program: Carrying prayer through the day by memory.** (1) In prayer, accept the yoke and the bittul from the point of the heart, not only from contemplation. (2) During work, when the mind is elsewhere, rouse the memory of that bittul; memory is above intellect. (3) Let the memory move you to the mitzvah or to bittul in deed. *When:* All day, in brief moments of recall after morning prayer. *For:* Working people whose awareness fades after prayer. *Not for:* Not a demand for constant detailed intention in every word (the text itself rejects that). `SA·Q14`

## A tempting thought keeps coming at me

An urge or thought of sin arises and he wrestles with it.

**What is really going on.** Arguing binds him to it. It should stay a 'passing encounter' and never become a joining. The urge comes mainly from coarseness of spirit.

**Program: The graded ladder against a tempting thought.** (1) Keep it a passing 'encounter': the thought rises and you turn from it without joining it. (2) Do not argue with the urge; rouse the good against it and push it away. (3) If it does not leave, engage in Torah study. (4) If still not, contemplate His unity (Shema) until love for God is aroused, which turns the trait itself. (5) If your mind is too coarse to go deep, recall the day of death. *When:* Immediately, at the moment the urge arises. *For:* Someone facing recurring temptations of desire. *Not for:* Obsessive intrusive thoughts (OCD), where combat and vigilance can feed the loop. The anxious or grieving should not recall death. `SA·Q25` · `SA·Q26` · `SA·Q27`

## I'm in its grip right now and can't even want to stop

In the moment of craving his very will is captured; resolutions don't reach.

**What is really going on.** His will, the point of the heart, is itself in the prison, and a prisoner cannot free himself. Help must come from above will.

**Program: Return in the moment of the surge.** (1) Know that your own will is captive right now and resolve alone won't free you. (2) Cry out to His essence, above wisdom and will, for great mercy. (3) Understand that everything is His essence, so how can you be far from it. (4) From that, uproot the will from the evil: true regret and a resolve not to want it, though it still rages. (5) Contemplate His unity until the traits melt like wax near fire. (6) Then reshape the melted desire into greater longing for God. *When:* In the moment of the evil's strength; the text also ties it to the order of daily prayer. *For:* One caught in active compulsion or craving. *Not for:* Not a substitute for real-world help in addiction or danger. `SA·Q30` · `SA·Q31` · `SA·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"I can't free myself; even my will is captive."* to **Don't rely on your will. Cry to what is above will. All is His essence, so how could you be far from it?** A prisoner cannot free himself. Mercy from the essence, above wisdom and will, frees the will, and then contemplation melts the traits like wax so they can be reshaped. `SA·Q30` · `SA·Q31` · `SA·Q32`

## I sinned, and I'll have to suffer a long time before I'm clean

He feels he must first pay by fasting or pain before he can come back.

**What is really going on.** He confuses the cleansing with the return. The return is immediate through regret and leaving the sin.

**Program: Teshuvah: regret, leaving, then cleansing.** (1) Lower teshuvah: regret and a broken heart with bitterness, and leaving the sin; this takes you out at once. (2) Cleansing (fasting and the like) comes after, like wiping mud after climbing out of a pit; it does not hold up the return. (3) Higher teshuvah: after the brokenness, return the soul to its source with self-sacrifice above reason, contemplating the root of all. *When:* When one resolves to return; the broken-heart stage precedes the higher return. *For:* One carrying guilt who thinks punishment must come before return. *Not for:* Depressed or suicidal people: the text's 'despising his life' in bitterness must not be applied; nor fasting for those with eating disorders. `SA·Q13` · `SA·Q28` · `SA·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"I must suffer and pay before I can come back."* to **You are back the moment you truly regret and leave the sin. Cleansing is only wiping off the mud after you've climbed out.** Regret and leaving the sin remove the concealment at once and rouse His truth, so the person is already out of the other side's domain. `SA·Q28` · `SA·Q29`

## My sadness, fear and pleasures pull me away from God

Daily feelings seem to be the enemy of devotion.

**What is really going on.** He looks only at the outside. Every feeling has an inner side that is God's power.

**Program: Looking at the inside of every feeling.** (1) In eating and pleasure, look past the outside to the inner taste, and delight in God. (2) In joy, rejoice in God. (3) In sadness or outward fear, turn to awe of God by seeing that all is Him alone, or turn the sadness to joy. (4) Aim anger and hatred at the evil itself. (5) Do this through a general awareness of unity, not special intention for every word. *When:* Throughout the day, in ordinary activity. *For:* Anyone who experiences daily emotions as rivals to devotion. *Not for:* Not a way to bypass grief or clinical depression; turning sadness into 'broken heart and lowliness' is not treatment for depression. `SA·Q20` · `SA·Q21`

**Reframe.** From *"My feelings are the enemy of my devotion."* to **Every feeling has an inside that is His power. The same energy can turn toward Him.** Looking at the inner side turns delight, joy, fear, sadness and anger into their holy counterparts instead of fighting them. `SA·Q20` · `SA·Q21`

## My mitzvos without feeling are worthless

He does things out of habit and faith and despairs because he feels nothing.

## Cautions

- Higher teshuvah asks for 'despising one's life' in bitterness (Q13). Never apply it to anyone depressed, self-harming or suicidal. - 'Recall the day of death' is the text's last resort. It is not for anxious, grieving or depressed people. - 'You are no entity at all' can feed worthlessness. Always pair it with the text's inward 'how good is our portion.' - Cleansing through fasting or mortification is secondary even in the text. Never prescribe it, especially where there is an eating disorder. - Turning sadness into 'broken heart and lowliness' is not treatment for depression. Prefer the text's own option of turning sadness to joy. - 'Rouse the good, don't argue, push it away' suits temptation. For OCD intrusive thoughts, combat and vigilance can worsen the loop. - 'Hate the evil' can slide into self-hatred or hatred of people. Aim it at the pattern, not the person. - The framework assumes a Jewish soul, Shema, prayer and mitzvot. Translate it with care and do not flatten it for others.
