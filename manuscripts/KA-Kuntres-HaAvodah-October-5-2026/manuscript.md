*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** A short manual (7 chapters, c.1909-10) for young men beginning the "service of the heart", prayer with contemplation. It argues that the aim of prayer's labor is love of G-d, which both binds the soul to G-d and refines the animal soul. Fear (acceptance of the yoke) is required at all times as its foundation. Most of its practical force goes to diagnosing false service: an excitement that leaves no mark, general contemplation that breeds bitterness, going cold at the start, reaching above one's level. It prescribes slow, detailed contemplation aimed at one's own particular unrefined trait. The pages come with the Previous Rebbe's preface and two letters.

**How much of it is medicine.** substantial: chapters 2, 6 and 7 are a sharp practical psychology of self-deception, heaviness, coldness and unchanged character in inner work. Chapters 1 and 3-5 and the appended letter are mostly a typology of loves and fears built on Kabbalah.

**Its model of the person.** Two souls. The G-dly soul is like a polished surface that takes in light and shines, but crookedness and habit can coat it over. The animal soul holds natural traits that strengthen with habit. Thoughts rise from the heart's desires into the mind. Delight is hidden at the soul's core and is stirred mainly through sight. Real change comes from detailed contemplation that grips the mind, then the natural intellect, and turns into love. That love weakens or burns up the animal soul's ego, working on the one unrefined trait each soul came to fix. Excitement that stays surrounding (makif) and never goes inward changes nothing.

**Its philosophy.** Every soul came down into a body to refine its animal soul and its share of the world. So each is given the power to do this, and no one is truly unfit for the work. G-d's life-force fills every creature, and grasping this in detail can make a person truly close to G-d. Truth comes only in stages: whatever is true at one's own level lasts, and whatever reaches past it is not true.

**What it adds.** It is a diagnostic manual of false inner work more than a system: imagined excitement that leaves no mark, general contemplation that turns into sadness posing as bitterness, going cold at the start and taking it as unfitness, and reaching above one's level. Its test is behavioral: did your particular trait change after prayer? It pairs this with an unusually humane rule of graded, truthful self-demand, and insists that no soul is unfit.

## I don't do anything wrong, but I can't stop looking at what pulls me

*Its own term:* המשיכה אל ההבטה וההסתכלות

Outwardly clean and even devout, but the eyes keep drifting. The looking seems casual and cool, with no felt reaction at the time.

**What is really going on.** His hidden core delight is sunk in and tied to something bad, even while his revealed powers do good. The coolness only shows how absorbed he is. The glance leaves a deep impression and in time brings the bad into the open.

**Program: Guarding the senses.** (1) Close the eyes from looking at what is bad; do not let sight open onto whatever is in front of you. (2) Shut the ears to mockery, frivolity and idle talk. (3) Hold back speech: no forbidden, mocking or idle talk, and do not open your mouth wide about anything. (4) If it is hard, overpower yourself firmly, knowing it concerns your own soul, and ignore what people say even if it shows. (5) Bear the heaviness of the first stage; over time it becomes habit, and you find rest from confusion and can do your inner work. *When:* Constant, all day. The difficulty belongs only to the beginning, until it becomes habit. *For:* Anyone. The text calls it an obligation on everyone and the precondition for any inner work to count. *Not for:* Not as an anxious self-policing regime for scrupulous or obsessive people (see cautions). `KA·Q01` · `KA·Q02` · `KA·Q03`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm not doing anything wrong; a glance means nothing to me."* to **The pull shows where your hidden delight is attached, and the glance leaves a deep mark even if you feel nothing.** Delight is the source-point of the soul and is stirred through sight. At the source-point there is no revealed excitement, so the coolness hides it; left alone it spreads into the open. `KA·Q02`

## I feel free to watch, say and do whatever I like; nothing holds me back

The senses stand open. He says whatever comes to mind and looks at and listens to everything, like a man alone at home who sprawls out and does not watch himself.

**What is really going on.** He has thrown off the yoke of Heaven. He feels no Master over him, not even a hidden feeling of one, so nothing restrains the senses that lead to desire.

## Unwanted thoughts keep flooding my mind

*Its own term:* מחשבות זרות והרהורים רעים

The thoughts vanish while the inner work is going well and come back when it slackens. At worst they arrive against his will and far beyond measure, until he is disgusted and cannot get rid of them.

**What is really going on.** The thoughts are the heart's unrefined traits spreading up into the mind. The ordinary person's nature is not transformed, so they return. A flood of unwanted thoughts follows from willfully dwelling on them and feeding the senses, which the text traces to lack of fear of G-d.

**Program: Meeting stray thoughts.** (1) Expect them: an ordinary person's nature is not transformed, so they return after prayer wears off. (2) Never entertain a bad thought willingly; the moment you notice it, push it away with both hands and turn your mind from it. (3) Keep away from what feeds them (sights, mocking talk). (4) Refine the traits at their root through inner work in prayer; when the traits are subdued the thoughts stop for long stretches. *When:* In the moment a thought arises, and daily in prayer for the root work. *For:* One who wants to guard his mind and is already doing some inner work. *Not for:* Not a treatment for clinical intrusive thoughts (OCD); suppression can backfire there. `KA·Q04` · `KA·Q05` · `KA·Q06`

## I know life should be about more, but I can't pull away from my habits

He senses that living only for this world, like a horse or mule, is not all a person is. His ingrained nature still will not let him leave his habits. Or he tries G-dly ideas and they fail to take.

**What is really going on.** From birth he gets used to physical things and becomes rooted in them. This crookedness covers the G-dly soul so light cannot enter it. He is not yet a vessel for contemplation.

**Program: Accepting the yoke first: leaving one's habits.** (1) Before trying to contemplate, take on the yoke of Heaven. (2) Leave the matters you are used to by nature and give yourself to G-d, serving as a servant does. (3) Base the leaving on its being against G-d's will, or at least on the hidden feeling of the yoke; it lasts best when it comes from some light felt in the soul. (4) This removes the animal soul's coarseness and makes you fit for contemplation. *When:* First step before all other work, then continual. *For:* One whose G-dly powers are hidden, who cannot yet feel anything in contemplation. *Not for:* The story of the chassid who did nothing his nature wanted is a model of rigor, not a rule for fragile or self-punishing people. `KA·Q08` · `KA·Q09`

## My prayer is dead; thinking feels heavy and I've become like a stone

Contemplation feels like a burden, so he slacks off. Prayer loses all vitality, the heaviness spreads to everything, and he turns to stone and cannot bring himself to do anything.

**What is really going on.** The work is heavy on him: he does not want to take on the yoke of real effort with mind and heart. He also lacks practice, so he grasps only the glimmer of an idea and not the idea itself.

**Program: Entering contemplation when it feels heavy or cold.** (1) Set aside a proper, adequate time for prayer. (2) Lay to heart that your descent into the world is for this work; without it you live like an animal eating and drinking. (3) Enter the contemplation as an act of accepting the yoke, even without feeling. (4) Do not worry when earlier warmth cools: it was imagined, not real heat. (5) Go deep into one detailed G-dly matter, bring it close, and fix your mind on it without minding the time. (6) Work to have your natural intellect understand it too, not only the G-dly soul. (7) Repeat; the idea grows clearer and becomes one with you, and love awakens. *When:* Daily in prayer, unhurried; the text echoes Tanya's 'a long hour'. Gets easier with steady practice. *For:* Those beginning the service of the heart who find it heavy, cold or bitter. *Not for:* Not a push-through for clinical depression or anhedonia; the text addresses reluctance and laziness in the work. `KA·Q10` · `KA·Q11` · `KA·Q12` · `KA·Q14` · `KA·Q16` · `KA·Q17` · `KA·Q18` · `KA·Q19` · `KA·Q30` · `KA·Q32`

## When I try to think deeply I go cold, so I'm not cut out for this

## I get moved, even weep, but afterward I'm exactly the same person

*Its own term:* התפעלות דמיוני / בבחי' מקיף

Fervent in prayer, or sobbing on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, but afterward the same levity, lust, pride or anger comes back. He may even feel more self-satisfied because of how moved he was.

**What is really going on.** The contemplation was general only, or the service was absent all year. The arousal stays surrounding and never reaches the inner powers. Regret and resolve are general and never touch the particular failings, so the unrefined trait stays at full strength.

**Program: Working on your one particular trait.** (1) Know which natural trait is strongest in you: the one you came to refine (lust and levity, or anger and cruelty, or laziness and sadness). (2) Contemplate in detail, with full grasp, until the idea is felt and love awakens. (3) Put all your strength in prayer into that trait. (4) Test the prayer by after-prayer conduct: if the trait is unchanged, the service was only general. *When:* Daily prayer, over time; the trait weakens little by little. *For:* One who is moved in prayer but unchanged in character. *Not for:* Should not become a harsh self-diagnosis of being essentially bad. `KA·Q20` · `KA·Q21` · `KA·Q30`

## My spiritual life is all bitterness and worry; there's no joy even in learning

*Its own term:* מרירות ולב דואג / רוח עצב

## I keep taking on big spiritual resolutions and dropping them; I'm different every day

*Its own term:* הולך לעילא מדרגי' / אין סדר בעבודתו

He demands every lofty stringency of himself and wants the highest levels. He is careful one day and lax the next, fits in with whoever is around under pressure, and cannot hold out when his core traits are tested.

**What is really going on.** Loud outward arousal from a general knowledge of G-dliness exposes a little of the G-dly soul but joins nothing to the animal soul. His wanting is not true, so it stops when the arousal stops. It is easier to want lofty things untruly than to hold a low thing truly.

**Program: Graded self-demand, one true step at a time.** (1) Know your own level and what you should ask of yourself (not the basic commandments, which bind everyone equally). (2) Do not demand everything of yourself. (3) Take on first the one stricter matter that truly matters to you now. (4) Once it is firm and unchanging, go on to the next. (5) Contemplate levels close to you, not lofty ones, unless you are already practiced. *When:* Ongoing; you move up only after the current step holds steady. *For:* Enthusiasts who take on lofty practices and keep dropping them. *Not for:* Not a license for apathy; the text says not caring about anything is also very bad. `KA·Q27` · `KA·Q28` · `KA·Q29` · `KA·Q35`

**Reframe.** From *"I should want everything lofty and be at the highest level."* to **Truth comes only step by step. Wanting what is above your level is not true, and it makes even your own level untrue.** A want born of passing, surrounding arousal stops when the arousal stops. What truly matters to a person at his own level stays firm, and that firmness is what lifts him to the next level. `KA·Q27` · `KA·Q28` · `KA·Q29`

## Cautions

- Pushing thoughts away with both hands and blaming floods of thoughts on willful dwelling can worsen OCD intrusions and add guilt; unwanted thoughts need clinical framing, not moral framing. - Guarding the senses and doing nothing your nature wants (the chassid story) can feed scrupulosity or self-punishment in anxious or perfectionist people. - Accepting the yoke to push through heaviness targets reluctance in the work, not clinical depression; do not tell an anhedonic person that his stone-like state is unwillingness. - The text warns that lingering bitterness is sadness; do not prescribe bitterness or a worried heart to someone depressed. - Fear-laden warnings (all work counts as nothing, falling to the lowest depths) can terrify a fragile reader; soften them or leave them out. - The claim that souls are reincarnated to fix one particular trait should be offered as meaning, not as a verdict that a person is essentially his worst trait. - Detailed contemplation assumes a Chabad teaching of worlds and divine life-force, and long daily prayer; a modern beginner needs simpler, near-at-hand material, as the text itself advises.
