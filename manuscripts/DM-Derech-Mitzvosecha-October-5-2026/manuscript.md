**What it is.** This is the Tzemach Tzedek's book on the inner reasons of the commandments. Each section starts from the halachic definition (Rambam, Chinuch) and asks what the commandment does at its root, mostly in Lurianic terms: sefiros, vessels, drawing down the light of Ein Sof. It ends with a long essay on the root of prayer and Shema, several discourses, and supplements. Most of it is cosmology and Kabbalah. Its therapeutic content comes as the "why" of a few commandments: faith versus knowing, loving God, loving one's fellow, confession and return, and how bitterness and joy work in prayer.

**How much of it is medicine.** some — Most of the text is Kabbalistic cosmology and halachic reasoning. Clear, usable psychology appears in about a dozen passages, mainly on faith and feeling, love, confession, and the two chambers of the heart.

**Its model of the person.** A person has a divine soul and an animal soul, soul levels up to yechida, and three garments: thought, speech and action. Faith is a crown that sits above the head (makif). It is always there, but it does not move the emotions. Only da'as, attention held on an idea and felt, opens the six emotions, and love follows contemplation by itself. Inborn traits stay as they were born; what changes is the will not to follow them. The heart has two chambers, bitterness and joy. A narrow mind fills entirely with one feeling. Traits that are hidden come out when something arouses them. Every soul contains every other soul.

**Its philosophy.** Ein Sof is beyond every quality, and all the worlds are only a drop of His light. The commandments draw that light down into the sefiros, and a sin blemishes the vessels and feeds the kelipos. Every Jewish soul is a limb of one body, the soul of Adam, so no one is truly separate. This world is the one place where good and evil mix, so a person can change his taste from bad to good. The soul comes down into a body so that the concealment can produce a love without limit (descent for the sake of ascent).

**What it adds.** This work reaches the soul through the reason behind each commandment. It draws a sharp line between faith and da'as (the thief praying at the tunnel), which explains why belief leaves feeling unmoved. It says love cannot be commanded or chased; only contemplation can be commanded. Confession works in two ways: regret takes the sin's 'soul' and spoken words take its 'body'. Loving one's fellow is a precondition of whole prayer. The love born of distance is like dammed water breaking through. The bitterness of the beinoni is set apart from the awe of the tzaddik.

## I believe in God, but it doesn't move me. My heart stays cold.

He never doubts God, yet his feelings and conduct go their own way. He concludes that his faith must be fake, or that love is beyond him.

**What is really going on.** Faith is a crown above the mind. It stays intact even in the thief praying at the tunnel, but it never moves the emotions. The emotions move only through da'as and contemplation. The commandment to love is really a commandment to contemplate.

**Program: Contemplation that turns faith into feeling.** (1) Accept that your faith is already whole, by nature. The command is not about believing. (2) Put the effort into the mind: recall and contemplate God's greatness, that He is the root and all is as nothing before Him. (3) Hold the mind on it (da'as). Distraction shuts the emotions, and attention opens them. (4) Let love come by itself afterward. Do not chase the feeling. *When:* In and before prayer. The text calls it effort in the mind, without a set length. *For:* Someone whose belief is intact but whose heart and conduct are unmoved. *Not for:* Someone who will read a lack of feeling as proof of failure. Someone expecting an emotion on command. `DM·Q08` · `DM·Q09` · `DM·Q10` · `DM·Q11` · `DM·Q12` · `DM·Q13`

**Reframe.** From *"If I really believed, I'd feel it. I must be a fraud."* to **Your faith is intact; even the thief at the tunnel prays. Faith just doesn't move feelings. Understanding and attention do.** Faith is a crown above the mind (makif), and da'as is the key to the six emotions. Changing where you put the effort, from forcing belief to contemplating, opens the emotions. `DM·Q09` · `DM·Q12` · `DM·Q13`

**Reframe.** From *"I have to make myself feel love."* to **'You shall love' is future tense: after contemplation, love comes on its own. Chasing it drives it away. ## I can't stand certain people. I carry resentment and feel cut off from others.

*Its own term:* שנאה / לא תשנא את אחיך בלבבך

He hates someone quietly in his heart and treats it as private. He feels the other person has nothing to do with him.

**What is really going on.** Souls are included in one another like limbs. Hating another pushes away the part of him that lives in you, and it leaves you blemished and unable to rise in prayer. The separation belongs only to the body.

**Program: Taking on love of one's fellow before prayer.** (1) Before praying, accept on yourself the commandment 'love your fellow as yourself'. (2) Contemplate that every soul includes all the others, and that the separation is bodily only. (3) See hatred as cutting away a part of yourself that makes you blemished. (4) Then pray, whole and 'healthy'. *When:* Daily, right before prayer. *For:* Someone carrying resentment, or feeling isolated from others. *Not for:* Should not be used to push someone to stay close to a person who harms them. `DM·Q01` · `DM·Q02` · `DM·Q03` · `DM·Q04`

**Reframe.** From *"He's separate from me. What I feel about him is my own business."* to **He is included in you and you in him. Hating him cuts away a piece of you.** Souls are limbs of one body, and separation is only bodily. Seeing the other as part of oneself turns love into wholeness rather than a burden. `DM·Q01` · `DM·Q02` · `DM·Q03`

## I did something wrong, it stays with me, and I can't undo it. Bad thoughts come every day.

He is weighed down by sins and by the daily flood of stray thoughts, and feels the damage is permanent.

**What is really going on.** A sin gives life to a 'kelipah': its soul comes from the desire and its body from the deed or the words, and it stands against the person. Teshuvah is healing. Regret pulls the will out of the sin and so takes its soul, and spoken confession removes its body. In this world a person can still change.

**Program: Teshuvah: uprooting the will and confessing in words.** (1) Leave the wrong from now on. (2) Regret, meaning uproot the will and desire from it. This takes away its 'soul'. (3) Confess out loud. The movement of the lips counts as a deed and removes its 'body'. (4) For daily stray thoughts: say the alphabetical confession (Ashamnu) and uproot the will from them too. (5) Know it works while you are alive in this world. *When:* Whenever needed. The alphabetical confession is daily. *For:* Someone stuck on past wrongs who wants a concrete way back. *Not for:* Scrupulous or OCD-prone people: the idea that every thought creates a kelipah can feed obsession. `DM·Q05` · `DM·Q06` · `DM·Q07` · `DM·Q19` · `DM·Q20`

**Reframe.** From *"What I did can't be undone."* to **Teshuvah is healing. Uprooting the desire removes the sin's life, and in this world you can change your taste from bad to good.** Only in this world do good and evil mix, so the will can still be turned. Regret acts on the source, which is the desire. `DM·Q05` · `DM·Q06` · `DM·Q19` · `DM·Q20`

## My heart is all money and wanting. I still crave, and it feels like it makes me far from God.

His aims are livelihood and leaving something to his children. Desire rises suddenly. He feels the body and the world disqualify him.

**What is really going on.** The in-between person (beinoni) has not come to despise evil, and his traits are as they were born. His task is two chambers of the heart: bitterness over the distance and joy in the commandments. The concealment the body creates is what lets a love without limit break through.

**Program: Filling both chambers of the heart.** (1) Contemplate the soul's descent and how your aims have become livelihood and material things. (2) Let the left chamber hold bitterness over that distance. (3) Let the right chamber hold joy in the commandments as God's will. (4) Let the distance itself drive the love, like water breaking through a dam. (5) When desire rises suddenly, the bitterness is at being aroused, and the will stays set on not following. *When:* In prayer, at 'with all your heart' in Shema. *For:* The beinoni: sincere, but still drawn to worldly wants. *Not for:* Depressed people: never the bitterness alone, without the joy. Not for someone who feels no craving. `DM·Q14` · `DM·Q15` · `DM·Q16` · `DM·Q17` · `DM·Q18` · `DM·Q27` · `DM·Q30` · `DM·Q31`

**Program: Charity to lift the animal soul.** (1) Give to the poor from your own toil. (2) Having mercy on him awakens great mercy on your own soul, lifting it from its lowliness toward love of God. *When:* Ongoing. The text gives no schedule. *For:* Someone whose animal soul's traits feel heavy and unchanging. *Not for:* Not a transaction for forgiveness, and not to be pressed beyond one's means. `DM·Q32`

**Reframe.** From *"My body and worldly wants make me far from God. I'm disqualified."* to **The concealment is exactly what produces the love without limit, like water bursting through a dam.** Love from the soul itself is bounded by what it understands. Love born from distance breaks past the vessel's limit. That is the purpose of the soul's descent. `DM·Q17` · `DM·Q18`

**Reframe.** From *"I still crave. I haven't changed at all."* to **Your traits are as you were born. What counts is that your will refuses to follow, and your bitterness at being aroused shows this isn't what you truly want.** The text separates emotion from will. The beinoni rules his spirit without despising evil, which ends the sense of total failure. `DM·Q16` · `DM·Q30` · `DM·Q31`

## I flare up when things go against me and can't see anything good in the person.

The first impression that 'this is bad for me' takes over his whole mind, and nothing else fits.

**What is really going on.** A small vessel: a narrow mind fills completely with anger and has no empty room left to consider the good side. The kindness is still there underneath. (This comes as a parable for Tohu and Tikkun, not as an anger program.)

**Reframe.** From *"He wronged me, so he's entirely bad."* to **A narrow mind fills with one judgment. A broad mind can see the good in the thing and the merit of the person at the same moment.** Anger takes over because no empty room is left. Widening the mind leaves space for another side, and the anger shrinks. (A parable in the text.) `DM·Q24` · `DM·Q25` · `DM·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm too angry to be kind right now."* to **Compassion is already hidden in you even in anger. Being addressed as merciful draws it out.** Arousal doesn't create a trait; it reveals a hidden one. This is the text's parable for why praise matters. `DM·Q21` · `DM·Q22`

## I feel God's absence and it weighs on me like worry.

Contemplating God's infinite greatness, he feels how little he grasps and falls into worry and bitterness.

**What is really going on.** This worry is not physical sadness. It comes from the absence of revelation. Torah study draws down the hidden light, and the heart rejoices even without seeing it.

**Program: From worry to Torah joy.** (1) In prayer, contemplate the greatness of Ein Sof. Worry over how little you grasp may follow. (2) Then study Torah, which draws the hidden light onto the soul. (3) Rejoice like someone holding a vessel full of coins he cannot see. *When:* Prayer first as the preparation, then study. *For:* Someone whose longing for God has turned into a heavy worry. *Not for:* Not a substitute for care in clinical depression. `DM·Q28` · `DM·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"This heaviness over God's absence means I'm sinking into sadness."* to **This worry is not physical sadness. It is longing for revelation, and Torah gladdens even unseen, like holding a closed vessel of coins.** Naming the worry as spiritual longing, and knowing the light is present though hidden, turns it into joy. `DM·Q28` · `DM·Q29`

## Cautions

- Q27's 'despising his life' and the breaking of the heart must never be applied to someone depressed or suicidal. - The bitterness over distance (G04) is always paired with joy in the right chamber. Never give it alone. - The idea that every stray thought creates a kelipah and none is lost can feed scrupulosity or OCD. - The worry-is-not-sadness reframe (F09) must not be used to dismiss clinical depression as merely spiritual. - The soul-inclusion basis for love is framed about Jewish souls. A universal guide should adapt it openly, not misstate it. - Seeing the merit of one who harms you (Q26) must not keep anyone in an abusive situation. - 'Toil in the mind' can turn into self-blame when the feeling doesn't come. Stress that love comes of itself (Q08).
