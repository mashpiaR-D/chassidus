## 1. The book as one meditative journey

The Gate of Unity walks a person from the edge of their own mind down a long ladder to the ground under their feet, and finds the same One on every rung. It calls itself "the tract on contemplation" (line 3), and it begins not with God but with how to look: a strong, unhurried gaze that stays on one idea until it knows its depth (line 10), like a river with its breadth, length and depth (lines 16-23), with the mind gathered into one point (line 52). It warns that thinking about God "in general" can feel very near and still be far: "from afar God appears to him" (line 124). So the road goes detail by detail, each joined to the one above it (line 139). At the door it gives a test. Real contemplation leaves two opposite marks in equal measure: joy at what you grasp, and bitul, the self going quiet, before what you cannot grasp (line 199). And it asks for honest tears over the distance before the joy (chapter 6).

Then the long walk. Chapters 7-9 lay out the chain from "I am first" to "I am last", and ask the reader to learn it until, "in one moment", looking at a physical thing, he sees the Infinite resting there from the beginning of all (lines 330-332). Chapters 10-17 climb above the chain: God as Yachid, truly alone (line 493); the reason for everything, "He desires kindness" (line 577); the contraction (tzimtzum) that is not a leaving (line 662); the point and the line; the first will that saw everything "in one glance" (line 904). Chapters 18-32 come down through the worlds: creation from nothing found inside your own wanting, opposites that cannot meet except in a compound or above the split, the Essence beyond even pleasure, lights and vessels and the discovery that limit itself is rooted in Him (lines 1601-1602), and the will that reaches from the soul's depth to the breath of the heart "in one moment" (line 1809). Chapters 33-52 keep going down: truth that is the same at the head and the end, speech in which a thing is "already done", letters that are one with the soul and also a garment over it, the scattered letters of a dream, the king who needs a people, the screen between the worlds, the stars, the blade of grass. Chapter 53 gathers it all: the root of the highest world and of ours "are exactly equal" before Him (line 2653), and the whole aim is that everything be included in the simple unity until it is fixed in the soul, down to the last detail of providence (line 2657). Chapter 54 turns the lamp on the reader: evil begins as a good thing that feels itself, even a fire in prayer (line 2703).

So the book goes from "I have an idea of what God is" to "every detail in front of me, and even my own feeling of myself, stands inside one simple will". The contemplation it asks for is almost entirely thinking through: long, exact and patient, and aimed at prayer (the text sets it in the Shema and the Amidah, lines 316-320, 1481, 1657). It explains God by the soul on nearly every page, and says where each picture breaks. It asks for weeping and joy together (chapter 6), for self-giving (mesirut nefesh, giving yourself over) at the Shema (lines 648, 1481), and it ends in watchfulness over the self. It uses no song and no imagined scenes. It is the hardest of the books, it says so (line 205), and it tells beginners to start with the general and go slowly (line 160). Much of its middle is a Kabbalistic map. This reading takes each chapter of the map for the one step it gives the mind, and says plainly where a chapter is mostly mechanism.

## 2. Unit by unit

*Skipped, with nothing to contemplate: The colophon (line 2712, "here ends the Gate of Unity"). The author's apology, blessing and signature (lines 4-7) are folded into the Introduction unit.*

## Introduction (lines 1-7)

**The movement.** It names the aim of the whole book before any teaching: the unity is not only at the top but in every single detail. It sets a long road in front of the reader and tells him the road is meant for him.

> מהות הקונטרס הלז מדבר בענין היחוד אלקות בכל פרט ופרט בסדר ההשתלשלות
> *The substance of this tract speaks of the unity of Godliness in every single detail of the chain of worlds.*

**Contemplating it.** Take the words wide first. "Every single detail" means all of it: every world and every level, down to "the end of the world of Asiyah", which is our world (line 2). Then step by step. The aim, the Mitteler Rebbe says, is a grasp that is "clothed and held in mind and heart" (line 2). Not a slogan. A knowing with handles on it. Then to the root. If the unity is in every detail, no detail is outside it: not the far worlds, not this table, not the chipped cup on it. He calls the book "the tract on contemplation" (line 3). He writes it for "every person who seeks God in truth and simplicity" (line 4), and signs it with "great love" for "all who seek God" (line 6). The picture under the whole book is a chain, each link holding the next. It breaks, as the book will show, because in a chain the top link is far from the bottom one. Here "their beginning is wedged in their end" (line 294).

**From inside.** A widening, and a patience. The sense that nothing is too small to count, and that the road is long but walked in company.

**At the edge.** Not yet. It names the goal. The edge comes in chapter 7, where the whole chain is seen "in one moment".

**Into the day.** Pick one ordinary thing you will use today, a spoon or a door handle, and tell yourself: this too is a detail the unity is in.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Chapter 1 (lines 8-55)

**The movement.** It slows the mind down. It shows the difference between a passing glance and a long look, and asks for the long look.

> מהות ההתבוננות בעצם הוא ענין ההסתכלות החזקה בעמקות הענין ולעמוד עליו הרבה
> *The essence of contemplation is a strong looking into the depth of the matter, and standing over it a long time.*

**Contemplating it.** The chapter's picture is the eye. One person glances at a thing "in passing". Later he forgets it, and can tell others only the general idea (lines 13-14). Another looks with a fixed eye, slowly, until he knows it inside and out (line 26). Contemplation is the second. Every idea has breadth, length and depth, like a river (lines 16-19). Breadth is explaining it from many sides. Length is bringing it down in parables until a small child could hold it. Depth is the main current in the middle, where the river runs from its source. The depth comes from wisdom (chochmah), which the text calls "nothing" (ayin): the spring before the river (lines 22-23). Then da'at, knowing: "binding one's feeling to the idea" (line 46). Its sign is that the mind seems to shrink and gather all its strength into this one thing (lines 50-52). Bring it home. Think of the last thing you read about God. Did you glance at it, or look? Pick one sentence and look again, the way you look at a face you love. The river picture breaks here: a river flows away from its source, but the depth of an idea stays joined to where it comes from.

**From inside.** A narrowing that feels like strength, not strain. The mind collects itself and the room goes a little quiet. Then the sense that an idea has an inside.

**At the edge.** It points to the edge. Below the depth of understanding lies "the depth of wisdom", which is called nothing (lines 29-31). The mind can go down toward it but cannot hold it.

**Into the day.** Read one line of prayer or Torah twice today: once at speed, once with a fixed eye. Notice the difference.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Chapter 2 (lines 56-89)

**The movement.** It moves an idea out of the head toward the rest of life. It names a second power, Tevunah, that draws deep understanding out into rulings, feelings and action.

> ואיש תבונה דווקא ידלנה וישאבנה מן ההעלם להביאה להתפשטות בכמה אופנים שונים לרוות את המקבלים
> *Only a man of understanding draws it up and pumps it out of hiding, to spread it in many ways and to water those who receive.*

**Contemplating it.** The verse is "Counsel in a man's heart is deep water, and a man of understanding draws it up" (Proverbs 20:5, lines 57-58). Deep water is understanding still hidden in itself. Tevunah is the bucket. Without it, a sharp mind holds an idea only "in its place" (line 63). The chapter's example: deep Talmud argument, many reasons this way and that, and still no ruling, guilty or innocent (lines 64-67). The idea is like raw material; nobody knows yet what will be made of it (line 68). With it, the same idea comes down into "a matter separate from it": a ruling, or "an arousal in the heart" (line 69). Tevunah is also where love and fear are born (lines 80-81). Bring it home. You know things about God that have never touched your Tuesday. Picture a deep well in the yard and a thirsty flock. The picture breaks: a bucket takes water out of the well, but drawing an idea out into life leaves its depth as deep as before.

**From inside.** A small, practical hunger: what does this mean for me? It is not a lowering of the idea. The text calls it bringing the depth near, "as if it were not deep at all" (line 60).

**At the edge.** It doesn't reach there. It shows the path from depth to life. The depth itself stays "in the hiding of the essence" (line 58).

**Into the day.** Take one thing you understand about God and draw one bucket from it: one sentence of what it asks of you today.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Chapter 3 (lines 90-112)

**The movement.** It turns the method on God for the first time, and chooses the nearest object: how He fills every world by making it from nothing. Then it warns about the gap between knowing and prayer.

> ועל זה נאמר וידעת כו' דע את אלה״י כו' שהוא בהשגה זו דממלא כל עלמין דוקא
> *Of this it says "Know" and "Know the God of your father": it means this very grasp, of how He fills all worlds.*

**Contemplating it.** The first object is "filling all worlds" (memale kol almin), which the text defines as "creation of something from nothing, the Godly power of the maker in the made" (line 91). Understanding looks at it long, in detail, until it reaches "the inside of the inside" (line 92). Length brings it close with parables, until a child could hold it (lines 93-94). Breadth looks at it many ways: the soul's life in the body, the motion of a living thing, letters of speech, a flame from a coal (lines 96-97). Only after all that does the depth come (line 101). Da'at is recognizing and feeling it (line 103). The verse "Know today" means this knowing (line 104). The Raya Mehemna adds: "in the heart everything is seen" (line 107). Then the warning. Without Tevunah a person understands perfectly while he hears it, but in prayer he cannot bring it in, and asks, "What is all this for?" To him they are "two separate things" (lines 111-112). Bring it home: is your learning in one room and your prayer in another? The flame-and-coal picture breaks, as the book itself says later: the flame comes out of the coal's own heat, and that is not true revealing (line 1058).

**From inside.** The world feels a little less solid and more given. Not unreal: made, now, from a source you cannot see.

**At the edge.** Yes. The depth of this very grasp reaches "the depth of wisdom that is found from nothing", which is "above the grasp of the mind" (lines 105-106).

**Into the day.** Before you pray today, think for one minute about one thing in the room being made from nothing. Carry that one thought into the first line of the prayer.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Chapter 4 (lines 113-160)

**The movement.** It corrects a feeling of closeness that is false. It asks the mind to climb one rung at a time, and says the whole is truly held only after the parts.

> אדרבא, על ידי זה מתקרב הדבר יותר אצלו בהיותו הולך מפרט לפרט העליון הימנו עד שכוללם יחד, ליחד יחוד כללי ולא יחוד פרטי
> *On the contrary, this brings it closer to him, as he goes from one detail to the detail above it until he includes them together, to make a unity of the whole and not of a part.*

**Contemplating it.** The question: contemplate in general or in detail? Each has its advantage (line 121). The general reaches toward the Essence. But it can fool you: a person "thinks the thing is very near him", and "in truth, from afar God appears to him" (lines 123-124). The detail fixes it in the soul (line 125). The chapter gives the order: first how the spheres and stars come to be from nothing, then the angels above them, then each world's ten powers, rung after rung, up to the kingship (malchut) of Atzilut, the highest world (lines 127-131). Each detail alone "is not Godliness in general" (line 132). Joined to the whole, it fixes the whole in you, "as is tried and tested by anyone who works deeply at contemplation" (line 133). Then the book says what it is after: "the Godly unity is a depth of understanding, in the self-nullification of the something to the nothing" (line 140). Even the widest unity is "one detail" next to the Essence (line 146). The aim is "to Him and not to His attributes" (line 153). Bring it home. Picture a staircase: you can't jump to the top landing, but each step is solid. The picture breaks: on a staircase the top stays far from the bottom; here each step, once joined, brings the top closer. And the text lets beginners start with the general and move to detail "little by little" (line 160).

**From inside.** Something like humility. The big warm feeling of "God is everything" gets quieter, and a steadier nearness comes, built out of small true things.

**At the edge.** Yes. Every unity the mind can make, even of the whole chain, "is reckoned a detail relative to the Essence" (line 146). Above it the text names only Him.

**Into the day.** Today, instead of thinking "God is everywhere", trace one thing you use one step back to what made it, and then one step more.

**Ladder.** L07 (Know it today, bring it to the heart)

## Chapter 5 (lines 161-205)

**The movement.** It gives the mind a test. Real contemplation always leaves two marks together: joy at what is grasped, and self-nullification (bitul: the self going quiet) before what is not. It empties out the comfort of having only one.

> ולפי ערך השמחה כך ערך הביטול ממש
> *As much as the joy, exactly that much the self-nullification.*

**Contemplating it.** Every contemplation of creation holds two things (line 165). First, how the limited "something" comes to be from nothing. The mind can grasp this with explanations and parables until it is certain (lines 166-167). Second, the Godly "nothing" that makes it, which is beyond it, "hidden from the eyes of the mind" (lines 168-169). The second is seen only "like lightning, in a mere glance", yet "as if it were confirmed by the eye's own sight" (lines 170-171). They move in opposite directions. In understanding, "the more he grasps, the better it is for him; the deeper he goes, the more he grasps" (line 176). In the sight of wisdom, the deeper he goes, the more wondrous the maker becomes, until he is pained (lines 177-178). So the joy and the bitul come together, and in proportion (lines 196-199). Whoever seems to grasp but has no bitul "has grasped nothing, without doubt"; whoever feels bitul without grasping holds "an empty fantasy" (lines 201-202). The text adds that many raised on Chassidus fool themselves here (line 205). Bring it home. Picture a climber: every ledge he reaches gives joy, and every ledge shows more mountain above. The picture breaks, because a mountain has a top.

**From inside.** Two things at once: a gladness in the chest, and the self going quiet, not sad, just smaller. Together they check each other. The joy doesn't swell into pride, and the smallness doesn't sink into gloom.

**At the edge.** Yes, and it is the book's own name for the edge: the Godly "nothing that makes it", which the mind does not grasp and sees only as a flash (lines 168-172).

**Into the day.** After you learn or pray today, ask two questions: what did I understand, and what did I see that I cannot understand? If you have only one answer, go back.

## Chapter 6 (lines 206-286)

**The movement.** It turns contemplation toward the heart, and to a surprising place: weeping and joy as one power in two lines. It asks for honest tears over the distance, and distrusts a joy that has none.

> והנה ידוע המאמר דבכיה תקיעא בלבאי מסטרא דא וחדווה מסטרא דא
> *The saying is known: weeping is lodged in my heart on this side, and joy on that side.*

**Contemplating it.** The soul becomes a vessel for knowing God, the chapter says, only through these two, though they are opposites, and both from one contemplation (lines 207-210). The picture is pleasure and pain. As much as you love a thing, so much it hurts to lose it: "it is truly one power, only divided into two lines exactly equal" (lines 225-228). So the joy of tasting God and the tears over His hiding grow together (lines 230-233). There are three levels (lines 250-253). Joy in the light you grasp, and tears where it is hidden. Higher, the hidden delight of wisdom, and the self given up. Highest, joy in the Essence itself, and tears when the mind cannot hold it, like Rabbi Akiva's eyes streaming at secrets too deep to contain (line 241), or David and Jonathan, who kissed and wept (line 254). The practice the chapter names is Tikkun Chatzot, the midnight lament, with "simple tears over the sins of youth" (lines 267-269). These tears repair the root, which is casting off the yoke, and wake the joy of the morning prayer (lines 271-273). "My tears have been my bread day and night" (line 269). Bring it home. Remember weeping at a parting from someone you love. The picture breaks where it must: here the distance is on your side only, and the joy waits in the same place as the tears.

**From inside.** A heart that can be sad without falling apart, and glad without floating off. The text says the tears become bread.

**At the edge.** Yes. The highest weeping comes when the mind "cannot contain" the Essence of the Infinite, "which is not grasped at all" (lines 240-242). Its twin is the laughter of the future, which "comes from its opposite", the weeping of exile (lines 265-266).

**Into the day.** Tonight before sleep, say one honest sentence to God about what feels far, then one about what you are glad of. For a Jew who knows it, say one line of Tikkun Chatzot. Not on a crushed day: the text's tears are over the distance from God, not over yourself, and if they flatten you, stop.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Chapter 7 (lines 287-351)

**The movement.** It gives the mind the whole map at once, and then asks it to fold the map into one moment while looking at the plainest physical thing.

> שבזה ממש שורה בבחינת אין סוף דוקא מבחינת תחלת הכל
> *that right here rests the Infinite itself, from the beginning of all.*

**Contemplating it.** The map is a chain from "I am first", the kingship of the Infinite, to "I am last", the lowest point of our world, "and their beginning is wedged in their end... like a chain whose last link is tied to the highest" (lines 291-294). "This is the whole of man": to contemplate every detail from head to end and join it all to the simple unity (line 296). Then higher. Even that joining is "one detail" next to the surrounding light, and that is one detail next to the Essence, which is "above being even a whole to parts" (lines 321-324). Then the practice. All your life, train mind and heart "to make the short out of the long", until "in one moment" you look at the physical heavens, the four elements, "every detail of this low world", and see the Infinite resting in it from the beginning of all (lines 327-332). That is why the Psalms praise God by sky and earth and not by the upper worlds (lines 333-335). Bring it home. Look at the table, the water in the glass, your hand. By count it is the farthest thing from the source; "in truth very near, with no break or hiding in between" (line 337). The chain picture breaks right here: in a chain, the middle links stand in between; here nothing does.

**From inside.** Seeing goes quiet and full at once. The plain thing in front of you does not change, but it stops being "just" a thing. The text asks for this in one moment, not as a long feeling.

**At the edge.** Yes. Past the whole map is the Essence, "called holy and apart", above being whole or part (lines 323-324). And it is found in the lowest thing.

**Into the day.** Once today, look up at the actual sky and say the verse the text quotes: "Lift up your eyes and see: who created these?" (line 331).

## Chapter 8 (lines 352-427)

**The movement.** It walks down again, for will, wisdom, feeling, thought, speech and deed, until the mind cannot find a place where the first source stops.

> איך שלא נבדל גם הפרט היותר אחרון מהמקור הכללי היותר ראשון
> *how even the very last detail is not separate from the very first source of all.*

**Contemplating it.** Will first. The one simple will becomes the will to emanate, to create, to form and to make, and "there is really no difference" between the will behind the highest world and the will behind ours (lines 343-351). A person, too, has one will that runs from wanting to the smallest act (lines 349-350). Wisdom next: "You made them all in wisdom" (line 361). The wisdom God gave Solomon, a man in our world, came down from the wisdom in the Essence, rung by rung, into his soul (lines 371-376). The stone tablets and the ark in the Holy of Holies held the Crown and Wisdom of the Essence (line 377). Kindness too. The kindness of the Infinite is the same one we ask for when we say, "May Your kindness be upon us", in the small, contracted kindness that shines in this world (lines 396-398). So when a person looks at the heavens, the earth, a stone, a plant, an animal, a speaking being, he sees that even the last detail is not separate from the first source (lines 403-405). Bring it home. The next time someone is kind to you in a small way, a held door, a plate passed, remember line 398. The picture of a person's one will in many deeds breaks: a person's will changes and tires; here it is all "one will... relative to the Essence" (line 402).

**From inside.** Small good things begin to feel as if they come from far and near at once.

**At the edge.** Yes. "He is the place of the world" (line 413), and He grasps all worlds and none grasps Him, "not even the first thought and will" (line 414).

**Into the day.** Say "May Your kindness be upon us" once today about something small, meaning the kindness right in front of you.

## Chapter 9 (lines 428-481)

**The movement.** It asks the hard question and answers it. If the world changes, does God change? It moves change to the receiving side, and so gives prayer a place to stand.

> על כן אין בעצמות אלהו״ת שום שינוי כלל
> *therefore there is no change at all in the Essence of God.*

**Contemplating it.** The question is honest (lines 428-443). Down here judgment turns to kindness, and "He renews the work of creation each day". If the lowest is tied to the highest, must the highest change too? And a person's own will does turn from strict to kind, so what are we asking when we say "May it be Your will"? The answer is the tzimtzum, the contraction (line 444). If the flow came as cause and effect, change would reach Him. But it comes as light and radiance, and through a contraction, and "so there is no change at all in the Essence" (lines 445-446). "After the contraction there is change, and that is from the side of the receivers alone" (line 448). So "every person must know the tzimtzum and contemplate it" (line 451). Before Him "darkness is as light" (line 453), and even in His filling of the world, "its upper and lower are equal" (line 457). Prayer is placed exactly. "May it be Your will, from before You" asks for a change in the flow after the contraction, "from judgment to kindness", not a change in Him (lines 479-481). Bring it home. The chapter's own comparison is a person, whose will really does turn (line 441). That picture breaks on purpose: in a person the will itself changes; in Him the change is only in what reaches us.

**From inside.** Steadiness. What changes in your day is real, but it does not reach the ground you stand on.

**At the edge.** Yes. "You are He before the world was created" (line 440): the Essence, unchanged, beyond anything the mind can picture. The text leaves it there and turns to prayer.

**Into the day.** When you ask God for something today, ask for a change in what reaches you, knowing He does not have to change for it.

## Chapter 10 (lines 482-572)

**The movement.** It takes the mind before the whole map, to God alone, and empties it of the picture of "one" as many pieces joined together.

> בחינת יחיד משמעו לבדו ממש … והוא למעלה מהתאחדות נפרדים שנקרא אחד
> *Yachid means truly alone ... and it is above the joining of separate things, which is called echad, one.*

**Contemplating it.** Before any world, contemplate the source "before the contraction... there too in detail" (lines 487-489). It has three levels: yachid, echad and kadmon (line 492). Yachid is "alone": "there is nothing besides Him, to say of Him a word of joining or of unity" (line 503). Even "ten sefirot" cannot be said there yet (line 495). The picture is a person's deepest self, the yechidah. A kind person is kind because kindness is in the very root of his soul (line 516). Before it meets any need, that kindness already holds everything he will ever do for anyone (lines 518-520). At that depth you cannot even say his traits are "united" in him: they are not separate from him at all (lines 529-530). Like the power of motion inside a living thing before it moves: it isn't "motion" yet, general or particular (lines 534-536). Like the rays inside the sun (line 547). Bring it home. Think of the kindest person you know, before they do anything today. It is all already there. The picture breaks, as the next chapter says: in the soul it is a likeness, and even His "wise and understanding" are said only from what He "estimated in Himself" for what would be (lines 505-506).

**From inside.** A hush. The mind stops counting. What is left is not empty. It is full in a way that has no parts.

**At the edge.** This is the edge. "Alone" is the word for Him as He is, above "one". Past it the text has no picture, only the soul's own depth as a hint.

**Into the day.** For a Jew: in the Shema today, when you say Echad, hold for a moment that He is also alone, and that "one" is His holding of a world. For anyone: say once, slowly, "You alone".

## Chapter 11 (lines 573-643)

**The movement.** It answers "why is there anything?" from inside the Essence: because He desires kindness. It turns the question of origins into warmth.

> ובודאי סיבת עליית הרצון הפשוט בעצמותו היינו רק כי חפץ חסד הוא בעצמו
> *Surely the reason the simple will arose in His Essence is only this: He Himself desires kindness.*

**Contemplating it.** The Essence before the contraction holds ten powers, "the ten sefirot hidden in the hiding of the Essence itself" (line 574). Among them is the desire for kindness (line 575). The first simple will, from which all things come, arose only "because He desires kindness" (line 577): "to do good with His kindness, to be the source of the source" of the whole chain (line 578). Then three levels in the source: yachid, beyond "united"; echad, "simple oneness", ready to go out (line 599); and kadmon, what He "estimated in Himself in potential" of all that would be (lines 606-614). The blessing "Blessed is He who spoke and the world came to be" is read in these levels. He "spoke" in hiding, in the simple will; "and the world came to be" from what He estimated in Himself (lines 629-633). "He tells the end from the beginning" (line 636). Bring it home. Picture a generous person planning a gift long before the day: the whole gift already exists in her mind. The picture breaks: she plans because the other person is there; He desires kindness before there is anyone to receive it.

**From inside.** The world stops feeling like an accident or a machine. Under it is a wish to give, and a person can feel addressed by that.

**At the edge.** Yes. The reason is "in Himself" (line 577), above any reason the mind can give. The text reaches it and stops there.

**Into the day.** Do one kindness today for someone who did not ask and will not know.

## Chapter 12 (lines 644-705)

**The movement.** It takes away the picture of a God who left. The contraction is like a teacher who states a law briefly: everything is still in him.

> ולא שנסתלק ונתעלם לגמרי … רק כמו השונה בדרך קצרה
> *and not that He withdrew and hid completely ... only like one who teaches the law in brief.*

**Contemplating it.** Why go deep here? "Because this is the main point of the true unity in the Essence of the Infinite", so that a person can direct his will in self-giving "to the Essence of the Infinite alone" (lines 646-648). The first picture: a great teacher and a student far below him. To reach the student he hides the whole depth of his thought and leaves one point: he states the law briefly (lines 660-663). He has not "withdrawn and hidden completely"; ask him, and he will open it all up at once (line 664). The whole Talmud is inside the short Mishnah (line 669). More pictures, each hiding more: the soul in the body, where a blind man can still father a child who sees (line 680); a craftsman's mark that holds the whole building (line 689); a sign made only to remember (line 690); a thrown stone, carried by a power already gone from the hand (lines 695-697); a hint with the hand, a finger writing (lines 701-703). All say one thing: the Essence stays, hidden and held in the point (line 705). Bring it home. Remember a teacher or parent who gave you one short sentence that you understood only years later held everything they knew. The picture breaks, as the next chapter admits: a teacher's short law still shows its long reasoning, while the "trace" left by the contraction shows that everything withdrew (lines 738-740).

**From inside.** The feeling of being left alone loosens. What looks like absence becomes a kind of care: the one who hid did it so you could receive.

**At the edge.** Yes. The aim is self-giving "to the Essence of the Infinite alone" (line 648): "Whom have I in heaven... and with You I want nothing on earth" (lines 654-655).

**Into the day.** When something today feels empty of God, say: "Not withdrawn. Said briefly."

## Chapter 13 (lines 706-782)

**The movement.** It applies the pictures and then tests them. It holds that He is fully present, admits that the best picture fails, and brings the mind to a trace that holds everything.

> כך גם אחר הצמצום הרי ישנו לאור העצמיות כמו שהוא בבחינת אין סוף ממש, רק שלא נגלה למטה לגבי שפע המקבלים
> *so after the contraction too, the light of the Essence is there as it is, truly infinite, only not revealed below, toward those who receive the flow.*

**Contemplating it.** As the Essence filled the whole "empty space" before the contraction, so after it, "only not revealed below", and "relative to Himself there is no change in this at all" (lines 710-711). "He made darkness His hiding place" is only "relative to below" (line 713). What later "reveals deep things out of darkness" is that very light from before (line 717). Then the text turns on its own picture: "in truth this is not a true likeness" (line 738). The trace (reshimu) "shows that everything withdrew from there" (line 739). The truer pictures are the craftsman's mark, the sign for remembering, and the thrown stone (lines 741-744). Yet the trace is what lets everything come into view (line 760). In the first thought He "sees everything in one glance" (line 764). "He is all-powerful, and within Him also the power that limits" (line 736). Bring it home. A builder's pencil line on a board: short, and the whole house is in it. The picture breaks both ways, and the text keeps both: the line holds everything, and the line is not the house.

**From inside.** Two things held at once without forcing: He is here fully, and I do not see Him. The tension becomes a kind of respect.

**At the edge.** Yes. "This is the whole of man: to deepen his mind in all this", for from it he comes to "the true unity, binding his soul to the Life of all life... the Essence of the Infinite itself" (lines 781-782).

**Into the day.** Make a small mark today on something you mean to finish, and let it remind you that a trace can hold the whole.

## Chapter 14 (lines 783-834)

**The movement.** It shows that hiding is where giving comes from. The point that seems to hold everything back is the source of the line that carries everything down.

> שבאמת הנקודה והקו אינם ב' הפכים, שהרי הקו נמשך מן הנקודה
> *in truth the point and the line are not two opposites, since the line is drawn out of the point.*

**Contemplating it.** The point looks like "a complete and true contraction", hiding everything so that no light comes down (line 792). How can the line, which spreads, come out of it (line 794)? The answer: the point is like raw potential (hyle). It does not spread because it is above spreading, "which shows that the whole essence is included in it" (lines 796-808). Like life inside a body before it animates, or motion before it moves (lines 800-801). The human picture: a student before his teacher. While he is busy swallowing, he shrinks to a point and gives nothing out. Afterwards, from that very contraction, all his giving to others flows (lines 810-813). The line itself is like the builder's measuring rod: it has no width or length of its own, yet every measure is taken by it (lines 821-826). It carries "a likeness of the Essence's light", measured to the receivers (lines 831-832). Bring it home. Remember times you were quiet and small, listening. They were not empty. The student picture breaks: a student is filled from outside; the point holds the Essence within.

**From inside.** Being small stops feeling like being less. The quiet, listening part of a person gets its dignity.

**At the edge.** It touches it: what is in the point is "the whole essence" (line 808), above spreading. It goes no further.

**Into the day.** In one conversation today, be the point: listen fully before you give anything back.

## Chapter 15 (lines 835-898)

**The movement.** It joins two opposite powers, joining and measuring, in one line, and roots both in the Essence. It sends prayer past every attribute, to "before You".

> אנת הוא דמייחד לון ומקשר לון כו', אנת הוא מלגאו ומלבר
> *You are the One who unites them and binds them; You are within and without.*

**Contemplating it.** The line has two opposite powers (lines 848-855). One joins: it includes left in right, so that the light of kindness can shine in the vessel of severity (lines 849-851). The other measures: it gives every light its exact size (line 853). They seem to fight. "Both are true, and each depends on the other" (lines 856-857). The joining comes from the Essence's own light in the line, which can unite all opposites (line 860). The measuring comes from the trace, which set the limit, like a craftsman's mark that keeps the work from leaning right or left (lines 864-868). So every new light, every change "from the time of the Temple to the time of exile", comes from the Essence before the contraction (lines 871-875). And so we say "May it be Your will, from before You": "from before Your very Essence, above even the ten essential powers", to change the measure "from judgment to kindness" (lines 876-879). "To Him and not to His attributes" means even His essential attributes (lines 884-885). Bring it home. You too have a part that joins and a part that measures: the part that forgives and the part that keeps the rule. The picture breaks: in you they fight; in Him they come from one place.

**From inside.** The either-or loosens. Strictness and mercy both start to look as if they come from a care larger than either.

**At the edge.** Yes. "You are One, and not in the count of ten" (line 886), the Essence above all attributes. Even "the power of limit and measure" comes "from the Essence of the Infinite alone" (line 887).

**Into the day.** Say "May it be Your will" once today for someone else, meaning: from before You, not from my picture of You.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Chapter 16 (lines 899-942)

**The movement.** It gives a picture of a mind that sees everything at once: a builder's decided will. It lets the contemplator feel how a whole creation can be held in one glance.

> שבמחשבה זו הכללית צופה ומביט עד סוף כל הדורות בסקירה א'
> *in this general thought He looks and sees to the end of all generations in one glance.*

**Contemplating it.** There are two kinds of will (line 905). The first is the simple will "included in His very Essence", not yet even a general thought (line 906). It is decided, yet "as if it were not" (line 916). The picture: a man who long ago decided to build a great house in a certain way (line 915). Everything is set in him: the pleasure, the plan, the measures. But it is all still inside him (line 916). Only when it turns to the deed does a general thought appear that holds the whole building "in one glance", and then it divides into particular wants, down to the last detail at the end of the work (lines 917-921). Above, the first will is in the Essence. The second comes after the contraction, as the line, and its first circle surrounds all four worlds "equally" (lines 927-939). Bring it home. Think of something you have decided to do and not yet started. It is complete in you and not yet anywhere. The picture breaks: your plan needs time to unfold; His one glance sees "to the end of all generations" at once.

**From inside.** Being seen whole. A person can feel that their life, beginning to end, is held in one look, not pieced together.

**At the edge.** It points there: the first will "remains included in the hiding of the Essence", above even "general and particular" (lines 925-926).

**Into the day.** Before you start something today, hold the whole of it in your mind for one moment, then begin.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Chapter 17 (lines 943-1002)

**The movement.** It says that what is last in deed was first in thought. Then it sets a limit: no thought grasps the Essence, not even the first thought.

> שאין פרט היותר אחרון שלא קדם להיות ברצון ובמחשבה זו
> *there is no last detail that was not first in this will and this thought.*

**Contemplating it.** The first will is called "the life of all worlds", from the head of the first Man (Adam Kadmon) to his heel at the end of our world: "nothing formed is hidden from You" (lines 950-951). No last detail was not first in it, "and on the contrary, the end of the deed, like the end of the seventh millennium, rose first in this first thought" (lines 952-953). In this will, pleasure is clothed, "and they became truly one" (line 955). There are two orders (lines 978-982). In the plan, the will surrounds everything. In the deed, the will hides inside, and the outermost thing is the act itself. The deed is lowest, and holds the will as its innermost. But "in the world of the Infinite there is not even above and below, only utterly simple oneness" (line 992), and "no thought grasps Him, not even the first thought" (line 997). Bring it home. What you do last today, putting a child to bed, locking the door, may be what your whole day was for. The builder picture breaks at line 997: a builder's plan can be read; this will cannot be grasped even by its own first thought.

**From inside.** Small final acts gain weight. The end of the day stops being leftovers.

**At the edge.** Yes, line 997. Above the first thought is the Essence that no thought grasps.

**Into the day.** Do the last small task of today, locking up or washing a cup, as if it had been thought of first.

## Chapter 18 (lines 1003-1046)

**The movement.** It finds creation from nothing inside the person's own wanting. It turns attention to a daily fact: wants come and go, so they come from somewhere.

> שהרי אנו רואים שלא בכל העתים יהיה לאדם רצון לרצון פרטי, כמו הרצון במסחור או בלימוד
> *for we see that a person does not at all times have the will for a particular want, like the will to trade or to learn.*

**Contemplating it.** The first world, Adam Kadmon, is called "Adam of creation", because it is true creation from nothing (line 1007). The picture is in you (lines 1013-1017). A deep will lives in you. From it comes a "will for a will": today you want to learn, or to trade. Some days you don't. Some days it shines strong and some days weak. So whatever makes that want must itself come "as creation from nothing" (line 1017). The text's sharp example: a bribe reaches a person's core, so it keeps making in him a will to find the giver innocent, and the reverse toward someone he hates (lines 1018-1020). The line itself, above all this, knows everything "in its own thought, and not as a will coming to be in a thought outside it at all" (line 1045). Bring it home. Notice the next time a want arrives: to eat, to call someone, to open a book. Where was it a minute ago? The picture breaks: your wants come from a soul that was given to you; His first will has nothing before it.

**From inside.** Curiosity about your own wanting, and a lightness about it. A want is not you. It arrives.

**At the edge.** It touches it: the source of a new will lies beyond the will, and the text calls its arrival creation from nothing. It goes no further here.

**Into the day.** When a strong want arrives today, pause and ask, in words: where did this come from?

## Chapter 19 (lines 1047-1101)

**The movement.** It corrects the picture of the highest world as a thing that was made. It is a revealing: something hidden coming into view, the way colour shows sweetness.

> כך כל עיקר בחינת עשר ספירות דאצילות הוא שיצאו מהעלמם לגלוי בלבד
> *So the whole of the ten sefirot of Atzilut is only that they came out of their hiding into view.*

**Contemplating it.** Hiding and revealing is not a cause making an effect, like a force acting on something (line 1053). It is like radiance: the sun's rays, the soul's life in the body (lines 1054-1055). Truer still: the motion of a living thing, or the letters of thought and speech (line 1056). The old picture of "a flame bound to a coal" fails, because the flame comes out of the coal's heat, "and that is not the truth of revealing" (lines 1057-1058). It is more like colour: "the colour of the apple, which is not of the apple's substance" (line 1059). A person whose nature is kind keeps producing kind wishes, in many matters, "and all from one source" (lines 1061, 1074). So the sefirot are called "lights": they only came out of hiding (lines 1080-1081). Like "He drew from the spirit of Moses", which did not take Moses' spirit, only its shine (lines 1083-1084). Bring it home. Watch a kind friend's face when they see you. The kindness you see is not a new thing; it is them, showing. The picture breaks: a face can show or hide on a whim; here the showing is the Essence's own light.

**From inside.** The people and things you love start to look like faces rather than objects: a showing of something deeper, not a separate thing.

**At the edge.** It stays short of the edge. It says what emanation is, and leaves the source hidden.

**Into the day.** Notice one good trait in someone today and tell them, as a showing of who they are.

## Chapter 20 (lines 1102-1149)

**The movement.** It shows the mind why pure opposites cannot meet, and where they can. It lowers the pride of one's own nature and makes room for the other.

> וזה מה שכתוב בספר יצירה טרף אש ומים ובללן זה בזה … דהיינו בחינת המורכב דאש ומים דוקא, ולא בעצם מהותן
> *This is what Sefer Yetzirah says: He took fire and water and kneaded them into each other ... that is, fire and water as a compound, and not in their very essence.*

**Contemplating it.** The world of chaos (tohu) had only "points": each trait pure, "bound in one vessel", not joining (lines 1116-1119). "They have no vessel in which two such opposites can be included" (line 1123). The text's plain proof: someone whose mind leans to kindness by nature cannot bear someone whose mind leans to strictness by nature (line 1124). In an argument like Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel, one side may fall silent, but it cannot come round (line 1125). Repair (tikkun) comes when a trait goes out beyond its essence (line 1126). A man cruel by nature can be kind to the one who saved his life; a kind man can lean to judgment against an enemy who hurt him badly (lines 1129-1131). That is kindness "compounded", not pure, and in the compound a thing and its opposite can live together (lines 1130-1144). Scripture has joy and anger at once (lines 1138-1139). Bring it home. Think of the person whose nature most grates on yours. At the level of pure nature you will not meet. In a shared task, a shared debt, a shared love, you can. The kneading picture breaks: dough stays mixed; people have to be kneaded again each day.

**From inside.** Less need to win. A person sees their own nature as one point and the other's as another, and looks for the compound.

**At the edge.** Not here. The next chapters take it up: the meeting above the split (ch. 21), and in the Essence (ch. 22).

**Into the day.** Find one thing you can do together today with someone whose temperament is the opposite of yours.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Chapter 21 (lines 1150-1192)

**The movement.** It lifts the mind above the split. The middle line can join right and left because it reaches a will that has no reason.

> דהיינו שעולה עד בחינת פנימית הכתר, שלמעלה מהתחלקות הקוין
> *that is, it rises up to the inner Crown, which is above the division into lines.*

**Contemplating it.** Right is kindness, left is strictness, and the middle joins them (lines 1150-1162). Da'at is the go-between: it can make the mind lean to kindness in this way and to judgment in that way, "even in one place" (lines 1163-1165). Mercy is the middle trait: it "comes only after judgment, and still mercy lets him off" (line 1167). The middle can do this because it rises to the inner Crown, above the division (line 1174). There, will has no reason, "not even a hidden reason" (lines 1184-1185): "Be silent, so it arose in thought" (line 1186). So we say "May it be Your will, from before You", from before the curtain, "to change from a will of judgment to a will of kindness" (lines 1191-1192). Bring it home. Remember a time you forgave someone and could not say why. Reasons pull left and right; a love with no reason can hold both. The text's picture is the middle bar that runs "from end to end" through the Tabernacle's boards (line 1173). It breaks: a bar only holds the boards; this middle also rises above them.

**From inside.** A quiet under the arguments. When you stop needing the reason, both sides can be held.

**At the edge.** Yes. Simple will "without any reason at all" (line 1184), above right and left.

**Into the day.** In one disagreement today, look for the thing you both want, before the reasons.

**Ladder.** L08 (What can't meet in me meets in Him)

## Chapter 22 (lines 1193-1269)

**The movement.** It shows how opposites sweeten each other and how a blow can be a healing. Then it goes to the deepest turn of all: essential evil made good, only through a self-giving that reaches the Essence.

> אך בבעלי תשובה אנו רואים שנהפך גם עצם הרע לטוב, וכן זדונות עצמן נעשו כזכיות … הרי הוא מצד מסירת נפש בעצמות אור אין סוף, שלמעלה גם מהתחלקות עצם החסד וגבורה
> *But in those who return we see that even the very essence of evil is turned to good, and deliberate sins themselves become like merits ... this is from self-giving to the Essence of the Infinite, which is above even the division of essential kindness and severity.*

**Contemplating it.** First, sweetening. An accuser becomes a defender, bitter water is sweetened, because "in sweetness there is bitterness hidden, and in bitterness sweetness hidden" (lines 1208-1213). The text's kitchen picture is a jam cooked from honey and radish: sharp and sweet in one pleasant taste (line 1214). Then a harder joining. The strength of rain comes contracted "so that it can be drawn in abundance", and "the contraction of the flow everywhere, so that it can be received" (lines 1228-1229). "Sufferings of love", "as a father to the son he favours": kindness dressed in judgment is "the force of kindness, more than kindness itself" (lines 1230-1232). "He sends the cure before the blow" (line 1233). But pure essences cannot join: the deep good of a good man and the deep evil of an evil one (lines 1261-1263). And yet in those who return, even essential evil turns to good (line 1264). The reason is self-giving (mesirut nefesh, giving yourself over) that reaches the Essence, above even the split between kindness and severity (line 1265). So one who gives himself can say "May it be Your will" at the root (line 1266). Bring it home. Think of one thing in your past you wish were not there. The text does not say it was good. It says it can become good, from a place higher than good and evil. The jam picture breaks: in jam both tastes remain; here the bitterness itself becomes the sweetness.

**From inside.** Hope without pretending. A person does not have to explain their worst to be free of it. They have to give themselves to Someone above it.

**At the edge.** Yes. The Essence "above even the division of essential kindness and severity" (line 1265): "to Him and not to His attributes, not even His essential attributes" (line 1269).

**Into the day.** Say one honest sentence of return to God today, and hand it over without arguing your case. Not on a crushed day: then stay with the rain and the jam.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Chapter 23 (lines 1270-1322)

**The movement.** It looks into the soul's deepest drives, will and pleasure, and shows they are one. Then it quietly denies that pleasure is the bottom of the self.

> כי מהות אחד הן הרצון והתענוג, ולא נודע מי קדם
> *for will and pleasure are one essence, and it is not known which came first.*

**Contemplating it.** Every will has pleasure in it: without pleasure in wealth or honour, no one would want them (lines 1277-1278). And without will there is no pleasure: what you don't want gives you none (lines 1280-1281). So they are one essence (line 1283). By nature the soul follows pleasure until almost all of it is drawn in, and shrinks from pain (lines 1285-1291). So the early Kabbalists said pleasure is the soul's essence, "nothing is above pleasure" (line 1295). "The truth is not so" (line 1296): rather, the soul fills with pleasure and is drawn after it until it nearly spends itself, "my soul longs, it even faints" (line 1296). Every trait lives on its pleasure. A kind person lives from the pleasure of doing good (lines 1299-1300). A person who must win, "if he does not win, falls ill" (lines 1302-1303). Without joy in learning, no new idea comes (lines 1305-1306). Bring it home. What is the pleasure behind your strongest want this week? Name it gently. The picture of the soul drawn after pleasure breaks at line 1296: what is drawn is not the same as what draws it. Under the pleasure, the soul is more.

**From inside.** Honesty about your drives, without shame, and a quiet discovery: I am not only what I enjoy.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: the soul's essence is not pleasure but what pleasure fills (line 1296). The next chapter carries this up to Him.

**Into the day.** Do one good thing today and notice, without pride, the pleasure in it.

## Chapter 24 (lines 1323-1384)

**The movement.** It carries the soul's pleasure up to God and then past it. The Essence delights, and is beyond delight.

> ועם כל זה באמת הרי עצמות אור אין סוף מובדל גם מבחינת תענוג פשוט … רק שנמשך ומאיר בבחינת העונג
> *And yet in truth the Essence of the Infinite is apart even from simple pleasure ... it only extends and shines as pleasure.*

**Contemplating it.** Above there is "the delight of the King in His own Essence" (line 1324), and all the ten hidden powers come from that simple delight (line 1325). Yet the Essence is apart even from simple pleasure; it only shines as pleasure (lines 1327-1328). That is why the old saying "nothing is above pleasure" is still true, of how it shines (line 1329). That pleasure and will is "clothed in every single sefirah, in every detail, and without it there is no existence at all" (line 1336), "and it also surrounds them all" (line 1337). "The absence of the light of pleasure is the absence of the light of their existence" (line 1354). The text's picture: a man in the joy of his son's wedding does great kindness to everyone, and then anything can be asked of him, even to turn judgment into kindness (lines 1362-1365). And: "Is there old age before the Holy One?" The Essence is above time; when it shines into time, time too becomes eternal (lines 1382-1384). Bring it home. Picture that father at the wedding. The picture breaks: his joy comes and goes; the delight that holds the world never stops.

**From inside.** Gladness that things exist at all, and a sense that under them is a joy that is not yours and is holding you.

**At the edge.** Yes. The Essence apart even from simple pleasure (line 1327). The text reaches it and names nothing past it.

**Into the day.** Once today, share someone's joy on purpose, the way the father at the wedding does, with an open hand.

## Chapter 25 (lines 1385-1450)

**The movement.** It gives the mind a reason to trust what it cannot see through. What is higher than reason looks dark from below, like high peaks the sun does not reach.

> אבל המדות שברצון נקרא טורי חשוכא, מדות שבהעלם הרצון
> *But the traits that are in the will are called "mountains of darkness": traits in the hiding of the will.*

**Contemplating it.** Most of the chapter maps the minds of wisdom and understanding. Wisdom is the point that "flashes like lightning, above the mind" (line 1395). Two surprising pictures. The overflow: when a mind is overfull, bits fall away from it, and their root is higher than the idea itself; so "the droppings of the wisdom above are Torah", said in praise (lines 1410-1411). Like a tightly sealed wineskin that bursts (line 1409). And the mountains of darkness (lines 1421-1427). Traits that come from reason and explanation are "mountains of light", for wisdom is called light. Traits that live in the will, above reason, are "mountains of darkness", like high mountains the sunlight does not reach (line 1424). The text's plain example of will before reason: a bribe makes the mind find arguments for the giver (lines 1431-1434). The will comes first; the reasons follow. Bring it home. There are loves in you, for a child, a place, for God, that you cannot explain. They look dark to your reasons. They are higher, not lower. The mountain picture breaks: a mountain is dark because light can't reach it; here the dark is where the light comes from.

**From inside.** Relief about what you cannot explain in yourself. The unexplained love stops needing to justify itself.

**At the edge.** Yes: traits above reason, rooted in the hidden wisdom the text calls "the secrets of wisdom" (line 1428).

**Into the day.** Tell God one thing you love without a reason, and leave it without one.

## Chapter 26 (lines 1451-1509)

**The movement.** It measures how deep a union goes by how much of the self is given. It turns the contemplator from the map to his own readiness.

> והיינו במסירת נפש דקריאת שמע, מה שאין כן בתורה ומצות בלא מסירת נפש אין היחוד רק בחיצוניות
> *This is through the self-giving of the Shema. Torah and mitzvot without self-giving make the union only on the outside.*

**Contemplating it.** The chapter is mostly about the inner and outer unions of wisdom and understanding (Abba and Imma). Shabbat is pleasure, the inner light of wisdom; a festival is joy, understanding (lines 1455-1458). Two small pictures. "Silence is a fence for wisdom": when you stop, the mind rises to its source and a new idea comes out (line 1470). And a firm mind is like legs that hold the body up; without it a person easily slips from one view to another (lines 1472-1474). Then the parenthesis that matters. In the Shema, "Havayah our God" reaches the inner union, through self-giving; Torah and mitzvot without it unite only on the outside (lines 1480-1481). The Ten Martyrs raised the inner union "only through self-giving" (line 1482). A very great sage, who knows an idea from its source, can teach it even to a small child (line 1494). The depth that goes highest can go lowest. Bring it home. Think of the good habit you keep most easily. Are you in it, or only your hands? The picture of inside and outside breaks: the outside is not worthless; it is where the inside comes to live.

**From inside.** A readiness, not a strain: the sense of being willing to be wholly there. It is more often a quiet yes than a burst.

**At the edge.** Yes. Self-giving is the book's key to the inner union here, and to the Essence itself in chapter 22 (line 1265).

**Into the day.** For a Jew: at the Shema today, before Echad, say in your heart "all of me". For anyone: do one good act today as if it were your whole offering.

## Chapter 27 (lines 1510-1559)

**The movement.** It widens the unity from the light to the container. Not only what shines is one with Him, but also what holds and limits it.

> דאיהו וחיוהי חד הן אורות, ואיהו וגרמוהי חד הן בחינת הכלים
> *"He and His life are one": these are the lights. "He and His causes are one": these are the vessels.*

**Contemplating it.** The Zohar's formula has two halves (line 1513). The lights are "without what": nothing definable (line 1514). The vessels are "the existence of something" (line 1516). The picture is sight in the eye (line 1535). Spiritual sight, clothed in the matter of the eye, becomes physical seeing, and becomes limited: you see only so far (lines 1536-1542). Matter is not foreign to form. It is form's last level made thick, "for the blood is the life" (lines 1552-1554). So they grow together: a child's mind grows and his brain grows with it, "day by day until seventy" (lines 1556-1559). A side note: in a sudden fright a person lifts a load far beyond his strength. That, the text says, is the surrounding power (makif), which has no limit (line 1548). Bring it home. Look at your hands: they limit what you can do, and they are how you do anything. The eye picture breaks: an eye's limit is a loss; the vessel above is one with Him as much as the light is.

**From inside.** A new tenderness toward limits: the body, the schedule, the small room. They are not only walls.

**At the edge.** It points there: "He and His causes are one" puts the container inside the unity too. Chapter 29 takes it further.

**Into the day.** Thank God once today for one limit that lets you do something.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Chapter 28 (lines 1560-1600)

**The movement.** It turns the limit from a loss into a way through. The container is what lets light reach anyone.

> על כן מצד בחינת הכלים המגבילים, על ידי זה דוקא יוכל להיות כל בחינת השפעות והמשכות למטה מטה
> *So it is through the vessels that limit, and precisely through them, that every flow and drawing-down can reach lower and lower.*

**Contemplating it.** The light of the highest world, as it comes from its source, "is still united with its very source" (line 1562), though it is only like appearance and colour (line 1564). Colour shows the essence: the white of the apple shows its sweetness (lines 1565-1566). The vessel is like the mind's light already mixed into the matter of the brain: it limits (lines 1575-1577). It is "the something-ness of the light", when it shows as a something (line 1585). Yet only through the vessel can anything go down (line 1595). Through the brain, understanding passes into feelings and to others; through the heart, love reaches someone (lines 1596-1597). Like a lens that helps the eye see further (line 1593). Bring it home. The words you use, the hours you have, your particular body: these are vessels. Without them your love reaches no one. The lens picture breaks: a lens is added from outside; these vessels grow from the light itself.

**From inside.** Peace with being particular. You do not have to be everything to carry something real.

**At the edge.** It stays near: "from the side of the Essence of the Emanator, they are called ten sefirot without what" (line 1600). It goes no further here.

**Into the day.** Put one feeling you have for someone into one concrete vessel today: a note, a call, a dish.

## Chapter 29 (lines 1601-1651)

**The movement.** It says what the whole map was for, and then goes further: the root of limit comes from the Essence, even before the root of light.

> שבאמת אין הפרש בין איהו וחיוהי חד לאיהו וגרמוהי חד
> *for in truth there is no difference between "He and His life are one" and "He and His causes are one".*

**Contemplating it.** The chapter opens with the purpose. In all this detail of lights and vessels, the whole aim is this one thing: there is no difference between the two (line 1601). Then the surprise: "from the side of the Essence of the Emanator, He made a root for the vessels before He made a root for the lights" (line 1602). Simple matter comes before form (line 1604). The picture is your hand. It has the power to limit any motion before it limits one (line 1609). That power is not a real limit. You could move further; you just "do not want to spread more" (lines 1613-1614). So above: "the All-Powerful has the power to limit too" (line 1628). Limit and light have their roots in the Essence "in one weighing and power and one source, which is truly the Infinite" (line 1644). And the limiting vessels are what let everything reach below (line 1649). Bring it home. When you hold back a word or stop your hand, that is a small likeness of this. The hand picture breaks: your limit is a choice inside a power that is itself limited; His comes from a power with no limit at all.

**From inside.** Respect for the ordinary, finite, solid world. The limit starts to look like something God does on purpose, not something that happens to Him.

**At the edge.** Yes. The root of the finite in the Infinite itself (line 1644). The text reaches it and holds both roots there.

**Into the day.** Hold back one sentence today that did not need saying, and notice that the holding back is also strength.

## Chapter 30 (lines 1652-1712)

**The movement.** It carries the unity into the mitzvah. Each commandment is a limb of the King with three vessels: the intention, the love and awe, and the plain deed.

> בחינת כח המעשה, לעשות רצון המלך בלבד
> *the power of the deed, only to do the King's will.*

**Contemplating it.** The Zohar says that without His light the vessels remain "like a body without a soul" (lines 1652-1653). Yet the vessels have a root of their own, so they are real (line 1656). So one who meditates on the Name in the Amidah should aim at the source of the vessel, and the light follows (lines 1657-1658). Every vessel has three levels (lines 1659-1672): thought, speech and deed. The mind of a kind act lives in thought, its love in speech, and its power to act in the hands. The mitzvot are "the 248 limbs of the King" (line 1698). The light of intention has the inner vessel; "love, awe and bitul" the middle one; and "the power of the deed, only to do the King's will", the outer one (lines 1700-1703). Through every mitzvah a revealed Godly light comes down (line 1706). Exile is like sleep, when only "a trace of life" remains (lines 1688, 1693), like a child in the womb with its head folded between its knees (line 1696). Bring it home. Pick up the thing you will use for your next good deed: a coin for charity, a candle, a book. The body picture breaks: a body can be alive without doing anything; this limb lives by doing the King's will.

**From inside.** Plainness. The deed becomes enough, and then more than enough: a limb of something alive.

**At the edge.** It touches it: the light in every mitzvah comes through the line from "the source of all kindness", "You fill all names" (line 1707).

**Into the day.** Do one mitzvah or good deed today "only to do the King's will", without adding a reason.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 31 (lines 1713-1766)

**The movement.** It finds the meeting of opposites not only above but low down, in the doing. It brings the lofty unity to the practical decision.

> ונמצא שמצד בחינת חיצוניות הכלי, שהוא למעשה, שם הוא עיקר בחינת ההתכללות
> *So it is on the side of the outer vessel, the one that is for doing, that the main joining of opposites happens.*

**Contemplating it.** The vessels have an advantage over the lights: in them a thing and its opposite can be included (lines 1713-1720). The light of kindness can shine in a vessel of severity (line 1716). The body shows it. Weak sight in the eye is healed through the nerves in the foot (line 1723). The heart can hold its own opposite: a person can "be angry in his heart at one he loves, and pity in his heart one he hates" (line 1732). "In gladness there is trembling" (line 1734). Each day of creation had one trait, yet each includes the others, because "when the light comes into a vessel called a day", opposites join (lines 1735-1737). A ruling for practice has to include kindness in judgment (lines 1740-1741). Miracles happen in the outer vessel, "above nature within nature", like the sea turned to dry land (lines 1756-1758). Division is the condition of union (lines 1761-1762), and the "Adam of repair" is formed "in a body of matter specifically" (line 1763). Bring it home. The hardest decision you face this week is where your kindness and your firmness have to meet. The ruling picture breaks: a judge weighs from outside; here the joining is the vessel's own nature.

**From inside.** Practical wholeness. The quarrel between being soft and being strong is settled, not in theory, but in the act.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: the outer vessel comes from "the limiting power in the surrounding light" (line 1754), which is why a miracle can live there.

**Into the day.** Make one decision today that holds both kindness and firmness, and carry it out.

**Ladder.** L14 (The unity in your hand)

## Chapter 32 (lines 1767-1816)

**The movement.** It takes the reader into his own heart: the deepest will is felt in the breath of the heart, all at once. From there it looks up at God.

> מתאחד ומתקשר ממקור הרצון העצמי עד הבל הלב ברגע אחד, כאילו הוא מהות אחד ממש
> *it is united and bound from the source of the essential will to the breath of the heart in one moment, as if it were truly one thing.*

**Contemplating it.** "From my flesh I see God" (line 1772): the book reads God through a person. The deepest levels of the soul, chayah and yechidah, are above any vessel: the essential will and pleasure (lines 1773-1775). Yet they shine down through mind and heart (line 1778). To go from mind to heart there must be a contraction first, like a seed that rots before it sprouts (lines 1794-1795). Yet the heart's feelings match the mind's "without a deviation of the tip of a yud" (line 1799). Even in the outermost breath of the heart the will is felt: "the heart's desire" (re'uta deliba), what the heart wants of good and loathes of bad (lines 1807-1808). So it is joined from the source to the heart "in one moment" (line 1809). Above, even the outer vessels of the heart of Zeir Anpin, the divine "heart", are "still counted the end of the world of the Infinite" (lines 1813-1815), "as in a person, whose heart and essential will are one" (line 1816). Bring it home. Think of what you most truly want for good. The seed picture breaks: the seed is lost to become a plant; here nothing is lost from top to bottom.

**From inside.** A wanting that is not a craving: a clean pull toward good, felt in the heart, and the sense that it comes from much deeper than you.

**At the edge.** Yes. The heart's desire is the book's name for the depth that reaches the surface; the text roots it in the essential will of the yechidah (lines 1806-1809).

**Into the day.** Say one sentence to God today from what your heart truly wants for good, not from what you think you should want.

## Chapter 33 (lines 1817-1861)

**The movement.** It names the method in one sentence and gives it two pictures: the child and the elder, the map and the globe. The whole chain becomes one thing seen at two sizes.

> אבל מכל מקום הכל אחד, רק שבא מן בחינת האריכות היותר מופלג אל הקיצור היותר מופלג
> *yet all the same, it is all one; only it came from the very longest to the very shortest.*

**Contemplating it.** Contemplation is gathering "one out of the many details, into one whole that includes them together" (line 1817). All that God desired came down through many levels, "and was done and drawn in one go" from the will hidden in the Essence to what is felt in the breath of the heart (line 1829). "He" is the Essence, "His life" the sefirot, "His causes" the vessels, and "one": all still infinite (lines 1830-1833). The pictures. A small child's traits beside a seventy-year-old's: the smallest and the greatest, yet "equal as a man", the same shape (lines 1836-1838). A map of the globe: nothing missing "even the tip of a yud", only very small, so that a short line on it is a river five hundred parsa long (lines 1847-1849). Bring it home. Look at a small child's face and an old face you love. The same person-shape, at two sizes. The map picture breaks: a map is a copy of the earth; here the small is not a copy but the same thing, come down.

**From inside.** Great and small stop feeling like rivals. The little version of something holy in your life is not a lesser thing.

**At the edge.** It reaches toward it: the one will hidden in the Essence at the head of it all (line 1829). The text calls the whole of it still infinite.

**Into the day.** Picture a small map of your day and say over it: all one, only small.

## Chapter 34 (lines 1862-1911)

**The movement.** It traces how a mind grows until it can rule its feelings. The unity is mostly in the background; this is a map of growing up.

> וכמשל חכם גדול ביותר, שיודע העומק של השכל ממקור הראשון, יודע יותר איך לברר וללבן להוציא הטעות
> *Like a very great sage who knows the depth of an idea from its first source: he knows better how to sort it and make it clear, and take the error out.*

**Contemplating it.** Israel is the one who "strove with God and prevailed" (line 1864): the mind that sorts and rules (line 1865). The picture is a child's growth (lines 1888-1904). Nursing, the limbs grow. At six, some mind enters the feelings. At nine, a feeling can begin from reasons. From thirteen to twenty, the will bends each feeling from above the mind, until he "knows how to sell his father's property", to move things from one domain to another. Before that, a person acts only by the traits already set in him, like animals that are clever at finding food (lines 1894-1895). The greater the sage, the better he knows how to clear away error, and even how to earn his living (lines 1909-1910). Bring it home. Which of your feelings still runs on its own, like a child's? Which have you grown to guide? The growth picture breaks: a child grows up once; this growing of mind over heart happens again in each sitting.

**From inside.** Patience with yourself as someone still growing.

**At the edge.** It does not reach there. The chapter is a map of the mind's growth; the tie to the unity is the book's stated aim, not said here.

**Into the day.** Name one feeling today, and let your understanding, not the feeling, choose your next step.

## Chapter 35 (lines 1912-1970)

**The movement.** It defines truth as what lasts because it holds both sides, and as what is the same at the bottom as at the top. It asks the contemplator to be that kind of person.

> שאין בו שינוי מראשו לסופו, כי כמו שהוא בראש כך הוא בסוף
> *there is no change in it from its head to its end: as it is at the head, so it is at the end.*

**Contemplating it.** Kindness and strictness limit each other; each cuts the other off, so neither alone is truth (lines 1921-1924). Truth is what endures. A lie (kazav) is like a river whose waters fail (line 1925). The middle line includes both, with nothing opposing, "so its waters do not fail" (line 1926). And it is the same from top to bottom (line 1928). The side lines lose their light as they go down; the thigh is cut off from the arm (lines 1933-1942). Emet, truth, is alef, mem, tav, the first, middle and last letters: "as its beginning, so its end, and that is the essence of truth" (line 1959). Mercy depends on knowing: "as the measure of da'at, exactly so the measure of mercy"; one with no da'at is cruel, like a small child (lines 1951-1952). Bring it home. Are you the same person at home and at work, at the start of a task and at its end? The river picture breaks: a river is true by never stopping; a person is true by staying the same when the waters are low.

**From inside.** A wish to be whole: the same inside and out, early and late. And a softening, since understanding more makes a person kinder.

**At the edge.** It does not reach the Essence here; it describes the middle line of the sefirot. It points on: "Jacob did not die", because death has no hold on the middle line (line 1960).

**Into the day.** Finish one thing today in the same spirit in which you began it.

**At the edge.** Yes: the root of giving "above the mind", in the Essence's delight (lines 1988, 1996).

## Chapter 37 (lines 2011-2071)

**The movement.** It locates creation in speech and not in the heart. It makes a person careful, and hopeful, about their own words.

> כי בדבור כבר נעשה, כי הוא אמר ויהי

**Contemplating it.** Thought and speech are the two letters hei of God's Name (lines 2012-2014). The voice is made of fire, water and wind, and so is the heart's breath (lines 2016-2018). When a person is glad the heart opens; when sad it narrows (lines 2020-2021). "If the heart is sad and narrowed, he speaks less; when it is open, he speaks more" (line 2040). In the heart it can still turn from judgment to kindness (line 2045). Bring it home. Before you say something hard today, it is still in your heart, and it can still turn. The picture breaks: your words fade in the air; His word "stands" and keeps the world in being.

## Chapter 38 (lines 2072-2123)

**The movement.** It shows that letters are how one will becomes a thing, level after level. The soul is full of letters from its first moment.

**Contemplating it.** "The soul is full of letters", from the very start of its being (line 2075): letters of thought and letters of speech (line 2076). **At the edge.** It points up: the letters "in the Essence of the Infinite" (line 2117). It describes them no further.

## Chapter 39 (lines 2124-2165)

**The movement.** It holds two opposite truths about one thing without choosing. The words that show you something also hide it, and both are right.

> ונמצא מובן דשניהם אמת בשרש בחינת האותיות … באחדות גמור עם העצם … בבחינת לבושים נפרדים מן העצם לגמרי

**Contemplating it.** As vessels, letters are part of the idea, like the letters of a seal (lines 2126-2127). When an idea is born its letters are born with it (line 2136). As garments, letters are separate and hide the idea: "only the letters show... as if there were nothing outside this form" (line 2147). "None of them reach its essence at all" (line 2150). Thought's garment "does not stay even a moment": it takes off and puts on, thought after thought (line 2157). So both are true (line 2161). The root of letters is in the surrounding lights, the root of both garments and vessels (line 2164). Bring it home. Think of a word you use for God. It brings Him near. It also stands in front of Him. Hold both. The seal picture breaks: a seal's letters are cut once; the soul's letters keep changing clothes.

**From inside.** Freedom from needing the one right word. You can speak, knowing the words are close to it and not it.

**At the edge.** Yes: the idea itself, which none of its letters reach (line 2150). Carried up, the text says, this holds "up to the Essence of the Infinite" (line 2165).

**At the edge.** Close: the "Who" that cannot be asked (line 2195). The chapter is psychology; the tie to the unity comes in chapter 43.

## Chapter 41 (lines 2209-2248)

**The movement.** It separates saying words in your head from thinking what they mean. It holds up a mirror to prayer.

> *The second is true thought ... thinking the meaning of the matter that is in the letters of thought.*

**Contemplating it.** There are three levels of thought: deed in thought, speech in thought, and thought in thought (line 2211). A small child has only pictures, no thinking in letters, "so a child has no thought" (lines 2213-2214). You can think the letters of "Adon Olam", Master of the world, "without thinking at all" about rule and mastery: only the letters (line 2234). That is still "speech in thought". True thought is thinking the meaning, and the letters arrange themselves to fit it (lines 2238-2240). Thought depends on da'at: as deep as the knowing, so deep the thought, "How very deep are Your thoughts" (lines 2247-2248). Bring it home. Say the first line of a prayer you know. Did you think the letters, or the meaning? The child picture breaks: a child grows out of pictures once; a grown person slides back into bare letters every day.

**At the edge.** It doesn't go there. It is about how thinking works, and prepares the ground for chapter 43.

**Into the day.** Say "Master of the world" once today, thinking what a master of the world is.

## Chapter 42 (lines 2249-2281)

**The movement.** It shows that the power which splits an idea into words comes from higher than the idea. What looks like a descent has a high root.

> ואף על פי שלכאורה בחינת ירידה וצמצום הוא, אבל שרשו בא מלמעלה מכח ההשגה שבגוף המושכל הזה
> *And though it seems to be a coming down and a contraction, its root comes from above the power of understanding in the idea itself.*

**Contemplating it.** Where does the power come from that breaks one idea into many combinations of words (line 2249)? From the "five strengths" of understanding: a contraction (lines 2250-2253). It looks like a coming down, but its root is higher (line 2255). The proof: a small child can tell good from bad before he can speak, yet has no words in his thinking until he can talk (lines 2256-2263). A wonder: speech is rooted higher than thought, in the source of wisdom, yet thought comes first (line 2273). The rule: whatever is higher in its root "comes last" in showing (line 2280). Bring it home. The hardest part of an idea is saying it simply. That is not its lowest part; it may be the highest. The child picture breaks: the child's first words are small; the power that forms them reaches above understanding.

**From inside.** Respect for the plain word. The simple explanation stops feeling like a comedown.

**At the edge.** It points to the edge: the power to divide and combine comes from above understanding (line 2264), and it names no more.

**Into the day.** Say one thing you believe in the simplest words you can, to yourself or to someone else.

## Chapter 43 (lines 2282-2321)

**The movement.** It carries the three kinds of thought up into the Essence, and then says the most daring thing in the book: the deeds of people below reach the first stirring of His will.

> מה שנתעורר בחינת חפץ חסד הראשון על ידי מעשה התחתונים … הרי הוא מגיע לתחלת התעוררות הרצון במחשבה העצמית
> *that the first desire for kindness was awakened by the deeds of those below ... reaches the very beginning of the awakening of will in His own thought.*

**Contemplating it.** In the Essence's own thought, "deed in thought" is "the end of the deed, first in thought" (line 2284). The Mitteler Rebbe adds: not as the Rambam puts it, that knowing Himself He knows all. He knows the worlds as one pictures a separate thing, "though there is nothing outside" (lines 2285-2286). "For He said", in His own thought, "and it was", through the first contraction (line 2289). Then the parenthesis: the first desire for kindness was awakened by the deeds of those below, so their deeds reach the very first stirring (line 2294). At the Shema, the large dalet of Echad is drawn out, because it is the root of the kingship in the four directions, the lower unity of "Blessed be the name" (lines 2309-2316). Above it is the simple unity, "hidden of all hidden, which no thought grasps" (line 2317). Humility is the heel and awe the crown: the hidden thought becomes the crown of speech, and if the thought stops, the speech stops (lines 2318-2320). Bring it home. The small good thing you do today: the text says deeds like it reach where His will first stirs. The picture of a person's thought breaks: what we picture as separate really is outside us; before Him "there is nothing outside".

**From inside.** Your life matters upward. You are held, and what you do also reaches back.

**At the edge.** Yes. The simple unity, "hidden of all hidden", which no thought grasps, not even the first (line 2317).

**Into the day.** For a Jew: in the Shema, draw out the dalet of Echad and think of the four directions. For anyone: do one small good act and know that it reaches Him.

## Chapter 44 (lines 2322-2361)

**The movement.** It turns a common belief over: the most solid, fixed form of an idea comes from higher than its flowing inner light. So too the world.

> ואמנם שרש הקרח הזה שבא דוקא בבחינת הגשמה גמורה, הוא מלמעלה מבחינת אור פנימי דבינה
> *Yet the root of this ice, which comes precisely as full solidity, is above the inner light of understanding.*

**Contemplating it.** The name Elokim is contraction and withdrawal (line 2325), but the root of its power is above the understanding it contracts (line 2332). The surrounding light is like knowing: when you know a thing, your knowing surrounds it on every side (line 2347). The text cites the Tanya: God's knowing of all creatures surrounds them (line 2348). The picture is water and ice (lines 2351-2355). An idea not yet grasped is "running water", able to take many forms. Once grasped in a fixed way, it is ice. And the root of the ice is higher than the inner light. Another picture, about love. A love that depends on something fades when the thing is gone, yet it stays around a person, hidden, and can wake again (lines 2343-2345). Bring it home. The solid things of your day, the chair, the bread, the rules you keep, are not the lowest; their root is high. The ice picture breaks: ice melts back into water; here the solid form stays and still points higher.

**From inside.** New respect for what is solid and settled, and hope for faded loves.

**At the edge.** Yes: the surrounding light, above the inner light, as the root of the solid (line 2356).

**Into the day.** Put your hand on one solid thing you rely on today and thank God for it as coming from high.

**Ladder.** L04 (Held from inside and all around)

## Chapter 45 (lines 2362-2401)

**The movement.** It explains the breaking of the vessels as a word too full to hold its meaning, and then states a rule: the lowest telling holds the deepest source.

> כל שבא בהשגה והסבר בהגשמה ביותר למטה מטה, שם דוקא בחינת העומק והמקור של גוף פנימית אור השכל
> *whatever comes into grasp and explanation in the most solid way, lower and lower, there precisely is the depth and the source of the inner light of the idea.*

**Contemplating it.** The breaking: the vessels could not hold so much light (line 2368). Like a very deep idea a mind cannot hold, so its letters get jumbled (line 2371). Like a word that falls apart: "vayomer", "and he said", loses its meaning and its letters scatter (lines 2374-2375). Like dreams: the day's thoughts come back at night in scattered letters, a letter from this word and a letter from that (lines 2376-2377). Yet no one dreams an elephant through the eye of a needle: dreams are still made of what you thought (line 2382). The scattered letters become the "garments of skin" (lines 2385-2386). Then the rule, from parables. The root of a parable is above what it explains; Solomon's proverbs are "very deep wisdom" (line 2389). The last parable in a chain holds the first depth (line 2395). Whatever comes lowest holds the depth and source (line 2397). Bring it home. Your dreams, your jumbled thoughts, your plainest explanations: the text says the deep things show there. The dream picture breaks: dreams "speak falsehood" (line 2378); a parable is full.

**From inside.** Kindness toward what is low and confused in yourself. Even there, the source can show.

**At the edge.** It points there: the depth and "the source" of the idea (line 2397), above the idea itself.

**Into the day.** Tell one idea of Torah today as a simple story or picture.

## Chapter 46 (lines 2402-2444)

**The movement.** It separates two kinds of shine: a light that looks bright only because of the dark around it, and a light bright from itself. It holds out a hope higher than contrast.

> וכאשר היש הוא בבחינת אין, הרי הוא יותר נעלה מבחינת אין דמ"ה שבחכמה המברר
> *And when the something is nothing, it is higher than the nothing of wisdom that refines it.*

**Contemplating it.** Joseph interprets dreams: exile is like a dream, and he makes good, ordered combinations out of its scattered letters (lines 2404-2407). A candle at night looks brighter than by day, but "this is not true brightness from its own side", only more visible to the eye because of the dark (lines 2411-2414). A jewel set in coarse matter shines brighter (line 2415). Nogah, "glow", has two meanings: light, and sticking out. Like thinking an idea through in words, where its depth shows more (lines 2417-2426). It is "the something that is felt and sticks out", like felt Godly fervour (lines 2429-2430). After repair, the felt something turns into real nothing, and then it is higher than the nothing that refined it (lines 2436-2437). Then the garment is itself made of light, like clear glass, and it shines from itself (lines 2441-2443). Bring it home. The goodness you are proud of shines partly by contrast. The text hopes for more: that it stop being felt as yours, and shine from itself. The candle picture breaks: a candle needs the night; the garment of light does not.

**From inside.** Less need to be seen. The good stops needing the dark to show it off.

**At the edge.** Yes: the "something" become "nothing", higher than the nothing (line 2437). This is the book's form of darkness turned to light.

**Into the day.** Do one good thing today that no one will see, not even by contrast.

**Ladder.** L12 (Darkness turned to light)

## Chapter 47 (lines 2445-2483)

**The movement.** It builds kingship, the power of speech that makes things real, and shows it holds nothing of its own. It teaches receiving.

> דהמלכות לית לה מגרמה כלום, רק מה שמקבלת מזעיר אנפין
> *Kingship has nothing of its own, only what it receives from Zeir Anpin.*

**Contemplating it.** Kingship (malchut) is the world of speech, "the revealed world" (line 2445). In speech each trait becomes a thing "separate, on its own" (line 2459). At first it is only a point beneath the foundation; later it is built into a full figure, "like the waning and filling of the moon" (lines 2466-2468). Its root is in kindness, "His throne is established in kindness" (line 2478), and it has nothing of its own, only what it receives, like the moon from the sun (lines 2478-2479). In the future "the light of the moon" will be like the sun's (line 2480). Bring it home. The things you say and do that make a difference are real because you received something first. The moon picture breaks: a moon only reflects; kingship speaks with authority, and in the end will shine like the sun.

**From inside.** Lightness about your own authority, and dignity in receiving.

**At the edge.** It does not reach the Essence here. The next chapter does, with its question about the king.

**Into the day.** Before you speak with authority today, as a parent, a boss or a teacher, remember what you received in order to have it.

## Chapter 48 (lines 2484-2509)

**The movement.** It asks why the contraction was made at all, and answers with a king who needs a people. It makes the reader's own existence part of the answer.

> ולזה היה הצמצום הראשון הנקרא מקום פנוי כו', כדי שיהיה דבר חוץ מן העצמות
> *For this the first contraction, called the empty space, came about: so that there would be something outside the Essence.*

**Contemplating it.** In a person, most traits flow from the self: kindness can well up even with no one there. But exaltation needs someone to be exalted over (lines 2492-2495). Its size depends on how much the people give themselves over (line 2496). Even a small child wants to rule over animals (lines 2500-2501). "There is no king without a people" (line 2498). Kingship alone "is not really drawn from the Essence" (line 2499). So the question: "there is nothing outside Him; over whom would He be exalted?" (line 2502). The answer: the first contraction was made so that there would be something outside the Essence (line 2506). Every Rosh Hashanah the kingship is renewed from its source, "I will reign" (line 2505). Bring it home. You are part of the "something outside" that the whole contraction was for. The king picture breaks: a human king needs his people to be great; God needs nothing, and chose to be King.

**From inside.** Being wanted. Your separateness, the fact that you are you, is not a mistake to erase. It is room made on purpose.

**At the edge.** Yes: "I will reign", in His Essence, "before there was a contraction" (line 2505).

**Into the day.** For a Jew: say one verse of kingship today, "God is King". For anyone: accept Him as King in one small choice.

## Chapter 49 (lines 2510-2546)

**The movement.** It brings the book's teaching of creation into this hour: renewed every day and every hour, through speech, from the first source.

> עד שבכל עת ושעה מתחדש אור ושפע ממקור הראשון
> *until at every time and hour light and flow are renewed from the first source.*

**Contemplating it.** Kingship has wisdom too: "the lower wisdom (Solomon's wisdom)", "wisdom at the end, whose root is wisdom at the head" (lines 2512-2513). The rabbis' picture of a king's day: three hours of Torah, three hours of judging, three hours of feeding the world (line 2516). He renews each day the work of creation (line 2518), until at every hour light is renewed from the source, the simple delight and will in the kingship of the Infinite (line 2519). Sometimes kingship is small, a point, "I am black" (line 2524). On Yom Kippur it rises to the inner Ancient One (line 2525). Bring it home. This hour is new, not left over from yesterday. The king's-day picture breaks: a king's day is divided into hours; His renewal is in every hour at once.

**From inside.** Freshness. The day feels less like a rerun.

**At the edge.** It points to the source, "the simple delight and will in the kingship of the Infinite" (line 2519), and goes no further.

**Into the day.** At the start of one hour today, say: this hour is new from the source.

## Chapter 50 (lines 2547-2578)

**The movement.** It shows nature as letters combined, and so as open to the One who combines them. It ends in a contemplation that sees vinegar burn.

> היינו מי שמצרף בי' מאמרות דשם אלהי״ם לשמן הטבעה זו, הוא יאמר צירוף אופן הטבעה זו לחומץ וידליק
> *that is, the One who combines, in the Ten Sayings of the name Elokim, this nature for oil, He will say this combination for vinegar, and it will burn.*

**Contemplating it.** Speech comes from five places in the mouth, and their letters join in many ways (lines 2547-2550). Speech has matter and form: the voice is one, and it joins all the combinations (line 2552). The text's picture: "your neck with strings of beads", one thread through many stones (line 2553). So one general thought runs through all the words (line 2554). A child who speaks knows at once how to join letters, with no study (line 2561). That power comes from wisdom, above understanding (line 2562). Rabbi Chanina ben Dosa said, "He who told the oil to burn will tell the vinegar to burn": the One who combined oil's nature can combine vinegar's (line 2574). All of it gathers into one contemplation, from the source of will to the last deed, "as the vinegar burns in very deed" (line 2578). Bring it home. Look at one "fixed" thing in your life. Its nature is words, spoken now. The bead picture breaks: a thread only holds the beads; this voice makes them.

**From inside.** Nature feels less closed. Not magic: just not the last word.

**At the edge.** Yes: the source of will in the kingship of the Infinite (line 2578), from which even nature's combinations are spoken.

**Into the day.** When something seems impossible today, say: He who told oil to burn can tell vinegar.

## Chapter 51 (lines 2579-2614)

**The movement.** It places the screen between the worlds and the two unities of the Shema on either side of it. It lets the contemplator stand with the tribes: the screen is real for us, and our heart still holds only One.

> כשם שאין בלבך אלא אחד בבחינת יחודא עילאה דאצילות … כך אין בלבנו, בבחינת מלכות דאצילות בבריאה, אלא אחד יחודא תתאה
> *just as in your heart there is only One, in the higher unity of Atzilut ... so in our heart, in the kingship of Atzilut within Beriah, there is only One, the lower unity.*

**Contemplating it.** Only "a radiance of a radiance" passes the screen; the light's essence stays above (line 2581). Like a seal pressed in wax: only a trace of the letter stays (line 2582). "First darkness, then light" (line 2584). The screen is made of letters out of order, like a heap of stones. Ordered letters let the light of an idea through; disordered ones block it (lines 2588-2591). The first contraction was not a real screen, "because all is still His Essence"; this one is a real break (lines 2596-2598). Yet the one who contracts made it, like one who makes a parable, so it is a break "only toward the receivers" (line 2599), like the snail whose shell is part of itself (line 2600). Then the Shema. The tribes said to Jacob: as in your heart there is only One, so in ours (line 2605). "Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom" is the lower unity, on our side of the screen (line 2602). Bring it home. You live below the screen. The world is really there, and it really hides Him. Your heart can still hold only One. The snail picture breaks: the snail hides for its own sake; He hides for ours.

**From inside.** Two kinds of oneness become livable: His, above, and ours, here. Neither cancels the other.

**At the edge.** It holds the edge: the higher unity, where "He, His life and His causes are one" (line 2605), above the screen.

**Into the day.** For a Jew: say "Blessed be the name..." slowly today, as the unity on our side. For anyone: say, "In my heart, only One".

## Chapter 52 (lines 2615-2645)

**The movement.** It walks the last stretch of the map: the created worlds, the angels, the heavens, down to the star over each plant. It brings the contemplator to the grass under his feet.

> על דרך שאמרו אין לך עשב שאין לו מזל
> *as they said: there is no blade of grass that has no star (mazal) over it.*

**Contemplating it.** From the lowest point of the highest world comes the will to create; then its wisdom and understanding; then its feelings; then the created world of souls and angels (lines 2615-2620). A picture: Rabbi Yishmael the High Priest in the Holy of Holies. God says to him, "Yishmael My son, bless Me", and he answers, "May it be Your will" (line 2617). Each lower world comes again through a screen: "for My glory, I created it, I formed it, I also made it" (line 2625). From the lowest level of the angels come the constellations, the roots of stone, plant, animal and speaker in every climate (line 2643), down to the star of a stone: "there is no blade of grass without its star" (line 2644). Over the stars rules the last kingship of our world (line 2645). Bring it home. Look at one weed on the path. The map comes all the way down to it. The star picture breaks: the sages spoke of a star over each thing; the book pictures a chain from God's first will, and the star is only its last link.

**From inside.** Nothing is left out. The small, the ugly and the ignored have their line to Him.

**At the edge.** It does not go past the map here. The next chapter gathers it.

**Into the day.** Stop once today at one plant and say: this too has its line from Him.

## Chapter 53 (lines 2646-2672)

**The movement.** It gathers the whole book into one moment and one aim: everything included in the simple unity, until it is fixed in the soul, down to the smallest detail of providence.

> כי שרש האצילות ושרש העשיה שוין ממש לגבי עצמותו
> *for the root of Atzilut, the highest world, and the root of Asiyah, ours, are exactly equal before His Essence.*

**Contemplating it.** As He desired to emanate, so He desired to create, form and make, all included in His simple will (line 2647). Like a person whose whole will "arose all at once" before it divided into steps (line 2648). So before Him there is no difference of level between the will for the highest world and the will for ours (line 2650): "I am first" and "I am last" (line 2651). The roots are exactly equal (line 2653), heaven and earth, surrounding and filling, "all in one equality" (line 2654). The purpose: "only that all be included in the simple unity... until it is fixed in the soul, also in the order of divine providence in particulars, down to the very last detail" (line 2657). "He arranges the stars in their watches... according to His will" (line 2659). Everything below is in bitul. The hosts bow (line 2662). Even the seventy powers of the nations are "like a horse to its rider" (line 2664). Balaam said, "I cannot go beyond the word of God" (line 2667). The spheres turn because they sing (line 2672). Bring it home. What happened to you today, planned or not, is inside that simple will. The picture of a person's will arising all at once breaks: a person's will can fail on the way down; here nothing is lost.

**From inside.** A trust that does not need to explain everything. The details of the day stop feeling scattered.

**At the edge.** Yes. The simple unity of the Essence, before which the highest and the lowest are equal. The book's ascent ends here.

**Into the day.** Tonight, look back at one thing that happened today and say: this too, within His will.

## Chapter 54 (lines 2673-2711)

**The movement.** It turns the lamp on the contemplator. After the whole ascent the book ends with where the other side begins: in a good thing that feels itself.

> וכמו שמהתלהבות הבולט בהרגשת עצמו בתפלה, יכעס על המנגדו להכותו ולרודפו כשנוגע בכבודו
> *as from a fervour that sticks out, in feeling oneself in prayer, a person grows angry at whoever opposes him, to strike and pursue him, when his honour is touched.*

**Contemplating it.** The husk (kelipah) comes before the fruit, as the peel grows first (line 2675). Oneg, pleasure, and nega, affliction, are the same letters (line 2676). The law: "whatever extends out from the essence surely has an opposing power, which also comes from the essence" (line 2678). Like a servant who crosses his master's will in every detail of it (line 2681). The glow (nogah) of the highest world has no evil at all. It is only the something that is felt and sticks out: a person who feels his own kindness, his own wisdom (lines 2699-2700). From there, branching down, comes kindness done only for pride (line 2701). The example is from worship: fervour in prayer that feels itself turns to anger when someone touches your honour (line 2703). The Satan and Peninah "meant it for the sake of heaven" (line 2704). Bring it home. Remember a time you felt proud of your own devotion, and how quickly it turned sharp. The husk picture breaks toward hope: a husk is thrown away, but the book says the glow can be refined, and even the worst waits for "the future" (line 2711).

**From inside.** A gentle watchfulness, not suspicion. The good in you does not need to be felt as yours.

**At the edge.** It shows the edge from the other side: the self that feels itself as separate. Past it the text says only that the wholly impure "have no refinement yet at all, only in the future" (line 2711).

**Into the day.** After a moment of real devotion today, do one small kindness for someone who annoyed you.

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations

1. **Chapter 7, line 332:** שבזה ממש שורה בבחינת אין סוף דוקא מבחינת תחלת הכל. *The book's signature: the whole chain folded into one moment, and the Infinite found in the plainest physical thing.* Learn one rung of the chain well, then look up at the sky and let the long become short, "in one moment".

2. **Chapter 53, line 2653:** כי שרש האצילות ושרש העשיה שוין ממש לגבי עצמותו. *The summary of the book: before Him, the highest world and ours have exactly the same root.* Hold one ordinary event of today next to the loftiest thing you know of, and let them be equal before Him.

3. **Chapter 10, line 493:** בחינת יחיד משמעו לבדו ממש … והוא למעלה מהתאחדות נפרדים שנקרא אחד. *God as He is: "alone", above even "one", before any world or part.* At the Shema's Echad, hold for a moment that He is also alone; for someone unsure, say "You alone" slowly.

4. **Chapter 5, line 199:** ולפי ערך השמחה כך ערך הביטול ממש. *The book's test for every sitting: joy at what is grasped and bitul before what is not, in exact proportion.* After any contemplation, ask what you understood and what you saw you cannot understand; if one is missing, go back.

5. **Chapter 32, line 1809:** מתאחד ומתקשר ממקור הרצון העצמי עד הבל הלב ברגע אחד, כאילו הוא מהות אחד ממש. *The soul's deepest will reaches the breath of the heart in one moment, and the book reads God through it.* Say one sentence to God from what your heart truly wants for good, and let it be felt as coming from far below you.

6. **Chapter 12, line 662:** ולא שנסתלק ונתעלם לגמרי … רק כמו השונה בדרך קצרה. *The contraction is not a leaving: the Essence is fully there, like the whole Talmud inside one short Mishnah.* When the world feels empty of God, say "not withdrawn, said briefly", and look for the whole in the short.

7. **Chapter 22, line 1264:** אך בבעלי תשובה אנו רואים שנהפך גם עצם הרע לטוב, וכן זדונות עצמן נעשו כזכיות … הרי הוא מצד מסירת נפש בעצמות אור אין סוף, שלמעלה גם מהתחלקות עצם החסד וגבורה. *Even essential evil turns to good, only through self-giving that reaches the Essence above good and evil.* Give one honest sentence of return to God without arguing your case; not on a crushed day.

8. **Chapter 26, line 1481:** והיינו במסירת נפש דקריאת שמע, מה שאין כן בתורה ומצות בלא מסירת נפש אין היחוד רק בחיצוניות. *How deep a union goes depends on how much of the self is given: without self-giving, only on the outside.* Before the Shema's Echad, or before one good act, say "all of me" in your heart.

9. **Chapter 48, line 2506:** ולזה היה הצמצום הראשון הנקרא מקום פנוי כו', כדי שיהיה דבר חוץ מן העצמות. *The first contraction was made so that there would be something outside Him: you, as room made on purpose.* Hold your own separateness not as a mistake but as the space He made to be King over, and accept Him in one choice.

10. **Chapter 54, line 2703:** וכמו שמהתלהבות הבולט בהרגשת עצמו בתפלה, יכעס על המנגדו להכותו ולרודפו כשנוגע בכבודו. *The book's closing warning: the first step toward evil is a good thing that feels itself, even fervour in prayer.* After a moment of real devotion, do one small kindness for someone who annoyed you.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

*Compared with the Chabad method as mapped in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md`, and with the other books in this index. The differences are kept, not smoothed.*

**Detail first, then the whole.** The hisbonenus ontology lists breadth and worked analogy among its techniques and treats "absolute unity" as one object among several. The Gate of Unity makes the order a rule. The whole, contemplated alone, can deceive, "from afar God appears to him" (line 124); it is really fixed in the soul only after rung-by-rung detail (lines 125-139), and the detail then folds back into the whole "in one moment" (line 330). It still lets beginners start with the general (line 160). The Tanya's chapter 33 asks a person to picture the whole at once; this book asks him to earn it.

**A test of proportion, inside the sitting.** The ontology's diagnostic layer sorts states by their signs, and it gives the Rebbe Rashab's test afterwards: has one's dominant trait changed? This book puts its test inside the contemplation: joy at what is grasped and bitul before what is not, "exactly" equal (line 199). Either alone is self-deception, "an empty fantasy" (lines 201-202). The manual's test ("a little quieter, a little less full of myself") is gentler. The book's is its own, and it is stricter.

**Tears as the vessel.** The ontology treats sadness and bitterness as an obstacle: take the resolution if it sharpens, stop if it flattens. The Gate of Unity makes weeping at midnight "the main preparation" for joy in prayer (line 279), and calls joy and weeping one power in two equal lines (line 228). The tears it means are over the distance of God's light and the casting off of the yoke (lines 271-274), not over the self. The index keeps the manual's guard: not on a crushed day, and stop if it flattens.

**The object is the chain of worlds, not the self.** Likkutei Amarim walks the unity through the person: two souls, the heart, the garments of thought, speech and deed. Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, by the same author, walks it through the states of arousal. The Gate of Unity makes the map itself the object, from before the contraction to the blade of grass. The self appears mostly as the analogy by which God is explained ("from my flesh I see God", line 1772), and, at the very end, as the place where the other side begins (chapter 54).

**To know, not only to believe.** The Tanya's Shaar HaYichud says that no change in Him is held "only by faith". The Gate of Unity argues it through the contraction, and says "every person must know the tzimtzum and contemplate it" (lines 444, 451). Where the Tanya quotes "we have no business with hidden things", this book tells everyone to contemplate the Essence before the contraction, "there too in detail" (line 489). What is above the mind still appears, but inside the contemplation, as a flash seen "as if by the eye's own sight" (line 171) and as bitul, rather than as a step outside it into faith.

**Aimed at the physical, through analogy, without scenes or song.** The Piaseczner pictures scenes "as if before your eyes" and uses melody and the body. The Gate of Unity forbids none of this and uses none of it. Its pictures are analogies from the soul, each one marked where it fails (for example lines 738, 1058). Its aim is the plain physical world: the Psalms praise God by sky and earth and "not by the upper worlds" (lines 333-335), and whatever comes lowest holds the deepest source (line 2397).

**Felt fervour is suspect.** The ontology records that the Mitteler Rebbe, in Kuntres HaHitpa'alut, names Godly arousal as the true aim of contemplation and warns that arousal without a prior "decay" of the self coarsens. Here that warning becomes a teaching about where evil begins: felt fervour in prayer, "sticking out in feeling oneself", is the first branch of the glow (nogah) toward evil (lines 2699-2703). It stands against any teaching that treats the feeling itself as proof, and it is kept as the book states it.

**A higher bar, said openly.** The Tanya puts a basic love of God within anyone's reach. This book says many raised on Chassidus deceive themselves (line 205), that joy and bitul fall away "at a little opposition" (line 212), and it asks for Tikkun Chatzot (line 279) and for self-giving at the Shema (line 1481). The reading does not soften this. The manual's gentler doors stay open for the days that need them.
