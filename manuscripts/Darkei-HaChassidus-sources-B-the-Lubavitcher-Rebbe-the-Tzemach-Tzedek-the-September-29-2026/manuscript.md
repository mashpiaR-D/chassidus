## 1a. What Chassidus is *for*: changing the nature of the middos

**M1. The whole point of Chassidus is to change the *nature* of one's traits, not only the traits.** The Alter Rebbe's definition, which the Lubavitcher Rebbe quotes and sharpens: it is not enough to change "his natural middos"; what must change is "the nature of his middos". · *«כל ענין החסידות הוא לשנות טבע מדותיו»; «לא רק לשנות מדותיו הטבעים אלא גם – "טבע מדותיו"».* · Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus §1, IS l.7. The same saying in the Rayatz's telling (the Tzemach Tzedek's question to the Alter Rebbe in yechidus), with the consequence drawn: Chassidus that does not reach actual avodah has gone wrong. «ואין הדבריס משפיעיס על עבודה בפועל … דבר הנעשה רק על ידי עבודה פנימית יוס יומית בכל שלושת לבושי הנפש» (Likkutei Dibburim vol. 1 p. 82, LDH l.574, **OCR**).

**M2. Knowledge, however deep, has to "come into avodah". Avodah means refining the middos and inner bonding.** This is Hayom Yom's own definition of the word. · *«כל ענין ידיעה והשגה אפילו בענינים היותר עמוקים, צריך לבוא בעבודה, היינו פעולת דבר בבירור וזיכוך המדות והתקשרות פנימית».* · Hayom Yom 6 Teves, HY l.34.

**M3. The exile of Egypt, read inwardly, is the animal soul confining the G-dly soul. The exodus is when the mind's understanding actually lights up the heart as good traits in deed.** This gives a test for integration: the idea is integrated when it shows in actual middos. · *«והיינו דהשכל שבמוח מאיר בלב במדות טובות בפועל ממש».* · Hayom Yom 4 Shevat, HY l.74.

**M4. Intellect and emotion are two worlds, one cold and settled, one boiling and agitated. The person's work is to join them. Then the agitation becomes aspiration and the intellect becomes a guide for a life of doing.** · *«השכל וההתפעלות שני עולמות הם … ואז הבהלה נהפכת לשאיפה, והשכל - למורה דרך בחיים של עבודה ופועל».* · Hayom Yom 12 Shevat, HY l.85.

**M5. The Chabad principle: the mind rules the heart by nature. It must *enslave* the heart to G-d's service through understanding and depth of knowledge of the Creator's greatness.** Chabad opens the gates of Chochmah and Binah, arouses the heart to the feeling that this understanding obliges, and shows each person, at his own level, how to serve with mind and heart. · *«המוח השליט על הלב בתולדתו, ישעבד את הלב לעבודת השי"ת, מתוך השכלה הבנה והעמקת הדעת בגדולת בורא העולם»* (16 Kislev, HY l.605); *«כל אחד ואחד לפום שיעורא דילי' יכול לגשת אל הקדש לעבוד את הוי' במוחו ולבו»* (17 Kislev, HY l.606).

**M6. Faith is to be brought into knowing. "Know" is the mind's obligation even for one who already believes.** · *«חובת המוח והשכל להביא אמונה זו בידיעה והשגה»* · Hayom Yom 19 Shevat, HY l.97.

## 1b. The ladder: learning → contemplation → prayer → the day

**M7. Three kinds of hisbonenus, "rungs of the ladder of feeling".** (1) *Learning* contemplation: after understanding a matter fully, one dwells in its depth until the idea shines. (2) Contemplation *before prayer*: its business is feeling the *vitality* of what was learned, not its intellectual content. (3) Contemplation *within prayer*: feeling the G-dliness in the matter. Sometimes G-dliness is felt with no work, from the soul's essence. By one's own work, all three are required. · *«שלשה מיני התבוננות הם … שקודם התפלה, ענינה הרגש חיות הענין שלמד … שלש אלה הם שליבות סולם ההרגשה».* · Hayom Yom 20 Tammuz, HY l.377.

**M8. Prayer is what carries the mind's grasp into the heart's feeling, and both together into practice: mitzvos in fear of Heaven and acquiring good middos.** · *«עבודת התפלה היא המביאה השגת המוח בהרגשת הלב, ושניהם יחד בעבודה בפועל».* · Hayom Yom 21 Cheshvan, HY l.568.

**M9. The Tzemach Tzedek: before prayer, the understanding that "all is as nothing before Him" is in the mind alone and "does not touch him at all". Through prayer, beginning with contemplation in Shema, it spreads into the body, so that he feels it in the body too.** This is the clearest statement in these sources of the mind-to-body mechanism. · *«כי קודם תפלה הארה זו הוא רק במוחו לבד … אבל מ"מ אינו נוגע כלל לעצמו אבל ע"י התפלה מתפשטת בגוף ג"כ שמרגיש ג"כ בגוף».* · Maamarei Admur HaTzemach Tzedek 5615, ד"ה ולהבין בתוס' ביאור ענין קשה לזווגם, MTZ l.287.

**M10. R. Yitzchak Eizik of Homel: prayer is contemplation in Shema of how G-d gives life to all worlds at every moment. From that grasp the heart is moved to love and fear. Torah and mitzvos are the higher, encompassing level, but they are not complete without prayer first.** · *«ומחמת זה מתפעל באוי"ר בלב מחמת השגה באלקות … אמנם אין בהם שלימות בתו"מ בלא תפלה מקודם».* · Chanah Ariel, Additions to Bereshis 2:1, HO l.76.

**M11. The measure of love and fear is a bodily feeling "in the flesh of the heart", like meeting a faithful friend. Love lifts the burdens and brings inner vitality and hope. Fear brings dread as he recalls his wrong thoughts, words and deeds.** The Rebbe Rashab compares it to tefillin: a fixed place, and one feels its weight. · *«הנה שיעור מצות אלו הוא אשר יורגשו בהרגש גופני בבשר הלב ממש».* · Hayom Yom 20 Menachem Av, HY l.416.

**M12. Three "garments" (thought, speech, deed) are one's own job. Heaven can sanctify "today and tomorrow", but "wash your garments" one must do oneself.** In the Alter Rebbe's words on the eve of Shavuos, explained by the Tzemach Tzedek. Also the Rashab: Chassidus "demands" that its understanding wash the flesh and its habits. The holy garments (Chassidic thought and speech, and contemplation before prayer) come from above; washing the flesh is by one's own strength. · *«וכבסו שמלותם - לבושי מחשבה דבור ומעשה, וואס דאס דארף מען אליין»* (5 Sivan, HY l.308); *«דער ורחץ במים את בשרו דאס דארף מען אליין»* (26 Nissan, HY l.247).

**M13. The order of integration in the three garments.** After Sinai the order is removing the covering of body, tongue and heart (deed, speech, thought). Avraham before Sinai went the other way: first he recognised his Creator (thought), then publicised G-dliness (speech), then circumcision (deed). · *«הסרת ערלת הגוף, הלשון, הלב - מעשה דבור מחשבה»* · Hayom Yom 7 Cheshvan, HY l.551. The Maharash, told to the Rebbe Rashab in yechidus (Selichos 5641): avodah includes prayer and refining middos, and "the beginning of avodah is to enslave the garments of the soul, thought, speech and deed". *«התחלת העבודה איז משעבד זיין די לבושי הנפש מחשבה, דיבור ומעשה»* (Toras Shmuel 5641, supplements, TS l.14786).

**M14. "Live through" what you learn.** The Rashab, citing the Alter Rebbe, read «וקנה לך חבר» as *kulmos* (pen) of the heart: everything one learns should be lived through. · *«אז יעדער זאך וואס מ'לערענט זאל מען איבערלעבען»* · Hayom Yom 14 Menachem Av, HY l.409.

**M15. Why the mind does not reach the middos directly (Lubavitcher Rebbe).** The mind can act on the middos only once they have been *weakened*. While they are at full natural strength the intellect cannot even clothe itself in them. This is the reason for itkafia (restraint) first: it weakens the trait so that understanding can enter. · *«כי אם לאחר שהמדות נחלשו … כאשר המדות הם בחלישות אזי שייך שתהי' בהם פעולת השכל».* · Toras Menachem 5715, Shabbos Shelach, TM l.22897.

**M16. The beinoni (every person) cannot turn his heart. He can *bind* it by itkafia, and must, all day long, or the mind's work is disturbed (Lubavitcher Rebbe on tefillin).** The mind is in one's power and can be "set" permanently (the head tefillin). The heart is not in one's power, so the mitzvah is the act of binding (the hand tefillin): keeping desire from turning into action. · *«אין לבו של אדם (בינוני — שהיא מדת כל אדם) … עליו לקשור את לבו, בדרך אתכפיא, לשלוט על עצמו שלא להוציא תאוותו מן הכח אל הפועל»* (LS l.35476); *«שגם לבו יהי' "קשור" ומשועבד (עכ"פ בדרך אתכפיא) במשך כל היום»* (LS l.35478). Likkutei Sichos vol. 39, Va'eschanan 2.

**M17. After Sinai, the self-nullification reached in the mind can be drawn down and *felt* in the middos, even in practice.** · *«שה"ביטול" שמצד עבודת המוחין יומשך ויורגש גם במדות האדם»* · Likkutei Sichos vol. 35, Toldos 1, LS l.20309.

**M18. Where belief and understanding stay in the head (Lubavitcher Rebbe, a letter in Yiddish).** Everyone believes that all is for the good and even understands it, "but it stays with the person in belief and understanding and, sadly, does not reach the feeling of the heart". So events break him. The remedy is to work on oneself "to bring the belief and understanding down into the feeling of the heart". · *«אז דאס בלייבט ביים מענטשן אין גלויבן און פארשטאנד, און דערגרייכט ליידער ניט צום געפיל אין הארצן»* (IK l.16757); *«אראפצוברענגען די אמונה און פארשטאנד אויך אין געפיל פון הארצן»* (IK l.16758). Igros Kodesh vol. 4 §955.

**M19. The Rayatz: every intellectual matter, even the loftiest, contains a trait (a middah). Da'as is the power to take the lesson "for one's own soul". Without it the idea never moves him.** (**OCR**) · *«כי בכל ענין שכלי יש בו מדה … בלא דעת אינו יודע ליקח מהענין לנפשו».* · Sefer HaMaamarim 5684, SMO l.3137.

**M20. The Rayatz: intellect proves itself when it comes into deed. The *pleasant* sweetness of an idea can satisfy and leave little action. The sense of the idea's *wonder* makes one thirsty and keeps renewing action, in mitzvos and in refining the middos.** (**OCR**) · *«דהתאמתות ההשכלה היא כאשר באה בפועל דבר … יכול להיות שפעולתו בפועל הוא מעט מזעיר».* · Sefer HaMaamarim 5700, SMO l.25229.

**M21. The Rayatz: Torah's inner light changes the middos "in their very essence", which learning alone does not do.** (**OCR**, a letter.) · *«אבל פעולת התורה מהמאור שבה הוא שהמדות משתניס בעצס מהותס».* · Igros Kodesh (Rayatz) vol. 1 p. 340, IKR l.2285.

## 1c. The natural soul itself, and what "a Chassid" is

**M22. The alef-beis of Chassidus is to wear away one's own naturalness in avodah. The first step is to wear away the nature of the powers, e.g. that the mind rules the heart (as the Alter Rebbe taught R. Moshe Meisels).** · *«דער אלף פון חסידות איז, אויסצוניצען די טבעיות אין עבודה».* · Hayom Yom 19 Adar II, HY l.183.

**M23. Chassidus reaches down to overturning the nature of the animal soul, and further, to one's portion in the world. The lower it reaches, the more its essence shows. Hence the Rayatz's insistence that "everything learned in Chassidus must be brought into practice, for only through actual avodah does one grasp the essence of Chassidus".** · *«עד להפיכת טבע נפש הבהמית»* (IS l.106); *«שכל ענין שלומדים בחסידות צריך להביאו בפועל, כי ע"י העבודה בפועל דוקא תופסים במהות החסידות»* (IS l.107). Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus §18.

**M24. "When one and another speak of avodah and learn together, it is two G-dly souls against one natural soul"** (the Mitteler Rebbe in yechidus). · *«צוויי נפש אלקית אויף איין נפש הטבעית»* · Hayom Yom 20 Teves, HY l.56.

**M25. Your particular avodah is found in your own bad leanings. Every soul has its own work in mind and middos by its nature. From the "enemies", the bad tendencies it feels in its natural traits, it learns how to repair them.** · *«כל נשמה יש לה עבודה פרטית במוחין ומדות כפי טבעה וענינה … מההטיות הרעות שמרגיש במדותיו הטבעיות».* · Hayom Yom 8 Nissan, HY l.212.

**M26. What "a Chassid" is in Chabad: one who knows his own essential standing in Torah knowledge and in keeping mitzvos, knows what he lacks, sets his heart to fill it, and increases discipline by kabbalas ol.** · *«יודע הוא מה שחסר לו ונותן לב ודואג למלאות את החסר».* · Hayom Yom 21 Adar I, HY l.144. Also the Rashab's three levels of "chassid" (14 Kislev): the first, "that the matters of the world do not preoccupy and confuse him", is one that *everyone can reach and everyone is obliged to reach*. *«שעניני העולם אינם מטרידים ומבלבלים אותו. כאו"א יכול להגיע לזה וכאו"א מחוייב להגיע לזה»* (HY l.602).

**M27. Chassidus is vitality: bringing life into everything and lighting it up, including the not-good, so that one knows one's own bad as it is in order to repair it** (the Tzemach Tzedek's answer to the Maharash). · *«חסידות איז חיות … מען זאל וויסען פון אייגענעם רע ווי ער איז, אויף מתקן זיין עס».* · Hayom Yom 24 Teves, HY l.62. Also: *«חסידות משנה את המציאות ומגלה את המהות»* (the Rashab, 13 Kislev, HY l.600).

**M28. Kabbalas ol changes one's essence.** The Tzemach Tzedek told a learned chassid, expert in Shas and a great maskil in Chassidus, that through the kabbalas ol of a simple servant, whose yoke shows even in his sleep, even a gaon can reach the level of a simple, wholehearted Jew of mesirus nefesh. · *«קבלת עול איז משנה דעם מהות».* · Hayom Yom 3 Adar II, HY l.159.

**M29. A Chassid is his Rebbe's emissary. When he acts on that, he is bound to him in everything: a chassid walks, eats and sleeps.** · *«עם געהט א חסיד, עסט א חסיד, שלאפט א חסיד».* · Hayom Yom 8 Iyar, HY l.264.

## 2a. The frame of the day

**P1. Modeh Ani (on waking).** The day begins with Modeh Ani, said before washing, even with impure hands, because "all the impurities in the world cannot make a Jew's Modeh Ani impure". He may be lacking here or there, but his Modeh Ani stays whole. The Rashab as a child: the dot after «בחמלה» in Modeh Ani must be spread over the whole day. · *«דער מודה אני בלייבט גאנץ»* (11 Shevat, HY l.84); *«כל הטומאות שבעולם אינן מטמאות את ה"מודה אני" של יהודי»* (IS l.63); *«צריכים לפושטה»* (IS l.54). *For anyone:* a morning sentence of thanks that no failure can spoil.

**P2. Learn Chassidus before davening, and again before Maariv. Learn nigleh after davening.** The Lubavitcher Rebbe's schedule (Shabbos Shuvah 5721): Chassidus in the morning before prayer and at night before Maariv; the revealed Torah after prayer, "from the shul to the study hall". · *«זמן לימוד החסידות – בבוקר קודם התפלה ובלילה קודם תפלת ערבית»* · TM l.48958. The Rayatz: an old Chassidic saying, "the understanding of Chassidus one gets from learning Chassidus; the *feeling* in avodah one gets when one davens with what one learned". *«דעם פאַרשטאַנד פון חסידות באַקומט מען אין לערנען חסידות. דעם געפיל אין עבודה באַקומט מען ווען מען דאַווענט מיט דעם וואָס מען האָט געלערנט»* (Sukkos 5706, SS l.6119).

**P3. The four elements of Chassidic avodah in prayer (the Rayatz, Pesach 5708): (a) learning Chassidus before davening, (b) preparation for davening, (c) contemplation within prayer, (d) the meaning of the words. "But above all, one must actually say the words with life."** · *«א) דער לימוד החסידות פאַר'ן דאַוונען, ב) הכנה צו דעם דאַוונען, ג) די התבוננות בתוך התפלה, ד) דער פירוש המלות»; «אַז מען דאַרף טאַקע זאָגן די ווערטער מיט אַ חיות»* · SS l.7956.

**P4. Graded minimum for prayer (Lubavitcher Rebbe, 18 Elul 5711, to yeshivah students): lengthy prayer for those it suits; *at least* contemplation before prayer; *or at least* praying word by word without rushing.** This is the clearest graded "floor" in these sources. · *«ולכל הפחות התבוננות קודם התפלה, או לכל הפחות תפלה מלה במלה (מבלי לחטוף)»* · TM l.6891. *For anyone:* the lowest rung, praying each word without grabbing at it, is open to everyone.

**P5. Lengthy study before davening is defended as medicine.** People complain that chassidim learn and contemplate so long before prayer that they delay Shema. The Rebbe answers that for the healthy it would be harmful, but for the sick soul there is no other way, "for otherwise he will pray only with his lips and not his heart". · *«יש מתאוננים על החסידים שמאריכים בלמוד החסידות קודם התפלה»* (IK l.10248); *«כי באם לאו יתפלל רק בשפתיו ולא בלבו»* (IK l.10249). Igros Kodesh vol. 3 §505.

**P6. Before prayer: take on ahavas Yisrael, and give tzedakah.** The Alter Rebbe's siddur: before praying, say "I take upon myself the mitzvah of love your fellow as yourself". Love of Israel is "the gate of entry" to stand before G-d. The Rashab: giving to a poor man before prayer, and so giving him life, adds much life to the prayer. The Rashab would look for a poor man to feed before davening. · *«מצות אהבת ישראל היא שער הכניסה»* (2 Tammuz, HY l.350); *«וע"י שנותנים צדקה לעני קודם התפלה ומחיים אותו, ניתוסף חיות הרבה בתפלה»* (2 Kislev, HY l.582). Also: a coin to tzedakah before *every* mitzvah draws the encompassing light (makif) inward. *«נכון במאד לתת קודם כל מצוה פרוטה לצדקה»* (6 Tammuz, HY l.354).

**P7. Heaviness of heart before prayer is what makes feeling possible within it.** The Rashab (Sukkos): through the sober heaviness before prayer, in which one is bitter over oneself, the middos can be moved in prayer. The Tzemach Tzedek reads "one stands to pray only from heaviness of head" as the lower teshuvah (regret, bitterness) that precedes prayer, which is the higher teshuvah. · *«שע"י הכובד ראש שקודם התפלה, שמתמרמר בנפשו»* (20 Tishrei, HY l.522); *«אין עומדין להתפלל אלא מתוך כובד ראש»* (MTZ l.747).

**P8. The structure of prayer is a ladder.** It stands on the ground: it starts with thanks (hoda'ah) alone. Its top reaches heaven, self-nullification, reached through the understanding in Pesukei deZimra, the blessings of Shema, and Shema. · *«התחלת התפלה היא בהודאה לבד»* · Hayom Yom 5 Kislev, HY l.585. In learning a Jew feels like a student before his teacher; in davening, like a child before his father (26 Tammuz, HY l.386).

**P9. Prayer is the root. Understanding is its preparation (the Rayatz, 10 Kislev 5707).** Certainly one must learn Chassidus and go deep, "but the root of it all is avodas hatefillah, heartfelt davening with a broken heart". Among chassidim the work of the mind was no more than preparation for the work of the heart. · *«אָבער עיקרא ושרשא דכולא איז עבודת התפלה»* (SS l.6781); *«די עבודת המוח איז מער ניט ווי אַ הכנה צו עבודת הלב»* (SS l.6786).

**P10. What comes *after* davening is the main thing.** The Rayatz: the pictures of after-davening are "a Chassidic Jew, a Chassidic householder, a Chassidic shopkeeper". What happens after depends on what happened before. After davening there is a "Shulchan Aruch Jew": how to get up, how to sleep, how to eat. "Inner education is how to live." · *«דער עיקר פון דעם דאַוונען איז דער נאָכן דאַוונען … חינוך פנימי איז ווי צו לעבן»* · SS l.6789. Also: prayer shows the one praying himself, "he sees clearly his own essence, the virtues he has and the faults he must clear out" (*«ער זעהט קלאָהר דעם מהות עצמו»*, SS l.6122).

**P11. Mincha: the prayer in the middle of the business day.** The Alter Rebbe's first teaching in Liozna: Mincha's advantage is that it falls "in the middle of the sea", when people are busy, and they stop for it. That is where the form visibly masters the matter. (Paraphrase of 22 Adar I, HY l.146.)

**P12. Daily fixed study.** Every day: a section of Chumash with Rashi from the week's portion (the Alter Rebbe's takanah, kept by all the Rebbeim), Tehillim as divided by the month, and the Tanya shiur. "One must guard this; it touches him, his children and grandchildren." The Rashab's own daily shiurim (Chumash, Tanach by heart, Mishnah, two blatt a week in depth, three a day in review, Yerushalmi, poskim, Midrash Rabbah) are set out on 3 Nissan. · *«אלע טאג זאל מען לערנען א פרשה חומש מיט פירש"י»* (19 Teves, HY l.55); see also 25 Shevat (HY l.105) and 3 Nissan (HY l.201). Live with the parsha: *«ניט נאר לערנען פרשת השבוע יעדען טאג, נאר לעבען מיט איר»* (2 Cheshvan, HY l.545).

**P13. Carry Torah by heart into the street, the store and the subway.** Every learned Jew should know something by heart (Chumash, Tehillim, Mishnayos, Tanya) so that at any time and place he can think or say holy words. This "purifies the air". One may not go into the street "empty". · *«מוז האבען עפעס אויסגעלערנט בעל פה»* (11 Teves, HY l.40); *«מ'טאר ניט געהן אין גאס אליבא רקניא»* (9 Adar I, HY l.123).

**P14. Empty-headedness breeds stray thoughts.** Thought is the servant of intellect and middos. When it has nothing to serve, it wanders. The cause of foreign or bad thoughts is "the emptiness of the head"; when the intellect is occupied there is no room for them. · *«די פוסטקייט פון קאפ»* · Hayom Yom 16 Cheshvan, HY l.562. *For anyone:* keep the mind supplied with something worth thinking about.

## 2b. Work, business, eating and the body

**P15. Two parts of a business person's avodah.** (a) *With himself*: in free moments in the shop, learn a Mishnah or a chapter of Tanya, and know texts by heart to review on the street. (b) *With others*: in business conversation, steer toward a meaningful story and an opening to encourage study. · *«העבודה עם עצמו»* (HY l.203); *«העבודה עם זולתו»* (HY l.204). Hayom Yom 4 Nissan. The Maharash envied R. Eliyahu Abeler, a simple man who in the middle of market deals would share a word of Ein Yaakov and encourage learning. *«אלי' איך בין דיר מקנא»* (16 Iyar, HY l.283).

**P16. Bitachon in business: trust that G-d provides generously, and be joyful as though one's livelihood were already set before him. Make only a *vessel* for livelihood, and keep it clean of deceit. The work is secondary; the blessing is the main thing.** · *«כאלו פרנסתו כבר מזומנת לפניו»* (16 Adar II, HY l.180); *«האדם עליו רק לעשות כלי לפרנסתו»* (27 Sivan, HY l.342); *«די גאנצע ארבעט, איז מער ניט ווי א טפל»* (4 Menachem Av, HY l.394).

**P17. Eating and the body: "better to eat for the sake of davening than to daven for the sake of eating."** The Tzemach Tzedek to his daughter-in-law, who would not eat before praying despite her doctor's instructions: a Jew must be healthy and strong in order to bring life into mitzvos, and do it with joy. · *«בעסער עסען צוליב דאווענען ווי דאווענען צוליב עסען»; «מען דארף אריינבריינגען א חיות אין מצות»* · Hayom Yom 10 Shevat, HY l.82.

**P18. Refine the body; do not break it with self-mortification.** The Besht on "if you see your enemy's donkey": the body lies under its load, lazy in mitzvos. One might think to break it with fasts, "but not in this way will the light of Torah dwell", rather refine and purify it. · *«לברר את הגוף ולזככו ולא לשברו בסיגופים»* · Hayom Yom 28 Shevat, HY l.109.

**P19. What is permitted, done for pleasure, is "complete evil". Put holiness into the permitted, so that it has a Torah purpose.** The Besht: in every permitted physical thing there is good and bad. The physical is the bad and the G-dly vitality is the good. Turn from the pleasure, want to be nourished by the vitality, and make peace between the two. The avodah is to make the physical a vessel for G-dliness, to make spirit out of matter. · *«דברים המותרים אם עושה אותם להנאתו הם רע גמור»* (27 Nissan, HY l.248); *«ענין העבודה הוא לעשות הגשמי כלי לאלקות»* (17 Sivan, HY l.325); *«פון די גשמיות רוחניות»* (29 Adar II, HY l.195); the Besht, 5 Menachem Av (HY l.395).

## 2c. Small daily acts of itkafia

**P20. The early chassidim's pact: any permitted thing for which one feels a desire and craving, do not do it. By that, desire is broken.** · *«שכל דבר המותר, אך שיש לו איזה רצון וחמדת הלב לזה לא יעשנו»* · Hayom Yom 27 Shevat, HY l.107.

**P21. "What is forbidden is forbidden; what is permitted is not necessary."** The Alter Rebbe's first saying in Liozna, which R. Mordechai of Horodok's group worked on for three or four years until it became their way of life. Only then did they go in to ask for a path in avodah. · *«וואס מען טאר ניט טאר מען ניט, און וואס מ'מעג דארף מען ניט»; «כשלש ארבע שנים עבדנו בזה עד שהבאנו אופן זה בעניני חיינו»* · Hayom Yom 25 Adar II, HY l.190.

**P22. Small is enough. Avodah is not overturning mountains. Any act with true intention suffices: one blessing with intention, one word of davening with a prepared heart knowing before Whom you stand, one verse of Chumash known as G-d's word, one verse of Tehillim, one kindness that draws a fellow's heart close.** To reach it takes great toil in learning and understanding, each by his measure. · *«כל עבודה ופועל, איזה שיהיו, בכוונה אמיתית די ומספיק: א ברכה בכוונה»; «צריכים יגיעת רבה ועצומה»* · Hayom Yom 2 Adar I, HY l.115–116. *For anyone:* this is the most universal instruction in the corpus.

## 2d. Relationships, ahavas Yisrael, tzedakah

**P23. Love of G-d and love of Israel are engraved together in every soul. Love of Israel is greater, "for one loves what the beloved loves". It is owed even to a Jew one has never seen.** The Besht: love of Israel is love of G-d. "Ve'ahavta lere'acha" is the explanation of "ve'ahavta es Hashem". · *«דאוהב מה שהאהוב אוהב»* (28 Nissan, HY l.249); *«מצות אהבת ישראל הוא ליליד עם ישראל אשר מעולם לא ראה אותו»* (3 Adar I, HY l.117); also 12 and 24 Menachem Av (HY l.405, 421).

**P24. What use is Chassidus and fear of Heaven if the main thing, love of Israel, is missing, and one even causes others pain?** (the Maharash's saying) · *«אז עס פעלט דער עיקר - אהבת ישראל»* · Hayom Yom 8 Menachem Av, HY l.399.

**P25. Rebuke: first remove the "nails" (don't sting; every jab is kelipah), then "wash the hands" (draw the intellect into the middos). The condition for rebuking is not hating. If the rebuke failed, you are at fault, since your words did not come from the heart.** · *«תחלה להוכחה צריכים להסיר את הצפרנים»* (22 Elul, HY l.466); *«שלא היו דברים היוצאים מן הלב»* (26 Iyar, HY l.297).

**P26. A Chassid makes an environment. If not, he should look into his own bag: what is going on with him? He should ask himself, "what am I doing in the world?"** · *«א חסיד מאכט א סביבה»* · Hayom Yom 30 Adar I, HY l.156. Also the duty of *hisaskus*: even while busy in the market, think what one can do for others' good and invite acquaintances to a shiur or farbrengen (21 Menachem Av, HY l.417).

## 2e. Evening, night and accounting

**P27. Bedtime Shema is a small-scale confession before death.** At death one leaves the fair entirely; at bedtime Shema one is still in the middle of the fair and can still get something done (the Rashab). · *«קריאת שמע שעל המטה איז, אין זעיר אנפין, ווי דער ווידוי»* · Hayom Yom 6 Kislev, HY l.586. The Rayatz quotes the chassid R. Gershon Dov after a bedtime Shema said as avodah: "one lies down, one does not want to lie down, but one lies down; one must get up an entirely different person". *«מען באַדאַרף אויפשטיין גאָר אַן אַנדערער»* (SS l.6123). The Lubavitcher Rebbe: preparing for sleep as a Jew, aware that G-d stands over him, makes even the sleep "the sleep of a servant", as a firm decision to wake at a set time works through the night. *«שהכנה זו (קודם השינה) פועלת על השינה, שגם השינה היא שינה של עבד»* (TM 5712, 2nd day of Sukkos, l.7327).

**P28. Count the hours. The Alter Rebbe's chassidim "always counted". The Rashab: the hours must be counted, then the days are counted. When a day passes, one must know what one did and what remains. Tomorrow should be much finer than today.** · *«די שעות דארפען זיין געציילטע, דאן זיינען די טעג געציילטע … דער מארגען זאל זיין א סך שענער ווי דער היינט»* · Hayom Yom 1 Iyar, HY l.252. And the Alter Rebbe: "a summer day and a winter night is a year" (*«אזומערדיגער טאג און אווינטערדיגע נאכט איז א יאהר»*, 17 Cheshvan, HY l.563).

**P29. Cycles of accounting: Elul, birthdays, and the "sum" of what was actually done.** Elul is "the month of accounting". As a businessman takes stock, each person, scholar or businessman, reckons the year, strengthens his virtues and repairs his lacks. On one's birthday, seclude oneself, recall one's memories, and repair what needs repair. The Lubavitcher Rebbe: in the end the main thing is the deed, "and one must know a true accounting of what was actually done". · *«חודש אלל הוא חדש החשבון»* (27 Menachem Av, HY l.425); *«ביום ההולדת, על האדם להתבודד»* (11 Nissan, HY l.215); *«העיקר הוא המעשה והבפועל ממש, וצריכים לדעת חשבון צדק מהנעשה בפועל»* (IK l.12407). The Rayatz: the beginning of avodah is a true account: who am I and what am I. That detailed contemplation is "ploughing" that breaks up the heart of stone. *«על האדס לעשות חשבון אמיתי, מי אני ומה אני»; «התבוננות פרטית כזו היא חרישה»* (Likkutei Dibburim vol. 1 p. 83, LDH l.576–577, **OCR**).

## 2f. Week and year: Shabbos, farbrengen, niggun

**P30. The week leans toward Shabbos.** Preparation for Shabbos starts Wednesday. On Thursday, one feels one must do something, "and if one sits Thursday night and learns Chassidus, then on Friday one hears 'for G-d is great'", and with that a Jew keeps Shabbos. On Shabbos Mevorchim: say all of Tehillim early, then learn "about an hour" a maamar understandable to all, then daven, then farbreng. · *«מ'לערענט חסידות, יעמאלט איז פרייטאג דערהערט מען»* (24 Kislev, HY l.15); *«ילמדו כשעה מאמר של חסידות שיהיה מובן לכל, ואחר כך תפילה»* (26 Kislev, HY l.21).

**P31. Farbrengen is "one of the foundations of the ways of chassidim and of Chassidus" and the gateway to ahavas Yisrael.** At a farbrengen the speakers demand that those present improve their conduct, fix times for Chassidus and keep them, and learn "in order to do". The rebuke is only in things that do not shame anyone, "each rebuked the other with love and great affection". The Rashab: a Chassidic farbrengen is like a kettle, where the fire from above boils out all the not-good. · *«היא אחת היסודות בדרכי החסידים והחסידות»* (HY l.530); *«אשר איש את רעהו הוכיחו באהבה ובחיבת גדולה»* (HY l.531), 24 Tishrei; *«אַ חסידישער פאַרבריינגען איז אַזוי ווי אַ קעסיל, דער אש של מעלה קאָכט אויס דעם גאַנצען לא טוב»* (SS l.7558). Kuntres Limud HaChassidus closes the same way: fix times for Chassidus, learn it to do it, "and from time to time gather in the love of friends to strengthen the ways of Chassidus". *«ומזמן לזמן להתועד באהבת ריעים אהובים לחזק את»* (KL l.1639, **OCR**).

**P32. Niggun and song.** The Rashab: a Chassidic word clears the head and cleans the heart; a Chassidic custom lights up the home; a Chassidic niggun strengthens hope and trust and brings joy. The Tzemach Tzedek would stop his learning or writing to sing. Chassidim sing in davening "because the voice arouses the intention", and when a matter settles in a chassid during prayer, "it sings". · *«א חסידעשער ניגון שטארקט די תקוה ובטחון»* (22 Tammuz, HY l.380); *«חסידים זינגען אין דאַוונען, ווייל קול מעורר הכוונה»* (SS l.6127).

**P33. Koshering the soul, in three steps: soaking in the Rebbe's words, salting in yechidus, rinsing with a niggun.** · *«שרי' - ווייקען זיך אין דברי הרב, מליחה - יחידות, הדחה - ניגון»* · Hayom Yom 26 Elul, HY l.473.

**P34. Stories of chassidim.** The Rashab to the young Rayatz: Chassidic stories are the general remedy "that makes one a person of good middos and a servant of G-d". The Rayatz: each story is a directive for life and should bring a good trait. · *«סיפורי החסידים איז די הטבה כללית וואָס מאַכט פאַר אַ בעל מדות טובות»* (SS l.6787); 26 Menachem Av (HY l.423).

## 2g. The mashpia, the chavrusa, yechidus, hiskashrus

**P35. Yechidus means "to clarify one's state and fix one's manner of avodah", in turning from bad and drawing close to good middos, and to bind oneself and hand over all one's wills.** A chassid's structure of intellect and middos accords with his first yechidus. · *«לברר את מצבו, ולקבוע אופן עבודתו בסור מרע ולקרב את המדות הטובות»* (10 Elul, HY l.446); *«די ערשטע יחידות איז לפי ערך מהות עצמו פון א חסיד»* (20 Sivan, HY l.328).

**P36. Consult the mashpia on method.** The Lubavitcher Rebbe: of the ways of contemplation before and during prayer set out in Tanya, some plainly do not fit a yeshivah student. Once the choice is down to two or three, "let him consult the mashpia which to choose". · *«אז יתייעץ עם המשפיע באיזה מהן לבחור»* · Likkutei Sichos vol. 34, supplements to Eikev, LS l.17033. Also the Rayatz: serving (*shimush*) the elder chassidim did more than learning. Learning made a person "understand a matter", serving made him "an oved Hashem and a chassid" (Sefer HaSichos, Reshimas HaMaasar, 255:8, found through the search tool and not line-verified; listed only as a lead).

**P37. Hiskashrus is by learning.** "True hiskashrus" is by learning the Rebbe's maamarim, reading his sichos, joining with the chassidim in their learning and farbrengens, and keeping the Tehillim and fixed study times. The Lubavitcher Rebbe: since the purpose of the maamarim is "drawing Chabad into the middos and changing the middos through them", hiskashrus means walking in his ways in middos and in thought, speech and deed. · *«ההתקשרות האמיתית היא ע"י לימוד התורה»* (24 Sivan, HY l.337); *«כיון שכוונת אומר המאמרים הוא המשכת חב"ד במדות ושינוי המדות על ידן»* (LS l.17063).

**P38. The chavrusa: one who learns with his fellow and kindles in him the "constant fire" has a merit that will never go out** (the Tzemach Tzedek, 21 Adar II, HY l.186; see M24).

## 2h. Kabbalas ol, simcha, bitachon, hashgachah

**P39. Accustom yourself to see Divine providence: how G-d renews the world and every creature every moment.** Tefillah wakes the heart. In business people this seeing comes "tangibly", so the Mitteler Rebbe held they have an advantage over scholars here. · *«להרגיל את עצמו לראות ענין השגחה פרטית»* (29 Sivan, HY l.345); 7 Tishrei (HY l.492).

**P40. Serve always inspired.** Considering how small man is and that he can give pleasure to the Greatest, "a person must always be enthused and do the avodah with heart and willing soul". · *«דארף דער מענש זיין תמיד באגייסטערט»* · Hayom Yom (Kislev), HY l.590.

**P41. From kabbalas ol to taste. The Rayatz's Haggadah: "now we are slaves" is service as a slave; "next year free" means free of serving by kabbalas ol alone, "one will have a taste in the avodah".** The Lubavitcher Rebbe likewise distinguishes the "field" (service under compulsion and kabbalas ol, like a servant) from the "vineyard" (service with pleasure and vitality). · *«לשנה הבאה בני חורין, דאָס מיינט אַז מען וועט זיין בני חורין פון די עבודה באופן קבלת עול, נאָר מען וועט האָבען אַ געשמאַק אין די עבודה»* (SS l.7952); *«כאשר מקיים רצון העליון מתוך כפי' וקב"ע, כעבודת עבד»* (LS vol. 34 Teitzei 2, l.16077).

## 3. Growth over time

**G1. It takes years, and they are counted in years.** The Horodok group spent three or four years on one saying before asking for a path (P21). R. Moshe Vilenker prepared three years for a yechidus and then stayed seven years in Liozna "to bring the yechidus into actual avodah". The Alter Rebbe said of him that in ten years of toil he had worked out broad mochin. An interpretation of "lechaim ulevrachah" is one "a chassid can say who has davened and worked in avodah for thirty years". · *«דריי יאר האט ר' משה ווילענקער זיך מכין געווען צו יחידות … זעבען יאר אין ליאזנא אויף בריינגען די יחידות אין עבודה בפועל»* (18 Tammuz, HY l.375); *«אין די צען יאר וואס ער האט געהארעוועט»* (HY l.374); *«וואס האט געדאווענט און געטאן אין עבודה דרייסיג יאהר»* (29 Adar I, HY l.155).

**G2. The pnimi needs time; the chitzon is quick. Once a middah has been changed, one must keep watching it so it does not come back.** The Rayatz on the "inward" and the "outward" person: the inward one learns, then reflects, then absorbs, until he warms and is moved, "and all this takes time". It takes a lot of toil to work a middah through, and even after one has changed a trait, "he must still examine well and watch himself so that it does not return". For the outward one, "the natural middos stay in their nature". (**OCR**) · *«בתחילה הוא לומד, לאחר מכן הוא מהרהר במה שלמד»* (LDH l.557); *«יש צורך לעמול הרבה עד שמעבדיס מדה … עדיין עליו לבקר היטב ולהשגיח על עצמו שלא יהיה חוזר וניעור … והמדות הטבעיות נשארות בטבען»* (LDH l.559). Likkutei Dibburim vol. 1 pp. 80–81.

**G3. Kuntres Limud HaChassidus: R. Yekusiel of Lepli's four months.** He "toiled with immense toil … to accustom myself to think one matter for several hours consecutively … and to repeat one matter several tens of times", with a young man who reviewed with him. After that he felt himself "a new creature". The principle: "nothing stands before the will". (**OCR**) · *«יגעתי ביגיעה עצומה ביגיעת נפש וביגיעת בשר להרגיל את עצמי לחשוב ענין»* (KL l.1508); *«אין לך דבר העומד בפני הרצון»* (KL l.1499). And the Mitteler Rebbe's floor for supported young men: *«יעשו להם קביעות ללמוד בכל יום דא"ח לא פחות משלש שעות»* (KL l.1421).

**G4. The Tzemach Tzedek's order of contemplation over time: first the outer structure of the worlds (cause and effect), which yields outer love and fear, then the inner, how all is created from nothing, which yields inner love and fear.** "One must begin first by contemplating the outside of the worlds." (**OCR**) · *«לכך צריך להתחיל תחלה להתבונן בחיצוניות העולמות … ומזה ההתבוננות שמתבונן בפנימיות שלמעלה מההשתלשלות»* · Or HaTorah, Beshalach p. 61, OT l.74776.

**G5. R. Yitzchak Eizik of Homel: grades of service under kabbalas ol.** At the base (*malchus*) one fulfils the mitzvos by compulsion, by the yoke he cannot throw off. *Yesod*: the earnest servant, working "with alacrity and wholeness (called *ernstkeit*)" but with no felt vitality. *Hod*: he acknowledges he ought to serve with longing but has no feeling now because he is preoccupied with himself. *Netzach*: he casts himself aside in the act, but "against the nature of his soul". The ladder runs from compulsion toward vitality. · *«דבחי' מל' הוא ע"ד עבודה בקבלת עומ"ש … צדיק העובד בזירוז ובתמימות (הנק' ערינסטקייט) … רק שזהו אצלו בע"כ נגד טבע נפשו»* · Chanah Ariel, Miketz 2:2, HO l.64.

**G6. R. Yitzchak Eizik of Homel: the beinoni's relapse after prayer.** In prayer and contemplation he may be moved, even to a G-dly feeling. Then "the second judge" (the animal soul) can rule his body to the point of cancelling "the impression (*reshimu*) that ought to remain from the excitement of prayer all day". Some cannot find even the excitement, though "he understands in his mind and grasps and agrees to do a good deed". · *«עד שיבטל בחי' הרשימו שראוי להיות נשאר מן ההתפעלות בתפלה על כל היום … אף שמבין במוחו ומשיג ומסכים לעשות איזה מעש"ט»* · Chanah Ariel (Rosh HaShanah section), HO l.373. This is the measuring stick: does the impression of the morning last into the day?

**G7. What goes first when one falls: prayer.** "The beginning of the descent is the lack of avodah in prayer." Everything dries up and goes cold, habitual mitzvos become hard, one rushes, the taste in Torah goes, "and the air grows coarse". · *«ראשית הירידה, ר"ל, הוא העדר העבודה בתפלה»* · Hayom Yom 23 Iyar, HY l.292.

**G8. Return is always possible.** Pesach Sheni: "there is no 'lost'; one can always make it right", even when the distance was by one's own will. The thief's seventh lesson: if he did not succeed the first time, he tries many times. · *«עס איז ניטא קיין "פארפאלען", מען קען אלע מאל פאריכטען»* (14 Iyar, HY l.280); *«אם לא הצליח בפעם הראשונה חוזר הרבה פעמים»* (3 Iyar, HY l.256).

**G9. Soul healing is like body healing: diagnose first.** Is the illness in the coarse body, in a leaning of the soul's powers (pride, falsehood), or in habit formed by bad upbringing or surroundings? Until that is clear, keep an upright order: mitzvos, fixed Torah times, good middos, turning from bad. Above all the sick one must (1) know he is sick and long to be cured, and (2) know he *can* be cured, and trust it. Two methods, as in medicine: treat the sick limb (teshuvah), and strengthen the healthy ones (good deeds). · *«אשר רפואת הנפש היא כרפואת הגוף»* (HY l.322); *«לכל לראש צריך לסמן מקום החולי»* (HY l.323); *«לידע שהוא חולה ויכסוף וישתוקק להתרפאות מחליו»* (HY l.324), 16 Sivan; *«תשובה ומעשים טובים»* (28 Menachem Av, HY l.427).

**G10. Measuring change: the tests these sources give.** (a) Does the understanding show in actual traits (M3, *«מאיר בלב במדות טובות בפועל ממש»*)? (b) Does the morning's impression last all day (G6)? (c) After davening, what kind of person is one: the shopkeeper, the householder (P10)? (d) The Rayatz, to students leaving yeshivah: "What have you bought? What are you taking with you?" (Sefer HaSichos, 19 Elul 5708, 267:5, found through the search tool and not line-verified; a lead). (e) "Tomorrow should be much finer than today" (P28). (f) In spiritual things, look at the one above you; being "content with one's portion" is a flaw there. *«אין רוחניות אבער איז א שמח בחלקו דער גרעסטער חסרון»* (30 Sivan, HY l.348); *«בענינים הרוחניים צריך להסתכל במי שהוא למעלה ממנו»* (24 Cheshvan, HY l.573).

**G11. Your own strength counts for more.** Being led by the hand is higher, but work by one's own strength is dearer. · *«טייערער איז אז עס איז בכח עצמו»* · Hayom Yom 29 Menachem Av, HY l.428.

## 4. Counterfeits of integration

**C1. Knowledge without avodah.** It is not enough to learn nigleh and cherish holiness by keeping mitzvos; avodah of the heart is also needed. Chassidus that does not reach actual avodah has gone off course (M1). · *«שאינו מספיק למוד תורה הנגלית וחבוב הקודש בשמירת המצות, כי אם צריכים גם עבודה שבלב»* · Hayom Yom 29 Teves, HY l.69. The Rayatz: "the word should not stay in understanding and grasp; it should be avodah in practice, with oneself and with the other". *«דער וואָרט זאָל ניט בלייבן אין השכלה והשגה, נאָר עס זאָל זיין עבודה בפועל»* (SS l.6792).

**C2. The pious animal soul: a desire to learn Chassidus that is really a way to avoid prayer.** The Maharash to the Rashab: the animal soul is sometimes a clever fox, and sometimes dresses as a righteous, humble, good-natured man. Sometimes a great desire comes to learn Chassidus or contemplate an idea well, "and in truth it is only the counsel of the yetzer … to hold him back from the avodah of prayer". The rule: anything that leads to actual avodah, *any* obstacle to it, even the loftiest, is the animal soul's scheme. The Rashab: "until then I did not know there could be a pious animal soul, let alone a *Chassidic* one." · *«ולפעמים הוא מתלבש בלבוש צדיק תמים ענו ובעל מדות טובות»* (HY l.332); *«ויש אשר מתעורר חשק גדול ללמוד חסידות או להתבונן היטב באיזה השכלה»* (HY l.333); *«כל דבר המועיל או מביא לעבודה בפועל»* (HY l.334); *«א חסידישער נה"ב»* (HY l.335). Hayom Yom 23 Sivan. Its twin in the Rayatz: on Shabbos, when there is time to daven at length, the animal soul argues that people will call him idle and that he should go home and teach the family the parsha. "But the truth is not so: he must daven, and daven, and daven." *«אם תתפלל באריכות ובבית יש לאשתו חברתות»* (SS l.555); *«ער דאַרף דאַוונען און דאַוונען און דאַוונען»* (SS l.556).

**C3. Warmth without inwardness: the "outward" person.** The chitzon "learns nigleh, learns Chassidus, prays frequently, and everything with warmth". Sometimes he looks warmer than the pnimi, but "the warmth too is outward". He learns a matter and thinks he already knows it, and "as he swallows it, so he spits it out". (**OCR**) · *«לומד נגלה, לומד חסידות, מתפלל לעתיס קרובות והכל בחמימות»* (LDH l.557); *«והחמימות היא גס כן חיצונית»* (LDH l.558).

**C4. Intellectual pleasure in davening instead of G-dliness.** After all the preparations (mikveh, contemplation before prayer), when he stands to daven "he becomes absorbed in the intellectual pleasure of the matter and does not hear the G-dliness of the idea" (the Rayatz, Likkutei Dibburim, Yiddish). (**OCR**) · *«פאַרטראַכט ער זיך אין ד;ם געשמאַק שכלי פון דעם ענין»* · LDY l.1319.

**C5. "Avodah" on one's own schedule, for oneself.** First he fixes what he needs and deserves. Then, on Mondays and Thursdays or on Shabbos after coffee, having chatted with everyone, he starts thinking about "avodah", to pray at length, "and in the end he does not pray. He says the words and discharges his duty." Self-love blinds. (**OCR**) · *«ולבסוף אין הוא מתפלל, הוא אומר את המליס ויוצא בכך»* · Likkutei Dibburim vol. 5 p. 12, LDH l.7254.

**C6. Chassidic form without inner content.** The Rayatz's list of what "does not suit a chassid" (preserved in the Toras Shmuel volume): not to be engaged in refining the middos, both removing bad ones and elevating good ones; not to be engaged in avodas hatefillah, "we don't mean clapping hands and snapping fingers, but actually davening with bonding". (**OCR**) · *«עס פאַסט ניט אַז אַ חסיד זאל ניט ליגן אין תיקון המדות»* (TSO l.44858); *«מען מיינט ניט פאַטשן מיט די הענט און קנאַקן מיט די פינגער»* (TSO l.44859).

**C7. Ecstasy without return.** The sin of Aharon's sons was running toward G-d without returning, *ratzo* without *shov*, "which is death". The truth of looking before G-d must be heard in actual deed. · *«רצוא בלי שוב, היא מיתה»* · Hayom Yom 27 Adar II, HY l.193.

**C8. Sighing instead of doing.** "Better one act than a thousand sighs." "By sighing alone one is not saved": the sigh is only the key that opens the heart and eyes to set out work and action. The true way is to know oneself, faults and virtues, and to fix the faults in action, "not to discharge one's duty with sighs alone". · *«טובה פעולה אחת מאלף אנחות»* (8 Adar II, HY l.166); *«באנחות לבד לא נושע»* (23 Teves, HY l.60); *«ולא לצאת ידי חובתו באנחות בלבד»* (26 Cheshvan, HY l.576).

**C9. Sadness passing as piety (the Lubavitcher Rebbe's letters).** - Two ways to humility: contemplating one's own lowness brings humility *with* sadness and bitterness. It is right in its place (Tanya ch. 31) and must end in victory. Contemplating the unity of Israel, that each needs the other, brings humility *without* sadness. *«הדרך הראשונה מביא לשפלות, עצבות ומרירות»* (IK l.5032); *«הדרך השני' מביאה לשפלות, אבל לא לעצבות»* (IK l.5033). Igros vol. 2 §219. - To one tormented about his spiritual state: for some weeks, stop thinking about it. Emptiness in Torah is cured by learning Torah, "not by sadness". *«במשך איזה שבועות יסיח דעת מלחשוב אודות מצבו ברוחניות … והעצה לזה ת"ת ולא עצבות»* (IK l.15630). Igros vol. 4 §863. - Don't dig into past failings. Here it is the yetzer's device to trip him into sadness and melancholy. Hold to joy. *«שישתדל לא להתעמק במה שעבר ופגם … זהו עצת יצר כדי להכשילו בעצבות ומרה שחורה»* (IK l.23279). Igros vol. 6 §1541. - Why struggle in "turn from evil" when you can choose "do good": contemplating G-d's greatness is itself a mitzvah and brings the turning-away too. *«מה לו להתעסק בקו העבודה דסו"מ בשעה שהברירה בידו להתעסק בקו העבודה דועשה טוב»* (IK l.18621). Igros vol. 4 §1123.

**C10. Harshness toward oneself, or excusing oneself.** The Rashab: truth is the middle path. Leaning right (being too strict with oneself, finding faults that are not true) and leaning left (covering one's faults, going easy on avodah out of self-love) are "both false ways". · *«אמת איז דרך המיצוע»* · Hayom Yom 27 Adar I, HY l.151. And: coarseness does not feel its own coarseness. *«דער וואס איז נידעריג און גראב הערט ניט אן די אייגענע גראבקייט»* (20 Elul, HY l.464).

**C11. Coldness.** The Rashab: between coldness and heresy there is only a thin partition. Learning and davening need fire of the heart. · *«צווישען קעלט און כפירה איז נאר א דינע מחיצה»* · Hayom Yom 16 Shevat, HY l.92.

**C12. Seeking a blessing in place of work.** The Tzemach Tzedek: to ask for a blessing for avodah is "not to heed empty words"; the work must weigh on the people. · *«בעטען א ברכה אויף עבודה איז דאס אל ישעו בדברי הבל»* · Hayom Yom 24 Tammuz, HY l.382.

**C13. Pride and belittling others.** "There is no one to be proud over. One must toil. With patience and closeness, one can accomplish everything. With belittling others and raising oneself, one loses everything." · *«מיט ביטול הזולת והגבהת עצמו ווערט מען חלילה אלץ אן»* · Hayom Yom 20 Iyar, HY l.289.

**C14. Justifying one's own rubbish.** The Rayatz (19 Kislev 5707): the way of chassidim is to see in the other his virtues and in oneself one's faults, in learning, prayer and practice. "One may not justify one's own garbage-ways". One must clean them out completely. · *«דרכי החסידים איז צו זען אין דעם צווייטען זיינע מעלות … מען טאָר ניט מצדיק זיין די אייגענע אשפה»* · SS l.6842.

**C15. "It's impossible": a false belief that holds back avodah.** Kuntres Limud HaChassidus: those who say a creature cannot grasp these matters hold "a false opinion that withholds grain from the service of G-d". (**OCR**) · *«היא דעה כוזבת המונעת בר בעבודת השי»* · KL l.1615.

## 5. For anyone

These are the items the sources themselves give to *every* person. Where a practice is specifically Jewish, it is named as what it is.

**E1. Every person can serve G-d with mind and heart, "each according to his measure"** (M5, HY l.606). Chassidus made it possible for anyone, "even one without a high soul who has not refined himself", to grasp G-dliness, because it explains the hidden through examples from the soul's own powers (IS §1, l.8, paraphrased).

**E2. The first level of "chassid", not being thrown by the world's affairs, is one "everyone can reach and everyone is obliged to reach"** (M26, HY l.602).

**E3. Small acts with full intention suffice** (P22, HY l.115). Taken beyond Jewish practice, this is: one sentence of thanks said meaning it, one word of prayer said knowing Whom one faces, one kindness that draws someone's heart close.

**E4. Say thank you on waking before anything else** (P1). Modeh Ani is a Jewish practice; the posture is open to all: gratitude that no impurity can spoil.

**E5. A graded floor for prayer or contemplation: at length if you can, at least a few minutes of contemplation first, at the very least each word said slowly without rushing** (P4, TM l.6891).

**E6. Keep the mind supplied** (P13–P14). Carry a few memorised lines of something holy, so that thought has something to serve. For Jews the sources name Chumash, Tehillim, Mishnah and Tanya.

**E7. The two-step evening review: at night "one is still in the middle of the fair". Take stock of the day, and aim for tomorrow to be finer than today** (P27–P28). Bedtime Shema is a Jewish practice; the nightly accounting is not limited to it.

**E8. Small refusals: of the permitted things you crave, sometimes don't** (P20–P21).

**E9. Everything you see or hear carries a lesson in how to live** (the Besht in Hayom Yom 9 Iyar, and the Maharash, M13 / TS l.14786). *«פון יעדער זאַך וואָס אַ איד בכלל און אַ חסיד בפרט זעהט אָדער הערט דאַרף ער אַרויסנעמען אַ הוראה»* (TS l.14786). The sentence is addressed to "a Jew generally and a chassid especially"; the practice of drawing a lesson is open to all.

**E10. Healing the soul: find where the illness is, want to be healed, believe you can be; strengthen the healthy parts as well as treating the sick one** (G9).

**E11. Love of others as the gate to love of G-d** (P23). Every person is also given the moral and spiritual strength to raise up those around him: *«כל אחד מישראל, בין איש ובין אשה, יש בו די כח מוסרי ורוחני»* (5 Cheshvan, HY l.549, stated for Jews).

**E12. No one is ever lost** (G8): "one can always make it right".

## What this means for integration (our synthesis, not the sources' words)

1. **These sources define the goal of Chassidus as change, and they measure it.** Integration is not a feeling about the idea. It is "changing the *nature* of the middos" (M1, M22, M23). The test they return to is simple: does the understanding "light up the heart as good traits *in deed*" (M3)? Does the morning's impression last into the day (G6)? After davening, is one a "Chassidic shopkeeper" (P10)? An integration layer for the Unity Index needs a test like this, not only a description.

2. **The route has fixed stations, and prayer is the hinge.** Learning (understanding) → contemplation before prayer (feeling the *vitality* of what was learned) → contemplation in prayer (feeling the G-dliness in it) → the day (M7, M8). The Tzemach Tzedek puts the mechanism in one line: before prayer the truth "is only in the mind and does not touch him"; through prayer it spreads into the body (M9). In Homel's version prayer is what makes Torah and mitzvos complete (M10). When prayer goes, everything else begins to go (G7). For the meditation manual this is the key point: the sitting is the *middle* of a daily cycle, not the whole of it. It must be fed by study beforehand and must discharge into conduct afterwards.

3. **Restraint before transformation, and why.** The Rebbe gives the reason behind the old order of itkafia before ishapcha. The mind cannot enter a trait while it is at full natural strength; it can clothe itself only in a weakened trait (M15). The heart cannot be turned at will, but it can be *bound* all day (M16). So the small daily refusals (P20, P21) are not asceticism. They open the door for the idea. Breaking the body is ruled out (P18).

4. **The three garments are where it is done, and they are one's own job.** Heaven gives the holy garments. Washing the flesh and the garments of thought, speech and deed "one must do oneself" (M12, M13). A practical programme should be organised by the three garments: what one thinks about in empty moments (P13–P14), how one speaks in business (P15), what one does with the permitted (P19).

5. **Time scales are long, and relapse is expected.** Three to four years on one saying, seven years to bring one yechidus into practice, thirty years of davening (G1). Even a changed trait must be watched "so that it does not return" (G2). Return is always open (G8). A realistic integration plan should be measured in years and should build in a regular accounting: nightly, monthly (Elul), and yearly (birthday) (P27–P29).

6. **The main counterfeits all move the self to the centre.** Learning used to escape davening (C2), warmth that is only outward (C3), intellectual pleasure in place of G-dliness (C4), "avodah" once the self is taken care of (C5), clapping instead of bonding (C6), ecstasy that does not return to deed (C7), sighing (C8), sadness (C9). The Rebbe's letters are consistent: sadness is the yetzer's counsel. The better road is "do good" and contemplation of G-d's greatness (C9). This matters for the meditation manual. Guidance built on self-scrutiny must be kept for set times and should end in joy and action.

7. **It is not done alone.** Farbrengen as a "kettle" (P31), "two G-dly souls against one natural soul" (M24), the mashpia who picks one's method of contemplation (P36), yechidus that sets one's path (P35), and ahavas Yisrael as the gate to prayer (P6, P23). In these sources integration is communal as well as inward.

## Gaps and leads not followed

- **Rebbe Rayatz:** deliberately not deepened after the scope change. Leads found but not line-verified: Sefer HaSichos 19 Elul 5708 (267:5, "what are you taking home?"); Reshimas HaMaasar 255:7–8 (serving the elder chassidim did more than learning); Igros Kodesh (scans) vol. 1 p. 195 (the Rashab's mashpi'im stationed in towns); Sefer HaMaamarim 5703 p. 199 (cheshbon at tikkun chatzos). These belong to the Rayatz file.
- **Lubavitcher Rebbe:** Likkutei Sichos and Igros Kodesh were sampled by targeted search, not read through. The Igros especially (134k lines) will hold many more individual directives on cheshbon hanefesh, the mashpia and sadness. Toras Menachem, though in the Rebbe's folder, was used for only six items. Hayom Yom was read in full (all 608 lines).
- **Tzemach Tzedek and Maharash:** practical directives in their own writings are few in these files. Most of their practical sayings reach us through Hayom Yom, which is where they are cited above. Derech Mitzvosecha and the Tzemach Tzedek's Igros were searched, but nothing was found that gives a daily or practical programme.
- **R. Yitzchak Eizik of Homel:** Chanah Ariel is mostly Kabbalistic exposition. Three passages speak directly to avodah (M10, G5, G6). His other works (e.g. Shnei HaMeoros) are not in the corpus.
- **Not found in these sources as a distinct directive:** a set length of time for contemplation (apart from the Mitteler Rebbe's three hours of study, R. Yekusiel's "several hours on one matter", and "about an hour" of maamar on Shabbos Mevorchim), and explicit teaching on marriage and relationships as avodah beyond ahavas Yisrael and rebuke.
