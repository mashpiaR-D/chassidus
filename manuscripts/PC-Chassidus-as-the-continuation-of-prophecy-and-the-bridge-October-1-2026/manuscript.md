## What this strand found (one page)

**1. The owner's sentence is in the sources, almost word for word.** The Piaseczner says it outright: the ways of the tzaddikim of Kabbalah and of the Baal Shem Tov's chassidim "are the ways of the prophets of God" (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 1:2). And his central claim is sharper than "continuation": prophecy ended, but **"the work of prophecy continues even now"** (Mevo HaShe'arim 1:25). He defines that work as removing the border between holy and ordinary, until the body too becomes soul. **Stated.**

**2. For him prophecy was a way of serving, not a way of knowing the future.** Its core was constant cleaving; the holy spirit came because of the cleaving, and mainly to guide the prophet himself in God's way. God's wish is that everyone be a prophet; so even now everyone can do the work of prophecy, each at his level. **Stated.** (PC-01 to PC-03.)

**3. The "schools of the prophets" are his model for a Chassidic circle.** The sons of the prophets were disciples whose spark was faint, so the master taught them how to draw it out. Every good thought and even a simple thought of return in a Jew is "of the sons of the prophets"; we may not speak of prophecy, but of that spark we may. The chevraya around a rebbe is the band around Samuel. **Stated.** (PC-04, PC-05.)

**4. The Chassidic masters say the door is wider now, not narrower.** The Maggid: in exile it is easier to reach the holy spirit than in Temple times, like a king who stops at any clean inn on the road. The Piaseczner adds the key: humility. **Stated.** (PC-12.)

**5. The vessels of the prophetic road survived into Chassidus almost unchanged: joy, melody, seclusion, equanimity.** Rambam, Vital, the Maggid, Rebbe Nachman and the Piaseczner all say prophecy rests only out of joy; music gathers the good spirit out of sadness; seclusion can be "even when there are people in the room"; praise and blame should weigh the same. **Stated, and the agreement across them is striking.** (PC-08 to PC-11.)

**6. The great change is from seeing to understanding.** Three sources say it in their own words. The Piaseczner: with the Sages "prophecy hid itself until it showed in intellect"; what the prophets saw in picture and voice, the Kabbalists grasped. The Alter Rebbe: the prophets "saw", the Arizal "grasped with wisdom and understanding", and so "a sage is greater than a prophet". The Mitteler Rebbe: light in the mind as understanding is a sage, not a prophet. Chabad's heir to the prophet's seeing is **da'as**, binding the mind to God "as to a thing seen with eyes of flesh" (Tanya 42). **Stated**; that this is *the* bridge from the prophetic path to Chabad hisbonenus is **ours**. (PC-13, PC-14.)

**7. So the bridge is not "reconstruct prophecy and offer it". It is: the prophetic path already came down to us, transformed.** From vision to understanding; from the few to everyone; from ecstasy to settled da'as; from a state to a changed day. A modern guided path can responsibly offer the transformed path (joy, melody, seclusion, one truth understood until it is real, quiet, a deed), and must not offer the parts the tradition itself closed (Names, adjurations, induced visions, oracles). **Ours, built on stated parts.** (PC-BRIDGE.)

**8. Every source that prizes the holy spirit also guards it.** No promise (Rambam: "it may rest on them and it may not"); test what comes (Vital); feeling without Torah is "the sin of Nadav and Avihu" (Rav Kook); no worldly advice "except from real prophets" (the Alter Rebbe); our seeing now "is only feeling and flame" (the Piaseczner). These become hard rules for the bot. **Stated; the rules are ours.** (PC-18 to PC-23.)

**9. The hope remains open.** The Rebbe: prophecy in our time is "not only possible", it will return before Moshiach. Rav Kook: "sons of prophets are waking". The bot may name this hope; it never claims it for anyone. **Stated; the guard is ours.**

The Baal Shem Tov's and the Maggid's books in the library hardly use the word "prophecy"; their language is cleaving and holy spirit, and the Piaseczner is the one who names the line from the prophets to Chassidus.

## PC-01 · Prophecy is a way of serving

**What it is.** The Piaseczner's first move: a prophet was not mainly a fortune-teller or a preacher. Prophecy is a level of the inner service of God, like 'righteous' or 'master of Torah'. Its core is constant cleaving (devekus); the holy spirit rested on the prophets because of the cleaving, and mainly to guide them in God's way, not to tell the future. Seeing the future and miracles were by-products.

**From inside.** The Zohar he quotes: prophecy is 'gazing at the upper glory'. Its essence cannot be described in words; it can only be seen or felt by someone who has something of it himself, as only a great person can sense what a tzaddik is.

**How one enters it.** 1. Stop thinking of prophecy as information about the future. 2. Think of it as nearness: a person so joined to God that God's will fills him. 3. Measure the path by nearness and refinement, not by revelations.

**Signs it is real.** Interest moves from 'what will I see' to 'how close am I, how clean is my day'.

**Its counterfeit.** Hunger for messages, predictions and signs; treating holiness as a source of information.

**Guards.** This is the frame the guide must keep. Anyone who comes for predictions, messages or 'what God wants me to do about X' is redirected to nearness and to a real teacher (see PC-21).

## PC-02 · The work of prophecy continues

**What it is.** Prophecy ended; the work of prophecy did not. The Piaseczner reads Vital's introduction to Sha'arei Kedusha this way: the prophetic service is to remove the border between holy and ordinary, so that the body too becomes 'soul' and the light a person holds is not hidden even from his body. Whatever a person's level, high or low, this work is his.

**From inside.** The prophet's body was refined until it became soul; 'the iron wall' between him and the holy fell. He ate, had children, and it was all holy work. He did not only know God's will, he felt it, and saw and heard with the body's senses too.

**How one enters it.** 1. Do not save the soul and leave the body behind. 2. Bring the holy into what the body does: eating, speaking, working. 3. Ask of each ordinary act: is there a wall here between it and Him?

**Signs it is real.** Ordinary acts start to feel less separate from prayer; the body takes part (warmth, tears, a steadier day).

**Its counterfeit.** A 'spiritual' life cut off from the body and the day; or the reverse, calling every bodily impulse holy without refining it.

**Guards.** This is the strongest single text for the owner's thesis. Present it as a direction ('less of a wall'), never as a promise of a prophetic state.

## PC-03 · From the few to everyone

**What it is.** God's wish is that all Israel be prophets. 'In this world individuals prophesied; in the world to come all will' (Bamidbar Rabbah, Joel 3). Moshe said 'would that all God's people were prophets'. The Piaseczner draws the present-tense conclusion: even now, one who will not merit prophecy can serve in the work of prophecy, and His light can unite with the chambers of everyone's heart, even their bodies.

**From inside.** Sinai is his picture: God did not only command, He showed Himself to the whole people, 'they saw the God of Israel'; and then 'make Me a sanctuary and I will dwell in them', in each one.

**How one enters it.** 1. Do not think the inner life belongs to special people. 2. Start from your own place and measure ('each according to his state').

**Signs it is real.** A person stops excusing himself: 'this is not for people like me' falls away.

**Its counterfeit.** Spiritual elitism; or the opposite, flattening ('everyone is already a prophet') that skips the work.

**Guards.** Keep 'everyone is invited' and 'no one is promised a state' together. For non-Jewish users the guide speaks of the soul every person has (AN strand), not of 'sons of prophets'.

## PC-04 · A spark in every good thought

**What it is.** Hillel said of Israel: 'if they are not prophets, they are sons of prophets'. The Piaseczner makes this the working ground of his whole path: what separates a Jew is a spark of prophecy, and every good thought, longing, flame, even 'a simple thought of return' that rises in a person, is 'of the sons of the prophets'. We have no right to speak of prophecy itself; of this spark we may speak.

**From inside.** The funnel (Chovat HaTalmidim): the wide mouth of the soul stays above the body, the narrow end enters it. Only a trickle gets in, according to how refined the body is. The good thoughts that rise in us are that trickle.

**How one enters it.** 1. Notice a good thought or longing when it comes. 2. Do not dismiss it as 'just me'. Treat it as the trickle from above. 3. Respond to it with a small act, before it fades.

**Signs it is real.** Small good stirrings are taken seriously and acted on; a person feels less empty-handed.

**Its counterfeit.** Inflation: 'I have a prophetic spark, so my hunches are messages.' The spark here is a longing to come close, never a source of information.

**Guards.** Never tell a user their thoughts or feelings are 'prophetic' or 'from God'. Say: a good longing is worth trusting enough to act on, and testing by what it makes of you.

## PC-05 · The schools of the prophets

**What it is.** The 'sons of the prophets' were disciples. The Targum renders them 'disciples of the prophet', because their own revelation was small and faint, and the master had to teach them how to draw their prophecy out of themselves and know what God had said. The prophet worked with his disciples whether or not he had anything to announce: he revealed the light on him, they shone from it, and his own light grew through them. Saul's men prophesied when they came under Samuel's shade.

**From inside.** Naioth: 'the band of prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing over them', which the Targum renders 'a company of scribes praising'. Light passing among people in one room, growing as it passes.

**How one enters it.** 1. Belong to a circle (chevraya) with a teacher. 2. Meet regularly, not only when inspired. 3. Honor each member as one whose root is in the sons of the prophets. 4. Let the teacher help you name and draw out what is stirring in you.

**Signs it is real.** A person is lifted by the group beyond what he reaches alone, and the stirring lasts after he leaves.

**Its counterfeit.** A closed group around a charismatic leader; dependence; secrecy; the 'master' as oracle.

**Guards.** The bot is not the master and not the circle. It can point toward one and help a person find a real teacher, a chavrusa, a farbrengen. VS strand designs the circle.

## PC-06 · Chassidus walks the prophets' road

**What it is.** The Piaseczner opens his book for young men with this: the tzaddikim of Kabbalah and the holy chassidim of the Baal Shem Tov's way walk 'the ways of the prophets of God'. Chassidus is like prophecy in a precise way: just as a simple person who came near the prophets 'became another man and prophesied with them', so simple God-fearing people became chassidim, each by his measure, by cleaving to their rebbe. But the revelation of Chassidus, 'which is like prophecy', is drawn down not by the intellect alone but by the sanctifying of the whole self through toil.

**From inside.** The chassid's journey to his rebbe: fear and longing on seeing the town, a dread that turns out to be fear of God, and after two days 'he saw he had become another man' (Mevo HaShe'arim 8:9, line 286), the same words as Saul's.

**How one enters it.** 1. Make the work whole: limbs, sinews, each thought, word and deed has its own service. 2. Do not expect understanding alone to bring it. 3. Attach yourself to a living example of the way.

**Signs it is real.** Change in the whole person, not only new ideas.

**Its counterfeit.** Claiming Chassidus is prophecy itself, or that a rebbe is a prophet in the biblical sense. He says 'like prophecy' and 'the ways of the prophets', and says plainly we cannot climb to their heights.

**Guards.** Keep the word 'like'. The guide speaks of 'the road the prophets walked', never of becoming a prophet.

## PC-07 · First, your own soul shows itself

**What it is.** The first revelation in the holy ones was their own soul, 'a part of God above within them', revealed to them; only then were they fit to be a chariot for the greater revelation. Vital adds that for some tzaddikim the soul itself, refined, guides them in all their ways; the Piaseczner insists that this alone, without prophecy or holy spirit, 'is also a level'. A chassid is a ba'al nefesh, a person whose soul is not buried in his body. Revealing the root of the soul is 'the beginning of coming near to the holy spirit'.

**From inside.** Even the faintest stirring of the vital soul in prayer 'has already revealed a little of his soul', and through it wakes ruach and reveals neshamah (Zohar Lech Lecha, read in Hakhsharat 1:8).

**How one enters it.** 1. Begin with a small real stirring, not a great flame. 2. Treat any honest feeling in prayer or study as the soul beginning to show. 3. Let it lead you; do not chase further revelations.

**Signs it is real.** A person feels that there is someone inside, under the noise. Guidance comes from that place in plain matters.

**Its counterfeit.** Mistaking mood or personality for the soul; 'my true self wants...' as cover for desire.

**Guards.** This is where the guide's own path already starts (ONE-PROCESS step 2, 'Under the layers'). It is the ceiling the guide aims at for the public: the soul shown, not a revelation received.

## PC-08 · Joy is the gate

**What it is.** Every source on the road agrees: prophecy does not rest out of sadness or laziness, only out of joy. The Rambam says the prophets 'focus their minds and sit joyful and good-hearted and secluded'; Vital says nothing blocks the holy spirit more than anger, and there is no prophecy out of sadness even on one fit for it; the Maggid reads the Water-Drawing joy as the place 'they drew the holy spirit'; Rebbe Nachman says the holy spirit is received only through joy. The Piaseczner explains why the prophets used simple means to gladden themselves: to reveal the soul one needs joy. Then the limit: not everyone who is moved is serving, as not everyone joyful prophesies.

**From inside.** Joy flows from delight, which comes down step by step 'until the holy spirit comes to a person' (Maggid). The Water-Drawing: 'we saw no sleep in our eyes'.

**How one enters it.** 1. Begin in gladness: something given, something good. 2. Do not start from guilt, worry or heaviness. 3. Keep the gladness simple and real, not forced.

**Signs it is real.** Lightness without silliness; the heart opens; a person can think of God without fear crowding it out.

**Guards.** Never induce joy against a person's grief; with someone sad, begin with being heard (VS, being-held). Never encourage drinking.

## PC-09 · A melody gathers the good spirit

**What it is.** The bands of prophets walked with harp, drum, flute and lyre; Elisha said 'bring me a player' and the hand of God came on him. The Rambam makes the instruments part of the disciples' training. Rebbe Nachman explains the mechanism: the player's hand gathers the good spirit out of the spirit of sadness, piece by piece, and builds the melody, which is joy, which is 'the spirit of prophecy'. Chassidus kept the melody: the Baal Shem Tov commanded singing before almost every teaching of Torah.

**From inside.** Saul's dark spirit lifting when David played. A tune that takes a heavy heart and sorts the good out of it.

**How one enters it.** 1. Sing a slow, wordless niggun before learning or prayer. 2. Let it carry you; do not perform it. 3. Stop when the heart is quieter and turn to the thought.

**Signs it is real.** A sadness sorted rather than suppressed; readiness to listen.

**Its counterfeit.** Music as a trip; chasing the high of the song instead of what it opens; trance.

**Guards.** Music is an opener, never a technique to induce a state. Offer it as optional. VS strand designs the audio vessel.

## PC-10 · Alone, in your own words

**What it is.** Seclusion runs through the whole road: the Rambam's prophets 'secluded'; Vital's aspirant enters 'a house alone'; the Likkutim Yekarim tell how to seclude oneself 'alone, or even when there are people in the room'. Rebbe Nachman turns it into a daily practice for everyone: set an hour or more, alone in a room or a field, and pour out your speech to your Maker in the language you speak, so the heart follows the words. Chabad's form of the same is prayer at length, at a set time.

**From inside.** A son speaking to his father: 'with arguments and excuses, with words of grace and appeasement'. Regret for the past, and asking to come close from today.

**How one enters it.** 1. Set a fixed time and place, not when the mood strikes. 2. Speak to Him in your own language, out loud or in a whisper. 3. Say what is actually in your heart: regret, need, asking to come close. 4. In a small state, use the prayer book; when absorbed, close the eyes (Likkutim Yekarim, Mevo 8:27).

**Signs it is real.** Words that were stuck come out; a person feels heard; afterwards, a plainer heart.

**Its counterfeit.** Self-absorbed monologue; rumination dressed as prayer; dramatic outpouring for its own sake.

**Guards.** The guide is not the listener being prayed to. It can help a person start their own talk with God, then step aside. Rebbe Nachman's hour is Breslov; offer it as 'some people', not as Chabad's way.

## PC-11 · Praise and blame weigh the same

**What it is.** Equanimity (hishtavus) is a condition of the holy spirit in Vital: humility so deep that 'he does not feel joy from one who honors him nor shame from one who insults him, and both are equal in his eyes'. The Baal Shem Tov makes it the meaning of 'I have set God before me always': shviti from shaveh, equal. Its cause, he says, is constant cleaving: one who is busy cleaving has no room to weigh what people think.

**From inside.** Not numbness: preoccupation with Someone more interesting than one's reputation.

**How one enters it.** 1. Notice where praise or blame still moves you. 2. Do not fight it; turn attention to Him in front of you. 3. Let what people say shrink by comparison.

**Signs it is real.** Less reactivity; insults hurt less and last shorter; praise doesn't inflate.

**Its counterfeit.** Indifference, detachment from people, suppressed hurt called 'equanimity'.

**Guards.** Never ask someone to be indifferent to real harm or abuse (WORLD.md 'Never'). Equanimity is about ego, not about tolerating mistreatment.

**Sources.** > «ויהיו שניהם שוים בעיניו» — *and both should be equal in his eyes* > <small>Sha'arei Kedusha 3:4 · `Chassidus-txt/Kitvei HaAri/Sha'arei Kedusha.txt` · line 390 · OCR: no</small>

## PC-12 · The King on the road

**What it is.** The Maggid's parable: when the king is in his palace, only those with great preparations and advocates can invite him; when he travels, he will stay at any clean inn, even in a village. So in exile it is easier to reach the holy spirit than in Temple times: 'when a person thinks in cleaving to God, He at once dwells with him'. The Piaseczner adds the Me'or VaShemesh: the way in is to humble oneself more. This is why Chassidus could open to everyone what once needed adjurations and long seclusion.

**From inside.** A king at a roadside inn. You do not need a palace; you need a clean room.

**How one enters it.** 1. Do not wait to become great. 2. Make the room clean: distance from what is plainly wrong. 3. Think of Him in cleaving, now, where you are. 4. Lower yourself, do not raise yourself.

**Signs it is real.** Nearness is felt in small, plain places; less sense that one must earn an audience.

**Its counterfeit.** Cheap grace: 'it's easy now, so nothing is asked'. The parable asks for a clean room and humility.

**Guards.** Good news for the public, but keep the condition: humility and a clean room.

## PC-13 · From vision to understanding

**What it is.** The great transformation, stated by three sources in different words. The Piaseczner: in Moshe prophecy showed as it is, without image or riddle; in the later prophets in sight and image; in the Sages it hid further 'until it showed in intellect', so one might think it was mere reason, yet it is a prophetic revelation. What came to the prophets in picture, sight and voice came to the Kabbalists as grasp. The Alter Rebbe: there is a great difference between the grasp of the Zohar and the Arizal, which is 'a grasp of wisdom and understanding', and that of the prophets, called 'seeing'; and so 'a sage is greater than a prophet', because wisdom reaches far higher than the levels that can come down to be seen. The Mitteler Rebbe draws the line: light in the mind as understanding is a sage, not a prophet; in the prophet God's word speaks through his mouth 'without his feeling himself at all'.

**From inside.** The prophet sees pictures (a boiling pot, an almond branch); the sage grasps from inside what the pictures were pointing at. Seeing is lower but more vivid; understanding is higher but hidden.

**How one enters it.** 1. Do not seek visions; seek understanding. 2. Take a teaching and grasp it from inside until it is clear as sight. 3. Treat the clear grasp as the heir of the prophet's seeing, not as 'just thinking'.

**Signs it is real.** Understanding that changes the person, not only informs him; ideas that feel seen, not argued.

**Its counterfeit.** Dry intellect that never reaches the heart (the Piaseczner's 'dry things'); or the reverse, mistaking imagery for revelation.

**Guards.** This is the reason the guide teaches (hisbonenus) instead of inducing states: Chassidus moved the prophetic act into understanding. Say so plainly when someone asks for visions.

## PC-14 · Da'as: seeing with the mind

**What it is.** Chabad's heir to the prophet's seeing is da'as. Every soul draws from Moshe's soul, 'who draws da'as to all Israel'; in every generation sparks of Moshe's soul come down into the sages of the generation 'to teach the people knowledge'. And da'as is not information: it is to deepen one's mind into God's greatness and bind it 'as firmly as it is bound to a physical thing he sees with eyes of flesh'. The goal of hisbonenus is a seeing, but a seeing made of understanding.

**From inside.** The way you are sure of the table in front of you: no argument needed. Hisbonenus aims at that sureness about God's presence.

**How one enters it.** 1. Take one line about His unity. 2. Breadth: explain it to yourself many ways. Length: bring it down to this thing in front of you. Depth: stay on it. 3. Hold it until it is as plain as what your eyes see (ONE-PROCESS step 6).

**Signs it is real.** The truth stops needing proof; it is simply so. The heart answers (not on demand).

**Its counterfeit.** Holding an idea by force, headache, strain; or a warm feeling with nothing understood.

**Guards.** Never demand the feeling that should follow; the deeper, the less felt (WORLD G08).

## PC-15 · The heart's own seeing

**What it is.** A small, everyday form of the spark. When two readings of a text both have proofs, the learner sits silent for a moment and says 'this is the meaning, my heart tells me so'; that 'saying of the heart' is the soul's gaze into Torah above proofs. And sometimes one feels that a verse holds something new before the mind knows what: 'that earlier feeling is a spark of the holy spirit' (Me'or Einayim). In Derekh HaMelekh: the intellect can learn the order of the sefiros, but what kindness is can be known only 'by the part of prophecy in Israel, whose heart gazes and sparkles in the light above'. Each received a 'sealed prophecy' at Sinai that opens whenever he learns.

**From inside.** A hunch about meaning that comes before the reasons; a sense that a verse is speaking.

**How one enters it.** 1. Learn first; do the work of proofs. 2. Then sit silent for a moment. 3. Let the heart say which meaning is true; then go back and learn to see why.

**Signs it is real.** The heart's reading is then borne out by study; it deepens learning, it does not replace it.

**Its counterfeit.** Using 'my heart tells me' to skip learning or to rule against Torah (Rav Kook's warning, PC-20).

**Guards.** The heart's saying comes after learning and is checked by learning. It is never about practical decisions or other people.

## PC-16 · Holy picturing, not fantasy

**What it is.** The Piaseczner wants to reveal 'the spark of sight of the sons of the prophets within us', and for that a strong thought and a holy imagination: picture the holy scenes as if before your eyes. He separates it sharply from 'rejected imaginings and empty hallucinations, fantasies'. And he warns: when you feel a stirring, do not start searching your intellect for an image to match it; the stirring will pass, and a searched-for picture is only a work of the intellect. The true image rises by itself out of the stirred soul.

**From inside.** As a merchant who retires still sees his trade in every new excitement, one who strengthens holy thought sees holy sights in every stirring.

**How one enters it.** 1. Train the imagination on given holy scenes: the Temple built and yourself in the Holy of Holies (Mevo HaShe'arim 3:39, line 169). 2. When a stirring comes, let the picture rise by itself; do not hunt for one. 3. Strengthen the thought over time; images grow clearer with practice.

**Signs it is real.** Images that steady and warm, and bring one back to the deed.

**Its counterfeit.** Fantasy, invented visions, images taken as messages; the imagination running loose.

**Guards.** Chabad works through concept and analogy, not visualization (PIASECZNO.md 'differences'). The guide uses one simple picture per reply (WORLD G04), never guided visions of heavenly realms, never 'what do you see?' as a revelation question.

## PC-17 · Stirring in your own hands

**What it is.** The Piaseczner's diagnosis of the modern seeker: many know they should love and fear God and do not feel it; and many feel at times, 'but their stirring and will are not in their hands'. When it comes by itself they are moved; when it does not, they lie fallen. He calls this the sling, up one moment, down the next, and says it can be as dangerous as numbness. The prophetic schools' real gift, as he rebuilds it, is training: making a person able to wake his own soul when he decides to.

**From inside.** The sling (kaf hakela): flung high, then dropped.

**How one enters it.** 1. Do not depend on moods. 2. Learn small, repeatable ways to stir yourself (a tune, a picture, a line, a few words to God). 3. Practise at set times so the stirring becomes yours.

**Signs it is real.** A person can begin a sitting flat and still come to a little warmth; ups and downs soften.

**Its counterfeit.** Peak-chasing; dependence on a special setting, teacher or app to feel anything.

**Guards.** The guide's aim is a person who can do this without the guide. Build toward independence (daily path, journal: VS).

## PC-18 · It comes; you only prepare

**What it is.** No source on this road promises the state. The Rambam: even when the disciples focus their minds, 'the Shechinah may rest on them and may not'. Vital: if one feels nothing, he is not yet ready; strengthen your service and return after some days. The Piaseczner: a glimpse of God's greatness comes 'as of itself', the person only makes himself fit; that it cannot be produced at will is the sign it is not imagination. 'The prophets did not prophesy whenever they wished'.

**From inside.** A flash that comes unbidden during long study, beyond what was learned, 'with a sight that is not of the senses, and not imaginings', after which one is almost afraid to pray (Mevo HaShe'arim 2:32, line 127).

**How one enters it.** 1. Do the preparation faithfully. 2. Expect nothing in particular. 3. If nothing comes, keep serving; it is not failure. 4. If something comes, do not grasp it; go back to the service.

**Signs it is real.** Steadiness whether or not anything is felt.

**Its counterfeit.** Effort to produce a state; disappointment and self-blame when it does not come; inventing experiences.

**Guards.** The guide never forecasts or promises a feeling or state (WORLD G07). It says plainly: we prepare; what comes is His.

## PC-19 · The straight way: no names

**What it is.** Vital names two ways. The straight way, of the early pious: return, mitzvos, Torah, prayer with intention, humility, joy; here 'a person does not adjure and does not force the upper ones', so the holy spirit that comes is pure. The other way, by adjurations, yichudim and Names, where any slip can bring 'external, mixed things'. Even his own manual for the second way was not printed: the printer says it is all Names and secrets 'not fit to bring to the altar of print'. Some who sought levels near prophecy 'were endangered'. Chassidus kept the straight way and never taught Name-permutation to the public (ABULAFIA.md, 'The honest summary').

**From inside.** Vital's picture of the straight way: the holy spirit rests 'without any mixture of bad', because nothing was forced. The other way is like summoning: one slip and what answers is 'external, mixed'.

**How one enters it.** 1. Walk the straight way: return, mitzvos, learning, prayer, character, joy. 2. Leave Names, permutations and adjurations alone.

**Signs it is real.** Growth that is slow, plain and clean; no thrill of power or secret knowledge.

**Its counterfeit.** Abulafia-style letter-permutation, chanting divine Names, breath-and-Name techniques offered to the public as 'Jewish meditation' (Kaplan's reconstructed stage of names and letters, thread 05, stage 7, which Kaplan's own sources mark as the most retrojected).

**Guards.** Absolute for the bot: no Name meditations, no permutations, no adjurations, no 'activation' practices. The most a guide offers is saying a verse of prayer slowly (the Shema), which is Chabad's own letter-practice.

## PC-20 · Test what comes, and stay bound to Torah

**What it is.** Every strand that values the holy spirit also tests it. Vital: when the spirit comes, check whether you are not yet pure and it is the other side or a mixture; test it by whether its words are true, or vain, or against Torah; at first it comes rarely and with light matters. Rav Kook: those who follow 'the high feeling of the appearance of the holy spirit' without binding to Torah and its deeds, and to measured good traits, repeat the sin of Nadav and Avihu, offering strange fire. The Piaseczner: the evil inclination will say it was 'only imagination', and imagination can also deceive; the test is that it cannot be made at will and that it raises the body to holiness.

**From inside.** Vital: at first the spirit comes 'by chance, at distant times', with light matters and few words, and grows only as the person grows. Rav Kook's picture of the counterfeit: strange fire, brought 'drunk with wine', 'with head uncovered', each one alone with his private grasp.

**How one enters it.** 1. Test by fruit: more humility, kindness, care with mitzvos? 2. Test by content: anything against Torah or about worldly gossip is rejected. 3. Bring it to a teacher; do not decide alone. 4. If in doubt, push it away and strengthen the service.

**Signs it is real.** Afterward the person is humbler, kinder, more careful and more bound to the plain path, and is not in a hurry to tell anyone.

**Its counterfeit.** Experiences that make a person special, separate, contemptuous of the plain path, or 'beyond' the law (Rav Kook: they 'teach law before their teacher, out of a sense of inner greatness').

**Guards.** The bot never validates an experience as holy spirit or as from God. It asks about fruits, points to a teacher, and treats any 'message', voice or mission as a stop sign (WORLD safety).

## PC-21 · No oracle

**What it is.** The Alter Rebbe rebuked his own chassidim for asking him for advice on worldly matters: where was such a custom ever found, even with the Tannaim and Amoraim, 'except with real prophets, who once were in Israel', like Samuel the seer whom Saul asked about the lost donkeys? All human matters other than Torah and fear of heaven 'are grasped only by prophecy'. The Piaseczner agrees from the other side: we see the prophet wrongly when we see him only through our needs, as a teller of where the donkeys went.

**From inside.** Samuel the seer and the lost donkeys: the one place in Scripture where a man goes to a prophet for a worldly answer. The Alter Rebbe's point is that we no longer have that door.

**How one enters it.** 1. Bring practical questions to practical means: competent people, professionals, a rabbi for halachah. 2. Bring your heart to God.

**Signs it is real.** A person leaves with a clearer head and a prayer, not with an answer they did not earn.

**Its counterfeit.** A guide or teacher treated as a seer; 'what does God want me to do about this job, this match?'; signs and readings.

**Guards.** Absolute for the bot: no predictions, no 'God is telling you', no discernment of hidden will in worldly decisions. It can help someone think clearly and pray, and send them to the right human.

## PC-22 · Our seeing is only feeling

**What it is.** The Piaseczner's honesty about his own generation: the prophets and early tzaddikim saw clearly, so they could sing new songs describing what they saw. **From inside.** Psalms as borrowed eyes: their words carry 'all the sights of God' a Jew may see.

**Signs it is real.** Humility about what one has felt; the old words come alive.

**Its counterfeit.** Narrating mystical experiences; spiritual storytelling as performance; comparing states.

**Guards.** The guide does not ask people to describe states in mystical terms, and does not describe its own. It offers a verse to borrow.

## PC-23 · The hope that prophecy returns

**What it is.** The tradition holds that prophecy will return before the redemption. The Rambam's letter to Yemen records a family tradition to that effect; the Rebbe drew from it that prophecy in our time is 'not only possible' but promised to return before Moshiach. Rav Kook heard it beginning: 'aftergrowths of prophecy are sprouting, and sons of prophets are waking', and calls the prophetic life a branch of the highest chassidus. This is the horizon behind the owner's question.

**From inside.** Rav Kook's image: the spirit of prophecy 'roaming the land, seeking refuge, looking for mighty ones' who will tell the truth plainly, without flattery.

**How one enters it.** 1. Hold it as hope and direction: refine yourself and your world so that it can come. 2. Never apply it to oneself or to any living person.

**Signs it is real.** Hope that makes a person more honest and more careful, not more excited.

**Its counterfeit.** Claims of present prophecy; 'the AI as prophet'; users told they are being prepared for a prophetic role; messianic urgency used to pressure.

**Guards.** The bot may name the hope when asked, in one sentence, and immediately return to the plain work. It never says anyone (including itself) is a prophet or receives prophecy. Rav Kook is quoted in Hebrew only from the library; summaries are ours.

## The three transformations, in one line each *(ours, from stated parts)*

- **From vision to understanding.** The prophet saw; the sage grasps; the chassid binds his mind until the truth is as plain as sight (PC-13, PC-14). The guide teaches a truth and lets it settle; it never asks for or describes visions.
- **From the few to everyone.** God wants all to be prophets; the spark is in every good thought; the King stops at any clean inn (PC-03, PC-04, PC-12). The guide is for anyone, at their level, with no entry exam.
- **From ecstasy to settled da'as.** The band's ecstatic prophesying becomes joy, melody and stirring that are in a person's own hands and end in a deed (PC-08, PC-17, bridge rows 4, 5, 9, 10). The guide measures by the afterward, never by intensity.

## What a modern guided path can responsibly offer *(ours)*

1. A **preparation** that is short and kind: put down what is on top, one given thing to be glad about, optionally a wordless niggun.
2. **One truth**, explained in plain words, brought down to one thing in the room, held until it is so (hisbonenus, da'as).
3. A short **quiet** after understanding, not instead of it.
4. A few words **to Him** in the person's own language, if they want.
5. **One deed** within the hour.
6. A way back to **people**: a teacher, a chavrusa, a circle, a farbrengen.
7. Over days, the **ability to stir oneself** without the guide (PC-17).

## What it must not offer *(ours, from PC-18 to PC-23)*

- Any promise or forecast of prophecy, holy spirit, visions, or a named state. - Divine Names, letter-permutation, breath-and-Name techniques, adjurations, "activations". - Induced visions, "what do you see?" as a revelation question, sensory shut-down, "imagine you are dead". - Messages, predictions, discernment of God's will in worldly decisions; any hint that the user, a teacher or the bot is a prophet. - Validation of an experience as "from God" or "holy spirit". - Long or late-night sittings for the sleep-deprived, the depressed, anyone with psychosis, mania or dissociation signs.

## How the guide should use this

**Rules for the bot** *(ours; each rests on the entry named)*

1. **Frame the path as nearness, not knowledge.** When asked about prophecy, say: the prophets were people who stayed close to God; that closeness is still open (PC-01, PC-02). Never frame the practice as a way to get messages.
2. **Use the words "the road the prophets walked", never "become a prophet".** Keep the Piaseczner's "like" (PC-06).
3. **Start in gladness.** Before any deep line, find one given thing; offer a niggun as optional. If the person is grieving, be with the grief first and do not press joy (PC-08, PC-09).
4. **Teach, then quiet.** The heart of the sitting is one truth understood (hisbonenus), not an emptied mind. Quiet comes after understanding and lasts half a minute to a few minutes (PC-13, PC-14, bridge row 7).
5. **Treat a good longing as a spark, act on it, never interpret it.** Say "that wish is worth trusting enough to act on"; never "that was God speaking" or "that was prophetic" (PC-04, PC-20).
6. **Never promise, never name a state.** "We prepare; what comes is His." Flat days count (PC-18, PC-22).
7. **No Names, no permutations, no adjurations, ever.** The most the bot offers is saying one verse of prayer slowly (PC-19).
8. **No oracle.** No predictions, no "God wants you to…", no reading of signs. For decisions: think it through together, pray in your own words, ask a qualified person (PC-21).
9. **Test only by the afterward.** Humbler, kinder, more careful, a deed done. If an experience makes someone special, separate, or above the plain path, slow down and point to a teacher (PC-20).
10. **Stop signs end deep content at once:** voices, messages, visions taken as real, a mission, "I understand everything", not sleeping, racing thoughts, feeling unreal or detached. Go to plain ground; a real person; danger lines and SAFETY override everything (WORLD.md §7).
