# The Tanya as a book of consciousness · Part 4: The Gate of Unity and the Letter on Return

**Only One research** · October 5, 2026 · early draft 0.1 · Result [030](../../CONTENTS.md#030) · manuscript 4 of 7 · [Cite](CITATION.bib) · [Quote check](../../verification/catalogue.md#the-tanya-as-a-book-of-consciousness-part-4-the-gate-of-unity-and-the-letter-on-october-5-2026)

---
This part holds two short books. *Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah*, the Gate of Unity and Faith, opens with a small preface called *Chinuch Katan*, "a small training," and then gives twelve chapters that take one state apart piece by piece: the state in which the world seems to stand on its own, and the state in which a person sees through it to the word that is saying it right now. It goes from the stone in your hand, up through the mind to the wall where the mind stops, and back down to the stone. The second book, *Iggeret HaTeshuvah*, the Letter on Return, takes the same seeing and turns it on a person who has done wrong. It moves from the plain law of fasting to the root of the soul, a breath blown from His inside and a rope tied above, and shows what it is like to feel that rope fray and to pull it tight again. Read this way, the whole part is a map of one movement: from "there is a world, and I am in it, and G-d is somewhere" to "there is only He, and He wants me here." Each chapter below names the state, retells the chapter as a place a person stands, gives the means that carry you across, a short contemplation, and the signs of the real thing and its fake. The Hebrew lines point to the Sefaria-Kehot text and every one was checked against it.

## Chinuch Katan · Two loves, and the ground under both

**The state.** Two kinds of love for G-d: *kelot hanefesh*, the soul's own pull toward its Maker that burns up by itself, and *ahavah asuyah*, a love that is made in the heart by thinking. Under both lies *emunah*, faith in His oneness, the first ground of every stirring.

**The chapter as consciousness.** It starts with a proverb: "Train a child according to his way; even when he grows old he will not leave it." The Tanya asks: if it is only "his way," a child's way, then it is not the full truth. So why is it praised that the old man never leaves it?

To answer, it lays out the heart. The roots of serving G-d are two states, awe and love. Awe is what holds you back from harm. Love is what moves you toward doing good. And the Torah says of love, "which I command you *to do*: to love G-d." How do you "do" a feeling?

There are two loves. The first is a flame that rises on its own. When the thinking soul has so mastered the body's weight that the body bends under it, the soul catches fire. It is glad in G-d the way a person is glad in a thing he loves, and it takes a wonderful pleasure in Him. From inside, this does not feel like effort at all. It feels like being lifted. The Tanya says plainly that not everyone reaches this. It needs a body refined a great deal, much Torah and good deeds, and a higher soul than most people carry. Those who have it are called tzaddikim, "the righteous rejoice in G-d."

The second love is one any person can reach. You think carefully, in the depth of your heart, about the things that wake love. There is a general way: He is your very life. A person loves his own soul and life without being told to. When he really sees that G-d is his true soul and his real life, the same love turns toward Him. And there is a detailed way: thinking about His greatness piece by piece, as much as the mind can hold and even what is above it. Then you think about His love for you. He went down into Egypt, "the nakedness of the land," to pull souls out of the iron furnace, from the lowest place to the highest holiness, and to bind them to His Name. "As water shows a face to a face," love answers love. That love is *made*. It is built in the heart through understanding. That is why it can be commanded: what is commanded is the work of setting your mind and heart on the things that wake it. The first love cannot be commanded. It is a gift, a taste of the world to come in this one.

Now the proverb. A person is called "one who walks," not "one who stands." He must go from level to level. Between one level and the next he is in a kind of fall. "The righteous falls seven times and rises." But the fall is only a fall compared to where he was. He still stands above most people, because a trace, a *reshimu*, of the higher level stays in him. What carries him through the gap is the love he was trained in when young, before he was great: the made love, the "child's way." That is why the old man never leaves it. When the flame goes dark, the love you built by thinking is still there.

The chapter ends by naming the ground under all of it: pure, faithful belief in His unity. That is what the rest of the book explains.

**The movement.** From a heart that does not feel love, or that once burned and has gone dark, to the made love, carried by *hisbonenus*, deep thinking in the heart, detail by detail, first on "He is my life," then on His greatness, then on His love for me.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "He is my true soul and my very life." - Think of how you love being alive: waking, eating, not wanting to die. Bring it down to one real thing you did this morning because you wanted to live. - Then ask what that life is. It is not yours by right. It is being given. The one who gives it is closer to you than the wanting. - The picture breaks here: your life can be lost and you fear it; He cannot be lost. Stop where the mind stops. - Say to Him: "You are my life. Let me love You the way I love being alive." - Do one thing today on purpose, as someone who is loved.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a quiet warmth toward Him is there after thinking, and it holds on a day with no flame. The fake is waiting for the flame and calling its absence a lack of love, or working up a feeling with no thought behind it.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, Chinuch Katan (line 777): שהיא אהבה עשויה בלב*

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this reading applies it to every person.*

## Chapter 1 · The word still being spoken

**The state.** The state of seeing that everything is being said right now: *hisbonenus* on *dvar Hashem*, G-d's word, standing inside the thing and keeping it from falling back into nothing. Its opposite is the plain sense that things simply are.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Torah says: "Know today and bring it back to your heart that G-d is the G-d in the heavens above and on the earth below; there is nothing else." The Tanya asks a sharp question. Would anyone think there is some other god sitting in the water under the earth? Why warn us so hard to take it to heart?

Because the problem is not a belief. It is how the world feels. It feels like it is just there.

So the chapter gives a new way to see. The Baal Shem Tov read the verse "Forever, G-d, Your word stands in the heavens" like this. The words G-d said, "Let there be a sky in the middle of the water," those very words and letters stand inside the sky right now. They are dressed in it and they keep it alive. If they went away for one moment and returned to their source, the sky would be nothing at all. It would be exactly as it was before those words were said.

This is true of every created thing in every world, even this heavy earth, even a stone. The Arizal taught that even a stone, dirt and water have a kind of soul, a spiritual life. That life is the letters of G-d's speech dressed inside it, making it be, out of nothing, right now.

A stone is not named in the ten sayings of creation. So how does it get its life? The letters of those sayings are turned and swapped and combined, stepping down level by level, until they spell "stone." (The chapter describes the method in the terms of Sefer Yetzirah, 231 gates of letter pairs; it is enough to say the letters are rearranged step by step.) The step-down is needed because the life in the original sayings is far too strong for any single small creature to hold. It has to come down and narrow until it can fit.

And so the name of a thing in the holy tongue is a vessel for the life narrowed into those letters. The name is not a label stuck on the outside. It is the thing's life.

What is it like to stand here? You look at a table and it is no longer only a table. It is a word being spoken, held up by the speaking. The heaviness of things is still there, but now it rests on something. Nothing is "just there" anymore. Everything is being done.

**The movement.** From "the world is simply there" to "the world is being spoken, now." What carries you across is taking the Baal Shem Tov's reading and applying it thing by thing: the sky, the earth, the stone, this object.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Your word stands in the heavens." - Pick one plain thing near you, a cup or a stone. Its being is not left over from yesterday. It is being said now. - Think: if the saying stopped for a moment, it would not break. It would simply not be, as before it was. - The picture breaks: a human word leaves the mouth and is gone; this word stays inside what it made. Stop there. - Say: "You are saying this, now." - Pick the thing up and use it knowing whose word it is.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when ordinary things feel lighter and closer, as if they lean on something. The fake is a poetic feeling of "everything is alive" with no sense of Who is speaking and no change in how you act.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 1 (line 785): כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע*

## Chapter 2 · Not the silversmith's cup

**The state.** The state in which a person sees that the world is not a finished product left to stand on its own. *Yesh me'ayin*, "something from nothing," is happening all the time, and the Maker's power has to be in the made thing always.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The chapter names a mistake, and the mistake is one we all live in. People picture G-d's work like a craftsman's. A silversmith makes a cup. Once it leaves his hands it no longer needs him. He can walk off to the market and the cup stays a cup. So too, they think, G-d made heaven and earth and they stand on their own now. From here comes a denial of His care for each detail and of the Torah's wonders. The world runs itself. Why would He be involved?

From inside, this is the most normal state there is. The world is a machine that works. G-d, if He is there at all, is back at the start.

The Tanya says their eyes are "plastered shut." The silversmith makes something from something. He takes silver and changes its shape. G-d makes something from nothing. That is a different kind of act.

Then comes the picture. At the Sea of Reeds, "G-d drove the sea with a strong east wind all night," and the waters stood like a wall. If He had stopped the wind for one moment, the water would have fallen back down, as water does. A wall of stones stands by itself, but water does not. That was a wonder. Making something from nothing is a far greater wonder, far above nature. So how much more, if the Maker's power left the made thing, it would go back to nothing at all. The power of the Maker must be inside the made thing always, to give it life and keep it. That power is the letters of speech from chapter 1.

The sages read the prayer "You give life to all of them" as "You *bring them into being*," from nothing. Even the word "You," *atah*, hints at this: its letters run from alef to tav, the whole alphabet, with a hei for the five places of the mouth where letters are formed.

Does G-d have a mouth? No. But the Torah says "G-d spoke" again and again. The prophets received the twenty two letters from above, dressed in their minds and in their speech: "The spirit of G-d spoke in me, and His word was on my tongue." Something like that is how the letters dress themselves in creatures, "by the word of G-d the heavens were made," only after many, many steps down, all the way into a body.

To stand here is to feel that the ground under your feet is the sea standing up. It is a wall that stays only because the wind is blowing. The picture breaks in one place and the chapter says so: at the sea there was water before the wind. Here, before the word, there is nothing.

**The movement.** From the silversmith picture, "He made it and left," to "He is making it and staying." What carries you is the comparison itself: seeing the difference between something from something and something from nothing, and letting the wonder of the sea grow into the greater wonder of now.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "The power of the Maker is in the made, always." - Think of something you made that no longer needs you. Then of the sea standing up only while the wind blows. - Bring it to your own hand. It is not a finished cup. It is the wall of water. - The picture breaks: the sea had water to begin with; your hand did not come from anything. Stop there. - Say: "You are holding this up, now. Thank You." - Take one ordinary step today knowing it rests on His power.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when "nature" stops feeling like a wall between you and Him and starts feeling like His wind. The fake is agreeing with the idea while still feeling, all day, that the world runs by itself.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 2 (line 795): אלא צריך להיות כח הפועל בנפעל תמיד*

## Chapter 3 · Sunlight inside the sun

**The state.** *Bittul bimetziut*, the self and the world gone quiet, not by being crushed but by being seen next to their source, the way sunlight counts as nothing inside the sun. This is how things are before Him; our sense that they are solid comes from not seeing the source.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "After these words and this truth," the chapter begins, anyone who thinks will understand that every created thing is truly counted as nothing next to the power that is making it now.

So why does the world look so real? Because we do not see the power of G-d and the breath of His mouth that is inside each thing. If the eye were allowed to see the life and spirit flowing into each thing from "the mouth of G-d," we would not see its body, its stuff, its weight at all. The body would be nothing in the face of the life in it. Without that life it would be nothing, as before the six days. That life alone is bringing it out of nothing, right now. So there is truly nothing but Him.

The picture is the sun. Sunlight fills the sky and lies on the earth. It is plain that the same light is also inside the sun itself. If it reaches so far, surely it shines where it starts. But there, inside the sun, the light counts as nothing. It is only a glow of the sun's body. Nobody calls it a thing there. Down here, under the sky, where the sun's body is not, the light looks like something real, and it gets the name "something." In its source it only gets the name "nothing."

That is exactly how all creatures are next to the flow of His breath. They are only a glow spreading from it. In their source they are nothing. They are called "something" only by our eyes of flesh, which do not see the source at all. So to us things look like complete, solid somethings, the way sunlight looks real when the sun is out of view.

Then the chapter admits where the picture breaks. With the sun, the source is really not there on earth. That is why the light looks real here. But creatures are always in their source. The source is right there, only not visible to eyes of flesh. So why are they not wiped out in it? That question needs the next chapter.

What is this like from inside? It is not feeling small or worthless. It is a stillness that comes when you picture the source. Your "I am here" goes quiet the way a candle goes quiet in daylight. It still burns. It just does not matter.

**The movement.** From "things are solid and I am solid" to "things are a glow, and the source is here." What carries you is the sun picture, held until it turns around on you: I am the light that looks real only because I am not looking at the sun.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "In its source, it is not even called light." - Picture sunlight on a wall. Then picture the same light inside the sun, where it is nothing. - Bring it to yourself: your weight, your name, your worries, as the glow far from the sun. - The picture breaks: the sun is far away; He is right here and still unseen. Stop there, at why. - Say: "Next to You, I am light in the sun." - Let one thing you were holding on to hard go quiet today.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the self softens without shame and there is more room for Him. The fake is self hatred dressed as humility, or a dreamy sense that nothing is real, which makes a person careless rather than quiet.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 3 (line 800): לא היה גשמיות הנברא וחומרו וממשו נראה כלל לעינינו*

## Chapter 4 · The sun and its shield

**The state.** The state of *tzimtzum*, contraction and hiding, seen from the right side: the world seems to stand on its own because He holds Himself back, and that holding back is His power. *Gevurah*, might, is the strength that hides so that a creature can exist.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Chapter 3 left a question: if everything is always inside its source, why isn't it wiped out? The answer starts with a verse: "For the sun and a shield is G-d, Elokim." The shield is the sun's sheath, what lets creatures bear it. The sages said that in the time to come G-d will take the sun out of its sheath. As a sheath shields the sun, so the name *Elokim* shields the name *Havayah*.

*Havayah*, the four letter Name, means "He brings all into being from nothing." Its form is the ongoing present, like "so Job would do, all the days." It is the life poured into every creature every moment, making it now. It is not enough that things were made once.

Then two of His traits. "The Great" is *chesed*, kindness: the spreading of life into worlds and creatures without end, out of free kindness, because it is the nature of the good to do good. Only He can make something from nothing, and no created mind can grasp how. He and His traits are simply one, so just as no mind can grasp Him, no mind can grasp His traits.

The same is true of "the Mighty," *gevurah*. This is His power to hold back the spreading of life, so that it does not show openly on creatures but works behind a hidden face. The life hides in the creature's body. It seems as if the body were a thing on its own, not a spreading of life like light from the sun.

In truth it is not a thing on its own. But that is His might: the All Powerful holds back and hides the life from His mouth, so that the creature's body is not wiped out in it. No created mind can grasp what this hiding is, any more than it can grasp creation from nothing. (The chapter adds a bracket in Kabbalah terms: the hiding is called "vessels" and the life "light," and it traces the vessels to five letters and five forces of might. I have left that as one sentence.)

What is it like from inside? This is the state we mostly live in. The world feels solid, separate, self running. The chapter does not say that feeling is a lie we should despise. It says the feeling itself is being made. The very sense "I am here, on my own" is His strength at work. The shield is there so you can stand in the sun and not vanish. So the solidity of the world is not a wall against Him. It is His holding back, for your sake.

**The movement.** From taking the world's solidity as proof that He is far, to seeing that solidity as His might, a hiding done on purpose. What carries you is knowing the two names and their jobs: Havayah makes, Elokim hides the making.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "He hid so I could be." - Think of the sun without a sheath, too strong to bear. Then of the sheath, letting you stand here. - Bring it down to one hard, heavy fact of your day. Its hardness is the sheath, not the absence of the sun. - The picture breaks: a sheath is a thing apart from the sun; His hiding is Himself. Stop at the edge of "how can He hide from Himself." - Say: "You made room for me by hiding. Thank You." - Meet one hard fact today without resentment toward it.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the plain world stops feeling like His absence and starts feeling like His care. The fake is a clever idea that "it's all G-d hiding," used to shrug off what the world asks of you.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 4 (line 808): וכאילו גוף הנברא הוא דבר בפני עצמו*

## Chapter 5 · Seams of light

**The state.** The state in which, inside a hidden world, the hiddenness breaks and something of Him shows: the *giluy*, revealing, that comes through tzaddikim and the Torah's wonders. With it, the state of the soul after this life that feeds on knowing Him.

**The chapter as consciousness.** A short chapter. The sages said: "At first it rose in thought to create the world with strict justice. He saw the world could not last, so He joined mercy to it." Strict justice here is the holding back of chapter 4. A world only of hiding would not last. So mercy was joined: G-dliness revealed through the righteous, and through the signs and wonders in the Torah. Here and there, the shield opens a crack.

From inside, this is the moment a person meets someone whose life shows G-d plainly, or a story from the Torah that does not fit inside nature. For a moment the self running world has a seam in it, and light comes through.

Then the Zohar: "Above, on the side of holiness, there is a right and a left," kindness and might. Both are G-d's own traits, above any created mind, one with Him. Even Moshe, the greatest prophet, did not see them as they are. He saw them only clothed in lower worlds and lower traits. (The chapter names the levels; I have compressed them.)

So what is it we cannot grasp now? The chapter says the reward of the righteous in the Garden of Eden is grasping the spread of life and light from these two traits. It is the food of souls who learned Torah for its own sake. From these two traits a sky is stretched over the souls, called "the secret of Torah," holding the secret of its twenty two letters. From that sky drips dew, and the dew is knowing. Torah is the food of the soul there, and the mitzvot are its clothing.

Where does this leave a person now? It shows what learning is for. What we learn here in the dark, word by word, is the same thing the soul will one day taste openly. The knowing is the same; only the veil differs.

**The movement.** From a sealed world to a world with seams, and from learning as information to learning as food. What carries you is paying attention to the places where G-d shows, and learning Torah for its own sake.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "He joined mercy to it." - Think of one person or one story where G-d showed through plainly. - Bring it down: that seam is mercy, done so the world could last, and done for you. - The picture breaks: even Moshe saw it clothed. Stop there, content to see through a garment. - Say: "Let me see You where You show Yourself." - Learn one line of Torah today as food, slowly.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a holy person or a wonder makes you want more of Him, not more of the wonder. The fake is chasing signs and miracles as a thrill, with no change in how you live.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 5 (line 812): התגלות אלהות על ידי צדיקים ואותות ומופתים שבתורה*

## Chapter 6 · The hiding is kindness too

**The state.** The state of *Havayah hu haElokim*, "G-d is Elokim": seeing that the name that hides and the name that makes are one, so nature, *hateva*, is not a second thing next to Him. From there, *ein od*, nothing else, not even as an add on.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The name *Elokim* is the name of might and holding back. In the Hebrew counting of letters it equals *hateva*, "nature." Because it hides the light that makes the world, it looks as if the world stands and runs by nature. This Elokim is the shield over Havayah, hiding the making so creatures are not wiped out.

Then the turn. This might, this holding back, is also kindness. "The world is built by kindness," and the hiding is what lets it be built. So it is might held inside kindness. The traits are mixed into each other, and that shows they are one with Him. Because they are one with Him, they join with each other. Elijah said: "You are the One who ties them and unites them, and apart from You there is no unity above."

Now the verse reads itself. "Bring it back to your heart that Havayah is Elokim": the two names are one. Even the name that hides is kindness like the name that makes. His traits are His names, and He and His name are one.

So "in the heavens above and on the earth below there is nothing else." Even the heavy earth that everyone sees as fully real is nothing before Him. The name Elokim hides only from those below, not from Him, since He and Elokim are one. So before Him the earth is not even called by a name. Not even *od*, "also," the word for something that tags along, the way the sages say "Yehudah, and also (*od*) Levi" and the way the body tags along after the soul. (The chapter adds a lovely reading: "I will praise G-d with my life, I will sing to my G-d with my *od*." Life comes from Havayah; the *od*, the body that tags along, comes from Elokim.) A soul does not make its body from nothing. But He makes everything from nothing, so everything is nothing next to Him, like light in the sun.

That is why the verse had to warn us. Do not think that heaven and earth are a separate thing and that G-d fills the world the way a soul fills a body, moving it and steering it. That picture fails completely. Soul and body really are separate at the root. The body comes from father and mother, and grows from food. Heaven and earth come from nothing at all, only from His word, which still stands and pours into them every moment. They are like the light inside the sun.

Only His might hides this, so that heaven and earth look like things of their own. But the hiding is only for those below. Before Him "everything is as nothing." His might does not hide anything from Him, because it is not a thing apart. "G-d is Elokim."

From inside, this is the state in which nature stops being the rival. A person no longer feels torn between "the real world" and "G-d." The real world is His hiding, and His hiding is His kindness.

**The movement.** From "G-d and nature, two things" to "Havayah is Elokim, one." What carries you is the verse itself, thought through word by word, and seeing that the soul and body picture, the one most people use, does not fit.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Havayah is Elokim." - Think of the laws of nature you rely on: gravity, the seasons, the body. That is Elokim, His hiding. - Bring it to one natural thing that worries you. It is not a force apart from Him. It is His kindness in a closed hand. - The picture breaks: a soul uses a body it did not make; He makes the body from nothing. Stop there. - Say: "Even what hides You is You." - Do one ordinary task today without calling it "just nature."

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you stop feeling that you must choose between the world and G-d. The fake is using "it's all one" to call nothing wrong and nothing required.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 6 (line 822): אך אין הצמצום וההסתר אלא לתחתונים*

## Chapter 7 · As much down here as up there

**The state.** Two states of oneness. *Yichuda ila'ah*, the higher unity: only He is, and the world is nothing next to Him. *Yichuda tata'ah*, the lower unity: He is right here, inside space and time, as fully on the earth below as in the heavens above. The chapter also names the fake of both: a G-d who has pulled back and watches from far off.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Zohar says the first line of the Shema, "Hear, Israel, G-d is One," is the higher unity, and the line after it, "Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever," is the lower unity. (The word for "forever," *va'ed*, has letters that swap into *echad*, "one.")

Why is there a hiding at all? Everyone knows the point of the world is that His kingship be shown. "There is no king without a people." The Hebrew for people, *am*, is related to a word for dim, far off things. A king is only a king over those who are separate from him, strangers, far below him. A man with many children is not their king, even with very many. Not even princes alone make a king. "In the multitude of people is the king's glory." So the world has to feel separate. That feeling is not an accident. It is what makes a people for the King.

His kingship, *malchut*, has its own name, *Adnut*, "Lord," as in "Lord of all the earth." This trait and this name are what make the world stand as a full "something," separate, not wiped out. Without them the world would go back into His word and be nothing there. Not even called a world.

What is a "world"? It is space and time. Space: east, west, north, south, up, down. Time: past, present, future. None of the higher traits touch these. Only kingship does: He is King above without end and below without end, and "G-d is King, G-d was King, G-d will be King." So the life of space and of time, their coming from nothing and their lasting, comes from His kingship.

And since His kingship is one with His very Self, space and time too are nothing next to Him, like light in the sun. So the two names are woven together. Havayah is above time: He was, is and will be in one moment. He is above space: He is making all of it. Yet He is also found below, in space and time, because He unites with His kingship, from which space and time come. That is the lower unity. His very Self, called *Ein Sof*, the Endless, fills this earth in time and space. Heaven and earth and the four directions are all full of the light of Ein Sof equally. It is as much on the earth below as in the heavens above. Kingship is what hides this, so that space and time do not vanish, even for us.

From inside, the lower unity is the most surprising state in the book. It is not a flight up. It is looking at the corner of your kitchen and knowing the Endless is there exactly as much as anywhere.

Then the chapter goes deeper. "I, G-d, have not changed." As He was alone before the world was made, so He is alone after. Not changed in Himself, and not in His knowing either. The Rambam says: He is the knower, the known, and the knowing, all one, and no mouth can say it, no ear hear it, no heart know it fully. A person's knowing is added onto him. He was there before he learned a thing; then the knowledge joined him. That is a mixed oneness. G-d is simple oneness. His Self and His knowing are one thing. So just as no one can grasp His Self, no one can grasp His knowing. We can only believe, with a faith above the mind, that by knowing Himself He knows everything, down to the smallest worm in the sea, and this adds nothing to Him.

Here the chapter names a mistake made by some scholars. They read the Arizal's word *tzimtzum*, contraction, literally, as if G-d took Himself out of this world and now only watches from above. The Tanya answers twice. A literal contraction is something bodies do; He is endlessly apart from that. And these same people believe He knows everything here. His knowing is His Self. So He is here. "There is no place empty of Him, not above and not below."

This mistaken state is worth naming as a state. It is the religious person's version of the silversmith. G-d is real, and He cares, but He is up there. The world down here is empty of Him. It feels pious and it is lonely.

The Zohar adds: "He holds everything and nothing holds Him." No mind can hold Him. And though He fills all worlds, He is not like a soul in a body. A soul is caught in its body. It feels the body's pain, cold and heat. He is not changed by the world's changes, summer to winter, day to night. "Darkness is not dark to You."

So what does "surrounding all worlds," *sovev*, mean? When a person thinks about something, his thought surrounds the picture of it in his mind, not the thing itself. His knowing surrounds each creature in fact, because His knowing is its life and its coming into being now. And "filling all worlds," *memale*, is the life dressed inside each thing, narrowed to fit its size. The sun is limited in size and in how far it shines, because it is created. So the life inside it had to be narrowed a great deal. The breath of His mouth could have spread without end and made endless worlds. He narrowed it into letter combinations and number values, each a step down, until even a stone could hold a tiny bit of it. (The chapter traces the stone's name to a Divine name plus one letter; I have left the details.) That tiny bit is the stone's soul, making it every moment.

**The movement.** From "G-d is up there, watching," to "He is here, as much as there." What carries you is the Shema and its second line said with understanding, the reasoning about knowing (His knowing is His Self, and He knows this place, so He is in this place), and then faith where the reasoning ends.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "As much on the earth below as in the heavens above." - Think of the place where you are sitting: its walls, its hour on the clock. Space and time are His kingship. - Bring it to one small spot in front of you. The Endless is there exactly as much as in heaven. - The picture breaks: a soul is caught in its body; He is not caught. Stop at "how can He fill and not be held." - Say, slowly: "Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever." - Do one thing in that very spot as if it were a sanctuary.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when "here" stops feeling empty and ordinary places carry weight. The fake is the pious distance, G-d kept safe in heaven, or a feeling that everything is G-d that erases the King and His people.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 7 (line 830): כי כך הוא בארץ מתחת כמו בשמים ממעל ממש*

## Chapter 8 · The ladder inside you

**The state.** Knowing your own inner ladder, from *chochmah*, the first flash of wisdom, down through understanding and knowing, to feeling, thought, speech and act, and seeing that each lower step is almost nothing next to the one above it. And the state of seeing that He is not a larger copy of that ladder.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Rambam said His Self and His knowing are one simple thing. The chapter says this is true of every trait and name given to Him: gracious, merciful, kind, wise, His will ("G-d wants those who fear Him," "He desires kindness," "He wants the wicked to return, not to die"). None of these adds anything to Him. They are all one simple oneness, which is His very Self.

And the Rambam said the mouth cannot say it and the heart cannot know it. Why? Because a person pictures everything he wants to understand the way it is in himself. When he tries to picture will, wisdom, mercy, he pictures his own. But G-d is holy, which means separate, endlessly, above every kind of praise a mind can form.

Then the chapter turns inward and draws the human ladder. It is worth standing on each rung.

First is *chochmah*, wisdom, called "the beginning," because it is the source of all life in a creature. From it come understanding and knowing. From those come all the feelings of the thinking soul, love, kindness, mercy. You can see it plainly. A small child without understanding is angry all the time and cruel, and loves little things not worth loving, because love follows understanding. From the feelings come the letters of thought: a person thinks about what he loves, or how to do the kindness. Every thought has some feeling inside it, which is its life. From thought come the letters of speech. Speech leads to the act, as a king tells his servants to give.

And each rung is almost nothing next to the one above. The soul's power in a deed is like nothing next to its power in speech, like a body next to a soul. So too speech next to thought, thought next to the feeling inside it, the feeling next to the mind it came from. This is true of every soul in every world. Wisdom is always the first source of life.

From inside, this is a clarifying state. You see where your hands get their life: from your words, from your thoughts, from what you feel, from what you understand. You see why a person with little understanding loves little things. And you see that to change the lower rungs you work on the higher ones.

**The movement.** From picturing G-d as "me, but bigger," to seeing your own ladder clearly, and then seeing that He is not on it. What carries you is looking honestly at how your own mind makes your feelings, words and deeds.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "A person pictures it as it is in himself." - Take one kind act you did this week. Trace it up: the act, the words, the thought, the feeling, the understanding it came from. - See how small the act is next to the understanding that moved it. - The picture breaks: when you picture G-d's mercy, you are picturing yours. Stop there. - Say: "You are not like my mind. Teach me to love what is worth loving." - Before one deed today, go up the ladder and think first.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you trust understanding more than mood, and your love grows up toward things worth loving. The fake is a G-d made in your image, merciful the way you are merciful, angry the way you are angry.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 8 (line 850): כי האדם מצייר בשכלו כל המושכלות שרוצה להשכיל ולהבין*

## Chapter 9 · Like touching an idea with your hands

**The state.** The mind at its own wall, knowing that He is not a very deep idea but beyond the whole category of grasping. What is left there is *emunah*, faith above the mind, which the Zohar calls *raza dimeheimnuta*, "the secret of faith."

**The chapter as consciousness.** For us, wisdom is the top rung. For G-d, wisdom, the very start of thought, is like the last rung, the deed. "You made them all in wisdom." In other words: as the life in a physical act is to the life in wisdom (five rungs apart: deed, speech, thought, feeling, mind), so wisdom, the source of all life in all worlds, is to Him, except that He is raised above wisdom not by five rungs but by countless thousands of rungs without end. We say "wisdom is like a deed to Him" only because the only ladder we can see is from wisdom down to the deed.

Then comes the sharpest picture in the book. You might want to praise Him by saying no creature can grasp Him because He is so deep. But grasping belongs to the world of ideas. You can say an idea can be grasped, or cannot because it is too deep. He is above ideas. To say "He can't be grasped" is like saying of a deep and lofty idea that it can't be touched with your hands because it is so deep. Anyone who heard would laugh. Touch belongs to things you hold in your hand. And to Him, all understanding, even that of the highest beings, even the highest wisdom that gives life to all, is like a thing held in the hand.

So from inside this state, a person stops straining. Straining assumes that with enough effort the mind could reach. Here the mind sees it is the wrong organ, like trying to taste a song. What is left is not frustration but a quiet: I do not grasp Him, and "not grasping" is not even the right word.

Why then is He called "wise" or "merciful"? Because He is the source of wisdom and mercy. They flow from Him. How exactly they flow, the chapter says, is known to those who study it. (A gloss adds Kabbalah terms: after the Endless light is clothed in the vessels of wisdom, understanding and knowledge, one can say "He is the knower and the known." Before that clothing, one cannot even say that. I have compressed it to this line.)

"We have no business with hidden things," only with what is revealed to us: to believe with full faith that He and His traits are one. How He unites with them cannot be grasped either. That is why the Zohar calls His traits "the secret of faith," a faith that is above the mind.

**The movement.** From trying to grasp Him, and feeling that failure as a problem, to a faith that stands where understanding ends. What carries you is thinking all the way to the wall, not stopping short, and seeing the mind's limit clearly enough that you laugh at the idea of pushing through it.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Like touching an idea with your hands." - Think of the deepest idea you know. Now try to touch it with your fingers. See why that is foolish. - Bring it down: that is what "I can't understand G-d" really is. - The picture breaks: even "not understanding" belongs to the mind. Stop there, and do not push. - Say: "I believe You are one with all You are." - Do one mitzvah today without needing to understand it first.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the mind rests at its edge with something like joy, and faith feels solid rather than second best. The fake is lazy faith that never did the thinking, or a sense of "mystery" used to avoid learning.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 9 (line 858): שאי אפשר למששה בידים מפני עומק המושג*

## Chapter 10 · A picture, and "not really like this"

**The state.** The state of holding a picture of G-d loosely: using it to understand a little and saying at once "but not really like this." With it, the state of seeing each day of the world as one of His traits shown outward.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "The Torah speaks in human language," to soothe the ear with what it can hear. So the masters of Kabbalah were allowed to speak of His traits, the *sefirot*, as "lights," a picture so we can understand a little how He and His traits are one. Think of the sun. The body of the sun is called the source of light. The glow spreading from it is called light. Inside the sun, the light is one with its source completely. There is only one thing there, the shining body. The light has no being of its own.

His traits and will and wisdom are one with His Self like that, and more. They came from Him the way light spreads from the sun. "But not really in this way. Only in a way far off and wondrous beyond our grasp, for His ways are higher than ours."

This is a posture of the mind. You need a picture, or you have nothing. And you must drop it, or it becomes an idol. The state is holding both at once: a clear picture and a steady "not really."

Still, to soothe the ear, we can learn from the sun. Light in its source has no name of its own. So too His traits are not called by their names at all, except toward creatures. He makes them and runs them by His will, wisdom, understanding and knowing, clothed in His traits. "With wisdom He founded the earth, with understanding He set the heavens." Elijah said: "You brought out ten forms and called them ten sefirot, to lead hidden worlds and revealed worlds, and in them You hid."

Then the chapter walks through creation as traits being shown. On the first day kindness was revealed, and He made light with "Let there be light." Light spreads, from one end of the world to the other, which is kindness. But because kindness held some might in it, the light was not as spiritual as the light above, and it was held inside a limited world. On the second day might was revealed, and He made the sky, "dividing water from water." That is holding back: hiding the higher spiritual waters from the lower ones, which became physical by being cut off. But kindness was inside that too, "the world is built by kindness," so that dry land could appear and a person could stand on it and serve G-d. And so for each day.

Elijah ends it: all this is "to show how the world is led by justice and judgment," but "not that You have a known justice that is strict, or a known judgment that is mercy, and not any of these traits at all."

So from inside: you look at light and see kindness, at a boundary and see might wrapped in kindness. And at once you add: this is how He shows Himself, not what He is.

**The movement.** From either having no picture of G-d or clinging to one, to holding a picture and letting it go in the same breath. What carries you is the sun picture followed by the sentence "but not really in this way."

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "But not really in this way." - Think of sunlight inside the sun, one with it. So are His traits with Him. - Bring it down to one thing you see today: light is kindness shown, a wall is might for your sake. - The picture breaks: this is how He shows, not what He is. Say "not really" and stop. - Say: "You showed me this so I could know You a little." - Use one boundary in your life today, a limit or a no, as kindness to someone.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when pictures of G-d help you and never fence Him in. The fake is either a fixed image you defend, or refusing every picture and so thinking of nothing.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 10 (line 863): אך לא ממש בדרך זה, רק בדרך רחוקה ונפלאה מהשגתינו*

## Chapter 11 · What was hidden in the heart, spoken

**The state.** The state of hearing the world as speech: *ma'amar*, a saying, His inner traits made known outward the way a person's words show what was hidden in his heart. And the state of knowing that your own soul comes from above even that speech, from His breath.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The ten sayings of creation are called "sayings" only in relation to creatures. The chapter builds this from the ladder of chapter 8. A person's kindness cannot come out in a real act unless he first thinks it through: what to give, how. There is no act without thought. And if he tells others to do it, like a king, then the kindness and the thought both dress in the letters of speech.

So too, as a picture, when His traits come to act on those below, that coming out is called "a saying" and "letters." Nothing can flow from His traits without such combinations. When light was made from kindness, the power drawn out to make it is called the saying "Let there be light." These are not letters like our thoughts. But they show how light came from nothing. That is why this power made light and not water, though water also comes from kindness. A different combination was dressed in the water.

There are two kinds of worlds. Hidden worlds come from hidden flows, like the letters of thought in a person. Revealed worlds come from those flows when they are shown, called "sayings," "the word of G-d," "the breath of His mouth," like the letters of speech in a person, "which show the listener what was hidden and sealed in his heart."

This is the heart of the state. The world is His speech, and speech is disclosure. Something inside is being told outward. From inside, a person who stands here does not see a stage set. He hears someone telling him something, through the sky, through the light, through the ground.

Then the chapter warns against taking "speech" to mean something low. His speech is far above created wisdom. Man was made by the saying "Let us make man in our image," and man has wisdom. Or by the breath alone: "He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life." So the speech and the breath above are the source of the wisdom in the soul of the first man, which held all the souls of the righteous, who are greater than the ministering angels. His letters are flows from His traits, which are one with Him, so they are called "letters" not in relation to creatures, but in relation to His traits themselves.

They are twenty two kinds of flow, no more and no less, as He wished, the twenty two letters set in the mouth and tongue. Even a person's letters of thought and speech are flows from his own mind and heart, from his very self.

**The movement.** From a silent world of objects to a world that speaks, and from hearing it as noise to hearing it as someone telling you what was in His heart. What carries you is the comparison to your own speech: you know what it is to say what you felt.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "What was hidden in the heart, spoken." - Think of a time you said something you had kept inside for a long time. - Now look at one real thing, the morning light, and hear it as said: His kindness spoken outward. - The picture breaks: your words come from your mind; His words are above all mind. Stop there. - Say: "I hear You. Tell me what You want." - Let one of your own words today carry something true from your heart.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the world feels addressed to you, and you want to answer. The fake is reading private messages into every event, making the world talk about you instead of about Him.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 11 (line 872): שהן מגלות לשומעים מה שהיה צפון וסתום בלבו*

## Chapter 12 · Moonlight, down to the stone

**The state.** The state of seeing the lowest thing, a stone or a handful of dust, as still lit by Him through many reflections: *hearah dehearah*, a glow of a glow, light of light. The descent is complete, and nothing is left outside it.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Creatures differ, in kinds and in details, by how letters are combined, swapped and exchanged. Each letter is a flow of its own particular power. When many letters join into a word, there is not only the sum of their powers. There is something more, above them all: a higher, general power that equals and exceeds all of them, joins them together, and gives life to the world made by that word, as a whole and in its details. (A gloss adds that each letter's written shape shows the shape of its flow.)

From inside, this is how a whole thing feels. A tree is not only its parts. There is a oneness in it that ties the parts, and that oneness is the word it was spoken with.

The example is the sky. The words of "Let there be a sky" made seven heavens and all their hosts. The sages named them: one where millstones grind manna for the righteous, one where the heavenly Jerusalem and Temple and altar stand, one with storehouses of snow and hail. The heavens as a whole come from those words as a whole. Each particular thing in them comes from some combination of those letters, or their swaps. In every combination the first letter leads and the rest follow and are held in its light, and so a new creature is made. By swapping letters, lower creatures are made.

Then the last picture. It is like light on the earth at night from the moon. The moon's light is from the sun. So the light on the earth is the light of the light of the sun. So too the letters in the sayings are the whole flow of life and light from His traits, making the worlds and keeping them as long as He wills. From that great light He drew out offspring and branches, the swaps and exchanges of letters, and made the particular creatures of each world. Then a glow of a glow of a glow, down and down, "until the silent things were made, stones and dust." Their names, *even* and *afar*, are swaps of swaps and exchanges of exchanges.

What is it like to stand here? You look at a stone at the side of the road. It is as low as things get. It does not move, does not grow, does not feel. And it is lit. The light has bounced so many times it looks like no light at all, the way moonlight looks like its own thing. But it is the sun's. The gate of unity began with a question, why warn a person that there is no god in the water under the earth? It ends at the bottom of the world, with the stone, and the answer is that even there, He is the only one speaking.

**The movement.** From a ladder that seems to stop somewhere above you, to seeing it reach all the way down to the dust. What carries you is the moonlight picture, followed to the ground.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Light of the light of the sun." - Think of moonlight on a road at night, and where it really comes from. - Take one stone or one bit of dust in your hand. It is a glow of a glow of His word, and it is being made now. - The picture breaks: moonlight is weaker than sunlight; here the source is fully present even in the dust. Stop there. - Say: "Even here, only You." - Treat one low, overlooked thing today with care.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when nothing feels too low or too plain to hold Him, and your respect reaches small things. The fake is treating objects as holy while treating people and duties carelessly.

*Source: Tanya, Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah ch. 12 (line 882): עד שנברא הדומם ממש כאבנים ועפר*

## Chapter 1 · Return is not the fast

**The state.** *Teshuvah*, return, as the Torah means it: *azivat hachet*, leaving the wrong, a whole hearted decision never to go back to it, and turning to G-d with all your heart. Its counterfeit is the belief that return means punishing yourself.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter opens with a teaching from the end of tractate Yoma. There are three kinds of atonement, and return goes with each. If a person failed to do a positive command and returns, he does not move from his place before he is forgiven. If he did something forbidden and returns, his return holds the matter open and Yom Kippur atones. If he did something whose penalty is *karet*, being cut off, or death by a court, return and Yom Kippur hold it open, and suffering finishes the cleaning.

In a long bracket the letter explains why the positive command, which is greater, is forgiven faster. Doing a mitzvah draws light into the worlds above and onto the soul. If you did not do it, you are forgiven for disobeying, but the light is missing. The sages said: "What is crooked cannot be made straight: this is one who skipped the evening Shema." From now on he may say it every night, but that one night stays unlit. Doing what is forbidden is different. The bad sticks to the soul and makes a flaw above, at its root, so the soul needs Yom Kippur, "before G-d you shall be pure." And do not learn from this to go easy on positive commands, least of all Torah study.

Then comes the center of the chapter. The mitzvah of return, from the Torah, is only this: leaving the wrong. A person decides in his heart, with a whole heart, never again to go back to his folly and rebel against the King. That is what the word means: to return to G-d with all your heart and soul, to serve Him and keep His commands. "Let the wicked leave his way, and return to G-d."

"Not as the common crowd thinks, that return is the fast."

What is it like to stand here? It is a turn of the will. It is not a mood and not a penalty. From inside it feels like facing a new direction: I will not do that again, and I am His. There is no need to earn the right to turn. The turn itself is the mitzvah.

What about the suffering in the teaching? Even for the gravest wrongs, it is G-d who brings the suffering. "I will visit with the rod," says the verse; He does the visiting. And when the return is from love, with all the heart, the awakening from below wakes an awakening above, "as water shows a face to a face." His love is aroused, and He cleans the wrong with small sufferings in this world. "Whom G-d loves, He corrects."

That is why the Rambam and the Semag, when they list the laws of return, mention no fast at all, even for the gravest wrongs. Only confession and asking forgiveness. The prophet Joel's call to "return with fasting and weeping" was to cancel a decree on the whole people, a plague of locusts, like all public fasts. And the many fasts in the books of mussar, for grave sins, have other reasons: to be spared suffering sent from above, to speed the cleaning, and perhaps because the person returned out of fear and not out of love.

**The movement.** From guilt that looks for pain, to a clean decision of the will and a turning of the heart toward Him. What carries you is knowing what the mitzvah actually is: leaving the wrong, confessing it, asking forgiveness.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Return is leaving the wrong, with a whole heart." - Think of one thing you did that you do not want to do again. Name it plainly. - Decide, not in a feeling but in a sentence: I will not go back to this. - The picture breaks: pain feels like payment, but He did not ask for your pain. Stop there. - Say: "I did this. I am sorry. I am coming back to You." - Do one thing today that belongs to the new direction.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the decision is quiet and firm and the heart turns toward Him, not toward yourself. The fake is self punishment that feels serious and changes nothing, or a fast used in place of deciding.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 1 (line 895): ולא כדעת ההמון, שהתשובה היא התענית*

## Chapter 2 · Forgiven, and wanted again

**The state.** The state past forgiveness: *ritzui*, being wanted again, dear before Him as before the wrong. It is the longing to see the King's face, and the acts, a fast in place of an offering, that express it.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Everything in chapter 1 is about atonement and pardon. When a person returns fully, he is completely forgiven for going against the King's word. On the day of judgment not a word or half a word is mentioned to punish him. He is fully free.

But there is another place a person wants to be. To be wanted before G-d, pleasing and dear to Him as before the wrong, so that his service brings his Maker pleasure again. For that, even for a light positive command with no great penalty, a person used to bring an *olah*, a burnt offering, "and it will be accepted for him." The Talmud says the olah is a gift brought after return, after the pardon.

The picture is a man who offended a king. He sent advocates, the king was appeased and pardoned him. Still, he sends the king a gift, so that the king will be willing to see his face again.

This is a real difference in the heart. Being let off is one state. Being welcome is another. A person who is forgiven can still feel the door is only half open. He does not want only to be spared. He wants to come in.

Now that there are no offerings, a fast takes their place. The Talmud has a prayer: "May the fat and blood I lost today be as if I offered them before You." That is why many sages fasted a great deal over small things. Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah did not stop his neighbor's cow from going out on Shabbat in a way the other sages forbade, and he fasted until his teeth turned dark. Rabbi Yehoshua once said "I am ashamed of your words, House of Shammai," and fasted until his teeth turned dark. Rav Huna's tefillin strap once flipped over, and he fasted forty fasts. And the Arizal taught his students a number of fasts for many wrongs, even ones with no grave penalty: for anger, for a rabbinic prohibition, for skipped prayer. (The chapter gives the numbers; I have left them out.)

In general, the chapter says, a fast is a wondrous way to bring out the supernal will, like an offering, "a pleasing scent to G-d." Isaiah calls a fast "a day of will for G-d." A fast that is accepted is a day of His wanting.

So the fast is moved to its true place. It is not the return. It is a gift after the return, from someone who is already forgiven and wants to be close.

**The movement.** From "I am off the hook" to "I want Him to want me." What carries you is the gift, an act of giving something of yourself after the return, not before it.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Dear to Him as before." - Think of someone who forgave you, and how you still wanted to bring them something. - Bring it to G-d: you are pardoned; now ask for more, to be welcome. - The picture breaks: a king needs nothing; He wants the giving itself. Stop there. - Say: "Let me be dear to You again." - Give one gift today, something that costs you a little, as a present to Him.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when after returning you feel a pull toward Him, not just relief. The fake is gifts and fasts used to buy a pardon you never asked for with your heart.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 2 (line 900): שיהיה לרצון לפני ה׳ ומרוצה וחביב לפניו יתברך כקודם החטא*

## Chapter 3 · Don't break the body; give instead

**The state.** The state of a person who wants closeness badly enough to do hard things, held inside wisdom: *ba'al nefesh hachafetz kirvat Hashem*, "a person of soul who wants closeness to G-d," who is also forbidden to harm himself, and turns what he cannot bear into *tzedakah*, giving.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Now a practical question. What if a person did the same wrong many times? Later teachers of mussar disagreed. Some said he must multiply the fasts by the number of times, like a sin offering, one for each time. Others compared it to the burnt offering, one for many. The accepted ruling was to fast three times the number for that wrong, because the Zohar says the first time a person sins it leaves a mark, and by the third time the stain spreads from side to side.

Then the letter turns, and this turn is the heart of the chapter. All this is for a person who is strong and healthy, whom many fasts do not harm at all, as in earlier generations. But one whom many fasts could harm, who could come to illness or pain, as in our generations, is forbidden to fast much, even for the gravest wrongs. Even long ago, only the healthy fasted like this. The Talmud calls one who fasts when he cannot bear it "a sinner," even when he fasts for his sins. And a person who learns Torah is doubly at fault, since the weakness from fasting keeps him from learning properly.

So what can he do? "Redeem your sin with charity." For each day of fasting, he gives a set amount (the chapter names a sum in the coins of its time), and a rich person gives more.

From inside, this is a quieter state than it sounds. A person who has done wrong often wants to hurt. The letter says no. Your body is not the thing to break. Give instead.

Yet the letter does not let the fire go out. Any person of soul who wants closeness to G-d, who wants to mend his soul and bring it back in the finest way, should be strict with himself to complete, at least once in his life, the number of fasts for each grave wrong. It names one: wasting seed, a sin in the area of intimacy that it counts as grave, with eighty four fasts once in a lifetime. And it makes this gentle. He can put them on the short days of winter, ten or fewer in one winter, finishing over nine years or more, as his strength allows. He can eat a little before dawn if he said so beforehand. For the rest of the count he can fast half days. And every fast beyond this, and all the fasts for lesser wrongs, down to skipped positive commands and Torah study, he redeems with charity if he cannot bear them.

Even if the sum grows large, he need not worry about the rule "do not give away more than a fifth." This is not waste. It is redeeming himself from fasts and pain, no less than paying for the body's healing. That is why, the letter says, all who tremble at G-d's word now give a great deal of charity, because the generation is too weak to afflict itself so much.

**The movement.** From the wish to punish yourself, to wanting closeness and acting on it within what your body can carry, and giving to others what you cannot suffer. What carries you is the rule itself, and generosity.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Whoever wants closeness to G-d." - Think of the wrong that still weighs on you, and the part of you that wants to pay for it with pain. - Bring it down: your health is not yours to spend; your money and your time can be given. - The picture breaks: charity is not a price He charges; it is how the wanting comes out. Stop there. - Say: "I want to be close to You. Take this gift." - Give tzedakah today, a set amount, for the thing you want to mend.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the longing for closeness stays strong and your body and your family are not harmed by it. The fake is harsh self denial that feels holy and leaves you too weak to learn, to pray and to be kind.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 3 (line 913): כל בעל נפש החפץ קרבת ה׳*

## Chapter 4 · Blown from the inside

**The state.** Knowing your soul as *chelek Havayah*, a portion of the four letter Name, breath blown from His inside and not only spoken from His mouth. It is also seeing the letters of that Name in the order of your own inner life, from the hidden flash of mind to the deed.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Everything so far, the chapter says, is about finishing the atonement and cleaning the soul after return. Now it goes to the beginning and the core of return: coming back to G-d truly, with a whole heart. That needs a long explanation.

The Zohar reads *teshuvah* as *tashuv hei*, "the hei returns." There are two hei letters in the Name. So there is a lower return and a higher return. The Zohar also says in places that return does not help one who damaged his covenant by wasting seed. That is very strange, since "nothing stands before return," not even the worst sins. Reishit Chochmah explains: the lower return is not enough there; the higher return is needed.

To understand this, the letter asks another question. The Torah says one who earns *karet* dies before fifty. Yet in every generation there are such people who live long and pleasant lives. How?

The answer begins with the soul. "His people is a portion of G-d," a portion of the Name Havayah. "He blew into his nostrils the breath of life," and "one who blows, blows from within himself." The Torah speaks in human language, and the picture is this. When a person speaks, the breath that leaves his mouth carries only a little of his life, an outer layer. When he blows, with force, the breath carries his inner life. So too, with an endless difference, above. The heavens and even the angels were made from nothing by the outer flow of His life, called "the breath of His mouth," the letters of the ten sayings. That is why angels are called *elohim* in the Torah, since that name is the outer side. The human soul came first from the inner flow, "He blew," and only then came down through the letters of "Let us make man" to enter a body. So the soul is a portion of Havayah, the Name of the inner life.

Then the Name itself. Its four letters hold all ten sefirot. The *yud*, a single point, is hidden wisdom before it opens. The little thorn on top of the yud is His will, higher still. The first *hei* is wide and long: wisdom spreading into understanding, and flowing down. The *vav*, a line down, is the flow through His six traits of the heart. The last *hei* is His kingship, "the word of G-d." Its root is breath rising from the heart and dividing into the five places of the mouth. The hei itself is only breath, "a light letter with no weight."

And the same letters are in you. Your hidden mind is the yud, able to come out and understand His truth and greatness, each person by his own measure. As you think deeply and broadly about His greatness, your understanding is the hei, wide and reaching down, giving birth to love and awe, first in the mind and the hidden heart, then in the open heart. From that comes true service, the vav and hei: learning and mitzvot in voice, speech and deed. And the thinking itself comes from Torah, which comes from wisdom, the yud.

From inside, this is an intimate state. Your soul is not something G-d said. It is something He breathed out of His own inside. And your inner life, from a thought to a deed, is spelled the way His Name is spelled.

**The movement.** From feeling like one more created thing to knowing your soul as breath from His inside. What carries you is the picture of blowing, and then tracing the Name through your own mind, heart and hands.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "One who blows, blows from within." - Think of the difference between a word said in passing and a breath pushed out from deep inside. The second carries more of you. - Bring it down: your soul is that inner breath from Him. Trace it: a flash of mind, the understanding, the love, the words, the deed. - The picture breaks: He has no body and no breath. Stop there. - Say: "I came from Your inside. Let me come back there." - Do one mitzvah today slowly, as the last letter of His Name spelled through you.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when your worth feels given from the inside and your wrongs feel like a wound to something precious. The fake is pride in a lofty soul, with no service coming out of it.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 4 (line 923): ביוצא בכח הנופח, דמתוכו נפח*

## Chapter 5 · The rope

**The state.** The state of *chevel*, the rope: feeling your soul tied above with its end reaching down into you, and knowing that nothing in the world stands between you and Him except acts against His will, which fray it, *pegam*, or cut it, *karet*.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The divine soul came down into the body from the inner source of speech, the higher breath, the lower hei. "He blew into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living soul."

"For G-d's portion is His people; Jacob is the rope (*chevel*) of His inheritance." A rope, the picture says, with one end tied above and the other end below.

Now think about blowing again. When you blow toward a place, if something stands in between, the breath does not reach. So too, if something stands between a person's body and the higher breath, it does not reach him.

But truly nothing physical or spiritual can stand in the way before Him. "I fill heaven and earth." "The whole earth is full of His glory." "There is no place empty of Him." "There is nothing else." So what could block it?

Only one thing: "Your sins have separated between you and your G-d." Why sins? Because they are against the supernal will, which gives life to everything, "all that G-d wished, He did." That will is the thorn on the yud, the root of the flow of the Name. A wall cannot block Him. A will set against His will can.

From inside this is a strange and important state. It is the discovery that distance from G-d is never a matter of place, or of how physical your life is, or of how busy you are. None of those stand between. What stands between is only what goes against what He wants.

That is *karet*, being cut off: the rope from the Name is cut. "That soul will be cut off from before Me," precisely "from before Me." Other wrongs, without karet, still damage the soul. The word *pegam*, flaw, is the word for a nick in a knife. Think of a thick rope made of 613 thin cords, one for each mitzvah. When a person goes against one of them, a thin cord snaps.

And even one who earns karet still keeps a trace, a *reshimu*, of his divine soul. That trace is what lets him live until fifty or sixty and no more. (A note about the Arizal is left out.) The rope is never fully gone.

**The movement.** From feeling G-d is far because of where you are, to knowing that only acts against His will make distance, and so you can see exactly where the rope is frayed. What carries you is the rope picture, cord by cord.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Tied above, reaching down." - Picture a thick rope of many thin cords, one end above, the other end in your hand. - Bring it down: which one cord did you fray this week? Name the one act. - The picture breaks: nothing else can stand between you and Him, not your job, not your body, not this room. Stop there. - Say: "Only my will against Yours separates us. I let it go." - Mend that one cord today by doing the opposite act once.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when distance from G-d feels specific and fixable, not vague and heavy. The fake is blaming the world, the body or life's busyness for a distance your own acts made.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 5 (line 939): כמו חבל, על דרך משל, שראשו אחד קשור למעלה וקצהו למטה*

## Chapter 6 · Pulling the rope, and bringing it home

**The state.** The state of seeing what a wrong act really does: *galut haShechinah*, the exile of the Presence. A person drags the life of his soul's root down into the "other side," *sitra achra*, and lives from there. And the state of *teshuvah tata'ah*, the lower return, which takes that flow back and brings it home.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Chapter 5 said the sinner lives only to fifty or sixty. That was true, the chapter says, when Israel stood high, when the Presence rested among them in the Temple. Then their bodies got life only through the divine soul, from the inner flow of the Name.

But after they came down from that level and by their acts caused the exile of the Presence, "for your sins your mother was sent away," the flow of the lower hei came down step by step, far down, until it was dressed in the mixed realm of *nogah*, "glow," which gives life through the stars and the forces over them to every living thing and plant in this world. "There is no blade of grass below without a star above it." Then even one who sins can get life for his body and his animal soul like any animal: "he is like the beasts."

More than that. The Zohar teaches that all the life a person receives at the very moment he does what is evil in G-d's eyes, in deed, speech or thought, comes to him from the chambers of the other side. A person has a choice. He can take his flow from the chambers of the other side, or from the chambers of holiness, from which all good and holy thoughts come. "This against that, G-d made." (The chapter places the other side's source in nogah, "the tree of knowledge," in Kabbalah terms; I have left it at that.)

This is hard to hear, and it should be said gently and plainly. At the moment of a wrong act, what is keeping you alive, the energy in the hand and the thought, is being taken from a place that is against Him. You are not cut off from life. You are drinking from the wrong well.

Now the rope again. "Jacob is the rope of His inheritance." If you pull the lower end of a rope, the upper end moves and follows as far as it can. So, as it were, a person by his bad acts and thoughts pulls down the flow of his soul's root, the lower hei, into the chambers of the other side, and from there he gets his thoughts and deeds. And because he is the one feeding them, he gets the choice portion. The sages said, "the ease of the wicked is not in our hands," in our time, in exile. That is the exile of the Presence: feeding the chambers of the other side, which His soul hates.

What is it like to see this? It is heavier than guilt. Guilt says "I did wrong." This says "I dragged something holy down with me." And it is also, strangely, a relief. Because if you pulled it down, you can pull it back.

"When a person does true return, he takes back from them the flow he drew down with his acts and thoughts. By his return he brings the flow of the Presence back to its place." That is *tashuv hei*, the lower hei returning from exile. "G-d will return with your captivity," the verse says, not "will bring back": He comes back with you.

**The movement.** From seeing a wrong as a private failure to seeing it as a pull on the rope that dragged the Presence down, and from there to return as bringing Him home. What carries you is honest seeing, then the choice of which well to drink from.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "By his return he brings the Presence back to its place." - Think of one wrong act. Ask: what was I living on in that moment, and where did it come from? - Bring it down: the energy in that act was holy life pulled into the wrong place. - The picture breaks: He is never truly dragged; "as it were," the chapter says. Stop there. - Say: "I pulled You down with me. I am bringing You back." - Today, at the moment the same pull comes, choose the other well once.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when your return feels like it is for Him, not only for you. The fake is a dramatic sense of cosmic ruin that crushes you and never becomes a choice.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 6 (line 956): כי בתשובתו מחזיר השפעת השכינה למקומה*

## Chapter 7 · The heart's cry, and an hour of honest counting

**The state.** The two states of *teshuvah tata'ah*, the lower return. First, *rachamim*, pity: a cry of compassion for your own soul, fallen "from a high roof into a deep pit," and for the Presence dragged down with it. Second, *lev nishbar*, a broken heart: the proud self pressed down to the dust, reached mainly by being a *mara dechushbana*, "a master of the reckoning," one who counts honestly.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The true and straight way to the lower return, the chapter says, is two things.

The first is to wake up the higher mercy, from the source of mercy, for your soul. It fell from the highest place, the Life of all life, into the deepest pit, the chambers of the unclean. And mercy for its source, the Name Havayah, whose flow came down into those chambers to keep them alive, through a person's acts, schemes and bad thoughts. "He returned to G-d, and He had mercy on him." The Song of Songs says "a king is held captive in the tresses," which the sages read as the running thoughts of the mind. That is the exile of the Presence.

The time for this is *Tikkun Chatzot*, the midnight lament, when one says "the crown of our head has fallen; woe to us, for we have sinned." That is why G-d is called "the insulted King" in the old texts. There is no insult greater. And when a person who thinks considers the greatness of the Endless, filling and surrounding all worlds, each as far as his mind can go, he will feel very bitter about it.

What is this like from inside? It is not self pity. It is pity for the holy thing in you, the way you would ache for a prince locked in a cellar. You are looking at your own soul from His side.

The second thing is to crush and humble the husk, the *kelipah*, the other side. Its whole life is coarseness and pride: "if you rise high as an eagle, from there I will bring you down." Being pressed to the dust is its death. That happens through "a broken and crushed heart," being "despised and lowly in his own eyes." "The offerings of G-d are a broken spirit": the spirit being broken is the unclean spirit.

How is the heart broken? A little, through fasting, in our generation that cannot fast much like King David. But the main thing is to be one of the "masters of the reckoning," in the depth of the mind. One hour each day or night, before Tikkun Chatzot, a person thinks deeply about what he did by his wrongs. He sent the Presence into exile. He tore his soul from the Life of life and brought it down to the place of impurity and death, and made it a chariot for the other side, taking its life from there for his body. That is why the sages said "the wicked are called dead while alive": their life comes from the place of death.

This hour is the center of the chapter as a state. It is not a vague bad feeling. It is counting. Calm, careful, in depth, for one set hour.

And even a person who did no grave wrong, only light ones, is not exempt. They too damage the soul, like thin cords snapping. Many of them can make a flaw like one grave sin, and so can one light wrong repeated many times. The prophet compared sins to clouds: "I have wiped away your transgressions like a thick cloud, and your sins like a cloud." The thick cloud is the grave wrongs, which darken the inner flow from the Name like a heavy dark cloud between the sun and the earth. The light cloud is "the small wrongs a person tramples with his heels." But if you put many thin, flimsy screens in a window, they darken it like one thick wall, or more. And some "small" wrongs the sages count as equal to idolatry, adultery and murder: turning your eyes from charity, speaking badly of others, anger, arrogance. Neglecting Torah study weighs against all of them.

That is why, in the Shema before sleep, one accepts upon oneself the four deaths of a court. (The chapter maps the four deaths to the four letters of the Name, and skipping Shema, tefillin, tzitzit and prayer to each letter; I have compressed it.) A thinking person can learn from this about all his other wrongs.

**The movement.** From numbness or vague guilt to two clear states: pity for the holy thing you pulled down, and a heart broken by honest reckoning. What carries you is a fixed hour of deep thinking about what the acts really did, and the time of midnight.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Be a master of the reckoning." - Set a time, before sleep or at midnight. Go over one day, act by act, and name what was a thin cloud and what was thick. - Bring it down to one act and ask: what did this do to the holy thing in me, and to Him? - The picture breaks: counting is not for despair; it is to break the pride that lives on the wrong. Stop at the ache. - Say: "Have mercy on my soul, and on Your Presence in it." - Tomorrow, give charity or hold back one harsh word, the "small" thing that mattered.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when your heart softens toward your own soul and your pride goes quiet. The fake is endless brooding that never becomes an hour with an end, or a broken heart that is really wounded pride.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 7 (line 966): להיות מ״מארי דחושבנא״ בעומק הדעת*

## Chapter 8 · Home, as before the breath left

**The state.** The state of *teshuvah shleimah*, full return: after the lower return, the soul washed clean rises back to its source and cleaves to Him, one with Him as it was before He breathed it out. This is the start of *teshuvah ila'ah*, the higher return.

**The chapter as consciousness.** After thinking deeply about all of chapter 7, a person can truly ask, from the depth of the heart, "By Your great mercy, wipe away my sins." Now it is not a formula. Now there is fixed in his heart a real and great pity for the G-dliness in his soul and above it.

And that pity wakes the higher mercy, the thirteen attributes of mercy. They come from the supernal will, the thorn on the yud, which is far above the flow that comes through the letters of the Name. That is why they can clean every flaw: "bearing sin and transgression, and cleansing." After that the outer forces and the other side can no longer feed from the lower hei. The lower hei returns to its place, joined to the other letters of the Name.

So too below, in the person's divine soul. "Your sins separate" is no longer true. "And cleansing": He cleanses those who return, washing their souls of the filthy clothes, the outer forces and the other side.

What is it like here? It is the moment after weeping, when the air is clear. Something heavy has been taken off, not by you. You did the asking. He did the washing.

"A wind passed and cleansed them." Then the soul can return all the way to Havayah and rise higher and higher to its source, and cleave to Him in a wondrous oneness, as it was one with Him before He breathed it out to go down and dress in a body. The picture is breath: before a person breathes out, the breath is one with him, inside him. That is full return.

This oneness is the higher return, which comes after the lower. The Zohar says the higher return is to engage in Torah with awe and love of the Holy One. And this is where those who return stand above the fully righteous: they draw near with a stronger longing of the heart and a greater force, to come close to the King. (The chapter gives these as Zohar references; the idea is kept.)

From inside, the higher return is not about sin at all. It is the soul going back to where it was before there was any distance, and going there with the extra force of someone who knows what distance is.

**The movement.** From the broken heart of the lower return, through a true request for mercy, to cleanness and then to cleaving, the soul at home in its source. What carries you is the honest asking, "wipe away my sins," from a heart that has done the reckoning.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "One with Him, as before the breath left." - Think of a breath before it leaves the body: it is still inside, still one. - Bring it down: your soul, washed, can be that again. Ask for it, once, for the thing you counted last night. - The picture breaks: you cannot wash yourself; the mercy comes from above even His Name. Stop there and wait. - Say: "By Your great mercy, wipe away my sins." - Learn Torah today with love, as someone who has come home.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when after the tears comes a clean nearness and a strong pull toward Him. The fake is feeling cleansed by the drama of crying, without the reckoning before it or the learning after it.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 8 (line 977): כמו שהיתה מיוחדת בו יתברך בתכלית היחוד*

## Chapter 9 · Stronger at the knot

**The state.** *Teshuvah ila'ah*, the higher return, as *binah*, understanding: love and awe born from thinking about His greatness, until there is *itdabkut rucha berucha*, "spirit cleaving to spirit," with every part of you. And the state of the retied rope, doubled at the knot.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Zohar says understanding, *binah*, is the higher return, "the mother hovering over the chicks." What does that mean? A person thinks about G-d's greatness with depth of mind, and from the spirit of his understanding he gives birth to love and awe that are thought through, with good sense and knowledge. "To love G-d, because He is your life." He is not satisfied with the natural hidden love alone. The same with awe, fear and shame. Then his understanding is the mother, sitting over what it gave birth to.

The core of love is "spirit cleaving to spirit," as the Song of Songs says, "let Him kiss me with the kisses of His mouth." And that is what "with all your soul" means: all the parts of the soul, mind and heart, and their clothes, thought, speech and deed, all cleaving to Him.

The chapter lays them side by side. Your traits cleave to His traits: "as He is merciful, be merciful." Your mind cleaves to His wisdom, which is learning Torah, since Torah comes from wisdom. Your thought to His thought. Your speech to His word, which is halacha, "I have put My words in your mouth." Your deeds to acts of charity, to revive the spirit of the lowly.

From inside, this is a full state. Nothing of you is left outside. Your pity is His pity, your thinking is His Torah, your mouth says His words, your hands do His kindness.

Then the chapter comes back to the hard case from chapter 4. Damaging the covenant by wasting seed, and all the more forbidden relations, damages the mind, the brain. So its repair is to engage in Torah, which comes from wisdom. Tanna Devei Eliyahu says: "A person sinned and was liable to death before G-d. What should he do and live? If he used to read one page, let him read two. If he used to learn one chapter, let him learn two."

And the picture: a rope that snapped and was tied again. At the place of the knot it is doubled and doubled again. So it is with "the rope of His inheritance." The place where you broke, once retied, becomes the strongest place on the rope.

"With kindness and truth sin is atoned," and "truth is only Torah." The sages said the sin of the house of Eli could not be atoned by offerings, but it could be atoned by Torah and acts of kindness.

This is a state worth resting in. It says your broken place does not have to stay your weak place. It can become where you hold on hardest.

**The movement.** From the lower return's broken heart to love and awe born from understanding, and from a snapped cord to a doubled knot. What carries you is more Torah than before, and acts of kindness.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "At the knot, it is doubled." - Think of the place in your life where you broke most. - Bring it down: add one measure there. If you learned one page, learn two. If you gave once, give twice. - The picture breaks: a knot is still a knot; here the retied place becomes stronger than it was. Stop there. - Say: "Let my broken place hold You most." - Learn one more piece of Torah today than you planned, and do one act of kindness.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the old wound turns into a place of strength and devotion. The fake is showing off how much you learn now, with the heart not in it.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 9 (line 987): שבמקום הקשר הוא כפול ומכופל*

## Chapter 10 · Low, then high, every day

**The state.** The daily rhythm of return: *teshuvah tata'ah*, lower return, as *koved rosh*, a weight of the head, submission, and then *teshuvah ila'ah*, higher return, as prayer with the heart, joy, and cleaving all day. Also the weekly rhythm, the week's low point before Shabbat and Shabbat as a higher return.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The higher return of chapter 9, spirit cleaving to spirit through Torah and kindness, comes as a flow from above to below: G-d's word in your mouth, "His right hand embraces me" through acts of kindness.

But a person down here has to go up, step by step. For him the higher return is the intention of the heart in prayer, above all in the Shema and its blessings, so that he says "you shall love G-d with all your heart and all your soul" truly, as the real truth. And "these words shall be on your heart, and you shall speak of them," so that G-d's word is really in his mouth. And the mitzvot, which we bless as "who has made us holy with His commands." The word for holy is the word of the wedding vow, "you are made holy to me," set apart. His holiness is the part of Him that cannot dress itself inside worlds, since before Him all is as nothing. It surrounds all worlds. It is His will. After prayer we say, "To You, G-d, I lift up my soul": to cleave spirit to spirit all day. All this comes from deep thinking about the greatness of the Endless in the blessings before the Shema and the psalms of praise.

Since prayer is the higher return, the lower return must come first. The Mishnah says, "One stands to pray only from a heaviness of head," which Rashi explains as submission. That is the lower return, waking mercy. But a teaching in the Talmud says, "one stands to pray only from joy." How can both be?

In this orphaned generation, the chapter says, not everyone can turn his heart in a moment from one end to the other. So the good advice is to put the lower return earlier, at Tikkun Chatzot at midnight. And if someone cannot do it every night, at least once a week, before Shabbat. Shabbat is the higher return. The letters of *Shabbat* spell "*tashev enosh*," "return, O man." On Shabbat the worlds rise to their source, especially in its prayers.

So the verse "Return to Me, for I have redeemed you" reads in order. "I have wiped away your sins like a cloud" is the removing of the other side. "I have redeemed you" from the outer forces, by waking the higher mercy through the lower return. And then, "return to Me," with the higher return.

From inside, this is a rhythm and not a single moment. A person does not live in a broken heart all the time. He goes low at a set time, so that in prayer he can be high and glad. The lowness is placed, the way you set a time for a hard conversation, and the rest of the day is free for joy.

**The movement.** From confusion about whether to be sad or happy before G-d, to a rhythm: lowness at midnight or before Shabbat, joy and cleaving in prayer and all day. What carries you is the schedule, the Shema said truly, and the verse after prayer.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "To You, G-d, I lift up my soul." - Think of when your low hour is: tonight at midnight, or this Friday before Shabbat. - Bring it down: set it now. Then set the next prayer as a time for joy, with the reckoning already done. - The picture breaks: you cannot turn the heart in a moment, and you are not asked to. Stop there. - Say, before praying: "I have made my reckoning. Now I come to You glad." - After your next prayer, say the line once and carry it into the day.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when prayer feels light and close because the heaviness had its own time. The fake is either sadness spread over every prayer, or joy with no reckoning under it.

*Source: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah ch. 10 (line 991): צריך לילך ממדרגה למדרגה ממטה למעלה*

## Chapter 11 · A thousand times is like once

**The state.** The state of broken and glad together, *hachna'ah*, submission, and *simchah*, joy, held in one heart with *bitachon*, sure trust, that He forgives at once, without a shadow of a doubt. And the memory of the wrong held "at a distance," *neged*, so that it keeps you humble and makes you slow to anger.

**The chapter as consciousness.** To have both in the heart together, the lowness of the lower return and joy in G-d, was already explained in Likkutei Amarim, the chapter says, as the Zohar has it: "weeping lodged in my heart on one side, and joy lodged on the other." To these add faith and trust. A person's heart can be firm and sure in G-d, that He desires kindness and is gracious, merciful and quick to forgive, the moment you ask Him. "By Your great mercy wipe away my sins; wash me, cleanse me." No doubt at all, not even a doubt of a doubt.

The proof is in the daily prayer. Right after "forgive us," we bless: "Blessed are You, G-d, gracious One who forgives abundantly." Jewish law says that when in doubt, skip a blessing, for fear of saying G-d's name in vain. So if there were any doubt that we were forgiven, we could not say this blessing. There is no doubt. We asked, "forgive us," so we are forgiven. And if we did not go back and sin again, we would be redeemed at once, as the next blessing says, "who redeems Israel."

Even among people this is so. A person must forgive at once when asked, and not be cruel, even one whose hand was cut off by the other. If asked three times and he refused, the one asking need not ask again. King David asked the Gibeonites to forgive Saul, and they would not, and David ruled they could not join the people, because this people is merciful. If so for flesh and blood, how much more, endlessly more, for G-d.

And why do we say "who forgives *abundantly*"? Among people, if someone wrongs you, asks, is forgiven, and then does it again, it is very hard to forgive a second time, let alone a third or fourth. But with G-d there is no difference between once and a thousand times. Forgiveness comes from His mercy, and His traits are not limited; they are endless. "His mercies do not end." Before the Endless there is no difference between a small number and a large one. "All before Him is as nothing," and He "makes small and great equal."

From inside, this is the most freeing state in the letter. The fear "I have asked too many times" simply has no ground. Counting belongs to finite things. His forgiving is not finite.

So "He removes our guilt year after year." Every Yom Kippur we confess the same sins again, though we did them again, and so forever. And not only every year: three times every day we bless "who forgives abundantly." Prayer takes the place of the daily offerings. The morning offering atoned for the night and the afternoon offering for the day. Now prayer with return does the same.

Is this not "I will sin and then return"? No. That applies only to one who could have overcome his urge at the moment of the wrong, but relied on return to let himself do it. Since return caused the sin, "he is not helped to return." And even then only "not helped"; if he pushes and overpowers his urge and returns, his return is accepted. But we, who ask every day "forgive us," first ask "bring us back in full return before You," meaning never to go back to folly. On Yom Kippur we ask, "may it be Your will that I sin no more." We are helped and helped. "One who comes to be purified is helped": the moment he comes. So the forgiveness is also at once.

Then a hard verse: "My sin is before me always." It does not mean to be sad and low all the time. The same psalm goes on, "let me hear joy and gladness." A person must be in the higher return all his days, and that is with great joy. The word *negdi*, "before me," also means "at a distance," as in "stand at a distance." The point is only that his heart not be proud, that he be humble before every person, with a memory between his eyes that he sinned against G-d.

On the contrary, the memory of the wrong helps joy. It lets a person accept with joy whatever comes, from heaven or from people, in words or deeds. (The chapter calls this good advice for being free of anger and of all kinds of irritation.) "Those who are insulted and do not insult back, who hear their shame and do not answer, who act from love and rejoice in suffering." And "whoever lets things pass, all his sins are passed over."

**The movement.** From fear of asking again and doubt that you are forgiven, to sure trust and joy, with the wrong remembered at a distance so that it makes you humble and easy with others. What carries you is the blessing said three times a day, understood, and the reasoning about the Endless.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "No difference between once and a thousand times." - Think of the wrong you are most tired of asking forgiveness for. - Bring it down: ask once more, now, and then say the blessing, knowing you would not be allowed to say it in doubt. - The picture breaks: people run out of patience; His mercy has no count. Stop there. - Say: "Blessed are You, gracious One, who forgives abundantly." - Today, when someone slights you, let it pass once, remembering your own wrong at a distance.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you can ask again without shame or fear, and slights from others lose their sting. The fake is using endless forgiveness as leave to do it again, or a humility that is only sadness.

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this reading applies it to every person.*

## Chapter 12 · The shadow and the sun

**The state.** The state of seeing how small things here are huge above. A little suffering accepted here cleans what would be far harder elsewhere, and a small act of holiness below moves worlds: *adam mekadesh atzmo me'at milematah*, "a person makes himself a little holy from below, and is made very holy from above."

**The chapter as consciousness.** The letter ends by explaining the joy of chapter 11 in hard things. Why can a person be glad even in the pains of the body? Because they are a great and mighty good for the soul that sinned: they clean it in this world and spare it the cleaning of Gehinnom. This is especially so now, when we cannot fast all the fasts listed by the Arizal that the soul would need for its cleaning.

The Ramban wrote in his introduction to Job that even Job's seventy years of suffering do not compare to one hour of the soul's pain in Gehinnom, since fire here is one sixtieth of that. But this world is "built by kindness," so light suffering here saves from hard judgment there.

This must be said gently, because it is easily misheard. The chapter is not telling a person to seek pain, and it has already forbidden harming the body in chapter 3. It is giving someone who is already suffering a way to hold it: this is not wasted, and it is not cruelty. It is kindness working in a way you cannot see.

Then the picture that gives the chapter its name. A shadow on the ground moves a handbreadth while the sun moves thousands of miles across the sky. A tiny shift down here matches a vast movement up there. And in the real thing it is endlessly more so, through all the worlds from the highest down to this physical one. The Zohar tells how the higher worlds rise through an awakening from below, when one bird, a young dove or a turtledove, or a handful of flour, was offered on the altar. And the Arizal taught the same of every mitzvah done with the body.

From inside, this is a state of seeing scale truly. A person usually measures an act by how big it looks. A coin, a candle, a short blessing. Here you see that each small thing is the shadow of something vast. The shadow moves a little because the sun moved a great deal.

"You shall make yourselves holy and be holy": "a person makes himself a little holy from below, and is made very holy from above." That is the holiness of chapter 10, "who made us holy with His commands," the holiness that surrounds all worlds.

And the same is true of reward and punishment. "The reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah itself." (The chapter says this is explained elsewhere, and closes: "knowledge is easy for one who understands.") What you do already is what you get.

The whole letter comes to rest here. It began with a person who did wrong and a false idea that return is pain. It ends with a person who knows that the smallest act of holiness, and even the pain he did not choose, is the shadow of something great, and that the mitzvah itself is his reward.

**The movement.** From measuring your life by how things look down here, to seeing each small act and each hardship as the shadow of a great movement above. What carries you is the shadow picture, and doing one small holy act on purpose.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "A little from below, much from above." - Think of a shadow creeping a finger's width while the sun crosses the sky. - Bring it down: one small act you will do today, a blessing, a coin, a kind word. It is the shadow; see the sun. - The picture breaks: the sun is far from its shadow; here the act below and the holiness above are joined. Stop there. - Say: "Make me holy, from above, for this little I do." - Do that one small act slowly and completely.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when small acts feel weighty and hard days feel less wasted. The fake is seeking out suffering as holy, or doing small acts to collect rewards rather than to be close.
