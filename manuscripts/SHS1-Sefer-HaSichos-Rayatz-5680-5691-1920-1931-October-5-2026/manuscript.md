*Part of the medicine map · all works*

**What it is.** This is a collection of the Rebbe Rayatz's talks, mostly in Yiddish and written down by listeners, from farbrengens in Soviet Russia and later Riga (1920-1931). The talks move between stories of earlier Rebbes and chassidim, replies to people at the table, communal urgings about Torah study under persecution, and short Chassidus teachings. It also contains his first-person diary of his 1927 arrest in Spalerno prison, and biographical notes. It is less a treatise than a live record of how a Rebbe spoke to, corrected and encouraged real people at the table.

**How much of it is medicine.** some. Most of the range is history, the arrest narrative, communal and yeshiva affairs, lineage and Kabbalistic asides, but farbrengen replies and the prison diary carry real, concrete counsel on fear, low mood, despair, self-deception and practice.

**Its model of the person.** The emotions (middos) by nature burn and boil. The mind (mochin) must first cool them, then break and sort them, then repair them, until they act with full warmth on what the mind understands ('living emotions'). Human reason alone is 'a head without a body' and cannot move the emotions. The divine reason of Torah joined to mitzvos can. The point of the heart is whole in every Jew, however low he has fallen. Change comes by one's own effort, at fixed times, with a friend, a student and an elder, and at the farbrengen, which softens the animal soul's 'hide'.

**Its philosophy.** Everything comes from God, and divine providence carries a person like a leaf in the wind. The world is a turning wheel with no fixed top or bottom. There is nothing besides Him, so worldly matters are not truly one's own. A Jew's purpose is to light up the world, and even darkness itself can be turned into light. Joy comes with the times God sets, not with one's personal state.

**What it adds.** This part gives lived cases rather than theory. It has a first-person record of mastering terror in a Soviet prison, step by step. It has homely Yiddish parables: the businessman's wheel and paying old debts bit by bit, and the drummer boy crying 'more joy'. It draws a sharp line between sadness and productive bitterness, and anchors joy in the time rather than one's state. It lays out a clear ladder of the mind working on the emotions. And it addresses busy working people directly, lowering the bar with 'darkness becomes light' through small fixed practice, a partner and an elder.

## I'm terrified, and my mind keeps painting the worst for me and my family

*Its own term:* בהלה; מחשבות הפועלות נמיכת רוח

Arrested, he pictured his mother weeping, his wife's pale face and his daughters' confusion. He broke into tears, his heart pounded and his body shook. His thoughts blurred and his legs faltered.

**What is really going on.** The pictures in his mind, not the facts, were bringing his spirit down. Under them lay the feeling that everything depended on him. He had forgotten who is really running events. Panic was also the captors' deliberate tool.

**Program: Mastering terror in the moment (from the prison diary).** (1) Notice the mental picture that is bringing your spirit down. (2) Stop it on purpose: I may not think these thoughts now; this is no place for sadness. (3) Ask: who did this? Everything is from God. (4) Separate your part from the outcome: I did what was mine; He will do what He wills. (5) Don't seek frightening details you don't need; not knowing kept him stronger. (6) Let your thoughts settle, make a firm decision about how to conduct yourself, and arrange your words in advance. (7) Fill your mind with verses of Torah and Psalms and with thoughts of God's unity, even in the dark place. *When:* In the acute moment of threat, repeated each time the frightening picture returns. *For:* Someone facing a real external threat who is being flooded by catastrophic pictures. *Not for:* Chronic anxiety, where thought-stopping becomes avoidance. Not a substitute for practical action: he also acted. `SHS1·Q01` · `SHS1·Q02` · `SHS1·Q03` · `SHS1·Q04` · `SHS1·Q05`

**Reframe.** From *"Everyone depends on me. If this goes badly, they are lost and it is on me."* to **They depend on me, and I depend on the One who created the world. I did what was mine; God will do what He wills.** The flood came from carrying the outcome. Handing the outcome back to God settled him: his thoughts organised themselves and his spirit rose into firm resolve. `SHS1·Q01` · `SHS1·Q02` · `SHS1·Q03`

## Money is tight, I'm at the bottom, and I feel crushed by my situation

*Its own term:* דוחק הפרנסה; עצבות ומרה שחורה מהמצב הלא טוב

He is squeezed for a living, has fallen inside himself, and his mood is broken by his bad situation. The same happens in spiritual life: sunk by past failures.

**What is really going on.** He forgets he stands on a wheel that keeps turning. Being swept up by a gain or a loss is foolish either way. Sadness paralyses, while bitterness of the right kind leads to action.

**Program: The wheel: a wise businessman's way through a downturn.** (1) Remember the world is a turning wheel, with no fixed top or bottom. (2) On top, think of the turning and stay a decent person (a mensch). (3) At the bottom, think of the top. (4) Do not be swept up by a big gain or a loss. (5) Keep the business running: pay old debts bit by bit, but never stop. (6) In spiritual life, repay past failings gradually, and replace sadness with bitterness that moves you to act. *When:* Whenever success or hardship shifts one's mood; an ongoing stance. *For:* A person demoralised by financial or spiritual setback, or puffed up by success. *Not for:* Someone who needs concrete financial help or crisis support; the wheel is a stance, not a remedy for real hardship. `SHS1·Q06` · `SHS1·Q07` · `SHS1·Q08` · `SHS1·Q09`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm at the bottom, and this is what I am now."* to **You are standing on a wheel. The bottom is a position, not a verdict; the wise person at the bottom thinks of the top.** Being swept up by one's state is called foolishness. Seeing the whole turning wheel frees him from being defined by the moment, so he keeps working. `SHS1·Q06` · `SHS1·Q07`

## How can I be happy when I'm in such a bad state?

At a time of joy he feels he ought to be bitter and broken, and treats his misery as seriousness. Grief or worry pulls him away from the joy around him.

**What is really going on.** The voice saying 'be bitter now' at the time of joy is the evil inclination. Joy that belongs to the time does not depend on one's personal state. Joy has an advantage over bitterness: certainty that the request will be granted.

**Program: Joy that belongs to the time.** (1) When the time itself is joyful (a festival, a farbrengen), be joyful whatever your personal state. (2) Recognise the voice urging bitterness now as the evil inclination. (3) Keep bitterness for its own time, and only as bitterness that leads to action. (4) Like the chassid crying by the washbasin: remember what day it is, wash your face, and go dance. (5) Like the drummer boy: even while crying, join the call of 'more joy'. *When:* At the times of joy themselves: festivals, Simchas Torah, farbrengens. *For:* One who uses gloom as piety, or whose private worry keeps him from shared joy. *Not for:* A fresh mourner or someone clinically depressed; joy cannot be ordered into them. `SHS1·Q08` · `SHS1·Q10` · `SHS1·Q11` · `SHS1·Q12` · `SHS1·Q13` · `SHS1·Q25` · `SHS1·Q26`

**Reframe.** From *"How can I be happy given my state?"* to **A feeling tied to yourself follows your state, but this joy is tied to the time. So be truly joyful, broken as we are.** Anchoring joy outside the self frees it from self-assessment. Joy brings certainty that the request will be granted, which bitterness lacks. `SHS1·Q12` · `SHS1·Q13` · `SHS1·Q11`

**Reframe.** From *"Being miserable shows I'm serious."* to **The urge to be bitter at the time of joy is the evil inclination. Sadness over a bad state is not bitterness; true bitterness leads to good action.** Once the gloom is unmasked as the evil inclination's disguise, it loses its claim to be piety. `SHS1·Q10` · `SHS1·Q08`

## What can you do? Times have changed. It's hopeless, so I just sigh

He meets decline, in himself or in others, with a shrug and a groan. He feels he has done his part by sighing.

**What is really going on.** The work names this a false view standing on three legs: falsehood, laziness and despair. Not being pained by another's state is a sign that one is not pained about oneself either.

**Program: The mirror, and covering the other.** (1) When you see a fault in another, take it as your own. (2) Stop rebuking in public; rebuke only with love and in private. (3) Look for the good in each person, as the Berditchever did. (4) Where another has fallen away, don't just sigh; ask what caused it. (5) Seek good counsel, with compassion and closeness, for how to bring him back. *When:* Whenever judgement of another person arises. *For:* The critical, the self-satisfied, and the resigned bystander. *Not for:* Victims of real harm; 'it's your own fault you see it' must never silence a report of abuse. `SHS1·Q17` · `SHS1·Q18` · `SHS1·Q19` · `SHS1·Q29`

**Reframe.** From *"What can you do? Times have changed."* to **That sentence is full of despair and grows from laziness and falsehood.** Despair is exposed as a lie that excuses inaction, which reopens the demand, and the possibility, to act. `SHS1·Q14` · `SHS1·Q29`

## I understand what's right, but it doesn't change how I feel or act

*Its own term:* קאפ אן א גוף; השגה אינה מאירה בלבו

He knows good traits are beautiful and self-love is ugly, yet his temper, envy and appetites run on unchanged.

**What is really going on.** Human reason alone is a head without a body. Understanding that does not light up the heart is not yet real knowing. The emotions must first be cooled by contemplating oneself, then broken, sorted and repaired.

**Program: The ladder of mind over emotions.** (1) Contemplate yourself: what is happening to you and what will become of you. Like a first bucket of water, this cools the fire. (2) Take stock of both the good and the bad parts in you. (3) Break: uproot plainly bad traits such as envy, lying, gossip and cursing. (4) Sort: clean the good parts of the dust of years of idle talk and mockery. (5) Repair: let the emotions run by the mind. (6) Let the emotions act with full warmth on what the mind understands, until mind and emotion are one. *When:* The work of the whole year, as ongoing daily toil in understanding and in refining traits. *For:* A person whose understanding does not reach his emotions or conduct. *Not for:* Fasts and self-mortification only within Torah limits and never harming health. Not for the depressed or those with eating disorders. `SHS1·Q15` · `SHS1·Q16` · `SHS1·Q17` · `SHS1·Q20` · `SHS1·Q21` · `SHS1·Q22`

## I'm too busy and worn out for real spiritual work; that's for special people

*Its own term:* קביעות עתים לתורה; מחשך לאור

A chassid says: there is no strength left to work, no senses and no powers. Businessmen feel the ideal of inner work is far beyond them.

**What is really going on.** Ordinary householders were over-frightened by the demands of spiritual work. The darkness itself becoming light means a shopkeeper thinking over a verse between customers.

**Program: Fixed times and Torah carried into the working day.** (1) First, fix times for Torah every day, even half an hour or a quarter of an hour. (2) Keep a verse, a psalm or Chumash with Rashi engraved in your mind. (3) In idle moments at work, think over a portion instead of joining idle talk. (4) Go to sleep and wake up with words of Torah. (5) Know that God's blessing, not scheming, brings wealth. *When:* Daily: a fixed slot plus the gaps of the working day. *For:* Working people who feel spiritual work is beyond them. *Not for:* Not a demand for heroic self-sacrifice; the text frames it as easing, not raising, the bar. `SHS1·Q23` · `SHS1·Q24`

**Reframe.** From *"Real inner work is for special people; I'm too busy."* to **The highest level is darkness itself becoming light: a man in the market who thinks over a Chumash portion.** The ideal is brought down to the reach of a working day, which dissolves the intimidation that caused giving up. `SHS1·Q23` · `SHS1·Q24`

## I've fallen too far; there's nothing good left in me

He sees himself only through the names others could call him: a man of appetite, senseless, confused.

**What is really going on.** The point of the heart is whole in every Jew, even if hidden. The true way is to see the good in each person, as the Berditchever did.

**Program: Friend, student, elder and farbrengen.** (1) Study one hour a day with a partner at a fixed time. (2) Have a friend and make yourself a student. (3) Let an elder see your faults, understand your merits, and pull you out of the mud. (4) At the farbrengen, let the hide soften and make a firm resolution. (5) Farbrengen alone is not enough: also toil in learning Chassidus and in prayer. *When:* An hour a day with a partner; farbrengens at set times. *For:* One who is isolated in his struggle or whose resolve has gone dry. *Not for:* Anyone with alcohol problems. Farbrengen warmth without the daily toil is warned against. `SHS1·Q27` · `SHS1·Q28` · `SHS1·Q30`

**Reframe.** From *"I'm too far gone; there's nothing good left in me."* to **Whatever names they can call you, the point of your heart is whole.** A permanent, untouched core is placed beneath all failure, so return is building on something present, not creating from nothing. `SHS1·Q31` · `SHS1·Q18`

## Cautions

- Stopping frightening thoughts helped in acute danger; used as a habit for chronic anxiety it can become avoidance. It does not replace practical action or professional care. - 'There must be no sadness' can shame a mourner. The Rebbe himself wept for his father; don't use it to cut short grief. - Calling despair 'laziness and falsehood' was aimed at communal fatalism. Never say it to someone who is clinically depressed. - Breaking the emotions, with fasts and mortifications, is limited by the text to what does not harm health. It is not for the depressed or those with eating disorders. - The farbrengen's mashke is part of the method; leave it out entirely for anyone with alcohol problems or in recovery. - 'The fault you see in others is your own' must never be used to dismiss someone reporting real abuse or harm. - Joy tied to the calendar should not be demanded of a person in acute grief or depression. - Stories of heroic self-sacrifice (children silent under torture) are testimony of persecution, not models to impose on ordinary people.
