## The Baal Shem Tov

**1.1 Knowing G-d "in all your ways" means joining, not only knowing.** The BeSHT reads *da'ehu* ("know Him") as *connection*: you join G-d to everything you do, even the physical. The second rule: the service is for G-d, not even a little for yourself. · *"דעהו הוא לשון חבור"* (*da'ehu* means joining); *"לחברם יחד בכל מעשיך אפי' בדברים גשמים שעושה"* (to join them together in all your deeds, even in physical things you do) · KST 2:16:1, line 351.

**1.2 A thread of attachment must never go out: "a small thought" all day, even at business.** Big fire comes and goes; what must stay is one live coal, a small thought of G-d, from which the fire can be blown up again. If even that goes out, the soul goes out. · *"אפי' כשהולך לעסקיו יקיים שויתי ה' לנגדו תמיד"* (even when he goes about his business he should fulfil "I have set G-d before me always"); *"כך לא יהיה דבוק במחשבה קטנה תמיד בו ית' יכבה נשמתו לגמרי"* (so, if he is not always attached to Him by a small thought, his soul will go out entirely) · KST 1:185:2, line 257.

**1.3 The physical is the seat of the spiritual; the rhythm of rising and resting keeps the soul alive.** Eating, business and rest are not interruptions but part of "running and returning" (*ratzo vashov*), so the soul is not consumed and returns to service stronger. Constant delight turns into nature and stops being delight. · *"זה נק' עובד ה' בגשמי וברוחני כל ימיו מאחר שהגשמי הוא כסא לרוחני"* (this is called serving G-d in the physical and the spiritual all his days, since the physical is a throne for the spiritual) · KST 2:47:2, line 456. And: *"כי תענוג תמידי נעשה טבע ואינו תענוג"* (constant pleasure becomes nature and is no pleasure), KST 1:88:1, line 137.

**1.4 (Maggid) The self must become "nothing" before G-d can clothe Himself in it.** Integration is not adding G-d to a full self. While a person thinks of himself as a "something" asking for his needs, the Infinite has no vessel in him. · *"צריך האדם לחשוב א"ע כאין וישכח א"ע מכל וכל"* (a person must think of himself as nothing and forget himself completely); *"כשחושב א"ע ליש ומבקש צרכיו אז אין הקב"ה יכול להתלבש בו"* (when he thinks of himself as a something and asks for his needs, G-d cannot clothe Himself in him) · MDL 68:6, line 156.

**1.5 (Maggid) Every attraction is G-d's life, narrowed.** The opening of Maggid Devarav leYaakov teaches the root exercise: in every thing there is a taste, a smell, a sight, a love or a fear; strip away the matter and what is left is spiritual, the Creator's life in it. So each love you feel is a narrowed ray of the root of love. · *"כי בכל מקום יש חיות הבורא ב״ה"* (for everywhere there is the life of the Creator) · MDL Introduction 7, line 7.

## Lizhensk

**1.6 Formulas said "in truth" become felt over time.** R. Elimelech's rule of life works by habit: say "I am doing this for the unification of the Holy One and His Presence" before everything, from the inside of the heart, and "over time he will feel a great light in saying it". The warning attached: say it truly, and do not fool yourself. · *"הריני עושה זאת לשם יחוד קודשא בריך הוא ושכינתיה לעשות נחת רוח להבורא יתברך שמו"*; *"ובהמשך הזמן ירגיש הארה גדולה באמירה זו"* (and in the course of time he will feel a great illumination in this saying) · NE, Tzetl Katan 4, line 26. The warning: *"אך יזהר שיהיה דובר באמת בלבבו"* (but let him take care that he speaks truth in his heart) ... *"ולא ישטה את עצמו להיות כגונב דעת עליונה"* (and not fool himself, as one who tries to deceive the Mind above), Tzetl Katan 3, line 25.

## Breslov

**1.7 The unity is reached in life only through self-nullification, and self-nullification only through *hisbodedus*.** Likutei Moharan 52 begins where the Unity Index begins: only G-d is necessary existence; everything else is possible existence. Man's task is to be re-included in that unity. The road there, says R. Nachman, is not contemplation but private speech to G-d, which slowly cancels the desires, the traits and the whole bodiliness. · *"כי רק השם יתברך לבד הוא מחיב המציאות"* (only G-d alone is necessary existence), LM 52:1:3, line 2688 · *"שיבטל עצמו לגמרי, עד שיהיה נכלל באחדותו יתברך"* (that he nullify himself entirely, until he is included in His unity), LM 52:3:1, line 2695 · *"ואי אפשר לבוא לידי בטול, כיאם עלידי התבודדות"* (and one cannot come to nullification except through *hisbodedus*), LM 52:3:2, line 2696.

## Karlin-Stolin

**1.8 Prayer is the "general", the day is its "particular".** R. Asher II of Stolin states the Karlin structure: the whole order of the day depends on that day's service of the heart. Joy in prayer is "a counsel that includes all the day's matters"; if prayer is mixed with sadness, the day will need a separate remedy for each thing. And wisdom without heart is nothing. · *"סדר היום תלוי בעבודה שבלב זו תפלה של אותו היום"*; *"העבודה שבלב הוא הכלל. וסדר היום הוא הפרט"* (the service of the heart is the general, and the day's order is the particular) · BA, R. Asher II 2, line 149; *"עבודה שבלב בשמחה הוא עצה כוללת לכל דברי היום"*, R. Asher II 4, line 151; *"שהחכמה בלא לב אינו כלום"* (wisdom without heart is nothing), R. Asher II 3, line 150. · R. Aharon II adds the other direction: the root of the day is the inner point (faith and awe), and one reaches the point through deeds: *"עיקר סדר היום הוא הנקודה שבלב ומה היא הנקודה אמונה ויראה"*; *"ואיך נוטלים הנקודה ע"י מעשיות באים אל הנקודה"* (how does one take hold of the point? through deeds one comes to the point), BA, R. Aharon II 44, line 120.

## Berditchev

**1.9 Start in awe, arrive at delight; and weigh each act so it yields "a hint of wisdom".** R. Levi Yitzchak: serve at every moment in awe, pleading Israel's merit; the beginning is awe and from it comes delight. Before acting, look at your deeds "with an open eye" so that nothing is done like an animal, but every act gives some glimpse of wisdom for service; if not, better to refrain. · *"האדם צריך לעבוד את הבורא בכל עת ורגע ביראה ואימה וללמד זכות על ישראל ושלא לצער לשום אדם מישראל"*; *"והתחלת העבודה הוא יראה ומהיראה בא לתענוג"*, KL Vaera 13, line 403 · *"שיעיין כל אדם בעינא פקיחא על כל מעשיו שלא ינהג מעשה בהמה"*; *"כדי שיוכל לבא מזה רמיזא דחכמתא לעבודתו יתברך"*, KL Vayigash 15, line 313.

## Ger, Izhbitz, Lublin, Zbarazh

**1.10 (Sfas Emes) Tie the deed to the power of the soul; keep "one small room" of Torah-light in every act.** Not everyone can become "all Torah" like Moshe; most live by R. Yishmael's way of work in the world. What is asked is that some light of Torah be in every act, so one is never separated from it. That, he says, is the main work of man in this world. · *"וקיטון א' עשה לי להיות עכ"פ איזה הארה מן התורה בכל המעשים"* (make Me one small room: to have at least some light of Torah in all the deeds), SE Terumah 26:2, line 2768 · *"שזהו עיקר עבודת האדם בעוה"ז לקשר המעשה בכח הנשמה"* (this is the main service of man in this world: to bind the deed to the power of the soul), SE Shmini 24:2, line 4008 · The ground of it: *"לעולם יש נקודה פנימית בלב איש ישראל"* (there is always an inner point in the heart of a Jew), SE Terumah 6:2, line 2704.

**1.11 (Izhbitz) Look to G-d in every act.** The Izhbitzer contrasts two lives: Yosef looks in every act to the law and does not budge from it; Yehudah looks in every act to G-d Himself. The image of integration: a weaned child, when it wants to take something, turns its head to see whether its mother is still there. · *"ושורש החיים של יהודא הוא להביט תמיד להש"י בכל דבר מעשה"*, MH Vayeshev 2, line 113 · *"כן צריך כל נפש מישראל להביט לה' ושלא ישוקע בתאוה וחמדה"* (so must every soul of Israel look to G-d and not be sunk in desire), MH Vaetchanan 6, line 391 · Bread and every pleasure as a dream needing interpretation: *"והאדם המבין בכל כי הכל הוא רק מהש"י"* (the man who understands in everything that everything is only from G-d) tastes true life in it, MH Miketz 6, line 124.

**1.12 (R. Tzadok) Faith in G-d must be followed by faith in oneself; and every use of a faculty is a place to recognize the Giver.** A person must believe that G-d has dealings with him and delights in his soul. And in every use of sight, hearing, doing or walking, he should recognize that G-d gives him that power: this is the help from heaven that saves from forgetting. · *"כשם שצריך אדם להאמין בהש"י כך צריך אח"כ להאמין בעצמו"*, TzT 154, line 158 · *"יכיר שהש"י הוא הנותן לו כח זה"* (he should recognize that G-d gives him this power), TzT 232, line 236.

**1.13 (Zbarazh) *Da'as* that fills the chambers.** From the Maggid's own school, in words close to Chabad's: the one whose knowledge of G-d is complete has "broad da'as"; it fills all the chambers of his heart and every movement of his limbs, and wherever he looks he sees G-d. This is the same *da'as* Chabad builds by contemplation (section 6). · *"וזה האדם נקרא דעתו רחבה"*; *"וכל תנועות אבריו פונים ומלאין מזה"* (and all the movements of his limbs turn to and are filled with it); *"מי שיש לו דעת רואה שכל חדרי העולם מלאים מכבוד השי"ת"* · YDE I 23:1, line 46.

## 2.1 Waking and the first hour

- **Rise briskly: you are a new person.** *"יקום בזריזות משינתו כי נתחדש ונעשה אדם אחר"* (he should rise from sleep with alacrity, for he has been renewed and become another person), TzR 20:1, line 38.
- **Carry the first mitzvah of the morning through the whole day.** *"באות' מצוה שהתחיל בה כשקם ממטתו ילך בה כל היום"* (the mitzvah he began with on rising, he should walk with all day), TzR 25:1, line 43.
- **Wake with thanks, said with a glad heart, even in one's own language** (Lizhensk). After *Modeh Ani* and the blessings over tzitzis and washing: *"ויראה שיהיה לבו מלא שמחה באמרו כנ"ל"* (let him see that his heart is full of joy as he says it), NE Tzetl Katan 9, line 31. *(Jewish practice.)*
- **The first thought of the day is preparation for prayer** (Karlin), and each morning blessing is a moment to recognize G-d's kindnesses, which brings the heart to good and to joy: *"בזמן קימה משנתו יהי' להכנה לעבודה שבלב זו תפילה"*, BA R. Aharon II 3, line 79; *"ועי"ז מתקרב הלב לטוב ולשמחה"*, R. Aharon II 4, line 80.

## 2.2 Before prayer, prayer, and after prayer (Karlin's centre; the BeSHT's manner)

- **No idle talk from waking until an hour after prayer** (and an hour around Minchah-Maariv); eyes within four cubits. *"שלא לדבר שיחת חולין מעת קומו ממטתו עכ"פ עד שעה אחר התפלה"*, BA R. Aharon the Great 8, line 19. The same in Lizhensk: *"וישמור את עצמו מלדבר שום דיבור קודם התפילה אפילו דבר אחד, כי זה מבטל כוונת התפילה"*, NE Hanhagot HaAdam 9, line 1533.
- **Tzedakah every day before prayer.** *"וירבה בצדקה בכל יכולתו. ובכל יום קודם התפלה"*, BA R. Aharon the Great 4, line 15.
- **Enter prayer through awe and contemplation of His greatness** (the BeSHT's school also contemplates before prayer): *"מתחלה כשרוצה להתפלל יהי' ביראה"*; *"ויתבונן בגדלותו וברוממותו ואח"כ יכול להיות בעולמות העליונים"*, TzR 66:1, line 121.
- **Do not spend yourself before the main prayer** (e.g. on many psalms). *"לא יאמר הרבה תהלים קודם התפלה שלא יחליש גופו"*, TzR 38:1, line 62.
- **Begin slowly and rise.** *"שלא יפסיד כל כחו בתחלת התפלה רק יתחיל במתון"*, TzR 32:1, line 55.
- **Wake the body first so the soul's power shines; later you can serve by thought alone.** *"צריך לעורר עצמו בתחלה בגוף שלו בכל כחו כדי שתאיר בו כח הנשמה"*; *"ואח"כ יוכל לעבוד במחשבה לבד בלי תנועות הגוף"*, TzR 58:3, line 111.
- **Pray with strength, aloud enough to wake the intention, face to the wall** (Lizhensk). *"ובקול המעורר הכוונה להדבק המחשבה לדיבור"* (with a voice that wakes the intention, to bind thought to speech), NE Tzetl Katan 11, line 33.
- **Prayer in joy is more accepted than prayer in tears.** *"התפלה שהוא בשמחה גדולה בודאי יותר מקובל לפניו יתברך"*, TzR 107:1, line 219.
- **In the Shema, make G-d king over every limb** (Karlin): *"שמחוייב כאו"א להמליך הש"י על כל אבר ואבר"*, BA R. Aharon II 11, line 87. This is Karlin's integration in one act: every limb, sense and thought put under one rule.
- **Before prayer, the tzaddikim pictured themselves lying in the grave in suffering, and someone saying "Rise and pray"**; R. Aharon II heard that his father prayed with this reckoning for a whole year: *"הצדיקים היו משערים בעצמם קודם התפלה שהם שוכבים ביסורים בקבר"*, BA R. Aharon II 61, line 137.
- **Stay an hour after prayer so its life and awe remain all day.** *"וחסידים הראשונים הי' שוהין שעה אחת אחר התפילה. היינו שישאר החיות והיראה של התפלה על כל היום"*, BA R. Aharon II 19, line 95.
- **The prayer's order is the "repair for the whole day"** (the *Kedushah d'Sidra*), and do not throw off the yoke of Torah straight after prayer: *"כי זה תיקון לאדם על כל היום"*, BA R. Asher I 8, line 35; *"לא ישליך מעליו עול תורה תיכף אחר התפילה"*, R. Asher I 10, line 37.
- **Minchah and Maariv are "the refining of the whole day".** *"יזהר להתפלל מנחה ומעריב בכוונה גדולה כי זה צירוף כל היום"*, BA R. Asher I 14, line 41.
- **"Would that one prayed all day"**, read as: keep in mind at every moment that nothing, small or great, happens without G-d's mercy; that is how one stays attached. *"הלואי יתפלל אדם כל היום היינו שיהיה במחשבתו תמיד שכל דבר הן קטן הן גדול א"א כ"א ברחמי השי"ת"*, BA R. Asher I 7, line 34.

## 2.3 Torah study

- **Some mussar every day, much or little.** *"ועיקר ללמוד בכל יום שיעור מוסר הן רב הן מעט"*, TzR 1:1, line 1. Lizhensk names the books (Reshit Chochmah, Shelah, Chovot HaLevavot): *"ויעסוק באימה וביראה באיזה ספרי מוסר בכל יום"*, NE Hanhagot HaAdam 4, line 1528.
- **Pause every hour of study to re-attach to G-d.** *"וכשלומד צריך לנוח מעט בכל שעה כדי לדבק עצמו בו ית'"*, TzR 29:1, line 48.
- **Learn in order to do; take a good trait out of each session** (Karlin): *"והעיקר שילמוד לעשות ולקיים"*, BA R. Asher I 17, line 44; *"והעיקר לקבל מהלימוד איזו מדה טובה"*, BA R. Aharon II 20, line 96.
- **A fixed daily quota, without fail** (Breslov): R. Nachman "longed to draw the world to action", that everyone should have a set obligation of learning each day; and, for everyone, some halacha every day of one's life. *"נכספתי מאד להמשיך את העולם אל עשיה"*, SHR 19:1, line 81; *"ואם יעשו להם חק קבוע וחיוב חזק ללמד בכל יום ויום כך וכך"*, SHR 19:3, line 83; *"כי צריך ללמד איזה דין ב"שלחןערוך" בכל יום ויום כל ימי חייו"*, SHR 29:6, line 142. *(Jewish practice.)*
- **Uninterrupted study, with nothing in the mind but the study and the Tzetl** (Lizhensk): *"יראה בכל כחו שלא לעשות שום הפסק אפילו במחשבה אחרת"*, NE Tzetl Katan 10, line 32.

## 2.4 Idle moments, the street, and the mind's "background"

- **In every free moment, and lying awake at night, picture the fire of *kiddush Hashem*** and yourself giving yourself to it: then "he is not lying idle but fulfilling a Torah commandment". This is Lizhensk's way of filling the gaps of the day. *"יהיה מהרהר במצות עשה זו של "ונקדשתי בתוך בני ישראל""*; *"ונמצא שאינו שוכב ויושב בטל, רק מקיים מצות עשה דאורייתא"*, NE Tzetl Katan 1, line 23.
- **Whatever you see, let it remind you**: love of G-d from anything lovely, awe from anything awesome. *"וכל מה שרואה יזכור בהקב"ה אם אהבה יזכור באהבת ה' ואם יראה יזכור ביראת ה'"*, TzR 22:1, line 40.
- **Take everything that happens as from Him.** *"שכל דבר שיזדמן יחשוב שהוא מאתו יתברך"*, TzR 4:1, line 8.
- **When you cannot study or pray (travelling, talking with people), serve in another way, and don't grieve over it**: G-d wants to be served in all ways. *"השי"ת רוצה שיעבדו אותו בכל האופנים"*, TzR 3:1, line 5; *"ואל יצער את עצמו בזה"*, TzR 3:3, line 7.
- **A limit: devekus is no excuse for chatter.** *"אבל כשמדבר רק דברים בטלים ויסמוך על הדביקות יזהר מאוד"* (but when he speaks only idle words and relies on the devekus, let him be very careful), TzR 30:2, line 51.

## 2.5 Eating and the physical (*birurim*)

- **The taste itself is the Divine life in the food.** *"וכן כשאוכל יחשוב שהטעם והמתיקות של דבר המאכל הוא בא מכח החיוני"* (when he eats, let him think that the taste and sweetness of the food come from the life-force), TzR 90:7, line 181.
- **Eating even for the body's need repairs the sparks that belong to your soul**, because you then serve G-d with the strength it gave. *"וכשהוא משתמש באותו הכלי או אוכל מאכל אפי' לצורך גופו הוא מתקן הניצוצין"*, KST 1:186:1, line 259. The Maggid: the tzaddikim turn "something into nothing", *"אפילו גשמיות כמו אכילה מעלין ניצוצין הקדושות"*, MDL 6:2, line 18.
- **The Lizhensk formula after *hamotzi***: *"אין אני אוכל להנאת גופי חס ושלום, רק שיהיה גופי בריא וחזק לעבודתו יתברך שמו"* (I do not eat for my body's pleasure, only that my body be healthy and strong for His service), NE Tzetl Katan 15, line 37. Karlin's first rule says the same of eating, drinking, sleep and marital life: *"היינו להיות האכילה והשתיה והשינה והמשגל כולם רק לעבודת הבורא יתברך שמו הכרחית"*, BA R. Aharon the Great 2, line 13.
- **The Apter**: eating in holiness joins you to the "life of truth" in the food: *"וכשאדם אוכל בקדושה נדבק בו החיות אמת"*, OY Re'eh 1:1, line 523.
- **Kozhnitz**: be glad to eat because it lets you bless and crown G-d over you: *"ומשבח ומהלל לבוראו שזיכהו לאכול שיברך וימליכהו עליו"*, AY Avot 4 5:11, line 569.
- **Moderation, and never "for himself"**: the BeSHT's measure is that the body be healthy (TzR 5:1, line 9, not quoted); Tzava'at's governing rule for every act is *"ובכל דבר שיעשה יחשוב שהוא עושה בזה נחת רוח לבוראו ית' ולא לצורך עצמו אפילו מעט"*, TzR 11:1, line 18.

## 2.6 Speech

- **Almost no speech but what is necessary, sifted "through thirteen sieves"** (no lie, flattery, gossip, shaming, or showing off one's deeds); and "teach your tongue to say 'I don't know'". *"ירגיל את עצמו שלא יתחיל לדבר לשום אדם זולת לצורך גדול ההכרח לו"*; *"למוד לשונך לומר איני יודע"*, NE Tzetl Katan 8, line 30.
- **When you speak, know that it is the life in you, G-d, that speaks.** *"אל יחשוב שהוא המדבר אלא החיות שבו הוא הבורא ית' המדבר בו"*, TzR 127:2, line 259.
- **Even talk with a friend in the market can be lifted**, by attending to the soul through which one hears. *"ובין בדיבור עם חבירו בשוק"*, KST 2:49:3, line 463.
- **The mouth was made for Torah and for pleading Israel's merit** (Berditchev): *"והפה לא נברא רק לדבר דברי תורה וללמד זכות על ישראל"*, KL Bo 32, line 442.
- **Speak gently, gladly and quietly, even at home** (Stolin): *"ולדבר הכל בנחת ובשמחה ובקול נמוך"*, BA R. Asher I 44, line 71.

## 2.7 *Hisbodedus*: private speech with G-d (Breslov's centre; also Karlin and Lizhensk)

- **What and how long**: at least an hour, alone, in a room or a field, speaking to G-d with arguments, pleading and appeasement, asking to be brought close. *"ההתבודדות הוא מעלה עליונה וגדולה מן הכל"*; *"לקבע לו עלכלפנים שעה או יותר להתבודד לבדו באיזה חדר או בשדה"*, LM II 25:1:1, line 6344.
- **In which language**: the one you speak, because the heart follows the language one lives in. *"ותפלה ושיחה זו יהיה בלשון שמדברים בו"*; *"כי הלב נמשך וקרוב יותר אל לשון אשכנז"*, LM II 25:1:2, line 6345.
- **Daily, a set hour; the rest of the day in joy.** *"ויזהר מאד להרגיל עצמו להתמיד בזה מדי יום ביום שעה מיחדת כנ"ל, ושאר היום יהיה בשמחה"*, LM II 25:1:4, line 6347. It is "a general counsel that includes everything": *"כי זאת היא עצה כלליות, שכולל הכל"*, LM II 25:1:5, line 6348.
- **When the words won't come**: standing ready is itself good, and one can make a prayer out of not being able to speak; or say one word, many times, even for days. *"ואפלו אם לפעמים נסתתמין דבריו"*, LM II 25:1:7, line 6350; *"וידבר אותו הדבור כמה וכמה פעמים בלי שעור וערך"*, LM II 96:1:3, line 7059.
- **Best at night, outside the town, where people don't walk by day**: *"אך עקר ההתבודדות הוא בלילה"*, LM 52:3:3, line 2697.
- **The content includes judging oneself on everything one did** (Breslov's form of the daily accounting): *"וידין וישפט את עצמו על כל הדברים שעושה"*, LE Torah Learning 18, line 1068. And to settle what one is doing in this world: *"ויישב עצמו היטב – מה הוא עושה בעולם הזה"*, SHR 68:1, line 402.
- **Karlin**: one day a week of *hisbodedus* in a special room, with fasting, return and Torah: *"התבודדות יום אחד בשבוע בחדר מיוחד"*, BA R. Aharon the Great 3, line 14. **Lizhensk**: before daybreak, to weep over the exile of the Presence, and sometimes also by day: *"ולהתבודד עצמו קודם אור היום"*, NE Hanhagot HaAdam 1, line 1525.
- **The BeSHT's caution**: when you want *hisbodedus*, have a companion in the room, each alone with G-d (TzR 63:1, line 117, not quoted); but one who is truly attached can be alone with G-d even in a house full of people: *"לפעמים כשהוא דבוק יכול להתבודד עצמו אפילו בבית שיש ב"א"*, TzR 63:2, line 118.

## 2.8 Relationships, love of Israel, chesed

- **Love every Jew whom it is possible to judge favourably, as yourself.** *"וכל שאפשר לדונם לזכות מחויב לאהוב כנפשו"*, NE Hanhagot HaAdam 8, line 1532.
- **Pray to see your friends' virtues, not their faults** (R. Elimelech's Prayer before Praying): *"שֶׁנִּרְאֶה כָּל אֶחָד מַעֲלַת חֲבֵרֵינוּ וְלֹא חֶסְרוֹנָם"*, NE Prayer before Praying 9, line 1554.
- **Find the small good even in a wicked man**; that lifts him (and the same toward yourself, 3.6). *"צריך לחפש ולמצא בו איזה מעט טוב"*, LM 282:1:1, line 5332.
- **Love of Israel and love of G-d are one** (the Apter): *"ועוד שאהבת ישראל נקשרת עם אהבת השי"ת. ושניהם הם אחד"*, OY Chayei Sara 2:1, line 64. Berditchev: never cause pain to any Jew, and plead their merit (1.9).
- **At home: no fear imposed, no irritability**; when you are irritated, take it as your own sins' doing: *"לא יטיל אימה יתירה בתוך ביתו, ולא יקפיד שום הקפדה בתוך ביתו"*, NE Hanhagot HaAdam 12, line 1536. Karlin: beware of pride and anger "even for a mitzvah": *"ליזהר מאד מגאוה וכעס אף לדבר מצוה"*, BA R. Aharon the Great 13, line 24.
- **A kindness to G-d every day; a mitzvah every day, and watch all day for one to come.** *"שבכל יום ויום צריך לעשות חסד לאל ית'"*, TzR 17:4, line 34; *"לא להניח שום יום בלא מצוה"*, TzR 17:1, line 31. Karlin: *"להיות שומר מצוה. פי' שימתין כל היום מתי יבוא לידו ויקיימנה"*, BA R. Aharon the Great 12, line 23; *"יזהר לכבד עניים"*, R. Aharon the Great 15, line 26.

## 2.9 Evening and bedtime: the accounting

- **At the bedtime Shema, think over everything done today, confess, weep and ask not to return to folly** (Stolin): *"והעיקר שיחשוב כל מה שעשה כל היום בזה היום ויתודה"*, BA R. Asher I 19, line 46. R. Aharon the Great: *"ויתודה ויתחנן ויבכה לפני הבורא להיות ממארי דחושבנא"* (to be among the "masters of the reckoning"), line 16. *(Jewish practice.)*
- **Going to sleep: the mind goes to G-d to be strengthened for His service.** *"וכשהולך לישן יחשוב הלא המוחין שלו ילכו להקב"ה"*, TzR 23:1, line 41.

## 2.10 Piaseczno: the working day (additions only; see `PIASECZNO.md` for the rest)

These are from the "Daily Instructions" (lines 268-304) and "Proposed Amendments" (305-335) of Bnei Machshava Tova, none of which is cited in `PIASECZNO.md` or `piaseczno/bnei-machshava-tova.md` except where noted there.

- **Live one teaching for a whole day, or two or three days, until "this is what all of you will be"; if it fails the first time, don't lose heart.** *"וְהַדָּבָר שֶׁקָּרָאתָ תַּעֲבֹד בְּכָל הַיּוֹם, אוֹ שְׁנַיִם וּשְׁלֹשָׁה יָמִים"*; *"אַל יִפֹּל לִבְּךָ"*, BMT Daily Instructions 2:1, line 273. This is his integration unit: not a new idea a day, but one idea worked into a day.
- **A fixed daily and weekly quota of learning, as binding as tefillin; once it is fixed, time appears.** *"לֹא יַעֲבֹר עָלֶיךָ יוֹם בְּלֹא תּוֹרָה"*, 9:1, line 291; *"כְּמוֹ ח"ו לְהִתְבַּטֵּל יוֹם מִלְּהַנִּיחַ תְּפִלִּין"*, 9:2, line 292. *(Jewish practice.)*
- **A broken personal resolution (e.g. to rise early, or skip a craved food) is to count in your eyes like a transgression.** *"יְהֵא בְּעֵינֶיךָ כְּאִלּוּ נִכְשַׁלְתָּ בַּעֲבֵר"*, 10:2, line 294.
- **In the street, keep mind and heart fixed on some word of Torah.** *"יהיו מַחְשַׁבְתְּךָ וְלִבְּךָ קְבוּעִים בְּאֵיזֶה דְּבַר תּוֹרָה"*, 12:3, line 300.
- **Budget time for everything in advance: how long you will eat, how long you will visit a friend; feel like a soldier under another's yoke.** *"תִּקְצֹב קִצְבָּה לְכָל מַעֲשֶׂיךָ"*, 13:1, line 301; *"תַּרְגִּישׁ אֶת עַצְמְךָ כְּאִישׁ הַצָּבָא"*, 13:2, line 302.
- **The busy man's rule: in the middle of the day's pressure, several times a day, say to G-d, in your own words, "I am now in places dangerous to my soul; do not leave me."** *"חוֹב עָלָיו שֶׁיֹּאמַר בְּאֶמְצַע טִרְדָּתוֹ זֹאת אֵיזֶה פְּעָמִים בַּיּוֹם"*; *"לֹא בְּלָשׁוֹן הַזֶּה דַּוְקָא צָרִיךְ הוּא לֵאמֹר"*, Amendments 11:1, line 328. (Close to Breslov's hisbodedus, cut into seconds for a working day.)
- **Look on each member of the fellowship as one of the "sons of ascent" and honour him**, *"תִּסְתַּכֵּל עַל כָּל אֶחָד מֵהַחַבְרַיָּא כְּעַל אִישׁ מִבְּנֵי עֲלִיָּה"*, Amendments 12:1, line 329; members in different towns gather *"לֹא פָּחוֹת מִפַּעַם אַחַת בַּשָּׁנָה"* (not less than once a year), for days of work and separation, quietly, 13:3, line 332.
- **The aim of the fellowship is integration itself**: not only in prayer, but always, a mind so clear and strong and bound to His holiness that it rules the senses: *"רַק גַּם תָּמִיד תִּהְיֶה מַחְשַׁבְתֵּנוּ כָּל כָּךְ בָּרָה חֲזָקָה וּקְשׁוּרָה בִּקְדֻשָּׁתוֹ"*, BMT Goals 6, line 6.

## 2.11 Joy (the whole day's ground tone, in Breslov, the BeSHT and Karlin)

- **"It is a great mitzvah to be always joyful"**, and force yourself to it with every means, even with silliness. *"מצוה גדולה להיות בשמחה תמיד"*, LM II 24:1:1, line 6334; *"ולשמח את עצמו בכל אשר יוכל, ואפלו במלי דשטותא"*, LM II 24:2:1, line 6341.
- **Brokenness only in a set hour; joy the rest of the day**, because from brokenness one slides easily into melancholy. *"רק בשעה מיחדת יהיה לו לב נשבר"*, LM II 24:2:3, line 6343; *"כי מלב נשבר בקל יכולין לבוא למרה שחרה"*, LM II 24:2:2, line 6342.
- **Joy and hisbodedus feed each other.** *"כשהאדם כל היום בשמחה, אזי בנקל לו ליחד לו שעה ביום לשבר את לבו"*, SHR 20:1, line 86; *"עלכן על ידי ששמחין בכל היום יכולין לפרש שיחתו בהתבודדות כראוי"*, LE Happiness 15, line 1015.
- **Joy is the hardest of all the services.** *"לזכות לשמחה זה קשה וכבד יותר משאר כל העבודות"*, LE Happiness 35, line 1035.
- **The BeSHT: fear without joy is black bile.** *"ויראה בלא שמחה הוא מרה שחורה"*, TzR 110:1, line 226. And weeping is bad unless it is from joy: *"הבכי' הוא רעה מאד שהאדם צריך לעבוד בשמחה"*, TzR 45:1, line 88.

## 2.12 Week and year

- **Shabbos**: prepare the body, purify it of the past and sanctify it for what comes, so Shabbos's holiness rests on every prayer and meal (Karlin): *"ובהגיע יום הכנת שבת מחוייב כ"א וא' להכין את גופו"*, BA R. Aharon II 21, line 97. *(Jewish practice.)*
- **Each day its own blessing**: *"הכלל כל יום ויום תן לו מעין ברכותיו"* (the rule: to each day give the like of its blessings, in awe, love, joy and gladness of heart, according to the time), BA R. Aharon II 31, line 107.
- **Elul (Kozhnitz)**: one who wakes only on Rosh Hashanah has "animal knowledge"; one must seek thirty days before, returning every day: *"שיתעורר כל אדם בתשובה בכל יום ולהתחרט על עונותיו"*; *"צריך לדרוש שלשים יום קוד"*, AY Re'eh 7, line 333.
- **The Tzetl Katan read daily, and its rules reviewed in Yiddish word by word**: *"יזהר מאוד ומאוד לחזור לפעמים בכל מעת לעת זה הצעטיל קטן"*, NE Tzetl Katan 14, line 36. (Stolin's hanhagos say the same of themselves: read daily, line 51.)

## 2.13 The guide and the friend

- **Tell every bad thought to your guide, or at least to a faithful friend, hiding nothing from shame**: bringing it out breaks its power for next time. *"לספר בכל פעם לפני המורה לו דרך השם, ואפילו לפני חבר נאמן"*; *"ולא יעלים שום דבר מחמת הבושה"*; *"משבר את כח היצר הרע שלא יוכל להתגבר עליו כל כך בפעם אחרת"*, NE Tzetl Katan 13, line 35.
- **A faithful friend who keeps secrets, half an hour a day** (Stolin): *"יזהר שיהי' לו חבר טוב ונאמן רוח ומכסה דברים שידבר עמו הגם חצי שעה בכל יום"*, BA R. Asher I 13, line 40.
- **When things are not good, the gathering helps: let them talk together**, of tzaddikim, of Shabbos and festivals (Karlin): *"ופעם כשנזדמן שאינו טוב לו. ע"ז טוב הקיבוץ שיתדברו ביניהם"*, BA R. Aharon II 55, line 131.
- **Attachment to the tzaddik keeps the accounting honest**: *"וכל ההולך ד' אמות באהלי צדיקים"* in true attachment..., BA R. Asher II 11, line 158 (the line opens: attachment to tzaddikim "works greatly so as not to err in the soul's accounting").

## 3. Growth over time

**3.1 Each at his own level; taking someone else's level fails.** *"כל אחד צריך להתנהג עפ"י מדריגתו"*, KST 1:2:1, line 2. Many tried to be like R. Shimon bar Yochai by self-affliction "and it did not succeed": *"הרבה עשו כר"ש ב"י ולא עלתה בידם"*, TzR 47:1, line 94.

**3.2 Falls are part of the path: descent for the sake of ascent.** *"והירידה הוא צורך עלי'"*, TzR 64:1, line 119. Stolin copies the rule: *"ויהיה עולה ויורד במחשבתו כי הירידה צורך עליה"*, BA R. Asher I 36, line 63. And one must "go down" several times a day to rest the mind: *"וצריך לירד למטה כמה פעמים ביום לנוח עצמו ממחשבתו מעט"*, TzR 135:2, line 281.

**3.3 "Small" states (*katnus*) are still devekus.** Sometimes one can only think "the whole earth is full of His glory" like a child with a small mind; even then *"ומ"מ אף שהוא עובד בקטנות עובד בדביקות גדול"*, TzR 67:1, line 122. The small thought (1.2) is what keeps the spark for later fire.

**3.4 Lizhensk: a trait is broken in forty days of doing the opposite.** Man was created "only to break nature"; the stubborn does the opposite of his first impulse for forty days in a row; the lazy does everything briskly for forty days (going to bed, rising, dressing, washing, going to shul); the shy prays aloud for forty days. *"האדם לא נברא בעולם רק לשבר את הטבע"*; *"ישבר את טבעו מ׳ יום רצופים לעשות דוקא להיפך ממה שיעלה במחשבתו"*; *"ירגיל את עצמו מ׳ יום רצופים לעשות כל דבר בזריזות"*; *"ירגיל את עצמו מ׳ יום להתפלל דוקא בקול רם"*, NE Tzetl Katan 16, line 38. And imagining a man standing next to you, loudly urging you to keep every rule, brings "in the course of time" a great awakening from the soul: *"כאילו איש אחד עומד סמוך לו ומעורר אותו בקול רעש גדול"*; *"אזי במשך הזמן יבוא עליו התעוררות גדול מצד נשמתו"*, Tzetl Katan 12, line 34.

**3.5 Breslov: years may pass; the words still work, like water on stone.** *"ואף אם לפעמים הוא רואה שמקים הנהגה זו ימים ושנים"* (and even if he sees that he has kept this practice for days and years [and is not yet close], let him strengthen himself more), SHR 68:5, line 406. Even speaking "without heart" is good, and in time the heart wakes: *"אף על פי שנדמה לאדם שמדבר בלא לב, אף על פי כן גם זה טוב מאד"*; *"כמו מים היורדים על אבן"*, LE Hitbodedut 18, line 343.

**3.6 Breslov: begin again every day; find your own good point; never despair.** *"שצריכין ממש בכל יום להתחיל מחדש"*, LM 261:1:2, line 5215. *"לבקש ולחפש למצא בעצמו איזה זכות ואיזה נקדה טובה"* (to seek and find in oneself some merit and some good point), LM II 48:4:1, line 6529. *"כי אין שום יאוש בעולם כלל"*, LM II 78:7:3, line 6848. And do not become "old": *"גם אין טוב להיות זקן"*, SHR 51:13, line 298.

**3.7 The measure is longing, not accomplishment.** *"שעקר הוא הרצון והכסופין שיהיה כוסף תמיד אליו יתברך"*, SHR 51:10, line 295.

**3.8 Karlin: go slowly and settledly; confusion is the danger.** *"ישוב הדעת ומתינות קודם כל דבר הן בדבור והן במעשה"*, BA R. Asher I 39, line 66; *"העיקר הוא שלא יהי' מבולבל ומטושטש"*; *"ויעשו כל דבר במתון ובנחת"*, BA R. Aharon II 46, line 122.

**3.9 Kozhnitz: a month of daily return before the year's judgment** (2.12; AY line 333).

**3.10 R. Tzadok: where your drive is strongest, there your vessel is.** Where the evil inclination attacks you most is the very place you are made to become pure; where you sinned most, there you can be clean. *"כל אחד ידע שבמה שיצרו תוקפו ביותר הוא כלי מוכן לאותם דברים ביותר"*, TzT 49, line 51. A way to read one's own history of failure as a map of one's task.

**3.11 Piaseczno (additions): aspire to the tzaddik's level; no good wish is lost; find the root of the bad trait.** - When you read of a tzaddik's trait, do not say "not for me": *"הֲלֹא גַּם הוּא הָיָה יְלוּד אִשָּׁה כָּמוֹנִי"* (was he not also born of woman like me?), BMT Daily Instructions 5:1, line 280. - Every thought and wish, good or bad, stays and accumulates (which is why a bad trait can suddenly appear "beyond one's level", and why unfulfilled good wishes are not wasted): *"שֶׁכָּל רַעְיוֹן וְרָצוֹן בֵּין טוֹבִים וּבֵין רָעִים ח"ו שֶׁעוֹבְרִים בְּאִישׁ אֵינָם חוֹזְרִים רֵיקָנִים"*, 5:3, line 282. - Double your work on your worst trait, and above all investigate what stops you from fixing it: *"וְכָל מִדָּה שֶׁתֵּדַע בְּעַצְמְךָ שֶׁיּוֹתֵר פְּגוּמָה בְּךָ תִּכְפֹּל עֲבוֹדָתְךָ בָּהּ"*; *"וּבְעִקַּר תַּחֲקֹר עַל הַמְּנִיעָה שֶׁמּוֹנַעַת אוֹתְךָ מִלְּתַקֵּן אֶת הַמִּדָּה הַפְּגוּמָה"*, 8:1, line 287. Go "to the kernel of the bad": *"וְיָבוֹאוּ עַד הַגַּרְעִין שֶׁבְּרָעָתָם"*, 8:2, line 288 (his example: one man's anger is really pride, so years of work on anger will not help, 8:3, line 289). - Love and awe are already in every Jewish soul, buried in the body; the whole program is to uncover them: *"כִּי בֶּאֱמֶת כָּל נֶפֶשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל יְרֵאָה וְאוֹהֶבֶת אֶת ד' הִיא"*, 14:2, line 304.

## 4. Counterfeits of integration

**4.1 Sadness dressed as piety (the BeSHT).** The evil inclination tells a man he committed a great sin, when it was a mere stringency or nothing at all, so that sadness will stop his service. *"וכוונתו שיהא האדם בעצבות ומכח זה. יבוטל בעצבותו מעבודת הבורא ית'"*, TzR 44:1, line 86. Even after a real sin: *"רק שיעצב על העבירה ויחזור לשמוח בהבורא ית'"* (let him be sad over the sin, and return to rejoicing in the Creator), TzR 46:2, line 90.

**4.2 Excess scrupulousness.** *"ואל ירבה בדקדוקים יתירים בכל דבר שעושה"*, TzR 46:1, line 89 (its aim is fear, which leads to sadness). Stolin copies it word for word in R. Asher I's letter (BA line 56) and adds: *"ויחזק עצמו לשמוח בבורא ית'"*, line 58.

**4.3 Pride in one's devekus.** *"ואל יאמר בלבו שהוא גדול מחבירו שהוא עובד בדביקות"*, TzR 12:1, line 19. The cure is constancy itself: *"כשאדם עובד תמיד אותו ית' בכל רגע אין לו פנאי להתגאות"*, TzR 52:1, line 100.

**4.4 Show before substance, and hiddenness too early.** The BeSHT: do your deeds hidden, so that people don't see "chassidus"; but before reaching a high level, act openly like everyone, lest wanting to be a chassid "inwardly" draws you after the world. *"צריך לעשות מעשיו בהסתר שלבני אדם נראה שהוא אינו חסידות"*, TzR 65:1, line 120. Equanimity is the test of freedom from show: *"הכל שוה אצלו בין בענין שמשבחין ב"א אותו או מבזין אותו"*, TzR 2:1, line 3.

**4.5 Self-deception in the heart (Lizhensk).** Saying the right words while not meaning them is "trying to fool the Mind above": *"ולא ישטה את עצמו להיות כגונב דעת עליונה"*, NE Tzetl Katan 3, line 25.

**4.6 Brokenness that turns into melancholy (Breslov, Karlin).** *"לב נשבר ועצבות – אינו ענין אחד כלל"* (a broken heart and sadness are not one thing at all), SHR 41:1, line 226; sadness is like one angry and complaining against G-d for not doing his will: *"עצבות הוא: כמו מי שהוא בכעס וברגז"*, SHR 42:1, line 232. Karlin: *"העיקר מכל המדות רעות הוא עצבות ומרה שחורה"*; the evil inclination sells it as repentance, but *"כי הסימן מן לב נשבר הוא שמחה"* (the sign of a broken heart is joy), BA R. Aharon II 49, line 125; and *"ועצבות נמשך ג"כ מגיאות"* (sadness too comes from pride), R. Aharon II 50, line 126.

**4.7 Cleverness instead of simplicity (Breslov).** R. Nachman wanted only holy doing, "all in simplicity, without any cleverness": *"והכל בפשיטות בלי שום חכמות"*, SHR 19:4, line 84. The goal is reached *"הוא רק עלידי תמימות ופשיטות"*, LM II 19:1:6, line 6300.

**4.8 Wisdom without heart; mockery (Stolin).** *"שהחכמה בלא לב אינו כלום"*, BA R. Asher II 3, line 150. *"מדת הליצנות הוא ההיפוך הגמור מחסידות"* (mockery is the complete opposite of chassidus), R. Asher II 12, line 159. And the mouth and heart must match: *"העיקר בזה רחמנא לבא בעי שיהיה פיו ולבו שוין לטובה"*, R. Aharon II 29, line 105.

**4.9 Service for one's own delight (Berditchev).** Some see G-d's greatness daily, are fired up, enjoy the joy, and then want to be fired up again *for* the pleasure: *"ועיקר כוונתו מחמת התענוג"*. The higher service is that G-d should have the delight: *"שעיקר כוונתו הוא רק בכדי שיגיע תענוג להבורא ברוך הוא"*, KL Vayechi 7, line 328. The BeSHT's rule is the same: "even if he does it so that he has pleasure in his service, it should not be for his own need" (TzR 11:1, line 18, quoted in 2.5).

**4.10 Giving up because the ideal is impossible for a working man (Zbarazh).** The mussar books demand a person free of all business, but "in this generation" everyone must deal in his trade; still, heaven forbid we slacken: *"וצריך בדור הזה לעסוק כ"א במו"מ שלו וכ"א בפרנסתו"*; *"אבל ח"ו מחמת זה לא נתרשל עצמינו"*, YDE II 33:1, line 60. And waiting to be pure before drawing near is backward: bad traits cannot be broken except by coming close to G-d: *"אם הוא מלא מדות רעות ורוצה לשברם א"א אם לא ע"י התקרבות להשי"ת"*, YDE I 25:2, line 50.

**4.11 Love of Israel without G-d (R. Tzadok).** Love of Jews alone, without love of G-d, is just love of company: *"כי אהבת ישראל לבד בלא הש"י הוא אוהב צוותא וחברותא"*, TzT 196, line 200. (The Apter's "the two are one", 2.8, is the positive form.)

## 5. For anyone

Items the sources themselves present as open to every person, or which work for anyone as they stand. Jewish practices are named as such.

- **Private speech with G-d in your own language, daily.** R. Nachman says outright that it is "equal for every soul, from small to great": *"והוא דבר השוה לכל נפש מקטן ועד גדול"*, LM II 25:1:9, line 6352.
- **Simplicity reaches the goal, "for this is all of man"**: LM II 19 reads *ki zeh kol ha-adam* as "this every person can do" (line 6301; the verified phrase is at line 6300).
- **Find one good point in yourself and in others** (LM 282; II 48).
- **Begin again each day; no despair** (3.6).
- **Joy as a practice, not a mood** (2.11).
- **Let everything seen remind you of love or awe of G-d; take what happens as from Him** (2.4).
- **Speak less, and teach your tongue to say "I don't know"** (2.6).
- **Tell your struggles to one trusted person** (2.13).
- **Budget your time; in the middle of a pressured day, say one sentence to G-d in your own words** (2.10).
- **An evening review of the day** (2.9; the bedtime Shema itself is Jewish practice).
- **See the taste of food as the life G-d put in it; eat to be well for service** (2.5; the blessings are Jewish practice).
- **Recognize G-d as the giver of each power as you use it** (1.12).
- **Where your struggle is strongest, there is your task** (3.10).

## 6. Compared with Chabad's way (hisbonenus; avodah through the mind)

For the comparison, two Chabad lines, verified in `Tanya (Liozna, 1786-1796).txt`: · the engine: *"כשמתבונן ומעמיק מאד בגדולת ה׳"* (when [the mind] contemplates and goes very deep into G-d's greatness, awe is born), Tanya ch. 3, line 85; · the carrying into the day: *"אלא העיקר הוא ההרגל, להרגיל דעתו ומחשבתו תמיד"*, that everything the eye sees is *"הכל הם לבושים החיצונים של המלך"* (all are the outer garments of the King), Tanya ch. 42, line 622.

**Where they agree (strongly).** 1. **The goal is the same unity, lived.** The BeSHT's *"יזכור בהקב"ה"* in everything seen (TzR line 40), the Zbarazher's "da'as that fills the chambers" (YDE line 46), the Izhbitzer's "look to G-d in every act", the Sfas Emes's "light of Torah in every act", and Breslov's "only G-d is necessary existence" (LM line 2688) are all the Tanya ch. 42 aim: to see everything as the King's garment and live accordingly. 2. **Contemplation before prayer is not Chabad's alone.** The BeSHT's school: *"ויתבונן בגדלותו וברוממותו"* before prayer (TzR line 121). What is distinctive in Chabad is not *that* it contemplates but how far it builds the contemplation out, in detail and length, as the main engine. 3. **Prayer as the day's centre.** Karlin's "prayer is the general, the day is the particular" (BA line 149) and "stay an hour after prayer so its life stays all day" (line 95) are very close to Chabad's avodas ha-tefillah. 4. **Deed as the proof.** "Learn in order to do", "take a good trait from the learning" (Karlin), the Tzetl's forty days (Lizhensk), Breslov's "draw the world to action": all share Chabad's insistence that the test is the changed life, not the feeling. 5. **Joy, not sadness; itkafia.** Every school here condemns sadness as a counterfeit (section 4). Lizhensk's "break nature" and Karlin's silence before prayer are forms of itkafia.

**Where they differ (the difference kept).** 1. **The main door.** Chabad enters through the *mind*: understanding, lengthened and deepened until it moves the heart. Breslov enters through *speech and plea* (hisbodedus), and says bittul is reached "only" that way (LM line 2696); Lizhensk through *imagination, formula and habit* (the pictured fire, "l'shem yichud", forty days); Karlin through *the voice and heart in prayer*; the BeSHT through *attachment of thought* in every activity and joy; Berditchev through *love of Israel and delight*; Izhbitz through *attentive looking* to G-d's will now. 2. **Depth of intellect versus simplicity.** Breslov explicitly prefers simplicity to "cleverness" (SHR line 84; LM line 6300), which differs from Chabad's demand to understand deeply. But Breslov's target is philosophical cleverness and self-important sophistication, not Chabad's contemplation of the unity; the two are not simply opposed. 3. **Where feeling fits.** Several schools (the BeSHT, Karlin, Lizhensk) wake the body and voice first so the soul follows (TzR line 111; NE line 33); Chabad's order is mind first, then heart. The BeSHT himself ends where Chabad points: "afterwards he can serve by thought alone" (TzR line 111). 4. **The gaps of the day.** Chabad fills them with *da'as* (the habit of Tanya 42); Lizhensk fills them with a pictured act of self-giving (Tzetl 1); Breslov with short speech to G-d; Piaseczno with a word of Torah held in the street and one sentence of prayer inside the business day. 5. **Accounting.** Karlin and Breslov make the nightly (or hourly) self-judgment a place of tears and speech; Chabad (from other files) sets it inside the bedtime Shema and teaches not to be overwhelmed by it. The sources here agree with Chabad that sadness must not follow (4.1, 4.6).

## 7. What this means for integration (our synthesis, not the sources' words)

1. **Every school here integrates by a *rhythm*, not by a single state.** The BeSHT names it (*ratzo vashov*, descent for ascent, "small" and "great" states), and every rule-book here (Tzetl Katan, Karlin's order of the day, Breslov's hour, Bnei Machshava Tova's Daily Instructions) is a way of building a day with fixed points: waking, before prayer, prayer, after prayer, study, meals, the street, bedtime. Integration is the thread between those points: the "small thought" that must never go out (KST line 257).
2. **The fixed points are few, and the same everywhere.** Waking with thanks; silence and preparation before prayer; prayer as the day's centre; an hour after it; a daily portion of Torah including something practical; the mitzvah and the kindness of the day; eating for service; speech cut down; an evening accounting; one trusted friend or guide; one hour (or one sentence) of speaking to G-d in one's own words; and joy as the ground tone. Chabad's hisbonenus fits this same frame, as the specific content of the "before and during prayer" point.
3. **Each school supplies one tool the others lack.** Chabad supplies the *content* of the thought (the contemplation of the unity) and the claim that understanding can govern the heart. Breslov supplies a *voice* for the person who cannot yet feel it (hisbodedus, even one word). Lizhensk supplies *habit and behavior change* (forty days of the opposite, the written rule read daily). Karlin supplies the *architecture of the day around prayer*. The BeSHT supplies *the physical as a place of service* and the refusal of sadness. R. Tzadok and the Piaseczner supply *diagnosis*: find the root of a trait, read one's strongest struggle as one's task. The Izhbitzer and Sfas Emes supply the inner test of each act: is G-d looked to, is some light of Torah in it?
4. **The shared test of real integration** is the one the Chabad sources also use: not intensity of feeling but a changed person. Traits broken (Lizhensk), mouth and heart the same (Karlin), a good trait taken from each learning, joy instead of melancholy, G-d's delight and not one's own as the motive (Berditchev), equanimity under praise and insult (the BeSHT).
5. **The shared counterfeits** are few and recurring: sadness posing as piety; scrupulousness; pride in one's spiritual level; taking another's level; words without heart; cleverness without heart; service for one's own pleasure; love of people without G-d.

## Gaps

- **Karlin's "outcry" as such** is not found in these files as a teaching about shouting in prayer. What Beit Aharon does say is prayer "with all his strength" (e.g. answering amen with all one's strength, lines 1214, 2058; not quoted), the whole day hung on prayer, and joy as the sign of true brokenness. The famous Karliner outcry is tradition about practice, not a written instruction in this book.
- **The Tzetl Katan** in the file is headed "all this I found in manuscript" (line 40); some printings attribute parts to his circle. It is quoted as printed in Noam Elimelech.
- **Kozhnitz and the Apter** gave little practical, day-structured teaching in these files; what was found is thematic (eating to bless, Elul, love of Israel).
- **Keter Shem Tov** is a later anthology drawn largely from R. Yaakov Yosef of Polonoye's books; *Tzava'at HaRivash* mixes the BeSHT's and the Maggid's sayings. Both are quoted as "the BeSHT's school".
- **Not read here:** Toldot Yaakov Yosef, Me'or Einayim (Chernobyl), Pri HaAretz (Vitebsk), Meor VaShemesh, Shem MiShmuel, Likutei Halakhot and Likutei Tefilot (R. Nosson), Chayei Moharan. Each probably holds more on the physical as service (Chernobyl, Polonoye) and on Breslov practice (R. Nosson), and would be the next place to look.
