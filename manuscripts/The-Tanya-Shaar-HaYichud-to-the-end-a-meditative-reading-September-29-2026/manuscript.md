## 1. The book as one meditative journey

This half of the Tanya walks a person from the head, down through the heart, into the hand, and ends in silence. It is a road, not a lecture. Read slowly, it does to a mind what it describes.

**It starts with a child.** Chinukh Katan, 'a small education', opens with the verse 'Train a child in his way'. It says there are two loves of God. One is a flame that rises by itself, a gift. The other anyone can make, by thinking until it is plain that God is 'your true soul and your very life'. Under both it lays one stone: faith in His oneness.

**Then the mind is cleared (Shaar HaYichud, the Gate of Unity).** Twelve short chapters take the solid world and show it being said, right now, by the word that made it. The sky stands because 'Let there be a firmament' is still being spoken inside it. Nothing stands on its own; the sense that it does is God hiding, and the hiding is His kindness. Then the gate brings the mind to its own wall: we picture God's wisdom as it is in us, and even the words 'He cannot be grasped' are like saying an idea is too deep to touch with the fingers. There the Alter Rebbe hands the rest to faith, 'the mystery of faith'. The gate ends not in heaven but at a stone, the last echo of the word.

**Then the same truth is turned on the person (Iggeret HaTeshuvah, the Letter on Return).** The soul is breath blown from God's inside, a rope tied above. Nothing in the world stands between it and Him; only acts against His will cut the cords. Contemplation here becomes the heart's cry: an hour of honest reckoning, mercy for the spark that was dragged into the dark. Then the soul goes home, 'as it was before He breathed it out', and the retied rope is double at the knot. Low return and high return, broken heart and joy, take turns every day.

**Then the truth goes out into a community's life (Iggeret HaKodesh, the Holy Letters).** These are real letters, written to people in debt, in grief, in quarrels, in prayer halls. Each one carries the unity into a place where it is hard to hold: pain ('no evil comes down from above'), anger ('His breath is in the one who cursed you'), a stranger's noise during prayer, a teacher's death, a poor harvest year. The letters keep coming back to one practice: giving. Charity is the armor made of coins, the seed that rots and grows, the wick for a light that is coming. It is also the gate to the heart's inner point, the place 'above knowledge' where love of God lives in exile.

**Then the mind bows to the hand (Kuntres Acharon, the last notes).** The boldest page says that even the finest contemplation reaches only that God's traits exist, not what they are; holding the etrog as the law says holds the life inside it, one with the Infinite. The purpose of the whole chain of worlds is not for us to climb up but for the highest to come down and live here. The book's last line is about Shabbat: resting from ordinary speech, as God rested from the ten sayings.

**What kind of contemplation it asks for.** All of them, each in its place. *Thinking through* in the Gate of Unity: slow, step by step, until the mind reaches its edge and stops. *Picturing*: the sun and its rays, the smith's cup, the split sea, the stone, the rope, the breath before it leaves the mouth, the seed, the chain mail, the hollow pillars, the wick. The text always says where its picture breaks. *The heart's cry*: mercy on the spark in exile, the midnight lament. *Bitul*: 'the nearer, the smaller'; acknowledging what you cannot grasp. *Prayer*: the King's hour, the Shema with eyes closed that must stay true with eyes open. *Song*: David singing the Torah's details, and then the hush. *Faith*, stated plainly where the mind ends. And, more than any other book of Chassidus, *the deed*: the coin, the etrog, the halacha learned, as the place where the contemplation is finished.

**From where to where.** From 'I love my life' to 'my life is His'. From a world that looks finished to a world being spoken. From the mind's pictures to the mind's edge. From the edge to the heart's inner point. From the heart to the hand. From the hand to Shabbat, where speech rests and what speech was saying is near.

## Chinukh Katan, the introduction to Part II

- **Where:** Chinukh Katan, the introduction to Part II, lines 768-779.
- **The movement:** It takes love of God down from the shelf of the saints and puts it in reach: a love you can make by thinking. Then it names faith in His oneness as the ground floor under everything that follows.
- **The line to hold** (line 775): > כאשר יתבונן וישים אל לבו כי ה׳ הוא נפשו האמיתית וחייו ממש > > *When he thinks it through and takes to heart that God is his true soul and his very life.*
- **Contemplating it:** There are two loves, the text says. One is a flame that rises by itself, a gift for tzaddikim. The other anyone can reach, by thinking well 'in the depth of the heart'. Start with the plainest fact you have. You love your own life. Nobody has to tell you to. Now take one step. If God is your true soul and your very life, then the life you already love is His. The love is not new. It only finds its real address. Bring it to your table. The hand that reaches for bread, the wish to live another day: that wish is already love of Him, dressed in ordinary clothes. The verse says love is something you 'make'. Like a meal, it is made from what is already in the house. The picture of 'my life' breaks here: your life is not a thing He owns beside Himself. As far as you are alive, it is Him.
- **From inside:** A quiet warmth more than a blaze. The text calls it a love 'made in the heart' by understanding. And when a person slips between levels, it says a mark (reshimu) of the earlier love stays, like the habits of a child raised well.
- **At the edge:** The first love, the flame that rises by itself, cannot be commanded; the text calls it a gift and the reward of tzaddikim. Under both loves it puts 'pure and faithful belief in His unity'.
- **Into the day:** Before eating, say quietly: the life I want from this bread is His life in me.
- **Ladder:** L01

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 1 (the Besht's 'Your word stands')

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 1 (the Besht's 'Your word stands'), lines 783-791.
- **The movement:** It takes the solid world and shows it as speech being spoken right now. It empties the mind of the picture of a finished world.
- **The line to hold** (line 785): > כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש > > *If the letters withdrew for an instant and returned to their source, all the heavens would be truly nothing and naught.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Besht read the verse 'Forever, God, Your word stands in the heavens' plainly. The words 'Let there be a firmament' did not stop. They still stand inside the sky, saying it. Widen it to every creature in every world, and 'even this physical earth', a stone. The Arizal says a stone has a soul: the letters that keep it a stone. Step down with the text. The stone's Hebrew name, even (אבן), is those letters, reached by long chains of swapped letters from the Ten Sayings, because one small stone could not hold the Sayings' full force. Now your room. The table, the cup, the wall are each being said this moment. If the saying stopped, the thing would not crumble. It would simply not be, as before the six days. Your own name is the same: the letters that keep you here. The picture of speech breaks where ours does: our words leave us and fade. His do not leave. They stay inside what they say.
- **From inside:** The room feels less heavy and more near. Things stop looking self-standing. The chapter asks why the Torah must warn 'return it to your heart', as if anyone thought God was missing from the water under the earth. From inside the answer shows: the heart assumes a finished world, and the warning undoes it.
- **At the edge:** The mind reaches 'without the word, nothing'. Where the word comes from is not opened here; the chapter closes on 'Torah and the Holy One are all one'.
- **Into the day:** Pick one object today, a spoon or a stone, and say its name slowly, as the word that keeps it here.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 2

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 2, lines 792-798.
- **The movement:** It breaks the craftsman picture: the idea that God made the world and stepped back, like a smith from a finished cup.
- **The line to hold** (line 795): > אלא צריך להיות כח הפועל בנפעל תמיד, להחיותו ולקיימו > > *Rather, the power of the Maker must be in the made always, to give it life and keep it in being.*
- **Contemplating it:** A silversmith makes a cup, goes off to the market, and the cup stays. That is how people picture creation, the text says, and they are wrong. The smith only changed a shape: something from something. Creation is something from nothing. The text's own picture: the sea stood as a wall only while the east wind blew all night. Stop the wind for a moment and the water runs down, because water does not stand by itself. How much more a world made from nothing, which has no self to fall back on. Without the Maker's power: not ruin, but nothing. The sages read 'You give life to them all' as 'You bring them into being'. Rest your hands on the table. They are not the smith's finished cup. They are the wall of water, held.
- **From inside:** A sense of being held with no gap in it. Many find it humbling and safe at once: there was never a moment when you were left to keep yourself going.
- **At the edge:** Creation from nothing is 'above nature', a wonder greater than the split sea. The mind can say it but cannot picture it; the chapter sends it to the prophets' 'The spirit of God spoke in me'.
- **Into the day:** When you set something down today and it stays, say: held, not left.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 3 (the sun and its light)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 3 (the sun and its light), lines 799-803.
- **The movement:** It turns the mind from 'the world is here' to 'from the source, it is not there at all'. Then it admits where its own picture fails and leaves the question open.
- **The line to hold** (line 800): > אילו ניתנה רשות לעין לראות ולהשיג את החיות ורוחניות שבכל נברא … לא היה גשמיות הנברא וחומרו וממשו נראה כלל לעינינו > > *If the eye were allowed to see and grasp the life and spirit in each creature ... the physicality, matter and substance of the creature would not be seen by our eyes at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** Sunlight on the earth looks like a real thing. You can stand in it. Inside the sun the same light is there too, yet there it is not called 'light' at all. It is lost in its source. Only the sun is there. So with creatures. We call them 'something' only because our eyes of flesh do not see the source that is saying them. Try it slowly with one thing near you. If your eye could see the word inside it, the wood and the weight would drop from sight, like a sunbeam folded back into the sun. 'If so, there is truly nothing besides Him.' Now the break, which the text points out itself: the sun is far away, but creatures are always inside their source. So why don't they vanish into it? The chapter stops: to understand this, we must first explain...
- **From inside:** A soft loosening of the grip things have on the eye. The world stays, but it looks borrowed.
- **At the edge:** It brings the mind to a real question it cannot yet answer: why does anything stand out at all, if it is always inside Him? The answer waits in the next chapter: the hiding.
- **Into the day:** Look at sunlight on a wall and say: in its source this is not even called light.
- **Ladder:** L03

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 4 (sun and shield)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 4 (sun and shield), lines 804-811.
- **The movement:** It answers the open question: the world stands because God hides. The hiding stops being an absence and becomes an act of His strength.
- **The line to hold** (line 809): > הן הן גבורותיו של הקדושברוךהוא, אשר כל יכול, לצמצם החיות והרוחניות הנשפע מרוח פיו ולהסתירו, שלא יבטל גוף הנברא במציאות > > *This is exactly the might of the Holy One, blessed be He, who can do all: to contract and hide the life and spirit flowing from His mouth, so the creature's body is not nullified out of existence.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'For a sun and a shield is God.' The shield is the sun's sheath, so creatures can bear it. The Name Havayah means He brings all into being, now and always. The Name Elokim is the sheath over it. His might (gevurah) is holding back: the life still flows, but it hides inside the thing, so the thing seems to be 'a thing of its own'. Walk it into your own life. The feeling 'I stand by myself' is not a mistake you made. It is something He is doing, so you are not dissolved. The note in the text calls the hiding 'vessels' and the life 'light'. A vessel covers what is in it. The sheath picture breaks: a sheath is separate from the sun. This hiding is His own act. Nothing outside Him does it.
- **From inside:** Gratitude for being allowed to be a self. The distance you feel from Him stops being a wall and becomes a kindness.
- **At the edge:** No created mind can grasp what the contraction and hiding are, the text says, just as none can grasp creation from nothing. Both are His, above the mind.
- **Into the day:** When you catch yourself thinking 'I did this alone', add: He hid so I could.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 5

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 5, lines 812-814.
- **The movement:** It shows why the hidden world still needs openings. Strict measure alone would not hold, so mercy was joined: Godliness showing through tzaddikim and wonders.
- **The line to hold** (line 812): > ראה שאין העולם מתקיים – שתף בו מדת רחמים״, דהיינו, התגלות אלהות על ידי צדיקים > > *He saw the world could not endure, and joined to it the attribute of mercy, meaning, the revelation of Godliness through tzaddikim.*
- **Contemplating it:** The sages say God first thought to make the world with strict measure only. The text reads it: a world of pure hiding could not last. So there are windows. Tzaddikim, and the signs in the Torah, let the hidden light show a little. Kindness and might are both 'above the mind of creatures'. Even Moshe grasped them only as clothed in lower levels. The souls in Gan Eden are fed by a sky spread out from these two, the secret of the Torah's letters, falling like dew. In your own day: a person who is kind in a plain way, a moment that fits too well to be chance. Small windows in the sheath. Picture a heavy curtain with a few seams of light. The picture breaks because the curtain and the light are the same One.
- **From inside:** Watchfulness for openings. Not hunting for miracles, just noticing where the hidden shows through.
- **At the edge:** Kindness and might are 'above the mind of creatures' and one with Him. The chapter touches this and moves on.
- **Into the day:** Name one person today through whom something good showed that was more than them.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 6 (Havayah is Elokim)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 6 (Havayah is Elokim), lines 815-822.
- **The movement:** It joins the two Names into one: the hiding (Elokim, 'nature') is itself kindness. Then it takes away even the word 'also' from the earth.
- **The line to hold** (line 816): > והרי – בחינת גבורה זו וצמצום הזה, הוא גם כן בחינת חסד שהעולם יבנה בו > > *This might and this contraction is also itself kindness, by which the world is built.*
- **Contemplating it:** Elokim has the same number as hateva, 'nature'. Nature is the hiding that makes the world look like it runs by itself. But this hiding is might 'included in kindness'. You could not be here without it. So it is love. 'Know... that Havayah is Elokim': the Name that makes and the Name that hides are one. So the hiding hides only from us, not from Him. Before Him, heaven and earth are not even an 'also', not even an extra beside Him. The text refuses the soul-in-a-body picture. A soul does not make its body from nothing. Heaven and earth have no root but His word. In your kitchen: water boiling, a door swinging shut. His kindness wearing the face of nature.
- **From inside:** Nature stops feeling cold. What looked like a machine looks like a covering laid down with care.
- **At the edge:** The mind can say 'before Him it is nothing', but it cannot stand where He stands. The text marks the line: the hiding is 'only for those below'.
- **Into the day:** When something works by 'nature' today, a kettle, a sunrise, say: Havayah is Elokim.
- **Ladder:** L05

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 7 (the lower unity; filling and surrounding)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 7 (the lower unity; filling and surrounding), lines 823-848.
- **The movement:** The longest chapter. It gives the lower unity (Baruch Shem): God is found inside place and time, as much below as above. Then it carries the mind to where only faith goes, and ends at the stone.
- **The line to hold** (line 830): > כי כך הוא בארץ מתחת כמו בשמים ממעל ממש > > *For so He is on the earth below just as in the heavens above, truly.*
- **Contemplating it:** Why hide at all? 'There is no king without a people.' A people means those who are other, far from the king. Even many children do not make a kingdom. His Kingship (Malchut, the Name Adnut) is what makes place and time: east and west, up and down; past, present, future. And through it His Essence fills the whole earth in place and time 'equally, truly'. As much down here as in heaven. Take it to your street. The pavement is not less of Him than the sky. Place itself is what He wears to be here. Then the chapter climbs: He knows every creature by knowing Himself, down to 'the smallest worm in the sea'. We know things from outside; He knows from within, and we cannot picture that. And down again to the stone: a very high Name let down through many contractions until a little life can wear rock. That is 'filling'. His knowing, which is each thing's being, truly 'surrounds' it.
- **From inside:** A steadiness in ordinary places. The low place is not farther from Him. Some feel held from two sides: from inside the thing, and all around it.
- **At the edge:** The text says it outright: this is held only 'by faith, which is above the mind and understanding'. The Rambam's 'He is the knower, the known and the knowing' is something 'the mouth cannot say, the ear cannot hear'.
- **Into the day:** Stand on the floor and say: as much here as in heaven.
- **Ladder:** L04 (also L03, L07)

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 8 (the ladder in the soul)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 8 (the ladder in the soul), lines 849-854.
- **The movement:** It empties the mind of its picture of God. We imagine His will and wisdom as they are in us; the chapter shows that this is exactly the mistake.
- **The line to hold** (line 850): > כי האדם מצייר בשכלו כל המושכלות שרוצה להשכיל ולהבין – הכל כמות שהם בו > > *For a person pictures in his mind everything he wants to understand as it is in himself.*
- **Contemplating it:** His will, wisdom, kindness and every trait are 'simple unity', His very Essence, not parts added on. Why can't we hold that? Because we picture wisdom and kindness as they live in us, where they are extras added to a self. Then a ladder inside you. The deed is as nothing next to speech, speech next to thought, thought next to the feeling that drives it, the feeling next to the mind it grows from. Watch it in one small act. A coin given comes from a word, the word from a thought, the thought from caring, the caring from understanding. Five rungs, each as nothing to the one above. Hold that, then hear that He is above wisdom by 'myriads of rungs like these, without end'. The picture breaks on purpose: the ladder is in you. He is not its top rung.
- **From inside:** The mind's images grow quiet. Not forced quiet; the mind sees they were its own reflection.
- **At the edge:** 'The mouth cannot say it, the ear cannot hear it, the heart cannot know it fully.' The mind is left before a oneness it cannot image.
- **Into the day:** Catch one moment today when you picture God as a very large person, and let the picture go without putting another in its place.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 9 ('we have no business with hidden things')

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 9 ('we have no business with hidden things'), lines 855-861.
- **The movement:** It takes the mind to its end and one step past: even saying 'He cannot be grasped' is too small. It hands the rest to faith.
- **The line to hold** (line 858): > והאומר עליו שאי אפשר להשיגו – הוא כאומר על איזו חכמה רמה ועמוקה, שאי אפשר למששה בידים מפני עומק המושג > > *Whoever says of Him that He cannot be grasped is like one who says of some lofty, deep wisdom that it cannot be touched with the hands because it is so deep.*
- **Contemplating it:** Wisdom is the first thing in every creature. Before Him, wisdom counts as 'the end of action', like a deed next to the mind. So to call Him 'too deep to understand' is like saying an idea is too deep to feel with your fingers. 'Anyone who hears would laugh.' Touch doesn't belong there at all. Try it with your own hands on the table. They can hold the table. They cannot hold an idea, not because it is heavy, but because hands are the wrong kind of thing. So is understanding, before Him. Not too weak. The wrong kind of thing. Then the turn: 'We have no business with hidden things.' What is given to us is to believe with whole faith that He and His traits are one. That is why the Zohar calls the Sefirot 'the mystery of faith': the faith above the mind.
- **From inside:** A mind that has run to its wall and stopped straining. Many describe a hush, and under it a trust that does not need to know how.
- **At the edge:** Here the book says it plainly: He is 'not in the category of grasping at all'. What lies past it is faith (emunah), which the text puts above understanding.
- **Into the day:** Put your hand flat on a table and say: this is my mind before Him.
- **Ladder:** L09 (and L07 from its far side)

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 10 (the parable allowed, and limited)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 10 (the parable allowed, and limited), lines 862-867.
- **The movement:** It gives permission to use a picture, the sun and its rays, and at once says how far it falls short. 'The Torah speaks in human language, to soothe the ear.'
- **The line to hold** (line 862): > כי הזיו והאור שם – עצם אחד ממש עם גוף המאור המאיר, ואין לו שום מציאות כלל בפני עצמו > > *For the ray and light there are one substance with the body of the luminary, and it has no existence of its own at all.*
- **Contemplating it:** The body of the sun is 'the luminary'. The rays spreading from it are 'light'. Inside the sun, the ray is one thing with it. So are His traits with Him, 'and more than this'. Then at once: 'but not truly in this way', in a way far beyond us. The traits have names only from our side, because through them He makes and leads the worlds. On the first day kindness showed and made light, spread end to end. On the second, might hid the upper waters from the lower, 'so the dry land would appear, and man on it to serve God'. Your own day has both: the giving, and the hiding that lets you stand on dry land and do something.
- **From inside:** A light hold on pictures: using them, and not leaning on them too hard.
- **At the edge:** Elijah says there is in Him 'no known justice, no known mercy'. The mind is left knowing its best picture only soothes the ear.
- **Into the day:** When you use a picture of God today, add under your breath: not truly in this way.
- **Ladder:** L09

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 11 (the sayings)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 11 (the sayings), lines 868-875.
- **The movement:** It shows that the Ten Sayings are 'sayings' only from our side, and that speech above is higher than any created wisdom.
- **The line to hold** (line 872): > כמו אותיות הדבור באדם על דרך משל, שהן מגלות לשומעים מה שהיה צפון וסתום בלבו > > *Like the letters of speech in a person, which show the listeners what was hidden and closed in his heart.*
- **Contemplating it:** A kind heart cannot act until it thinks. If it asks others to act, it speaks. The kindness goes into the words. So with Him: His traits, when they come out to act, are called 'letters' and 'sayings'. There are hidden worlds, made as if by thought, and revealed worlds, made as if by speech, which shows what was closed in the heart. So the world in front of you is what was hidden in Him, spoken out. And speech above is higher than our wisdom. The saying 'Let us make man' is where our wisdom came from. Twenty-two letters, no more and no less, fixed in mouth and tongue. The picture breaks because His 'letters' are not like our thoughts at all; they are His traits reaching out.
- **From inside:** Listening changes. The world is heard as someone speaking, not as noise.
- **At the edge:** The letters are joined with traits 'united with His Essence', infinitely above created wisdom. The chapter says it and does not open it further.
- **Into the day:** When you speak kindly to someone today, notice: what was closed in me is now open. That is what the world is.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 12 (down to the stone)

- **Where:** Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 12 (down to the stone), lines 876-882.
- **The movement:** It carries the one speech down, step by step, to dust and stone. The book's account of the unity ends at the lowest point.
- **The line to hold** (line 882): > והוריד עד למטה מטה בבחינת השתלשלות, עד שנברא הדומם ממש כאבנים ועפר > > *He drew it down lower and lower through the chain, until the inanimate itself was created, like stones and earth.*
- **Contemplating it:** Each letter is its own kind of life. When letters join into a word, a new, larger life joins them, weighing more than all of them together. The first letter leads. The others are drawn into its light, and a new creature comes to be. Swapped and substituted letters make lower creatures. The picture: moonlight on the ground at night is light of light of the sun. So down and down: a radiance of a radiance of a radiance, until stone and dust, whose names are 'swaps of swaps'. Pick up a pebble. It is the last echo of the first word, still being said.
- **From inside:** Low things become dear. Dust is not outside the word. It is where the word finally arrives.
- **At the edge:** This chapter does not climb to the edge; it goes down to the other edge. The Part ends at the stone.
- **Into the day:** Hold a stone or touch the ground and say: moonlight of the sun.
- **Ladder:** L10 (also L02)

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 1 (return is not the fast)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 1 (return is not the fast), lines 887-898.
- **The movement:** It clears away a mistake about return. Teshuvah is not the fast. It is the heart deciding, whole, not to rebel again.
- **The line to hold** (line 894): > וזהו עיקר פירוש לשון ״תשובה״ לשוב אל ה׳ בכל לבו ובכל נפשו > > *This is the main meaning of the word 'teshuvah': to return to God with all one's heart and all one's soul.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Letter opens with law: three kinds of atonement, depending on the sin. Then its first real point. The Torah's command of return is only leaving the sin: deciding with a whole heart never again to rebel against His kingship. 'Not as the crowd thinks, that teshuvah is the fast.' Return has a direction: to God, with heart and soul, to serve Him. In your own day the question is not how much you suffer for it. It is which way your heart now faces. The picture of 'return' fits a road. It breaks because He was never far down that road; the next chapters say so.
- **From inside:** Relief. The weight moves from punishment to direction.
- **At the edge:** This chapter stays with law and does not reach the edge. It leaves one door open: a positive mitzvah draws light 'from the radiance of the Infinite Light', and a missed one leaves that light missing.
- **Into the day:** Name one thing you want to stop, and say only: I am turning toward You.
- **Ladder:** none

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 2 (wanted again)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 2 (wanted again), lines 899-905.
- **The movement:** It moves from being forgiven to being wanted again. Past pardon there is being dear to Him as before.
- **The line to hold** (line 900): > שיהיה לרצון לפני ה׳ ומרוצה וחביב לפניו יתברך כקודם החטא > > *That he be desired before God, pleasing and beloved before Him as before the sin.*
- **Contemplating it:** Forgiveness wipes the debt. The text says there is more: to be wanted again. Its picture: a man who wronged a king was pardoned through advocates. Still he sends a gift, so the king will be glad to see his face. Once there were offerings for this. Now the fast stands in their place: 'may the fat and blood I lost be as if I offered them'. The fast, the text says, is 'a wonderful way to reveal the supernal will', like an offering 'pleasing to God'. In your own life: after an apology is accepted, the wish to be close again remains. That wish is holy. The king picture breaks: He needs no gift. The gift changes the giver, so the face can open.
- **From inside:** A longing to be near, not only to be clear.
- **At the edge:** It stops at 'the revelation of the supernal will'. It does not climb further here.
- **Into the day:** After you make something right with someone, do one small kind thing more.

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 3 (the limits of fasting)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 3 (the limits of fasting), lines 906-918.
- **The movement:** It sets limits with gentleness. Fasting must not harm; charity can take its place. The body is not to be broken for the sake of return.
- **The line to hold** (line 913): > כל בעל נפש החפץ קרבת ה׳, לתקן נפשו להשיבה אל ה׳ > > *Whoever has a soul that wants closeness to God, to mend his soul and bring it back to God.*
- **Contemplating it:** The later teachers counted many fasts for each sin. The text records the accepted middle way. Then it turns: all this is for a strong, healthy person. One whom many fasts might make ill is forbidden to fast much, even for grave sins. A Torah scholar who weakens himself 'sins twice', because he cannot learn. The way out: redeem the fasts with charity, and do not worry about the one-fifth limit, since this is healing for the soul. Still, one who 'wants closeness' may once in his life finish a count, spread over short winter days, as his strength allows. In your own life: caring for the body is part of return, not against it.
- **From inside:** Kindness toward yourself inside the work of return, without making return cheap.
- **At the edge:** This is practical law and does not reach the edge.
- **Into the day:** Give a coin today in place of a harshness you might have used on yourself.
- **Ladder:** none

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 4 (the soul as a part of the Name)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 4 (the soul as a part of the Name), lines 919-937.
- **The movement:** It shows the soul's root: a part of the Name itself, blown from within, not spoken from outside. Then it maps the four letters of the Name onto a person.
- **The line to hold** (line 922): > חלק משם הוי׳ ברוךהוא, כדכתיב: ״ויפח באפיו נשמת חיים״, ו״מאן דנפח מתוכו נפח > > *A part of the Name Havayah, as it says, 'He blew into his nostrils the breath of life', and 'one who blows, blows from within'.*
- **Contemplating it:** Speak a word and a little breath leaves you, carrying a little of you. Blow hard and the breath comes from deep inside. Angels live from the outside, from the letters; they are called Elokim. The soul comes from the inside of His life. It is part of Havayah. The four letters in you: the yud is your hidden mind, able to grasp His greatness. The first hei is understanding spreading wide and long, giving birth to love and awe. The vav and the last hei are the service that flows out in voice, word and deed: Torah and mitzvot. The breath picture breaks: He has no body. 'The Torah speaks in human language.'
- **From inside:** Dignity at the root. Whatever you have done, what you are came from His inside.
- **At the edge:** The tip of the yud points to the supernal will, 'far above' wisdom. The chapter names it and does not enter.
- **Into the day:** Before a hard word today, remember what you are made of: breath blown from His inside.

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 5 (the rope)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 5 (the rope), lines 938-946.
- **The movement:** The rope: the soul hangs from above, one end tied high, one end here. Nothing in the world blocks it, except going against His will.
- **The line to hold** (line 941): > אך באמת, אין שום דבר גשמי ורוחני חוצץ לפניו יתברך > > *But in truth, nothing physical or spiritual stands as a barrier before Him.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'Jacob is the rope of His inheritance.' A rope, tied above, reaching down. If you blow toward a place and something stands in between, the breath does not arrive. Is anything standing between the body and the breath above? In truth, no. 'I fill heaven and earth.' 'No place is empty of Him.' Only sins separate, because they go against the will that gives life to all. The rope is woven of 613 thin cords. A sin cuts one. Karet cuts the rope. Even then a trace remains. Sit where you are. Between you and Him there is no wall, no distance, no stone.
- **From inside:** The feeling of distance loses its authority. It is still felt, but it is not believed.
- **At the edge:** The will that makes all things is 'the source of the Name's flow', hinted in the tip of the yud. The mind reaches that will and stops.
- **Into the day:** When you feel far today, say: nothing stands between. Only what I do against Him, and that can be retied.

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 6 (pulling the rope)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 6 (pulling the rope), lines 947-957.
- **The movement:** It makes the person the one who moves the divine flow. Deeds pull the rope down into dark places, and return pulls it home.
- **The line to hold** (line 956): > כי בתשובתו מחזיר השפעת השכינה למקומה > > *For by his return he brings the flow of the Shechinah back to its place.*
- **Contemplating it:** When the Temple stood, bodies lived from the Godly soul alone. After the fall, the flow came down through the stars and the mixed realm (Nogah), and so even sinners live. Then the harder picture: pull one end of a rope and the other end moves. A bad deed or thought draws the flow of the soul's root down into 'the chambers of the other side'. That is the exile of the Shechinah, as it were. And return takes it back. 'The last hei returns.' In your life: what you do is not small. It moves something far above you. The rope picture breaks: nothing can truly bind Him. The text says 'as it were'.
- **From inside:** Responsibility without despair. What I pulled down, I can bring back.
- **At the edge:** The text says 'let the one who understands, understand'. It hints at more and leaves it.
- **Into the day:** Undo one small thing today, and say: back to its place.

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 7 (the heart's cry; 'masters of accounting')

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 7 (the heart's cry; 'masters of accounting'), lines 958-973.
- **The movement:** It turns contemplation into the heart's cry. Two things: mercy for the Godliness you dragged down, and breaking the hardness that lives on pride.
- **The line to hold** (line 959): > לעורר רחמים העליונים ממקור הרחמים על נשמתו ונפשו האלקית, שנפלה ״מאיגרא רמה״ > > *To arouse the higher mercies from the Source of mercy on his soul, the Godly soul, which fell 'from a high roof'.*
- **Contemplating it:** The first: have mercy on the part of God in you that fell from the Life of life 'into a deep pit'. The time for it: Tikkun Chatzot, the midnight lament. 'The crown of our head has fallen; woe to us, for we have sinned.' The more a person thinks about the greatness of the Infinite, who fills and surrounds, the more bitter this is. The second: break the kelipah, whose whole life is coarseness and self-raising, with a broken and crushed heart. How? Not mainly by fasting. By being one of the 'masters of accounting': one hour a day or night, think deeply about what your sins did. Thin sheets over a window, many of them, darken like one thick board. So do small sins. Anger, gossip, pride and turning from the poor are counted like idolatry.
- **From inside:** Real sorrow, but sorrow for Him, not for your own image. It is heavy, and it is clean.
- **At the edge:** It reaches the King 'bound in the tresses', as it were, and the pain of that. It does not go past the lament; the next chapter does.
- **Into the day:** Set a short, fixed time to look honestly at one thing, and end it there when the time is up.

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 8 (complete return)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 8 (complete return), lines 974-978.
- **The movement:** After the cry, the union. The soul returns to Him as it was before it was breathed out.
- **The line to hold** (line 977): > ולדבקה בו יתברך ביחוד נפלא, כמו שהיתה מיוחדת בו יתברך בתכלית היחוד, בטרם שנפחה ברוח פיו יתברך > > *To cleave to Him in a wondrous union, as it was united with Him completely before He breathed it out.*

The Thirteen Attributes of mercy come from the will above the letters of the Name. So they clean every flaw, and the last hei returns to its place. Then the soul can rise to its source. The text's picture: breath inside a person, before it leaves the mouth, is one with the person. So was your soul before it was sent. That is where return goes. 'This is complete teshuvah.' The picture breaks only where breath does: His 'breath' never truly left Him.
- **From inside:** A homecoming more than a pardon. Some feel it as simple closeness, without words.

Love is 'spirit cleaving to spirit': feelings to His ways, mind to His wisdom in Torah, speech to halacha, deed to charity. A sin that damages the mind is mended by Torah, which 'comes from wisdom'. Tanna d'Bei Eliyahu: if you used to learn one page, learn two. The rope: where it was cut and tied again, it is thicker than before. Look at your own weak place. After return it can be the strongest part of the rope.

- **Contemplating it:** Higher return comes down from above: the word of God 'in your mouth' through Torah, His right hand embracing through kindness. A person below climbs step by step, by the heart's intention in prayer, most of all in the Shema. After prayer: 'To You I lift my soul', to stay joined all day. Before prayer: 'heaviness of head', humility, lower return. Yet the sages also say to pray only from joy. Its letters spell 'tashev enosh': return, man.

## Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 11 (broken and joyful; forgiven at once)

- **Contemplating it:** Lowliness and joy at once, the text says, and with them trust that He forgives the moment you ask. 'Before me' means at a distance: a reminder not to be proud. The memory even helps joy. It lets you take insults and hard events calmly: 'good advice against anger'. In your day: the same failing again. The text says ask again; it is received again.

## Iggeret HaKodesh 5 (who makes the holy Name)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 5 (who makes the holy Name), lines 1061-1084.
- **The movement:** Who 'makes the holy Name' each day? The one who gives charity. Kindness below draws high wisdom down into this lowest world.
- **The line to hold** (line 1061): > מאן עביד שמא קדישא בכל יומא מאן דיהיב צדקה למסכני > > *Who makes the holy Name every day? One who gives charity to the poor.*
- **Contemplating it:** This world was made with the letter hei: breath, the stuff of speech before it becomes letters. Your lips move by the soul's will to say a letter; the soul does not plan how the lips move. Speech comes from a hidden mind above understanding. That is why a baby who understands still cannot talk. Our world is too small for the inner form of the letters. It gets only the breath, and inside the breath the form hides. Picture a great sage squeezing his whole mind into one letter. That is how great a contraction creation was. At creation no one below woke it up; it was pure kindness. Now kindness below wakes kindness above. Torah without kindness, the sages say, is not even Torah. The text's last picture: a person does not speak to himself, only to others. Kindness is how wisdom speaks to the world.
- **From inside:** Your giving feels like speech. Something in you is being said.
- **At the edge:** Inside the breath are clothed forms 'united with the Infinite completely'. The mind stops at that.
- **Into the day:** Give to someone today and think: I am making the Name.

## Iggeret HaKodesh 6 (equally below; the truth of God)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 6 (equally below; the truth of God), lines 1085-1098.
- **The movement:** God is equally present below as above, and unchanged. From that comes mercy for the spark in the dark body, and through charity the 'truth of God' comes down.
- **The line to hold** (line 1090): > שכמו שהוא יתברך מצוי בעליונים – כך הוא ממש בשוה בתחתונים > > *As He is found in the higher worlds, so He is truly, equally, in the lower.*
- **Contemplating it:** The worlds differ only in how letters are combined. Times and events differ the same way. But He does not change. As He was alone before the six days, so He is now. Everything is as nothing to His Essence, like one letter of your speech next to your whole soul. Even that picture is only to soothe the ear: 'none compare to You'. So the pity is great for the spark living in a dark body, 'the serpent's skin'. There is a love made by thinking, and a love given from above. The given one is 'truth'; all created truth is nothing before it. How is it received? Great mercy for the spark in your own soul, Jacob's trait. And charity, which the text calls sowing: 'truth will sprout from the earth'.
- **From inside:** The low place is not a place of less. And tenderness for your own soul, as for someone lost in the dark.
- **At the edge:** 'God's truth' is given, not produced. The text puts it past the reach of any created mind.
- **Into the day:** In a low, dark hour today, say: He is here equally.
- **Ladder:** L03 (also L13)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 7 (our portion and our lot)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 7 (our portion and our lot), lines 1099-1113.
- **The movement:** How can anyone have a 'portion' in God, who has no parts? He Himself fills heaven and earth, yet creatures live from a radiance; and each soul is given a particular light, like a lot.
- **The line to hold** (line 1101): > ״אני״ ממש, דהיינו מהותו ועצמותו כביכול ולא כבודו לבד > > *'I' truly: meaning His Being and Essence, as it were, and not His glory alone.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'Happy are we, how good is our portion, how pleasant our lot.' 'Do I not fill heaven and earth?' 'I': Himself, not only His glory. Yet He is holy and apart, not caught inside the world the way a soul is caught in a body. So creatures live from a radiance of His Name, which comes down as 613 kinds of light for the 613 parts of the soul. Each soul, and each generation, has a mitzvah it guards more than the rest. 'In what was your father most careful?' That special light is above reason, like a lot drawn. Yours was chosen for you.
- **From inside:** Feeling chosen without pride. Your particular thing matters because it was given.
- **At the edge:** 'His Essence, not His glory alone' stands next to 'a radiance of His Name' in one letter. The mind holds the two without joining them.
- **Into the day:** Name the mitzvah you feel most drawn to, and do it with extra care today.
- **Ladder:** L14

## Iggeret HaKodesh 8 (the seed)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 8 (the seed), lines 1114-1124.
- **The movement:** Charity is like a seed that rots in the earth and becomes one with the earth's power, and far more comes up than was sown. Prayer's light comes as a gift.
- **The line to hold** (line 1119): > שנרקבין בארץ וכלה כל כחם בכח הצומח שבארץ, ונתאחדו והיו לאחדים > > *They rot in the earth, and all their power is spent into the earth's power of growth, and they become one.*
- **Contemplating it:** Rabbi Elazar gave a coin to a poor man, then prayed: 'Through charity I will see Your face.' The light that shines in your prayer is given as charity, as a gift. The higher worlds are nullified in His light; from there it shines down on those who pray. The seed: its own being rots away and joins the earth's power. Then the earth brings up a stalk with many grains, better than the seed. 'Wherever you find His greatness, there you find His humility', like water running downhill. In your life: what you give disappears. It is supposed to. It becomes one with a power bigger than you.
- **From inside:** A willingness to disappear into what you give, and then surprise at what grows.
- **At the edge:** The seed's self-emptying is the condition of growth. The text also names the Holy Land as the place 'where nothing blocks'. It stays with the picture.
- **Into the day:** Give something today and do not follow it; let it rot into the earth.
- **Ladder:** L06 (also L12)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 9 (the Shema with open eyes)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 9 (the Shema with open eyes), lines 1125-1127.
- **The movement:** The Shema said with closed eyes must still be true with open eyes. Business is for giving life, and in the last times the main service is the deed of charity.
- **The line to hold** (line 1125): > שגם בעניני ארץ לא יפרידו … מאחד האמת > > *That even in earthly matters they not separate from the One Truth.*
- **Contemplating it:** In the morning, eyes covered, you say 'God is One', in four directions, above and below. Then you open your eyes and, as it were, 'fly your eyes over Him and He is not there'. The text calls that false testimony. So let your dealings not be 'for themselves' but to give life to souls, parts of God. That is how you resemble the One, whose kindness gives life to the world every moment. Now, in the heels of Moshiach, the Shechinah has fallen to its heels. It is reached by action: giving life down to one who has nothing. Your desk, your wallet, your shop: the same One as in the Shema.
- **From inside:** The Shema no longer ends when the Shema ends.
- **At the edge:** Giving that overcomes the self turns 'darkness into the light of God'. The text says it and stops.
- **Into the day:** At work today, say once: this is for giving life.
- **Ladder:** L13 (also L07)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 10 (great kindness)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 10 (great kindness), lines 1128-1142.
- **The movement:** There is bounded kindness and boundless kindness. Every world is 'poor' before Him, having nothing of its own; so boundless giving to the poor draws the boundless light.
- **The line to hold** (line 1137): > שכולם הם בבחינת דלי״ת אצלו יתברך, דלית להון מגרמיהון כלום > > *For all of them are as 'dalet' before Him: they have nothing of their own.*
- **Contemplating it:** The mitzvot come to us through His might, so they have measures: tzitzit of twelve thumbs, a sukkah of seven handbreadths. Even love and awe are bounded. Boundless love would not let the soul stay in the body, as at Sinai when 'their souls flew'. Charity also has measures: a fifth, a tenth. But one who has damaged needs the light above the worlds, 'great kindness' (rav chesed), which has no measure. It is drawn by giving without measure to the 'dal', the poor one who has nothing of his own. And every world is that poor one before Him. So are you.
- **From inside:** Humility and freedom together: you have nothing of your own, so you can give without counting.
- **At the edge:** That light 'surrounds them from above, from the top of all levels to the end'. The text names the surrounding light as the only full repair.
- **Into the day:** Give once today without working out the amount first.
- **Ladder:** L06 (also L04)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 11 ('no evil comes down from above')

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 11 ('no evil comes down from above'), lines 1143-1151.
- **The movement:** The Tanya's contemplation for pain. Picture yourself being made from nothing this moment, out of His wisdom, the source of all good; then nothing evil comes down from above.
- **The line to hold** (line 1144): > וכשיתבונן האדם בעומק הבנתו, ויצייר בדעתו הווייתו מ״אין״ בכל רגע ורגע ממש, האיך יעלה על דעתו כי רע לו > > *When a person contemplates in the depth of his understanding and pictures in his mind his coming into being from nothing at every single moment, how could it enter his mind that things are bad for him?*
- **Contemplating it:** 'Nullify your will.' Not wanting anything of this world for its own sake: children, life, food. Creation from nothing is happening at every moment, from His wisdom, the 'nothing' that is the source of life, good and delight. Picture it: you, right now, coming out of that nothing. What reaches you from there is good, only too great to grasp. So it looks bad. 'This is the heart of the faith for which man was created': no place is empty of Him. So joy comes first. The text says one who mopes shows he feels something is missing, 'like a denier'. For the believer, yes and no are equal. And by this faith the seeming bad is truly taken up and raised into the hidden good.
- **From inside:** Not numbness, but living by faith, 'rejoicing at every moment'. The pain is held inside something larger. Described here, not demanded: the text itself says it is 'not grasped'.
- **At the edge:** The hidden good is 'the Eden above the World to Come', not grasped. The text calls the same act deep contemplation and faith, and does not rank them.
- **Into the day:** When something hurts today, say: from the nothing, now; too good to grasp.
- **Ladder:** L02 (also L05)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 12 (fire and water; wine on its lees)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 12 (fire and water; wine on its lees), lines 1152-1170.
- **The movement:** Fire and water make peace when the Infinite shows in them. Charity as 'deed' makes peace for a while; charity as 'service', against one's nature, settles the lees for good.
- **The line to hold** (line 1154): > והיו לאחדים ממש, ובטלים באורו יתברך המאיר להם בבחינת גילוי > > *They become truly one and are nullified in His light, which shines to them openly.*
- **Contemplating it:** Michael is water, flowing down. Gabriel is fire, rising up. Opposites. They do not put each other out, because the Infinite, which is not a trait at all, shines in them. Then they are one. Charity wakes that light. In prayer a person tastes a little of the good separated from the bad. But after prayer, the text says soberly, the bad can easily stir up again. 'Deed' is the kindness that comes naturally. 'Service' is giving more than your nature, against your own will. Wine left to stand: the lees sink, the wine clears. That is what service does.
- **From inside:** A settling. Not a flash of feeling, but clearer wine.
- **At the edge:** The Infinite is 'not a trait at all', above even the source of the traits. Opposites join there, and only there.
- **Into the day:** Give a little more than you naturally would, and notice what settles.
- **Ladder:** L08

## Iggeret HaKodesh 13 (right and left; the hidden good)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 13 (right and left; the hidden good), lines 1171-1184.
- **The movement:** Right and left souls, open and hidden. Everyone needs both. The hidden good is as great as the open, and great kindness has no opposite because it comes from what surrounds all.
- **The line to hold** (line 1180): > אף שהוא טוב הגנוז וצפון, אףעלפיכן, הוא רב וגדול מאד > > *Even though it is good that is stored and hidden, still it is very great.*
- **Contemplating it:** Some serve God quietly and with limits: 'walk humbly'. Some serve wide open, without limit. Everyone must hold both. Beit Shammai were strict by root, yet lenient in some things. Beit Hillel were lenient, yet strict in some. Above, 'there is no cutting and no separation'. Avraham, who was love, showed fear at the binding of Isaac. The hidden good in quiet people is as great as the open good of generous people. What is hidden in a soul is not inside the body. It surrounds from above and shines into mind and heart when it is needed for a deed.
- **From inside:** Respect for your own quiet side, and for other people's.
- **At the edge:** 'Great kindness' has no judgment set against it, because it comes from 'surrounding all worlds' and 'the most hidden of all hidden'.
- **Into the day:** Notice one quiet person today and credit the good you cannot see.
- **Ladder:** L08 (also L04)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 14 (a new light every year)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 14 (a new light every year), lines 1185-1195.
- **The movement:** Every year a new light comes down that never shone before, and every morning prayer draws higher. Its showing waits on what people below do.
- **The line to hold** (line 1189): > ובכל שנה ושנה יורד ומאיר מחכמה עילאה אור חדש ומחודש, שלא היה מאיר עדיין מעולם > > *Every single year a new and renewed light comes down and shines from supernal wisdom, which has never yet shone.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'The eyes of God are always on it, from the beginning of the year to the end of the year.' Why 'the end of the year', and not forever? Because each year's light goes back to its root on the eve of Rosh Hashanah. Then, through the shofar and prayer, a higher light comes, never seen before. The same in small: each morning prayer brings higher light than yesterday's. Because He and His wisdom are one, and the Infinite has no end of height. In your life: today is not a repeat. It has never been lit this way.
- **From inside:** Freshness. The day is new, not recycled.
- **At the edge:** The light hides in 'the land of life'; its showing 'depends on the deeds of those below'. The text leaves it waiting on us.
- **Into the day:** In the morning, say: this light never shone before.
- **Ladder:** L02

## Iggeret HaKodesh 15 ('from my flesh I see God'; dust and ashes)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 15 ('from my flesh I see God'; dust and ashes), lines 1196-1229.
- **The movement:** 'From my flesh I see God': the soul's traits let us sense the Sefirot. Then Avraham's 'dust and ashes' breaks the likeness. What is left is acknowledgment where understanding ends.
- **The line to hold** (line 1221): > ואף שאין אנו משיגים איך הוא הכל אפס ממש קמיה, אףעלפיכן, מודים אנחנו בהודאה אמיתית שכן הוא באמת לאמיתו > > *Even though we do not grasp how all is truly nothing before Him, still we acknowledge with a true acknowledgment that it is so, in utter truth.*
- **Contemplating it:** Your soul holds the ten Sefirot in small, so you can learn a little of them from yourself. But Avraham said 'I am dust and ashes'. Ash is what is left of a great tree after fire, water and air are gone. It is the tree's own substance, yet nothing like the tree. So is our love, even Avraham's, next to love above: 'separated by thousands of separations'. The father teaching his son: he breaks the idea into small pieces, grinds it like wheat, pushes past his own impatience, and binds his mind to his son's with love. In the Godly soul all traits are 'for God alone'. Hod is bowing and admitting that all is nothing before Him, even without grasping how. And da'at must stay fixed: look away from the idea for a moment and the feeling it bore goes back into hiding.
- **From inside:** The honest relief of saying 'I do not understand, and it is true'. And a patience like a father's.
- **At the edge:** This is the Alter Rebbe's form of the edge: hoda'ah, acknowledgment where grasping stops.
- **Into the day:** Say once today, about something you cannot understand: I acknowledge that it is true.
- **Ladder:** L07 (also L06)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 16 (hard times)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 16 (hard times), lines 1230-1239.
- **The movement:** A letter in hard times: do not close your hand. If we do not have mercy on them, who will? Mercy below wakes mercy above.
- **The line to hold** (line 1232): > ואם אנו לא נרחם עליהם חס ושלום, מי ירחם עליהם > > *And if we do not have mercy on them, who will have mercy on them?*
- **Contemplating it:** Times are hard; people are borrowing to eat. Still, some hands that were always open have closed. 'Your life comes first' applies only when both need the same thing equally, like one jug of water in the desert. The poor man's bread, firewood and warm clothes come before your fine clothes and meat. We all need mercy from heaven every hour. The one who holds back his mercy, for whatever reason, causes it to be held back above. And 'no one on earth is righteous and never sins'; charity shields. In your house: the extra you would spend on yourself is someone's bread.
- **From inside:** The heart's cry turned outward: who else will?
- **At the edge:** This letter does not reach the edge. Its one tie is that mercy below wakes mercy above.
- **Into the day:** Before one purchase for yourself this week, give first.
- **Ladder:** none

## Iggeret HaKodesh 17 ('Your commandment is very wide')

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 17 ('Your commandment is very wide'), lines 1240-1250.
- **The movement:** Charity is 'Your commandment', the one God Himself does always. It is wide: a vessel for the light that will shine without limit at the resurrection.
- **The line to hold** (line 1243): > מה שהקדושברוךהוא בכבודו ובעצמו עושה תמיד להחיות העולמות > > *What the Holy One, blessed be He, in His glory and Himself, does always: giving life to the worlds.*
- **Contemplating it:** Mercy below brings the 'fruits' in this world: children, life, food. The 'principal' is kept for the World to Come, and beyond it, for the resurrection. 'Your commandment is very wide': charity is God's own commandment, what He Himself does all the time. Gan Eden is a bounded light, understanding by measure. The resurrection will be the surrounding light, without measure. The highest, the crown, called 'nothing', shows exactly when the soul is back in a pure body. 'Their beginning is lodged in their end.' So when you give, you do what He is doing every moment.
- **From inside:** Being joined to His own act. Giving feels like standing next to Him.
- **At the edge:** The crown, 'nothing', beyond wisdom, revealed only in a body. The text puts the edge in the body, at the end of days.
- **Into the day:** When you give, say: this is what He does always.
- **Ladder:** L13 (also L04)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 18 (two loves)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 18 (two loves), lines 1251-1261.
- **The movement:** Two loves: love in delights, a gift that comes after the toil of awe; and the hidden longing in every Jew, even the wicked, like a child longing for his father, like fire rising.
- **The line to hold** (line 1259): > כבן הכוסף להיות תמיד אצל אביו, וכמו אש העולה למעלה תמיד בטבעה למקורה > > *Like a son who longs to be always with his father, and like fire that always rises by its nature to its source.*
- **Contemplating it:** One love is delight in God, a taste of the World to Come. Not everyone reaches it. Toil cannot reach it; it falls from above after you have worked at awe. The second is longing: the soul wants to be near and hates to be far. It is hidden in every Jew's heart, even in the wicked. Regret comes from it. It is in exile in the body. First drive the husk out of thought, speech and deed. Then think, always, how He is the Life of all life, and your soul's life in particular. The longing grows until it reaches the mouth and the limbs, in Torah and mitzvot, 'for Torah and the Holy One are all one'.
- **From inside:** Thirst: 'my soul thirsts for You', like one who has no water yet. The text says there is no delight in it yet. Only wanting.
- **At the edge:** Love in delights is a gift, and it comes only 'to dwell and unite with the awe'. Toil and gift meet there.
- **Into the day:** Once today, say: I want to be near You. Let the wanting be enough.
- **Ladder:** L01 (also L11)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 19 (the back and the face)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 19 (the back and the face), lines 1262-1284.
- **The movement:** Moshe saw the 'back'; the sage can grasp higher by wisdom. The reasons of the mitzvot, and every story in the Torah, clothe a wisdom above understanding.
- **The line to hold** (line 1270): > הן דברי תוכחה והן סיפורי מעשיות, מלובש בתוכם בחינת חכמת אלהות שלמעלה מהשכל וההבנה > > *Whether words of rebuke or stories, a Godly wisdom above understanding is clothed in them.*
- **Contemplating it:** Prophecy is 'seeing', and only lower levels can come down to be seen. Wisdom can reach higher. 'A sage is better than a prophet.' The reasons of the mitzvot were not revealed. Even where a reason is given, 'the reason we understand is not the whole reason'. Every story in Scripture holds divine wisdom beyond the mind. The written form (ketiv) is above understanding; the read form (kri) wears a garment. The face of each Sefirah is one with the Infinite. What comes down to creatures is its back. All that comes down is as nothing before the face. The Torah that came down is for doing: every person returns until all 613 are done in deed.
- **From inside:** Respect for a verse you do not understand. It is not empty; it is full above you.
- **At the edge:** 'You will see My back, and My face will not be seen.' The chapter shows the edge exactly where Moshe stood.
- **Into the day:** Read one Torah story and say: there is more in this than I can see.
- **Ladder:** L14

## Iggeret HaKodesh 20 (only He makes something from nothing)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 20 (only He makes something from nothing), lines 1285-1321.
- **The movement:** The book's strongest word on the Essence: only He, whose being is from Himself, can make something from absolute nothing. And His power shows most in the lowest earth, which sprouts every year.
- **The line to hold** (line 1295): > ולכן הוא לבדו בכחו ויכלתו לברוא יש מאין ואפס המוחלט ממש > > *Therefore He alone, by His power and ability, can create something from absolute nothing.*
- **Contemplating it:** A chain of causes cannot make matter. However many steps you take down from spirit, you get thinner spirit, not a stone. His being is from Himself, caused by nothing. So only He makes 'something' out of nothing. From above, the created thing is nothing. From below, it is truly separate. The text says both, one after the other. The earth shows the power most: it brings plants up from nothing every year, forever. The stars only tell a sprout to grow; they cannot tell nothing to become something. Why the earth? Because right under the 'feet' of the first spiritual man, at the bottom of all, the surrounding Infinite shines. Look at a plant on your windowsill. That growing is the plainest trace of the One who simply is.
- **From inside:** Wonder at plain things. The more ordinary, the more straight it points to Him.
- **At the edge:** The Essence, 'whose existence is from Himself'. The mind can only say it; the text grounds it in soil.
- **Into the day:** Water a plant or look at grass and say: something from nothing; only He.
- **Ladder:** L10 (also L03)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 21 (give often)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 21 (give often), lines 1322-1329.
- **The movement:** Give often, not all at once. Each gift draws life to the fallen Shechinah and lifts her from the dust, little by little.
- **The line to hold** (line 1329): > להקימה מעפר מעט מעט > > *To raise her from the dust, little by little.*
- **Contemplating it:** The Rambam: a thousand gifts of one coin are better than one gift of a thousand, because many acts refine the soul. The text adds: 'the deed of charity is for life'. It draws life from the Life of life to the Shechinah. The Shechinah is 'the fallen booth of David', fallen to the dust. Each gift makes a union above. Many gifts, many unions. 'Refining the soul': the Shechinah is called 'soul', for she is our life. Your small weekly coin is a hand under her arm, raising her a little.
- **From inside:** Patience. Redemption comes like lifting someone slowly from the ground.
- **At the edge:** It does not reach the edge; it stays with the deed and with the Shechinah in exile.
- **Into the day:** Give a small amount every day this week instead of once.
- **Ladder:** L13

## Iggeret HaKodesh 22 (face to face; one body)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 22 (face to face; one body), lines 1330-1351.
- **The movement:** Two short letters. In pain, turn your face toward the Father, not away. And Israel is one body: a bad thought about another is to be pushed away like a thought of idolatry.
- **The line to hold** (line 1335): > רק להיות ״ישר יחזו פנימו״ עם אביו פנים בפנים, לסבול הכאותיו באהבה > > *Only to be 'upright; they will see His face', face to face with his father, bearing his blows with love.*
- **Contemplating it:** The letter scolds people who travel from town to town asking sages for worldly advice. That, it says, was for prophets. A wise son, struck by a loving and wise father, does not run to find help against the father. He turns his face to him. 'Face' above means will and desire. The 'other gods' get life only from His back, without desire. The counsel: look into your deeds, and see how great His love is, like a great king washing his only son with his own hands. The picture of a father breaks where pain has no lesson you can see. The text asks only that the face stay turned toward Him, not that the pain be explained. The second letter: we are one body. When one limb is hurt, the heart feels it. Push away a bad thought about a friend 'like smoke'.
- **From inside:** Turning toward instead of away. The text says the hidden love that was inside the rebuke can then show openly.
- **At the edge:** Forgiveness is 'above wisdom', and so is the simple will it asks for in prayer. The text points above reason.
- **Into the day:** When something hurts, turn once to Him in your own words before turning anywhere else. And when a bad thought about a friend comes, let it go like smoke.
- **Ladder:** L05 (also L06)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 23 (ten who learn)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 23 (ten who learn), lines 1352-1365.
- **The movement:** When ten learn, the Shechinah rests on them from above, a light too great to be felt. Felt arousal belongs to the smaller light inside; the resting is beyond it.
- **The line to hold** (line 1356): > ואינו יכול להתלבש בנפש גבולית, כי אם מקיף עליה מלמעלה – מראשה ועד רגלה > > *It cannot clothe itself in a bounded soul, only surround it from above, from its head to its feet.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'Ten who sit and learn, the Shechinah rests among them.' The soul came down for this. One who learns alone gets reward: light inside the soul, sized to it. That is why seekers are moved in prayer. They feel it. The resting on ten is different: boundless, so it cannot come inside. It surrounds, from head to foot. So we do not feel it. 'There is no reward for a mitzvah in this world.' An angel standing among ten Jews, even if they were not learning, would be so awed that it would cease to be. And idle talk in that place shames the Shechinah, who stays even so, 'a king bound in the tresses'.
- **From inside:** Something you do not feel may still be the greatest thing present. The text asks you not to measure by feeling.
- **At the edge:** The surrounding light (makif), stated plainly: it cannot be grasped in this world.
- **Into the day:** Join ten for learning or prayer and think: surrounded, head to foot, whether I feel it or not.
- **Ladder:** L04

## Iggeret HaKodesh 24 (the King's hour)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 24 (the King's hour), lines 1366-1374.
- **The movement:** Prayer is the hour the King chooses to appear. His glory wraps itself in the words of the ordinary prayer book, shown to each person by his understanding.
- **The line to hold** (line 1368): > המתעטף ומתלבש בתוך תיבות התפלה הסדורה בפי כל > > *Wrapping and clothing Himself in the words of the prayer set in everyone's mouth.*
- **Contemplating it:** Prayer is the time He chose out of the whole day to be revealed. The text's picture: a great king hides in inner rooms, and people wait years to see him. One day he calls everyone in to see his glory. And someone stands in the hall talking about his own affairs. The glory is inside the words of the siddur, the same words for everyone, and it shows to each 'by his understanding'. Talking in prayer 'shows separation', a split the One does not allow. In your siddur: plain printed words are His wrapping.
- **From inside:** The siddur feels inhabited.
- **At the edge:** It stays in the King's hall and does not go past it.
- **Into the day:** At one prayer today, stop at one word and think: He is wrapped here.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Iggeret HaKodesh 25 (anger, Shimi, and the noise in prayer)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 25 (anger, Shimi, and the noise in prayer), lines 1375-1414.
- **The movement:** The contemplation for anger. The one who harms you lives this moment only by God's breath. And a stranger's noise that disturbs your prayer holds a spark of the Shechinah, in exile, pushing you deeper.
- **The line to hold** (line 1378): > כי אילו נסתלק רוח פיו יתברך רגע אחד מרוחו של שמעי – לא יכול לדבר מאומה > > *For if the breath of His mouth withdrew for one moment from Shimi's spirit, he could not have said a thing.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'Whoever is angry is like one who serves idols.' In the moment of anger faith has left: had he believed this came from God, he would not be angry at all. The one who hurt you is guilty for his choice. Still, what happened to you was decreed. More: at that very moment God's breath is in him, keeping him alive. Of Shimi cursing David: 'God told him, curse.' The Besht: the words 'Let there be a firmament' still stand in the sky. So in every created thing, stones too. Even the nations and the husks live from a spark, in exile. The man talking loudly while you pray: a spark of the Shechinah is in his words, in exile. It came so you would pray more deeply, until you no longer hear him.
- **From inside:** Anger loses its grip because its target loses its separateness. Where the heat was, there is often a strange quiet.
- **At the edge:** This belongs to 'the revealed things': simple faith in 'Do I not fill heaven and earth'. The text asks for belief, and warns against trying to work out with human reason how.
- **Into the day:** When someone angers you, say inside: His breath is in him right now.
- **Ladder:** L02 (also L05, L06)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 26 (the law and the husk)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 26 (the law and the husk), lines 1415-1450.
- **The movement:** The halacha is the unity; the object is the husk. When you eat what is permitted, for God's sake, the food becomes letters of Torah and prayer. Learning halacha frees sparks.
- **The line to hold** (line 1430): > ונמצא שנעשה אותיות התורה והתפלה העולה לה׳ – מכח הנברר מהמאכל ההוא > > *It turns out that it becomes letters of Torah and prayer rising to God, from the strength refined out of that food.*
- **Contemplating it:** A Zohar passage seems to call the study of 'forbidden and permitted' the tree of good and evil. The letter proves that cannot be. The answer: the objects belong to the mixed realm, not the learning. 'Forbidden' (assur) means bound; 'permitted' (mutar) means untied. A permitted food eaten for God, or by someone who then learns and prays with its strength, rises. On Shabbat even the mixed realm rises, so we eat meat and drink wine then. Halacha is a spark of the Shechinah, the word of God. Clearing up a hard ruling lifts it out of the husk; the hard question itself is 'from the side of evil'. The higher beings come to hear new Torah from those below, because only people in bodies can do this work.
- **From inside:** Your meal matters. Your learning of a small law matters to the angels.
- **At the edge:** When the refining is done, the inner Torah will lead, 'for the root of the mitzvot is very high in the Infinite'.
- **Into the day:** Before eating, say: may this become words of Torah and prayer.
- **Ladder:** L12 (also L14)

## Iggeret HaKodesh 27 (consolation on a tzaddik's passing)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 27 (consolation on a tzaddik's passing), lines 1451-1469.
- **The movement:** A consolation. The tzaddik's life was faith, awe and love; after his passing it spreads to those bound to him, more than before, like the sun lighting the stars from below the earth.
- **The line to hold** (line 1461): > שחיי הצדיק אינם חיים בשרים כי אם חיים רוחניים, שהם אמונה ויראה ואהבה > > *The life of a tzaddik is not a life of flesh but a spiritual life: faith, awe and love.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'He left life to all the living.' A tzaddik's life is faith, awe and love, not flesh. While he lived, those traits were in a vessel, a body in a place. Students received only the light that shone outside the vessel. After his passing his spirit is in no place. Those bound to him in love can take part of the thing itself. And from his rising, a light shines on them from above, bringing thoughts of return: 'growths of growths'. It is hidden, like the sun lighting the stars from under the earth.
- **From inside:** Grief with a thread in it. What was loved is not gone but spread out.
- **At the edge:** It points to a light that 'does not clothe itself in their minds' but shines from above. Mostly it stays with the teacher and his students; the index marks this letter as thin on the one idea.
- **Into the day:** Do one thing today the way a teacher you loved did it.
- **Ladder:** none

## Iggeret HaKodesh 28 (Miriam and the red heifer)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 28 (Miriam and the red heifer), lines 1470-1478.
- **The movement:** Why Miriam's death is set next to the red heifer: a tzaddik's passing reveals all the hidden toil of his life, and like the heifer's water it purifies even what lies far below.
- **The line to hold** (line 1475): > שכל עמל האדם שעמלה נפשו בחייו למעלה בבחינת העלם והסתר, מתגלה ומאיר בבחינת גילוי מלמעלה למטה בעת פטירתו > > *All the toil a person toiled with his soul in his life, above, in hiding, is revealed and shines openly from above to below at his passing.*
- **Contemplating it:** Offerings on the altar atone only for mistakes that come from the animal soul. The red heifer is burned outside the camp. Its water draws from the highest hidden wisdom, where 'darkness turns into light', so it purifies the impurity of death, the lowest there is. Tzaddikim gave their souls in every Shema. That toil was hidden. At their passing it shows, and it atones even for deliberate sins. In your own life: hidden effort is not lost. It is stored above. The picture breaks because we cannot see the showing. The text says it happens.
- **From inside:** Trust that quiet, unseen effort counts.
- **At the edge:** It reaches 'the hidden brain' of the highest level, above the ordinary order. The text names it and gives no more.
- **Into the day:** Do one hidden good thing today and tell no one.
- **Ladder:** L12

## Iggeret HaKodesh 29 (the crown of Torah; hollow pillars)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 29 (the crown of Torah; hollow pillars), lines 1479-1505.
- **The movement:** No creature can grasp the Creator; the light would put out the soul like a candle before a torch. So the mitzvot become garments, and halachot are 'the crown' because they reveal His will.
- **The line to hold** (line 1494): > כמו העמודים, נצבים מרום המעלות, הוא רצון העליון ברוךהוא, עד הארץ הלזו החומרית > > *Like pillars, standing from the height of all levels, the supernal will, down to this material earth.*
- **Contemplating it:** The soul must wear garments made of all 613 mitzvot, or it cannot bear the light. It would go out 'like a candle before a torch'. So a little of that very light comes down and becomes a garment, like looking at the sun through clear glass. Moshe went into the cloud and saw through it. His will is the outside of His delight. Like us: a new idea gives joy, and the joy is higher than the idea. The mitzvot are like pillars in a great house, reaching from the ceiling to the floor. They are hollow, and the soul rises through them. The Written Torah hides the will; the Oral Torah gives it birth, like a mother bearing what was hidden in the father's seed. Tefillin: the verse does not say how; the halacha does. Your tefillin, your Shabbat: pillars from the highest will down to your floor.
- **From inside:** The mitzvah feels like a place you can stand inside.
- **At the edge:** Beyond the crown is His delight, 'the pleasantness of God'. The garment lets the soul look at it without vanishing.
- **Into the day:** While doing one mitzvah today, picture standing inside a pillar that reaches from your floor to the highest will.
- **Ladder:** L14

## Iggeret HaKodesh 30 (peace joins two ends)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 30 (peace joins two ends), lines 1506-1510.
- **The movement:** Keep giving as you always have. Charity is peace: the thing that joins two opposite ends, the greatness without limit and the world of numbers.
- **The line to hold** (line 1510): > הוא דבר המחבר ומתווך ב׳ קצוות הפכיים > > *It is the thing that joins and mediates between two opposite ends.*
- **Contemplating it:** If someone always comes to shul and one day does not, God asks after him. So with the charity you are used to giving: do not let it shrink. Each coin joins 'a great sum', and 'great is God' when He is in 'the city of our God', the place of counting. The kindness charity draws comes from the Life of life, whose greatness has no end, into the realm where everything is counted. 'Peace' is what joins the two ends of heaven: the top, without limit, and the bottom, with limit and number. Your coin is the joint where the endless meets the counted.
- **From inside:** Steadiness. Keeping a habit becomes a way of holding two ends together.
- **At the edge:** 'His greatness cannot be searched.' The text sets the infinite resting on a small number.
- **Into the day:** Give your usual amount today and hold it as the joint between endless and counted.
- **Ladder:** L08

## Iggeret HaKodesh 31 ('the Shechinah is sick in exile')

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 31 ('the Shechinah is sick in exile'), lines 1511-1519.
- **The movement:** 'The Shechinah is sick in exile.' Souls are her limbs, she is the heart. When souls are bound together, life circulates; when they are divided, she falls ill.
- **The line to hold** (line 1518): > כי כאשר כל הנשמות דבוקות ומקושרות יחד אזי סיבוב והילוך החיות וההשפעה סובב סובב > > *For when all the souls are joined and bound together, then the circling and flowing of life goes round and round.*
- **Contemplating it:** Health is life moving with the blood from the heart to every limb and back. A blockage anywhere, and the person falls ill. The souls of Israel are the Shechinah's limbs. Creatures receive life only through them, since before Him all count as nothing. When souls hold together, life runs round in a circle, 'their end lodged in their beginning', all bound to the One. The Temple fell for 'hatred without cause and division of hearts'. In your life: a grudge with someone blocks a vessel in the one body.
- **From inside:** Your feeling toward another person becomes a matter of the whole body's health.
- **At the edge:** It points to the circuit of 'direct light and returning light', and says it cannot be well explained in a letter.
- **Into the day:** Mend one small division with someone today.
- **Ladder:** L01

## Iggeret HaKodesh 32 (a wick for the light to come)

- **Where:** Iggeret HaKodesh 32 (a wick for the light to come), lines 1520-1529.
- **The movement:** Kindness done now stays in this world until the resurrection, when the surrounding light will shine here. That light needs a vessel, as fire needs a wick; kindness is the wick.
- **The line to hold** (line 1523): > מה אש אינה מאירה בעולם הזה אלא כשנאחזת ומתלבשת בפתילה > > *Fire does not shine in this world except when it takes hold of and clothes itself in a wick.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'His charity stands forever.' All the kindness Jews do now stays alive in this physical world until the resurrection. Then the Infinite Light, the surrounding kind, will shine here, in physical things. For that it needs a vessel, like a body for a soul, like a wick for a flame. The wick is kindness: a heart that gives to one who has nothing. Your small gift today is a length of wick laid down for a light that is coming.
- **From inside:** Patience with a future. What you give is not spent; it is kept.
- **At the edge:** The surrounding light revealed below, in the body, at the resurrection. The text puts the edge in the future, and here.
- **Into the day:** Give something today and think: wick for the light to come.
- **Ladder:** L13

## Kuntres Acharon 1 (speech, not thought alone)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 1 (speech, not thought alone), lines 1533-1538.
- **The movement:** Why speech and deed, not thought alone: thought stays above; speech and deed draw a little light down, and that is why the soul came.
- **The line to hold** (line 1536): > מה שירדה נשמתו לעולם הזה רק להמשיך אורות עליונים למטה > > *His soul came down to this world only to draw higher lights down.*
- **Contemplating it:** How is someone reading the Torah's stories bound to supernal wisdom? Speech breaks through and rises: to the highest world, or lower ones, depending on love and awe. Thought alone 'does nothing' down here: it adds light above but draws nothing down. So one who only thinks the Shema has not fulfilled it. The soul came down to draw light down. Raising things up does need good thought: without love and awe it does not fly. In your life: say the words, even quietly.
- **From inside:** The mouth gets respect. Moving lips are part of the prayer.
- **At the edge:** It speaks of 'the image of the upper man' as the soul's root, and goes no further.
- **Into the day:** Say your blessings with your lips, not only in your head.
- **Ladder:** L14

## Kuntres Acharon 2 (face to face through deeds)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 2 (face to face through deeds), lines 1539-1544.
- **The movement:** Face to face above comes only from practical mitzvot. Deeds cut away the thorns that cling to the back.
- **The line to hold** (line 1539): > שאין החזרת פנים בפנים כי אם עלידי מצות מעשיות דוקא > > *There is no return to face to face except through practical mitzvot, specifically.*
- **Contemplating it:** The upper union, 'face to face', comes only through deeds. Why deeds? First the 'feminine waters' must rise, and they belong to the realm of action. Good deeds clear the thorns that hold on to the 'back'. Speech counts as deed: 'moving the lips is a deed'. Those who know the higher intentions give their very selves as the rising waters, giving their souls in Torah and in bowing down. In your life: the act of the hand turns the face above toward you.
- **From inside:** The deed feels like turning a face.
- **At the edge:** It names self-giving (mesirut nefesh) as what rises highest, and stops.
- **Into the day:** Do one mitzvah with your hands and think: face to face.
- **Ladder:** L14

## Kuntres Acharon 3 (learning without intention)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 3 (learning without intention), lines 1545-1551.
- **The movement:** Torah without intention still rises, unless it is for self-greatness. Even a child's breath, learning for fear of the strap, rises.
- **The line to hold** (line 1551): > לא גרעא מהבל פיהן של תינוקתשלביתרבן, דסליק לעילא מפני שהוא הבל שאין בו חטא > > *It is no worse than the breath of schoolchildren's mouths, which rises because it is breath without sin.*
- **Contemplating it:** Torah or prayer without intention makes angels in lower worlds. Learning in order to be made great stays 'under the sun'. The thought of self clothes the letters and keeps them from rising. Prayer with stray thoughts is easily mended later, because its aim was heaven. Plain learning, from the natural hidden love, rises like children's breath. Schoolchildren learn even out of fear of the teacher's strap, and still their breath rises. In your own learning: when you are tired and unmoved, it still rises, as long as it is not for show.
- **From inside:** Relief for the dry days. Learning counts even when you feel nothing.
- **At the edge:** The angels carry the children's breath up to the highest world. The text leaves it there.
- **Into the day:** Learn a few lines when you are tired, simply, and let it rise.
- **Ladder:** L06

## Kuntres Acharon 4 (the etrog; the highest comes down)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 4 (the etrog; the highest comes down), lines 1552-1609.
- **The movement:** The Tanya's boldest turn. Contemplating His traits reaches only that they exist, not what they are; holding the etrog as the law says holds the life inside it, one with the Infinite. The purpose of everything is the highest coming down.
- **The line to hold** (line 1574): > הרי זה תופס ממש חיותו המלובש בו מנוקבא דאצילות, המיוחדת באוראיןסוף המאציל ברוךהוא > > *He is truly holding its life-force, clothed in it from the Nukva of Atzilut, which is one with the Infinite Light, the Emanator, blessed be He.*
- **Contemplating it:** Prayer draws the light itself to change things: the sick healed, rain falling. Only He, who alone can do all, changes what things are. By thinking about His traits, even with the great love and awe of the mind, you cleave only to that they are, not to what they are. 'I am dust and ashes.' A creature cannot grasp anything of His essence. Without grasping there is no true holding. The etrog's life comes down from the very essence of the highest vessels. Hold it and wave it as the law says, and you hold that. Knowing how the worlds unfold is also a great mitzvah, 'above them all': 'know today'. It brings a whole heart. But it is one of 613. The purpose: not for the lower to rise, but for the highest to come down and have a home below, to raise it 'to be one in One'.
- **From inside:** The mind bows to the hand. Many find it restful: you do not have to understand in order to hold.
- **At the edge:** The mind's own limit is stated: existence, not essence. The deed goes past it. This is the book's plainest 'beyond the mind'.
- **Into the day:** The next time you hold a mitzvah object, tefillin, a candle, a coin for charity, say: I am holding it.
- **Ladder:** L14 (also L13)

## Kuntres Acharon 5 (laws that never happen)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 5 (laws that never happen), lines 1610-1619.
- **The movement:** Even laws that never happen are real in supernal wisdom. Each is a 'hair' drawn from the Infinite clothed in that wisdom.
- **The line to hold** (line 1613): > וכל פרט הלכה, הוא שער נמשך מחכמה עילאה > > *Each detail of halacha is a hair drawn from supernal wisdom.*
- **Contemplating it:** Some laws never happened and never will, like rare cases of a spoiled offering. Every forbidden thing has a root of life in the husks, or it could not exist. Even a man curling his hair for vanity lives that moment from them. A mistake no one ever made still exists in supernal wisdom, which spread into that detail for Moshe at Sinai. Because the spreading of that wisdom is 'the Infinite, clothed in it in actual fact'. Each detail is a hair from the head. Learning it separates the husk from holiness. In the future the prohibitions will be sweetened into the positive commandments and joined with them.
- **From inside:** No detail is too small. Care with details stops feeling like fussiness.
- **At the edge:** The Infinite clothed in each detail. The text states it and does not open it.
- **Into the day:** Learn one tiny detail of a law today and treat it as a hair of the King.
- **Ladder:** L14

## Kuntres Acharon 6 (David's songs)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 6 (David's songs), lines 1620-1634.
- **The movement:** David sang the Torah's praise: all worlds hang on one detail. He was punished, because that is only the Torah's back. Its inside is God's own delight, before which the worlds are not even worth mentioning.
- **The line to hold** (line 1625): > ואיך כולם בטלים במציאות לגבי דקדוק אחד מדקדוקי תורה > > *And how they are all nullified in existence before a single fine point of the Torah.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'Your statutes were songs to me.' Why was David punished for calling them songs? If an offering's blood is received in the left hand, all the worlds lose their rising. One detail holds them all. Think on it: the vast worlds and their hosts, and all of them as nothing before one fine point of law. This was David's joy in his troubles. But that praise comes from the Torah's back. The inside of the Torah is one with the Infinite, and before the Infinite the worlds are nothing, so they cannot even serve as its praise. The inner Torah is 'the King's delight', 'playing before Him'. The repair was to carry the ark on the shoulders, joining the back to the face.
- **From inside:** Song, then silence. The joy of how much a detail holds, then a hush before what is above even that.
- **At the edge:** The inner Torah is 'hidden from the eyes of all living', His own delight known 'in knowing Himself'. The mind stops at 'before Him'.
- **Into the day:** Sing one verse today as a song, and then be quiet for a moment.
- **Ladder:** L03 (also L09)

## Kuntres Acharon 7 ('charity like a mighty stream')

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 7 ('charity like a mighty stream'), lines 1635-1640.
- **The movement:** In the future, the Infinite and His unity will shine inside the heart's inner point. Now that spark is in exile. Mercy for it, and a gift of gold or silver, draw a little of that light in.
- **The line to hold** (line 1635): > שיתגלה אז אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא ויחודו יתברך תוך פנימית נקודת הלב > > *That then the Infinite Light and His unity will be revealed within the inner point of the heart.*
- **Contemplating it:** 'Charity like a mighty stream (eitan).' The letters of eitan mean 'I will be revealed'. In the future the light will shine into the heart's inner point, and the heart will be nullified in His unity from its depths. Now, too, there is counsel to let a little in. As long as that has not happened, the spark in your soul is truly in exile. Have great mercy on it. Mercy below wakes mercy above. So giving physical gifts, gold and silver, is itself the mighty stream. 'All that a man has he will give for his soul', his Godly soul.
- **From inside:** Tenderness toward your own depths. The heart is felt as a place waiting to be lit.
- **At the edge:** Self-nullification in the heart's inner point 'until the soul's expiry'. That is the edge, set in the future and tasted now.
- **Into the day:** Give to someone and say: for the spark in me.
- **Ladder:** L11

## Kuntres Acharon 8 (prayer is the main service now)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 8 (prayer is the main service now), lines 1641-1648.
- **The movement:** A rebuke about prayer leaders, and inside it: in the last times the main service is prayer, and some contemplation before the Shema is a Torah duty, to bring the hidden love into the heart.
- **The line to hold** (line 1644): > לעורר בהן האהבה המסותרת בלב כל ישראל, לבא לבחינת גילוי בהתגלות הלב בשעת קריאתשמע עצמה > > *To awaken with them the love hidden in the heart of all Israel, so it comes to be revealed in the heart at the time of the Shema itself.*
- **Contemplating it:** Do not push away the leader who prays at length. Lengthening prayer lengthens life. In early generations Torah was the main service. Now, in the heels of Moshiach, it is prayer. For those who understand, some contemplation, each by his measure, in the Psalms and the blessings before the Shema, is 'a real Torah duty'. Its aim: the love hidden in every Jew comes out and is felt in the heart during the Shema itself. That is the love the Rambam counts first of the 613. When the love reaches the left side of the heart, where the animal soul lives, it turns it or bends it. Once a moment was enough; now it takes longer.
- **From inside:** The Shema feels like the place where the hidden becomes open.
- **At the edge:** It stays close to the heart and the prayer. The edge here is the heart's opening, not beyond it.
- **Into the day:** Take a few more minutes in the blessings before the Shema, and think one thought through.
- **Ladder:** L07

## Kuntres Acharon 9 (communal rules; the last line on Shabbat)

- **Where:** Kuntres Acharon 9 (communal rules; the last line on Shabbat), lines 1649-1660.
- **The movement:** Communal rules, then the last line of the Tanya: the inside of Shabbat. 'Remember' is cleaving to the One; 'Keep' is resting from ordinary speech, as God rested from the ten sayings.
- **The line to hold** (line 1660): > ובחינת ״שמור״ בפנימיות – היא השביתה מדיבורים גשמיים, כמו ששבת ה׳ מיו״ד מאמרות > > *The inner side of 'Keep' is resting from mundane speech, as God rested from the ten sayings.*
- **Contemplating it:** The letter pleads: pray together, word by word, not one this way and another that way. Choose steady prayer leaders. Finish the whole Talmud each year. Know the laws of Shabbat. Then: every mitzvah has an outside and an inside. Shabbat's outside is resting from physical work, as God rested from making heaven and earth. Its inner 'Remember' is prayer and Torah on Shabbat, to cleave to the One God. Its inner 'Keep' is resting from mundane speech, as God rested from the ten sayings that made heaven and earth. The book that began its account of the world with the word that makes it, ends with the word that rests.
- **From inside:** A quiet that is not empty. Speech stops, and what speech was covering comes near.
- **At the edge:** The last line does not say how God 'rested' from sayings that, in Shaar HaYichud, still stand in everything. It leaves the question open, and so does this reading.
- **Into the day:** This Shabbat, keep one hour free of ordinary talk.
- **Ladder:** L02 (also L09)

## 3. The ten deepest contemplations, ranked

1. **Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 1 (the Besht's 'Your word stands')** (line 785): כי אילו היו האותיות מסתלקות כרגע חס ושלום וחוזרות למקורן, היו כל השמים אין ואפס ממש *If the letters withdrew for an instant and returned to their source, all the heavens would be truly nothing and naught.* The world is being spoken right now; stop the word and there is nothing. The widest contemplation in the book; every other one stands on it.
2. **Iggeret HaKodesh 11 ('no evil comes down from above')** (line 1144): וכשיתבונן האדם בעומק הבנתו, ויצייר בדעתו הווייתו מ״אין״ בכל רגע ורגע ממש, האיך יעלה על דעתו כי רע לו *When a person contemplates in the depth of his understanding and pictures in his mind his coming into being from nothing at every single moment, how could it enter his mind that things are bad for him?* Picture yourself coming from nothing this moment, out of the source of good; then nothing evil comes down. The Tanya's contemplation for pain.
3. **Kuntres Acharon 4 (the etrog; the highest comes down)** (line 1574): הרי זה תופס ממש חיותו המלובש בו מנוקבא דאצילות, המיוחדת באוראיןסוף המאציל ברוךהוא *He is truly holding its life-force, clothed in it from the Nukva of Atzilut, which is one with the Infinite Light, the Emanator, blessed be He.* The mind reaches only that He is; the hand holding the etrog holds the life that is one with the Infinite. The book's plainest step beyond the mind.
4. **Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah, ch. 9 ('we have no business with hidden things')** (line 858): והאומר עליו שאי אפשר להשיגו – הוא כאומר על איזו חכמה רמה ועמוקה, שאי אפשר למששה בידים מפני עומק המושג *Whoever says of Him that He cannot be grasped is like one who says of some lofty, deep wisdom that it cannot be touched with the hands because it is so deep.* Even 'He cannot be grasped' is wrong, like saying an idea is too deep to touch. The mind meets its own wall and hands over to faith.
5. **Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 5 (the rope)** (line 941): אך באמת, אין שום דבר גשמי ורוחני חוצץ לפניו יתברך *But in truth, nothing physical or spiritual stands as a barrier before Him.* Nothing physical or spiritual stands between you and Him; only acts against His will cut the rope, and a rope can be retied.
6. **Iggeret HaKodesh 4 (the inner point of the heart)** (line 1052): למען כי ה׳ לבדו הוא כל חייך ממש *For God alone is your whole life, truly.* The heart's inner point, above knowledge, is the spark of God in exile; 'He alone is all your life'. The book's clearest word on the heart's deepest will.
7. **Iggeret HaKodesh 25 (anger, Shimi, and the noise in prayer)** (line 1378): כי אילו נסתלק רוח פיו יתברך רגע אחד מרוחו של שמעי – לא יכול לדבר מאומה *For if the breath of His mouth withdrew for one moment from Shimi's spirit, he could not have said a thing.* The one who harms you lives this moment only by God's breath. The contemplation for anger, and for the noise that disturbs prayer.
8. **Iggeret HaTeshuvah, ch. 8 (complete return)** (line 977): ולדבקה בו יתברך ביחוד נפלא, כמו שהיתה מיוחדת בו יתברך בתכלית היחוד, בטרם שנפחה ברוח פיו יתברך *To cleave to Him in a wondrous union, as it was united with Him completely before He breathed it out.* Complete return: the soul goes home as it was before it was breathed out, like breath still inside the mouth.
9. **Iggeret HaKodesh 6 (equally below; the truth of God)** (line 1090): שכמו שהוא יתברך מצוי בעליונים – כך הוא ממש בשוה בתחתונים *As He is found in the higher worlds, so He is truly, equally, in the lower.* He is found below exactly as above, and has not changed since before creation. So the low, dark hour is not farther from Him.
10. **Iggeret HaKodesh 23 (ten who learn)** (line 1356): ואינו יכול להתלבש בנפש גבולית, כי אם מקיף עליה מלמעלה – מראשה ועד רגלה *It cannot clothe itself in a bounded soul, only surround it from above, from its head to its feet.* When ten learn, a light too great to be felt surrounds them from head to foot. The greatest presence may be the one you do not feel.

## 4. Where this book's way of contemplating differs

Compared with the Chabad method in `Chabad Library/hisbonenus/00_HISBONENUS.md` and with the other books. Kept, not smoothed.

- **Faith is set above understanding at the key points.** The Chabad method in `00_HISBONENUS.md` runs on binah: take a point, unfold it in length and breadth, hold it with da'at until the heart moves. This half of the Tanya teaches that method (IT 9, IH 15, KA 8) but, at the three places where the unity is deepest, it stops the mind on purpose. Shaar HaYichud 7 says the oneness of His knowing and His Essence is held 'only by faith, which is above the mind'. Shaar HaYichud 9 says 'we have no business with hidden things'. Holy Letter 25 calls God filling the world one of 'the revealed things', simple faith, and says investigating it began only with 'newcomers'. Yet Kuntres Acharon 8 makes contemplation before the Shema 'a real Torah duty' for those who understand, and KA 4 calls knowing the order of the worlds a mitzvah 'above them all'. Both sides are in the text; this reading keeps both.
- **The highest love is a gift, not a product of contemplation.** The hisbonenus ontology's process runs from contemplation to arousal. This book agrees for the love 'made in the heart' (Chinukh Katan, IH 15), but says the highest love (love in delights, IH 18; the 'truth of God', IH 6; the love from the heart's inner point, IH 4) is given from above and cannot be reached by toil. Contemplation prepares for it; it does not produce it.
- **It sides, in one letter, on the felt-arousal question.** The ontology records a live dispute over felt arousal: R. Aharon of Strashelye defends it, with da'at as its guard; the Mitteler Rebbe calls divine arousal the true intent but warns it coarsens without a prior 'decay' of the self; the Rashab describes an arousal so absorbed in its idea that it is not felt as one's own. IH 23 says felt arousal in prayer comes from the bounded light that fits inside the soul, while the Shechinah resting on ten is boundless and 'cannot be grasped' or felt in this world. Feeling is real, but it is not the measure of what is present. That is not quite either side of the dispute, and this reading does not fold it into one.
- **The deed completes the contemplation, and outranks it in one respect.** The ontology's last stage, 'drawing down', is the step it says is most often skipped. Here it is the whole point of Kuntres Acharon: thought alone 'does nothing' below (KA 1); face to face above comes only through deeds (KA 2); contemplation reaches existence, not essence, while the etrog in the hand holds the essence (KA 4). The mind is not dismissed. It is told where it ends.
- **Giving is a contemplative practice.** The ontology lists techniques of the mind: duration, breadth, worked analogy, repetition. These letters add one it does not list: charity, as the way the heart's inner point is freed (IH 4), 'God's truth' is drawn down (IH 6), the seed's self-emptying (IH 8), and the light of the future is given a wick (IH 32, KA 7). Giving against your nature ('service', IH 12) settles the lees; contemplation in prayer only gives a taste that 'can easily stir up again' afterward.
- **Bitterness has a fixed hour, and joy the rest of the day.** The ontology marks the bitter or sad state as needing care: keep it if it sharpens, stop if it flattens. The Letter on Return gives it a time and a place: an hour of reckoning, at Tikkun Chatzot or at least before Shabbat (IT 7, IT 10), with 'my sin is always before me' read as 'at a distance', never gloom (IT 11). Holy Letter 11 goes further and calls moping 'like denial'. The Alter Rebbe keeps bitterness and joy in two separate rooms rather than mixing them.
- **Contemplation here is communal.** The hisbonenus sources mostly describe one person's mind. These letters keep putting the practice in a group: ten who learn (IH 23), one body whose blood must circulate (IH 22, IH 31), a congregation praying word by word together (KA 9), a teacher whose spirit spreads to students bound to him in love (IH 27).
- **Against the other books.** The Mitteler Rebbe's Gate of Unity (SPINE, 7:10-7:46) trains a person to join every detail of the worlds to the simple unity 'in one moment', contemplating the lowest physical thing. Shaar HaYichud is the seed of that, but short, and it ends with faith rather than a trained moment of sight. The Rashab's Kuntres HaTefillah asks for long contemplation in prayer; Kuntres Acharon 8 agrees that our generation needs longer than the early ones, where 'a moment' was enough. Part I of the Tanya (chapters 41-50) builds love and awe from contemplation before every act; this half keeps that and adds the claim that the act itself reaches further than the contemplation behind it. The Tanya's own tensions (His Essence fills the world, IH 7:1101, yet creatures live from 'a radiance of His Name', IH 7:1102; faith against knowing) are left standing as the text leaves them.
