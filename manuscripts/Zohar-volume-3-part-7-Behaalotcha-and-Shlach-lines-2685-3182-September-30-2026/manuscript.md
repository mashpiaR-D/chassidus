## The shape of the range

Beha'alotcha opens with the lamps and incense of the priest, and holds the Zohar's most famous teaching on how to read the Torah: the story is a garment, the commandments are the body, and the soul of the Torah lies under both (2742-2748). It goes on to the ark that "turns its face" to Israel (2812-2816), and to Moshe's cry "kill me" read as a lesson about anger (2820-2833).

Sh'lach has three parts: - **The spies as seekers.** Rabbi Shimon reads the scouting of the land as a picture of anyone who sets out to study Torah and gets discouraged (2889-2902). - **A long journey tale.** Two companions enter a cave in the desert and are led through the heavenly academies of the desert generation (2927-2965). Then a messenger from the "head of the academy" visits Rabbi Shimon over several nights. The messenger describes the courts of the future Temple, the palaces of righteous women, the new garments of the soul, and the way suffering lights a body (3016-3140). - **The commandment of tzitzit** (3143-3182).

## The unity of G-d

**1. Three worlds, and "found and not found."** Rabbi Abba answers a hard question (why create a person who will die?) by saying that the hidden ways are His. Then he gives a map. G-d has three worlds and "He is hidden within them." The highest is known only to Him. The middle is the gate. The lowest is "the world of separation," where He is present and not present:

> וקודשא בריך הוא אשתכח ביה, ולא אשתכח. אשתכח ביה השתא, כד בעאן לאסתכלא ולמנדע ליה, אסתלק מנייהו, ולא אתחזי (Zohar, Sh'lach 8:4; line 2882)
> The Holy One is found in it and not found. He is found in it now; when they want to look and know Him, He withdraws from them and is not seen.

A human being, made "in the image," has the same three worlds. In the body-world a person too is "found and not found" (line 2883). *Ours:* this is a precise description of how G-d is felt in ordinary life. He is truly here, and the moment a person grabs at Him as an object, He is gone. It fits the Chabad teaching that the light is hidden in the lowest world so that it can be found there only through service, not through looking.

**2. Garment, body, soul, and soul of the soul.** The Torah has four layers, and so does reality:

> שמיא וחיליהון. אלין אינון לבושא. וכנסת ישראל, דא גופא... ונשמתא לנשמתא, דא איהו עתיקא קדישא. וכלא אחיד דא בדא (Zohar, Beha'alotcha 12:8; line 2747)
> The heavens and their hosts are the garment. The Assembly of Israel is the body... and the soul of the soul is the Holy Ancient One. And all is held one in the other.

Whoever thinks the story is the Torah itself "may his spirit expire" (line 2744). "Wine sits only in a jug" (line 2748), so the garment is needed, but one looks at what is under it. *Ours:* the same four layers apply to the world. The visible world is the garment of G-d's presence, and the one who stops at the garment has not seen the thing.

**3. One root, no separation.** In the reading of the spies, those who have faith, when they study, "see one root and one principle, and no separation is found in them" (ומסתכלן שרשא חד ועקרא חד, ולא אשתכח בהו פרודא, line 2896). Those who study not for its own sake carry the cluster "on a pole between two" (line 2896), that is, split in two.

**4. The heart of the world fed from the hidden brain.** The body has a heart in its middle, fed from the brain. The world has Jerusalem, and inside it the Holy of Holies, "the heart of the whole land and world," fed from the brain above (2918-2921). The chain goes all the way up, "until all is fed from the upper brain, hidden of all; and when things are looked at, all is tied one to the other" (וכד יסתכלון מלי, כלא אתקשר דא בדא ודא בדא, line 2924).

**5. The end.** The range closes with the verse "on that day Hashem will be One and His name One" (ביום ההוא יהיה יי אחד ושמו אחד, line 3182).

## States of consciousness

- **The dry start of study.** The spies "went up in the Negev" means that people come to Torah "with a lazy heart, like one who toils for nothing, in dryness, thinking there is no reward in it" (בלבא עצלא, כמאן דאשתדל במגנא, line 2893). Then they come to "Hebron": they join it, read it and repeat it (line 2894). Then comes the voice of despair: "What do we care? Till today we have seen no good. I toiled in it and the house is empty. I sit in the scorn of the people" (line 2897). This is an accurate map of the dry middle of any inner work. Near S12 (the flat world) and S05. **Proposed state: the dry middle.**
- **The answer: only the heart.** The faithful say, "if Hashem desires us." Whoever toils with the will of the heart toward the Holy One, "He asks nothing of us but the heart":

> כיון דישתדל בר נש ברעותא דלבא לגבי קודשא בריך הוא, לא בעי מנן אלא לבא (Zohar, Sh'lach 10:7; line 2900)
> When a person toils with the will of the heart toward the Holy One, He asks nothing of us but the heart.

Torah "does not need wealth" and "a broken body, if it toils in Torah, finds healing in everything" (line 2901). Maps to S84 (trust). - **Being struck until the light rises.** Why does a righteous person suffer? The answer comes from the head of the academy:

> גופא דלא סלקא ביה נהורא דנשמתא, יבטשון ביה, ויסלק נהירו דנשמתא, ויתאחדון דא בדא לאנהרא (Zohar, Sh'lach 25:12; line 3040)
> A body in which the light of the soul does not rise: they strike it, and the light of the soul rises, and they join one to the other to shine.

Then the body "praises, prays, blesses its Master, and then all shines" (line 3041). Rabbi Shimon bows and kisses the dust: "I have chased this word all my life, and now I know it from its root" (line 3042). Maps to S05 (bitter with a purpose). The picture is of a smoldering log that is shaken so it can catch. - **Two sides of one inner room: joy like no joy, fear like no fear.** Inside the turning firmament there are two sides: "one in joy, there is no joy like that joy in the world... and one in fear, there is no fear like that fear" (line 2989). Joy and awe are both at full strength, and neither cancels the other. Maps to S41-S46 and S53. - **Only the thought of joy.** In the Temple to come "all thoughts will be forgotten except the thought of the joy of the Holy King" (גו מקדשא דא כל הרהורין ישתכחון, בר הרהורא דחדוה דמלכא קדישא, line 2998). Maps to S53 (glad) and S32. - **The palace that grows as you look.** At first look the palace seems small. Look more, it is bigger. "However much he looks, so it grows... until it seems like a hair's breadth that has no measure" (line 3112). A state of widening attention where the object keeps opening. Near S32. It is the Zohar's picture of the infinite met through looking. - **Standing as silence.** When the holy beings have no answer, they "stand." In speech seven parts move (heart, lungs, windpipe, tongue, teeth, lips, flesh); "in silence they stand in their place, without moving. So silence is called standing" (line 3130). Maps to S32 (quiet). *Ours:* a plain bodily description of inner silence. - **Small here, great there.** "Happy is he who makes himself small in this world; how great and high he is in that world" (line 3044). Maps to S57. - **Love seen at the moment of death.** Joseph's hand on Jacob's eyes means: "your vision of this world is lost; here, I am your vision in its place. From now on another vision will be set for you, of that world" (line 3059). A gentle picture of dying held by love.

## Meditation and practice

- **Looking under the garment.** In every reading, ask what is under the story. David's prayer "open my eyes that I may see wonders from Your Torah" means "what is under the garment of the Torah" (line 2744). Ties to `garment_look`.
- **Only the heart; start dry and keep going.** When study or prayer is dry, name the stage (the Negev) and go on to "Hebron": read it and repeat it (line 2894). Ties to `chazarah` and `hold`.
- **Pray without "examining."** "Whoever enters two doorways into the synagogue cleaves to his Master, and his will is done. Whoever examines his prayer and looks into it is far from his will" (line 2965). The Talmud's "iyun tefillah" (expecting an answer) is read as watching yourself pray, which blocks it. Ties to `before_whom`. **Proposed technique: praying without watching the result.**
- **Bowing so as to stand.** "The tree of eighteen, a person's spine: when it bends before its Master, it will stand up at the resurrection. If it does not bend at Modim, it becomes a snake" (line 2964). A practice of fully bowing at Modim. Ties to `act`.
- **Shake the smoldering log.** "A burning log whose flame does not rise: shake it and the light rises" (line 3021). Whoever does not merit in one place should move to another. Ties to `edge`.
- **Tam and yam every day.** "A person must be every day tam yam" (בר נש אצטריך למהוי בכל יומא, ת"ם י"ם, line 2957). *Tam* means whole and clean of what is foreign; *yam* ("sea") means holding all the holy levels at once. Tzitzit and then tefillin do this each morning, "in one moment she is set above and the person is set below" (line 2960). Ties to `binding` and `act`.
- **The morning witnesses.** When a person stretches his feet to walk, witnesses announce "guard your foot"; when he opens his eyes, "let your eyes look straight"; when he speaks, "guard your tongue" (line 3170). Putting on tefillin, he lifts his head and "sees the high holy Name," and the straps hang over his heart: "he has looked at the glory of his Master" (line 3172). Ties to `details` and `carry`. **Proposed technique: the morning walk-through with the witnesses.**
- **Evening prayer when the sun weakens.** "When the sun leans to set... judgment is in the world; then a person must pray and direct his will before his Master" (line 2835). Ties to `before_whom`.
- **Midnight partnership.** When the north wind wakes at midnight, "whoever wakes to toil in Torah at that hour is a partner with her" and his name is written among "the children of the palace" (line 2838). Continues the midnight rising from part 1.
- **A chiddush on Shabbos.** After Shabbos the extra souls stand before the King, who asks "what new thing in Torah did you have?" and gathers His court: "hear the new teaching that the soul of so-and-so said" (line 3128). A practice of saying one fresh insight each Shabbos. Ties to `own_words`.
- **Guarding the speech of anger.** Moshe said "kill me" out of anger, and it was received. "A person should not, when anger rests on him, curse himself, for many stand ready to receive that word" (line 2822). Ties to `lips`.
- **Giving to the poor wakes the tree of life.** "Whoever gives to the poor wakes the tree of life to add to the tree of death... and when he needs it, that tree of life stands over him" (line 2780). Ties to `act`.

## The big human questions

- **Why was a person made at all, if he will die?** Rabbi Abba does not explain it away. "The hidden things are for Hashem," and who can join his mind to His hidden mind, let alone ask (line 2878). The revealed is ours to study. Then he gives the map of three worlds, and death is a passage between them. The righteous "are not lost," so do not gash yourselves (line 2886).
- **Why do the righteous suffer?** The body must be struck so the soul's light can rise in it (3040-3041). The other side strikes the wicked too, "and the lamp of the wicked goes out" (line 3042). The same blow lights one and puts out the other.
- **What does He want from me?** "Only the heart" (line 2900).
- **Where does G-d hurt?** On the ninth of Av He remembers His children and drops two tears into the great sea (line 3107). The fathers in the cave of Machpelah wake in fear and are told, "Do not fear, holy beloved ones; for your sake He remembers your children" (line 3119).
- **What of shame?** A man is kept outside the gate of Eden because he once told the companions to be silent so that another would stumble in Torah and be shamed (line 3028). "The Holy One does not let go of a wrong in Torah even by a hair."

## For the guide (ours)

1. The spies passage (2889-2902) is the best ancient map in this volume of the dry middle of study and practice. It names the excuses (no reward, empty house, scorn, "you need wealth, you need a strong body") and gives one answer: only the heart. The guide should use it for people who feel their practice has gone dead.
2. "Found and not found; when they look, He withdraws" (2882) should enter the guide as the teaching behind why grasping fails. It pairs with the new technique of praying without watching the result (2965).
3. The log that is shaken until it catches (3021, 3040-3041) gives the guide a non-punishing picture of suffering. It should be offered carefully and never as an explanation of another person's pain.
4. The layers of garment, body, soul and soul of the soul (2747) are a clear four-step ladder for `garment_look`.
5. Silence as "standing" (3130) and the palace that grows as one looks (3112) are two simple bodily and visual descriptions of quiet attention the guide can use.
