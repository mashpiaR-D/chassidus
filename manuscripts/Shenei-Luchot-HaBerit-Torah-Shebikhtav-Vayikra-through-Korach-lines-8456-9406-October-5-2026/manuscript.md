The Shelah reads all of Leviticus as one work: *Torat Kohanim* is "the repair of man". Before the first sin "all times were equal in holiness", and man "himself was an offering to G-d" (line 8457). Everything after, the offerings, the priests, the laws of purity, is a way back. The unity lines of this range come mostly out of that theme. "There is no existence at all that is apart from Him" (line 8709). He "longed to have a dwelling in the lower worlds" (line 8779). The Cause wants to give more than the caused wants to receive (line 8782). And in Korach: "the Cause of all causes is the true general that includes all" (line 9340). The practical side is unusually rich. It includes the holiness of the heart, "mercy, grace and removing anger" (line 8843); each person's own measure (line 8854); a way to pray (line 9129); the gates of return "always open" (line 9230); saying "with G-d's help" over every act (line 9259); and a clear warning about going past one's level (lines 8731, 9338, 9401).

## 1.1 Nothing apart from Him

On the four elements, the four angels, the four living creatures and the four letters of the Name, each rooted in the one above it (Vayikra, Torah Ohr 48, line 8709):

> ויש בכל מדריגה אלפים ורבבות מדריגות בלי ערך עד שישוב הכל אל שרשו הנעלם

> כלומר אין בנמצא שום מציאות שיהיה מזולתו כי הכל מאתו יתברך

"In every level there are thousands and myriads of levels beyond compare, until all returns to its hidden root. That is, there is no existence at all that is apart from Him, for all is from Him." He is reading "the Lord is G-d in the heavens above and on the earth below; there is no other" (Deut. 4:39). The same verse is the root of *Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah*.

The spiritual root is in everything: quoting the Ari, "there is nothing at all that has no holiness in it" (line 8707: כי אין לך שום דבר שאין בו קדושה); food keeps the soul in the body because "the word of G-d is in it, for He spoke and it was" (line 8707: כי יש בו פי ה', כי הוא אמר ויהי).

## 1.2 The Cause longs for the caused

In Shmini (Torah Ohr 6 and 9, lines 8779, 8782):

> בשעה שברא הקב"ה את העולם נתאוה שיהיה לו דירה בתחתונים כמו בעליונים

> ויותר ממה שהעגל רוצה לינק פרה רוצה להניק

> כי דביקות עילה בעלול מורה אקישור ואחדות וכי אין עוד מלבדו ואין שום דבר זולתו יתברך

"When G-d created the world He longed to have a dwelling in the lower worlds as in the upper." "More than the calf wants to suck, the cow wants to nurse." "The clinging of Cause and caused shows bond and oneness, and that there is none besides Him and nothing apart from Him." And "everything caused wants to cling to its Cause" (line 9104: וכל עלול רוצה לידבק בעילתו). The Tanchuma's "dwelling in the lower worlds" is the line on which Tanya ch. 36 is built.

## 1.3 The Cause of all causes is the true general

In Korach, on the first rule of Torah reading, general and particular (Torah Ohr 3, line 9340):

> עד שתאמר סיבת כל הסיבות עילת כל העילות הוא כלל האמיתי הכולל הכל

"Until you say: the Cause of all causes is the true general that includes all", and all things "are the particulars". In the same line: "there is nothing in the general except what is in the particular; only in the particular it is revealed, and in the general it is hidden". *Ours:* this is a good plain-language form of "He is all that exists": the One is not one more thing beside the many; He is what holds them all.

## 1.4 Set apart, and the earth full of His glory

On "holy, holy, holy" (Korach, Torah Ohr 37, line 9374):

> שהוא נבדל מכל שלשה העולמות. מכל מקום מלא כל הארץ כבודו

"He is set apart from all three worlds; and still the whole earth is full of His glory." This is the Ramak's balance in one line.

## 1.5 One day, not two powers

The twelve orders of the Name are six of judgment and six of mercy, "and all goes to one place" (line 9306: והכל הולך אל מקום אחד). Splitting them is dualism (Sh'lach, Torah Ohr 35, line 9307):

> כמו שאמרו המינים מי שברא אור לא ברא חושך, היפך מן ויהי ערב ויהי בקר יום אחד

"As the heretics said, the one who made light did not make darkness; the opposite of 'and it was evening and it was morning, one day'."

## 1.6 Endless ascent

"The grasp of Him and the clinging to Him go always from height to height without pause", "and there is no end to grasping Him" (line 9076). In the sefirot, "from ten to ten without end or limit, and all is one oneness" (line 9079: מעשר לעשר עד אין סוף ותכלית והכל אחדות אחד).

## 1.7 Greatness and humility together

"Wherever you find G-d's greatness, there you find His humility" (line 9367); He "dwells with the crushed" and is "father of orphans" (lines 9369-9371).

## 3.1 Offer yourself; a broken heart now (proposed: `self_offering`)

"One who offers himself willingly, this is an offering to G-d" (line 8711). Now, with no Temple, "we can make a likeness of it, that is, to have a broken and humbled heart" (line 8712). Saying the order of offerings "with pain in the heart that he cannot do the actual offerings", "G-d joins this to the deed" (line 8716); it was given "so they would not despair of clinging" (line 8721). "Atonement depends on submission" (line 8936). *Tie:* `before_whom`, `lips`. *Ours:* a short daily reading that ends in handing oneself over, without the book's self-affliction (see Not carried).

## 3.2 The holiness of the heart and each person's measure (proposed: `own_measure`)

Holiness has three parts: body (deeds), heart, soul (line 8842). "The holiness of the heart is the holiness of thought, the duties of the heart", "thousands of myriads of traits, such as mercy, grace and removing anger" (line 8843). "The holiness of the soul, which is a part of G-d above, is to join the part to the All" (line 8844). And the measure is personal: "each person has a Torah of his own", and "what you feel is excess for the matter and for your nature is then forbidden to you by the Torah" (line 8854). *Tie:* `discern`, `cheshbon`. *Ours:* no one else's rule of how much; one's own honest sense of "too much" is the rule.

## 3.3 A small Shabbat every day (proposed: `daily_shabbat`, from R13)

"Every day a taste of Shabbat, to be made holy from work and cling to the world that is all Shabbat" (line 8846). Festivals forbid work "so that he not be busy with his work and forget his clinging to G-d" (line 9016). "Every day is a Day of Atonement" (line 8896).

## 3.4 How to pray: eyes below, heart above (proposed: `eyes_below_heart_above`)

"When a person comes near to pray let him think, what am I, a sinner, and be afraid and ashamed; this is 'his eyes below'. Then he thinks of G-d's greatness, high and lifted up and able to do all, and is glad to speak with Him; this is 'his heart above'" (line 9129). "G-d, the Cause, watches below, and we in our hoping pour our heart upward" (line 8800). Before it, "purify his heart" (line 9131); in it, "see himself as a priest" (line 9132). *Tie:* `before_whom`, `breadth`, S53. *Ours:* the Shelah's order is smallness first, then joy; the guide should keep the joy as the end point.

## 3.5 Anger, peace, rebuke, love

- **Anger.** Moses erred in anger; "see how far the power of anger reaches; so let a person always be in good will" (line 8952). *Tie:* S15.
- **Peace.** "The sum turns on the center of oneness... that the body return to the soul, and then there is peace in all" (line 8915). "Whatever a person can do to find ways to make peace and put out the fire of quarrel, let him do" (line 9403); Moses went himself to those who rebelled against him.
- **Rebuke.** "Tell him: you are a good man, why do you do this"; "rebuke him as a wise man, and then he will love you" (line 8874). "Gently, so that he will accept it" (line 8998); "one who rebukes his fellow rebukes himself too" (line 8971).
- **Love.** "Since they are one body and one soul"; would you take revenge on your own hand for hurting you? (line 8972). "'To your fellow' is the leg on which the whole Torah stands" (line 8876).
- **Let it pass.** "A trait of piety: let things pass most of the time, so that all his own wrongs will be passed over" (line 9000).
- **Compassion.** "Israel are compassionate"; a person who turns cruel "wakes a cruel judgment on himself" (line 9014).
- **Truth.** "One who says the truth, even if he loses the case, always gains... the world stands on truth" (line 8984).

## 3.6 Return: three parts, the gate always open (proposed: `open_gate`)

"Return tears up every bill of debt in the world"; "the gates of return are always open", like the sea (line 9230). The word means rest: "in returning and rest you will be saved" (line 9231). Its parts: return in the good days, not only in trouble (lines 9124-9125); shame, for "whoever does a thing and is ashamed of it is forgiven all his sins" (line 9125); and strength, "while he is still a man in his strength" (line 9126). Say it aloud: confessing "shows he truly believes all is open before G-d" (line 9221). Speak to your heart: "why do you go after emptiness" (line 9223). And afterward, honor: "do not say to him, remember your earlier deeds... the one who returns deserves honor" (line 8942). *Tie:* S77/S78, `own_words`. *Ours:* the guide keeps the open gate and the honor, and leaves the fasting (see Not carried).

## 3.7 Stray thoughts: cut short and switch (Tie: `quieting`)

"If a thought of those bad views rises in his heart, let him cut short his thought on them and change to thinking of the true and good ways of the Torah" (line 9272). *Ours:* this is the Tanya ch. 12 method of *hesech hada'at*, not fighting the thought but turning away.

## 3.8 "With G-d's help" over every act (proposed: `with_his_help`)

"For every act or movement a person makes, let him say 'if G-d wills' or 'with G-d's help'... so the Name of heaven is always on his lips when it rises in his thought and when he acts" (line 9259). The aim: "clinging to Him in going and returning" (line 9192). *Tie:* `lips`, `hold`.

## 3.9 Think that everything depends on you (Tie: `act`)

"Let every person think of himself as one who counts, as if all depends on him" (line 9209). "Each one includes the whole world" (line 9109). "Let no good you do for His sake seem small, even a word or a look, for the little from you is much to Him" (line 9099).

## 3.10 Guard the edge (proposed: `keep_your_place`)

Nadav and Avihu "drew so near to the holy in the depth of their grasp that they stayed clinging above" (line 8731); Abraham and R. Akiva "entered in peace and came out in peace" (line 8803). The Torah is fire: it "warms those who keep a little distance, and burns one who comes too close" (line 9338). The 250 men sought closeness for heaven's sake, "but they erred and went past 'do not seek what is too wondrous for you'" (line 9401). *Tie:* `edge`. *Ours:* this is the *ratzo* that must end in *shov*; the guide should teach the return to the body and the day after every height.

## 3.11 Other practices

- **Every person a high priest; seclusion.** "Let him be alone in a place of being alone, with no one else in this tent, and cling to G-d and not leave the sanctuary" (line 9013). *Tie:* `arrive`.
- **Consistency.** "Sometimes he does and sometimes he does not" is walking "by chance" (line 9020).
- **Eating.** Ordinary food "eaten in the purity of holiness" (line 8786); eat "to keep the body healthy to serve" (line 8705); the festival meal "to wake the spirit to clinging" (line 9015).
- **Do not curse yourself** (line 9261).
- **Charity.** The corner of the field "is the poor man's from the start" (line 8863); it "makes peace between the poor man and his Creator" (line 8967); give "in secret" (line 9133).

## 4. The big human questions

- **Is anything outside G-d?** "There is no existence at all that is apart from Him" (line 8709); He is "the true general that includes all" (line 9340).
- **Does He want me?** More than I want Him: "the cow wants to nurse" more than the calf wants to suck (line 8782).
- **Am I important?** "Each one counts... a small world"; "I am the Lord your G-d" was said to each one (line 9109). Read as the Shelah reads Hillel: "how very important I am" (line 8883).
- **Is it too late?** The gate is always open (line 9230). But do not wait for old age (lines 8992, 9124).
- **How much is too much?** Each person has "a Torah of his own" (line 8854). The body is a tool; one who has not sinned should not weaken it (line 9251).
- **Is it chance?** Omens say "all is chance and not providence" (line 9003); "it is by the will of the Maker, not by the force of the hours" (line 9004).
- **Why can't I win by myself?** "When you say 'my strength and the might of my hand', then you will fear them" (line 9290). "Envy, lust and honor drive a person out of the world" (line 9317).
- **Can one go too far toward G-d?** Yes (lines 8731, 9338, 9401).

## 5. With Chabad (kept)

- **"He is all that exists."** Lines 8709, 8782 and 9340 are as strong as anything in the book. Line 9340 (the Cause of all causes as "the true general that includes all") is close to *Shaar HaYichud* ch. 7 and to the Ramak's first half.
- **"Not all that exists is He."** Line 9374 ("set apart from all three worlds; still the whole earth is full of His glory") and line 9340's hidden general keep the second half.
- **Dwelling below.** Line 8779 quotes the Tanchuma source of Tanya ch. 36 word for word.
- **Tanya ch. 2.** Line 8844 ("the soul, which is a part of G-d above") repeats R13's line 8138.
- **Tzimtzum.** Line 9056: the tefillin of skin are "a contraction of the upper lights" after the sin. Line 9162, quoting the *Pardes*, says the "entry" of the husks into holiness is "not literal". Both fit the non-literal reading.
- **Stray thoughts.** Line 9272 is Tanya ch. 12 and 28 in practice.
- **Return.** Line 9231 (*teshuvah* as rest), 9230 (gates always open) and 8942 (honor the penitent) fit *Iggeret HaTeshuvah*. The Shelah's insistence on fasting (9222, 9228-9251) is where Chabad differs: *Iggeret HaTeshuvah* ch. 3 says that in our generation one should not fast much, and should give charity instead. The guide follows Chabad here.
- **The Rambam.** Line 8703-8706 (the *Akeidah* on food and the soul) and line 9366 (duty to people) are close to the Rambam's *Shemonah Perakim*. Line 9003-9004 against omens fits *Hilchot Avodah Zarah* 11.
- **Not carried.** The legal count (8460-8661, 9026-9048, 9270-9272 in part, 9329-9337); the Kabbalah of forbidden relations (8746-8754); rankings of the nations and their food (8758, 8786 in part, 8954, 9200-9202, 8412); Esau and Edom (8790-8791); Azazel (8745); death called holiness or "good" (8822, 8828, 9157); martyrdom and suffering as refinement (8713, 9110, 9142, 9194, 9228); self-affliction and fasting as teshuvah (8932, 9222, 9235-9251); mourning practices; divine names used as protection (9254); reincarnation lore (9168, 9378). All wine passages (8734, 9170) are read only as support for not drinking.

## 6. What the guide should take (ours)

1. **There is no existence apart from Him; He is the general that holds all** (lines 8709, 9340).
2. **He wants to give more than you want to receive; He longed for a dwelling below** (lines 8779, 8782).
3. **Set apart from all worlds, and the earth full of His glory** (line 9374).
4. **Offer yourself; now a broken heart is the offering** (lines 8711-8712).
5. **Your own measure: what feels like excess for you is forbidden for you** (line 8854).
6. **Pray with eyes below and heart above** (line 9129).
7. **The gate of return is always open; honor the one who returns** (lines 9230, 8942).
8. **Say "with G-d's help" over every act** (line 9259).
9. **Anger makes even Moses err; always be in good will. Make peace at any cost** (lines 8952, 9403).
10. **Keep your place near the fire** (lines 8731, 9338, 9401).

The guide should cite it as a reading, not as the plain sense of the Mishnah.
