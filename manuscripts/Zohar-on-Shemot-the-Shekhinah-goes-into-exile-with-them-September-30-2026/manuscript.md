## 1. What the range is

Parashat Shemot in the Zohar is a book of exile. It opens with the tree of life whose colors "settle and do not settle" when they go down into a light that does not shine (lines 1-5), and asks one question over and over: when Israel goes down, who goes down with them? The answer, given a dozen ways, is that the Shekhinah (the indwelling Presence) goes down, with all the hosts of heaven, and that God Himself weeps.

* **Exile and the Presence (lines 6-66, 267-280).** Ezekiel's visions in Babylon, Jacob's descent with 600,000 angels, the tribes' dead souls going down with their living children. * **Tosefta and the chariot song (lines 40-47, 211-213).** Short "mishnah" pieces in high style: the wheels of the chariot singing, the holy well that fills from three. * **The Messiah and the bird's nest (lines 96-156).** R. Shimon's long vision of the end: the pillar of fire, the Messiah hidden in the "bird's nest" in Eden, weeping at the image of Rachel; then the "secret of the letters" of the Name, broken in exile and whole at the end. * **The birth of Moses (lines 176-206, 324-339).** Amram and Yocheved; the soul sent down by Gabriel; the daughter of Pharaoh as the attribute of judgment; the crying child. * **Travelers' tales (lines 216-231, 276-279, 302-313).** The Jew who went down into a fragrant cave and saw the souls of the righteous; the father who cannot rest in his grave because of his captive son; R. Yosi chased by a snake for one hour of idle thought. * **R. Shimon's house (lines 234-252).** R. Chiya outside the curtain, weeping; the fire inside; two days without food. * **Prayer (lines 253-257, 340-357, 364-369).** R. Akiva's parable of the beloved and the servant; the sigh, the cry and the scream; prayer in the place of sacrifice. * **The shepherd and the bush (lines 358-396).** The rose among thorns; Moses the shepherd; the bush that burns and is not eaten.

About a third of the range (the Midrash HaNe'elam and the passages from line 314 on) is in Hebrew, not Aramaic.

## 2. The unity of G-d

**One thought, one pattern, all one.** The clearest statement of the unity in the range is a Midrash HaNe'elam passage. The worlds were not made one by one:

> כל העולמות עלו במחשבה אחת, ובמחשבה זו נבראו כולם (line 358)

"All the worlds rose in one thought, and in this thought they were all created." Then he gives the pattern:

> וכל מה שיש למעלה, כדוגמתו למטה. וכל מה שיש למטה, כדוגמתו בים. והכל אחד (line 359)

"Everything above has its likeness below, and everything below has its likeness in the sea. And all is one." This is the Zohar's own way of saying the unity: not "nothing exists but Him" (the Tanya's way), but **one pattern repeated at every level, so that each thing is the same thing in another place**. R. Abba weeps when he sees a tree whose fruit turns into a bird: "if people knew what these hint at, they would tear their clothes to the navel" (line 263). Nothing on earth is only itself; it points up.

**The unity is a marriage, and exile is a split in God's Name.** The Zohar's unity is not a fact that stands still. It is a union that can be whole or broken. When all was whole, the Holy One and the Shekhinah were never apart, and one was forbidden to separate them even in thought:

> ואסיר לאפרשא דא מן דא, אתתא מבעלה, לאו ברעיוני, ולאו בדכירו, בגין דלא לאחזאה פרודא (line 139)

"It is forbidden to separate one from the other, a wife from her husband, not in thought and not in memory, so as not to show separation." But now, in exile, "we make a separation", because she lies in the dust (lines 139-140). The long passage that follows (lines 141-156) reads Jewish history as the letters of the Name coming apart: the upper spring (the yud) withdraws "up and up to Ein Sof" when the Temple falls, the lower heh is darkened, and at the end the letters are "engraved in wholeness, above and below" (line 151), "the letters of the Holy Name all standing whole" (line 153). This is F02 (the higher and lower unity) as drama: the lower unity is the one that breaks, and our deeds break it or mend it.

**Voice and speech.** R. Shimon reads "Come with me from Lebanon, bride" as "the secret of the unity of faith": voice says to speech "come with me", למהוי כחדא בלא פרודא כלל, "to be as one with no separation at all" (line 19). Voice is the general, speech the detail; דהא לית קול בלא דבור, ולית דבור בלא קול, "there is no voice without speech and no speech without voice" (line 19). The pair (Tiferet and Malkhut, the Holy One and the Shekhinah) is one as a voice and its words are one. **(ours)** This is a picture a person can use at once: my own voice and the words it carries cannot be pulled apart.

**Joseph, the place of all union.** Egypt comes "from the place of separation" (ומשם יפרד, Genesis 2:10), and the new king "did not know Joseph", אתר דכל יחודא שריא ביה, דאקרי צדיק, "the place where all union dwells, called Tzaddik" (line 158). Union has a place (Yesod) and forgetting it is what exile is.

**Before Him, nothing.** The Zohar also has the plain biblical form: "all the kings of the world and all the peoples of the world count as nothing before the Holy One" (line 89). And the morning stars praise ביחודא חדא, "in one union" (line 159).

**The Name is one and spreads into seventy.** R. Huna: "as His Name is one and spread into seventy, so Israel is one and spread into seventy" (line 288), tied to "on that day the Lord shall be one and His name one".

**What Chassidus does with this (ours).** The Tanya takes up the Zohar's "higher unity" and "lower unity" (Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah 7) as two ways of seeing. Here the Zohar has not yet made them ways of seeing. They are events: the union above is whole or it is broken by exile and sin, and mended by prayer, tears and redemption. Chassidus inherits the drama and turns it inward.

## 3. States of consciousness

**Held in exile: "He does not leave them" (ties to S84 Trust, S73 Exile inside, S68 The hiding seen as His).** In Babylon the exiles thought "they have no standing forever, for the Holy One has abandoned them" (line 10). God tells His whole court "get up, go down to Babylon, and I with you" (line 11). Ezekiel is allowed to tell everything he saw for one reason: so that they would know. When they heard, לא חיישו על גלותהון כלל, דהא ידעו דקודשא בריך הוא לא שביק לון, "they did not worry about their exile at all, for they knew the Holy One does not leave them" (line 12). The same line is taught as a rule: בכל אתר דישראל גלו, תמן שכינתא גלתה עמהון, "wherever Israel went into exile, the Shekhinah went into exile with them" (line 13). From inside: the pain is still here, the place is still foreign, but I am not alone in it. The fear that goes is the fear of being left. How one enters: by hearing, from someone who saw, that He came down too. **(ours)** This is the shape of the guide's "being held" in its oldest form: not that the trouble is taken away but that He is in it.

**He goes up and down with you (proposed new state ZS2-01).** God's promise to Jacob, "I will go down with you", is read as a rule of companionship:

> כל זמנא דיהוי לך עלייה, כביכול עלייה אית לי, ובשעתא דיהוי לך ירידה, כביכול אנכי ארד עמך (line 268)

"Whenever you have a rising, as it were, I have a rising; and when you have a going down, as it were, I go down with you." Inside: my low time is not a place He has left; my fall is His fall too. It differs from S84 Trust (which expects help) and S63 The world held (which sees Him holding all). It is the feeling of a companion who shares the descent. Entry: read the verse in the first person on a bad day.

**The gazelle that looks back (ties to S68).** Israel's wish when they cause Him to leave: "flee like a gazelle", because a gazelle runs a little and always turns its head back to the place it left (line 236); and it sleeps with one eye open (line 236). This is the hiding felt from inside: He is going, and still looking at me. **(ours)** A gentle picture for someone who feels God far: far is not gone; the face is turned back.

**The child who cries (ties to S51 The sudden cry, S49 Weeping and joy in one heart).** "Everything in the world hangs on teshuvah and on prayer, and all the more on one who sheds tears in his prayer, for there is no gate those tears do not enter" (line 203). The Shekhinah stands over Israel כאימא על בנין, "like a mother over her children" (line 203), and when they weep before Him כברא דבכי קמי אבוי, "like a son crying before his father", every harsh decree is broken and He is moved to mercy (line 204). The Zohar sees Israel as "soft of heart" (line 205) and holds that the redemption "hangs only on weeping" (line 206).

**The wordless cry (proposed new state ZS2-02).** The range sets out three kinds of calling: the sigh, the shout (with words), and the cry (without words). The cry is the greatest, צעקה שצועק ואינו אומר כלום... שצעקה היא בלב, "a cry in which one says nothing... for the cry is in the heart" (line 354). Then:

> האי מאן דצלי ובכי וצעיק, עד לא יכיל למרחש בשפוותיה, האי צלותא שלימתא דהיא בלבא, ולעולם לא הדרא ריקניא (line 356)

"One who prays and weeps and cries out until he can no longer move his lips: this is the complete prayer, which is in the heart, and it never comes back empty." Inside: words have run out and the heart is still speaking. It "tears up a person's decree for all his days" (line 356) and "rules over the attribute of judgment above" (line 357). It differs from S55 Own words to Him, which is speech; this is what comes after speech. **(ours)** For the guide this answers "I can't pray, I have no words": the Zohar says that is the highest prayer.

**Lost in the light (ties to S57 No self to watch, S52 The song that sings itself).** R. Elazar stands and explains secrets of wisdom, his face shining like the sun; they sit two days without eating or drinking, "and did not know if it was day or night" (line 251). R. Chiya, let in behind the curtain, cannot open his mouth; afterwards he says "it is good to die in the fire of good gold" (line 239). These are states of absorption, told as stories, not taught as methods.

**Cleaving that is returned (ties to S60 The essential bond).** "As they cleave to Him always, so He cleaves to them and never leaves them" (line 185). And "a person who sanctifies himself below, the Holy One sanctifies above" (line 184). The bond runs both ways.

**Hearing song, the soul joins (proposed new state ZS2-03).** The Levites are named from "joining": they join above "as one", and והשומע, נלוה ונדבק נפשו למעלה, "and the one who hears, his soul is joined and cleaves above" (line 333). When the singers above and below stood in their places, "companions as one, and the worlds one, and one King dwelling over them" (line 332). Inside: listening, not singing, and being carried up with what is heard. It is near S52 but passive: the song is someone else's and it takes me with it.

## 4. Meditation and practice

**Tears in prayer (ties to technique 20, Your own words to Him; technique 16, Love and awe).** Not a method with steps, but a clear instruction: weeping in prayer opens every gate (line 203); the child cries before the father (line 204). Guard: the Zohar's tears are for exile and for sin, before a Father, never tears worked up for their own sake.

**The cry without words (proposed technique ZT2-01).** How: pray, weep, and when words stop, let the heart keep crying without forcing speech (lines 354-357). What it is for: the heart's prayer that "never comes back empty". Samuel "left everything and took up the cry" all night (line 355). Guard **(ours)**: the guide offers it as permission, not as a goal; no one should try to cry.

**Prayer in the place of sacrifice (lines 364-369).** The rose is red and becomes white only in fire; so judgment turns to mercy. A person fasting "offers his fat and his blood" and warms them in the heat of his weak body; R. Elazar prayed during fasts that the breath rising from his mouth be "like the scent rising from the offering on the altar fire, and accept me" (line 368). Prayer was set in place of the offerings, ובלבד שיתכוין למה דאמרן, "provided he intends what we said" (line 369). Guard **(ours)**: the guide does not teach fasting. What it can keep is the intention: I bring what I am, the red and the white, and ask that it rise.

**Rest in the smell.** "The soul is sustained only by scent", so myrtle at the end of Shabbat (line 363).

**The shepherd's way.** The good leader carries the newborn lambs in his bosom "so they will not tire" and leads with mercy, not cruelty (line 375). Character work for anyone who leads.

**Do not despair of Torah even for an hour.** R. Yosi let his mind wander to worldly things on the road while R. Acha thought Torah; a snake chased him and he fell. "If for one hour it is so, one who gives it up altogether, how much more" (line 302). God does not want the wise "separated from the tree of life even for a moment" (line 304). A practice of the mind kept on Torah while walking.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why suffering, and who bears it.** R. Shimon's first teaching: one used to pain carries his load and does not mind; one raised in comfort, when pain comes, it is "complete pain", and he must weep (line 7). Egypt was borne because Jacob's children were used to hardship; Babylon broke them. The Zohar does not explain the pain away. It says who is with you in it (lines 11-13, 268) and that the Holy One Himself weeps: He "remembers His children and lets two tears fall into the great sea" (line 135), weeps "in secret places" (line 309), and R. Elazar sits "with his hand on his mouth, sad" (line 310) because the King is sad. The two tears are lowered into ים החכמה להמתיקן, והופך מדת הדין למדת רחמים, "the sea of wisdom, to sweeten them, and He turns judgment into mercy" (line 343).

**Why the righteous suffer.** Solomon's "there are righteous who are treated like the wicked" (lines 163-175): sometimes they are taken early for the sins of the generation or before they would fall; and the wicked sometimes prosper because a good son will come from them (line 169). Then a softer reading: "as He is honored by the deeds of the righteous, so He is honored by the wicked when they do a good deed" (line 173). And from the bush: "the mercy of the Place on the wicked": it burns and is not consumed (line 389).

**The dark place turned (F11).** Many wicked men bring forth better sons than the righteous, and the good son from the wicked is better still: נהורא מגו חשוכא. חכמתא מגו טפשותא. ודא איהו מעליא מכלא, "light from within darkness, wisdom from within folly, and this is the best of all" (line 208). Chassidus will build on this line.

**Unanswered prayer.** R. Eliezer fasts forty days and no rain; R. Akiva prays once and it rains. R. Akiva's parable: R. Eliezer is the king's beloved, and the king is in no hurry to grant his wish "so that he will not leave him" (line 255); the servant gets his request at the door. **(ours)** This is one of the kindest answers anywhere to "why doesn't He answer me": perhaps because He wants you to keep talking.

**The soul.** The spirit is engraved inside like a seal, and after death it appears in the lower Eden in the exact shape of the body (lines 177-178); the soul has "a father and a mother, as the body has" (line 200); the souls of the righteous are "known above" before they come down (lines 193, 200). The dead pray for the living (lines 276-279).

**Love.** "If people knew the love with which the Holy One loves Israel, they would roar like young lions to run after Him" (line 61). "He chose me and I chose Him" (line 360). God calls the lower ones, not the angels, "children" (line 360).

**Faith at the end.** Whoever "holds on in faith" through the last troubles "will merit the light of the King's joy" (line 97).

## 6. For the guide (ours)

1. **"I go down with you" (line 268)** is the range's gift. It is the plainest way to say to a person in a low place that the low place is shared.
2. **The wordless cry (line 356)** makes the end of words into the fullest prayer. A person who says "I don't know how to pray" can be told this.
3. **R. Akiva's parable (line 255)** answers the delay of an answer with love, not with judgment.
4. **"All the worlds in one thought... and all is one" (lines 358-359)** is a universal way in to the unity: one pattern at every level.
5. **"Light from within darkness... the best of all" (line 208)** stands behind F11 and the guide's U8.
