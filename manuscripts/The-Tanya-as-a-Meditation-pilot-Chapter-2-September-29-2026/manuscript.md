## Why this form (the frame, for the book's opening)

The Piaseczner Rebbe opens *Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim* by saying that the ways of the tzaddikim, the Kabbalists and the Baal Shem Tov's disciples "are the ways of the prophets of G-d" (1:2), and that "the first revelation in the holy ones was their own soul, the part of G-d above within them, revealed to them" (1:6). Chassidus, on this reading, is the prophetic path brought back: not ideas about G-d, but the experience that the prophets trained for.

The owner's view, which frames this book: Judaism began as a contemplative, Eastern tradition. The Torah says Abraham gave "gifts" to the sons of his concubines and sent them "eastward" (Genesis 25:6), and Rashi, from the Talmud, says what he gave them was a hidden name. On this view the Eastern schools carried something of the prophetic inheritance, and a meditator trained in them would recognize what Chassidus is restoring. Aryeh Kaplan made the case that Jewish meditation is a missing link between East and West, and that Jewish masters were in dialogue with the Sufis; his claim about "the schools of India" has not been traced to a source (see `Aryeh Kaplan/threads/06-east-west-bridge.md`).

So each chapter becomes a sitting, written the way a contemplative teacher speaks: posture, stillness, the mind settling, direct seeing, silence, then the teaching arriving as something seen, and a return to the day.

## Chapter 2 · The breath from within

Sit down. Let your back be straight and your body still. Close your eyes.

Let the day settle. Thoughts will come. Don't follow them and don't push them away. Watch one arrive, and watch it go.

Now breathe out, slowly, from the bottom of your chest. Not from the lips. From deep inside.

Notice where that breath comes from. It's warm. It carries some of you out with it.

The Tanya says this is how you were made. "He breathed into him the breath of life." And the sages add: one who blows, blows from within himself, from his innermost.

Rest there. Before you were a name, a history, a list of what went wrong, you were breath, breathed out from the innermost of the One.

Now look for the one who is looking. Not the thoughts. Not the feelings. The one who sees them. Go under that.

The Tanya calls it "truly a part of G-d above." Not near Him. A part.

Now let it widen. The person in the next room. The stranger on the street. Someone who knows nothing at all. Each one breathed out from the same depth, and each still tied to it "in a wonderful and mighty union."

The mind will want to grasp how this can be. It can't. The Tanya says so: no human mind can understand it fully. Let the mind stop at its edge. Don't push. Rest where knowing ends.

Come back slowly. Open your eyes. Look at your hands.

Before you stand, say quietly: "Under all of this, a part of G-d above."

Carry it to the first person you meet today.

**The prophetic path in it.** This is the first revelation the Piaseczner names: the soul revealed to itself (Hakhsharat HaAvrekhim 1:6). Everything else in the book is built on it.
