Tiferet Yisrael ("the Glory of Israel") is the Maharal's book on the Torah: what it is, why it was given, how it joins a person to G-d, and how the giving at Sinai happened. The first half runs from the Introduction to the start of his long reading of the Ten Commandments.

Four teachings in this half matter most for the guide:

- **The Torah joins a person to G-d on every side.** A good trait joins you to G-d in one way only. The Torah, taken as a whole, joins you fully. The mitzvot "refine" (tzeruf, a smelting that takes out what is not gold) the soul out of mere nature.
- **He is found as the receiver can take Him.** When the Torah says "G-d came down" or "G-d was grieved", this is not a picture of G-d as He is in Himself. It is how He truly is found by man. The Maharal calls this "a very great foundation" to keep before your eyes always (472).
- **From the One comes a many that becomes one.** Against the philosophers, he holds that the many can come from the One. The world begins with bet (two, the start of many); the Torah begins with alef (one), and it binds the many into one again.
- **Simplicity and the middle receive.** Moses was fit for the Torah because his humility made him "fully simple". Man's place is under the Throne as a receiver. Everything has an opposite except the middle.

## 2.1 He is found as the receiver can take Him

This is the center of the range. The Rambam taught that words like "He came down" describe no real motion and must be read away (Onkelos: "He was revealed"). The Maharal says these words bring no bodily picture at all, because "הם נאמרים כלפי האדם. כי כך נמצא השם יתברך אצל האדם" (464, 33:2): "they are said toward man, for that is how G-d is found by man."

He grounds this in the naming. Adam named all creatures by how they stand toward man, the center of the lower world. He also named G-d: "Adon" (Master), because that is how G-d stands toward man. And G-d agreed: "אני הוא הוא שמי שקרא לי אדם" (466, 33:4): "I am; that is My name, which Adam called Me." Adam did not call Him by the unique Name, "כי במה שהוא מיוחד הוא נבדל מן הנמצאים" (468, 33:6): "for as He is unique He is apart from all beings."

The rule: "כי השם יתברך נמצא לפי המקבל" (471, 33:9): "for G-d is found according to the receiver." Then comes the key step. This is not only appearance: "רק הוא כך באמת נמצא, כי אל האדם הוא יורד על הר סיני" (472, 33:10): "rather He truly is found so: toward man He comes down on Mount Sinai." And he adds: "ודבר זה הוא יסוד גדול מאד מאד, ראוי לך שיהיה" [הזה נגד עיניך תמיד] (472): "this is a very great foundation; it is fitting for you to have it before your eyes always."

So "He was grieved" and "Awake, why do You sleep?" are how He is found when people do not do His will, "ועם שהשמחה במעונו" (473, 33:11): "though joy is in His dwelling." At the sea He was seen as a warrior, at Sinai as a scribe teaching Torah, in Daniel's time as an elder: "הרי התבאר לך כי השם יתברך נמצא לפי המקבלים כאשר הם" (474, 33:12): "so it is clear that G-d is found according to the receivers, as they are." When creatures are whole, "ישמח ה' במעשיו" (475): "G-d rejoices in His works."

## 2.2 Nothing can stand with Him

The other side of 2.1: in Himself He has no partner in being. At Sinai the people's souls left them at each word, "כי במדרגת מציאות השם יתברך, אין נמצא שום מציאות כלל" (445, 31:11): "for at the level of G-d's being, no being at all is found." "ולפיכך אי אפשר לומר שיהיה שום מציאות נמצא קיים עמו יתברך" (445): "so one cannot say that any being exists and stands with Him." They lived only because "שהיו דבקים בו במדרגה העליונה, שמשם החיות בא למתים" (445): "they cleaved to Him at the high level from which life comes to the dead." They drew back twelve mil at each word, meaning "שהיו נדחים ונבדלים מן השם יתברך לגמרי בעבור שאין שתוף עם השם יתברך" (447, 31:13): "they were pushed back and fully set apart from G-d, because nothing can share with G-d."

The same point is his reading of holiness. Nadav and Avihu died at the Tabernacle's dedication to show "כי אף לצדיקים אינו מתאחד עמהם, ואין לו צירוף עמהם, וזהו קדושתו" (264, 18:1): "for even with the righteous He does not become one with them and is not joined with them; this is His holiness." The angels' "Kadosh" means "שהוא יתברך בלבד נבדל וקדוש מן החומר" (177, 11:13): "He alone is set apart and holy from matter." Even Moses could not know beforehand what G-d would say to him, "כי אי אפשר להתחבר עם השם יתברך" (308, 21:9): "for it is impossible to be joined with G-d."

(Ours) These two sections together are the Maharal's picture of unity: in Himself He stands alone and nothing stands with Him; toward us He is truly found as we can take Him.

## 2.3 From the One, a many that becomes one

Each word at Sinai split into seventy tongues. The Maharal draws a rule: "שמן השם יתברך שהוא אחד, מחייב הרבוי" (436, 31:2): "that from G-d, who is One, the many must come." This is "הפך מה שאומרים הפילוסופים, כי מן האחד לא יבא הרבוי" (436): "the opposite of what the philosophers say, that from the One the many cannot come." The many that comes from Him is special: "אבל מן השם יתברך שהוא אחד, יבא הרבוי, כאשר הרבוי הוא מתדמה לאחד" (436): "but from G-d, who is One, the many comes, when the many is like the One." Seventy, a whole number, "הוא רבוי שהוא מתאחד" (436): "is a many that becomes one." So the people heard one voice from all sides, then seven, then seventy, "כל אחד ואחד היה שומע לפי כחו" (437, 31:3): "each one heard according to his strength."

The letters say the same. The world was made with bet, "כי הבי"ת בפרט הוא התחלת הרבוי" (480, 34:3): "for the bet in particular is the start of the many", and the many is blessing. But the Torah begins with alef, in "Anochi". G-d told the silent alef: "אתה אחד, ואני אחד, והתורה היא אחת" (484, 34:7): "you are one, and I am one, and the Torah is one." And: "כי התורה הפך העולם; שהוא הרבוי, והתורה היא סדר העולם וקשור העולם, עד שהיא אחד, מורה על מסדר שהוא אחד" (484): "for the Torah is the reverse of the world: the world is the many, and the Torah is the order and binding of the world, until it is one, showing an Orderer who is One." So the ten words were said as one utterance, "כי התורה היא סדר העולם, וכל סדר מקשר דבר עם דבר, עד שהכל נעשה אחד" (478, 34:1): "for every order ties one thing to another until all becomes one."

## 2.4 All opposites belong to Him

The todah (thanksgiving) offering had both leavened and unleavened bread, two opposite things. It will remain when other offerings end, "להודיע כי כל הכחות אשר הם מחולקים, הכל הוא אליו יתברך" (431, 30:10): "to show that all the powers that are divided all belong to Him", "עד שהוא יתברך הוא אחד, ואין זולתו. ודבר זה יהיה לעתיד" (431): "until He is One and there is none besides Him. And this will be in the future." Gladdening a bride and groom is "as if he brought a todah", because two fully different beings are joined by Him: "וזה מורה כי כל חלופי המציאות, כמו שהם זכר ונקבה, הם אל השם יתברך" (432, 30:11): "this shows that all the differences in being, like male and female, belong to G-d."

## 2.5 "He is all and there is none besides Him"

In the order of the commandments, "Lo yihyeh" (have no other gods) comes after "Anochi". One who serves another power may not deny G-d, but he takes away from Him, since "שהוא יתברך הכל ואין זולתו" (506, 36:1): "He is all and there is none besides Him." "ולכך העובד זולתו, אינו נותן אל השם יתברך מדרגה זאת, שהוא הכל ואין זולתו" (506): "so one who serves another does not give G-d this level, that He is all and there is none besides Him."

The same care for the world being one runs through the range. One may not pray "Your mercy reaches the bird's nest", since that makes G-d's decrees into His favoring one species, "ובזה עושה חלוק ופרוד בין הנמצאים, עד שאין העולם אחד" (98, 6:4): "and so he makes division and separation between beings, until the world is not one." And man must receive the Torah as a decree, not as his own choice, "עד שיהיה העולם אחד לגמרי" (241, 16:13): "until the world is fully one." Without a command from above, man, the head of the lower world with free choice, "היה האדם לעצמו, ולא היה עליו עול מלכות שמים, והיה הוא אלוה חס ושלום" (248, 16:20): "would be for himself, with no yoke of heaven on him, and he would be a god, heaven forbid."

## 2.6 Nature as the covering, and "Master" as the bond

Why was circumcision given first to Abraham? The foreskin is a covering. Until Abraham, "כי הטבע שהיא חמרית, היא מבדלת בין השם יתברך ובין הבריות" (281, 19:6): "for nature, which is bodily, is what separates between G-d and the creatures." Abraham was the first to call Him "Adon" (Master), "כי האדון מצטרף ומתחבר אל אשר הוא אדון לו, ואינו נבדל הימנו" (282, 19:7): "for a master is joined and bound to the one he is master of, and is not apart from him."

## 2.7 King from His own side

"Anochi" is not phrased as a command ("I will be your G-d"). "אף אם לא יקבל אותו האדם לאלוה, הוא אלקים שלו, והוא מלכו מצד עצמו" (519, 37:2): "even if a person does not accept Him as G-d, He is his G-d and his King from His own side." A king cannot decree "accept me as king"; he says, "ידעתם שאני מלך שלכם, ולכך כן תעשו לקבל מלכותי" (520, 37:3): "you know that I am your King; so do this and accept My kingship."

## 2.8 The final form of all

G-d is called "zeh" (this) because "zeh" points at the form of a thing: "והשם יתברך על הכל, שהוא צורה אחרונה אל הכל, משלים הכל" (195, 12:15): "and G-d is over all; He is the final form of all, completing all." He quotes the Zohar's layers of the Torah (garment, body, soul) and above (heavens, Knesset Yisrael, Tiferet, and the Holy Ancient One): "ונשמתא לנשמתא דא איהו עתיקא קדישא, וכלא אחיד דא בדא" (200, 13:4): "and the soul of the soul is the Holy Ancient One, and all is held one in the other."

**Proposed MH-S19 "My place under the Throne".** Inside: I feel small before stronger forces and greater people, as if their breath could burn me. And yet I have a place of my own, as the receiver. When the angels objected, Moses said, "מתירא אני שמא ישרפוני בהבל פיהם" ["I fear they will burn me with the breath of their mouths"]; the Maharal explains "שהאדם אינו נחשב לכלום נגד העליונים" (339, 24:7): "for man counts as nothing before the upper beings." G-d answered "hold My Throne": "כך יש לאדם מקום תחת כסא הכבוד בעצמו" (340, 24:8): "so man has a place of his own under the Throne of Glory." The angels circle the Throne; man stands under it, because he receives: "כלל הדבר, כי האדם כלי מוכן לקבל כבוד ה'" (341, 24:9): "the rule is: man is a vessel ready to receive the glory of G-d", "שאין לו בעצמו המדרגה העליונה, אבל הוא מיוחד לקבל, וזורח עליו כבוד השם יתברך" (341): "he has no high level of his own, but he is made to receive, and G-d's glory shines on him." How to enter: when you feel you do not count, do not argue for your own worth. Say: "My place is the place of the one who receives. I hold the Throne." Source: 339-343. (Ours) This is a state of worth without pride: the worth is the place of receiving, not a rank of one's own.

**Proposed MH-S20 "Two crowns".** Inside: a readiness to do His will at once, before the mind has weighed it, and the freedom that comes with that. Usually a person first learns that a thing is good and then does it. The people said "we will do" first, like angels: "והידיעה בפעולה זאת נמשך אחר המעשה" (416, 29:3): "and the knowing of this act follows the doing." Then neither the soul nor the mind rules the other: "ולפיכך שניהם הם מלכים, שאין צריך זה לזה" (419, 29:6): "so both are kings, and neither needs the other." Sin takes the crowns away, "כי החוטא הוא משועבד אל חומרו ויצרו, ואינו מלך" (420, 29:7): "for the sinner is a slave to his body and his inclination, and is not a king." How to enter: when a clear mitzvah is in front of you, do it before the inner debate starts. Notice the lightness afterward. Source: 414-420. Caution (ours): this is for clear commands of the Torah. It is not a rule to act before thinking in other matters.

## 4.1 Aim at the Giver (`before_whom`; new: `mh_aim_at_the_giver`)

The Introduction opens with comfort. The Torah is "set down" before all, "אבל התורה נתנה לכל" (1, Intro 1): "but the Torah was given to all." Do not say "I may err." The blessing is "to engage in" Torah, not "to master" it. The way to be safe from error is to aim at the Giver: "כאשר האדם מכוין בתורה להשם יתברך אשר נתן התורה, גם כן השם יתברך מציל אותו מן השגיאה" (16, Intro 16): "when a person in his learning aims at G-d who gave the Torah, G-d also saves him from error." The first generation's failing, as he reads it, was that they did not bless first: they loved the Torah and forgot the one who gave it. The blessing is "היא האהבה הגמורה והדבקות אל השם יתברך, לאהוב השם יתברך במה שנתן תורה" (11, Intro 11): "the complete love and cleaving to G-d, to love G-d for having given the Torah." How: before learning, say the Torah blessing slowly. At "who gives the Torah" (present tense, 237: "כי עתה מלמד תורה לעמו" "for He is now teaching Torah to His people"), think: He is giving it now, to me. Then learn.

## 4.2 Fear as setting yourself aside (`bittul`; with MH-S16)

The Maharal contrasts love and fear. In love, one joins G-d "כמו שני אוהבים שמתחברים זה לזה" (160, 10:11): "like two friends who join each other", keeping one's own existence. In fear: "אבל היראה, מפני שהירא מאחר אין מחשיב עצמו לכלום, ומבטל מציאותו אצל אחר" (160): "but fear: the one who fears another counts himself as nothing and sets aside his existence before the other." "נמצא שאין לו מציאות מצד עצמו, רק מה שיש לו מן השם יתברך. וזהו קיומו בודאי" (160): "so he has no existence of his own, only what he has from G-d. And this surely is his lasting." How: once a day, stand still and say, "All I have of being is what I have from You." Source: 159-164. Caution (ours): this is setting aside the self before G-d. It is not despising oneself; see 4.4 and 26 ("You do not despise what You formed").

## 4.3 Say "yes" to every word (`accept`; new: `mh_yes_to_every_word`)

At Sinai, for Rabbi Yishmael the people answered each word as it was: "yes" to a positive command, "no" to a negative one. For Rabbi Akiva they said "yes" to both, because receiving a decree means only this: "שהוא מוכן לעשות רצון הגוזר, וזה שיאמר 'הן'" (488, 34:11): "that he is ready to do the will of the One who decrees, and this is saying 'yes'." How: when you learn a law, whether "do" or "do not", say inwardly "yes" to it before anything else. Source: 488; with MH-S20.

## 4.4 The todah over opposites (`heart_after`; new: `mh_todah_all_to_him`)

The todah offering holds two opposite breads, to show that all divided powers belong to Him (431). How: when you thank G-d, do not thank only for the sweet part. Name one thing that felt like leaven and one that felt like matza, one hard and one easy, and say: "Both are Yours." End with the Maharal's words for the future: "עד שהוא יתברך הוא אחד, ואין זולתו" (431): "until He is One and there is none besides Him." Source: 431-432. Caution (ours): this is about one's own life held before G-d. It is not a way to call another person's pain good.

## 4.5 Count each day as nearer (`act`; new: `mh_count_each_day`)

After leaving Egypt, the people were like a newborn, body without mind. Counting fifty days took them to the Torah: "וצריך לספור יום אחר יום, כי כל יום ויום הוא קירוב יותר" (368, 25:20): "one must count day after day, for each day is a nearer drawing close." The fiftieth is not counted, since the mind is apart from the forty-nine. And the gift needs its own time to arrive: "הרצוי הזה הוא זמן בפני עצמו, והגעת המתנה אל האוהב הוא זמן בפני עצמו" (395, 27:8): "the agreeing is one time, and the gift reaching the friend is another time." How: in the Omer, and in any long effort, say each day: "Today I am one day nearer." Do not demand the gift on the day it is promised. Source: 360-368, 395.

## 4.6 Hold the Throne (`quieting`; new: `mh_hold_the_throne`)

This is the practice for MH-S19. When you feel you do not count, picture yourself not among the circling angels but under the Throne, holding it, as Moses did. Say: "I am a vessel made to receive." Wait. Source: 339-343.

## 4.7 Be simple (`quieting`; with `mh_remnant` and MH-S11)

Satan looked for the Torah in the earth, the sea, the deep and in destruction; none was simple enough. Moses said "who am I?", and this was his fitness: "אבל מי שיש בו הענוה, עד שאינו מחשיב עצמו לדבר כלל, הרי הוא פשוט לגמרי, שאינו מיוחד בדבר מה" (330, 23:9): "but one who has humility, who does not count himself as anything at all, is fully simple; he is not set apart by anything." "וזה שהוא ראוי ומיוחד אל התורה, שהיא שכל פשוט" (330): "and this is what makes him fit for the Torah, which is simple mind." "כי עצם הכנת משה לתורה מה שהיה פשוט לגמרי מכל" (331): "for the essence of Moses' readiness for the Torah was that he was fully simple, more than all." How: before learning, let go of what sets you apart (what you know, your place, your wish to be right). Come plain.

## 4.8 Rise for the Shechina (`act`; new: `mh_rise_for_the_shechina`)

Father, mother and G-d are partners in a person. "בזמן שהאדם מכבד אביו ואמו, דומה כאילו דר הקב"ה ביניהם, וכבדוהו" (510, 36:5): "when a person honors his father and mother, it is as if the Holy One dwelt among them and they honored Him." When Rav Yosef heard his mother's steps he said: "איקום מקמי דשכנתא דאתיא" (510): "I will stand up before the Shechina who is coming." How: when a parent comes into the room, stand, and know whom you are standing for. The second half (634) adds why the mother in particular. Caution (ours): this honors parents; it does not ask anyone to stay in harm's way.

## 4.9 Guard: the mitzvah is a decree, not a medicine

The red heifer: "the dead does not defile and the water does not purify". The Torah is not a book of medicine or nature. A person should not say "I cannot stand pork", but "אפשי, ומה אעשה שאבי שבשמים גזר עלי" (131, 8:5): "I would like it, but what can I do, since my Father in heaven has decreed against it." (Ours) The guide should not sell mitzvot as health or calm techniques. Their work is to join a person to G-d: "על ידי שעושה המצוה, שהיא סדר שכלי, יש לאדם דביקות בשכלי" (118, 7:6): "by doing the mitzvah, which is an order of the mind, a person has cleaving to the mind", and through it to Him.

## 5. The big human questions

**Why can I never rest in what I already am?** "כי אין מעלת נפשו האחרונה בפעל" (54, 3:1): "for the final level of his soul is not yet in act." Angels are finished; animals do not change; man alone is made in potential and must bring himself out. So the soul is never full: "לפי שהנפש יודעת שכל אשר תיגע לעצמה תיגע, לפיכך אינה שבעה מצות ומעשים טובים" (65, 3:12): "because the soul knows that whatever it toils for, it toils for itself, so it is never sated with mitzvot and good deeds." She is a king's daughter married to a villager; nothing he brings is enough.

**What do the commandments do for me?** "והם הם כמו החבל שמעלין את האדם מבור התחתיות" (69, 4:1): "they are like the rope that lifts a person from the lowest pit", "להושיב אותו עם המלך ה' צבאות, עין בעין" (69): "to seat him with the King, the L-rd of hosts, eye to eye." "וכאשר האדם עושה המצוה שהשכל מחייב, הרי בזה יוצאה נפשו מן הטבע החמרית, ודבקה בשכלי" (69): "when a person does the mitzvah that the mind requires, his soul goes out of bodily nature and cleaves to the mind."

**Can small, dry laws matter?** Yes. "כי השונה הלכות, אף כי שונה בדבר קטון, דבר זה הולך ועולה עד למעלה" (210, 13:14): "for one who learns laws, even about a small matter, this goes up and up to the very top." The Torah's garment is joined to its body and its body to its soul: "המלבוש קרוב אל הגוף, והגוף הוא קרוב אל הנשמה, עד שהכל מתחבר ומתדבק בהשגה עליונה" (208, 13:12): "the garment is near the body and the body near the soul, until all is joined and cleaves to a high perception." "אל תסתכל בקנקן אלא במה שיש בו" (198, 13:2): "do not look at the jug but at what is in it."

**If I have pushed myself far away, will He still take me?** David's prayer: "כי אחר שאתה יצרתני, אין אתה מואס ביצוריך" (26, Intro 26): "for since You formed me, You do not despise what You formed." If my deeds pushed me away, You are my redeemer.

**Does G-d change? Is He sad, or glad?** In Himself, "though joy is in His dwelling" (473). Toward us He is truly found as we are (474). When His works are whole, "G-d rejoices in His works" (475).

**Why did I have to be given the Torah; could I not choose it?** If the world's completion hung on our choice, "ואם כן שלמות העולם היה תולה בדבר אפשרי" (453, 32:3): "then the world's completeness would hang on something merely possible." So: "ובזה די לבאר כי התורה לישראל ברצון מצד עצמם, אבל הכרחי היה להם מצד השם יתברך" (462, 32:12): "the Torah is theirs by will from their side, but necessary from G-d's side." (Ours) Both are true of a person's bond with G-d: freely chosen, and not ours to cancel.

**Where do I belong?** "כי השם יתברך הוא מקום ובית לכל" [הנמצאים אשר הם דבקים בו] (163, 10:14): "for G-d is the place and home of all beings that cleave to Him."

## 6. With Chabad (ours)

- **"Found as the receiver can take Him"** (464-475) is close to the Tanya's Gate of Unity (chapters 6-7): all change is on the side of the receivers; from His side nothing changes. The difference is in the weight. The Tanya uses this to teach that the world's separate being is, from His side, not there at all ("ein od milvado", there is none besides Him). The Maharal uses it to say that how He is found by us is real, "truly found so" (472), and must not be read away as mere figure (against the Rambam's reading of Onkelos). The guide can hold both: in Himself, none besides Him; toward us, truly Master, truly near.
- **"No being at His level"** (445) matches the Tanya's bittul (self-nullification): created being is as nothing before Him (Tanya ch. 20-21 on "Anochi" and "Lo yihyeh"). The Maharal reaches it through the Sinai story (souls leaving), not through a teaching on speech that creates constantly.
- **Tzimtzum.** The Maharal has no tzimtzum here. The nearest idea is 281: nature, as bodily, is the covering between G-d and creatures, removed first with Abraham. The Tanya's concealment of the light is a hiding from above for the sake of worlds; the Maharal's covering is the bodily as such. Do not merge them.
- **The Rambam's negative attributes.** The Maharal keeps the Rambam's rule that G-d is not a body (177: "Kadosh" means He alone is apart from matter; the second half, 541, adds three kinds of separation). But he rejects the Rambam's way of reading "He came down" as figure. He also rejects the Rambam's and the Ramban's reasons for the mitzvot (98-116).
- **The Ramak's formula** ("He is all, and all is not He"). The first half appears plainly at 506: "שהוא יתברך הכל ואין זולתו" "He is all and there is none besides Him." The second half, that all is not He, is the Maharal's constant theme of separation (264, 445-447). He never puts the two halves into one sentence.
- **Love and fear** (160): the Maharal makes fear the higher, because fear sets one's existence aside, while love keeps two. The Tanya (ch. 41-43) makes awe the gate and love the goal. The guide should keep the Maharal's point as one voice, not as a ranking over the Tanya.
- **Humility as simplicity** (330) is close to Chabad bittul and to the Tanya's "one whose heart is broken" as a vessel.
