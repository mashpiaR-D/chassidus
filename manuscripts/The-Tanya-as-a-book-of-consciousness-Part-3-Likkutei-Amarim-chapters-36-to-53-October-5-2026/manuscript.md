# The Tanya as a book of consciousness · Part 3: Likkutei Amarim, chapters 36 to 53

**Only One research** · October 5, 2026 · early draft 0.1 · Result [030](../../CONTENTS.md#030) · manuscript 3 of 7 · [Cite](CITATION.bib) · [Quote check](../../verification/catalogue.md#the-tanya-as-a-book-of-consciousness-part-3-likkutei-amarim-chapters-36-to-53-october-5-2026)

---
This part of the Tanya turns from the inner war to its purpose, and then to the states of the heart that carry a person through a day of service. It opens with a way of seeing this coarse, dark world as the very place the One wants to live (36), and a way of feeling a plain deed, a coin, a spoken word, lift your life up to Him (37). It then asks what an intention is from inside, and how a little of it makes a lifetime of plain deeds fly (38 to 40). Chapter 41 begins a run of states to enter before prayer and study: standing before the King who looks at you, then handing your soul back to Him (41); tying the mind to Him until everything you see is His clothing (42); the lower awe and the higher awe, the love that comes from understanding and the love that burns (43); the love you were born with (44); mercy for the spark in exile (45); love waking love, like a face in water (46); leaving Egypt this morning (47). Then the book shows the ground under all these states: He hid His endless light so that we could exist, and He did it out of love (48, 49). The heart burns and comes back (50). The Presence rests where a person makes room for it (51, 52), and the part ends on a candle whose wick turns into fire (53). Every chapter below was read in the Hebrew and retold as states a person can be in. Every Hebrew line was checked against the source file, `Tanya — Hebrew text.txt`, at the line number given.

## Chapter 36 · At home in the dark

**The state.** Seeing this thick, low world, where G-d seems absent, as the very place He wants to live: *dirah batachtonim*, "a dwelling in the lowest place." It is the state of standing in darkness and knowing the darkness is where the light is meant to break through.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The chapter starts with a saying everyone knows: G-d created this world because He wanted a home in the lowest place. Right away that sounds wrong. For Him there is no high and low. He fills every world the same. So the chapter moves the difference to where it really lives: in the ones who receive. Before the world was made He was alone, filling this very space, and He still is, the same as before. What changed is us. We take in His light through many garments that cover it. "No person can see Me and live." Even the highest angels do not see.

So the long chain of worlds, one step down after another, is a chain of states of seeing less. Each world is a thicker garment. At the bottom is this world, physical and heavy, where the hiding is doubled and doubled again. It is so dark that it is full of forces that say, "I am, and there is nothing besides me." That is a state you know from inside. It is the feeling that things just stand on their own, that I stand on my own, and that G-d is an idea somewhere else.

Now the turn. The purpose of the whole descent is not the high worlds. For them the chain is only a coming down. The purpose is this low world, because the One wanted the pleasure of seeing the other side bent and darkness turned into light. When His light shines in a place that was dark, it shines with more force than it does above, the way light is most striking against darkness. Above, the light comes through garments so the worlds are not wiped out by it. Here, when it breaks through, it breaks through the thickest thing there is.

To bear that light without vanishing, He gave the Torah, which is called strength. The chapter looks forward to a time when people will see Him with no garment at all: "your eyes will see your Teacher." It says the days of Moshiach and the rising of the dead are the goal this world was made for. And it says the world has already had a taste of that state once, at Sinai. "You were shown, to know that G-d is the One; there is nothing else besides Him." Shown, really shown, with the eyes. The people saw the sounds. Wherever they turned, the Word came at them. There was no hiding at all, and so they were nullified: at each word their souls flew out, and He brought them back with the dew of Torah. Afterward the wrong of the golden calf made them, and the world, thick again, until the end of days, when the body and the world will be refined enough to hold the light. Then the light shining on Israel will light up the darkness of the nations too: "Nations will walk by your light."

The picture of Sinai breaks in one place. At Sinai the light overwhelmed the people. The goal is a light that people can bear and stay themselves. That is what the Torah, "strength," is for.

**The movement.** From the state where the world is thick and self-standing and G-d is somewhere else, to the state where this very thickness is known as His chosen home, waiting for His light. What carries a person across is understanding the purpose: seeing that the darkness is not a mistake but the place the whole plan is aimed at, and that your deeds here are how the light breaks in.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "He wanted a home in the lowest place." - Think it through: He fills this place now as He did before the world. Only I don't see. The not-seeing is the garment. - Bring it down to one real thing: the room you are in, the work waiting on your desk. This is the low place He chose. - Where the picture breaks: a home here is not a house He moves into; He is already here. The home is made when the hiding stops ruling. - Stop at the edge: I can't picture a light with no garment. Let the not-knowing stand. - Words to Him: "You wanted to live here. Let me be a room in it." - One act: do one good deed today in the plainest place you go.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the dull, heavy parts of your day stop feeling like exile from G-d and start feeling like the job site. The fake is contempt for the world dressed as spirituality: fleeing the low place instead of lighting it.

## Chapter 37 · The hand that lifts

**The state.** Feeling that a plain deed, done because He wants it, lifts the life inside you and inside the thing up to Him. Hebrew: *ha'ala'ah*, "lifting," and *nefesh hachiyunit*, "the animal soul," the plain life-force that runs the body.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Chapter 36 described the goal: the light of the Infinite shining in this physical world. Now the chapter says that goal hangs on what we do now, in the long exile. The reason is simple. The reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah itself. When a person does one, he draws the Infinite's light down into a physical thing that was until now under *kelipat nogah*, "the glowing husk," the mixed force that gives life to everything allowed and neutral in this world. The parchment of tefillin, an etrog, a coin given to charity that was not stolen. The moment it is used for His will, the life in it rises and melts into His light, because in His will there is no hiding at all.

The same happens to you. The life-force in your hands and legs that does the mitzvah is dressed in the deed, rises out of the husk, and is folded into holiness. Even mitzvot of speech, like learning, the Shema and prayer, need the lips to move. Thought alone does not count. The G-dly soul cannot move lips and tongue by itself. It needs the plain body-life. So the harder you speak, the more of that plain life you pour into holy words. "All my bones will say." That is why the sages said learning that is arranged in all your limbs is kept, and learning that isn't is forgotten. Forgetting comes from the body's heaviness; when you tire it out in holy work it joins the holy.

There is more. That body-life grows from blood, and the blood came from food and drink, which lived under the glowing husk. So when you pour your strength into holy words, all that food rises with you. The chapter widens the picture: if every soul keeps the whole Torah, the whole life of this physical world will rise. The 365 "don't" commandments cut the body-life off from the forces that can't be lifted at all. The 248 "do" commandments draw the Infinite down and tie the body-life to it until they are truly one. When that happens, even the glowing husk that runs this world now will become a chariot for Him, and the fully dark forces, which only feed through it, will dry up.

Each soul holds a share of the whole world, the share it uses: its food, its home, its tools. It came down into a body only to fix that share. The soul itself needs no fixing. The drop is enormous. Even a perfect tzaddik here does not reach the closeness he had before he came down. He came down to lift what is his.

That is why charity is called simply "the mitzvah." In most mitzvot only one power of your body-life is dressed in the act. But in charity you give from the work of your hands. All the strength you put into earning that money is inside it, and when you give it, your whole life goes up to G-d. Even if you didn't earn it by toil, that money could have bought your life, so you are giving your life. So one act of charity lifts more than many other deeds, and so it brings the redemption near.

Learning Torah is "equal to all of them" for another reason. It uses speech and thought, the inner garments of the body-life, and the very mind of the animal soul is changed into holiness when a person learns with real thinking. The feelings are harder to change; the chapter says a beinoni cannot turn them. And the light in Torah learning is to the light in deeds as the mind is to the limbs. Still, "the deed is what matters," and a person stops learning to do a mitzvah no one else can do, because the whole point is the home down here. Learning is also a calling. The one who learns calls G-d to come to him, like a small child calling his father to come and be with him and not leave him alone. Not a cry of "Father! Father!" with no Torah in it, but a call made by learning. Knowing this, a person can bring great awe into his learning. (I compressed the Kabbalah of the husks and the counts of limbs and sinews into plain sentences.)

**The movement.** From the state where the body and its work feel like a weight that pulls you away from G-d, to the state where that very weight is what rises. What carries you across is the act itself, and the knowing, during the act, that your strength and your hours are going up with it.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "When I give it to charity, my whole life goes up." - Think it through: the hours I worked, the strength in my hands, the food that became that strength. It is all in this coin. - Bring it down to one real thing: the next money you give. Look at it and count what is in it. - Where the picture breaks: the coin doesn't fly anywhere. The lifting is that it now belongs to His will, not to my wanting. - Stop at the edge: how a coin joins the Infinite I cannot see. Leave it there. - Words to Him: "Take my day with this." - One act: give charity today, and learn a few lines out loud, calling Him like a child calls his father.

**Signs and the fake.** You know you touched it when giving feels like handing over yourself, not like paying a fee. The fake is giving to feel generous, where the self rises and nothing else does.

## Chapter 38 · A word with a soul in it

**The state.** The state of a deed or a prayer that has an inner aim, *kavanah*, "intention," to cleave to Him by doing His will. And the difference, from inside, between intention that comes from understanding and intention that comes from the love you were born with.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The chapter starts from a law. If you say the Shema only in your heart, with all your focus, you have not done it; you must say it again out loud. If you said it out loud without paying attention, you have done it. Only the first verse of the Shema and the first blessing of the Amidah need intention to count. The reason fits chapter 37: the soul itself needs no fixing. It came to draw light down onto the body and its life, and that happens through the lips and the hands.

But the sages also said: a prayer without intention is like a body without a soul. The chapter draws the picture carefully. Everything in this world has a body and a soul, even a stone has some light that keeps it from falling back into nothing. It is one and the same light in all of them, hidden under the same garment. Yet the light that shines in a body is nothing next to the light that shines in a soul. There are four levels: the stone, the plant, the animal, the speaking person. Each has more life showing.

So too with deeds. The light in a plain deed, and in holy words said without thought, is His inner will with no hiding at all. That is a great deal. But the light in the *intention* of that deed, when a person does it in order to cleave to Him, is greater without end, as the soul is greater than the body. Not because thinking about G-d is higher in itself than doing His will; the chapter says a later chapter will show otherwise. It is because He wants the intention too, and the light of that wanting is bigger. The same one will is in both, yet it shines in a small and narrow way in the deed, and widely in the aim.

There are four levels here too. The body of the mitzvah is two levels: deeds of the hand, and deeds of speech and thought. The soul of the mitzvah, the intention, is two levels, like the two souls in a living body, the animal and the speaking person.

The speaking level is the one whose mind is strong enough to think about His greatness and give birth, from that thinking, to a higher awe in the mind and a love in the right side of the heart, a soul thirsty for Him, and who learns and prays with that aim. That is like a person who speaks from knowledge and choice.

The animal level is the one whose mind is too short to give birth to open love and awe. He does not feel them in his heart. But he remembers the love he was born with, the hidden love every soul carries, and brings it up into his mind at least, so that in his depths he truly agrees to give his life for G-d's oneness, to be joined to Him. Awe is folded into it: he accepts His rule and will not rebel. With that aim he keeps away from bad, does good, learns and prays, understanding the words but without feeling. That intention is like an animal's soul. An animal has no reason or choice. Its fear of what hurts it and its love of what it likes are natural, not from understanding. So is the love inherited from our fathers: it is like a nature in the soul. And an animal soul is still a living soul, far above a stone.

**The movement.** From words and deeds that are a body with no soul, to deeds that have an aim in them. What carries you across is the intention itself: either born from thinking about His greatness, or, if you can't, woken by remembering the love you already have and deciding in your depths to be His.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "A prayer without intention is a body without a soul." - Think it through: the words I say every day are the body. What do I want when I say them? - Bring it down to one real thing: one blessing you will say today, over bread or water. - Where the picture breaks: a body without a soul is dead, but a prayer said plainly still counts and still draws light. It is a body that is alive, just not awake. - Stop at the edge: if no feeling comes, don't force it. Remember that deep down you would give yourself for Him. - Words to Him: "I say this in order to be close to You." - One act: before one blessing today, stop a moment and put in that aim.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a small blessing feels like it has someone inside it. The fake is chasing a warm feeling during prayer and calling the feeling the intention, when the intention is the aim to cleave to Him by doing what He wants.

## Chapter 39 · Where your service goes

**The state.** The states of service, laid out by where they reach: service with love and awe born of understanding, service with the love and awe you were born with, service by habit, and service for your own honor. Hebrew: *lishmah*, "for His sake," and *shelo lishmah*, "not for His sake."

**The chapter as consciousness.** The chapter keeps going with the picture of the animal. Angels are called "beasts" in the prophet's vision because they have no choice; their love and awe are natural to them, the way an animal fears what hurts it. That is why a tzaddik is higher than an angel. Most angels live in the world of *Yetzirah*, "Formation," where only G-d's feelings shine: His love, His awe. So they stand forever in fear, or forever in love, and don't rest day or night. The souls of tzaddikim live one world up, in *Beriah*, "Creation," where His wisdom and understanding shine, the source the feelings come from. A soul that served with love and awe born from thinking about His greatness lives there, and the "wish of the heart" it had becomes its garment in the upper Garden of Eden.

This is a map of states, retold as places. Each world is a kind of awareness. Yetzirah is the heart moved by feeling. Beriah is the heart moved by understanding. The joy of a created mind is only in what it understands, so the soul's pleasure there is to grasp what it can of the Infinite through His wisdom. Souls that served only with the hidden, natural love go up there only on Shabbat and the new month, to delight in Him. Yet even they rise above the angels, because they had a choice. The other side was dressed in their bodies, they could have chosen bad, and they chose good. Their natural love bent the other side, and that is light out of darkness.

That is where the souls stand. Their Torah and service go further: into the ten *sefirot*, "the ten powers," which are G-dliness itself. Service with love and awe from understanding enters the sefirot of Beriah; service with natural love and awe enters the sefirot of Yetzirah; and through them into Atzilut and into the Infinite. The souls stand outside in their rooms and enjoy the radiance, and the radiance is their own Torah and service. The reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah.

Above that is *Atzilut*, "Emanation," which no created mind can grasp. It is the home of the great tzaddikim who were a chariot for Him, who gave themselves over to Him all their lives so that they had no self apart from His will, like the Patriarchs. A smaller soul reaches that only at moments, like the bowing in the Amidah, when you are truly as nothing before Him. The chapter says: leave the hidden things to the great ones. Know your own.

And know the lowest levels too. Service with no love or awe woken at all, not even in the mind, where the natural love stays asleep in the heart just as it was before you started, stays down in the world of separateness. "Without awe and love it does not fly up." This is even if you aren't serving for any selfish reason, only by habit, the way you were trained from childhood. Real "for His sake" needs at least the natural love and awe brought up into the mind, because no one does a thing for another's sake unless he loves him or fears him. And love alone isn't called service without at least the lower awe.

When a person learns for his own honor, to be a scholar, that motive, which comes from the husk, is dressed inside his Torah, and the Torah is in exile there for a while, until he returns to G-d; then his Torah comes back with him. That is why the sages said: learn even not for His sake, because from not for His sake you come to for His sake. But learning done plainly, neither for His sake nor for yourself, doesn't wait for return. The moment you learn that same thing again for His sake, the plain learning joins it and flies up with it, since no husk was dressed in it. Until then plain learning rises only to the outer halls where angels stand. Learning for your own honor doesn't rise at all; it stays down here. (I compressed the Kabbalah of the worlds and sefirot into plain sentences.)

**The movement.** From service that is habit, or that serves yourself, to service that flies. What carries you across is waking even the natural love and awe into the mind before you begin, and going back over what you did plainly with an aim for His sake, which lifts the old along with the new.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "The moment I learn it again for His sake, what I learned plainly joins it and flies up." - Think it through: so much of what I did by habit is not lost. It is waiting. - Bring it down to one real thing: a prayer you said by rote this week, a page you read half asleep. - Where the picture breaks: nothing really "flies." It means the deed now belongs to Him and not to the outside of things. - Stop at the edge: I don't know where my service goes. I only know what I aim it at. - Words to Him: "Everything I did without thinking, I give it to You now." - One act: repeat one thing you learned or said plainly, this time with that aim.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when old habit suddenly feels like it belongs to someone. The fake is a quiet pride in being "sincere," which is the honor motive coming back in by a side door.

## Chapter 40 · Standing in the river

**The state.** The state of words and deeds that rise because love and awe carry them, and the opposite state: a thirst for G-d that does not drink. Hebrew: *dechilu urechimu*, "awe and love," called *gadpin*, "wings."

**The chapter as consciousness.** Learning and prayer without intention, as long as you have not gone back over them for His sake, do not rise even into the sefirot of the lowest worlds. They rise only to the outer halls where angels stand. The chapter quotes that plain learning makes angels in Yetzirah, and plain mitzvot make angels in Asiyah, the world of action. Learning for your own honor does not rise even there; it stays in this world. "Happy is the one who comes here with his learning in his hand," meaning, it did not stay down below.

But the Torah and the Holy One are one, and He fills every world the same. So why does it matter? Because the difference is in the receivers, in two ways. Higher worlds get more light, and get it with fewer screens. This world is low both ways. Its light is squeezed small to the very edge, which is why it is physical. And it comes through many screens, down to the glowing husk that gives life to everything allowed in this world, the talking life of a person included. When that life speaks idle words, the husk hides His holiness. He is here, "there is no place empty of Him," yet He is "the hidden G-d." When it speaks words of Torah and prayer, even without intention, the husk hides nothing; it even turns good. Still, the light in them is as small as light gets, because a voice is a physical thing.

Intention changes this. The intention is the root of the words; you say them because of it. So it is dressed in them and lifts them up to its own place, to the sefirot of Yetzirah or Beriah, depending on whether the love and awe were natural or born of understanding. There His will, dressed in those letters, shines with a light that cannot shine here at all, until the end of days, when the world rises from its thickness.

That is why awe and love are called wings. "With two it would fly." Those who learn with love and awe are called "sons"; those who don't are called "chicks," who can't fly yet. The picture is exact. A bird's wings are not the bird. Its life isn't in them; a bird with its wings cut off is still kosher. The head and body are the main thing, and the wings serve them so they can fly. So too the Torah and mitzvot are the main thing, the real union with Him, through His will shown in them. Love and awe lift them to the place where that will and union shine.

And though love and awe are commandments too, they are called wings, because the whole point of love is service from love. Love without service is "love in delights," a taste of the World to Come. "Today to do them, tomorrow to receive their reward." Now picture the one who has not reached that taste. His soul still thirsts for G-d and longs all day, and he does not quench his thirst with the Torah right in front of him. He is like a man standing in a river crying, "Water! Water to drink!" The prophet cries over him: "Ho, all who are thirsty, go to the water." Plainly read it makes no sense: if you are thirsty to learn, you would learn. The chapter's answer is that you can long and long and not drink. (I left aside the Kabbalah of which part of the Torah shines in which world, and the letters of the Name in the bird.)

**The movement.** From words that stay where they are said, to words that fly. And from a thirst that only cries, to a thirst that drinks. What carries you across is intention, the love and awe woken before you begin, and then turning the longing into learning.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Like one standing in a river, crying, Water!" - Think it through: when I long for closeness, the book is on my shelf. The water is at my knees. - Bring it down to one real thing: the last time you felt far from G-d and didn't open anything. - Where the picture breaks: the wings don't make the bird. Love that doesn't serve is not the main thing, however sweet. - Stop at the edge: I don't need the feeling to fly first. The body of the bird is the deed. - Words to Him: "I'm thirsty. Let me drink what You gave." - One act: next time you long for Him, learn a few lines at once.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when longing turns into a hand reaching for the book. The fake is longing that feeds on itself, a sweet sadness about being far that never picks up the water.

## Chapter 41 · Before the King, then handing yourself back

**The state.** Two states that begin all service. First *kabbalat ol*, "taking on the yoke": standing before the King who sees you. Then *mesirat nefesh*, "handing over the soul": giving your soul back to Him to be one with Him. The first is the servant, *eved*; the second is the child, *ben*.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The chapter says: always remember the beginning and the root of service. Awe is the root of turning from bad; love is the root of doing good. But love alone is not enough even for doing good. First wake at least the natural awe hidden in every heart, the refusal to rebel against the King of kings, so that it is open in your heart, or at least in your mind.

How? Think, at least in your mind, about the greatness of the Infinite. His kingship is over every world, high and low. He fills every world and surrounds every world. "Do I not fill heaven and earth?" And He sets aside the high worlds and the low and places His kingship on His people, and on you in particular, because each person must say, "The world was created for me." And you, too, take Him on as your King, to serve Him and do His will in every way a servant serves. And look: G-d stands over you. "The whole earth is full of His glory." He looks at you and searches your heart, whether you serve Him as you should. So serve Him in awe, like one standing before a king. Go deep in this thought and stay with it as long as your mind and your time allow, before learning or before a mitzvah, before putting on the tallit and tefillin.

Think also how the Infinite, who fills and surrounds all worlds, is dressed in these letters of Torah, in these tefillin and tzitzit, and by reading or wearing them you draw His light onto the part of G-d inside your body, to be folded into Him. In tefillin, your wisdom and understanding give themselves to His, to use them only for Him; your heart's love and awe give themselves to His knowing. "To make the heart and mind serve." In the tallit, draw His kingship onto you. "Set a king over yourself."

Now the honest part. You may do all this and feel no fear in your heart at all. Still, you took His rule upon yourself in your mind and will, and that acceptance is true beyond doubt, because it is the nature of every soul not to rebel against the holy King. So the Torah and mitzvot you do because of it are called "whole service," like a servant's service to his master. Service out of love alone, to cleave to Him, is not called a servant's service. The Zohar says: like an ox that first takes the yoke so it can bring good to the world, a person must first take the yoke of heaven, and if he hasn't, holiness does not rest on him. Every person must be both, servant and child.

Even if you feel no awe or shame even in your mind, because your soul comes from a low root, if you mean to serve the King, it is full service. And really you have awe: in that moment you hold back from what isn't right, the way you would if even an ordinary person were watching. That is the lower awe. The higher awe is shame before His greatness. Without any awe, love alone does not fly; a bird cannot fly with one wing. And awe alone is also one wing. So be a child too: wake the natural love into your mind, to cleave to Him.

Then widen the aim. Don't take yourself out of the whole. Aim to unite the Holy One with the *Shechinah*, "the Presence," the breath of His mouth that lives inside every world and is the source of your soul and every soul, the very power giving you the words you are learning now. To mean this truly you would need a great love, to give Him pleasure and not to quench your own thirst, like a son who loves his father and mother more than himself. You may not have that. Get used to the aim anyway: a little of your heart truly wants it, from the love you were born with to do whatever He wants.

But one thing every heart wants fully: to be one with Him and never cut off, even at the cost of life. And learning and prayer are themselves a giving of the soul, as when it leaves the body at the end of seventy years and no longer thinks about the body's needs, only about His words. That is why the morning begins: "My G-d, the soul You gave me is pure. You breathed it into me, and You will take it from me." Since You breathed it in and will take it, from now I hand it over and give it back to You, to be one with You. "To You, G-d, I lift my soul." By tying my thought to Your thought and my speech to Your speech, in the words of Torah and prayer, and most of all when I speak to You face to face: "Blessed are You."

With this readiness start the morning blessings, and start your set learning after prayer, and prepare again before learning at midday. If you learn for hours, renew it every hour, because each hour a new flow of life comes down and the old one returns up with all the good done in it. And let the whole aim of giving yourself be only to give Him pleasure, like the joy of a king when his only son comes home from captivity. That aim is true in every soul, at every hour. Still, set times to think about His greatness, to reach love and awe from understanding. (I compressed the Kabbalah of the tefillin sections and the hourly names into plain sentences.)

**The movement.** From service that begins in the middle, with love and no yoke, to service that has a root: first standing as a servant before the King who looks at you, then as a child handing the soul back. What carries you across is the thinking, drawn out as long as you can, before the act, and the words said to Him face to face.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "From now I hand it over and give it back to You." - Think it through: He fills this place. He stands over me and looks, now. And this soul was His before it was mine. - Bring it down to one real thing: the first words you say in the morning, or the first page you open today. - Where the picture breaks: a king can be asleep, and his eyes see only the outside. He does not sleep, and He sees the heart. - Stop at the edge: if no fear comes, don't wait for it. Take the yoke in your mind; that is enough. - Words to Him: "You breathed it into me. It's Yours. Blessed are You." - One act: say one blessing today slowly, as a handing over.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when you begin learning or prayer as someone who has already given himself. The fake is a warm, loving closeness that has no yoke in it, where you enjoy Him and He is not your King.

## Chapter 42 · The mind tied to Him

**The state.** *Da'at*, "knowing" as binding: the mind fastened on G-d's greatness as firmly as it fastens on a thing it sees, until the lower awe, *yirah tata'ah*, settles in and runs the day. Everything you see becomes the King's outer clothing.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The Torah asks, "What does G-d ask of you, but to fear Him?" The Talmud asks back, "Is fear a small thing?" and answers, "Yes, for Moses it is small." That seems to miss the point, since the verse asks it of *you*. The chapter's answer: every soul has something of Moses in it. Moses is the shepherd who draws *da'at*, knowing, down to every soul, each by its own measure, and in every generation sparks of him dress in the wise ones who teach the people to know G-d. The service of the heart follows knowing. "Know the G-d of your father, and serve Him with a whole heart."

But knowing is not just information, hearing about His greatness from teachers and books. It is sinking the mind deep into His greatness and fastening your thought on Him with the strength of heart and mind, until the thought is tied to Him with a strong knot, the way it is tied to a physical thing you look at hard. *Da'at* means joining, as in "Adam knew Eve."

Every soul has this power, from Moses. But because the soul is dressed in a body it needs double work. The work of the flesh: to beat down the body so it does not darken the soul's light, which is done through thoughts of return from the depth of the heart. And the work of the soul: not to find it too heavy to think deeply about His greatness for a long, unbroken stretch. How long differs by soul. A clear soul feels awe as soon as it thinks: "When a person considers that the great King whose glory fills the earth stands over him and sees what he does, awe comes at once." A lower soul finds G-dliness in thought only with difficulty, especially if stained by the wrongs of youth. Still, with force and effort and depth, for a long while, the lower awe will surely come. "I toiled and found; believe it." "If you seek it like silver and search for it like treasure, then you will understand the fear of G-d." Dig, the way a man digs for treasure deep in the earth, for the awe hidden in the understanding of every heart, a place above time.

Then bring it up into real thought and hold it there long enough that it acts: turning from bad and doing good in thought, speech and deed, before the One who watches and listens and understands everything you do. "An eye that sees and an ear that hears." He has no body, but everything is known to Him, far more than by seeing. The chapter gives a picture: the way you know, in your mind, everything happening in every part of your body, even heat in your toenail if it is burned. So He knows everything that happens in every creature, because all of it comes from Him. And then it says where the picture breaks: this is only to quiet the ear. Your soul is affected by the body's pain. He is not affected by the world at all. It changes nothing in Him.

Whoever you are, if you think about this for a long time each day, how He fills the heavens and the earth, "the whole earth is full of His glory," really, and watches and searches your heart, every word and step, then awe will be fixed in your heart for the whole day. Whenever you think of it again, even lightly, you will turn from bad and do good, so as not to defy the eyes of His glory. For the knowing part of the soul, the Moses in you, this awe is a small thing.

One more thought. With a human king, the awe is of his inner life, not his body; when he sleeps nobody fears him. You don't see his inner life with your eyes; you see his body and clothes and know he is inside them. So fear G-d by seeing heaven and earth with your eyes and knowing His light is dressed in them, giving them life. The notes add that the sky itself seems to bow each evening as it sets in the west, and even someone who never saw the king feels awe entering a court where great ministers all bow to one man. It makes no difference how many layers of clothes the king wears. The main thing is habit: train your mind until it is fixed there that everything you see, heaven and earth and all they hold, is the outer clothing of the King, and so you always remember what is inside. This is what *emunah*, "faith," means too: training, like a craftsman training his hands. And remember "taking on the yoke of heaven," which you do in words in the Shema and then in the act when you bow in the Amidah.

**The movement.** From knowing about G-d, to knowing Him the way the mind knows what it is fixed on. What carries you across is effort: long, deep thought each day, digging for the awe you already have, and habit, until the clothing of the world keeps pointing you inward all day.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Everything I see is the outer clothing of the King." - Think it through: He fills this room. He sees what I do, as I feel my own hand. My eyes see the clothes; my mind knows who wears them. - Bring it down to one real thing: the street outside, the table, the sky tonight. - Where the picture breaks: I feel my body's pain; nothing in the world changes Him. - Stop at the edge: if nothing comes, keep digging. Treasure is deep. - Words to Him: "You are here, and You see me." - One act: before one choice today, remember the eye that sees, and choose for Him.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when awe stays with you after the thinking ends and quietly stops you from something small. The fake is a thrill of awe during the thinking that changes nothing in the day.

## Chapter 43 · Two awes, two loves

**The state.** A ladder of heart states. The lower awe, *yirah tata'ah*, fear of the King that keeps you from wrong; the higher awe, *yirah ila'ah*, "shame" before Him, felt when you see everything coming out of nothing. And two loves: *ahavat olam*, "worldly love," the love born from understanding, and *ahavah rabbah*, "great love," a flame given from above.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The lower awe, the one that keeps you doing His will, is the one the sages meant by "If there is no awe, there is no wisdom." It has a small form and a big form. The big form comes from thinking about His greatness: He fills all worlds; from earth to the sky is a walk of five hundred years; the worlds go up and up past measure. Even so this awe is called outer and lower, because it comes from the worlds, and the worlds are only the King's clothing, where He hides to give them life. It is the gate into doing the Torah.

The higher awe is shame, an inner awe. It comes from the G-dliness *inside* the worlds, and of it they said, "If there is no wisdom there is no awe." *Chochmah*, "wisdom," is read as *ko'ach mah*, "the power of What": "wisdom is found from nothing." "Who is wise? One who sees what is being born": who sees each thing being born from nothing into something, by the word of G-d and the breath of His mouth. Seeing that, heaven and earth and everything in them are nullified in His word, truly nothing, like the sun's rays inside the sun itself. And don't leave yourself out of it: your body and soul too are nothing inside His word. That is a different kind of seeing than the first. The first awe sees a huge King. The second sees that nothing is there but Him, and blushes. But you reach it only by first keeping the Torah through the lower awe.

Love also has two levels. The great love is "love in delights," a flame that rises by itself and comes from above as a gift to one who is whole in awe. The sages said a man goes looking for a woman; love is the man, and awe is the woman. Without awe first, you can't come to this love, because it belongs to a place where there is no cutting off.

The worldly love comes from understanding His greatness: He fills and surrounds all worlds, and everything before Him is as nothing, like one word inside the mind that has not yet said it. From that thinking, the love in the soul takes off its clothes. It stops dressing itself in any pleasure, physical or spiritual, and wants nothing in the world but Him, the source of every pleasure, next to whom all of them are nothing. "Whom do I have in heaven? And besides You I want nothing on earth." Even someone whose love is not dressed in any pleasure can set his soul on fire this way.

Sometimes love comes before awe, depending on how the knowing gives birth to them. That is how a sinner can return out of a love born in his heart when he remembers G-d. Awe is folded into it then, small and hidden. But that is a special case, a gift for the moment, like the story of Rabbi Elazar ben Durdaya. The set order of service, which depends on your choice, is awe first: keep the Torah with at least the small lower awe, light the soul with the Torah's light, and then the light of love will shine on it. (I left aside the note on the number value of "you shall love.")

**The movement.** From the outer awe of a great King, through keeping His will, to the inner awe of seeing everything born from nothing; and from love born of understanding to the love that burns by itself. What carries you across is the order: awe first, deeds first, then understanding, and then the gift.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "One who sees each thing being born from nothing into something." - Think it through: this cup is not simply here. This moment it is coming out of nothing by His word. So is my hand. So am I. - Bring it down to one real thing: one object in front of you. Look at it as it is being born. - Where the picture breaks: the sun's rays are still seen as rays. Here there is really nothing but Him. - Stop at the edge: when the self starts to go quiet, don't grab a feeling. Stay small. - Words to Him: "Everything is Your word. I am Your word too." - One act: keep one mitzvah today out of simple fear of the King, and leave the love to come when it comes.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched the higher awe when you feel a kind of shyness, as if you are standing somewhere too holy for you. The fake is a grand, dizzy feeling about the size of the universe that leaves you feeling big.

## Chapter 44 · The love you were born with

**The state.** Two loves hidden in every soul and inherited: "because You are my soul, I long for You," the longing for G-d as for your own life; and the love of a child who wants his father more than his own life. The state is bringing that hidden love, *ahavah mesuteret*, out into the open.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Each of the two loves, great love and worldly love, has countless levels. "Her husband is known in the gates": the Holy One is known to each person by the measure he makes in his heart. That is why love and awe are called "the hidden things," which belong to G-d. The Torah and mitzvot are "the revealed things," the same for all of us, while love and awe go by each person's knowing.

But there is one love that holds all the levels, and it is the same in every soul, inherited from our fathers. The Zohar reads "My soul, I long for You at night": a person loves G-d the way the soul loves the body and the body loves the soul. Because You, G-d, are my soul and my true life, I long for You. The way a person who is weak and worn out longs for his strength to come back, the way a person going to sleep wants his soul to come back when he wakes. So I long for the light of the Infinite, the true Life of life, to draw it into me by learning Torah when I wake at night.

And there is a bigger love than that, also hidden in every soul: "like a son who strives for his father and mother, whom he loves more than himself and his soul." We all have one Father. Who would dare to reach even a thousandth of Moses's love? Still, a tiny spark of his light shines in every generation. It is deeply hidden in the soul.

To bring that hidden love out into the open, in heart and mind, "is not wondrous and not far. It is very near, in your mouth and in your heart." Make it a habit on your tongue and in your voice to wake the aim of heart and mind, to sink your thought into the Life of life, because He is truly our Father and the source of our life, and to wake love for Him like a child's love for a father. Do it always, and habit becomes nature. If it seems to you that this is only imagination, pay no attention. It is true in itself, as a hidden love. The point of bringing it out is to bring it to deeds: learning and mitzvot done to give Him pleasure, like a son serving his father. "A good thought the Holy One joins to the deed." That makes wings. The pleasure is like the joy of a king whose son comes home from prison, or like His wish for a home below. The longing "my soul, I long for You" also comes out easily, through habit, when mouth and heart agree. And if you can't bring it out in your heart, you can still learn and act for His sake by picturing that love in your mind.

These inherited loves, with the fear folded in them, the fear of being cut off from the source of our life, count as "natural" only while they stay in the mind and the hidden depths of the heart. Then they lift your Torah to Yetzirah. When they come out openly in the heart, they are called "the wish of the heart," and they lift your Torah to Beriah, because bringing them out takes knowing, fixing the thought strongly on how He is truly our life and our Father. These two loves even hold something of the great love, and are higher than the love that comes from understanding. Still, work with your mind to reach the love from understanding too, to make the fire bigger, until "many waters cannot put out the love." A love like burning coals has an edge over these two loves when they don't burn, like gold over silver. And this is the whole of a person: to know the glory of G-d, each as much as he can carry.

**The movement.** From a love that is there but asleep, to a love awake in mind and heart and then in the hand. What carries you across is habit: saying it, with mouth and heart agreeing, again and again, until it becomes nature; and, when the heart won't open, picturing it in the mind and acting anyway.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Because You are my soul and my true life, I long for You." - Think it through: when I am tired, I want my strength back. When I sleep, I want to wake. He is the life under that life. - Bring it down to one real thing: the moment you wake tomorrow. That wanting to be alive is wanting Him. - Where the picture breaks: I can lose my strength; He is never gone. The longing is not for something absent but for something I don't feel. - Stop at the edge: if it feels like pretending, keep going. It is true even when hidden. - Words to Him: "You are my Father. I want You more than myself." - One act: learn one thing today as a child does something for his father.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a plain word to Him feels like calling home. The fake is a love for the feeling of love, kept for yourself and never turned into a deed.

## Chapter 45 · Mercy for the spark

**The state.** *Rachamim*, "mercy," turned toward G-d's own spark inside you, which has fallen into exile. The heart breaks for Him, not for yourself, and the breaking opens into great love.

**The chapter as consciousness.** There is another straight road to serving for His sake: the way of Jacob, whose trait is mercy. First wake, in your thought, great mercy before G-d for the spark of G-dliness that gives your soul its life. Think where it came from: the Life of life, who fills and surrounds all worlds, before whom everything is nothing. And think where it fell: dressed in "the skin of the snake," as far as anything can be from the light of the King's face, because this world is the thickest of the husks.

It is worse when you remember everything you have done, said and thought since the day you were born that was not good. "A King is held captive in the tresses": in the running of your mind. "Jacob is the rope of His inheritance," like a rope pulled from below that tugs at the top. That is the secret of the exile of the Presence. About this it says, "Let him return to G-d, and He will have mercy on him": wake great mercy for G-d's name that lives with us, "who dwells with them in the middle of their impurity."

This is a new state. You are not sorry for yourself. You see that your wrongs dragged something holy down, and you feel for it. The chapter reads the verse "Jacob kissed Rachel, raised his voice and wept." Rachel is the source of all souls. Jacob, with the mercy of the highest world, wakes mercy for her. He raises his voice up to the Father of mercy, and weeps, to draw mercy down on all souls and their source, to lift them from exile and join them to the Infinite in a "kiss": spirit touching spirit. "Let Him kiss me with the kisses of His mouth." Your speech joined to His word, which is the law you learn; your thought to His thought; your deed to His deed. Learning, speaking and thinking it, is a kiss. A mitzvah done, especially charity and kindness, is an embrace: "His right hand embraces me." Through this road a person can come to great love, open in the heart. "Jacob, who redeemed Abraham": mercy redeems love.

**The movement.** From a heaviness about your past, to mercy for the spark, to great love. What carries you across is thinking in detail about where the spark came from and where it now is, the remembering of your own deeds for His sake and not for your own shame, and then speech and deed as kiss and embrace.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "Great mercy for the spark of G-dliness that gives my soul its life." - Think it through: it came from the Life of all life. It sits in me, in this body, in my small days. - Bring it down to one real thing: one thing you did that you are not proud of. See the spark that was dragged into it. - Where the picture breaks: G-d is not weak or hurt. The mercy is for His light hidden in my life, and the hiding is real. - Stop at the edge: let the heart break for Him, not for my image of myself. - Words to Him: "Have mercy on Your own spark in me." - One act: give charity or do a kindness today, as an embrace; learn a line, as a kiss.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when sorrow for your past turns soft and warm toward Him and makes you want to act. The fake is self-pity: grieving over your own failure and calling it mercy for the spark.

## Chapter 46 · Love waking love

**The state.** Love that wakes in answer to love: *kamayim hapanim lapanim*, "as water reflects a face to a face." The state of seeing how much He loved you, until your own heart melts and wants to give everything back.

**The chapter as consciousness.** There is a straight road, the same for every soul and very, very near, to wake the love stuck and hidden in the heart until it burns openly like fire, to give yourself to G-d with all your heart, soul and might, especially during the Shema. It is this: "As water shows a face to a face, so a heart to a heart." The face you show the water is the face you see. So a person who truly loves another wakes love in the other's heart, especially when the other sees it.

That is how people are, even equals. Now picture a great king showing his great love to a lowly man, despised, filthy, lying on a garbage heap. The king comes down from his place of honor with all his ministers, lifts him up out of the garbage, brings him into his palace, room within room, where no servant or minister goes, and there is alone with him in true closeness, embraces him, kisses him, spirit touching spirit, with all his heart. How much more would love wake, doubled and redoubled, in the heart of that lowly man for the king, from the depth of his heart. Even if his heart were a heart of stone, it would melt and become water. His soul would pour out like water in longing for the king.

All of this, and far more without end, our G-d did for us. His greatness has no end. He fills and surrounds all worlds; there are countless worlds and halls, each with countless angels, and all of them are nothing before Him, like one word next to the soul that has not yet spoken it. They all ask, "Where is the place of His glory?" and answer, "The whole earth is full of His glory." He passed over the high and the low and chose Israel, and brought them out of Egypt, the lowest and filthiest place, not by an angel or a messenger, but "the Holy One in His glory, Himself, came down there." "I came down to rescue them." To bring them close in a true union: a kiss, mouth to mouth, which is speaking His word, the law; spirit touching spirit, which is understanding the Torah, His will and wisdom, all truly one; and an embrace, which is doing the mitzvot with all the limbs.

So we say, "who sanctified us with His commandments," like a man who betroths a woman to be one with him: "and they shall be one flesh." So, and infinitely more, is the union of the soul that learns and does mitzvot with the light of the Infinite. That is why Solomon wrote the Song of Songs as a bride and groom. "Holy" means set apart: the part of Him that is above the worlds and cannot be dressed in them. The soul joined to Him reaches that very holiness. "You shall be holy to Me, for I am holy." And "I am your G-d" as He is "the G-d of Abraham," because the fathers were His chariot, folded into His light.

This is so for every soul at the time of learning or a mitzvah. That is why the sages said to stand up for anyone doing a mitzvah, even a simple, ignorant person: because of G-d dwelling in his soul in that moment. His soul does not feel it; the body's thickness darkens its eyes. "I was a fool and did not know; I was like a beast with You; and I am always with You." Even though I am like a beast and don't feel this union, no awe first and then burning love like the tzaddikim, still I am always with You. The body does not block the soul's union with Him. "Even darkness is not dark to You." That is why Shabbat and Passover bind the simplest person as fully as a tzaddik: their holiness shines in him too. (I left aside a closing note on the plural "beasts.")

The picture breaks where the king is still a man and the lowly man is still a separate man. Here the King does not just bring you close. He makes you one with Him, and He was never far.

**The movement.** From a heart of stone that feels nothing, to a heart that melts in answer. What carries you across is putting the picture into the depth of heart and mind, in detail: who He is, how low you were, how far He came down, and what He does when you learn and keep His will.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "The Holy One, in His glory and Himself, came down there." - Think it through: countless worlds and angels, all nothing before Him. And He came down Himself, to the lowest place, for me. - Bring it down to one real thing: the Shema you will say next, or the blessing "who sanctified us." - Where the picture breaks: the king and the beggar stay two. He makes me one with Him, and was never far. - Stop at the edge: if you feel nothing, say it anyway: "I am always with You." - Words to Him: "You came all the way down. I give You everything." - One act: stand up inside, today, for someone doing a mitzvah, however simple he is.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when your heart answers His love, without being pushed, with a wish to hold nothing back. The fake is being moved by a story of love while nothing in you is actually offered back.

## Chapter 47 · Leaving Egypt this morning

**The state.** *Yetziat Mitzrayim*, "leaving Egypt," as a state renewed each day: the G-dly soul going out of the body's narrow prison into His oneness. And the knowledge that only your own will stands in the way.

**The chapter as consciousness.** "In every generation, and every day, a person must see himself as if he left Egypt today." This is the G-dly soul going out of the prison of the body, "the snake's skin," to be folded into His oneness, through Torah and mitzvot, and most of all through taking on His kingship in the Shema, when you draw His oneness onto yourself in words: "G-d is our G-d, G-d is One."

"Our G-d" is like "the G-d of Abraham," because Abraham was nullified and folded into His oneness. But Abraham earned it by his deeds, climbing in holiness step after step: "Abram journeyed, going on and on." For us it is an inheritance and a gift. He gave us His Torah and dressed His will and wisdom in it, which are one with His very Self. It is as though He gave us Himself. The Zohar reads "take for Me an offering" as "take Me."

That is why we say, "You gave us, in love," "by the light of Your face You gave us." So nothing keeps the soul from cleaving to Him except the will. If a person does not want to cleave to Him, he doesn't. But the moment he wants it, and takes on and draws His G-dliness over himself and says "G-d is our G-d, G-d is One," his soul is folded into His oneness by itself. "Spirit brings spirit and draws spirit." That is leaving Egypt. So the passage about leaving Egypt was set inside the Shema, though it is a separate mitzvah, because they are one thing. And it ends, "I am G-d, your G-d."

From inside, this is a quiet shock. The prison was never locked from outside. Abraham had to climb. You were handed the key. What you have to do is want it and say it.

**The movement.** From the narrow state of a soul shut inside the body's concerns, to the soul folded into His oneness. What carries you across is the will, the wanting, and the words of the Shema said as a real taking-on.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "It is as though He gave us Himself." - Think it through: the words I learn are His will and wisdom, one with Him. They are in my hands. - Bring it down to one real thing: this morning's Shema, or the next time you say "G-d is One." - Where the picture breaks: Egypt was one place, left once. This going out happens every day, from inside. - Stop at the edge: I can't feel the prison open. I only know that nothing blocks it but my will. - Words to Him: "I want You. You are my G-d. You are One." - One act: say the Shema today slowly, as a going out.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when the day's worries lose their grip for a moment after you say the Shema. The fake is waiting to be lifted, as if the gift had not already been given and the wanting were not yours to do.

## Chapter 48 · Around, and truly inside

**The state.** The state of knowing that what is called "surrounding," *sovev kol almin*, "surrounding all worlds," is not far above you but inside you, only not showing. Hebrew: *memale*, "filling," the small light that shows, and *sovev*, "surrounding," the endless light that is hidden.

**The chapter as consciousness.** When a thinking person considers the greatness of the Infinite, he sees it is as its name says: without end, no limit at all to the light and life that spreads from Him by His simple will, one with His Self. If the worlds came from that light step by step, cause to effect, with no contraction, this world could never have come into being as it is, with limits: a walk of five hundred years from earth to sky, the thickness of each sky. Even the World to Come and the upper Garden of Eden, even the souls of the great tzaddikim, and surely the angels, have limits. There is a limit to how much they grasp and to how much they enjoy, because no created being can take in pleasure without end without being wiped out and going back to its source.

The details of the contractions, the *tzimtzumim*, are not for here. In general they are a hiding of His light, so that only a tiny bit shines into the worlds, just enough that they can exist within limits. That bit is truly nothing next to light without limit. There is no ratio between them. One is a millionth of a million; a million millions are not even one compared to the endless. That tiny light, dressed in the worlds and giving them life, is called *filling*. The hidden, endless light that does not dress itself in the worlds openly is called *surrounding*.

Here the chapter stops us from a wrong picture. "Surrounding" does not mean above in space. Space does not apply to the spiritual. It means: not showing. A flow that shows itself in the worlds, that they grasp, is called "dressed" in them. A flow that is hidden, that they do not grasp, is called "surrounding." Take this earth. "The whole earth is full of His glory." "Do I not fill heaven and earth?" Yet only a tiny bit of life shows in it, enough for stone and plants. And all the light of the Infinite is called surrounding it, though it is truly inside it, because it only acts there hidden. A hidden world is higher than a revealed one.

The chapter gives a picture to bring it near. When you picture in your mind a thing you saw, its outside and inside and inside of the inside, your mind "surrounds" it. But only in imagination. His thoughts are not our thoughts. His knowing surrounds every creature from top to bottom, inside and inside of the inside, in reality, because that very knowing is the life of the whole globe and brings it into being from nothing. It became limited earth, with only the life of stone and plant, only through many great contractions. He knows everything by knowing Himself, not by a knowing outside Him as we know things; this no one can fully grasp. Since that knowing is endless, it is not called dressed in the earth, but surrounding, even though it holds all its thickness, in reality.

So from inside, the state is this: the thing in front of you is not empty of the endless. The endless is all through it. It just isn't showing. What you see is the small part that shows.

**The movement.** From picturing G-d as far above, "surrounding" like a sky, to knowing He is entirely here and only hidden. What carries you across is understanding in detail: that "surrounding" means not showing, and that His knowing of this thing is its very being.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "All the light of the Infinite is called surrounding it, though it is truly inside it." - Think it through: this stone shows only a tiny life. The endless light is in it completely, hidden. - Bring it down to one real thing: the ground you stand on. His knowing of it is its being, inside and inside of the inside. - Where the picture breaks: my mind holds a picture of a thing; His knowing holds the thing itself. - Stop at the edge: I can't grasp a knowing that is His own Self. Stay there. - Words to Him: "You are all here. Only I don't see." - One act: treat one plain thing today as a place where He is fully present.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when "higher" stops meaning "farther" and the plain world feels full. The fake is a vague sense of a cosmic presence out there, which puts Him at a distance again.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 48 (line 703): וכל אוראיןסוף ברוךהוא נקרא סובב עליה, אף שהוא בתוכה ממש*

## Chapter 49 · He set His light aside for you

**The state.** The state of seeing *tzimtzum*, "contraction," G-d's hiding of His endless light, as an act of love aimed at you, and answering it in kind: the readiness to set aside everything you have to cleave to Him. Hebrew: *ahavah dochakat habasar*, "love pushes the flesh," love makes the great one make himself small.

**The chapter as consciousness.** The ways His light is hidden on the way down to this physical world are countless, one kind inside another. In general, though, there are three great contractions for three general worlds. *Atzilut* is G-dliness itself. To create *Beriah*, where souls and high angels serve Him by grasping His wisdom, there first had to be a great contraction. The tiny light in Beriah is still endless compared to *Yetzirah*, so another contraction was needed to come down there, and another from Yetzirah to *Asiyah*. (The chapter says the details are explained elsewhere; I have kept only the outline.)

The purpose of all these contractions is to create the physical human body, to bend the other side, and to bring out the light that shines from darkness, when a person lifts his G-dly soul and his animal soul, with their garments and all the body's powers, to G-d alone. This is the goal of the whole chain of worlds.

Now the heart of the chapter: "As water shows a face to a face." The Holy One, as it were, set aside His great and endless light, hid it and concealed it in three kinds of contraction, all for love of the lowly human being, to raise him up to Him. "Love pushes the flesh": a great man, out of love, makes himself small for the one he loves. How much more, then, doubled without end, is it right for a person to set aside and leave everything he has, soul and body, and let it all go, to cleave to Him with longing and desire, with nothing holding him back, inside or outside: not body, not soul, not money, not wife and children.

This explains why the sages set two blessings before the Shema. They seem unrelated to it. But the heart of the Shema is "with all your heart, all your soul, all your might": to stand against everything that holds you back from loving G-d. "Your heart" means also the wife and children your heart is tied to by nature; "your soul" and "your might" mean life and livelihood. Let everything go for the love of G-d.

How does a physical person come to that? So the first blessing, "who forms light," describes at length the angels standing at the height of the world, all nullified to His light, saying in awe "Holy," meaning He is set apart from them and not dressed in them openly. "The whole earth is full of His glory," meaning His people. The angels say "Blessed is the glory of G-d from its place," because they don't know His place. Then the second blessing: "With a worldly love You loved us." He passed over all the holy hosts above and rested His Presence on us, to be called "our G-d." "Worldly love," *ahavat olam*, is the love that made Him contract His endless light into the limit called *olam*, "world," for the sake of His people, to bring them close to be folded into His oneness. "Great and extra compassion": more than the closeness of all the hosts above. "You chose us from every nation and tongue": that is the physical body, which looks like any other body. "And You brought us close to thank You and to make You one."

When a thinking person puts these things into the depth of heart and mind, then, as water shows a face, his soul will catch fire and dress itself in a willing spirit, to set aside everything he has and only cleave to Him in a kiss, spirit touching spirit. How is that done? "These words shall be on your heart, and you shall speak them." The kiss is mind joined to mind, which is studying the Torah; the mouth is where the breath goes out and shows itself, which is speaking words of Torah. And thought alone is not enough; you must say the words, to draw His light down into the body's life, which grows from stone and plant and animal, and lift it all to Him with the whole world, so that His glory fills this earth openly, turning darkness into light and bitter into sweet. That is the goal of a person's service: to draw the Infinite down. But first comes the lifting from below: giving Him your soul and your might.

**The movement.** From holding on to your life, your family, your money as what is yours, to a willing spirit that lets go of it all to cleave to Him. What carries you across is thinking, deep in heart and mind, about how He made Himself small for you, and then the Shema said and spoken, not only thought.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "He set aside His great and endless light, for love of the lowly human." - Think it through: the whole chain of worlds was a narrowing, step after step, so that I could exist and come close. - Bring it down to one real thing: the thing you hold tightest, your home, your children, your income. - Where the picture breaks: He did not lose anything by hiding. I let go of things to find that they were never in the way. - Stop at the edge: I can't picture light without end. I can only know it was hidden for me. - Words to Him: "You made room for me. I make room for You." - One act: say the Shema today with "all your might" meant as your money and your life.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when giving up something feels like an answer, not a loss. The fake is a grand vow to give up everything that leaves you holding everything the same as before.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 49 (line 714): הניח וסילק לצד אחד, דרך משל, את אורו הגדול הבלתי תכלית*

*The Tanya speaks here of the Jewish soul and its particular root; this reading applies it to every person.*

## Chapter 50 · Burning, and coming back

**The state.** A love higher than all the others: *ahavah kerishpei esh*, "love like flames of fire," the soul burning to leave the body and go up. And the state that must follow it: *ratzo veshov*, "running and returning," turning the burning back down into life here.

**The chapter as consciousness.** Every level of love so far is from the right side, the side of kindness. It is called "holy silver," from the word for longing: "you long and long for your father's house." But there is a love above all of them, as gold is above silver. It is love like flames of fire, from the higher strength of understanding.

It comes from thinking about the greatness of the Infinite, before whom all is truly nothing. The soul catches fire at the glory of His greatness and wants to gaze at the King's glory, like flames of fire rising upward, wanting to pull away from the wick and wood they hold on to. This is the element of divine fire in the G-dly soul growing strong. From it comes thirst: "My soul thirsts for You." Then "lovesick." Then the soul actually fading away: "My soul longs and even faints." From this love comes the root of the Levites, whose service was to raise a voice of song and thanks, in music and melody, running and returning, like the flash that comes and goes.

The chapter admits this cannot be explained well in writing. Every person with heart and understanding, who thinks deeply and ties his knowing to G-d, will find the good and the light stored up in his soul, each by his own measure. Some are stirred one way, some another. But only after awe of sin comes first: turning from bad completely, so that "your wrongs separate" do not stand between.

Now the turn that makes this a Tanya chapter. The order of service that comes from this strong love is only in "returning." The Book of Formation says: "If your heart runs, return to the One." When the soul's longing in the right side of the heart grows and burns and flames, until the soul is ready to pour itself out into the bosom of its Father, the Life of life, and leave its prison in the physical body to cleave to Him, then a person should bring this back to his heart. The sages said: "Against your will you live." You live in this body to give it life, in order to draw the higher life down from the Life of life, through the Torah of life, to make a home below for His oneness, openly. "To be one in One": the hidden oneness showing in the revealed world. That is why we say "Come, my beloved": come down. And that is how to understand "against your will you live": then what should his will be? That he live, with the help of the Life of life.

From inside, the running feels like the truest thing a soul can feel, and it wants to leave. The return is not a cooling. It is the same fire turned to a purpose: stay, and bring Him down here.

**The movement.** From a burning longing to escape the body and be absorbed, back to life in the body, with the fire now aimed at making a home for Him below. What carries you across is deep thinking about His greatness to light the fire, awe of sin first as the ground, and then remembering why you are alive.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "If your heart runs, return to the One." - Think it through: He is everything; all is nothing before Him. Let the wanting rise toward Him. - Bring it down to one real thing: when the longing is strongest, remember the task in front of you today. - Where the picture breaks: a flame leaves the wick and goes out. This soul must stay with the wick. - Stop at the edge: don't chase the burning. It comes to each by his measure. - Words to Him: "I want to be with You. You want to be here. Here I am." - One act: take the wish to be close and turn it into one act of Torah or kindness today.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a moment of great longing comes back to earth as a quiet resolve to do His will here. The fake is spiritual thrill-seeking: loving the feeling of rising and finding ordinary life a burden.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 50 (line 730): ואם רץ לבך – שוב לאחד*

## Chapter 51 · As there, so here

**The state.** Knowing that He is exactly as present in the lowest place as in the highest, and that what differs from place to place is only how much of His life shows. Hebrew: *hashra'at haShechinah*, "the resting of the Presence," and *gilui*, "showing."

**The chapter as consciousness.** Chapter 35 quoted a child in the Zohar who said the light above a person's head needs oil. To explain that, the chapter first asks: what does it mean that the Presence rested in the Holy of Holies, or anywhere at all? "The whole earth is full of His glory." "There is no place empty of Him."

The answer comes from a verse: "From my flesh I see G-d." Look at your own soul. It fills your whole body from head to foot, and yet its main home is the brain. From the brain it spreads to each limb, and each limb takes the power that fits it: the eye to see, the ear to hear, the mouth to speak, the feet to walk. And in the brain you feel everything that happens to every limb.

The difference is not in the soul itself. The soul is not cut into pieces shaped like the body, one piece in the head and one in the foot. That would give it a physical shape. It is one simple spiritual thing, above place and size. You cannot say it is more in the head than in the feet. It holds, folded in itself, all the powers that come out to give the body life. What is "mainly in the brain" is the *showing* of those powers. The brain receives first and holds the whole flow, and from there it shines out to the limbs like sunlight into inner rooms. Even the heart gets from the brain, which is why the mind rules the heart by nature.

So too, as a picture, the Infinite fills all worlds to give them life, and there are endless worlds and creatures in each. His very Self is the same above and below, as the soul is. He is "the hidden of all hidden": even in the highest hidden worlds He is hidden inside them, just as He is hidden in the lowest, because no thought grasps Him even there. So as He is found there, He is truly found here.

The difference between higher and lower worlds is only in the flow of life He draws out and shows, which is why that flow is called "light." The higher worlds take it in a little more openly. The lower ones, even the spiritual ones, take it through many garments. The garments grew so strong that He made this physical world with them. Here no light or life is seen at all, only physical things, which look dead. But inside them is light and life bringing them into being from nothing all the time, or they would go back to nothing. That light is from the Infinite, dressed in many garments. (I compressed the Kabbalah of which level lives inside which.)

From inside: the room around you, the chair, the stone outside, look dead. That look is the garment. The One is not less here. What is less is the showing.

**The movement.** From feeling that holy places are where G-d is and ordinary places are where He isn't, to knowing He is equally everywhere, and holiness is where His life shows. What carries you across is the picture of your own soul, which is whole in your foot and shows itself in your mind, thought through until it lands.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "As He is found there, so He is truly found here." - Think it through: my soul is whole in my hand, not a piece of it. It just shows more in my mind. - Bring it down to one real thing: something that looks dead to you, a wall, a stone, a street. - Where the picture breaks: my soul lives in my body; He does not live in the world as a thing in a thing. He is its being. - Stop at the edge: He is hidden even in the highest worlds. There is no place where I would finally see Him with my mind. - Words to Him: "You are here as much as anywhere." - One act: do one mitzvah today in a place that feels far from holy.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when a plain place stops feeling empty. The fake is thinking that everything is "the same" so nothing needs to be made holy; the chapter keeps the difference in showing, and the next chapters say how it is made.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 51 (line 737): כמו שמצוי שם – כך נמצא בתחתונים ממש*

## Chapter 52 · The garment that lets you come near

**The state.** The state of meeting the *Shechinah*, "the Presence," the first showing of the Infinite, through the one garment that can hold it without being wiped out: His will and wisdom in the Torah and its mitzvot. It is being close to what would otherwise erase you.

**The chapter as consciousness.** In the soul the whole flow of life shows in the brain, and the limbs get only a light that shines to them from there. So too, as a picture, the whole flow of life to the worlds is dressed and held in His will and wisdom and understanding and knowing, called "the brains," and these are what is dressed in the Torah and its mitzvot. What shows of that flow is the source each world draws its own life from, and from it only a light spreads, like sunlight from the sun.

That source has many names: "the revealed world," "the queen," "the lower mother," and *Shechinah*, from "I will dwell among them," because it is the first showing of the Infinite's light, and from it each thing gets its own light and life, and the Presence dwells in them. It is called "mother of the children" and "the gathering of Israel," because all souls come from it. Everything in all the worlds is only light spreading from it, as light spreads from the sun.

But the Presence itself, the source, the worlds cannot bear to have dwell in them without a garment that hides it. Without that they would be nullified entirely in their source, like sunlight inside the sun, where you see no light, only the sun. So what garment can hide it and hold it, and not be nullified in its light? His will and wisdom dressed in the Torah and its mitzvot, which are "revealed to us and to our children." The Torah comes from the highest wisdom, above the revealed world; He is wise, but not with a knowable wisdom. He and His wisdom are one. That wisdom came down step after step through the worlds until it was dressed in physical things, the 613 mitzvot.

As it came down world by world, the Presence came down with it and dressed in it in each world. That is the Holy of Holies of each world. In Beriah it made the souls and angels there, and from there comes the Talmud, the reasons of the laws. In Yetzirah it made the spirits and angels there, and from there comes the Mishnah, the laws decided, whose reasons are hidden inside them. The laws themselves come from His traits: kindness to permit, so that a thing can rise to G-d; strictness to forbid. And so it came down into the Holy of Holies of Asiyah. From the Presence dressed in the Holy of Holies of each world, light and life spread to that world and all in it, since everything was made by the ten sayings of creation, which are "the word of G-d," called *Shechinah*. (I compressed the long Kabbalah of the worlds into these lines.)

From inside, this is a strange intimacy. The closest a person can come to the One, without vanishing, is through a law, a page, a deed. The mitzvah is not a wall between you and Him. It is the one garment His Presence can wear so that you can stand near.

**The movement.** From wanting G-d with no garment, which would erase you or leave you with nothing but a feeling, to coming close through His will and wisdom in Torah and mitzvot. What carries you across is understanding why the garment is needed, and then learning and doing as a way of standing in the Holy of Holies.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "What garment can hide it and not be nullified in its light? His will and His wisdom." - Think it through: His Presence would erase me. His Torah lets me hold it. - Bring it down to one real thing: one law you know, one thing permitted or forbidden. His wisdom is in it. - Where the picture breaks: a garment covers a person; this garment is one with Him. It hides and reveals at once. - Stop at the edge: the wisdom above the revealed world I cannot know. I can hold its garment. - Words to Him: "Let me come near You through what You gave." - One act: learn one law today as an entering into His Presence.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when learning a dry law feels close, like standing somewhere holy. The fake is wanting the "real" G-d without the Torah's garment and calling it depth.

## Chapter 53 · The wick and the flame

**The state.** The Presence resting on a person, pictured as a candle: the flame of the *Shechinah* held to the wick, which is the animal soul. The wick burns and turns into fire. For the tzaddik the animal soul itself turns; for the *beinoni*, "the in-between person," its garments of thought, speech and deed turn.

**The chapter as consciousness.** When the First Temple stood, with the Ark and the Tablets in the Holy of Holies, the Presence rested there, dressed in the Ten Commandments, with a showing greater than in the Holy of Holies of the upper worlds. The Ten Commandments hold the whole Torah, which comes from the highest wisdom. To engrave them in physical stone, it did not come down step by step through the worlds, the way nature works. "The Tablets were the work of G-d, and the writing was G-d's writing." It was above nature. In the Second Temple, with no Ark and no Tablets, the sages said the Presence did not rest; that is, not in that way. It rested there by the ordinary chain, through the worlds, and so no one could enter except the high priest on Yom Kippur.

"Since the Temple was destroyed, the Holy One has in His world only the four cubits of law." "Even one who sits and learns Torah, the Presence is with him." With him by that same chain. And most of the mitzvot are deeds, and even those of speech need the lips; thought alone does not count, and moving your lips is a deed. The 613 mitzvot with the seven of the sages equal the number of *keter*, "crown": His will dressed in His wisdom.

Now the child's words from the Zohar are clear. "The high light burning over his head," which is the Presence, "needs oil": it must be dressed in wisdom, called "the holy anointing oil." "And these are good deeds": the 613 mitzvot that flow from His wisdom, to hold the light of the Presence on a wick. The wick is the animal soul in the body. As in a physical candle the light shines by the wick burning and turning into fire, so the light of the Presence rests on the G-dly soul by the burning away of the animal soul and its turning from darkness to light and bitter to sweet, in the tzaddikim. Or at least, in the beinonim, by the burning away of its garments, thought, speech and deed, and their turning from the darkness of the husks to the light of the Infinite that is dressed in the thought, speech and deed of the mitzvot.

Turning the animal soul from darkness to light is a lifting from below, which draws the light of the Presence, the showing of the Infinite, onto the G-dly soul in the mind. "For G-d your G-d is a consuming fire."

From inside, this is the whole book in one picture. You are not asked to be a flame floating in the air. You are asked to be a candle. Your plain life, your wanting and your habits, is the wick. It is not thrown away. It is burned into light. For most of us what burns is not the wanting itself but its clothing: the thought I choose, the word I say, the thing I do. Each one given to His will is a bit of wick turning into fire, and the flame stays on.

**The movement.** From a life where the body's soul is a dark weight, to a life where it is the very fuel of the light. What carries you across is the good deeds, the mitzvot, which are the oil, and the daily giving of your thought, speech and deed to them.

**Entering it.** - Hold one line: "The light shines by the burning of the wick, which turns into fire." - Think it through: my plain soul, my habits and wants, are the wick. Without them there is nothing for the flame to hold. - Bring it down to one real thing: one word you will say today, one thing you will do. That is wick. - Where the picture breaks: a wick is used up and gone. What burns here becomes light and is not lost. - Stop at the edge: I don't see the Presence resting on me. The candle doesn't see its flame either. - Words to Him: "Take my thought, my word, my deed. Let them burn for You." - One act: light a candle, if it is the time for one, or do one mitzvah, and think: this is the wick.

**Signs and the fake.** You have touched it when effort against your own grain feels like light, not loss. The fake is trying to get rid of the wick, despising the body and its life, when the wick is where the flame rests.

*Source: Tanya, Likkutei Amarim ch. 53 (line 762): כמו שבנר הגשמי, האור מאיר על ידי כליון ושריפת הפתילה הנהפכת לאש*
