# Brief: how Chassidus THINKS about the unity of G-d — a framework an AI can reason with

## Why The owner: "It looks like it's just imitating unity awareness in whatever position. It doesn't feel like it can actually think about unity. How do we get it to think about the unity of G-d according to Chassidic teaching, with a principled framework to understand and then communicate? That is the central question of the whole study." The guide has learned the CONCLUSIONS ("He gives you being", "nothing besides Him") and pastes them on. A maamar instead REASONS: it raises a real question, separates levels, builds a mashal and says where it breaks, climbs to where the contradiction resolves, and draws it down into avodah. We want that process written down explicitly enough that an AI can follow it step by step, check itself against it, and apply it to any question for any person.

## What to produce (per strand) 1. TERMS: the basic concepts as the Rebbeim use them, each with: id, term (English + Hebrew), a plain one-sentence definition, its RELATIONS to other terms (gives being to, clothes itself in, conceals, is above, is nullified in, is the same as from His side ...), common misreadings, and verified sources. 2. MOVES: the reasoning moves the texts actually make, each with: id, name, what it does, when it is used, the steps, a worked example taken from the text (with the verified line), and what goes wrong when it is done badly. 3. GUARDRAILS: what the reasoning must never conclude (e.g. the world IS G-d / pantheism; tzimtzum taken literally; picturing anything physically; the self erased rather than revealed), each with the source that rules it out. 4. WORKED EXAMPLES: 6-8 real questions a person today might ask (doubt, pain, prayer, purpose, the self, evil, suffering, why the world looks separate ...). For each: the question -> the step-by-step reasoning the way this text would do it (naming each move and term) -> the sources at each step -> a plain 120-180 word answer a guide could give, and how it would shift for (a) a beginner, (b) someone advanced, (c) a doubter. 5. A one-page SUMMARY in plain English: what is distinctive about how this text/author reasons.
