**001. The Chassidus Unity Index: Chassidus is one point.** Shows that all of Chassidus is one point, as the Rebbe said of it in 1965: the Essence of G-d, present in everything, so that "there is nothing besides Him" holds even in the lowest place. The index tests that claim against the whole library. It names the 12 recurring forms ("faces") the one idea takes, the 14 moves by which the sources tie any subject back to it, and files 197 subjects and 220 works under them, with 2,275 Hebrew citations checked at their lines. It marks where a tie is the text's own (*stated*) and where it is ours (*reading*), keeps nine disagreements between the Rebbeim open, and says where the claim is thin. ([Quote check](verification/catalogue.md#result-001))

&emsp;[Chassidus Unity Index](manuscripts/Chassidus-Unity-Index-September-29-2026/manuscript.md)

The claim is the Lubavitcher Rebbe's. In Inyanah Shel Toras HaChassidus (1965) he says that Chassidus is "one point, not composed of distinct details": the Essence of God, drawn down and present in everything. Put as unity, that point reads "there is nothing besides Him", true even in the lowest earth, and that is His dwelling below. The Mitteler Rebbe says the same of his father's whole teaching, about 1821.

&emsp;[The spine of the Chassidus Unity Index](manuscripts/The-spine-of-the-Chassidus-Unity-Index-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

The index's claim: Chassidus is not 220 books but one idea in 220 ways. This file sets out what that idea is, according to the Rebbeim themselves, the twelve faces it takes across the corpus, and the grammar by which any subject is shown to be one of those faces.

&emsp;[Subjects of Chassidus — for the Unity Index](manuscripts/Subjects-of-Chassidus-for-the-Unity-Index-September-29-2026/manuscript.md)

Each subject below is to be traced back to one idea: the unity of God. This file is the subject list only. The passages come in the next stage. 197 subjects in 16 clusters. Tie to the unity: stated 129 · reading 60 · thin 8. How the list was made.

&emsp;[Eight sources on the unity of God: what they say together](manuscripts/Eight-sources-on-the-unity-of-God-what-they-say-together-September-29-2026/manuscript.md)

Each book says the one idea: there is nothing but God. Each also sees something the others do not. The Tanya says it from the person's side. The soul is a part of Him, the struggle is where He is hidden, and the deed is where He is held. Its test is a life.

**002. 197 subjects in 16 clusters, each shown as a face of the one idea.** Takes 197 subjects of Chassidus, from tzimtzum and the sefiros to food, money, Shabbos and death, and shows for each one how the sources themselves make it a way of saying the one idea. &emsp;[K01 · God: Unity and Essence](manuscripts/K01-God-Unity-and-Essence-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These thirteen subjects are the one idea itself, seen from thirteen sides. No subject here needs to be connected to the unity from outside, because each is a way the sources say it. What separates them is which side they say it from: the name of the unity (S001-S002), its bearer (S003-S004), its exclusivity (S005), its two directions (S006-S007), its creative power (S008-S009, S012), its knowledge and care (S010-S011), and its purpose (S013).

&emsp;[K02 · Tzimtzum, Light and the Chain of Worlds](manuscripts/K02-Tzimtzum-Light-and-the-Chain-of-Worlds-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

The claim of this cluster. Tzimtzum, light and the chain of worlds are the part of Chassidus where the one idea is most exposed to being misread as its opposite. They seem to be a map of God's distance from the world: a withdrawal, a void, a ladder of worlds, screens.

&emsp;[K03 · Sefiros, Names and the Supernal Order](manuscripts/K03-Sefiros-Names-and-the-Supernal-Order-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These twelve subjects are the supernal order: the sefiros, their parts, their names and the will beneath them. A system of emanations looks like the opposite of a single point, since it has ten measures, partzufim, unions and names.

&emsp;[K04 · Holiness, Kelipah and the Sparks](manuscripts/K04-Holiness-Kelipah-and-the-Sparks-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

The moral cosmos of Chassidus (holiness, the husks, the permitted middle, the sparks) looks at first like a dualism: two sides at war. Read against the spine, every one of these subjects is defined by its relation to the one idea, and the sources say so in so many words. Holiness is what is nullified to Him (Tanya 6:5).

&emsp;[K05 · The Soul](manuscripts/K05-The-Soul-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These twelve subjects are the soul. Here the one idea turns inward: the Essence that is present in everything is present in a person in a special way, as 'a part of God above, literally', breathed from His inwardness (Tanya 2), and at its innermost point, the yechidah, 'always united with the Essence' and giving 'no room to anything besides …

&emsp;[K06 · Faculties of the Soul](manuscripts/K06-Faculties-of-the-Soul-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

The faculties of the soul look like a psychology: ten powers, a mind and a heart, a will, a delight, three garments. Read against the spine, they turn out to be the Chabad instrument for thinking the unity at all.

&emsp;[K07 · Torah, Letters and the Study of Chassidus](manuscripts/K07-Torah-Letters-and-the-Study-of-Chassidus-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These thirteen subjects are the Torah and the study of it. Here the one idea turns from things to words. If nothing exists besides Him, then what He says is not separate from Him.

&emsp;[K08 · Mitzvos](manuscripts/K08-Mitzvos-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

The mitzvot look like a legal code: 613 commands, each with its own measure, time and object. Read against the spine, the sources treat each mitzvah as a single act in which the unity becomes actual (F10), and the cluster shows it from three sides. First, the mitzvah as such is a bond.

&emsp;[K09 · Prayer and Contemplation](manuscripts/K09-Prayer-and-Contemplation-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These eleven subjects are the service of the heart. Here the one idea stops being described and is enacted. If there is nothing besides Him, prayer cannot be one self petitioning another; the sources rebuild it as the essence of the soul joining the Essence of Ein Sof ('to Him and not to His attributes', Hayom Yom 11 Tishrei).

&emsp;[K10 · Love, Awe, Cleaving and Bitul](manuscripts/K10-Love-Awe-Cleaving-and-Bitul-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These thirteen subjects are the inner life of the service: love, awe, the yoke, cleaving, the rhythm of running and returning, fervor, bitul and its opposite, humility, self-sacrifice and truth. In this cluster the one idea shows as the person's answer to it.

&emsp;[K11 · Teshuvah and Refining Character](manuscripts/K11-Teshuvah-and-Refining-Character-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These thirteen subjects are the moral life seen from the one idea. If there is nothing besides Him, then sin cannot be a private debt between two parties. Tanya defines it as separation from His unity, like idolatry, because it opposes 'the will that gives life to all' (Tanya 24; IT 5).

&emsp;[K12 · Inner Life: Joy, Suffering, Faith and Death](manuscripts/K12-Inner-Life-Joy-Suffering-Faith-and-Death-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These twelve subjects are the one idea as it is lived from inside: felt (joy, sadness, bitterness), suffered (suffering, trials), held (faith, trust, simplicity), lost and regained (doubt, despair and hope), and met at the edge (death, reward and punishment).

&emsp;[K13 · Time: Shabbos, Festivals and Months](manuscripts/K13-Time-Shabbos-Festivals-and-Months-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These thirteen subjects are time itself seen from the one idea. Shaar HaYichud 7 puts time on the created side: a 'world' is space and time, both brought from nothing at every moment by His Malchus, both nullified before the Essence, and yet 'though above space and time He is found below in space and time' (SH 7:4-9).

&emsp;[K14 · The Tzaddik and the Community](manuscripts/K14-The-Tzaddik-and-the-Community-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These eleven subjects are the one idea as a community. They start with one person in whom the yesh has become ayin (the tzaddik), run through the lines that bind souls to him and to Moshe, and reach the body of Israel as one stature, the couple, the child, the peace between opposites, the table of friends, and the teaching …

&emsp;[K15 · Exile, Redemption, the Nations and the Land](manuscripts/K15-Exile-Redemption-the-Nations-and-the-Land-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These twelve subjects are the one idea read along history and geography. The spine's F07 says the dwelling below is made 'by our deeds throughout the exile' (Tanya 37:1). This cluster is that clause opened out. Exile is the soul's and the Shechinah's descent into real concealment.

&emsp;[K16 · The Physical: Body, Food, Money and Nature](manuscripts/K16-The-Physical-Body-Food-Money-and-Nature-September-28-2026/manuscript.md)

These twelve subjects are the one idea at its lowest point, where the spine says it shows most fully (Inyanah 19:4; Tanya 36). Each is read the same way. First, the thing exists and lives now by the word of God within it: food, stone, body (F01; SH 1:4-5, 2:5; Keter Shem Tov 1:159); livelihood comes down as creation from …

In Chassidus, to know that there is nothing but Him is already to meditate on it: knowing (da'at) means holding a truth until it is real, so each rung of the unity is known only by a matching depth of contemplation and of self-giving (bitul), and each one wakes a deeper layer of the soul, until at the bottom of the self, where the mind stops, the heart's simple will is already one with Him; and from there it comes back down, into the body and the day.

**006. The Tanya, line by line.** Reads every line of the Tanya (1,639 lines with text) and asks each one how it expresses the unity of G-d. Finds that the Tanya is not five books on five subjects but one idea said from every side: more than half the lines say it outright, and more than eight in ten say it or build toward it. Lines with no tie are marked "none". ([Quote check](verification/catalogue.md#result-006))

&emsp;[The Tanya, line by line: one idea](manuscripts/The-Tanya-line-by-line-one-idea-September-29-2026/manuscript.md)

The Tanya is not five books on five subjects. **022. The Ramak: one Cause, ten garments, and a person who walks like his Maker.** Reads the Ramak, the great organizer of Kabbalah before the Arizal. One simple Cause beyond change; all the change we see belongs to His ten garments, the sefiros; and a person is asked to walk the way his Maker acts. ([Quote check](verification/catalogue.md#result-022))

&emsp;[The Ramak: one Cause, ten garments, and a person who walks like his Maker](manuscripts/The-Ramak-one-Cause-ten-garments-and-a-person-who-walks-like-his-Maker-September-30-2026/manuscript.md)

The Ramak is the great systematizer of Kabbalah before the Arizal, and the Chabad masters quote him by name. His unity has three parts. One simple Cause, beyond change. The Infinite is "the simple One, cause of all causes and reason of all reasons, removed from change and from attributes" (pardes-rimmonim line 353). He is one "not as one that is counted" (ohr-neerav line 432). All the changes we see, judgment and mercy, anger and favor, belong to His ten measures, the sefirot, and not to Him.

&emsp;[Derishot BeInyanei HaMalachim (Inquiries on the Angels): the whole book](manuscripts/Derishot-BeInyanei-HaMalachim-Inquiries-on-the-Angels-the-whole-book-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the Ramak's treatise on angels, in seven "inquiries": how many ranks there are, what they are, why they exist, their order, how a person receives from them, how a person "rides" them, and how a person can bind them to his will. Most of it is angelology: ranks, names, thrones, the Merkavah (the chariot vision).

&emsp;[Elima Rabbati: an OCR skim](manuscripts/Elima-Rabbati-an-OCR-skim-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Elima Rabbati is the Ramak's second great system after the Pardes. It is built as four "springs" (maayanot), each with "palm trees" (tamarim) of chapters: Ein Kol HaAretz (His existence, unity and providence), Ein HaBedolach (the Crown and emanation), Ein Ro'i, and Ein Shemesh (the chain of the sefirot).

&emsp;[Ohr Ne'erav (The Pleasant Light): the whole book](manuscripts/Ohr-Neerav-The-Pleasant-Light-the-whole-book-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Ohr Ne'erav is the Ramak's short door into the inner Torah. The Pardes is the house. This book stands at the gate and talks to three kinds of people who stay outside: those who say there is nothing inside, those who say it is too high for them, and those who are afraid of making a mistake.

&emsp;[Or Yakar: on prophecy, and on Bereshit 1:1](manuscripts/Or-Yakar-on-prophecy-and-on-Bereshit-1-1-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Or Yakar (Precious Light) is the Ramak's huge commentary on the Zohar. Only two short portions were found in open typed form: a section on prophecy and a section on the first verse of Genesis. Both were read in full. The texts are typed copies from grimoar.cz, not checked against a printed edition.

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 1-428: why there are sefirot at all](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-1-428-why-there-are-sefirot-at-all-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Pardes Rimmonim ("The Orchard of Pomegranates") is the Ramak's great system of Kabbalah, finished in 1548 when he was about 27. It has 32 gates. This range holds the front of the book and its first two gates. Gate 1 argues that the sefirot (the ten "measures" through which God acts) are exactly ten.

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 429-581: the Infinite, the crown, and the light inside the vessels](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-429-581-the-Infinite-the-crown-and-the-light-inside-the-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

These two gates are the heart of the Ramak's teaching on the unity of God. Gate 3 separates the Infinite (Ein Sof) from the first of the sefirot, the Crown (Keter), and says what sets the Infinite apart. Gate 4 asks the question that decides everything else: are the sefirot God Himself, or are they tools He uses?

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 582-753: the order of the sefirot, and a world renewed every moment](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-582-753-the-order-of-the-sefirot-and-a-world-renewed-every-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Three technical gates: in what order the sefirot came out (Gate 5), how to picture them standing (Gate 6), and the "channels" by which one passes light to another (Gate 7). Most of the pages settle disputes among earlier Kabbalists.

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 754-982: judgment as a hidden face, and the awakening from below](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-754-982-judgment-as-a-hidden-face-and-the-awakening-from-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Gate 8, "Essence and Conduct" (Mahut veHanhagah), is the longest gate before the lexicon, and for the guide it may be the most useful. It asks what the sefirot are like as we meet them: which is mercy, which judgment, where judgment comes from, and how the upper world is moved.

Four gates. Gate 9 asks who makes peace between Chesed (giving) and Gevurah (holding back), and answers that the whole one is the one who holds both. Gate 10 is about the colors of the sefirot: they are not real colors, yet a color seen by the eye or pictured in the mind can wake the soul.

Four gates that turn the Ramak's map toward time and toward the world we live in. Gate 13 counts the fifty gates of Binah (understanding) through the fifty times the Torah says "I brought you out of Egypt", and from there reads time itself: hours, days, weeks, years, each a channel from the one above; Egypt as a furnace for …

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 1456-1630: the moon made small, and the Names as handles](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-1456-1630-the-moon-made-small-and-the-Names-as-handles-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Four gates. Gate 17 reads the Zohar on the angel who struck Jacob's thigh, and uses it to say something the guide needs: the shells never enter the divine world; their "entry" is only an accusation that makes mercy turn its face.

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 1631-1801: the detailed Names, and a person as a chariot](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-1631-1801-the-detailed-Names-and-a-person-as-a-chariot-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Two gates. Gate 21 is the densest in the book: the long "particular Names" of God (the 72-triplet Name from Exodus 14, the 42-letter Name from Genesis 1, the 22-letter Name from the priestly blessing, the 12 diagonal boundaries, the 54 and 72 permutations). Most of it is tables.

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 1802-2380: the lexicon of names, alef to kaf](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-1802-2380-the-lexicon-of-names-alef-to-kaf-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Gate 23 is a dictionary: every word the Zohar and the Tikkunim use for a sefirah, in the order of the alef-bet, with what it means and where. It is the longest gate and the one later kabbalists used most. Much of it is technical ("which sefirah is 'arm', which is 'thigh'").

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 2381-2962: the lexicon of names, kaf to tav](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-2381-2962-the-lexicon-of-names-kaf-to-tav-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the second half of Gate 23, the dictionary of God's names, from kaf to tav. Most entries are short and technical. But in the second half the Ramak keeps stopping to teach, and some of his clearest lines on the unity and on the inner life sit here.

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 2963-3106: palaces, angels, and the shells](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-2963-3106-palaces-angels-and-the-shells-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

These three gates move from God's own levels down to the worlds below them. Gate 24 describes the seven palaces of holiness, with their angels and gates. Gate 25 asks where evil comes from and why it exists. Gate 26 describes the seven palaces of the "other side".

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 3107-3270: letters, vowels, and cantillation](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-3107-3270-letters-vowels-and-cantillation-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

These three gates are about the stuff that holy speech is made of. Gate 27 covers the twenty-two letters, Gate 28 the vowel points, and Gate 29 the cantillation marks. Most of the letter-by-letter and vowel-by-vowel pages are technical. They say which letter hints at which sefirah, and what Sefer HaTemunah and Brit Menuchah say about each shape.

&emsp;[Pardes Rimmonim, lines 3271-3445: combination, the soul, and intention](manuscripts/Pardes-Rimmonim-lines-3271-3445-combination-the-soul-and-intention-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

These are the last three gates of the Pardes. Gate 30 lays out the tables of letter-combination, substitution and gematria. It is technical, and its one practical passage, name-permutation in seclusion, is not for the guide. The Ramak himself hedges it (line 3277).

&emsp;[Sefer Gerushin (The Book of Wanderings): the whole book](manuscripts/Sefer-Gerushin-The-Book-of-Wanderings-the-whole-book-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

&emsp;[The Ramak on Sefer Yetzirah: the whole commentary](manuscripts/The-Ramak-on-Sefer-Yetzirah-the-whole-commentary-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Sefer Yetzirah (the Book of Formation) is the oldest book of Jewish mysticism: 32 paths of wisdom, ten sefirot and twenty-two letters by which God made the world. The Ramak wrote a commentary on it as "gates" 14 to 19 of a larger work. Most of it explains the sefirot and the letters.

&emsp;[Gevurot Hashem, lines 444-982: the bush, the Name, the plagues, the sea, the three bonds](manuscripts/Gevurot-Hashem-lines-444-982-the-bush-the-Name-the-plagues-the-sea-the-three-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This range carries the Exodus story from Moshe at the well of Midian to the splitting of the sea, then turns to the mitzvot that came out of it (tefillin, the first-born, the festivals). It is the richest part of the book for the unity of G-d.

&emsp;[Gevurot Hashem, lines 983-1863: the Song, the Haggadah, and the return of all things to their Cause](manuscripts/Gevurot-Hashem-lines-983-1863-the-Song-the-Haggadah-and-the-return-of-all-things-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

The last part of the book. Chapter 47 reads the Song at the Sea. Chapters 48-50 are the laws of the seder night. From chapter 51 the Maharal reads the Haggadah line by line, then Hallel. Chapters 66-72 step back and ask what the whole Exodus was for.

&emsp;[Gur Aryeh on Bamidbar, lines 1-440: The One That Is Not a Number, the Torah as the Middle That Joins, Every Act Needs a Doer Below, and No Quarrel Beyond the Line](manuscripts/Gur-Aryeh-on-Bamidbar-lines-1-440-The-One-That-Is-Not-a-Number-the-Torah-as-the-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the first half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Bamidbar: the census and the camps, the levites, the nazir, the priestly blessing, the gifts of the princes, the menorah, the cloud, the complainers, the spies, the libations and challah, tzitzit, and Korach. Much is law, and the sotah and other passages are left out under D28.

&emsp;[Gur Aryeh on Bamidbar, lines 441-804: Oneness Is Kingship, Faith Is Joy, Look Up and Bind the Heart, and Cleave to the One Who Renews the Light](manuscripts/Gur-Aryeh-on-Bamidbar-lines-441-804-Oneness-Is-Kingship-Faith-Is-Joy-Look-Up-and-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the second half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Bamidbar: the red cow, the death of Miriam, the waters of strife, the death of Aharon, the copper snake, Sichon and Og, Balak and Bilam, Pinchas, the new census, the daughters of Tzelofchad, the daily and holiday offerings, vows, the war on Midian, the journeys, and the cities of …

&emsp;[Gur Aryeh on Bereishit, lines 1-707: One Day With No Twoness, Words That Name His Acts, the Screen Lifted, and Man Who Makes All One](manuscripts/Gur-Aryeh-on-Bereishit-lines-1-707-One-Day-With-No-Twoness-Words-That-Name-His-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Gur Aryeh is the Maharal's commentary on Rashi. Most of it is close work on words: why Rashi said this, why the verse wrote that. Many lines are grammar or law. But every few pages the Maharal stops and opens a large idea in a few lines.

&emsp;[Gur Aryeh on Bereishit, lines 708-1416: Only One in the Heart, the Place of the World, Being Is Good, and the Father Who Did Not Die](manuscripts/Gur-Aryeh-on-Bereishit-lines-708-1416-Only-One-in-the-Heart-the-Place-of-the-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the second half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Bereishit: Yitzchak and Rivka, Yaakov and Esav, the ladder, Lavan, the return, Yosef and his brothers, the descent to Egypt, and Yaakov's blessings and death. As in the first half, most lines are close reading of Rashi.

&emsp;[Gur Aryeh on Devarim, lines 1-294: He Is All and Nothing Is Outside Him, the Voice That Does Not Stop, One Now and One in the End, and Love That Makes Two Into One](manuscripts/Gur-Aryeh-on-Devarim-lines-1-294-He-Is-All-and-Nothing-Is-Outside-Him-the-Voice-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the first half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Devarim: Moshe's rebuke in hints, the judges, the spies recalled, the journeys past Edom and Moav, Moshe's prayer to enter the Land, the Ten Commandments again, the Shema and its second part, the laws of the chosen place, and "you are children to Hashem." Some passages about nations and …

&emsp;[Gur Aryeh on Devarim, lines 295-653: One Power With None Beside It, Kingship Is Oneness, He Returns With Us From Exile, and the Level With No Division](manuscripts/Gur-Aryeh-on-Devarim-lines-295-653-One-Power-With-None-Beside-It-Kingship-Is-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the last part of Gur Aryeh: the laws of food and tithes, the festivals, judges and kings, prophets, witnesses, war, the laws between people, bikkurim, the curses, Nitzavim, the song of Haazinu, Moshe's blessings, and his death. This is the first half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Shemot: the slavery, the burning bush, the plagues, Pesach, the sea, the manna, Amalek, and Yitro's coming. Most lines are close reading of Rashi. But some of the Maharal's clearest words on the Names of G-d are here.

This is the second half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Shemot: the judges, Sinai and the Ten Commandments, the laws, the Mishkan and the priests' garments, the golden calf, Moshe's prayers, the Thirteen Attributes, and the building of the Mishkan. Much of it is law.

&emsp;[Gur Aryeh on Vayikra, lines 1-460: Called by Name, the Low Near the High, Moshe the Simple, and the Menorah From the World of Oneness](manuscripts/Gur-Aryeh-on-Vayikra-lines-1-460-Called-by-Name-the-Low-Near-the-High-Moshe-the-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the first half of the Maharal's commentary on Rashi's Vayikra: the offerings, the eight days of the Mishkan's start, the death of Nadav and Avihu, the laws of kosher and impure animals, and the laws of skin disease. It is the most legal part of Gur Aryeh so far.

Much is law, and a large part is left out under D28.

&emsp;[Ner Mitzvah, lines 1-90: The One and the Four Sides, and the Candle of the Soul](manuscripts/Ner-Mitzvah-lines-1-90-The-One-and-the-Four-Sides-and-the-Candle-of-the-Soul-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Ner Mitzvah (The Candle of the Mitzvah) is a short book for Chanukah. Volume I explains why there are four kingdoms that rule the world before the end. This range is the Maharal's handbook of prayer and of giving. The Path of Service (Avodah) walks through the day: waking, the Shema, the Amidah, bowing, Amen, blessings over food, grace after meals, and the Shema at bedtime. Then come the paths of chesed (lovingkindness), tzedakah (charity), judgment and truth.

&emsp;[Netivot Olam, lines 637-815: Faith, Peace, Love of a Fellow, Humility, Awe of G-d, and the Tongue](manuscripts/Netivot-Olam-lines-637-815-Faith-Peace-Love-of-a-Fellow-Humility-Awe-of-God-and-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Lines 796-811 are letters and bans from the Maharal's time against people who called certain families mamzerim. They are history, not teaching, and are not used below. Some lines on the nations (698) and on women and slaves (766) are also set aside under D28.

&emsp;[Netivot Olam, lines 816-930: Love of G-d, Silence, Holiness, Modesty, the Evil Inclination, and the Righteous](manuscripts/Netivot-Olam-lines-816-930-Love-of-God-Silence-Holiness-Modesty-the-Evil-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This range opens with the Path of Love of G-d, which contains the Maharal's most direct words on the unity of G-d in the whole book: love of G-d is possible because He is One and there is nothing besides Him, so all returns to Him.

&emsp;[Netivot Olam, lines 931-1248: Returning through Decent Conduct](manuscripts/Netivot-Olam-lines-931-1248-Returning-through-Decent-Conduct-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Left out of this report under D28: the harsh words on nations in the Shame path (1156), and the ranking of women and slaves as witnesses in Derech Eretz (1242). The bitter lament over his own community (1150) is history and is not used.

&emsp;[Netzach Yisrael, lines 1-450: Exile, the One and the Many, the Bond That Cannot Break](manuscripts/Netzach-Yisrael-lines-1-450-Exile-the-One-and-the-Many-the-Bond-That-Cannot-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Netzach Yisrael ("The Eternity of Israel") is the Maharal's book on exile and redemption. It asks a hard question. If a people is bound to G-d, why does it suffer so long? The first half, covered here, is mostly about exile: why it came, why it lasts, and why it cannot be forever.

&emsp;[Netzach Yisrael, lines 451-970: Ruin as the Start of Being, and 'His Name Will Be One'](manuscripts/Netzach-Yisrael-lines-451-970-Ruin-as-the-Start-of-Being-and-His-Name-Will-Be-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

The second half of Netzach Yisrael turns from exile to redemption. It asks what the "end" is, why it is hidden, what changes when it comes, and what kind of bond with G-d will then be complete. Absence comes before being. Nothing new comes to be unless the old has first fully passed.

&emsp;[Ohr Chadash, lines 1-427: The Hidden Miracle, Knowing I Lack, the Water Channel From the One, and the Door Left Open Upward](manuscripts/Ohr-Chadash-lines-1-427-The-Hidden-Miracle-Knowing-I-Lack-the-Water-Channel-From-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Ohr Chadash ("New Light") is the Maharal's commentary on Megillat Esther. It asks why a book with no open miracle and no written Name of G-d is still a book of His hand. This first half covers the long introduction and the first two chapters: the king's feast, Vashti, and the rise of Mordechai and Esther.

&emsp;[Ohr Chadash, lines 428-990: The Heart in One's Power, Nature as His Messenger, Straightness With No End, and Standing as Nothing](manuscripts/Ohr-Chadash-lines-428-990-The-Heart-in-Ones-Power-Nature-as-His-Messenger-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This is the second half of the Maharal's commentary on Megillat Esther: Haman's rise, the decree, Mordechai in sackcloth, Esther's fast and her going to the king, the sleepless night, Haman's fall, the second letters, and the setting of Purim. Most of it reads the story line by line.

&emsp;[Tiferet Yisrael, lines 1-520: The Torah That Joins, and 'He Is Found as the Receiver Can Take Him'](manuscripts/Tiferet-Yisrael-lines-1-520-The-Torah-That-Joins-and-He-Is-Found-as-the-Receiver-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Tiferet Yisrael ("the Glory of Israel") is the Maharal's book on the Torah: what it is, why it was given, how it joins a person to G-d, and how the giving at Sinai happened. The first half runs from the Introduction to the start of his long reading of the Ten Commandments.

&emsp;[Tiferet Yisrael, lines 521-1042: 'Anochi' Is His Very Self, and Faith Is the Cleaving](manuscripts/Tiferet-Yisrael-lines-521-1042-Anochi-Is-His-Very-Self-and-Faith-Is-the-Cleaving-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

The second half of Tiferet Yisrael reads the Ten Commandments word by word, then turns to the lasting of the Torah: why it cannot change, why it seems forgotten, why the world to come is not written in it, how mitzvot are paid, and why the Torah is split into written and oral.

**024. The Shelah: nothing but His existence, renewed each moment.** Reads the Shelah, the bridge between Safed and the Chassidic masters. There is no existence but His, it is renewed each moment, and the book builds a whole day to live that out. ([Quote check](verification/catalogue.md#result-024))

&emsp;[The Shelah: nothing but His existence, renewed each moment, and a day built to live it](manuscripts/The-Shelah-nothing-but-His-existence-renewed-each-moment-and-a-day-built-to-live-September-30-2026/manuscript.md)

The Shelah is the great bridge between Safed and the Chassidic masters. He gathers the Zohar, the Ramak, the early Ari circle, the Reshit Chochmah and the Chovot HaLevavot into one book of practice, and the Chassidic masters read him daily. His unity has three parts. There is no existence but His. "There is no other reality in the world but His reality, for if He hid it, all would be lost" (line 3938).

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Asara Hillulim and Vavei HaAmudim (lines 10782-10925): a daily ten-gate summary of the duties of the heart, and his son's six pillars; He brings all into being and gives all life always; truth is His seal because He is One](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Asara-Hillulim-and-Vavei-HaAmudim-lines-10782-10925-a-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This last range holds two works. The first, Asara Hillulim ("Ten Praises"), is the Shelah's short summary of the Chovot HaLevavot (Duties of the Heart) in ten gates. He asks that one gate be read every day, "in heart and tongue", and then started again, forever (line 10783). It is the most practical page in the whole book.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Asara Maamarot (lines 836-1296): the ladder of service](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Asara-Maamarot-lines-836-1296-the-ladder-of-service-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

"The Ten Utterances" is the first of the three parts the Shelah promised in Toldot Adam. Where Toldot Adam built the theory (man in the image, the Torah as the Name, service as a need on high), this part is a ladder of the inner life.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Aseret HaDibrot, Pesachim (lines 3355-4263): kindness, renewal every moment, the inner search, the story that joins](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Aseret-HaDibrot-Pesachim-lines-3355-4263-kindness-renewal-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Pesach is the pillar of kindness (chesed); "Pesach" means "He had pity" (line 3356). The tractate holds the laws of Rosh Chodesh, Pesach and the Omer; a long section on kindness (weddings, the sick, the dying, guests); six sermons for Shabbat HaGadol that the Shelah calls Matzah Ashirah ("rich matzah"); his Haggadah commentary Matzah Shemurah ("guarded matzah"); and a short …

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Aseret HaDibrot, Rosh HaShanah and Yoma (lines 4960-5802): return as grace, seclusion with G-d, against needless self-affliction, the heart as His dwelling](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Aseret-HaDibrot-Rosh-HaShanah-and-Yoma-lines-4960-5802-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Rosh HaShanah is the pillar of judgment (din); Yoma is the pillar of return (teshuvah). Together this is the book's manual of return. Much of it is quoted: from the Shelah's father's Emek Beracha and Brit Avraham, from Rabbeinu Yonah, the Rambam, Reishit Chochmah, Sefer Charedim, the Ramak and the Ari's circle.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Aseret HaDibrot, Shevuot and Taanit (lines 4264-4959): the Torah as His oneness, nothing but His existence, study that acts, the broken heart as offering](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Aseret-HaDibrot-Shevuot-and-Taanit-lines-4264-4959-the-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Shevuot is the pillar of Torah. The Shelah gives the laws of the festival night and a long code of how to learn; then a great essay on Psalm 19 ("the Torah of G-d is whole"), in six steps: the Giver, the Torah, those who learn it, those who do it, Moshe the go-between, and "these and these are words …

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Aseret HaDibrot, Sukkah, Tamid and Megillah (lines 5803-6169): joy of body and soul for His Name, peace as unity of souls, the Shelah's manual of prayer, the three levels of the attributes, joy with the poor](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Aseret-HaDibrot-Sukkah-Tamid-and-Megillah-lines-5803-6169-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This range closes the Aseret HaDibrot part of the book. Sukkah is the pillar of peace. Tamid (the daily offering) holds the Shelah's longest teaching on prayer. Megillah joins Purim to charity. Three findings matter most for the guide.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Aseret HaDibrot: introduction, Chullin, Shabbat (lines 2668-3354): holy weekdays, the one day, rest of the heart](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Aseret-HaDibrot-introduction-Chullin-Shabbat-lines-2668-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Here the Shelah opens his second great book, the "Ten Utterances" (Aseret HaDibrot). He builds it on ten pillars (line 2668): Torah, service, kindness, justice, truth, peace, return, prayer, charity, and faith, which holds them all. Each pillar gets a season and a tractate. Weekdays (Chullin, "ordinary things") are the pillar of truth; Shabbat is the pillar of faith.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Kedushat HaAchilah (lines 1727-2325): the table as altar](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Kedushat-HaAchilah-lines-1727-2325-the-table-as-altar-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

"The holiness of eating" closes the letter kuf (holiness). It is long and mostly law: forbidden foods and the Shelah's own stricter rulings (lines 1732-1818), the laws of the Land of Israel (1819-1871), health and danger (1872-1888), and a whole code of the blessings on food taken from his father's Emek Beracha (2113-2325).

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Shaar HaOtiyot (lines 1297-1726): the alphabet of traits](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Shaar-HaOtiyot-lines-1297-1726-the-alphabet-of-traits-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

"The Gate of Letters" is the Shelah's book of traits, one trait for each letter of the alphabet (line 1297). It is his most practical part: how to get up, how to do business, how to treat a wife, a child, an enemy, a stranger, one's own body.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Shaar HaOtiyot end (lines 2326-2667): holy union, good will, silence, longing](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Shaar-HaOtiyot-end-lines-2326-2667-holy-union-good-will-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

The last pages of the Gate of Letters close the letter kuf (holiness) and then give the last three letters: resh for ratzon (good will, being easy to please), shin for shetikah (silence), tav for teshukah (longing). The Shelah ends by joining the three into one word, shevach, praise: silence, the nightly check, and desire.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Toldot Adam (lines 1-835): the unity, the states and the practice](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Toldot-Adam-lines-1-835-the-unity-the-states-and-the-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Toldot Adam ("The Generations of Man") is the long opening of the Shelah. R. Yeshayahu Horowitz wrote it as the door to the whole book, an ethical will to his children made as he left Prague for the Land of Israel (line 23).

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Torah Shebe'al Peh (lines 10293-10781): the whole Torah is His Names; the plain sense is above and ours is its garment; these and these are the words of the living G-d; learn in pairs, aloud, with joy, and ask for help](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Torah-Shebeal-Peh-lines-10293-10781-the-whole-Torah-is-His-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This part is a handbook of Talmud method. The Shelah wrote it in Jerusalem (line 10293). It is built on 27 "rules", one for each letter of "for by these words I made a covenant with you" (line 10294). Most of it is technical.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Torah Shebikhtav, Chukat through V'Zot HaBerachah (lines 9407-10292): He is all, one without division; the more you grasp, the more you grasp that He cannot be grasped; hidden in essence, revealed in deeds; cling in every act; humility is the root of all traits; praise and blame equal](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Torah-Shebikhtav-Chukat-through-VZot-HaBerachah-lines-9407-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This range closes the Shelah's commentary on the Written Torah. It has the book's plainest statements of the unity. The Zohar's "He is all; He alone is all; He is one without division" (line 10242). "There is none but Him" (line 9695).

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Torah Shebikhtav, Introduction through Vayetzei (lines 6170-7002): the world as His guests every moment, He is the place of the world, awakening from below, readiness counts as the deed, the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Torah-Shebikhtav-Introduction-through-Vayetzei-lines-6170-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

Here the book turns to the weekly Torah portions. For each portion the Shelah gives the mitzvah it holds (Ner Mitzvah), its reasons and secrets (Torah Ohr), and the traits and practices it teaches (Derech Chayim). This range covers the introduction and the first seven portions of Genesis.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Torah Shebikhtav, Mishpatim through Pekudei (lines 7832-8455): He contracted Himself to bring limit into being; He sees and is not seen; the Essence Name is not grasped; souls a part of G-d above; what is below is dearer than what is above; a small Shabbat every day](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Torah-Shebikhtav-Mishpatim-through-Pekudei-lines-7832-8455-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This range covers the laws of Mishpatim and the building of the Mishkan (the desert sanctuary). It is one of the richest stretches of the book for the unity. Three things stand out.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Torah Shebikhtav, Vayikra through Korach (lines 8456-9406): nothing exists apart from Him; He longed for a dwelling below; the Cause of all causes is the true general that holds all; the offering is yourself; a small Shabbat every day; the gates of return are always open; guard the edge](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Torah-Shebikhtav-Vayikra-through-Korach-lines-8456-9406-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

The Shelah reads all of Leviticus as one work: Torat Kohanim is "the repair of man". Before the first sin "all times were equal in holiness", and man "himself was an offering to G-d" (line 8457). Everything after, the offerings, the priests, the laws of purity, is a way back.

&emsp;[Shenei Luchot HaBerit, Torah Shebikhtav, Vayishlach through Yitro (lines 7003-7831): know it in the heart, not only by tradition; if He withheld for a moment they would be as nothing; before the will only necessary being, and that by negation; the higher, the more one; all Israel one soul](manuscripts/Shenei-Luchot-HaBerit-Torah-Shebikhtav-Vayishlach-through-Yitro-lines-7003-7831-October-5-2026/manuscript.md)

This range runs from Jacob's meeting with Esau to the giving of the Torah. Two passages carry most of the weight for the unity. In Vaera the Shelah says we were commanded to know that G-d is the only power, "with true knowledge", and that faith from tradition "is not enough" (line 7491).
